# Eliminating ramification in a purely inseparable extension of prime degree

Module `StacksFrontier.RingTheory.DiscreteValuationRing.PurelyInseparable`, an extract of the file of the same path of the Lean library AxiomMath/Stacks. The origin of the file and what the extract leaves out are given at the end of this page.

Let A → B → C be injective local maps of discrete valuation rings with compatible fraction fields K → L → M. Assume B is Nagata, the maximal ideal of A extends to the maximal ideal of B, and M/L is purely inseparable of prime degree p.

`IsDiscreteValuationRing.weaklyUnramified_or_adjoin_root_or_after_totallyRamified_baseChange` gives three alternatives for any uniformizer π of A.

1. The maximal ideal of A extends to the maximal ideal of C.
2. C is generated as a B-algebra by an element whose p-th power is π.
3. There is a separable degree-p extension K₁/K totally ramified over A. The tensors L ⊗[K] K₁ and M ⊗[K] K₁ are fields. The integral closures A₁, B₁ and C₁ of A, B and C in the corresponding fields are discrete valuation rings. The compatible injections A₁ → B₁ → C₁ each extend the source maximal ideal to the target maximal ideal.

For a field generated by a p-th root of an element of a discrete valuation ring with non-p-th-power residue, `fieldExtAlgEquiv` identifies the field with the standard root extension. `isDiscreteValuationRing_integralClosure_and_irreducible_of_pow_eq` makes its integral closure a discrete valuation ring preserving the base uniformizers, and `maximalIdeal_map_eq_of_pow_eq` says that the maximal ideal of the base generates its maximal ideal. `integralClosureEquivOfIsIntegral` compares integral closures in a common algebra across an integral extension of base rings.

If the base uniformizer fails to remain a uniformizer, a uniformizer upstairs has p-th power a unit times π. The proof expands that unit in powers of π. Approximations by p-th-power coefficients at every order yield a root in a completion, and the Nagata hypothesis descends it to obtain the second case. A first coefficient with non-p-th-power residue instead determines a separable Eisenstein base change and a new root equation to which the residue criterion applies.

The three-way theorem places all rings and fields, including the witness K₁, in one universe. Here weak unramifiedness means maximal-ideal equality after extension, and the proof derives characteristic p from the purely inseparable degree.

Tag 09EY is the three-way theorem. Tag 09EX is the root-extension result transported by the auxiliary equivalence. The module docstring also announces the degree of the root field and the description of its integral closure as generated by the root, which the file does not contain.
