# A dimension criterion for regular sequences

Module `StacksFrontier.RingTheory.CohenMacaulay.RegularSequence.DimensionCriterion`, an extract of the file of the same path of the Lean library AxiomMath/Stacks. The origin of the file and what the extract leaves out are given at the end of this page.

In a Noetherian Cohen–Macaulay local ring, the drop in Krull dimension after quotienting by a list in the maximal ideal detects whether the list is regular. The file also extends such a list to full length and computes the dimensions and Cohen–Macaulayness of the initial quotients.

## Main results

`Module.IsMaximalCohenMacaulay.isRegular_iff_ringKrullDim_add_length_eq_ringKrullDim` assumes `Module.IsMaximalCohenMacaulay R R` and that every entry of `xs` lies in the maximal ideal of the Noetherian local ring `R`. It gives three conclusions.

- The list `xs` is `R`-regular exactly when `dim(R/(xs)) + xs.length = dim(R)`.
- A regular `xs` extends by appending a list in the maximal ideal to a regular sequence of length `dim(R)`.
- If `xs` is regular and `i ≤ xs.length`, the quotient by the ideal generated by `xs.take i` is a Cohen–Macaulay local ring and its dimension plus `i` equals `dim(R)`.

Helpers relate support dimensions under successive quotients, pass regularity to prefixes, and transport tails to regular sequences over the quotient ring.

## Proof idea

The forward implication uses Mathlib's dimension formula, and the converse inducts using the fact that each cut drops dimension by at most one and the single-element criterion that a drop of one forces regularity and preserves Cohen–Macaulayness. The depth extension theorem gives an appended tail whose images, together with the rest of the original list after a prefix, form a regular sequence of full dimension on the prefix quotient.

## Scope

The public equivalence is for the ring over itself. The private converse `isRegular_of_isCohenMacaulayAtMaximalIdeal` works for finite nonzero Cohen–Macaulay modules, with support dimension in place of ring dimension. The additive dimension formula uses `WithBot ℕ∞` and forces the quotient to be nonzero. The local ring structures on initial quotients are existentially bound, and their depth is computed over the quotient rings.
