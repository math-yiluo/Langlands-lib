# Kummer theory: recovering a radical group from its extension

Module `Nyll.GaloisCohomology.Kummer.Concrete.InfiniteInverse`, copied unchanged from the Lean library NYLL. The origin of the file is given at the end of this page.

Let `Ω` be a separable closure of `K` (`IsSepClosure K Ω`), `K` containing a primitive `n`-th root of unity, and `Δ` a `KummerSubgroup K n` (a subgroup of `Kˣ` containing `Kˣⁿ`). The file proves that the elements of `Kˣ` having an `n`-th root in `K(ⁿ√Δ)` are exactly those of `Δ`: the subgroup-side round trip of the Kummer correspondence, for possibly infinite `Δ / Kˣⁿ`.

## Main results

- `finiteKummerRadicalSubgroup_kummerRadicalExtension_le`: every `a ∈ Kˣ` with an `n`-th root in `K(ⁿ√Δ)` lies in `Δ`.
- `finiteKummerRadicalSubgroup_kummerRadicalExtension_eq`: if moreover `n ≠ 0` in `K`, the group of such `a` equals `Δ`.

## Finite-support stage

For a finite set `T` of roots of elements of `Δ`, with `Δ₀` the library's admissible finite-support subgroup (`Kˣⁿ` together with the chosen `n`-th powers of the elements of `T`):

- `finiteSupportField`: the field `K(T)`; it is finite-dimensional (`finiteSupportField_finiteDimensional`) and, over a separable closure, Galois (`finiteSupportField_isGalois`).
- `admissibleFiniteSupportSubgroup_le_finiteKummerRadicalSubgroup`: every element of `Δ₀` has an `n`-th root in `K(T)`.
- `kummerRadicalExtension_admissibleFiniteSupport_eq`: `K(ⁿ√Δ₀) = K(T)`; the inclusion into `K(T)` (`kummerRootSet_admissibleFiniteSupport_subset`) uses that two `n`-th roots of the same element differ by a root of unity, which lies in `K`.
- `finiteSupportField_internalRoots_adjoin_eq_top`: `K(T)` is generated by its own roots of elements of `Δ₀`.

## Proof

If `a` has an `n`-th root `β ∈ K(ⁿ√Δ)`, then `β` lies in `K(T)` for some finite `T` (finite support file). The finite inverse theorem `finiteKummerRadicalSubgroup_eq_of_adjoin` from the library's restricted finite Kummer file, applied to the finite Galois extension `K(T)/K` and `Δ₀`, shows that the elements of `Kˣ` with an `n`-th root in `K(T)` form exactly `Δ₀`. Hence `a ∈ Δ₀ ≤ Δ`. The reverse inclusion in the equality is the library's `le_finiteKummerRadicalSubgroup_kummerRadicalExtension`, which uses `n ≠ 0` in `K`.

## Points to watch

- The inclusion `≤` does not need `n ≠ 0` in `K`; the equality does.
- `Ω` must be a separable closure of `K`; `KummerSubgroup`, `kummerRadicalExtension` and the Galois property of radical extensions come from other files of the library.
