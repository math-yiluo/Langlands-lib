# Finiteness and multiplicativity of the Herbrand quotient

Module `Nyll.GaloisCohomology.Cyclic.Herbrand.HerbrandFiniteness`, copied unchanged from the Lean library NYLL. The origin of the file is given at the end of this page.

Let G be a finite group generated by an element σ, and let 1 → A → B → C → 1 be a short exact sequence of G-modules, written multiplicatively: commutative groups with a G-action by group automorphisms, `i : A → B` injective and equivariant, `j : B → C` surjective and equivariant, with kernel of `j` equal to the image of `i`. The Herbrand quotient h(M) = |Ĥ⁰(G, M)| / |Ĥ⁻¹(G, M)| is "defined" (`HerbrandQuotientDefined G M σ`) when both groups `HerbrandH0 G M` and `HerbrandHMinusOne G M σ` are finite. This file states that definedness for two terms of the sequence implies it for the third, and packages multiplicativity accordingly.

## Main results

- `herbrandQuotientDefined_middle_of_left_right`, `herbrandQuotientDefined_right_of_left_middle`, `herbrandQuotientDefined_left_of_middle_right`: if the Herbrand quotients of two of A, B, C are defined, so is that of the third. `herbrandQuotientDefined_anyTwo` collects the three implications.
- `herbrandQuotient_multiplicative_of_shortExact`: if all six groups are finite, then h(B) = h(A)·h(C).
- `herbrandQuotient_multiplicative_of_left_right_defined`, `_of_left_middle_defined`, `_of_middle_right_defined`: if two of the quotients are defined (given as finiteness instances), the third is defined, and multiplicativity holds for the finiteness witness so obtained (stated with an existential witness).
- `herbrandQuotient_eq_one_of_finite_module`: if A is finite, then h(A) = 1.

## Proofs

Each result is a direct consequence of two imported facts: the "any two imply the third" finiteness statement coming from the periodic exact hexagon (`herbrandQuotientDefined_anyTwo_of_periodic`), and multiplicativity once all quotients are defined (`herbrandQuotient_exact_multiplicative`). The finite-module case is likewise an imported result restated.

## Points to watch

- The Herbrand quotient and the group Ĥ⁻¹ are the library's own definitions, and formally depend on the chosen generator σ.
- Modules are multiplicative commutative groups with a distributive action (`MulDistribMulAction`), not Mathlib's `Rep` objects; A, B, C are required to live in one universe.
- In the "two defined" forms, the Herbrand quotient of the third term is computed with the finiteness witness produced by the theorem, which is why it is stated existentially.
