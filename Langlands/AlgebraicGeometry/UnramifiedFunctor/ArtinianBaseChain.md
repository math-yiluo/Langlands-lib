# Step 1 of Murre's induction: chains of length-one extensions

Module `Langlands.AlgebraicGeometry.UnramifiedFunctor.ArtinianBaseChain`, a supporting module of
`murreArtinianBase` (module `MurreArtinianBase`). Namespace
`Langlands.AlgebraicGeometry.UnramifiedFunctor.ArtinianBase`. Notation as in `ArtinianBaseTools`.
In particular, `IsOver ι π η w` means that `w` lies over the field point `η` pointwise.

The module iterates (F4′) along chains of subalgebras whose consecutive members differ by
length one. This is how a field point `η ∈ F(Spec ℓ)` is lifted from a bigger ring to a smaller
one with the same residue field. Murre's reduction of (F4) to (F4′) (Bourbaki exp. 294,
Corollary 1, PDF p. 6: "using (F1b) and (F5)") is not spelled out in the source. This module
and the next two are the corresponding internal argument.

## One step

* `adjoinStep N y hy`: for a `C`-subalgebra `N ⊆ X` and `y ∈ X` with `y² ∈ N + N y`, the
  subalgebra `N + N y`. `le_adjoinStep` and `mem_adjoinStep_self` are its basic properties.
* `step`: `N` local artinian with residue field reached from `C`, `y ∉ N`, `m_N y ⊆ N`. An
  element of `F(Spec (N + N y))` over `η` gives one of `F(Spec N)` over `η`. The quotient
  `(N + N y)/N` is generated by `y` and killed by `m_N`, so it is isomorphic to the residue
  field of `N` and has length one. The step is then `artLift`.

## The chain argument

Fix `C ⊆ X` with `C` local artinian, `X` finite over `C`, and a nilpotent ideal `J` of `X`
containing the image of `m_C`, with `C → X` injective.

* `subD J`: the subalgebra `C + J`.
* `isNilpotent_of_mem`, `one_notMem`, `isUnit_or_mem`, `not_isUnit_of_mem`: in a subalgebra
  `N ⊆ C + J`, an element is a unit or lies in `J`.
* `isLocalRing_of_le`: such an `N` is local, with maximal ideal `N ∩ J` (`mem_maximalIdeal_iff`).
* `isArtinianRing_of_le`: `N` is artinian, being finite over `C`.
* `residue_comp_surjective`: the residue field of `N` is reached from `C`.
* `chain`: if `η` has an element over it on `C + J`, then it has one on every subalgebra
  `N ⊆ C + J` containing `C`. The proof is well-founded induction downwards on `N`.
  - If `N ≠ C + J`, let `i + 1` be the least exponent with `J^(i+1) ⊆ N`. Then `i ≥ 1`, and
    there is `y ∈ J^i \ N`.
  - Then `y² ∈ J^(i+1) ⊆ N` and `m_N y ⊆ J^(i+1) ⊆ N`.
  - `N + N y ⊆ C + J` is strictly larger than `N`. By induction it carries an element over
    `η`, and `step` descends it to `N`.
* `descend`: the case `N = C`. The element on `C` restricts to `η` (`IsOver.res_eq`).

## Auxiliary lemmas

* `exists_factor`: an `R`-algebra map killing the kernel of a surjection factors through it.
* `prod_field_cases`: a map from `A × B` to a field factors through one projection.
* `isOver_of_surjective`: from `res π' w = η` to `IsOver q (π' ∘ q) η w` for a surjection `q`.

## Consequences

* `descend_local`: let `C ⊆ A` be local artinian rings, `A` finite over `C`, with the maximal
  ideal of `C` going into that of `A` and the residue field of `A` reached from `C`. Then an
  element of `F(Spec A)` over `η` gives `w ∈ F(Spec C)` with `res π w = η`. Here `C + m_A = A`,
  and `descend` applies with `J = m_A`.
* `inter_closed`: let `P` be local artinian with residue map `π : P → ℓ`, and `I, I'` ideals of
  `P`. If `η` has elements over it on `P/I` and on `P/I'`, then it has one on `P/(I ∩ I')`.
  - Proper ideals are the main case.
  - `Q = P/(I ∩ I')` embeds in `X = P/I × P/I'`. With `J = m_Q X` and `D = Q + J`, one has
    `X = D + D·(1, 0)`.
  - `(1, 0) ∉ D`: two maps `X → ℓ` agree on `D` but not on `(1, 0)`.
  - (F1a) (`exists_prod`) glues the two elements to `X`.
  - `step` descends from `X` to `D`, and `descend` from `D` to `Q`.
