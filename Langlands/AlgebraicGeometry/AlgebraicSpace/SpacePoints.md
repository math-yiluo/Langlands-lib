# Points of presheaves and algebraic spaces and their topology

Author: Worker 105.

This module defines the set of points `|F|` of a presheaf `F` on schemes over a scheme `S`, the
maps of points induced by natural transformations, the points of a scheme as points of its
functor of points, and the topology on `|F|`. For an algebraic space `X` (from
`Langlands/AlgebraicGeometry/AlgebraicSpace/Basic.lean`) the points are those of its presheaf
`X.presheaf`. Only definitions and their defining identities are given.

The source is the Stacks Project, registered as
`stacks-spaces-properties-morphisms-ed88ff78-2026-11-05` (folder fingerprint
`cfb36cfc55110aebc6c164e21700e0231c545ae581586883cc1505c67292a90f`), chapter *Properties of
Algebraic Spaces*, Section 4 (Tag 03BT), PDF pp.4–6. Stacks defines, for a presheaf `F` and a
field `K`, an equivalence of pairs `(Spec K, p)`, `p ∈ F(Spec K)`: two pairs are equivalent "if
there exists a third field `Ω`" with field extensions `K → Ω`, `L → Ω` such that "`p` and `q` map
to the same element of `F(Spec(Ω))`". Definition 4.1 (Tag 03BU): "A point of `X` is an
equivalence class of morphisms from spectra of fields into `X`." Lemma 4.6 (Tag 03BX)
characterizes the topology. In its proof, `W ⊂ |X|` is open if and only if its preimage in
`|U|` is open, for a surjective étale `U → X` from a scheme.

## Definitions

Throughout, `S : Scheme.{u}` and `F` is a presheaf `(Over S)ᵒᵖ ⥤ Type (u + 1)` (the Library's
`Presheaf S`).

- `FieldPoint F`: a field `K : Type u`, a morphism `base : Spec K ⟶ S`, and a section
  `val : F(Spec K)` over the test scheme `Over.mk base`. This is a morphism `Spec K → F` in the
  Yoneda sense.
- `FieldPoint.extendTest p i` and `FieldPoint.extendMap p i`: for a field homomorphism
  `i : K → Ω`, the test scheme `Spec Ω` over `S` through `Spec Ω → Spec K → S`, and the morphism
  `Spec Ω ⟶ Spec K` over `S`.
- `FieldPoint.Rel F p q`: there are a field `Ω : Type u`, homomorphisms `i : K_p → Ω`,
  `j : K_q → Ω` with `Spec j ≫ base_q = Spec i ≫ base_p`, such that the pullbacks of the two
  sections to `Spec Ω` are equal. This is Stacks' relation.
- `Points F`: the quotient `Quot (FieldPoint.Rel F)`. Lean's `Quot` takes the equivalence
  relation generated by `Rel`. Stacks states that `Rel` is already an equivalence relation,
  so the quotient is Stacks' set of points.
- `FieldPoint.map f p` and `Points.map f`: the image under a natural transformation
  `f : F ⟶ G`, which keeps the field and structure morphism and applies `f` to the section
  (Stacks: `|f|` maps `x` to `f ∘ x`). `FieldPoint.map_rel` proves compatibility with the
  relation, by naturality of `f`. `Points.map_mk`, `Points.map_id` and `Points.map_comp` are
  the defining formula and functoriality.
- `schemePoint U u`: for a scheme `U` over `S` and a point `u` of its underlying space, the
  point of the functor of points `(schemeYoneda S).obj U` given by the canonical morphism
  `Spec κ(u) ⟶ U`. Stacks, Lemma 4.2 (Tag 03BV), identifies the points of a scheme with these.
- `pointTopology F`: the final topology on `Points F` for all maps
  `Points.map a ∘ schemePoint U : U → Points F`, where `U` is a scheme over `S` and
  `a : h_U ⟶ F` is étale in the Library sense `IsEtale` (representable, with étale represented
  projections). Each `U` carries its Zariski topology.

## Relation to the source and derived content

Stacks topologizes `|X|` for an algebraic space `X` as the quotient of `|U|` for one surjective
étale atlas `U → X`, and proves independence of the atlas and property (3) of Lemma 4.6: for
every étale `U → X` from a scheme, `|U| → |X|` is continuous and open. Every étale chart is
continuous for the quotient topology, so the quotient topology of one atlas is coarser than or
equal to the final topology for all charts. The final topology is in turn coarser than or
equal to the quotient topology, since the atlas is one of the charts. The two therefore agree.
This module defines the final topology for all étale charts directly, which needs no choice
of atlas. The agreement is a separate statement.

The definitions make sense for every presheaf, which is used for fibre products of algebraic
spaces computed as pullbacks of presheaves. Stacks' "we omit the verification that this
defines an equivalence relation" is not formalized; it is not needed for the definition.
The comparison of `|h_U|` with the underlying space of a scheme `U` (Stacks, Lemmas 4.2 and
4.6(1)) is a separate statement. The names are chosen here.
