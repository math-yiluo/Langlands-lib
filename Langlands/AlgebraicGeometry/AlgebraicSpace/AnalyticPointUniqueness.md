# Uniqueness of the analytic point structure

This derived internal proof unit implements the uniqueness part of Conrad's Proposition 5.4 (printed pp.15–16, `conrad-2011-weil-grothendieck-adelic-points`). It compares arbitrary admissible families on actual rational points, retaining the published étale-chart topology. The comparison needs only a nontrivially normed field; completeness is used in the separate existence construction. All universes and dimensions, including dimension zero and empty point sets, are retained.

A smooth scheme of pure relative dimension n supplies an object of the family through its identity atlas. Polynomial affine space has this relative dimension by its standard-smooth polynomial presentation. `AnalyticIdentity` is the proposition that the identity is analytic in one direction between two families' structures on the same point set. The exact coordinate normalization makes this identity analytic on affine space: compose the first family's coordinate diffeomorphism with the inverse of the second family's coordinate diffeomorphism. Both coordinate functions are the prescribed variable-value function, so this composite is the identity.

Analytic identity comparisons pull back along an étale map by composing the map, the identity on its target, and the local analytic inverse in the second family. The inverse composite agrees with the identity on a neighborhood. Comparisons also descend at the image of an étale source: the identity on the source followed by the map is analytic, and the map is a local analytic diffeomorphism in the first family. The published local-diffeomorphism descent lemma then gives analyticity of the target identity at that image. The two charted-space structures are explicit throughout these comparisons.

For an affine scheme with standard-smooth coordinate algebra of relative dimension n, Mathlib's `Algebra.IsStandardSmoothOfRelativeDimension.exists_etale_mvPolynomial` supplies an actual étale algebra map from the polynomial algebra in n variables. The affine scheme's canonical global-section isomorphism therefore gives an étale scheme map to affine n-space. Its Yoneda image is an étale morphism of algebraic spaces. Pullback of the affine-space identity comparison proves agreement on this standard-smooth scheme.

For a rational point of a general space, choose the published affine étale source of the same relative dimension. Algebraic smoothness gives a principal neighborhood of the lifted point whose actual global-section algebra is standard smooth of that dimension. The whole image of the rational point lies in this open because its value ring is a field, so the point lifts to the neighborhood. The neighborhood has the same smooth relative dimension, and its composite map to the original space is étale. The preceding standard-smooth comparison descends to the original rational point. This proves `analyticIdentity` globally. Applying the result in each direction gives `equivalent_of_isAdmissible`, exactly the published `Equivalent` condition, without any extra field or geometric hypotheses.
