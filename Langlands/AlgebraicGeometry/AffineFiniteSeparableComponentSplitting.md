# Finite separable splitting for affine schemes of finite type

Let `K` be a field and `A` a commutative `K`-algebra of finite type, both in an arbitrary common universe `u`. The construction `affineComponentSplittingField K A` chooses an actual intermediate field `E` of `SeparableClosure K / K`. Its three specification theorems prove that `E` is finite dimensional and separable over `K`, and that every actual maximal irreducible component of `Spec(E ⊗[K] A)` is geometrically irreducible over `E`. The structural morphism is the spectrum map of `algebraMap E (E ⊗[K] A)`. The component predicate uses each closed component's vanishing-ideal reduced scheme structure, as defined in `SchemeComponentGeometry`.

This is an internal derived construction for the affine finite-type case of the finite separable component-splitting problem. Its proof combines the coefficient models from `FiniteTensorIdealModel`, the quotient criterion from `TensorSpectrumBaseChange`, and the finite closed-cover comparison from `GeometricallyIrreducibleClosedCover`. Those imported modules supply the algebraic descent and actual scheme comparisons; the construction assumes neither a splitting field nor geometrically irreducible descended components.

The first helper, `componentsGeometricallyIrreducible_of_tensorIdealModel`, works over any separably closed extension `L/K`. It assumes a finite family of ideals of `L ⊗[K] A` whose zero loci are nonempty irreducible subsets covering that spectrum, together with one finite coefficient model. The model supplies an intermediate field `E` and ideals `J_i` whose extensions equal the given ideals. The quotient schemes `Spec((E ⊗[K] A)/J_i)` are actual closed subschemes; the quotient geometric-irreducibility criterion proves that their morphisms to `Spec E` are geometrically irreducible. This conclusion does not require the descended ideals to be prime or radical.

The coefficient map `Spec(L ⊗[K] A) → Spec(E ⊗[K] A)` is surjective because its square over the surjective map of field spectra `Spec L → Spec E` is a pullback. The inverse-image identity for zero loci, the model's ideal-extension equalities, and this surjectivity descend the asserted cover to the zero loci of the `J_i`. The quotient spectrum maps have precisely these ranges. The finite closed-cover theorem then identifies every actual maximal component with the support of a geometrically irreducible quotient scheme and transfers geometric irreducibility to its vanishing-ideal reduced closed structure. The finite family may be empty when the spectrum is empty; no nonempty spectrum hypothesis is added. The helper's finite index type can lie in an independent universe `v`.

For `exists_finite_separable_tensor_components`, take `L = SeparableClosure K`. Finite type over `L` makes `L ⊗[K] A` Noetherian. Its actual maximal irreducible components form a finite index type. The vanishing ideal of each component is finitely generated, and its zero locus equals that component because it is closed. The components cover the spectrum. Since `L/K` is algebraic, `FiniteTensorIdealModel.construct` supplies a common finite intermediate coefficient field and descended ideals. Applying the first helper gives the stated existential result. Classical choice defines `affineComponentSplittingField` from that proved existence result; its finiteness and component theorems are the two parts of the choice specification. Separability follows from the intermediate field's inclusion in the separable closure.

No reducedness, separation, perfectness or characteristic assumption is imposed. Finite type is used here to obtain Noetherianity after extension and finite generation of component ideals. Passing from these tensor spectra to general affine structure maps, choosing a common extension for a finite affine cover, and the unrestricted locally finite-type splitting theorem are separate downstream steps. This module alone does not prove the unrestricted `FiniteSeparableComponentSplitting` statement.
