# The generation locus of a tensor product of line-bundle sections

This module states one proposition and proves nothing:

```lean
def Langlands.SchemeModule.TensorSectionGenerationLocusStatement : Prop :=
  ∀ (X : Scheme.{u}) (L M : LineBundle X)
    (s : GlobalSections L.obj) (t : GlobalSections M.obj),
    trivializationOpen (Langlands.Scheme.Modules.tensorObj L.obj M.obj)
        (tensorSection L.obj M.obj s t) =
      trivializationOpen L.obj s ⊓ trivializationOpen M.obj t
```

It has one universe parameter `u`. `LineBundle` is
`Langlands.AlgebraicGeometry.Picard.LineBundle`; the other names are in
`Langlands.SchemeModule`, except the tensor object, which is the published
`Langlands.Scheme.Modules.tensorObj`.

## Meaning

Let `X` be an arbitrary scheme in `Scheme.{u}`, with no affine, separated,
quasi-compact or noetherian assumption. `L` and `M` are line bundles in the
Library sense. Each is a module sheaf on `X` that is isomorphic, on some open
neighbourhood of every point, to the structure sheaf of that open. `s` and `t`
are arbitrary global sections of the two underlying sheaves, and either may be
zero.

The ingredients are the published constructions, not local replacements:

- `Langlands.Scheme.Modules.tensorObj L.obj M.obj` is the actual tensor
  product of module sheaves. It is the sheafification of the sectionwise
  tensor product of the underlying presheaves of modules.
- `tensorSection L.obj M.obj s t` is the published product section
  `s ⊗ t`. Its section morphism from the structure module is the inverse left
  unitor of the structure sheaf followed by the tensor of the two section
  morphisms (`sectionHom_tensorSection` in
  `Langlands.AlgebraicGeometry.Modules.TensorSections`).
- `trivializationOpen N r` is the supremum in `X.Opens` of all opens `U` on
  which `TrivializesOn N r U` holds. That predicate says that the restriction
  to `U` of the morphism from the structure module sending `1` to `r` is an
  isomorphism. It is therefore the whole maximal open on which `r` generates
  `N`, not a chosen neighbourhood or an affine subopen.

The proposition says that these whole opens satisfy

```text
trivializationOpen (L ⊗ M) (s ⊗ t) = trivializationOpen L s ⊓ trivializationOpen M t
```

as an equality in `X.Opens`. In words, the product section generates exactly
on the intersection of the two generation loci. The inclusion `⊇` says that
the product generates wherever both factors do. The inclusion `⊆` says that
the product can generate only where both factors already generate.

No hypothesis is added. In particular, the statement assumes no residue-field
comparison for the tensor product, no rank-one or line-bundle property of the
tensor object, and no flatness, quasi-coherence, coherence or noetherian
condition. It also assumes no monoidal-preservation, pullback or ampleness
property. The rank-one property enters only through the types of `L` and `M`.

## Source correspondence

The original source is The Stacks Project, Section 28.27, "Ample invertible
sheaves", tag [01PR](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/01PR). It is
registered as `stacks-ampleness-tensor-isomorphism-2026-10-24`, with retained
file
`Stacks Project - Ampleness tensor and isomorphism transport (HTML snapshot 2026-10-24)/01PR.html`
and directory SHA256
`23bfc9d1d2b4467556c7308dd203f22596c80d2f5276f1d5f9829673427e2f2d`.
The opening paragraph of the section recalls that for an invertible sheaf
`𝓛` and a global section `s`, the set
`X_s = {x ∈ X | s ∉ 𝔪_x 𝓛_x}` is open. It then makes the general remark
`X_s ∩ X_{s'} = X_{ss'}`, where `ss'` denotes the section
`s ⊗ s' ∈ Γ(X, 𝓛 ⊗ 𝓛')`. The proof of Lemma 28.27.5 uses this remark for
products of sections of positive powers. The present proposition formalizes
that remark for two line bundles and the product of one section of each,
with the two sides of the source equality exchanged.

The representation of the source locus is an internal translation. The source
describes `X_s` pointwise, by nonvanishing of `s` modulo the maximal ideal of
the stalk. This statement uses instead the Library's whole trivialization open,
the largest open on which `𝒪_X → 𝓛`, `1 ↦ s`, is an isomorphism. The lemma
recalled in the source, Stacks Lemma 17.25.10, also states that
`s : 𝒪_{X_s} → 𝓛|_{X_s}` is an isomorphism, so `X_s` lies in the
trivialization open. Conversely, at a point `x` of an open where this map is
an isomorphism, `𝓛_x ≅ 𝒪_{X,x}` is generated by the stalk of `s`, so
`s ∉ 𝔪_x 𝓛_x`. In the Library this agreement for line bundles is the published
theorem `Langlands.SchemeModule.lineBundleResidueOpenComparison`
(module `Langlands.AlgebraicGeometry.Modules.SectionResidueComparison`). For
every line bundle `L` and global section `s`, it proves

```lean
nonvanishingLocus L.obj s = (trivializationOpen L.obj s : Set X).
```

Here `nonvanishingLocus` is the set of points at which the pullback of `s` to
the residue-field point is nonzero. This comparison explains why the
trivialization opens of `s` and `t` represent the source's `X_s` and `X_{s'}`.
It is neither imported nor assumed by the statement, which involves only
trivialization opens. For the product, the left-hand side
`trivializationOpen (tensorObj L.obj M.obj) (tensorSection L.obj M.obj s t)`
is used directly as the generation locus. The statement does not assume that
the tensor object is a line bundle, so no residue-field form of that side is
asserted or needed.

Further translations are as follows. The source's invertible modules are
represented by the Library's locally free rank-one sheaves (`LineBundle`).
Stacks Lemma 17.25.4 identifies the two notions when all stalks of the
structure sheaf are local rings, as they are on a scheme; the `LineBundle`
Markdown records this. Lemmas 17.25.4 and 17.25.10 are in the registered
source `stacks-local-freeness-invertibility-sections-2026-10-21`, in the
retained file `01CR.html` of its folder, whose registered content SHA256 is
`6d1641a9deb284be889ee6e2630a2be6e57cc5985c80af2c9da7d17543ed0785`.
The source's product `ss'` is the published `tensorSection`, and its tensor
product is the published sheafified tensor. The source works on a scheme `X`.
It writes `𝓛` and `𝓛'` for two invertible sheaves that may differ, and the
present statement likewise keeps two independent line bundles `L` and `M`.

The current source and page comments were checked in the registered snapshot
`stacks-01pr-current-errata-check-2026-10-26`, content SHA256
`10e5729facc1f46a826ad3adad02c107aa2d7aec3f87b7650122c46c5d9a59c1`.
Its opening paragraph has the same wording, including the remark
`X_s ∩ X_{s'} = X_{ss'}`. Its five comments concern ampleness on a scheme
