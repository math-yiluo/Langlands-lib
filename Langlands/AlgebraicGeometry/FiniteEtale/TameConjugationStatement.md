# Tame conjugation of finite ℓ-group images along a regular local divisor

The module `Langlands.AlgebraicGeometry.FiniteEtale.TameConjugationStatement`
defines a cyclotomic condition on an exponent `q` and the morphism from the
generic point of a strictly Henselian trait to `Spec R[1/t]`. It then states
`RegularDivisorTameConjugationStatement`, the finite-group form of the
cyclotomic conjugation law for tame inertia along a regular divisor of a
Henselian regular local scheme. The module states the proposition and does
not prove it.

## Definitions

- `CyclotomicPowerCondition k ℓ q`, for a field `k` and natural numbers `ℓ`,
  `q`, is the conjunction of `1 < q` and the following: for every `n : ℕ`
  there is a `k`-algebra automorphism
  `φ : SeparableClosure k ≃ₐ[k] SeparableClosure k` with `φ ζ = ζ ^ q` for
  every `ζ` in `SeparableClosure k` with `ζ ^ (ℓ ^ n) = 1`. The automorphism
  `φ` may depend on `n`; the exponent `q` is the same for every `n`. For
  example, if `k` is a finite field with `q` elements, the automorphism
  `x ↦ x ^ q` of `SeparableClosure k` satisfies the condition for every `n`.
- `traitGenericRingHom L t ht : Localization.Away t →+* L` is defined for
  rings `R`, `W` (a domain), a fraction field `L` of `W`
  (`IsFractionRing W L`), algebras `R → W → L`, `t : R` and
  `ht : algebraMap R W t ≠ 0`. It is Mathlib's `IsLocalization.Away.lift` of
  the composite `R → W → L`; the image of `t` in `L` is nonzero, hence a unit,
  so the composite extends uniquely to `R[1/t]`.
- `traitGenericMorphism L t ht : Spec L ⟶ Spec (Localization.Away t)` is
  `Spec.map` of `traitGenericRingHom L t ht`. In the statement it is the
  morphism `j : Spec L → U = Spec R[1/t]`.

## Statement

`RegularDivisorTameConjugationStatement` has universe parameters `u` (rings,
schemes and the geometric point) and `v` (the finite group). It quantifies
over the following data:

- a domain `R` that is a regular local ring (`IsRegularLocalRing R`, Mathlib's
  notion: Noetherian local with maximal ideal generated by `dim R` elements)
  and Henselian (`HenselianLocalRing R`). Its residue field
  `k = ResidueField R` need not be separably closed;
- an element `t` with `t ∈ maximalIdeal R`, `t ∉ maximalIdeal R ^ 2` (a
  regular parameter) and `IsRegularLocalRing (R ⧸ Ideal.span {t})` (`R/(t)`
  is regular). The divisor `D = V(t)` is a regular divisor and
  `U = Spec R[1/t] = Spec R - D`;
- a natural number `ℓ` with `Fact ℓ.Prime` and `IsUnit (ℓ : R)`;
- a discrete valuation ring `W` that is Henselian with separably closed
  residue field (`HenselianLocalRing W`, `IsSepClosed (ResidueField W)`),
  that is, a strictly Henselian discrete valuation ring, and a fraction field
  `L` of `W` (`IsFractionRing W L`);
- an `R`-algebra structure on `W` whose structure map is a local homomorphism
  (`IsLocalHom (algebraMap R W)`), together with
  `ht : Irreducible (algebraMap R W t)`. In a discrete valuation ring the
  irreducible elements are the uniformizers, so `R → W` sends `t` to a
  uniformizer;
- an algebraically closed field `Ω` and a geometric point
  `s : Spec Ω ⟶ Spec L`;
- a natural number `q` with `CyclotomicPowerCondition (ResidueField R) ℓ q`;
- a group `H` with `Finite H`, the discrete topology (`DiscreteTopology H`)
  and `IsPGroup ℓ H`.

Put `j = traitGenericMorphism L t ht.ne_zero`. The geometric point of `U` is
`s ≫ j`. For every group homomorphism

`ρ : FundamentalGroup (s ≫ j) (geometricFiberFinite (s ≫ j)) →* H`

with `Continuous ρ`, and every `g : FundamentalGroup s (geometricFiberFinite s)`,
put

`jg = fundamentalGroupMap j s (s ≫ j) rfl (geometricFiberFinite s)
(geometricFiberFinite (s ≫ j)) g`,

the image `j₍*₎(g)` of `g` under the homomorphism `π₁(Spec L, s) → π₁(U, s ≫ j)`
induced by `j`. The conclusion is that there exists
`σ : FundamentalGroup (s ≫ j) (geometricFiberFinite (s ≫ j))` with

`ρ (σ * jg * σ⁻¹) = ρ jg ^ q`.

Here `FundamentalGroup` is the Library's finite étale fundamental group (the
automorphism group of the fibre functor at an actual geometric point, with
its profinite topology), `fundamentalGroupMap` is the Library's homomorphism
induced by a morphism of schemes and compatible geometric points, and
`geometricFiberFinite` is the Library's proof that geometric fibres are
finite. No property of `ρ` other than continuity is assumed. The element `σ`
may depend on `ρ` and `g`; the exponent `q` does not.

## Relation to the sources

SGA 7 I (*Groupes de monodromie en géométrie algébrique*, directed by
A. Grothendieck, Lecture Notes in Mathematics 288, 1972), Exposé I
(*Résumé des premiers exposés de A. Grothendieck*, written by P. Deligne),
registered as `deligne-1972-sga7-i-expose-i-monodromie`, page numbers of the
exposé:

- 0.3 (pp. I-3 and I-4), (0.3.1): for a Henselian trait with generic point
  `η` and residue characteristic exponent `p`, `Gal(η̄/η) ⊃ I ⊃ P` with
  `Gal(η̄/η)/I ≅ Gal(s̄/s)`, `I/P ≅ lim_n μ_n(k(s̄)) = Ẑ(1)(k(s̄))` and `P` a
  pro-`p`-group; the action of `Gal(η̄/η)/I` on `I/P` by inner automorphisms
  is the natural action of `Gal(s̄/s)` on `Ẑ(1)(k(s̄))`. The map
  `I/P → μ_n` is read off from the action on an element `u` of valuation
  `1/n`: `σ(u) ≡ φ_n(σ)·u` modulo elements of higher valuation.
- 0.4 (p. I-4), (0.4.1): for a local regular `S` (on the qualifier see
  below), a regular divisor `D` and the characteristic exponent `p'` of the
  generic point of `D`, Abhyankar's lemma gives the analogous dévissage `π₁(S - D, η̄) ⊃ I ⊃ P` with
  `π₁(S - D, η̄)/I ≅ Gal(s̄/s)`, `I/P ≅ Ẑ(1)(k(s̄))`, `P` without quotient of
  order prime to `p'`, and the action of `π₁(S - D, η̄)/I` on `I/P` by inner
  automorphisms equal to the natural action of `Gal(s̄/s)` on `Ẑ(1)(k(s̄))`.
  The paragraph begins with "S strictement local régulier", but (0.4.1)
  contains the quotient `Gal(s̄/s)`, and (1.3.1) applies it to the
  Henselian local schemes `S_i = Spec B_i` of 0.5. The Lean statement takes
  `R` Henselian, as in that use. When `k` is separably closed, the
  hypothesis `CyclotomicPowerCondition k ℓ q` fails: the identity is then
  the only `k`-automorphism of `SeparableClosure k`, and `ζ ^ q = ζ` for all
  `ℓ`-power roots of unity, with `ℓ` different from the characteristic of
  `k`, would force `q = 1`.
- (1.3.1) (p. I-7): the dévissages (0.3.1) and (0.4.1) are compatible:
  `Gal(η̄/η) → π₁(S_i - D_i, η̄)` carries `I` into `I_i` and `P` into `P_i`
  and induces the identity of `Ẑ(1)` on the tame quotients.
- Variante 1.4 (p. I-7): with the notation of 0.4, let `ρ` be a continuous
  `ℓ`-adic representation with `ρ(P)` finite, and suppose that `k(s)`
  satisfies condition (✱ℓ); then `I` acts quasi-unipotently. The printed
  text names the group `Gal(η̄/η)` and the target `Gal(n, ℚ_ℓ)`; with the
  notation of 0.4 these are `π₁(S - D, η̄)` and `GL(n, ℚ_ℓ)`. The present
  statement is the finite-group form of the conjugation law on which such
  arguments rest.

The Lean statement is derived from these as follows, using only the
`ℓ`-primary factor of tame inertia. Write `x = s ≫ j` and let `I` be the
kernel of `π₁(U, x) → π₁(Spec R, x)`; since `R` is Henselian,
`π₁(Spec R, x)` is the Galois group of `k`.

1. The morphism `Spec L → Spec R` factors through `Spec W`, whose finite
   étale fundamental group is trivial because `W` is strictly Henselian.
   Hence `j₍*₎(g)` lies in `I`. This is the statement of (1.3.1) that the
   trait lands in inertia.
2. The image under `ρ` of the wild part `P` is a finite `ℓ`-group quotient
   of `P`; since `ℓ` is invertible in `R`, `ℓ ≠ p'`, so this image is
   trivial. The finite quotients of the prime-to-`ℓ` factor of
   `I/P ≅ Ẑ(1)` have order prime to `ℓ` and also die under `ρ`. So on `I`,
   `ρ` factors through the action on the `ℓ^n`-th roots of `t` for some `n`,
   that is, through `μ_(ℓ^n)`.
3. Take `φ` from `CyclotomicPowerCondition` for this `n`, and `σ` in
   `π₁(U, x)` mapping to `φ` in the Galois group of `k`; it exists because
   `π₁(U, x) → π₁(Spec R, x)` is surjective, `R` being a normal domain and
   `U` a nonempty open subscheme. By the last clause
   of (0.4.1), conjugation by `σ` acts on `μ_(ℓ^n)` as `φ` does, that is, by
   `ζ ↦ ζ ^ q`, so `σ · j₍*₎(g) · σ⁻¹` and `j₍*₎(g) ^ q` have the same image
   in `μ_(ℓ^n)`. Applying `ρ` gives the conclusion. The `q`-th power map
   commutes with every automorphism of the cyclic group `μ_(ℓ^n)`, so the
   condition does not depend on how `μ_(ℓ^n)` is identified with the roots
   of unity in `SeparableClosure k`.

The corresponding statement over a strictly Henselian regular local ring,
that a finite `ℓ`-group quotient of `π₁(Spec R[1/t])` is killed by a Kummer
cover `R[1/t][X]/(X^(ℓ^n) - t)`, is the explicit form of step 2.
