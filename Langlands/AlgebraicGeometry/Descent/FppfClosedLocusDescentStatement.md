# fppf descent of closed loci

Author: Worker 766.

This module states that a closed subscheme defined fppf-locally descends. It is
phrased for sieves on a scheme `Y`. If a sieve is closed for the fppf topology, and
an fppf covering of `Y` cuts it out by closed immersions, then a single closed
immersion into `Y` cuts it out. This is the form of effective fpqc descent of
closed immersions (Stacks Project, Descent, Lemma 35.37.2, Tag 03I0) that is
needed for loci where two sections of an fppf sheaf agree. The module imports
only Mathlib, and it proves nothing.

## The proposition

In namespace `Langlands.AlgebraicGeometry`, the declaration
`FppfClosedLocusDescentStatement.{u} : Prop` is

```lean
def FppfClosedLocusDescentStatement : Prop :=
  ∀ (Y : Scheme.{u}) (E : Sieve Y), Scheme.fppfTopology.IsClosed E →
    (∃ R ∈ Scheme.fppfTopology Y, ∀ ⦃Y' : Scheme.{u}⦄ (f : Y' ⟶ Y), R f →
      ∃ (Z : Scheme.{u}) (ι : Z ⟶ Y'), IsClosedImmersion ι ∧
        ∀ ⦃T : Scheme.{u}⦄ (g : T ⟶ Y'), (∃ k : T ⟶ Z, k ≫ ι = g) ↔ E (g ≫ f)) →
    ∃ (Z : Scheme.{u}) (ι : Z ⟶ Y), IsClosedImmersion ι ∧
      ∀ ⦃T : Scheme.{u}⦄ (g : T ⟶ Y), (∃ k : T ⟶ Z, k ≫ ι = g) ↔ E g
```

It has the single universe parameter `u`. All schemes are Mathlib schemes in
`Scheme.{u}`, and all morphisms are morphisms of schemes, not of schemes over a
fixed base. The ingredients have the following meanings. All names are Mathlib
names.

- `Y : Scheme.{u}` is arbitrary. It need not be affine, quasi-compact,
  quasi-separated, noetherian or reduced.
- `E : Sieve Y` is a sieve on `Y` in the category of schemes: a collection of
  morphisms `g : T ⟶ Y` with any source, closed under precomposition. It plays the
  role of a subfunctor of the functor of points of `Y`.
- `Scheme.fppfTopology` is Mathlib's fppf topology on schemes
  (`Mathlib/AlgebraicGeometry/Sites/Fpqc.lean`). It is the Grothendieck topology
  generated by the precoverage `Scheme.fppfPrecoverage`. A family of morphisms
  `Yᵢ ⟶ Y` belongs to that precoverage when every morphism in it is flat and
  locally of finite presentation and the family is jointly surjective. No
  quasi-compactness is required.
- `Scheme.fppfTopology.IsClosed E` is Mathlib's notion of a closed sieve
  (`Mathlib/CategoryTheory/Sites/Closed.lean`). It says that for every
  `g : T ⟶ Y`, if the pullback sieve `E.pullback g` is an fppf covering sieve of
  `T`, then `g` belongs to `E`. Informally, membership in `E` can be checked
  fppf-locally on the source. It is not a topological closedness condition.
- The second hypothesis asks for a covering sieve `R ∈ Scheme.fppfTopology Y`.
  For every member `f : Y' ⟶ Y` of `R` it asks for a scheme `Z` and a morphism
  `ι : Z ⟶ Y'` that is a closed immersion (`AlgebraicGeometry.IsClosedImmersion`).
  This `ι` must cut out the pullback of `E` to `Y'`: a morphism `g : T ⟶ Y'`
  factors through `ι` exactly when the composite `g ≫ f` belongs to `E`.
- The conclusion asks for one scheme `Z` and one closed immersion `ι : Z ⟶ Y`
  that cuts out `E` itself: a morphism `g : T ⟶ Y` factors through `ι` exactly
  when `g` belongs to `E`.

Closed immersions are monomorphisms. So in the hypothesis and in the conclusion
the factorization `k`, when it exists, is unique. Each `ι` therefore represents
the corresponding subfunctor, and the closed subscheme it defines is determined
by the sieve.

The hypothesis is asked for every member of `R`, not only for the members of a
generating fppf family. This is not stronger. If it holds for a morphism `f`,
then it holds for every composite `h ≫ f`: the base change of `ι` along `h` is a
closed immersion, and it cuts out the pullback of `E` along `h ≫ f`.

## Original source and translation

The statement is derived from two results of the Stacks Project chapter
*Descent*, cited from the registered source `stacks-descent-ed88ff78-2026`. This
is the chapter PDF, version ed88ff78, compiled 14 July 2026 (file SHA-256
`28e718bac216bb7c4584e9edb1d25d6420ee19a6cd536742c58599242ff8cad4`). The chapter
numbers 35.x below appear in the PDF without the chapter prefix 35.

- Lemma 35.37.2 (Tag 03I0), PDF p.89:

  > Let $S$ be a scheme. Let $\{X_i \to S\}_{i\in I}$ be an fpqc covering, see
  > Topologies, Definition 9.1. Let $(V_i/X_i, \varphi_{ij})$ be a descent datum
  > relative to $\{X_i \to S\}$. If each morphism $V_i \to X_i$ is a closed
  > immersion, then the descent datum is effective.

- Lemma 35.23.21 (Tag 02L6), PDF p.58: the property "$f$ is a closed immersion"
  is fpqc local on the base.

Descent data relative to a family and effectivity are Definitions 35.34.3
(Tag 023W, PDF p.79) and 35.34.11 (Tag 02VU, PDF p.82). Effectivity means that
there is a scheme $U$ over $S$ with $(V_i, \varphi_{ij}) \cong (X_i \times_S U,
\mathrm{can})$.

fppf coverings are fpqc coverings. In Mathlib this is
`Scheme.fppfPrecoverage_le_fpqcPrecoverage` in `Sites/Fpqc.lean`. The fppf
covering families of Mathlib are those of Stacks Definition 34.7.1 (Tag 021M),
cited from the registered source `stacks-fppf-covering-021m-2026-11-01`
(`021M.html`):

> Let $T$ be a scheme. An fppf covering of $T$ is a family of morphisms
> $\{f_i : T_i \to T\}_{i \in I}$ of schemes such that each $f_i$ is flat,
> locally of finite presentation and such that $T = \bigcup f_i(T_i)$.

The Lean proposition reformulates Lemma 35.37.2 for closed subschemes given by a
sieve, as follows.

- *The covering and the closed immersions $V_i \to X_i$.* An fppf covering
  family $\{f_i : Y_i \to Y\}$ generating a sieve inside `R` plays the role of
  $\{X_i \to S\}$, with $S = Y$. The closed immersions `ι : Zᵢ ⟶ Yᵢ` given by the
  hypothesis for the members `fᵢ` play the role of $V_i \to X_i$.
- *The descent datum.* The Lean hypothesis does not supply isomorphisms
  $\varphi_{ij}$. They are determined by the sieve. Over $Y_i \times_Y Y_j$, the
  base changes of $Z_i$ and of $Z_j$ are closed subschemes that both cut out the
  pullback of `E`. A closed subscheme is determined by the morphisms that factor
  through it, so the two base changes are the same closed subscheme. This gives a
  unique isomorphism $\varphi_{ij}$ over $Y_i \times_Y Y_j$, and uniqueness gives
  the cocycle condition. Compare Lemma 35.39.1 (Tag 02W5, PDF p.90), which matches
  descent data relative to an fppf covering with fppf sheaves $F$ over $Y$ whose
  products with the $h_{Y_i}$ are representable. Here $F$ is the subfunctor of
  the functor of points of `Y` given by `E`. It is an fppf sheaf because `E` is
  closed and the fppf topology is subcanonical, and its product with $h_{Y_i}$
  is represented by $Z_i$.
- *Effectivity and the closed immersion.* Lemma 35.37.2 gives a scheme $U$ over
  $Y$ whose base changes are the $Z_i$. By Lemma 35.23.21, $U \to Y$ is a closed
  immersion, because its base changes to the fppf, hence fpqc, covering are
  closed immersions. This is the `ι : Z ⟶ Y` of the conclusion.
- *The factorization property of the conclusion.* This is part of the claim and
  is not stated in Lemma 35.37.2. A morphism `g : T ⟶ Y` factors through a closed
  subscheme exactly when each base change `T ×_Y Yᵢ ⟶ Yᵢ` factors through its
  base change $Z_i$. Indeed `g` factors through `Z` exactly when the closed
  immersion `T ×_Y Z ⟶ T` is an isomorphism, and being an isomorphism can be
  checked after the fpqc base change to the `T ×_Y Yᵢ`. Those base changes factor
  exactly when `T ×_Y Yᵢ ⟶ Y` belongs to `E`. The base changes of the covering
  form an fppf covering of `T`, so the closedness of `E` turns this into `E g`.
  The converse direction uses that `E` is a sieve.

The fpqc covering of Lemma 35.37.2 is specialized to an fppf covering, which is
the case needed for loci in fppf sheaves. The hypothesis that `E` is fppf-closed
replaces the explicit descent datum. Without it the conclusion can fail. Let
`Y = Spec ℚ ⊔ Spec ℚ` and let `E` be the sieve generated by the two inclusions.
It is an fppf covering sieve, and the second hypothesis holds with `R = E` and
`ι = 𝟙`. But `𝟙 Y` is not in `E`, while a closed subscheme through which both
inclusions factor contains every point of the reduced scheme `Y`, so it is all
of `Y`.

## Hypotheses that are not assumed

There is no finiteness, separatedness, noetherian or quasi-compactness
hypothesis on `Y`, on the covering or on the closed immersions. Mathlib's fppf
covering families need not be finite or quasi-compact, as in Stacks
Definition 34.7.1. Any reduction to finite or quasi-compact coverings is an
obligation of the later proof, not a hypothesis of the statement.
