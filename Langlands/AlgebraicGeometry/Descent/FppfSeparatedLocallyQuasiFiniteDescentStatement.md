# Effective fppf descent of separated locally quasi-finite schemes, sheaf form

`Langlands.AlgebraicGeometry.FppfSeparatedLocallyQuasiFiniteDescentStatement.{u}`
is a proposition definition for every universe `u`. This module states the result;
it contains no proof, descent construction, or formalized equivalence with descent data.

## Exact statement

For every scheme `S : Scheme.{u}`, actual fppf sheaf `G : FppfSheaf S`, scheme
`T : Over S`, natural transformation `p : G.obj ⟶ (schemeYoneda S).obj T`, and
sieve `J : Sieve T`, the proposition assumes:

1. `J ∈ fppfTopology S T`.
2. For every `T' : Over S` and every `g : T' ⟶ T` belonging to `J`, there exist
   `X' : Over S`, `x' : X' ⟶ T'`, and
   `a' : (schemeYoneda S).obj X' ⟶ G.obj` such that
   `IsPullback a' ((schemeYoneda S).map x') p ((schemeYoneda S).map g)` and
   `IsSeparated x'.left ∧ LocallyQuasiFinite x'.left ∧ LocallyOfFiniteType x'.left`.

The pullback condition says that `X'`, with its two displayed maps, represents
`G ×_{h_T} h_{T'}` in presheaves on `Over S`. Each local witness may depend on
`g`; no compatibility choices are additional hypotheses.

The conclusion is the existence of `X : Over S`, `x : X ⟶ T`, and an actual
natural isomorphism `e : (schemeYoneda S).obj X ≅ G.obj` satisfying
`e.hom ≫ p = (schemeYoneda S).map x`. Thus `G` is represented by a scheme over
`T`, and the representation respects `p`. The conclusion asserts no further
property of `x`.

All schemes use the same arbitrary universe `u`. No affineness,
quasi-compactness, noetherianity, finite-presentation, or nonemptiness condition
is imposed on the base or local representing morphisms beyond the displayed
hypotheses. The local finite-type condition is retained as explained below.

## Site and covering conventions

The current Library module `Langlands.AlgebraicGeometry.AlgebraicSpace.Basic`
defines `FppfSheaf S` to be sheaves on `Over S` for
`fppfTopology S = Scheme.fppfTopology.over S`, valued in `Type (u + 1)`. Its
`schemeYoneda S` is `uliftYoneda.{u + 1}`, the lifted functor of points.

Mathlib's `GrothendieckTopology.over` in
`Mathlib/CategoryTheory/Sites/Over.lean` defines a covering sieve on `T` by
requiring that its image under `Over.forget S` cover `T.left` for
`Scheme.fppfTopology`. By `Mathlib/AlgebraicGeometry/Sites/Fpqc.lean`, the latter
topology is generated by the fppf precoverage: jointly surjective families of
flat morphisms locally of finite presentation. This agrees with Stacks,
Topologies on Schemes, [Definition 34.7.1 (Tag 021M)](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/021M).
The local finite-presentation requirement here concerns the covering arrows,
and is not an added requirement on the morphisms `x'`.

A covering sieve `J` contains the sieve generated by an fppf covering family
`{T_i → T}`. Consequently the second hypothesis applies to each arrow of that
family. The proposition quantifies over every covering sieve and every member
of it, including members that are not themselves fppf morphisms. Conversely,
local representations on one covering family extend by base change to all
arrows of its generated sieve: those arrows factor through family members,
and the three required morphism properties are stable under base change. This
explains the family-to-sieve passage without imposing a factorization condition
on an arbitrary larger covering sieve.

## Comparison with effective descent

Stacks, More on Morphisms, [Lemma 37.57.1 (Tag 02W8)](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/02W8)
asserts that a descent datum `(V_i/X_i, φ_ij)` relative to an fppf covering
`{X_i → B}` is effective when every `V_i → X_i` is separated and locally
quasi-finite. In this application its descent base `B` is the underlying scheme
of our `T`; the scheme denoted `S` in Lean is an arbitrary ambient base.

Stacks, Descent, [Lemma 35.39.1 (Tag 02W5)](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/02W5)
identifies descent data relative to a covering `{T_i → T}` with sheaves `F` on
the big fppf site of `T` for which each `h_{T_i} × F` is representable. Its
part (1) identifies the local representing schemes with those in the descent
datum, and part (2) identifies representability of `F` with effectivity.

Here the pair `(G, p)` encodes such a sheaf over `T`: over a test scheme
`U → T`, take sections of `G` over `U → S` whose image under `p` is the given
map `U → T` in the lifted functor of points. Thus `h_{T_i} × F` on the site of
`T` corresponds to `G ×_{h_T} h_{T_i}` on the site of `S`. The pullback
hypothesis supplies its representing scheme over `T_i`. On overlaps, the two
pullbacks of these schemes represent the same fibre product sheaf, giving the
canonical transition isomorphisms of 02W5; their uniqueness gives the cocycle
condition. Compatibility of the local choices therefore comes from `G` itself.

Apply this comparison to a covering family contained in `J`. The displayed
properties of `x'` give the hypotheses of 02W8, so the associated datum is
effective; 02W5(2) translates effectivity into the representation over `T` and
its compatibility with `p` in the conclusion. This is the mathematical source
translation motivating the proposition, not a Lean proof implemented here.
Stacks uses a big site with a fixed set-theoretic size bound; Lean uses
`Scheme.{u}` and `Type (u + 1)` throughout.

## Why local finite type is explicit

Stacks, Morphisms of Schemes, [Definition 29.21.1 (Tag 01TD)](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/01TD)
defines quasi-finiteness at a point using affine neighbourhoods with a
finite-type ring map, and defines locally quasi-finite to mean quasi-finite at
every point. Local finite type is therefore included in the Stacks convention.
Mathlib's `LocallyQuasiFinite`, in
`Mathlib/AlgebraicGeometry/Morphisms/QuasiFinite.lean`, instead asks that every
applicable affine-chart ring map be quasi-finite in Mathlib's sense, and
explicitly does not require local finite type. Under `LocallyOfFiniteType`,
Mathlib's `locallyQuasiFinite_iff_isDiscrete_preimage_singleton` identifies this
condition with discrete fibres. Thus the conjunction
`LocallyQuasiFinite ∧ LocallyOfFiniteType` expresses the Stacks locally
quasi-finite hypothesis used in 02W8. The Lean statement keeps both factors and
does not strengthen local finite type to finite type or finite presentation.
