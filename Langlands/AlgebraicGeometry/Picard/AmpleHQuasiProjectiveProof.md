# Proof: quasi-compact projective immersions from an ample line bundle

The module `Langlands.AlgebraicGeometry.Picard.AmpleHQuasiProjectiveProof` proves

```lean
theorem ampleHQuasiProjective : AmpleHQuasiProjectiveStatement.{u}
```

Let `R` be a commutative ring, `f : X ⟶ Spec R` locally of finite type, and `L` a line
bundle on `X` that is ample in the Library sense (`LineBundle.IsAmple`). The proof builds
sections `σ_0, …, σ_N` of one tensor power `M` of `L` and takes the morphism `X → ℙᴺ_R` that
they define. It shows that this morphism is a quasi-compact immersion lying over `f`. This
is the argument of Stacks Lemma 29.40.3 (Tag 01VS), cited in the statement's Markdown.

The construction is the one in the proof `properAmpleClosedImmersionBySections`
(module `Langlands.AlgebraicGeometry.Picard.ProperAmpleClosedImmersionBySectionsProof`), and this module reuses
its auxiliary lemmas from the namespace
`Langlands.AlgebraicGeometry.Picard.ProperAmpleClosedImmersionBySections`. Those lemmas need
neither properness nor a noetherian base. The two proofs differ only in the final step: there,
properness makes the morphism a closed immersion; here, the chart restrictions give an
immersion. The new auxiliary declaration lives in the namespace
`Langlands.AlgebraicGeometry.Picard.AmpleHQuasiProjective`.

## Assembly

`isHQuasiProjective_of_sections` takes the following data and hypotheses.

- Data: a quasi-compact scheme `X`, a module `M`, sections `σ : Fin (N + 1) → Γ(M, ⊤)`,
  indices `base i` and opens `T i` with `trivializationOpen M (σ (base i)) = T i`.
- Hypotheses: the `T i` cover `X` and are affine, and the functions on each `T i` are
  generated by the image of the base and by the ratios `r` with
  `σ_j|_{T i} = r • σ_{base i}|_{T i}`.

Its conclusion is `IsHQuasiProjective f`. The proof:

1. The generation opens `U t = trivializationOpen M (σ t)` cover `X`, and each `σ t` is a
   frame on `U t` (`frame_of_trivializesOn`, `trivializesOn_trivializationOpen`). The
   theorem `AlgebraicGeometry.Scheme.Modules.exists_projPresentation_of_iSup_eq_top` (module
   `Langlands.AnthropicFLT.Theorems.Thm_AlgebraicGeometry_Scheme_Modules_exists_projPresentation_of_iSup_eq_top`,
   ported from the Anthropic Lean formalisation of Fermat's Last Theorem) gives a presentation
   `𝔓` with `𝔓.σ = σ` and `U t ≤ 𝔓.toProj ⁻¹ᵁ D₊(X t)`.
2. Conversely, `𝔓.frame` makes `σ t` a frame on `𝔓.toProj ⁻¹ᵁ D₊(X t)`, so this preimage lies
   in `U t` (`le_trivializationOpen_of_frame`). Hence `𝔓.toProj ⁻¹ᵁ D₊(X (base i)) = T i`.
3. By `isClosedImmersion_restrict_chart`, `𝔓.toProj ∣_ D₊(X (base i))` is a closed immersion
   for every `i`, hence an immersion.
4. The opens `D₊(X (base i))` contain the range of `𝔓.toProj`, because the `T i` cover `X`.
   Being an immersion is local on the target, and Mathlib's
   `IsZariskiLocalAtTarget.of_range_subset_iSup` reduces it to the restrictions over these
   opens. That lemma also needs immersions to be stable under composition with open
   immersions on the right (`MorphismProperty.RespectsRight`). The proof supplies this as a
   local instance, from Mathlib's instances that open immersions are immersions and that
   immersions compose. So `𝔓.toProj` is an immersion.
5. `𝔓.toProj ≫ π = f` (`𝔓.toProj_π`) is quasi-compact, because `X` is quasi-compact and
   `Spec R` is affine. The projection `π` is proper (the Library instance
   `Langlands.AlgebraicGeometry.projectiveSpaceToSpec_isProper`), hence separated and
   quasi-separated. So `𝔓.toProj` is quasi-compact
   (`QuasiCompact.of_comp`).
6. The witnesses `N`, `𝔓.toProj`, steps 4 and 5, and `𝔓.toProj_π` give
   `IsHQuasiProjective f`.

## The main proof

Fix `R`, `f` and an ample `L`. `X` is quasi-compact (`IsAmple.compactSpace`) and
quasi-separated (`LineBundle.IsAmple.quasiSeparatedSpace`, from the published module
`AmpleSectionOpenBasisProof`).

1. `LineBundle.IsAmple.finiteAffinePowerSectionCover` gives a degree `d > 0` and sections
   `s_0, …, s_n` of `L^d` whose generation opens `T i` are affine and cover `X`. Let `L'` be
   the line bundle `L^d` (`tensorPow_isLocallyFreeRankOne`).
2. For each `i`, `exists_generators` gives a finite set `G i` of functions on `T i` that,
   together with the image of the base, generate the ring of functions on `T i`. This uses
   that `f` is locally of finite type.
3. For each `r ∈ G i`, the Library's clearing theorem `lineBundleSectionClearing` (for a
   quasi-compact, quasi-separated scheme) gives `m₀` and a global section `a₀` of `L'^{m₀}`
   with `a₀|_{T i} = r • (s_i^{m₀})|_{T i}`. Let `m` be one more than the maximum of these
   exponents, so `m > 0`. Then `exists_clear_of_le` gives sections `a_{i,r}` of `M = L'^m`
   with `a_{i,r}|_{T i} = r • (s_i^m)|_{T i}`.
4. The finite index type `Σ i, Option (G i)` has cardinality `N + 1` and is enumerated by
   `Fin (N + 1)`. Its entries are the sections `s_i^m` (index `⟨i, none⟩`) and `a_{i,r}`
   (index `⟨i, some r⟩`).
5. The assembly applies with `base i = ⟨i, none⟩`. The generation open of `s_i^m` is `T i`
   (`trivializationOpen_sectionPow`, since `m > 0`). Each generator `r ∈ G i` is a ratio, via
   the index `⟨i, some r⟩`.

Steps 1–5 repeat the main proof of `properAmpleClosedImmersionBySections` almost verbatim. The
only changes are the source of quasi-separatedness and the final assembly.

The proof uses only the axioms `propext`, `Classical.choice` and `Quot.sound`.
