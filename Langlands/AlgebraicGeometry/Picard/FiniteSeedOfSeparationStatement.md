# Finite section seeds from pointwise separation (statement)

This module states the proposition
`Langlands.AlgebraicGeometry.Picard.FiniteSeedOfSeparationStatement`. It supplies a
statement target, not a proof; the proof is the theorem
`Langlands.AlgebraicGeometry.Picard.finiteSeedOfSeparation` in the sibling module
`Langlands.AlgebraicGeometry.Picard.FiniteSeedOfSeparationProof`.

## Statement

Fix a universe `u`, a field `K : Type u` (not assumed algebraically closed), a scheme
`X : Scheme.{u}` with `[IsNoetherian X]` (no integrality, separatedness, finite type or
smoothness), a morphism `p : X ⟶ Spec K` and a morphism `z : Spec K ⟶ X` (not assumed
rational). Call a pair `(L, s)` of a line bundle `L : LineBundle X` and a global section
`s : GlobalSections L.obj` a **good section** if `VanishesAlong L z s` (it vanishes along
`z`) and there is a rational point `y` (`IsRationalPoint p y`) with `¬ VanishesAlong L y s`
(it does not vanish along some rational point). Assume:

1. every rational point `x : Spec K ⟶ X` with `x ≠ z` is detected by some good section
   `(L, s)`, that is `¬ VanishesAlong L x s`;
2. every nonzero rational tangent vector `t : Spec K[ε] ⟶ X` at `z`
   (`IsNonzeroTangentVectorAt p z t`, where `K[ε]` is `DualNumber K`) is detected by some
   good section, `¬ VanishesAlong L t s`.

Then there are `n : ℕ`, line bundles `L : Fin n → LineBundle X` and sections
`s : (i : Fin n) → GlobalSections (L i).obj` with
`IsFinitePointTangentSectionSeed p z n L s`. The size `n` may be zero.

The published `LineBundle`, `GlobalSections`, `IsRationalPoint`, `VanishesAlong`,
`IsNonzeroTangentVectorAt` and `IsFinitePointTangentSectionSeed` are used unchanged.

Every family of good sections satisfies clauses 1 and 2 of the seed predicate (each section
vanishes along `z` and does not vanish along some rational point). The content of the
statement is therefore the passage from pointwise separation, with one good section per
rational point or tangent vector and so possibly infinitely many sections, to a finite
family that detects all of them at once (clauses 3 and 4).

## Intended proof

* Points: by the descending chain condition on closed subsets of the Noetherian space `X`,
  choose finitely many good sections whose common zero locus (the complement of the union of
  their generation opens) is minimal among such loci. A section vanishes along a field-valued
  point exactly when the image of the point lies outside its generation open. If a rational
  point `x ≠ z` in the minimal locus were detected by a good section, adding that section
  would shrink the locus; so no such point remains.
* Tangent vectors: by the ascending chain condition on ideals of the Noetherian stalk at the
  image point of `z`, choose finitely many good sections such that the ideal generated by
  the germs of their local equations is maximal. Vanishing of a section along a
  `K[ε]`-point through `z` is detected by the image of such a germ in `K[ε]`. If a tangent
  vector at `z` is detected by none of the chosen sections, it kills their germs, hence the
  whole maximal ideal, which contains the germ of every good section's local equation; so
  no good section detects it, and by hypothesis 2 it is not a nonzero tangent vector.

The implemented proof is described in `FiniteSeedOfSeparationProof.md`.

## Source and correspondence

J. S. Milne, *Abelian Varieties* (corrected author version, January 2, 2022; registered
source `milne-abelian-varieties-1986-author-2022`), proof of Theorem 7.1, p. 11, first step.
The registered errata `milne-abelian-varieties-1986-errata-2026-10-25` contain no
correction to Sections 6 or 7. Over an algebraically closed field, Milne chooses a finite
set `{Zᵢ}` of prime divisors through `0` with (a) `⋂ Zᵢ = {0}` and (b) for every nonzero
`t ∈ T₀(A)` some `Zᵢ` with `t ∉ T₀(Zᵢ)`. The published `FiniteSectionSeed.md` explains
the misprint `tᵢ ∉ T₀(Zᵢ)` in (b) and the passage from closed points to rational points.
Here the prime divisors are replaced by good sections, and the hypothesis that each point
and each tangent vector is separated by one of them replaces the existence of enough prime
divisors; the finite choice is the conclusion. This is a derived formulation, not a literal
transcription.

## The case `n = 0`

When no rational point other than `z` and no nonzero tangent vector at `z` exists, both
hypotheses are vacuous, and the empty family is the expected seed: its clauses 1 and 2 are
vacuous and its clauses 3 and 4 hold because there is nothing to detect. If `z` is not
rational, the hypotheses may be unsatisfiable. For example, let `X = Spec K` with `p = 𝟙`
and let `z` be induced by a nontrivial automorphism of `K`: then `z` is not rational, the
rational point `𝟙` differs from `z` but has the same image point, and a section vanishes
along a field-valued point exactly when that image point lies outside its generation open,
so no section vanishes along `z` but not along `𝟙`. The statement is then vacuously true,
which is harmless for its use at the unit of a group scheme, a rational point.
