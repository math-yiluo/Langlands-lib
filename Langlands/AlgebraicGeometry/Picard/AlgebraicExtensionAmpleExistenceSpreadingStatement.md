# Spreading ample existence to a finite intermediate field

Author: Worker 303.

`Langlands.AlgebraicGeometry.Picard.AlgebraicExtensionAmpleExistenceSpreadingStatement.{u}`
is a proposition definition. It states a result; it does not prove it.

## Exact statement

The proposition quantifies over:

- fields `K Ω : Type u` with `[Algebra K Ω]` and `[Algebra.IsAlgebraic K Ω]`;
- an actual scheme `X : Scheme.{u}`;
- a morphism `p : X ⟶ Spec (CommRingCat.of K)`.

It has two hypotheses. The first is `IsProper p`. The second is that some
actual `M : LineBundle (pullback p (Spec.map (CommRingCat.ofHom (algebraMap K Ω))))`
satisfies `M.IsAmple`.

The conclusion is that there is `E : IntermediateField K Ω` with
`FiniteDimensional K E`, together with an actual
`M : LineBundle (pullback p (Spec.map (CommRingCat.ofHom (algebraMap K E))))`
satisfying `M.IsAmple`.

`pullback` is Mathlib's categorical pullback
`CategoryTheory.Limits.pullback` in `Scheme.{u}`. Its second leg is the
spectrum map of the actual structure map of the extension. In the conclusion,
`E` is used as the type `↥E : Type u`. That type carries the field structure
and `K`-algebra structure that Mathlib gives an intermediate field, so
`algebraMap K E` is the inclusion of `K` into `E`. `FiniteDimensional K E`
says `E` is finite-dimensional over `K` for that module structure. The finite
dimension and the ample bundle are in one existential, so a downstream proof
gets the finite-dimensionality from the witness. No isomorphism is required
between the ample bundle at the finite stage and the bundle on the base
change to `Ω`: both clauses only assert existence.

Both clauses use the published `Langlands.AlgebraicGeometry.Picard.LineBundle`
and `LineBundle.IsAmple` unchanged. `IsAmple` means two things. The scheme is
compact. Every point lies in the generation locus of a global section of a
positive actual tensor power, and that whole locus is affine; see
`Langlands/AlgebraicGeometry/Picard/Ample.md`.

There is no hypothesis of separability, perfectness, algebraic closedness,
finite dimension of `Ω` over `K`, dimension, projectivity, nonemptiness or
reducedness. The statement also takes no given finite-stage model, line
bundle or compatibility. It covers every universe `u`, every characteristic,
and inseparable and imperfect extensions. Properness is deliberate: it holds
in the parent application.

## Derived mathematical provenance

This is a derived specialization, not a literal quotation of the source. It
is the first limit, or spreading, step in the proof of Stacks Lemma 33.15.1,
for an algebraic extension. The source lemma says: if `X_K` has an ample
invertible sheaf for some field extension `K/k`, then `X` has one. Its proof
runs as follows.

1. Show that `X` is quasi-compact and quasi-separated.
2. Write `K = colim A_i` over the finite-type `k`-subalgebras `A_i ⊆ K`, and
   set `X_i = X ×_{Spec k} Spec A_i`, so that `X_K = lim X_i`.
3. Find an index `i` and an invertible sheaf `𝓛_i` on `X_i` that pulls back
   to the given sheaf (Limits, Lemma 32.10.3).
4. After enlarging `i`, make `𝓛_i` ample (Limits, Lemma 32.4.15).
5. Restrict to `X_{k'}` for a residue field `k' = A_i/𝔪` at a maximal ideal,
   which is finite over `k` by the Nullstellensatz.
6. Descend along the finite locally free morphism `X_{k'} → X` (Divisors,
   Proposition 31.18.9).

The present proposition is steps 1–4, with the source's `k` and `K` renamed
`K` and `Ω`. Algebraicity of `Ω/K` changes two things.

- Every `K`-subalgebra of `Ω` is an intermediate field. This is Mathlib's
  `subalgebraEquivIntermediateField` in
  `Mathlib/FieldTheory/IntermediateField/Algebraic.lean`. A finite-type
  subalgebra is generated by finitely many algebraic elements, so it is
  finite-dimensional over `K`. Therefore the source's index `A_i` is already
  a finite intermediate field `E`, and the statement states the conclusion at
  `E`.
- Step 5 is not needed, because `A_i` is already a field. Step 6 belongs to
  the separate finite field-extension descent statement task
  `b6d41202-09e5-4b0c-8f49-42a7c418e0df`, which was unpublished at this
  module's working Library base. It is not part of this proposition.

In step 1 the source does not use properness. It deduces quasi-compactness
and quasi-separatedness of `X` from the ample sheaf on the base change. Here
properness of `p` gives both directly. That is how the limit lemmas in steps
3 and 4 get their hypotheses.

This module states the proposition only. A future proof must construct or
import actual finite-stage module sheaves and actual finite-stage ampleness,
with whole affine generation opens. It may not take the spreading conclusion
as an extra premise. The proof will have to read and register the cited Stacks
limit lemmas (32.10.3 and 32.4.15) and their prerequisites, which this
statement unit does not rely on.
