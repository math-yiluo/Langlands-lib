# Restricting a ring étale equivalence relation to an invariant ideal

This internal helper restricts the published `RingEtaleEquivalenceRelation t` to the ordinary quotient rings B/I and C/s(I). Here B and C are commutative rings in the same arbitrary universe, the source map s is the given algebra map, and the ideal I has equal extensions s(I) = t(I). There are no Noetherian, reducedness, properness or Artinian hypotheses in this construction.

The source algebra is Mathlib's canonical `Ideal.Quotient.algebraQuotientMapQuotient`. The definition `quotientTarget I hI` is the target quotient ring map, sending the class of b to the class of t(b). Equality of the extended ideals makes that quotient map well-defined. The source and target formulas after composition with B → B/I are definitional identities.

Étaleness of each quotient map follows by base change. The helper `etale_quotientMap` first installs the algebra structure induced by the supplied ring homomorphism. Mathlib's `quotIdealMapEquivQuotTensor` identifies the quotient by the extended ideal with the tensor product with B/I as a B/I-algebra. Tensor base change and transport along this algebra equivalence preserve étaleness. The variant with an explicitly equal codomain ideal handles the target without changing the canonical source algebra.

For the relation laws, `quotientPoint` factors a ring-valued point of C through C/s(I) whenever its source factors through B/I. The source factorization says that I dies under the composite with s; the extended ideal therefore lies in the point's kernel. Its displayed source and target formulas are checked after the surjection B → B/I.

The theorem `quotient` supplies the unchanged `RingEtaleEquivalenceRelation` structure on the quotient maps. To prove the generation condition, apply algebra-adjoin induction to a representative in C, using the original generation hypothesis. Target generators map to target quotient generators, source scalars map to source quotient scalars, and addition and multiplication survive the quotient. Reflexivity applies the original reflexivity law after precomposing with B → B/I, then factors the returned point through C/s(I). Symmetry and transitivity apply the original laws after precomposing their points with C → C/s(I), and factor the resulting point by its source. The quotient formulas verify the required compositions. Both étaleness fields are the base change results above.

This is an internal derivation implemented by Worker 122 from the unchanged Library relation axioms and the pinned Mathlib quotient, algebra-adjoin and étale base change APIs. It does not construct a coaction, prove finite projectivity, or assert the nonzero invariant theorem. No affine quotient or descent conclusion is assumed.
