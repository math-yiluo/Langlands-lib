# Homogeneous chart fractions and equations of bounded degree

For a form `F` homogeneous of degree `d`, `homogeneousChartSection i hF`
is the regular function `F / X i^d` on the standard affine chart
`D₊(X i)` of polynomial projective space. `HomogeneousVanishes I hF` says
that these fractions belong to the defining ideal `I` on every standard
chart. For the kernel of a quasi-compact morphism this is equivalent to
vanishing of the pulled-back fractions on those opens.

`HasBoundedHomogeneousEquations I D` means that on each standard chart,
the defining ideal is generated by the fractions of homogeneous forms
of degree at most `D` that vanish on the entire closed subscheme. This
property is monotone in the bound. If two closed subschemes have the
property for the same bound and agree on which forms of those degrees
vanish, their ideal sheaves agree by the standard affine cover.

The existence lemma over a noetherian affine base starts with the
published homogeneous-ideal presentation of an actual closed immersion
of locally finite presentation into polynomial projective space. The
standard affine section rings are noetherian, so on each chart finitely
many of the supplied generating fractions suffice. The maximum of their
degrees over all the charts is a bound. Every selected form vanishes on
the entire subscheme, not just on the chart for which it was selected.

The bound survives arbitrary coefficient-ring extension: the local
ideal of the pullback is the extension of the original local ideal,
and the pullback of a fraction is the fraction of the coefficientwise
mapped form. There is no flatness or noetherian hypothesis on the new
base ring in this lemma.

This is a derived projective-space interface. It uses the published
`ProjSpace.exists_ideal_fg_forall_ker_ideal_basicOpen_eq_span_of_isClosedImmersion`,
`Scheme.IdealSheafData.ideal_comap_of_le`, and projective coordinate-change
lemmas. It supplies chartwise bounded equations for a single family over
a noetherian ring and their pullbacks, not a numerical bound depending
only on a Hilbert polynomial.
