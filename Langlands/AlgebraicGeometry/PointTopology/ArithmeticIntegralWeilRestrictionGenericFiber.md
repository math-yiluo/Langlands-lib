# Actual arithmetic generic fibers of integral Weil models

These are the two arithmetic instances of the derived original-model generic-fiber construction used for Conrad Example 4.2, printed pp.9–10. Both use the original fields, coefficient rings, and scalar maps, in any common universe. The input is an actual locally finite type model with a specified relative immersion into a finite-dimensional projective space. The model and immersion are supplied; their global existence is a separate obligation.

For number fields `K` and `L` with the original algebra structure `K → L`, `numberFieldIntegralWeilRestrictionGenericFiberIso` identifies the actual `K`-generic fiber of `affineChartWeilRestriction (𝓞 K) (𝓞 L) Y` with `affineChartWeilRestriction K L (ringBaseChange (𝓞 L) L Y)`. Number-field instances supply the finite extension, the finite integral closure `𝓞 L` of `𝓞 K` in `L`, the specified fraction fields, and the original scalar towers. The established `IntegralTopology.integersProjective` supplies projectivity of the original ring-of-integers extension; none of these properties is an additional hypothesis of this wrapper.

For a function field `K` over a finite constant field `k`, retaining the specified rational-function algebra and scalar tower, and any finite extension `L/K`, `functionFieldIntegralWeilRestrictionGenericFiberIso` identifies the `K`-generic fiber of the Weil model over the original valuation ring `v.toValuationSubring` with `affineChartWeilRestriction K L (ringBaseChange (curvePlaceIntegralClosure L v) L Y)`. The upper integral ring is literally the integral closure of that original valuation ring in `L`. Its finite generation follows from the proved finite-normalized-chart and localization argument in `CurvePlaceIntegralClosure`, and its projectivity is inferred for the finite torsion-free module over the valuation ring. Original scalar towers and fraction-field structures supply the remaining inputs. No separability of `L/K` or of the specified rational-function presentation is assumed.

The two comparison lemmas quantify over every actual test scheme over `K` and every morphism into the generic fiber of the original integral Weil model. They identify the destination's original field Weil counit after the constructed isomorphism with the specified tensor comparison using `integralClosureFractionTensorEquiv` and its original integral-ring inclusion formula. Thus these are identifications of the actual original Weil schemes and their counits, with no assumed representability data or scheme isomorphism.

The implementation applies `projectiveImmersionIntegralClosureWeilRestrictionGenericFiberIso` and its proved all-test counit identity to each actual arithmetic setting. The local finite-type condition on each target generic fiber is inherited by the original scheme pullback. The relative projective immersion derives separatedness and the finite affine-neighborhood property inside the generic construction.

The source is Brian Conrad, *Weil and Grothendieck approaches to adelic points* (December 31, 2011), Example 4.2, printed pp.9–10. These derived arithmetic wrappers supply the original generic-fiber identification for specified integral models. Existence of a common global projective model outside a finite set and the adelic Weil homeomorphism remain separate obligations. The function-field construction here is a model over one original valuation ring, and does not assert the existence of a common global model.
