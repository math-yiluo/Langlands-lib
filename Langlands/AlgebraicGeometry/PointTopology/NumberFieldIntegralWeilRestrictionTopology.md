# Actual integral Weil point topology at all upper number-field places

This is a derived local integral comparison for Conrad, *Weil and Grothendieck approaches to adelic points* (December 31, 2011), Example 4.2, printed p.10. It supplies one integral-model ingredient of that example's passage to adelic points. It does not assert the full adelic comparison or the existence of a chosen global model.

Let K → L be the original algebra map of number fields in any common universe, v a finite prime of the original ring of integers O_K, and Y an actual scheme over O_L that is locally of finite type and has a specified immersion into a finite projective space over O_L, commuting with its structure map. The coefficient rings are the original completed integer rings O_v and O_q, and q runs over every original upper prime above v. The Weil scheme is the Library's actual `affineChartWeilRestriction O_K O_L Y`, constructed using the projective immersion. Both its O_v-points and each factor's O_q-points carry their independently defined scheme point topologies, and the target carries the product topology.

`numberFieldIntegralWeilRestrictionPointHomeomorph` constructs the homeomorphism from the original O_v-points of this Weil scheme to the product of all original O_q-points of Y. The implementation applies the generic projective-immersion completed-tensor comparison to the canonical integral tensor equivalence `IntegralTensor.tensorEquiv`. The helper `IntegralTopology` proves that O_L is projective over O_K, that every O_q is finite and projective over O_v, and that its original topology equals its finite-module topology. It supplies the inherited topological ring structures, open units and continuous scalar actions. The tensor equivalence's original one-tensor formula verifies the coefficient compatibility required by the generic comparison.

The accompanying `_counit` theorem states that each upper component is the original relative Weil counit evaluated along the actual completed-tensor factor test spectrum. Thus the homeomorphism retains the original completion embeddings and does not obtain its factor topologies by transport along the equivalence.

All upper factors occur without a supplied finite-module structure, topology comparison or tensor isomorphism. Global spreading, generic-fiber compatibility, a common adelic model and the global adelic Weil comparison remain separate obligations. The helper's generic compact module-topology lemma is reused from `Langlands.Global.CurvePlaceIntegralTopology`; the use here is independent of any function-field hypothesis.
