# Coefficient naturality of the original completed-tensor Weil point map

This derived compatibility lemma supports the integral-model passage in Conrad, *Weil and Grothendieck approaches to adelic points* (December 31, 2011), Example 4.2, printed p.10. The paper's local completed-tensor comparison and its adelic conclusion require compatibility between the original coefficient embeddings. The present unit proves that compatibility for arbitrary commutative coefficient rings with specified tensor decompositions. It does not assert that an arbitrary such decomposition comes from a global field, and it does not yet instantiate the integral-to-field or global adelic comparisons.

Fix A → B, where B is finite projective as an A-module, and an actual locally finite type B-scheme Y. Let C be an A-algebra, D_i commutative rings with their given C- and B-algebra structures, and e a C-algebra equivalence from C tensor_A B to the product of the D_i. The original coefficient condition is e(1 tensor b)_i = b in D_i. No topology or finiteness of the index is needed to define `completedTensorWeilRestrictionPointMap`: at every i it is the original relative Weil counit on the actual factor test spectrum supplied by e. The separate first-projection lemma proves that this test map retains the given C → D_i map; its B-structure was already established by the original coefficient condition.

Now take another such system C', D'_i and e', an A-algebra map f : C → C', and B-algebra maps g_i : D_i → D'_i. Assume the original coefficient square commutes: g_i(c in D_i) equals f(c) in D'_i for every c and i. `completedTensorWeilRestrictionFactorTestMap_natural` proves the square of actual test spectra by the uniqueness of a map into the original pullback: its first projection is precisely this coefficient identity, and its second projection is the given B-structure. Naturality of the original Weil counit then proves `completedTensorWeilRestrictionPointMap_natural`. Thus extending the lower point by f and then taking its i-th component agrees with taking the component first and extending it by g_i. No extra compatibility between the chosen tensor equivalences is assumed.

The two homeomorphism corollaries retain this identity for the independently defined scheme point topologies. Here the index is finite, B remains finite projective over A, each D_i is finite projective over C and each D'_i over C', all coefficient and factor rings are local topological rings with open units, and the given factor topologies are their respective module topologies. The general corollary assumes Y separated and with finite affine neighborhoods; the projective-immersion corollary derives these geometric properties from a specified relative immersion into finite projective space. These corollaries are proved from the existing original-counit formulas and the algebraic naturality theorem. They require no continuity hypothesis on f or g_i for the equality itself; continuous coefficient maps have their usual point-topology properties supplied separately by the coefficient-change API.

All rings, schemes, tensor products, test spectra, coefficient-change maps and counits are the Library's actual constructions in an arbitrary common universe. The factor map's first projection is checked by reducing to e's C-algebra law. No topology is transported along the comparison map, and no field, valuation or separability hypothesis is introduced.
