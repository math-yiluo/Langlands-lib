# Bounded families of rational points of étale algebras cluster

Author: Worker 105.

This module proves that over a henselian ultrametric valued field, bounded families of
rational points of an étale algebra cluster, as long as they converge on the base. It is
the analytic core of the total disconnectedness of rational points of separated algebraic
spaces (Proposition 5.4 of Brian Conrad, *Weil and Grothendieck approaches to adelic
points*, December 31, 2011, printed p.15; source
`conrad-2011-weil-grothendieck-adelic-points`, SHA-256
`fe4a9193aff4ef575665b40971f3da32f850a74d87b3f2d0fa28bba26b9cbabb`). The argument is the
author's. All results are proved in Lean.

## Main result

`EtaleBoundedCluster.exists_tendsto_of_bounded`. Let `K` be a nontrivially normed field
with ultrametric norm and `IsHenselianValuedField K`. Let `R → A` be an étale map of
`K`-algebras (`Algebra.Etale R A`, compatible `K`-algebra structures). Let `l` be a
nontrivial filter on the `K`-points `A →ₐ[K] K`. Assume:

- every `a ∈ A` stays bounded along `l`;
- the restrictions to `R` converge pointwise to a `K`-point `ψ₀` of `R`.

Then there are a `K`-point `φ₀` of `A` restricting to `ψ₀` and a finer nontrivial filter
`l' ≤ l` along which the points converge pointwise to `φ₀`.

## Proof

1. `exists_finset_isStandardEtale`: `Spec A` is covered by finitely many basic opens
   `D(f)` such that `A[1/f]` is standard étale over `R`. Mathlib's
   `Algebra.IsEtaleAt.exists_isStandardEtale` gives such an `f` outside each prime, and
   the unit ideal is then finitely generated by them.
2. `exists_frequently_le_norm`: writing `1 = Σ c_f f`, boundedness of the `c_f` forces
   some `f` with `‖φ(f)‖ ≥ δ > 0` frequently along `l`. The filter is restricted to that
   event.
3. On `A[1/f] ≅ R[X]_g/(P)` (Mathlib's `StandardEtalePresentation`, `P` monic, `P'`
   invertible), let `t_φ` be the value of the coordinate `x`. Every element of `A[1/f]`
   times a power of `f` comes from `A` (`exists_mul_pow_eq`). With `‖φ(f)‖ ≥ δ`, this
   bounds `t_φ` and bounds `g(t_φ)` away from zero. The relation `P' p₁ + P p₂ = g^n`
   then bounds the derivative `P'_φ(t_φ)` away from zero. Here `P_φ` is `P` with
   coefficients specialized by `φ`, and `t_φ` is a root of `P_φ`.
4. Let `P∞` be `P` specialized by `ψ₀`. Since the coefficients converge and `t_φ` stays
   bounded, `P∞(t_φ) → 0`, and `P∞'(t_φ)` stays bounded away from zero
   (`tendsto_eval_map_sub`). The Taylor coefficients of `P∞` at `t_φ` are uniformly bounded
   (`norm_taylor_coeff_le`). The henselian Newton lemma
   (`exists_root_of_isHenselianValuedField`, module `UltrametricNewtonRoot`) gives a root
   `τ_φ ∈ K` of `P∞` with `‖τ_φ - t_φ‖ ≤ ‖P∞(t_φ)‖ / (η/2) → 0`.
5. `P∞` has finitely many roots, so one root `τ` is frequently chosen. Along the refined
   filter `l'`, `t_φ → τ`, and `g(t_φ) → g∞(τ) ≠ 0`.
6. `φ₀` is defined on `R[X][Y]/(P, Yg - 1)` by `X ↦ τ`, `Y ↦ g∞(τ)⁻¹`, with coefficients
   specialized by `ψ₀`, and composed with `A → A[1/f]`. Every `a ∈ A` is the image of a
   bivariate polynomial `F`. For `φ` with `φ(f) ≠ 0`, `φ(a)` is `F` evaluated at
   `(t_φ, g(t_φ)⁻¹)` with coefficients specialized by `φ`; this uses Mathlib's
   `Polynomial.ringHom_ext'`. That evaluation converges to `φ₀(a)` (`tendsto_eval₂_eval₂`).
