# Target morphisms and isomorphisms for actual affine-chart Weil restriction

This derived representability helper supports the model and generic-fiber identifications in Conrad, *Weil and Grothendieck approaches to adelic points* (December 31, 2011), Example 4.2, printed p.10. It constructs target functoriality for the Library's actual glued Weil schemes, so that an isomorphism of actual target models can be used in a subsequent base-change comparison. It is an internal consequence of the already established all-test Weil representing equivalence, rather than a separately numbered claim in that example.

Let A → B be commutative rings in any common universe, with B finite projective over A, and let Y and Z be actual locally finite type schemes over B. Given a target morphism f : Y → Z, assume Z is separated and has the property that every finite set of its scheme points lies in an affine open. `affineChartWeilRestrictionTargetMap` defines the actual morphism from the glued Weil scheme of Y to that of Z: apply the inverse of Z's proved all-test representing equivalence to the original counit of Y followed by f.

`affineChartWeilRestrictionTargetMap_counit` is the direct inverse-equivalence identity. `affineChartWeilRestrictionTargetMap_comparison` states the corresponding identity on every actual A-test scheme: applying the Weil map and then the original comparison equals the original comparison for Y followed by f. The proof uses naturality of that original comparison and associativity. Injectivity of the comparison proves that target identity maps induce identity maps of the actual Weil schemes and that target composition is preserved. For the composition theorem Z and the final target W each satisfy the stated separation and finite-affine-neighborhood conditions; Y requires only local finite type.

When both Y and Z are separated and have finite affine neighborhoods, `affineChartWeilRestrictionTargetIso` sends any actual isomorphism Y ≅ Z to the corresponding actual isomorphism of the independently glued Weil schemes. Its hom and inverse are the induced maps just defined; the identity and composition laws prove both inverse identities. The accompanying all-test comparison theorem retains the original counit followed by the given target isomorphism. This determines the comparison map concretely, without a choice of an unspecified representing isomorphism.

No topology, field or separability hypothesis is used. The usual scheme point topology API may be applied to this actual scheme isomorphism. This helper does not itself identify generic fibers, spread a target model or prove an adelic comparison; those remain subsequent constructions.
