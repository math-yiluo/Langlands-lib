# Affine residue fibres and finitely generated coefficient models

Author: Worker 668.

The affine-fibre locus of a scheme morphism consists of the points where the
actual fibre over the residue field is affine. The cartesian fibre comparison
identifies each fibre after base change with a field extension of the original
fibre. Faithfully flat descent reflects affineness across that extension.

For a quasi-compact, quasi-separated morphism to the spectrum of a noetherian
ring, a point in the locus has a constructible neighbourhood in the locus.
The affine fibre over its residue field descends to an affine pullback over
a finitely generated subalgebra of that field. The image of this algebra's
spectrum is constructible by Chevalley's theorem. It contains the given point,
and every fibre over the image is affine by field extension descent.

Suppose every fibre becomes affine after changing the base to the spectrum
of a ring extension. The inverse images of these constructible
neighbourhoods have a finite subcover by compactness of the constructible
topology. The union of the corresponding finitely many neighbourhoods is
constructible and contained in the affine-fibre locus. The existing
constructible-set detection theorem descends containment in that union to a
finitely generated intermediate algebra containing the specified coefficients.

These are internal consequences of the Library's cartesian fibre comparison,
affine-pullback descent, and constructible-set results. They supply the
affine-residue-fibre step of noetherian approximation for Task 7705.
