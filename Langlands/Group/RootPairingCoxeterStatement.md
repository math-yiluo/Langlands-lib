# The Weyl group of a finite crystallographic root pairing is a Coxeter system

`Langlands.Group.RootPairingWeylCoxeterStatement.{u₁, u₂, u₃, u₄}` is a closed
proposition with four universe parameters, for the index type `ι`, the scalar
field `R`, the weight space `M` and the coweight space `N`. It states:

> For every linearly ordered field `R` (`[Field R] [LinearOrder R]
> [IsStrictOrderedRing R]`), every root pairing `P : RootPairing ι R M N` with
> finitely many roots (`[Finite ι]`) that is crystallographic
> (`[P.IsCrystallographic]`), and every linear form `f : Module.Dual R M` with
> `f (P.root i) ≠ 0` for all `i`:
>
> 1. `Finite P.weylGroup`;
> 2. there are a Coxeter matrix `Mx` indexed by the set
>    `IsAddIndecomposable.baseOf P.root (f : M →+ R)` and a Coxeter system
>    `cs : CoxeterSystem Mx P.weylGroup` such that
> 3. `cs.simple b = RootPairing.weylGroup.ofIdx P b` for every `b` in that set;
> 4. for every `w`, `cs.length w` is the `Set.ncard` of the set of indices `i`
>    with `0 < f (P.root i)`, with `n • P.root j ≠ P.root i` for all `n : ℕ`
>    with `2 ≤ n` and all `j`, and with `f (w • P.root i) < 0`;
> 5. for every `w` and every `b` in that set,
>    `cs.length w < cs.length (w * cs.simple b) ↔ 0 < f (w • P.root b)`.

This module only states the proposition, as the target of a separate proof
task. It proves nothing.

## The objects in the statement

`RootPairing ι R M N` is Mathlib's root pairing. It consists of a perfect
pairing `M × N → R`, an injective family of roots `P.root : ι ↪ M`, an
injective family of coroots `P.coroot : ι ↪ N`, the condition that each root
pairs to `2` with its own coroot, and the stability of roots and coroots under
the associated reflections. Since `P.root` is an embedding, distinct indices
give distinct roots, so counting indices counts roots. Every root is nonzero
because it pairs to `2` with its coroot. `P.IsCrystallographic` is Mathlib's
abbreviation for `P.IsValuedIn ℤ`: every pairing value `P.pairing i j` (the
root `i` paired with the coroot `j`) is an integer.

`P.weylGroup` is Mathlib's Weyl group: the subgroup of the automorphism group
`RootPairing.Aut P` generated by the reflections `RootPairing.Equiv.reflection P i`.
`RootPairing.weylGroup.ofIdx P i` is the reflection in the `i`-th root, viewed
as an element of `P.weylGroup`. An element `w` acts on `M` through Mathlib's
`DistribMulAction (RootPairing.Aut P) M` instance, which applies the weight
space map of the automorphism. In particular,
`RootPairing.weylGroup.ofIdx P i • m = m - ⟨m, P.coroot i⟩ • P.root i`, with the
pairing taken in `P`. The roots need not span `M`.

`CoxeterSystem Mx W` is Mathlib's notion of a Coxeter system: a group
isomorphism between `W` and the group presented by the Coxeter matrix `Mx`.
`cs.simple b` is the image of the generator `b`, and `cs.length w` is the
smallest number of simple generators whose product is `w`. The Coxeter matrix
is existentially quantified. Clause 3 fixes the simple generators to be the
reflections in the simple roots. By the classical theory of Coxeter groups,
the off-diagonal entries of `Mx` are then the orders of the products of two
such reflections, where Mathlib's convention uses `0` for infinite order.

## Sign conventions

The linear form `f` is regular: it is nonzero on every root. A root `P.root i`
is *positive* when `0 < f (P.root i)` and *negative* when
`f (P.root i) < 0`. Each root is exactly one of the two. The Weyl group
permutes the roots, so `f (w • P.root i)` is nonzero as well, and
`f (w • P.root i) < 0` says that `w` sends the root to a negative root.

With this convention, clause 4 counts the indivisible positive roots `α` that
`w` sends to negative roots. This is the set `R(w) = {α ∈ R⁺ | w.α ∈ −R⁺}` of
Springer 8.3.1 (for a reduced root system). Clause 5 is the exchange criterion
in the same convention: right multiplication by the simple reflection `s_b`
raises the length exactly when `w` keeps the simple root `b` positive. The
form `f` plays the role of Springer's `λ ∈ X^∨` with `⟨α, λ⟩ ≠ 0` for all
roots `α` (7.4.5).

## The simple roots `baseOf`

`IsAddIndecomposable.baseOf P.root (f : M →+ R)` is Mathlib's set of indices
`j` with `IsAddIndecomposable P.root {i | 0 < f (P.root i)} j`. Unfolded, `j`
lies in it when:

- the root `P.root j` is positive, and
- for all indices `k`, `l` of positive roots,
  `P.root j = P.root k + P.root l` implies `P.root k = 0 ∨ P.root l = 0`.

Roots are nonzero, so the second condition says that the root `P.root j` is
not the sum of two positive roots. The indices `k` and `l` may be equal.
Hence `baseOf` is the set of positive roots that are not the sum of two
positive roots. This is the classical description of the base defined by a
regular linear form. Springer 8.2.8 characterizes his base `D` by a closely
related property: the roots in `R⁺` that are not combinations with strictly
positive integral coefficients of at least two other roots in `R⁺`. The
coercion `(f : M →+ R)` only forgets `R`-linearity and does not change the
set. Mathlib proves that `baseOf` is linearly independent
(`RootPairing.linearIndepOn_root_baseOf'`), but this statement does not use
that fact.

## Indivisibility and nonreduced pairings

A root `P.root i` is *indivisible* when no root `P.root j` and no natural
number `n ≥ 2` satisfy `n • P.root j = P.root i`. Clause 4 writes this
condition out literally. The pairing may be nonreduced: both `α` and `2α` may
be roots, as in a root system of type `BC`. The formula quantifies over every
`n ≥ 2`, so it relies on no classification of the possible multiples.

For a nonreduced pairing, the count in clause 4 omits the divisible positive
roots, such as `2α`. It therefore counts the positive roots of the reduced
subsystem of indivisible roots that `w` makes negative. This is how the
length formula is stated for nonreduced relative root systems (Renard,
V.3.4 and V.4.6, below). Counting all positive roots instead would count `2α`
together with `α` and would not give the Coxeter length.

`baseOf` never contains a root of the form `2β`, because `2β = β + β` with
`β` positive is a sum of two positive roots. Classically, in a finite
crystallographic pairing a root `nβ` with `n ≥ 2` can occur only for
`n = 2`, so every simple root is indivisible. The reflections in `α` and in
`2α` coincide classically. The Coxeter generators are indexed by `baseOf`
alone, so they are not duplicated. These classical facts are explanations;
the statement asserts them only insofar as they follow from clauses 1–5.

## Scope

- **Rank zero.** If `ι` is empty, the Weyl group is trivial, `baseOf` is empty,
  the Coxeter matrix is the empty one, all lengths are `0`, and clause 5 is
  vacuous. The hypothesis on `f` is then vacuous too. This case is included.
- **Non-spanning roots.** The roots need not span `M`. The reflections fix
  every vector of `M` on which all coroots vanish.
- **Finiteness is a conclusion.** `Finite P.weylGroup` is stated as clause 1,
  not assumed. Mathlib at the pinned commit has no finiteness theorem for
  `P.weylGroup`.
- **Fields.** The scalars form a linearly ordered field, for example `ℚ` or
  `ℝ`. Crystallographic pairings over `ℚ` include the relative root systems
  of reductive groups.

## Consumer interface

Given `h : RootPairingWeylCoxeterStatement`, a root pairing `P` with the
instances above, a linear form `f` and `hf : ∀ i, f (P.root i) ≠ 0`, the term
`h ι R M N P f hf` has type
`Finite P.weylGroup ∧ ∃ Mx cs, (clause 3) ∧ (clause 4) ∧ (clause 5)`.
The instance arguments are found by instance search. A private check
confirmed that this application elaborates, as does the formula for the action
of `RootPairing.weylGroup.ofIdx` quoted above. Neither check is part of the
module.

## Provenance and sources

This is a **Mathlib-native formulation** of the classical theorem that the Weyl
group of a finite (possibly nonreduced) root system is a Coxeter group on the
reflections in a base. The length of an element is the number of indivisible
positive roots it makes negative, and right multiplication by a simple
reflection raises the length exactly when the element keeps that simple root
positive. It is not a quotation of any single source. The derived features
are these: Mathlib's root pairings over an ordered field with arbitrary
weight space, the base given as Mathlib's `baseOf` of a regular linear form,
finiteness as a conclusion, and the explicit indivisibility filter for
nonreduced pairings.

- Springer, *Linear Algebraic Groups*, 2nd ed. (1998), source
  `springer-1998-linear-algebraic-groups`. Printed page numbers are given; the
  PDF page is the printed page plus 14.
  - 7.4.1 (p. 124): root data, and the Weyl group generated by the `s_α`.
  - 7.4.2 (c) (p. 124): exercise, the Weyl group is finite.
  - 7.4.4 (p. 125): the root system of a group is reduced.
  - 7.4.5 (p. 125): systems of positive roots `R⁺ = {α ∈ R | ⟨α, λ⟩ > 0}`
    for `λ` with `⟨α, λ⟩ ≠ 0` for all roots.
  - 8.2.6 to 8.2.8 (pp. 139–141): the basis `D` and the simple reflections
    `S`. Theorem 8.2.8 states "(i) S generates W", and its proof characterizes
    `D` as above.
  - 8.2.9 (p. 141): the proof of 8.2.8 is elementary, that is, it does not
    use algebraic groups.
  - 8.3.1 and 8.3.2 (pp. 142–143): `R(w) = {α ∈ R⁺ | w.α ∈ −R⁺}`.
    Lemma 8.3.2 (ii) says "The number of elements of R(w) is l(w)". Lemma
    8.3.2 (iii) says that for `α ∈ D`, `l(ws_α) = l(w) + 1` if `w.α ∈ R⁺`
    and `l(ws_α) = l(w) − 1` if `w.α ∈ −R⁺`.
  - 8.3.4 (p. 143): `S` with the relations `s² = 1` and `(st)^{m(s,t)} = 1`
    is a presentation of `W`, the Coxeter presentation.

  Springer works with reduced root data and real coefficients. The
  nonreduced case and general ordered fields are part of the present
  formulation, not of Springer's statements.
- Renard, *Représentations des groupes réductifs p-adiques* (2026 version),
  source `renard-2026-representations-groupes-reductifs-padiques`. Printed
  page numbers are given; the PDF page is the printed page plus 6.
  - V.3.4 (p. 110): relative roots `Σ′(A)` may be nonreduced. Renard writes
    "On note Σ(A) l'ensemble des racines réduites de Σ′(A), c'est-à-dire les
    éléments α de Σ′(A) tels que α/n ∉ Σ′(A) si n ≥ 2", which is the
    indivisibility filter of clause 4.
  - V.3.7 (p. 111): the Weyl group `W(A_M)` is identified with the Weyl group
    of the root system `Σ(A_M)`, citing Borel–Tits [7], 5.3 and Cor. 5.8.
  - V.4 preamble and V.4.1 (p. 118): the groups `U_γ` have weights `γ` and
    `2γ`. `S_∅` is the set of reflections in the simple roots `Δ_∅`, and
    `(G, P_∅, N_G(A_∅), S_∅)` is a Tits system.
  - V.4.6, proof of (i) (p. 121): "la longueur l(w) d'un élément w ∈ W_G est
    égale au nombre de racines γ dans Σ⁺_∅ telles que w·γ ∈ −Σ⁺_∅", where
    `Σ⁺_∅` consists of reduced roots. Also: "Si w ∈ W_G et γ ∈ Δ_∅, alors
    l(ws_γ) > l(w) si et seulement si w·γ ∈ Σ⁺_∅."
- Bourbaki, *Lie Groups and Lie Algebras*, Ch. VI §1.5 Thm. 2 and §§1.6–1.7,
  covers the abstract theorem, including nonreduced root systems. It is not in
  the central library and is mentioned only for orientation.
