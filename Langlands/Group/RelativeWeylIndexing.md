# Intrinsic finite Coxeter indexing for the actual relative Weyl quotient

This module constructs the indexing objects used in Aubert 1995,
printed pp.2180–2183, Theorem 1.7 and its proof. The source is
`aubert-1995-duality`, SHA-256
`57af9c46769f6edd8e142ddaf465e89acfa2f2147db222d26879a28a06df9693`.
The constructions are internal implementations using Mathlib's
`CoxeterSystem` and actual subgroup operations. They assume a Coxeter
system, and assume a finite group when constructing the finite representative
sets and their longest member. They prove no reductive-group structural
assertion or representation-theoretic identity.

The combinatorial parameters are a type `B` of simple indices, a group `W`,
a Coxeter matrix `M`, and `cs : CoxeterSystem M W`. In an actual relative-Weyl
application `ActualCoxeterSystem T M` is definitionally a Coxeter system on
`RelativeWeyl.Weyl T = N_G(T)/Z_G(T)`. The actual subgroup quotient and its
embedding from a Levi are imported from `Langlands.Group.RelativeWeyl`.
Neither an arbitrary bijection to labels nor a package containing the desired
Aubert identities occurs in this construction.

## Constructed objects

`standardSubgroup cs I` is the subgroup generated by the actual simple
reflections with indices in the finite subset `I`. The empty subset generates
the trivial subgroup, the full subset generates `W`, and inclusion of
subsets gives inclusion of subgroups.

`doubleCoset cs I J w` consists of the elements `a * w * b` with
`a ∈ W_I` and `b ∈ W_J`. `IsMinimal cs I J w` states that `w` has least
Coxeter length among all such elements. `minimalReps cs I J` filters the
finite group by precisely this predicate. The module proves that every
double coset has a minimal member by minimizing length on the finite
double coset. It also proves the inverse symmetry of minimality.
It does not yet prove uniqueness of that member or equivalence with
the relative-root positivity criterion used by Aubert.

`longestRep cs J` chooses a maximal-length member of `D(J,∅)`. Its membership
and maximality are proved by the finite maximum construction. The assertion
that there is exactly one such maximum is separate. Thus the construction
does not require the conclusion of the longest-representative theorem
as a data field.

`conjugateStandardSubgroup cs I w` is the mapped subgroup `w W_I w⁻¹`.
`rightIntersection cs I J w` is `W_J ∩ w⁻¹ W_I w`. Its candidate generating
subset `intersectionSubset cs I J w` is the subset of `J` whose simple
reflections lie in that intersection; containment in `J` is proved.
The standard-parabolic intersection theorem for minimal representatives
remains a separate assertion.

`positiveSimpleSet cs w` uses the strict length inequality
`length w < length (w * simple i)`. The assertion identifying it with
`{α ∈ S | wα ∈ Φ⁺}`, Aubert's `S_w` on p.2182, requires the root/length
comparison. `simpleImage cs w I` uses equality of conjugated simple
reflections. Reflection equality by itself forgets the sign of a root;
its root-image interpretation requires a positivity assertion.
`MapsIntoSimple` requires every index of `I` to have an image in `J`,
so it cannot silently omit conjugates which are not simple.
`associatedSubset cs J` uses the inverse of the constructed longest
representative, in the direction `J' = w_J⁻¹(J)` of Theorem 1.7.
The relative-root and actual-Levi interpretations remain separate assertions.

## Scope and retained obligations

No nonempty simple-index type is required. If `B` is empty, Mathlib's
simple-reflection induction proves that every element of `W` is the identity;
the module derives `Subsingleton W` and that the longest representative
is the identity. This is conditional on a Coxeter system on the ambient
quotient. The earlier quotient module's triviality result for the minimal
Levi alone does not establish ambient rank-zero triviality.

The actual relative-root realization, its simple reflections and Coxeter
presentation, the finiteness of the quotient, the identification of
`standardSubgroup` with the image of the actual Levi quotient, the
positivity comparisons, and the subgroup/Levi conjugation comparisons are
not proved here. The source boundary is Renard V.3.7 and V.4.6, printed
pp.111 and 120–121; the reviewed Chapter V ledger distinguishes the external
inputs S09 and S17–S18 from the retained deductions D06. In particular
S17–S18 do not automatically prove all those deductions.
