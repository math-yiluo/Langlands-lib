# Compact conjugacy in a self-adjoint algebraic matrix group

The compact-conjugacy argument uses positive definite matrix units. The sum
of the real traces of a positive unit and its inverse has compact sublevel
sets. Spectral diagonalization bounds the matrix and inverse by compact
families with bounded nonnegative eigenvalues.

At the midpoint constructed by internal positive square roots, the trace
energy is strictly below the mean of its values at distinct endpoints. The
difference for each trace is the trace of a conjugated Hermitian square.

On positive elements of the closed polynomial group, minimize the maximum
of this energy along the congruence orbit of the given compact subgroup.
The identity gives an initial compact sublevel set. At the midpoint of two
different minima, choose a maximizing orbit point and apply the strict
inequality. This proves uniqueness. The minimizer is fixed under congruence
by the compact subgroup, and its internal square root conjugates the compact
subgroup into the unitary group.
