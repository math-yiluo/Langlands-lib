# Bruhat decomposition and parabolic subgroups of an abstract Tits system

This module proves the standard group-theoretic consequences of the Tits-system
predicate `Langlands.Group.IsTitsSystem B N π S` from
`Langlands.Group.TitsSystem`. Here `G` and `W` are groups, `B` and `N` are subgroups
of `G`, `π : N →* W` is surjective with kernel `B ∩ N`, `B ⊔ N = G`, and `S` is a
generating set of involutions of `W`. The predicate also requires the multiplication
axiom `n b m ∈ C(π m) ∪ C(s π m)` for each `n` over `s ∈ S`, and, for each `n` over
`s ∈ S`, some `b ∈ B` with `n b n⁻¹ ∉ B`. Here `C(w) = TitsSystem.cell B π w = B n B` for any `n` over `w`.
The module adds no hypotheses or axioms. Its results are the abstract forms of
Borel–Tits 5.17, 5.18 and 5.20 (printed pp. 101–102). There, 5.18 is deduced from 5.17
and Tits' general theorems, cited as [33], th. 2, 3, 4, and 5.20 from 5.15–5.17.

## Definitions

- `TitsSystem.length S w` is the word length of `w` in the alphabet `S`, defined as
  the infimum of the lengths of lists in `S` with product `w`.
- `IsTitsSystem.standardParabolicSubgroup h hX` is the subgroup with carrier
  `TitsSystem.standardParabolic B π X = B ⟨X⟩ B`, for `X ⊆ S`.

## Results

- `exists_mem_cell` and `eq_of_mem_cell` give the Bruhat decomposition. Every
  element lies in a cell, and the cells of distinct elements of `W` are disjoint.
- `mul_mem_cell_of_length_le`: if `ℓ(w) ≤ ℓ(sw)` then `C(s) C(w) ⊆ C(sw)`.
- `mem_closure_of_mem`: if `H ⊇ B` contains a point of `N` over `w`, then `w` lies in
  the subgroup generated by the `s ∈ S` that have a representative in `H`.
- `coe_eq_standardParabolic`: let `P ⊇ B` be a subgroup. If `π` maps the points of `N`
  in `P` into `⟨X⟩`, and every element of `⟨X⟩` has a representative in `P`, then
  `P = B ⟨X⟩ B` as sets.
- `exists_eq_standardParabolic`: every subgroup containing `B` equals `B ⟨X⟩ B` for
  some `X ⊆ S`.
- `normalizer_eq_of_le`: every subgroup containing `B` equals its normalizer.
- `exists_doubleCoset_equiv`: let `X, Y ⊆ S`, and let `V = ⟨X⟩`, `V' = ⟨Y⟩`. Let
  `P, Q` be subgroups with underlying sets `B ⟨X⟩ B` and `B ⟨Y⟩ B`. Then there is an
  equivalence `DoubleCoset.Quotient V V' ≃ DoubleCoset.Quotient P Q` that sends the
  class of `π n` to the class of `n` for every `n ∈ N`.

## Proof route

The proofs are the classical Tits-system arguments (Bourbaki, *Lie* IV §2). The route
implemented in Lean is as follows.

- Existence of the Bruhat decomposition: the union of the cells is closed under
  products. A cell `C(s₁⋯s_q)` lies in `C(s₁)⋯C(s_q)`, and each `C(s)` maps a cell into
  the union of two cells. The union also contains `B` and `N` and is closed under
  inverses, so it is all of `B ⊔ N = G`.
- Disjointness: by strong induction on `ℓ(w)`, assuming `ℓ(w) ≤ ℓ(w')`. Write
  `ℓ(sw) < ℓ(w)` and move a point of `C(w) ∩ C(w')` by a representative of `s`.
- `C(s) C(w) ⊆ C(sw)` when `ℓ(w) ≤ ℓ(sw)`: induction on `ℓ(w)`, peeling a final
  letter `s'` with `ℓ(ws') < ℓ(w)`. The other possibility is excluded by disjointness.
- Overgroups of `B`: suppose `ℓ(sw) < ℓ(w)`, and choose `b` with `n_s b n_s⁻¹ ∉ B`.
  Then `n_s b n_s⁻¹ = (n_s b n_{sw}) (n_s n_{sw})⁻¹` lies in `C(w) C(w)⁻¹` and in
  `C(s)`. So a subgroup containing `B` and a point of `C(w)` contains a representative
  of `s`. Induction on length then gives `mem_closure_of_mem`.
- Self-normalization: let `n_s` be a representative of `s ∈ S` that normalizes
  `P ⊇ B`. It conjugates some `b ∈ B` into `C(s) ∩ P`, so `P` contains a
  representative of `s`.
- Double cosets: the map `w ↦ P n_w Q` is well defined, since representatives of
  `x w y` with `x ∈ V`, `y ∈ V'` differ from `n_x n_w n_y` by an element of `B`. It is
  surjective by the Bruhat decomposition. For injectivity, `P C(w) Q` lies in the union
  of the cells `C(u w u')` with `u ∈ V`, `u' ∈ V'`, and disjointness finishes.

## Provenance

The predicate and its conventions come from task f9b58c31 (Workers 913 and 1239). The
consequences proved here are internal helper results for the proof of
`Langlands.GroupScheme.StandardParabolicPointsStatement`. In that proof, `B` is the
rational points of the minimal standard parabolic, `N = normalizerPoints i` and
`W = relativeWeylGroup i`. Source context: Borel–Tits 5.15–5.20 (printed pp. 100–102),
source ID `borel-tits-1965-groupes-reductifs`.
