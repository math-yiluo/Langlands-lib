# The actual cell in the zero normal slice

An upper direction \(U\) near zero gives an upper triangular unit \(1+U\). For every lower inversion direction \(R\), the matrix \(1+R\) is lower unipotent and conjugation by the inverse permutation makes it upper unipotent. These are exactly the two unipotent conditions used by the Bruhat factorization. Consequently the tangent parameter map takes values in the actual cell near its center.

Conversely, near \(bPv\) use the published cell homeomorphism to recover the factors \(b',v'\) of a cell point. Their displacements are \(b^{-1}b'-1\) and \(Pv'v^{-1}P^{-1}-1\). They have the two required entry patterns, vary continuously on the cell, and are zero at the center. The tangent parameter map takes this pair back to the point. Injectivity of the open chart on its source identifies those tangent coordinates with the coordinates of the point. Hence locally the actual cell is precisely the zero normal slice.

The membership and recovery argument is a derived use of the actual Bruhat cell homeomorphism for Shalika's Proposition 2.10, printed p. 182.
