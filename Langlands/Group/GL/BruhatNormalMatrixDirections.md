# Lower normal matrix directions for upper Bruhat cells

The normal entry condition selects strictly lower entries
whose order is preserved by the inverse permutation. If
every adjacent pair were reversed, the inverse permutation
would be strictly antitone, hence the long Weyl
permutation. Thus every other permutation has a simple
lower root among the selected entries.

An upper conjugation of a lower simple matrix unit may
change its coefficient by diagonal factors and introduce
upper entries. The product formula for its entries shows
that its original position is its sole possible lower
entry. More generally, choose a nonzero entry of maximum
lower height of a matrix supported on the selected
positions. In its upper conjugate only the two diagonal
factors can contribute to that entry, since each other
term would use a greater lower height. The coefficient
is nonzero, giving injectivity of the normal projection.

These are matrix calculations for the transverse and
tangential directions of Shalika (1974), Lemmas 2.7–2.8.
