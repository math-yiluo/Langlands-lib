# Cartan decomposition of the general linear group

Author: Worker 292.

`Langlands.GeneralLinear.CartanDecompositionStatement` is a closed,
universe-polymorphic `Prop`-valued definition. It states a theorem to be proved;
this pair supplies no proof of the Cartan decomposition and assumes no such
proof as data.

## Statement and conventions

For every field `F : Type uF`, every linearly ordered commutative group with zero
`Γ₀ : Type uΓ`, every valuation `v : Valuation F Γ₀`, and every field unit `ϖ`
satisfying `Iwasawa.IsUniformizingElement v ϖ`, the proposition asserts that for
every natural rank `m` and every `g : Matrix.GeneralLinearGroup (Fin m) F`,
there is a unique tuple `lam : Fin m → ℤ` such that

- `Antitone lam`, meaning `lam 0 ≥ lam 1 ≥ ⋯ ≥ lam (m - 1)`;
- there are `k₁, k₂` in `Iwasawa.integralRange v m` with
  `g = k₁ * Iwasawa.torus ϖ lam * k₂`.

The second condition is existential in the two integral factors. The `∃!`
binder applies only to `lam`; neither integral factor is asserted unique.
Repeated exponents and arbitrary negative exponents are allowed. Rank zero
is included: its exponent function and invertible empty matrix are unique,
and antitonicity is vacuous. No positivity assumption on `m` is hidden.

All mathematical objects are imported unchanged from the published module
`Langlands.Representation.Whittaker.Iwasawa`, in namespace
`Langlands.GeneralLinear.Iwasawa`:

- `IsUniformizingElement v ϖ` means `v (ϖ : F) < 1` and that every nonzero
  `x : F` has valuation `v (ϖ : F) ^ z` for some `z : ℤ`. Thus the nonzero
  valuation image is infinite cyclic with a specified generator below one.
  The valuation is nontrivial; surjectivity onto all of `Γ₀` is not required.
- `v.integer` is the actual valuation subring `{x | v x ≤ 1}`, in Mathlib's
  multiplicative convention. The symbol `ϖ : Fˣ` denotes a unit in the
  ambient field, not a unit in this subring.
- `integralRange v m` is the range of the coefficient homomorphism
  `GL_m(v.integer) → GL_m(F)`. In particular an integral factor and its
  inverse both have integral entries. The published `integralRange_succ`
  identifies this subgroup in rank `n + 1` with
  `Langlands.Conductor.integralSubgroup v.integer n`.
- `torus ϖ lam` is the actual invertible diagonal matrix with entries
  `((ϖ ^ lam i : Fˣ) : F)`. There is no normalization by a scalar or by
  determinant: common shifts of all exponents remain distinct tuples.

Equivalently, the double cosets `K * torus ϖ lam * K`, with
`K = integralRange v m` and `lam` antitone, cover `GL_m(F)` and are pairwise
disjoint. This equivalence explains the statement; no quotient equivalence or
chosen Cartan-exponent function is constructed in this pair.

## Source correspondence and derived scope

Benedict H. Gross, *On the Satake isomorphism* (1998), retained 17-page author
text, is the original source read for this statement. Section 1, equation
(1.1), on PDF page 2 defines the dominant cocharacter chamber using the
positive roots of a chosen Borel. Section 2, equation (2.1), on PDF page 3
sets `K = G(A)` for the ring of integers `A`. Proposition 2.6 on PDF page 4
states that the dominant cocharacters index a disjoint covering by the double
cosets `K λ(π) K`. The following paragraph explicitly identifies elementary
divisors as the proof method for `GL_n`. PDF pages 1–4 and 17 were read.

For `GL_m`, choose the diagonal torus and upper triangular Borel. Its positive
roots are `e_i - e_j` for `i < j`, so the dominance inequalities are
`lam i - lam j ≥ 0`. These are exactly `Antitone lam`, with Lean's indexing
starting at zero. The cocharacter evaluated at `ϖ` is precisely
`Iwasawa.torus ϖ lam`, and Gross's `G(A)` is the image
`Iwasawa.integralRange v m`. Disjointness and coverage give the unique-exponent
form used in Lean.

Gross assumes a nonarchimedean local field. The present generality over an
arbitrary field with the explicit uniformizer condition is a **derived
algebraic extension**, not a quotation of that broader scope from Gross.
Here is its mathematical rationale. The uniformizer condition makes
`O = v.integer` a discrete valuation ring: every nonzero element is a unit of
`O` times an integer power of `ϖ`, and integrality is equivalent to the
exponent being nonnegative. Every nonzero ideal of `O` is generated by an
element of least exponent. Multiplying an invertible matrix by a sufficiently
large nonnegative power of `ϖ` clears its finitely many denominators. Smith
normal form over this principal ideal domain supplies integral invertible
left and right changes of basis and diagonal powers of `ϖ`. Subtracting the
common denominator-clearing exponent and reversing the order of the diagonal
entries produces the decreasing integer tuple in the statement. Permutation
matrices and their inverses lie in the integral group.

For uniqueness, for each `1 ≤ r ≤ m` the fractional ideal generated by the
`r` by `r` minors is unchanged by either integral invertible factor: the
Cauchy–Binet formula gives one containment, and the inverse integral matrix
gives the reverse containment. On a diagonal matrix with decreasing tuple
`lam`, this ideal is generated by `ϖ` to the sum of the `r` smallest
exponents. These successive sums determine the entire tuple. Thus neither
completeness, residue-field finiteness, topology, nor local compactness is
needed for the matrix assertion. This paragraph gives the provenance rationale;
these algebraic steps are not formalized by this statement file. The empty
rank is the additional vacuous boundary case described above.
