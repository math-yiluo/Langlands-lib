# Linear coordinates for the Bruhat charts

Decompose a matrix into its upper triangular entries, the
lower entries indexed by inversions of the inverse
permutation, and the remaining lower entries. The three
entry projections are inverse to the sum of these
subspaces.

The derivative calculation at a point of a Bruhat cell
conjugates the last set of directions by the inverse
upper triangular factor. To show that the resulting sum
map is injective, apply the projection to the third
matrix pattern. This kills the first two summands.
Injectivity of the normal projection under upper
conjugation then kills the third summand. The original
coordinate decomposition gives equality of the finite
dimensions, so the injective sum map is a linear
equivalence.

This is the linear transversality calculation for
Shalika (1974), Lemma 2.7.
