# Tangent matrix fields in a local Bruhat chart

At a cell point \(bPv\), a matrix field \(x\mapsto Xx\) is tangent if \(b^{-1}Xb\) has zero normal entries. That conjugate splits into the upper and lower inversion directions. The line through zero in those two tangent coordinates gives a differentiable curve in the actual cell, with initial velocity \(XbPv\).

The inverse chart takes this curve into the zero slice near its initial parameter. The normal projection of the curve is locally zero, so its derivative is zero by the chain rule. This gives the normal velocity of the pulled-back field and will apply to the diagonal and complementary simple-root fields in Shalika's Proposition 2.10.
