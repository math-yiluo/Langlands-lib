# Chains of rank-one steps in a finite Coxeter group

Module `Langlands.Group.RelativeWeylRankOneChain`, namespace
`Langlands.Group.RelativeWeylIndexing`. It is a helper module for the proof
`Langlands.Global.intertwiningElementaryChain` (module
`Langlands.Global.AdelicIntertwiningElementaryChainProof`) and has no statement of its own.

## Setting and notation

`B : Type u`, `W : Type v`, `[Group W]`, `M : CoxeterMatrix B`, `cs : CoxeterSystem M W` and
`[DecidableEq B]` (used for `insert`). Write `s_i = cs.simple i`, `ℓ = cs.length` and
`W_I = standardSubgroup cs I`, the subgroup generated by the `s_i` with `i ∈ I`. The main theorem
also assumes `[Fintype B]` and `[Finite W]`.

## Provenance

The recursion is Bernstein–Zelevinsky's proof 6.5 of their Lemma 2.17 (registered source
`bernstein-zelevinsky-1977-induced-i`, I. N. Bernstein and A. V. Zelevinsky, *Induced
representations of reductive p-adic groups I*, Ann. Sci. ENS 10 (1977), 6.5(2)–(4), journal
pp. 469–470, PDF pp. 30–31). In 6.5(4) the element `w` is reduced by a simple root `α ∉ Γ`
with `w(α) < 0`, through the elementary map `s_{M'} s_{M}` of 6.5(3) for the standard subset
generated by `Γ ∪ {α}`. The module `Langlands.Group.RelativeWeylElementaryChain` formalizes the
same recursion with an abstract transport relation and records, for each step, only a superset
`S` of the two index sets and a coset count. Here the recursion is repeated so that each step
records the extra index `a ∉ L` with `S = L ∪ {a}`, and the conjugation data of the step. All
Coxeter facts are taken from `Langlands.Group.RelativeWeylElementaryChain` and its inputs.

## Declarations

* `mem_standardSubgroup_iff_of_simple_bijection`: if conjugation by `g` sends every `s_i`
  (`i ∈ I`) to some `s_j` (`j ∈ J`) and reaches every `s_j` (`j ∈ J`), then
  `h ∈ W_J ↔ g⁻¹ h g ∈ W_I`. The image of `W_I = ⟨s_i⟩` under `MulAut.conj g` is the closure of
  the image of the generators (`MonoidHom.map_closure`), which is `W_J`.
* `IsRankOneStep cs I J a g` (a `Prop`): `a ∉ I`, `J ⊆ insert a I`,
  `g ∈ W_{insert a I}`, `ℓ g < ℓ (g s_i)` for `i ∈ I`, and conjugation by `g` maps the
  simple reflections of `I` into those of `J` and reaches all of them.
* `HasRankOneChain cs I J x` (a `Prop`): there are `m`, index sets
  `Ls : Fin (m + 1) → Finset B`, indices `as : Fin m → B`, steps `gs : Fin m → W` and partial
  products `xs : Fin (m + 1) → W` with `Ls 0 = I`, `Ls (Fin.last m) = J`, `xs 0 = 1`,
  `xs (Fin.last m) = x`, and for every `t`, `IsRankOneStep cs (Ls t) (Ls (t+1)) (as t) (gs t)`
  and `xs (t+1) = gs t * xs t`. Thus `x = g_{m-1} ⋯ g_0`.
* `hasRankOneChain_refl`: the empty chain from `I` to `I` for `x = 1`.
* `hasRankOneChain_cons`: from a step `g` from `I` to `J` along `a` and a chain from `J` to `K`
  for `x`, a chain from `I` to `K` for `x * g`, built with `Fin.cons`; the new partial products
  are `Fin.cons 1 (fun t => xs t * g)`.
* `hasRankOneChain_of_right_minimal` (`[Fintype B] [Finite W]`): for every `n`, every `x` with
  `ℓ x = n` that is right `IL`-minimal (`ℓ x ≤ ℓ (x y)` for `y ∈ W_IL`) and satisfies
  `g ∈ W_IM ↔ x⁻¹ g x ∈ W_IL` for all `g` has `HasRankOneChain cs IL IM x`.

## Proof of `hasRankOneChain_of_right_minimal`

Strong induction on `n = ℓ x`, following the proof of `hasElementaryChain_of_right_minimal`.

* If `x = 1`, then `W_IL = W_IM`, so `IL = IM` (`standardSubgroup_injective`), and the chain is
  empty.
* Otherwise take a right descent `α` of `x` (`exists_rightDescent_of_ne_one`). Minimality gives
  `α ∉ IL` (`length_lt_mul_simple_of_right_minimal`). Put `S = insert α IL`, let `w₀` and `wI`
  be longest elements of `W_S` and `W_IL` (`exists_longest_mem`), and `ŵ = w₀ wI`.
  `longest_mul_conj_simple` and `exists_simple_image` give `K ⊆ S` onto which conjugation by `ŵ`
  maps the simple reflections of `IL` bijectively, and `length_lt_longest_mul_mul_simple` gives
  `ℓ ŵ < ℓ (ŵ s_i)` for `i ∈ IL`. So `ŵ` is a rank-one step from `IL` to `K` along `α`, and
  `mem_standardSubgroup_iff_of_simple_bijection` gives `g ∈ W_K ↔ ŵ⁻¹ g ŵ ∈ W_IL`.
* As in the Library proof, `x wI` has every `s_j` (`j ∈ S`) as a right descent; for `j = α` this
  uses left minimality of `x` for `W_IM` (`conj_left_minimal`, `length_mul_of_left_minimal`).
  Writing `x wI = x' u` with `u ∈ W_S` and `x'` right `S`-minimal
  (`exists_right_minimal_decomposition`), length additivity forces `u = w₀`
  (`eq_longest_of_forall_descent`), so `x' = x ŵ⁻¹`, and `ℓ x' < ℓ x`
  (`length_mul_simple_of_not_mem` at `α`).
* `x'` is right `K`-minimal (since `K ⊆ S`) and conjugates `W_K` onto `W_IM`, so the induction
  hypothesis gives a chain from `K` to `IM` for `x'`. Prepending the step `ŵ`
  (`hasRankOneChain_cons`) gives a chain for `x' ŵ = x`.

Unlike `hasElementaryChain_of_right_minimal`, no hypothesis `∃ a, a ∉ IL`, no transport
relation and no coset count are needed.
