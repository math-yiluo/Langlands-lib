# The rigidified sign extension on the coroot lattice

For a split pinning over a field \(F\), `CorootLattice` is the integral
coroot span in the cocharacter lattice of the supplied torus.
`corootLatticeInclusion` is the submodule inclusion. Flipping the root datum
and its base gives `simpleCorootBasis`. Under the inclusion its vectors are
the actual simple coroots. A finite numbering `simpleCorootOrder` puts this
basis in increasing order \(e_0,e_1,\ldots\).

Given an integral bilinear form \(B\) on the span, `rigidifiedCorootForm`
takes the strictly lower triangular part \(D\) of \(B\) in the numbered
basis. Thus \(D(e_i,e_j)=B(e_i,e_j)\) for \(j<i\) and is zero for \(i\le j\).
`rigidifiedCorootGroupExtension` is the central sign-cocycle extension for
\(D\) by \(F^\times\). Each `simpleCorootLift` is the point \((e_i,1)\), and
its projection is the corresponding basis vector.

If \(B\) is symmetric and all \(B(e_i,e_i)\) are even, the difference
\(B(v,w)-D(v,w)-D(w,v)\) is even on every pair of points of the lattice.
`rigidifiedCoroot_commutator` combines this parity identity with the
sign-cocycle commutator formula. With \([x,y]=xyx^{-1}y^{-1}\), the
commutator is the central image of \((-1)^{B(v,w)}\) for the respective
projections \(v,w\) of \(x,y\). `simpleCorootLift_commutator` specializes
the formula to the chosen lifts.

The vanishing \(D(e_i,e_i)=0\) makes \(n\mapsto (ne_i,1)\) a group
homomorphism from the additive integers, so it computes every integer
power of the \(i\)-th lift. In an increasing product, each head basis
multiple pairs trivially under \(D\) with the sum of the later basis
multiples. Induction on the list proves that the product has unit
coordinate \(1\). Taking the exponents to be the basis coordinates of \(v\)
therefore yields \((v,1)\). In `rigidifiedCoroot_ordered_coordinates`,
left multiplying by the coefficient inclusion of \(a\) recovers \((v,a)\).
The subgroup generated by the distinguished lifts and the coefficient
group is consequently the whole extension. The proofs apply to any
characteristic and include the empty basis, whose ordered product is \(1\).

Gan–Gao, *The Langlands–Weissman program for Brylinski–Deligne extensions*,
§§2.4–2.5, printed pp. 197–198 (PDF pp. 215–216), give the coroot lattice and
its rigidifying generators with their commutator relations. The triangular
form realizes these relations using the group law of §2.6, printed p. 200
(PDF p. 218), and makes the increasing products have coefficient \(1\).
