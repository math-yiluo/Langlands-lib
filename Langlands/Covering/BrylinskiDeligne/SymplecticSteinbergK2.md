# Symplectic Steinberg cocycles and their universal abelian group

`Langlands.Covering.BrylinskiDeligne.SteinbergCocycle F A` is a map
`c : Fˣ → Fˣ → A` with the following laws, for a field `F` and a commutative
group `A` in independent universes.

- (S1) `c(a,b) c(ab,d) = c(a,bd) c(b,d)`.
- (S2) `c(1,1) = 1` and `c(a,b) = c(a⁻¹,b⁻¹)`.
- (S3) `c(a,b) = c(a,(1−a)b)` for `a ≠ 1`.

In (S3), `oneSubUnit a ha` has field value `1 − a` and is a unit by
`ha : a ≠ 1`. The two axis identities `SteinbergCocycle.one_left` and
`SteinbergCocycle.one_right` follow by applying (S1) to `(1,1,a)` and
`(a,1,1)`, respectively, and cancelling a common factor using `c(1,1) = 1`.

`SymplecticSteinbergK2 F` is Matsumoto's abelian presentation `S(F)`. The
implementation uses the abelianization of the free group on `Fˣ × Fˣ`, then
quotients by the subgroup generated by the left side divided by the right
side of each equation in (S1)–(S3). Its group law is written multiplicatively.
The class of `(a,b)` is `SymplecticSteinbergK2.symbol a b`. Each defining
relator belongs to the quotient subgroup, so the symbols form
`SymplecticSteinbergK2.universalCocycle F`. The axis identities specialize
to `symbol_one_left` and `symbol_one_right`.

For any `c : SteinbergCocycle F A`, `SymplecticSteinbergK2.lift c`
sends `symbol a b` to `c a b`. Lean first extends the assignment to the
free group and factors the map through abelianization using commutativity
of `A`. The cocycle equations put each relator in the kernel, so the map
descends to `S(F)`. The proof of `lift_unique` reduces equality of two
homomorphisms successively to the quotient representatives, the free
group, and its letters.

This presentation is defined over every field. The relations are algebraic
cocycle laws. For the comparison with Steinberg groups the symbol is
oriented as the torus defect
`h_α(a) h_α(b) h_α(ab)⁻¹` at a long simple root. Matsumoto's kernel
comparison in Corollary 5.11 has the infinite-field hypothesis of
Theorem 5.10.

The source is H. Matsumoto, *Sur les sous-groupes arithmétiques des groupes
semi-simples déployés* (1969), §5, printed p. 25 (torus defect), p. 26
(Proposition 5.5), p. 28 (definition after Lemma 5.6) and pp. 30–31
(Theorem 5.10 and Corollary 5.11), PDF pp. 26, 27, 29 and 31–32.
