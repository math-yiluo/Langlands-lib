# Norms in finite unramified extensions of local fields

Serre, *Local Fields*, Chapter V, §1, Lemma 2 and §2, Propositions 2 and
3 and the corollary, give the norm computation via the residue norm,
the trace on the higher quotients, and completeness. The finite-residue
case gives surjectivity on integer units and identifies the field norm
image with the subgroup of orders divisible by the extension degree.

The actual integer rings form a finite free extension. Finiteness makes
all upper integers integral over the base integers, and an element
integral over the base integers has upper valuation at most one. This
identifies the upper integer ring as the integral closure. Localization
then compares the determinant norm of an upper integer with its field
norm. The induced map on integer units commutes with the inclusions into
the field unit groups.

For ramification factor one the image of the base maximal ideal generates
the upper maximal ideal. The finite-level determinant norm theorem gives
a correction to each approximate preimage, including the first correction
by the finite residue norm. The closed sets of units that solve the norm
equation modulo increasing powers of the maximal ideal are nested,
nonempty subsets of a compact group. A point in their intersection solves
the norm equation by Krull intersection.

The image of a base uniformizer has upper order one. Any upper field unit
is therefore a power of this image times an integer unit. The determinant
norm of the uniformizer image is the base uniformizer to the extension
degree, and the integer-unit factor has norm of order zero. Conversely,
a base unit whose order is divisible by that degree can be made of order
zero by dividing by an appropriate norm. The surjectivity on integer units
then supplies the remaining factor. This proves the stated criterion on
the actual norm subgroup in every characteristic.
