# Positive unit grades and coinvariants of a Frobenius lift

An automorphism of the integer ring preserves each power of its maximal ideal, hence each congruence unit subgroup. `unitFiltrationMap` transports this action to the Library's actual filtration subgroups of field units, and `unitGradeMap` is the resulting homomorphism on the successive quotient.

At a positive index `n`, let `π` be a uniformizer. In the residue coordinate of a unit `x`, one writes `x - 1 = π^n a` and takes the residue of `a`. Under the integer-ring automorphism, the coordinate is transformed by the induced residue automorphism and multiplied by the residue of the uniformizer ratio to the power `n`. This follows by applying the automorphism to the displayed identity and cancelling `π^n` in the integer ring. If the residue automorphism is the `q`-power Frobenius, the fixed coordinates solve `c X^q = X` for that common multiplier `c`. For `q > 1` the polynomial `c X^q - X` is nonzero (its degree-one coefficient is `-1`) and has degree at most `q`. Thus there are at most `q` fixed grade elements.

The abstract lemma `exists_hom_factor_of_fixed_card_le` works for a finite abelian group, an endomorphism, and an invariant surjective homomorphism. The quotient by the image of the endomorphism divided pointwise by the identity has size equal to the number of fixed elements. If the target has at least that size, the induced surjection of this quotient onto the target is a bijection. Any invariant homomorphism to a commutative group then factors through the target.

These constructions and lemmas are internal support for the coinvariant step in the direct proof of Henniart's Proposition 3.3 (`prop:H33` in the 16 November 2026 numerical LLC route note). They use a count of fixed points for one Frobenius lift, rather than separate trace and norm computations for unramified and totally ramified extensions. The module does not assert that a particular norm or character has the required invariance; those comparisons are consumer obligations.
