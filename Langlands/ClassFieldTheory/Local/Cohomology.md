# Multiplicative Galois coefficients and actual cohomology

This construction uses the actual automorphism group `Gal(L/K)` acting on
the actual field unit group `Lˣ`. `Additive` changes its notation to the
additive convention used by Mathlib's cohomology complexes; it does not
replace the group. `relativeCohomology` is Mathlib's ordinary group
cohomology of this action. `relativeTateCohomology` uses Mathlib's complex
obtained by joining group chains and cochains with the norm map. The
trivial integral coefficients used by Tate reciprocity are also explicit.

The finite relative and continuous absolute objects are defined separately.
No identification with a direct limit or a central-simple-algebra Brauer
group is made by definition.

## Universes and coefficients

The pinned group and Tate cohomology interfaces use a common universe for
the scalar ring, group and coefficient module. We therefore use
`IntegerScalars := ULift ℤ`, with the explicit ring equivalence
`integerScalarsEquiv` to `ℤ`. Its action on every additive group is the
ordinary integer action: `r • a` is `r.down • a`. The representation is
Mathlib's actual integral unit representation, with its linear maps
restricted along this ring equivalence. The representation identities
are proved from the original identities.
No field carrier, algebraic closure or Galois group is resized. The base
and extension fields of the coefficient construction have independent
universes. Tower maps currently use one shared carrier universe.

In particular, the module action is the actual action on units, and the
standard cochain differential is used. The scalar lift does not assume a
comparison theorem or a vanishing theorem. Mathlib's bundled Hilbert 90
instance is restricted to `Type 0`; its elementary cocycle theorem is
universe polymorphic. No use of the restricted instance narrows these
definitions.

## Absolute coefficients and topology

`discreteUnitsRepresentation K L` equips `Additive Lˣ` with the discrete
topology and promotes each actual Galois action map to a continuous linear
map. The representation identities are inherited from the algebraic action.
Its discrete-topology instance exposes this chosen topology downstream.
There is no assertion that the field topology on `Lˣ` is discrete.

Continuity of each operator alone is weaker than the usual discrete
Galois-module condition. Accordingly, for an algebraic extension,
`unitOpenStabilizer K L x` supplies the actual open subgroup fixing the unit.
It is the stabilizer of its value in `L`, with openness proved by Mathlib's
`stabilizer_isOpen_of_isIntegral` for the Krull topology.
`mem_unitOpenStabilizer_iff` proves, using injectivity of the unit-value map,
that this is exactly the stabilizer for the specified unit representation.
These are intrinsic certificates of the coefficient construction, not
assumptions of a local vanishing or invariant theorem.

`absoluteUnits K` specializes this discrete module to `AlgebraicClosure K`
and `Field.absoluteGaloisGroup K`, with its actual Krull topology.
`absoluteCohomology K n` is Mathlib's `continuousCohomology n` of that
module, computed from the complex of invariant homogeneous continuous
cochains. All finite extensions of Q_p have characteristic zero, so their
algebraic closure is also a separable closure. The construction itself does
not narrow the field carrier universe.

The finite-quotient/direct-limit comparison is a separate theorem, not a
definitional equality in this file. In particular, neither replacing these
groups by Q/Z nor assuming their classification is part of the construction.

## Maps and invariant codomain

For a tower `K → L → M`, `relativeRestriction` comes from the inclusion
`Gal(M/L) → Gal(M/K)` and the identity on `Mˣ`.
When `L/K` is normal, `relativeInflation` comes from normal restriction
`Gal(M/K) → Gal(L/K)` and the algebra-map inclusion `Lˣ → Mˣ`.
These are `groupCohomology.map` on the actual cochain complexes. The
coefficient maps include proofs of linearity and equivariance. In the
finite Galois setting they are the usual restriction and inflation maps.
Corestriction and cup products are not silently identified with these maps.

For `absoluteRestriction K L n`, a specified algebra structure embeds
`AlgebraicClosure K` into `AlgebraicClosure L`, compatibly with `K → L`.
The group map is the actual continuous
`Field.absoluteGaloisGroup.mapOfAlgebra`. The coefficient map
`absoluteRestrictionCoefficients` is inclusion of closure units, and its
equivariance is proved by `AlgEquiv.restrictNormal_commutes`. Applying
`ContinuousCohomology.map` gives the actual map on continuous cohomology.
This records the closure embedding explicitly. Independence of the induced
cohomology map from that choice is not claimed as proved here.

`RationalModIntegers` is the quotient of the additive rational numbers by
the subgroup generated by `1`. `denominatorSubgroup n` consists of the
classes annihilated by `n`, the subgroup usually written `(1/n)ℤ/ℤ` for
positive `n`. `reciprocalClass n` is the class of `1/n`.
An invariant map or fundamental class has not been chosen here.

## Original source and scope

Jean-Pierre Serre, *Local Fields*, English translation by Marvin Jay
Greenberg, GTM 67, Springer, 1979. Source
`serre-1979-local-fields`, SHA256
`109b461d42915da70771c70c4b551a363fc1f33412331ae2671c5c85be433112`,
retained file `C:/Central-Library/Serre--Local Fields--1979.pdf`.

Chapter XI, section 1, printed pages 164–166 (PDF pages 170–172), defines
the multiplicative Galois formation and its finite relative cohomology,
restriction and inflation maps; its condition (*) on page 165 is the
open-stabilizer condition verified for the unit module above.
Chapter X, section 3, printed page 154 (PDF page 160), defines the
direct-limit cohomology with discrete coefficients and states that it
can be computed by continuous cochains. Section 4, pages 155–156
(PDF pages 161–162), describes the compatible field/closure maps inducing
cohomology maps and distinguishes the absolute Brauer cohomology from
finite relative groups. Proposition 5 there proves independence from the
closure embedding; our map construction retains that embedding and does
not add a proof of that proposition.
Chapter XI, section 2, printed pages 166–168
(PDF pages 172–174), specifies the invariant codomain and the finite
denominator subgroup. Section 3, page 168 (PDF page 174), normalizes the
fundamental class to invariant `1/[L:K]`. Chapter XIII, section 4,
pages 195–196 (PDF pages 200–201), specializes the formation to the
separable closure and field multiplicative groups. Worker 42 read these
original passages and visually checked the fundamental-class formulas.
