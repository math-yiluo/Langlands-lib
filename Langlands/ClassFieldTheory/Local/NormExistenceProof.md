# The local norm-subgroup existence theorem

The theorem `normSubgroupExistence` proves the existing proposition `Langlands.LocalClassField.NormSubgroupExistence` unchanged, for every prime and arbitrary carrier universe. The topology is the canonical topology in that proposition, the norm is the determinant norm on actual units, and the resulting finite abelian extension is an actual intermediate field of the algebraic closure.

The cofinality argument first takes the closed, downward directed family of finite abelian norm subgroups. Every element in the intersection belongs to the universal norm subgroup, which lies in every finite-index subgroup by the proved divisibility theorem. Compactness of the integral-unit subgroup U then produces a finite abelian norm subgroup N with N intersect U contained in the prescribed open subgroup H. The subgroup V generated by U and N intersect H has finite index. The unramified containment theorem supplies a finite unramified abelian norm group contained in V. Directedness produces a third finite abelian norm group D contained both in N and in that unramified norm group. The elementary subgroup identity N intersect (U join (N intersect H)) contained in H shows D is contained in H. No exact intersection law for norm groups or prescribed-subgroup existence is used in this step.

Finite saturation above D now constructs an intermediate field E of the chosen finite abelian extension with norm group exactly H. Lift E into the original algebraic closure and transport its finite dimensionality, abelian Galois property, and actual norm subgroup through the canonical lift algebra equivalence. Finally, the proved reversed norm order for finite abelian subfields implies that any two fields having norm group H are equal.

This is a proof assembled from internal supporting results: actual norm transitivity, compatible same-family class-formation cup equivalences, finite Galois correspondence, unramified norm containment, compact norm fibers, universal norm lifting and divisibility. It assumes no reciprocity completion or target existence theorem.
