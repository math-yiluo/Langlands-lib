# Corestriction on finite splitting charts

Let \(L/K\) be a finite separable extension of nonarchimedean local fields
with compatible valuations and a continuous algebra map. The comparison
of chart classes and the construction and existence of common splitting
charts are in this setting.

An ambient finite Galois stage is the fixed field of an open normal subgroup
of the base field's separable Galois group. The stage contains the image of
the upper base field and embeds compatibly into both separable closures.

The finite-to-continuous transfer square identifies the inflation of the
relative corestriction of a splitting-chart class with continuous transfer
of the inflated upper class. The comparison on actual Brauer classes
identifies this continuous transfer with the class of the tensor-descent
corestriction. Injectivity of relative inflation in degree two gives equality
of the two finite-relative classes whenever both charts are given.
The transferred cocycle of the upper chart's scalar factor therefore
represents the class of the corestriction chart. Its unit values are the
Schreier product computed by the ordinary cochain transfer. The Kronecker
lift built from the upper chart has exactly those scalar factors, by the
matrix product calculation with the same coset representatives. The matrix
product identity holds for the underlying fields and separable stage data.

A relative cohomology class has a regular descended central simple algebra
representative split by that finite field. Apply this to the relative
corestriction of the upper chart class. Its inflation is also the class of
the actual corestriction, so the two algebras have equal Brauer classes.
The regular representative is split over the stage. Equality of Brauer
classes and the scalar-extension homomorphism imply the same splitting for
the actual corestriction. This produces a positive-size chart for it. The
finite-stage containment result enlarges an upper splitting field to an
ambient finite Galois stage, giving common charts for every upper algebra.

This proof uses the finite inflation and descent comparison of Serre's
*Local Fields*, Chapter X, section 5, and the degree argument for
corestriction in Chapter XI, section 2, Proposition 1(ii).
