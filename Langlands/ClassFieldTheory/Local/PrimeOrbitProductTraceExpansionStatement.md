# Prime-order orbit product expansion with an ideal-square witness

Worker 107 designed the interface. Worker 726 prepared the Lean proposition
and this explanation after reading the original source and the registered
corrections. The module defines
`Langlands.LocalClassField.PrimeOrbitProductTraceExpansion.{u,v}`. It is a
statement only: nothing here proves the expansion, and nothing here is a
field norm or trace formula.

## The proposition

The universes `u` and `v` are independent and arbitrary. The proposition
quantifies:

- every `G : Type u` with `[Group G]` and `[Fintype G]`;
- every `R : Type v` with `[CommRing R]` and `[MulSemiringAction G R]`, the
  given action of `G` on `R` by ring automorphisms, written `g • y`;
- the hypothesis `Nat.Prime (Fintype.card G)`, so `G` has prime order;
- every ideal `I : Ideal R` that is invariant under the action, in the
  literal form `∀ (g : G) (y : R), y ∈ I → g • y ∈ I`;
- every `x : R` with `x ∈ I`.

For these data it asserts that some `z : R` satisfies `z ∈ I ^ 2` and the
following equality in `R`:

```text
(∏ g : G, (1 + g • x)) - 1 - (∑ g : G, g • x) - (∏ g : G, g • x)
  = ∑ g : G, g • z.
```

The products and sums are Mathlib's ordinary `Finset.univ` products and sums
over the finite type `G`. `I ^ 2` is the square of `I` in Mathlib's ideal
multiplication, so it is the ideal generated by products of two elements of
`I`. The right side is the orbit sum of `z` under `G`. It plays the role of
the trace: in Serre's setting, where `G` is the Galois group of `L/K` acting
on `L`, the sum of the conjugates of `z` is `Tr_{L/K}(z)`. Likewise
`∏ g : G, g • y` plays the role of the norm `N_{L/K}(y)`. The Lean
proposition itself mentions no field, trace map or norm map.

So the statement says that the difference between `∏ g : G, (1 + g • x)` and
`1 + ∑ g : G, g • x + ∏ g : G, g • x` is the orbit sum of an element of
`I ^ 2`. The witness is in the ring itself: no trace map, trace-image
assumption, or expansion certificate is supplied.

## Scope and boundary cases

The proposition includes the following cases:

- arbitrary commutative rings, including the zero ring;
- any characteristic. No division by `Fintype.card G` and no unit or
  nonvanishing assumption is used;
- every prime order, including order two. Then no subset of `G` has between
  two and `Fintype.card G - 1` elements, and the expected witness is `z = 0`;
- `x = 0` and `I = ⊤`.

`G` need not be given as a cyclic group with a chosen generator, although a
group of prime order is cyclic. There are no field, valuation, ramification
or arithmetic hypotheses, and no arithmetic conclusion.

## Source and derived provenance

Jean-Pierre Serre, *Local Fields*, translated by Marvin Jay Greenberg, GTM 67,
Springer, 1979, Chapter V, §3, Lemma 5 and its proof, printed pp.83–84 /
PDF pp.90–91. The registered source is `serre-1979-local-fields`, the file
`/home/taskhub/central-library/Serre--Local Fields--1979.pdf`, SHA256
`109b461d42915da70771c70c4b551a363fc1f33412331ae2671c5c85be433112`.

In that section `G` is cyclic of prime order `l` and `L/K` is totally
ramified. Lemma 5 states that for `x` in `p_L^n`,
`N(1 + x) ≡ 1 + Tr(x) + N(x) mod Tr(p_L^(2n))`. In the proof Serre writes
`N(1 + x) = ∏_{s ∈ G} (1 + x^s)`, where `x^s` is the transform of `x` by `s`,
and expands this product as a sum of terms `x^u`, where `u` runs over sums
`s_1 + ... + s_k` of distinct elements of `G`. The terms with `k` equal to
`0`, `1` and `l` give `1`, `Tr(x)` and `N(x)`. For `2 ≤ k ≤ l - 1` the
translates of `u` by the elements of `G` are distinct. Together they give
`Tr(x^u)`, and `x^u` lies in `p_L^(2n)` because `k ≥ 2`.

That combinatorial step uses only the action of the group on the ring and
the invariance of the ideal. This proposition is an explicitly derived
abstraction of it. It does not claim that Serre states this generality. The
encoding makes these choices:

- an arbitrary finite group `G` of prime order, with no chosen generator,
  replaces the cyclic Galois group;
- an arbitrary commutative ring with a `MulSemiringAction` replaces the ring
  of integers of `L` with its Galois action;
- an arbitrary invariant ideal `I` replaces `p_L^n`. Then `I ^ 2` replaces
  `p_L^(2n)`, since `(p_L^n)^2 = p_L^(2n)`;
- Serre's exponential notation `x^s` is written as the left action `g • x`.
  The products and sums run over all of `G`, so the two notations give the
  same expressions;
- the congruence modulo `Tr(p_L^(2n))` is replaced by its literal
  existential meaning in `R`: some element of `I ^ 2` has orbit sum equal to
  the difference. The orbit sum is the ring-level form of `Tr`;
- the total-ramification and field hypotheses are dropped, because the
  combinatorial step does not use them.

The published `Langlands.LocalClassField.PrimeNormTraceExpansion.{u,v}` in
`Langlands.ClassFieldTheory.Local.PrimeNormTraceExpansionStatement` states
Lemma 5 for the actual field norm and trace in the canonical totally ramified
p-adic setting. That statement is unchanged here. The present proposition is
meant as a separate input to its proof, applied with the actual Galois action
on the integer ring and `I = m_L ^ n`. Turning the orbit sums and products
into field traces and norms is left to that proof.
