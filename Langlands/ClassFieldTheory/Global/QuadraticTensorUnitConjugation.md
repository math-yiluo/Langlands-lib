# Conjugation and norm-one units of a quadratic tensor algebra

For a separable quadratic field extension `B/F`, the left tensor
factor gives the involution `τ` on `B ⊗[F] S`. Its action on
left-factor units is the ordinary conjugation of units in `B`.
Extending the determinant norm along `S → B ⊗[F] S` computes it
by the two embeddings of `B` into itself. Evaluation at the identity
and at conjugation gives `x` and `τ(x)`, so the embedded norm is
`x τ(x)`. Transport of the determinant along the coefficient-algebra
automorphism also gives invariance of the norm under `τ`.

When `S` is a field and the tensor algebra is reduced, any norm-one
unit `x` is `t/τ(t)`. If `x = −1`, a nonzero difference of an element
of `B` and its conjugate provides an anti-invariant unit. Otherwise
take `y = 1 + x`. The identity `τ(y) = y/x` implies that a zero norm
would give `y² = 0`. Reducedness then contradicts `x ≠ −1`. The inverse
of `y` is `τ(y)/N(y)`, so its corresponding unit has the desired
quotient. The full tensor with an infinite completion is reduced by
its decomposition as a product of field completions.

These internal identities use the existing separable-tensor norm formula
and quadratic conjugation for the archimedean approximation of
norm-power pairs.
