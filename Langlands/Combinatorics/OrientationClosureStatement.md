## Source setting and Boolean encoding

In §2.8, `Γ` is a finite graph, `𝒪(Γ)` is the set of *all* orientations of `Γ`,
and `Γ⃗(λ)` is the orientation attached to an ordering `λ`. Condition (★)
defines the lattice of subsets `𝒪 ⊂ 𝒪(Γ)` such that whenever `Γ⃗ ∈ 𝒪` and an edge
`γ` is directed differently in `Γ⃗` and in `Γ⃗(λ)`, the orientation obtained from
`Γ⃗` by reversing `γ` also belongs to `𝒪`. For each edge `γ` the halfspace
`𝒪(λ, γ)` consists of the orientations agreeing with `Γ⃗(λ)` on `γ`. Proof step
(1) observes that, if the opposite orientation `Γ⃗ᵗ` lies in a (★)-closed `𝒪`,
then `𝒪 = 𝒪(Γ)`; the proof of Proposition 2.10 uses the identities
`⋂_γ 𝒪(λ, γ) = {Γ⃗(λ)}` and `⋃_γ 𝒪(λ, γ) = 𝒪(Γ) \ {Γ⃗ᵗ}`.

The Lean statement abstracts this. The edges form an arbitrary type
`E : Type uE` with `[Fintype E]` and `[DecidableEq E]`. After labelling the two
directions of each edge by `Bool`, an orientation is a function `E → Bool`, and
every such function is one; reversing the direction of an edge flips its value.
The reference orientation `Γ⃗(λ)` becomes an arbitrary `o₀ : E → Bool`. Reversing
an edge `e` on which `o` disagrees with `o₀` produces `o₀ e` there, which is
exactly `Function.update o e (o₀ e)`. Inside the proposition, local `let`
bindings name

- `opposite := fun e => !(o₀ e)`, the encoding of `Γ⃗ᵗ`;
- `H e := {o | o e = o₀ e}`, the encoding of the halfspace `𝒪(λ, e)`;
- `Closed S`, meaning `∀ o ∈ S, ∀ e, o e ≠ o₀ e → Function.update o e (o₀ e) ∈ S`,
  the encoding of condition (★).

No vertex set, ordering, acyclicity or graph structure appears. The statement
does not claim that every orientation of an arbitrary graph is `Γ⃗(λ)` for some
ordering `λ`; cyclic orientations are simply further Boolean functions here,
and the reference `o₀` is arbitrary.

## The four clauses

For every `E` and `o₀` as above, the proposition is the conjunction of:

1. Every closed `S` satisfies `S.Nonempty ↔ o₀ ∈ S` and
   `S ≠ Set.univ ↔ opposite ∉ S`.
2. `∅`, `Set.univ`, `{o₀}`, `{opposite}ᶜ` and every halfspace `H e` are closed.
3. For every set `𝒮 : Set (Set (E → Bool))` of closed sets, both `⋂₀ 𝒮` and
   `⋃₀ 𝒮` are closed. Quantifying over sets of subsets rather than over indexed
   families keeps the single universe parameter `uE` while covering every
   indexed family, through its range.
4. `(⋂ e : E, H e) = {o₀}` and `(⋃ e : E, H e) = Set.univ \ {opposite}`.

Clauses 1 and 2 together say that `{o₀}` is the unique minimal nonempty closed
set (every nonempty closed set contains it) and `{opposite}ᶜ` is the unique
maximal proper closed set (every proper closed set lies inside it). Clause 3
says the closed sets form a complete sublattice of all subsets. Clause 4 is the
Boolean form of the two identities in the proof of Proposition 2.10.

The second part of clause 1 restates the observation of proof step (1). Its
first part, and the remaining closure facts, are not printed as separate claims
in the source; they are derived elementary content made explicit here. The
statement does not assert the source's full lattice equality that the
(★)-closed sets are exactly the lattice generated by the halfspaces.

Finiteness of `E` is essential for the first part of clause 1: for an infinite
edge type, the orientations disagreeing with `o₀` on infinitely many edges form a
nonempty closed set omitting `o₀`. No nonempty-edge or positive-cardinality
assumption is made. For empty `E` the orientation type is a singleton, so
`o₀ = opposite`; the intersection over no halfspaces is `Set.univ = {o₀}`, their
union is `∅ = Set.univ \ {opposite}`, and every set is closed.

## Scope

This is the Boolean lattice lemma only. It is not a representation theorem. The
source's construction of the edge submodules `π(λ, γ)`, the identification of
the submodule lattice of `π(λ)` with the (★)-closed sets (Theorem 2.8, steps
(2)–(4), using Lemma 2.6 and §1.11), and the resulting unique irreducible
submodule and quotient of Proposition 2.10 remain separate obligations. They
are not implied by this statement, and no representation-theoretic or
classification result is imported.
