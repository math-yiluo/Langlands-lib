# Integral elements in an Eisenstein power-basis algebra

`Langlands.NYLL.PortInternal.M346.d001` proves the imported
`Langlands.NYLL.PortInternal.M346.d001Statement`. For a discrete valuation
ring `R` with fraction field `K'`, a finite separable extension `L / K'`,
and an integral power-basis generator `B.gen` whose minimal polynomial over
`R` is Eisenstein at the ideal generated by an irreducible `pi`, every
element `z : L` integral over `R` belongs to `Algebra.adjoin R {B.gen}`.

The theorem retains all three independently universe-polymorphic types,
the ring, domain, discrete valuation ring and field instances, the three
algebra structures and their scalar tower, the fraction field,
finite-dimensionality and separability instances, and all arguments
`B`, `pi`, `hpi`, `hBint`, `hei`, implicit `z` and `hzint` in the
published order. Its written conclusion is the fully applied imported
proposition. The proof unfolds that proposition by `change` and adds no
hypothesis.

The implemented proof follows the pinned source. First,
`Algebra.discr_isIntegral` shows that the discriminant of `B.basis` is
integral because the basis entries are powers of the integral generator.
Integral closedness of the discrete valuation ring pulls this discriminant
back to an element `d : R`. Separability and
`Algebra.discr_not_zero_of_basis` show that `d` is nonzero.
`IsDiscreteValuationRing.eq_unit_mul_pow_irreducible` then writes `d` as
a unit times a power of `pi`. The discriminant membership theorem
`Algebra.discr_mul_isIntegral_mem_adjoin` puts the discriminant multiple
of `z` in the generated subalgebra. Scalar-tower compatibility and
`Subalgebra.smul_mem` cancel the unit by its inverse, leaving
`pi ^ m • z` in that subalgebra. Finally,
`mem_adjoin_of_smul_prime_pow_smul_of_minpoly_isEisensteinAt` removes the
prime power, using the given integrality and Eisenstein hypotheses and
the fact that an irreducible in this unique factorization domain is prime.
