# Proofs of completeness and Henselianity of finite integral closures

This module proves the two closed proposition targets published in
`Langlands.NYLL.ValuedFieldTheory.Valuation.DiscreteValuationField.FiniteIntegralClosure.CompletenessStatements`
(NYLL M245.003 and M245.004). Both targets have the three independent
universe parameters `u, v, w`. Both proofs keep them independent.

| Theorem (namespace `Langlands.NYLL.ValuationTheory.DiscreteValuationField.ValuedExtension`) | Universe parameters | Complete unapplied type |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `integralClosure_base_maximal_map_isAdicComplete_of_finite_separable` | `u, v, w` | `IntegralClosureBaseMaximalMapIsAdicCompleteOfFiniteSeparableStatement.{u, v, w}` |
| `integralClosure_henselianRing_of_finite_separable` | `u, v, w` | `IntegralClosureHenselianRingOfFiniteSeparableStatement.{u, v, w}` |

Each theorem's type is the named target constant itself, with its universe
parameters in order and no further arguments. Every mathematical parameter is
bound inside the target. The proofs open the target with `intro` and use only
the hypotheses it supplies.

## The targets

Both targets quantify, in order, over
`(K : Type u) (L : Type w) [Field K] [Field L] [Algebra K L]
[FiniteDimensional K L] (base : CompleteDVF.{u, v} K)
[Algebra.IsSeparable K L]`. They then set
`A := base.valuation.valuationSubring`, the valuation subring of `K` for the
valuation stored in `base`, and `B := integralClosure A L`, the actual
integral closure of `A` in `L`. Write `I` for
`(IsLocalRing.maximalIdeal A).map (algebraMap A B)`, the ideal of `B`
generated by the image of the maximal ideal of `A`. The first target
concludes `IsAdicComplete I B`. The second concludes `HenselianRing B I`.

The universes are those of the targets: `K` and `A` lie in `Type u`, the value
group `base.ValueGroup` lies in `Type v`, and `L` and `B` lie in `Type w`. No
two of them are identified.

All scalar maps are Mathlib's canonical instances, as described in the
statement Markdown. The algebra `A → L` is `Algebra.ofSubsemiring`, sending
`a` to `algebraMap K L a`. The algebra `A → B` is `Subalgebra.algebra` for the
subalgebra `B` of `L`. So `algebraMap A B` is the composite `A → K → L`
corestricted to `B`. The ideal `I` is the extension of the base maximal ideal
along this map. The proofs do not assume or show that `I` is a maximal ideal
of `B`, or that `B` is local.

## Completeness: `integralClosure_base_maximal_map_isAdicComplete_of_finite_separable`

After `intro K L _ _ _ _ base _ A B`, the proof records six facts as local
`have` hypotheses. None is a new global instance.

1. `IsNoetherianRing A`, by instance search. Here `base` makes
   `base.valuation` rank-one discrete. Mathlib's instance
   `Valuation.valuationSubring_isDiscreteValuationRing`
   (`Mathlib/RingTheory/Valuation/Discrete/Basic.lean`, line 449) then makes
   `A` a discrete valuation ring, hence a principal ideal ring. The instance
   `IsPrincipalIdealRing.isDedekindDomain`
   (`Mathlib/RingTheory/DedekindDomain/Basic.lean`, line 170) supplies
   Noetherianity. A private check printed this instance path.
2. `IsFractionRing A K`, by instance search. This is the instance of
   `Mathlib/RingTheory/Valuation/ValuationSubring.lean` (line 153) for a
   valuation subring of `K`.
3. `IsIntegrallyClosed A`, by instance search. This is the instance for a
   valuation subring in `Mathlib/RingTheory/Valuation/LocalSubring.lean`
   (line 38).
4. `Module.Finite A B`, by Mathlib's `IsIntegralClosure.finite A K L B`
   (`Mathlib/RingTheory/DedekindDomain/IntegralClosure.lean`, line 175). Its
   remaining hypotheses are found by instance search: `L` is finite-dimensional
   and separable over `K`, `A` is a domain, the scalar towers `A → K → L` and
   `A → B → L` hold, and `B` is an integral closure of `A` in `L`
   (`integralClosure.isIntegralClosure`).
5. `IsAdicComplete (IsLocalRing.maximalIdeal A) A`. This is the field
   `isAdicComplete` of the class `IsCompleteDiscrete base.valuation`, read
   from the instance `base.instCompleteDiscrete` stored in `base`.
6. `hB : IsAdicComplete (IsLocalRing.maximalIdeal A) B`, by the published
   `Langlands.NYLL.ValuationTheory.DiscreteValuationField.isAdicComplete_of_moduleFinite.{u, w}`
   applied to `A`, `B` and the maximal ideal of `A`. That theorem states that
   a finite module over a Noetherian ring complete for an ideal is complete
   for the same ideal. Its instance arguments are facts 1, 4 and 5. It is used
   at the universes `u` of `A` and `w` of `B`, which stay independent. Here `B`
   is an `A`-module through `algebraMap A B`.

Finally, Mathlib's
`IsAdicComplete.map_algebraMap_iff (IsLocalRing.maximalIdeal A) B`
(`Mathlib/RingTheory/AdicCompletion/Basic.lean`, line 713) says that `B`, as a
module over itself, is complete for `I` exactly when it is complete as an
`A`-module for the maximal ideal of `A`. It uses the scalar tower
`A → B → B`. Its reverse direction applied to `hB` gives
`IsAdicComplete I B`.

## Henselianity: `integralClosure_henselianRing_of_finite_separable`

After `intro K L _ _ _ _ base _ A B`, the proof applies the first theorem,
at the same universes `u, v, w`, to `K`, `L` and `base`. The remaining
instance arguments are those just introduced. This gives
`IsAdicComplete I B` as a local hypothesis. Mathlib's instance
`IsAdicComplete.henselianRing B I` (`Mathlib/RingTheory/Henselian.lean`,
line 170) then proves `HenselianRing B I`: a ring complete for an ideal is
Henselian along it.

The implemented proofs follow the source route, with these differences:

- M245.003: the source obtains Noetherianity, the fraction-ring property and
  integral closedness of the base valuation ring through the NYLL wrappers
  `base_valuationSubring_isNoetherianRing`,
  `base_valuationSubring_isFractionRing` and
  `base_valuationSubring_isIntegrallyClosed` (`IntegralClosure.lean`,
  lines 471–488). Those wrappers are not ported. Here the three facts come
  directly from the Mathlib instances listed above. The source makes these
  instances and `Module.Finite` with `let`; here they are `have` hypotheses.
  The source reads completeness of the base ring from the NYLL projection
  `base.isAdicComplete`; here it is the field of `base.instCompleteDiscrete`.
  The source calls the generic finite-module theorem with the named argument
  `(I := base.maximalIdeal)`; the published theorem takes `A`, `B` and the
  ideal explicitly. The source's final step uses the upstream name
  `isAdicComplete_map_algebraMap_iff`. The current Mathlib name is
  `IsAdicComplete.map_algebraMap_iff`, with the ideal and module explicit.
- M245.004: the source installs M245.003 with `let` and closes the goal by
  `infer_instance`. Here M245.003 is a `have` hypothesis and the goal is
  closed by naming the instance `IsAdicComplete.henselianRing` directly.

## Scope

The module adds two public theorems. It adds no definition, instance, alias of
the `CompleteDVF` projections, or axiom. Kernel inspection lists only
`propext`, `Classical.choice` and `Quot.sound` for both theorems. The proofs
add no hypothesis. They do not assume `Module.Finite A B`, completeness or
Henselianity of `B`, localness of `B`, a discrete valuation ring structure
on `B`, an extension valuation on `L`, or residue-field finiteness;
`Module.Finite A B` is proved inside the first proof. The module does not
state or prove M245.001/.002 (injectivity and faithfulness of the scalar
action), M245.005 onward (localness and the discrete valuation ring property
of `B`), existence or uniqueness of an extension valuation, local-field
packaging, residue actions, inertia or reciprocity.
