# Local norm equations for quadratic extensions of number fields

`Langlands.QuadraticNormLocalGlobalStatement` says that, for nonzero
\(a,b\) in a number field \(F\), the equation \(x^2-ay^2=b\) has a solution in
\(F\) whenever it has a solution in every finite and infinite completion of
\(F\). This is the norm equation associated with the ternary isotropy case
of the Hasse–Minkowski theorem.

For an extension generated by a square root of \(a\), a square image of \(a\)
in a completion makes the completed extension have degree one. Its norm is
surjective, and the split binary norm equation represents every scalar when
\(a\ne 0\). In degree two the image of \(a\) is nonsquare. Sending the
quadratic algebra generator to the image of the global square root then
identifies the multiplication determinant with \(x^2-ay^2\).

The finite and infinite cases use the actual completed extensions, their
local degree formulas and the determinant norms. This is the coordinate
norm interpretation used in J. S. Milne, *Class Field Theory*, version 4.03
(2020), Chapter VIII, Proposition 3.7(c), printed p. 236 (PDF p. 245).

`isSquare_of_all_finite` applies the existence of a nonsplit finite prime in
any nontrivial cyclic extension. If \(a\) is nonsquare, the quadratic field
\(F(\sqrt a)\) has such a prime, where a square root in the base completion
would instead force local degree one.

For \(a,b\in F^\times\), `eventually_exists_eq` represents \(b\) by the
binary norm form at all but finitely many finite places. In the nonsquare
case, the extension is unramified outside a finite set, and the principal
idele of \(b\) is a valuation unit at all but finitely many finite places.
Surjectivity of unramified norms on valuation units gives the required local
preimages there.

`exists_eq_of_local` assumes the cyclic Hasse norm implication. In the
nonsquare case, the local equations give norm preimages at chosen places
above each place. At the cofinite set where integral-unit preimages exist,
the proof chooses such preimages. Their valuations ensure that both they and
their inverses are integral at almost every finite place. The idele lifting
criterion then gives an idele with norm equal to the principal idele of
\(b\). Cyclic Hasse norm supplies a global field-unit preimage. Its two
coordinates solve the global equation.

The binary and ternary applications correspond to Milne, Chapter VIII,
Theorem 3.1 and Proposition 3.7(b,c), printed pp. 234 and 236
(PDF pp. 243 and 245).
