# Complexifying polynomial semisimple directions

The real matrix complexification commutes with scalar multiplication and polynomial evaluation after extension of coefficients. A real semisimple matrix has a squarefree minimal polynomial. Over the perfect field of real numbers the polynomial is separable, and its complex coefficient extension is still separable. It annihilates the complexified matrix, which is therefore semisimple.

The complexification of the polynomial semisimple part of a real matrix \(C\) acts by the scalar \(a\) on the maximal generalized \(a\)-eigenspace of the complexification of \(C\). Indeed, the semisimple part commutes with \(C\) and differs from it by a nilpotent matrix. These properties persist under complexification, and the generalized eigenspace lemma for a commuting semisimple part gives the stated scalar action.

Entrywise complex conjugation is a ring homomorphism on complex square matrices. On a polynomial evaluated at a complexified real matrix, this conjugates its coefficients. Complexifying the entrywise real part of a complex matrix gives the average of the matrix and its entrywise conjugate. The real part of a complex polynomial in a complexified real matrix lies in the real algebra generated by the original matrix. For a monomial the real part is the matching real power with its coefficient replaced by its real part, and the real part is additive. These internal lemmas support interpolation of real semisimple directions from matched complex roots.
