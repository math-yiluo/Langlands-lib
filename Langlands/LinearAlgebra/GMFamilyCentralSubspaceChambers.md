# Coarse chambers and the affine vertex sum for a coarse subspace containing the center

The declarations for the general coarse subspace are in `Langlands.GMFamily.CentralSubspaceDescent`. The shared slice constructions are in [`Langlands.GMFamily`](GMFamilySliceCoarse.md).

Work in a finite-dimensional real inner product space with a root set \(R\), a Levi flat \(a_1\), a subspace \(a \subseteq a_1\) containing the central space \(z\) of \(R\), and a displacement \(\xi \in a_1 \cap a^\perp\). Under `DescentPosition`, if a Levi flat \(L\) of \(a_1\) has nonzero descent coefficient, every point of \((\xi + (a \cap z^\perp)) \cap L \cap z^\perp\) is regular in \(L\). The lift and vanishing lemmas allow any such \(a\). The coarse simple-root bijection, the vertex-sum formula for a fine chamber over a coarse chamber, and the translated-chamber description below use the additional hypothesis `IsLeviSpace R a`.

A fine chamber containing the positive signs of a coarse chamber has the coarse chamber in its closure (`closure_of_positive_subset`). When \(a\) is a Levi flat, a compatible ambient chamber can therefore be chosen for both. Its two simple-root projection formulas identify the nonzero projected fine simple roots with the coarse simple roots and give a bijection preserving the vectors (`exists_coarseSimpleEquiv`). There is only one projected basis in such a fine chamber. It is feasible exactly when the offset of each zero projected normal is positive. In that case its Gram-normalized weight is the inverse coarse denominator (`vertexSum_eq_of_coarse_extension`).

A common strictly positive functional for the nonzero projected fine normals would give a point \(p\) of the coarse space at which every fine simple root is nonnegative, and positive when its projection to \(a\) is nonzero. The restriction to \(a_1\) of a root of the fine chamber is a natural combination of the fine simple roots, and its projection to \(a\) is nonzero only if a simple root with nonzero projection occurs in it. Hence every root of the fine chamber with nonzero projection to \(a\) is positive at \(p\), so \(p\) is coarse regular and its positive set lies in the fine chamber. When the closure contains no coarse chamber, `vertexSum_eq_zero_of_no_coarse` applies offset invariance to give a zero vertex sum. This uses the hypothesis that the parameter \(\Lambda\) is nonzero on every nonzero projection of a root of \(R\) to \(a\).

The projected simple roots of any fine chamber span the noncentral part of the coarse space (`span_sliceNormal_eq_top`), so some of them form a basis (`nonempty_basisIndex`) and a transverse Levi flat with nonzero descent coefficient exists. For every coarse chamber the displacement determines a unique lift (`existsUnique_sliceLift`). A fine active root that vanishes on the coarse space must evaluate nontrivially on the displacement. Otherwise it would vanish at a transverse intersection and hence on its entire Levi flat by general position. Its fine restriction would then lie in the kernel of the bijective normal projection. A small positive displacement of a coarse regular point now gives a fine regular point. The signs of the coarse roots and the signs of the constant slice inequalities characterize its fine chamber. A fine chamber contains at most one coarse chamber (`coarse_chamber_unique`).

The recession cone of the selected fine chamber's slice in \(\xi+a\) is the closure of the coarse chamber in \(a\). The recession inequalities require nonnegative pairings with the fine positive roots, and those roots with nonzero restriction to \(a\) are exactly the coarse positive roots.

When \(a\) is a Levi flat, the slice is a translate of the corresponding open coarse chamber. The constant inequalities hold by the choice of lift. By `exists_coarseSimpleEquiv`, the remaining normals form the coarse simple-root basis of \(a \cap z^\perp\), so a translation absorbs all their offsets. Arthur describes translated chambers in the special-subspace setting in *The invariant trace formula. I. Local theory*, Appendix, printed p. 381.
