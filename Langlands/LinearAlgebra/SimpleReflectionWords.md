# Simple reflections, the deletion lemma and strictly dominant stabilizers

Module `Langlands.LinearAlgebra.SimpleReflectionWords`, namespace
`Langlands.LinearAlgebra.WeylCharacter`.

This module collects the combinatorics of the reflection group of a root pairing
`P : RootPairing ι ℤ M N` with a base `b : P.Base` that the Weyl character
formula needs. These are standard facts about Weyl groups, for example
Humphreys, *Introduction to Lie Algebras and Representation Theory*, GTM 9,
§10.2–10.3, and Bourbaki, *Lie Groups and Lie Algebras*, Ch. VI §1.5–1.6. The
Lean proofs below are direct and integral; they do not pass through a real
form of the root system. Positivity of roots is measured by the Library's
`DominantTriangular.positiveCone P b`, the additive submonoid generated by the
simple roots. The reflection group is the Library's
`DominantTriangular.reflectionGroup P`, the subgroup of `M ≃ₗ[ℤ] M` generated
by the root reflections.

## Positive roots

- `positiveCone_eq_closure_image`: the positive cone is the additive closure of
  the image of `b.support` under `P.root`. This holds definitionally.
- `root_mem_or_neg_mem`, `neg_root_notMem_of_mem`, `neg_root_mem_iff`: every
  root lies in the positive cone or its negative does, and not both. The first
  comes from Mathlib's `RootPairing.Base.root_mem_or_neg_mem`. The second uses
  the Library's `eq_zero_of_mem_positiveCone_of_neg_mem` and the fact that roots
  are nonzero.
- `isPos_iff_mem`: Mathlib's positivity `b.IsPos i` (positive height) agrees
  with membership of `P.root i` in the positive cone. The proof writes the root
  as a natural-number combination of simple roots, or the negative of one
  (`Base.exists_root_eq_sum_nat_or_neg`), and then computes the height with
  `Base.height_eq_sum`.
- `root_reflectionPerm_self_notMem`: `s_a(α_a) = -α_a` is not positive.
- `reflection_root_mem_of_ne`, `root_reflectionPerm_mem_iff` (finite, reduced):
  a simple reflection `s_a` maps every positive root other than `α_a` to a
  positive root. This is Mathlib's `Base.IsPos.reflectionPerm`, moved across
  `isPos_iff_mem`. The `iff` form applies the result to the root
  `s_a(root i)` as well, since `s_a` is an involution.

## Conjugation and words

- `mul_reflection_mul_inv`: if `w` is in the reflection group and
  `w(α_i) = α_j`, then `w s_i w⁻¹ = s_j`. The proof uses the Library's
  `exists_perm_of_mem_reflectionGroup`, which gives a permutation `σ` of the
  roots with `w(α_k) = α_{σ k}` and `α^∨_{σ k}(w x) = α^∨_k(x)`. The formula
  `s_i(x) = x - α_i^∨(x) α_i` then transforms directly.
- `reflection_mul_self`: `s_i s_i = 1`.
- `word b L` is the product `s_{a₁} ⋯ s_{aₙ}` along a list `L` of simple
  indices. `word_nil`, `word_cons`, `word_append`, `word_concat`,
  `word_reverse` and `word_mem` give the obvious algebra. `word_reverse` holds
  because each simple reflection is its own inverse.
- `exists_word_eq_reflection` (finite, reduced): every root reflection is a
  word. The proof is Mathlib's induction principle `Base.induction_reflect`.
  It covers negation (`s_{-i} = s_i`), simple roots, and the step
  `s_{s_j(α_i)} = s_j s_i s_j` (`RootPairing.reflection_reflectionPerm`).
- `exists_word_eq` (finite, reduced): every element of the reflection group is
  a word in the simple reflections, by induction on subgroup closure. Inverses
  are handled by `word_reverse`.

## Deletion lemma and its consequences

- `exists_word_delete` (**deletion lemma**): if `word b L` sends the simple
  root `α_a` to a negative root, then there is a list `L'` with
  `L'.length + 1 = L.length` and `word b L' = word b L * s_a`. The proof is by
  induction on `L = c :: L₀`. If `word b L₀` already sends `α_a` to a negative
  root, the induction hypothesis applies and `c` is prepended. Otherwise
  `word b L₀ (α_a)` is a positive root that `s_c` sends to a negative root, so
  it equals `α_c` by `reflection_root_mem_of_ne`. Then
  `word b L₀ * s_a * (word b L₀)⁻¹ = s_c` by `mul_reflection_mul_inv`, and
  `L' = L₀` works.
- `word_eq_one_of_forall`, `eq_one_of_forall_simple`: an element of the
  reflection group that sends every simple root to a positive root is the
  identity. The proof is by strong induction on the word length. If a word
  `L₁ ++ [a]` has this property, then `word b L₁` sends `α_a` to a negative
  root. The deletion lemma then gives a shorter word for the same element.

## Strictly dominant elements

- `rootForm_root_mul_pairing`: for the canonical form `P.RootForm`,
  `RootForm(α_j, α_j) · ⟨x, α_j^∨⟩ = 2 · RootForm(α_j, x)`. This is obtained by
  evaluating Mathlib's `rootForm_self_smul_coroot` at `x`.
- `rootForm_root_self_pos`: `RootForm(α_j, α_j) > 0`. In the sum of squares
  `∑ᵢ ⟨α_j, α_i^∨⟩²`, the term `i = j` equals `4`.
- `IsStrictlyDominant b x` means `0 < ⟨x, α_a^∨⟩` for every simple index `a`.
  `IsStrictlyDominant.isDominant` gives the Library's `IsDominant`.
- `eq_one_of_isDominant_apply`: if `x` is strictly dominant, `w` is in the
  reflection group and `w x` is dominant, then `w = 1`. Suppose `w(α_a)` were
  a negative root `α_j`. Then `⟨w x, α_j^∨⟩ = ⟨x, α_a^∨⟩ > 0`. But the
  Library's `rootForm_nonneg_of_mem_positiveCone` applied to the dominant
  `w x` and `-α_j` in the cone gives `RootForm(α_j, w x) ≤ 0`. Together with
  the two previous lemmas this is a contradiction (`nlinarith`). So `w` sends
  every simple root to a positive root, and `eq_one_of_forall_simple` applies.

## Determinants

These results assume `[Module.Free ℤ M] [Module.Finite ℤ M]`. Mathlib's
`LinearMap.det` is defined to be `1` on modules without a finite basis, so the
hypothesis is needed.

- `det_reflection`: `det s_i = -1`. In a chosen basis the matrix of `s_i` is
  `1 - u vᵀ`, where `u` is the coordinate vector of `α_i` and `v` is the
  coordinate vector of `α_i^∨`
  (`LinearMap.toMatrix_smulRight`, `Matrix.vecMulVec_eq`). The matrix
  determinant lemma `Matrix.det_one_sub_mul_comm` gives
  `det = 1 - vᵀu = 1 - ⟨α_i, α_i^∨⟩ = -1`.
- `det_mul`: the determinant is multiplicative on linear automorphisms, via
  `LinearEquiv.det`.
- `det_word`: `det (word b L) = (-1)^{L.length}`.

## The half sum of positive roots

- `pairing_rho_eq_one`: if `2 • ρ` is the sum of the positive roots, then
  `⟨ρ, α_a^∨⟩ = 1` for every simple `a`. Write `h i` for `⟨α_i, α_a^∨⟩` when
  `α_i` is positive and `0` otherwise. Pair the reflection permutation
  `π = reflectionPerm a` with this function. Then `h(π i) + h(i)` is `2` when
  `i = a` or `i = π a` (the root `-α_a`), and `0` otherwise. In the last case
  `π` preserves positivity and `⟨π α_i, α_a^∨⟩ = -⟨α_i, α_a^∨⟩`. Summing over
  all roots gives `2 · ⟨2ρ, α_a^∨⟩ = 4`.
