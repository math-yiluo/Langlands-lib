# Bounded cyclic decomposition of an annihilated polynomial module

`Langlands.PolynomialModuleBoundedCyclicDecompositionStatement.{uK, uV}` is
a proposition. This module defines the statement and supplies no proof or
chosen decomposition. The published theorem
`Langlands.polynomialModuleBoundedCyclicDecomposition.{uK, uV}` in
`Langlands.LinearAlgebra.PolynomialModuleBoundedCyclicDecompositionProof`
proves it.

## Statement and conventions

For every field `K : Type uK`, every finite-dimensional `K`-vector space
`V : Type uV`, every `K`-linear endomorphism `f : V →ₗ[K] V`, and every
polynomial `c : Polynomial K`, assume that `c ≠ 0` and
`Polynomial.aeval f c = 0`. The latter equality is an equality of endomorphisms:
evaluating `c` at `f` gives the zero endomorphism.

Then there exist a natural number `m` and a tuple
`q : Fin m → Polynomial K` such that:

1. `m ≤ Module.finrank K V`.
2. For every `i : Fin m`, the polynomial `q i` is monic and divides `c`.
3. There exists a `Polynomial K`-linear equivalence
   
   `Module.AEval' f ≃ₗ[Polynomial K] ((i : Fin m) → Polynomial K ⧸ Ideal.span ({q i} : Set (Polynomial K)))`.

The existence in item 3 is expressed by `Nonempty`; no equivalence is selected
as part of this definition. In Lean the existential quantifiers come first,
followed by the bound, the pointwise conjunction of monicity and divisibility,
and the `Nonempty` equivalence, in that conjunction order.

Mathlib's `Module.AEval' f` is the vector space `V` equipped with the
`K[X]`-module structure in which a polynomial `p` acts by the endomorphism
`Polynomial.aeval f p`. In particular, `X` acts by `f`. Each quotient on the
right is the quotient ring of `Polynomial K` by the principal ideal generated
by `q i`, regarded as a module over `Polynomial K` through the quotient map.
The right side is the dependent function type, with componentwise module
operations. Its index is precisely `Fin m`, so the statement produces a bounded
finite tuple of monic divisors of `c`.

The universes of `K` and `V` are independent. The field has arbitrary
characteristic, and no algebraic closure, splitting field or finiteness of `K`
is assumed. There is no extra hypothesis on `f`. The zero-dimensional case is
included: the bound forces `m = 0`, and the product indexed by `Fin 0` is the
zero module. A nonzero constant annihilator can occur in this case. The
statement does not require positive degree of each `q i`; a construction can
omit zero quotient factors to achieve the displayed bound.

## Provenance and intended argument

This is an internal algebraic prerequisite specified by Worker 39 for the proof
of finiteness of matrix conjugacy classes with fixed characteristic polynomial
in task `e82ee8d6-204c-40ac-85b0-968fb0a6a298`. Worker 597 formalized its
statement and documentation. The published proof
`Langlands.matrixCharpolyFiniteConjugacy` of that finiteness uses it. It is
not a quotation from an external source. The type synonym and scalar action
are Mathlib's existing constructions in
`Mathlib.Algebra.Polynomial.Module.AEval`; the quotient and finite-dimensional
constructions are also existing Mathlib definitions. No new data definition is
introduced.

The intended proof starts from the PID structure theorem, available as
`Module.equiv_directSum_of_isTorsion` in `Mathlib.Algebra.Module.PID`.
Finite-dimensionality over `K` gives finite generation over `K[X]`: a finite
`K`-spanning family still spans when the scalar ring is enlarged. The nonzero
polynomial `c` annihilates the entire module, so it is a torsion module over the
PID `K[X]`. The structure theorem gives finitely many cyclic polynomial
quotients. Omit all zero summands, including those presented by unit
generators, and multiply each remaining generator by a nonzero constant to
make it monic. This normalization preserves its principal ideal and quotient.
Since `c` acts as zero on every quotient, applying its action to the class of
`1` shows that `c` lies in the ideal generated by its monic generator; thus
that generator divides `c`.

Each remaining cyclic quotient is a nonzero finite-dimensional `K`-vector
space and therefore has dimension at least one. The sum of their dimensions
is `Module.finrank K V`, so their number `m` is at most this dimension.
Reindex this finite collection by `Fin m` and use the equivalence between a
finite direct sum and its finite product to obtain the required form. If there
are no nonzero summands, use the empty product. This paragraph explains why
the proposed interface follows from the PID structure theorem. The published
proof `polynomialModuleBoundedCyclicDecomposition` implements these steps with
one change. It bounds `m` by an injective `K`-linear map from `Fin m → K` to
the product, sending each coordinate to a multiple of the class of `1` in its
factor, instead of summing the dimensions of the factors.
