# The scalar centralizer in the even Clifford algebra

Let `Q` be a quadratic form over a commutative ring with `2` invertible. For a linearly ordered orthogonal basis, the products of distinct basis vectors, in increasing order, are a basis of the Clifford algebra. The module uses the linear equivalence between the Clifford and exterior algebras. Orthogonality kills each contraction term when the equivalence is applied to an ordered basis product.

If the basis vectors have unit values, every element fixed by the grade involution and commuting with all vectors is a scalar. Conjugation by a basis vector composed with the grade involution changes the sign of precisely the basis products containing that vector. The given element is fixed by these maps, so its coordinates at all nonempty subsets vanish because `2` is invertible.

This elementary Clifford-algebra calculation supplies the scalar-kernel ingredient for the spin covering in the exact sequence discussed by Kneser, *Lectures on Galois Cohomology of Classical Groups* (1969), §5.11, p. 99.
