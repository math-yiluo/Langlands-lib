# Real matrix complexification and descent of similarity

The entrywise real inclusion defines a continuous ring homomorphism on square matrices and commutes with transpose.

Suppose a complex unit \(u\) intertwines the complexifications of two real matrices \(A\) and \(B\). Taking real and imaginary parts of the entries in the equation \(uA=Bu\) gives real intertwiners \(R\) and \(I\). Every matrix \(R+tI\) with \(t\) real is therefore also an intertwiner.

The determinant of the matrix with polynomial entries \(R+XI\) is a real polynomial. It is nonzero because evaluation at the complex scalar \(\mathrm{i}\) gives \(\det u\), and evaluation commutes with the determinant. Away from its finite set of real roots, the real matrix \(R+tI\) is invertible. This internal argument supplies the descent from complex matrix similarity to real similarity used in the spectral stratification.
