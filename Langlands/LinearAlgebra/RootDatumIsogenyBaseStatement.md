# Statement: isogenies of root data carry bases to bases

Worker 75.

`Langlands.LinearAlgebra.RootDatumIsogenyBaseStatement.{u₁, u₂, u₃, u₄, u₅, u₆} : Prop`
is a statement-only, `Prop`-valued definition. This module does not prove it, and it adds
no other definitions, theorems or axioms. It imports
[`LinearAlgebra.RootDatumIsogeny`](RootDatumIsogeny.md) (for `RootDatumIsogeny`) and
Mathlib's `Mathlib.LinearAlgebra.RootSystem.Base` (for `RootPairing.Base`,
`RootPairing.Base.support` and `RootPairing.Base.IsPos`).

It states Conrad's Lemma 6.1.12: a `p`-morphism of reduced root data carries a positive
system of roots and its base of simple roots in the source to a positive system and its
base in the target.

## Statement and quantifiers

The proposition quantifies over:

- a natural number `p`;
- types `ι : Type u₁`, `X : Type u₂`, `Y : Type u₃`, `ι₂ : Type u₄`, `X₂ : Type u₅`,
  `Y₂ : Type u₆`, with additive commutative group structures on `X`, `Y`, `X₂`, `Y₂`,
  instances `[Finite ι] [Finite ι₂]`, and instances `[Module.Finite ℤ _]` on the four
  lattices;
- Mathlib root data `P : RootDatum ι X Y` and `Q : RootDatum ι₂ X₂ Y₂` with instances
  `[P.IsReduced]` and `[Q.IsReduced]`. These are exactly the parameters of
  `RootDatumIsogeny p P Q`; they say that `P` and `Q` are finite reduced root data on
  finitely generated lattices, with roots indexed by `ι` and `ι₂`;
- an isogeny of root data `f : RootDatumIsogeny p P Q`
  ([`LinearAlgebra.RootDatumIsogeny`](RootDatumIsogeny.md)). It consists of
  `f.characteristicExponent : p = 1 ∨ Nat.Prime p`, injective linear maps
  `f.weightMap : X → X₂` and `f.coweightMap : Y₂ → Y` that are transpose to each other for
  the pairings of `P` and `Q`, a bijection `f.indexEquiv : ι ≃ ι₂` and a function
  `f.rootFactor : ι → ℕ` with values powers of `p`, such that for every `i : ι`
  `f.weightMap (P.root i) = f.rootFactor i • Q.root (f.indexEquiv i)` and
  `f.coweightMap (Q.coroot (f.indexEquiv i)) = f.rootFactor i • P.coroot i`;
- a base `b' : Q.Base` of `Q` (Mathlib `RootPairing.Base`).

## Conclusion

There is a base `b : P.Base` of `P` such that

- for every `i : ι`, `i ∈ b.support ↔ f.indexEquiv i ∈ b'.support`: the simple roots of
  `b` are the roots of `P` corresponding under `f.indexEquiv` to the simple roots of `b'`;
- for every `i : ι`, `b.IsPos i ↔ b'.IsPos (f.indexEquiv i)`: the positive roots of `b`
  are the roots of `P` corresponding to the positive roots of `b'`.

## Mathlib's bases

A base `b : P.Base` (Mathlib, `RootPairing.Base`) consists of a finite set
`b.support : Finset ι` of indices such that the roots `P.root i` and the coroots
`P.coroot i` for `i ∈ b.support` are linearly independent over `ℤ`, and every root
`P.root i` and every coroot `P.coroot i` lies in the additive submonoid generated by the
simple roots (respectively coroots) or its negative lies there. The Mathlib module
docstring of `Mathlib.LinearAlgebra.RootSystem.Base` states that for reduced root pairings
this is equivalent to the usual definition of a base of a root system (Serre,
*Complex semisimple Lie algebras*, Ch. V, §8; Bourbaki, Lie VI §1.5); for non-reduced
pairings it is more restrictive because it also constrains the coroots. Both root data here
are reduced.

`b.height i : ℤ` is the sum of the coefficients of `P.root i` in the simple roots, and
`b.IsPos i` is `0 < b.height i`. Mathlib defines `height` and `IsPos` under a
`[CharZero R]` instance on the coefficient ring; here `R = ℤ`, so this is automatic. A root
is positive exactly when its coefficients are all non-negative.

Linear independence over `ℤ` and over `ℚ` agree in these lattices: the lattices are
torsion-free because the pairing of a `RootDatum` is perfect.

## Derivation from the source

The proposition is Lemma 6.1.12 with `R = P` and `R′ = Q`. Its terms translate as follows.

- **Direction.** A `p`-morphism `(h, d, q) : R′ → R` has `h : X → X′` from the lattice of
  the target `R` to the lattice of the source `R′`. The Library's `RootDatumIsogeny p P Q`
  follows the direction of the weight map (Milne, Definition 23.1): `f.weightMap` goes from
  the lattice of `P` to that of `Q`. So `P` is Conrad's `R`, `Q` is Conrad's `R′`, and
  `h = f.weightMap`.
- **The root bijection and the factors.** Conrad's `d : Φ′ → Φ` sends the root
  `Q.root (f.indexEquiv i)` of `R′` to the root `P.root i` of `R`; that is, `d` is the
  inverse of `f.indexEquiv` on indices. Conrad's `q` takes the value `f.rootFactor i` at
  `a′ = Q.root (f.indexEquiv i)`. With these, `h(d(a′)) = q(a′)a′` is `root_weightMap`,
  and `h∨(a′∨) = q(a′)d(a′)∨` is `coroot_coweightMap`, since `h∨ = f.coweightMap` by
  `weight_coweight_transpose`. Condition 1 of Definition 6.1.8, that `h_ℚ` is an
  isomorphism, is equivalent to injectivity of `h` and of its transpose between these
  free lattices of finite rank, which are `weightMap_injective` and
  `coweightMap_injective`. The values `p ^ n` with `p = 1` or prime are Conrad's
  `{pⁿ}_{n≥0}` with `p` a prime or 1. So `RootDatumIsogeny p P Q` is exactly a
  `p`-morphism `Q → P` in Conrad's sense, with `Q` and `P` re-indexed by `ι₂` and `ι`
  (the root maps are injective since the root data are reduced, so roots and indices
  correspond bijectively).
- **Positive system and base.** For a reduced root datum, a positive system `Φ′⁺`
  determines its base `Δ′`, and every base `Δ′` determines the positive system of roots
  that are non-negative combinations of `Δ′`. So the data `(Φ′⁺, Δ′)` of the lemma are a
  base `b'` of `Q` together with its positive roots `{Q.root j : b'.IsPos j}`, and
  `Δ′ = {Q.root j : j ∈ b'.support}`.
- **Conclusion.** `Δ = d(Δ′)` is the set `{P.root i : f.indexEquiv i ∈ b'.support}`, and
  `Φ⁺ = d(Φ′⁺)` is `{P.root i : b'.IsPos (f.indexEquiv i)}`. Conrad's conclusion is that
  `Δ` is the base of simple roots of the positive system `Φ⁺`; that is, there is a base
  `b` of `P` with simple roots `Δ` and positive roots `Φ⁺`, which is the conclusion of the
  proposition. The second clause is implied by the first (a base determines its positive
  roots), but it is stated to match the two assertions of the lemma.

The universes of the six types are independent. Conrad's root data have finite free
lattices and finite root sets; here the lattices are finitely generated `ℤ`-modules, which
are free because the perfect pairing makes them torsion-free.

## Scope

The proposition does not state:

- the converse, that every base of `P` arises from a base of `Q` in this way (it follows
  by symmetry of the argument, but is not asserted);
- the uniqueness of `b` (which holds, since a base is determined by its support);
- Proposition 6.1.13, which uses the lemma to compare Borel subgroups and pinnings of
  split reductive groups along an isogeny.

## Relation to the Library and Mathlib

- Mathlib's `RootPairing.Base.map` pushes a base forward along a `RootPairing.Equiv`, an
  isomorphism of root pairings. That is the analogue of the lemma for isomorphisms of root
  data; the proposition concerns isogenies, with arbitrary root factors and lattice maps of
  finite index.
- [`GroupScheme.Reductive.AffineSplitIsogenyTheoremStatement`](../GroupScheme/Reductive/AffineSplitIsogenyTheoremStatement.md)
  and
  [`GroupScheme.Reductive.AffinePinnedIsomorphismTheoremStatement`](../GroupScheme/Reductive/AffinePinnedIsomorphismTheoremStatement.md)
  state the Isogeny Theorem and the pinned Isomorphism Theorem over an affine base; Conrad
  uses Lemma 6.1.12 to transport pinnings along isogenies in their proofs (Proposition
  6.1.13 and Corollary 6.1.15).

This module proves none of these relations.
