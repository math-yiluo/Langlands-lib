# Trace form on the semisimple polynomial directions

The internal definition `matrixSemisimplePart` chooses the semisimple matrix from the Jordan–Chevalley decomposition over a perfect field. It lies in the polynomial algebra generated by the original matrix and has nilpotent difference from it. The commuting decomposition is unique.

In characteristic zero the trace form is nondegenerate on the polynomial algebra generated by a semisimple matrix. Every element \(A\) of that algebra is semisimple and has squarefree minimal polynomial. If \(A\ne 0\), a Bézout identity for \(X\) and the part of the minimal polynomial prime to \(X\) gives a polynomial \(u\) for which \(A u(A)\) is a nonzero idempotent. Since \(u(A)\) lies in the same polynomial algebra, \(A\) pairs there with an element whose product with \(A\) has nonzero trace. The trace of a nonzero idempotent is the positive dimension of its range, viewed in the field. This proves nondegeneracy using symmetry of the trace form.

For real matrices this applies to the conormal formed from the conjugation tangent and the polynomial algebra of the semisimple part. Its adjusted-transpose orbit closure follows from the trace projection lemma.
