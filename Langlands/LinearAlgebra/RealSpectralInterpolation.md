# Interpolating real semisimple shifts

The complex characteristic roots of a real matrix are closed under conjugation because their characteristic polynomial has real coefficients. A bijection of the distinct roots of two real matrices which moves each root by less than \(r\) commutes with conjugation if distinct roots of the base matrix are more than \(2r\) apart. This follows from conjugation being an isometry and from the triangle inequality.

Given a complex shift for each distinct root of \(A\), take the Lagrange polynomial with those values, evaluate it at the complexified polynomial semisimple part of \(A\), and take the entrywise real part. The result lies in the real algebra generated by the semisimple part. It depends continuously on the shifts since Lagrange interpolation has a fixed finite basis. The zero shifts give the zero matrix.

If the shifts commute with conjugation, the interpolating polynomial and its coefficient conjugate have the same prescribed value at each root. The complexification of the real part is their average after evaluation. On each generalized eigenspace of the original matrix this average acts by the specified shift, because the complexified semisimple part acts by the original eigenvalue. This internal construction supplies the real transverse displacement for nearby matched spectra.
