# Comparing matched generalized eigenspaces

On the maximal generalized \(a\)-eigenspace of an endomorphism \(f\), the restriction of \(f-aI\) is locally nilpotent, with an exponent that can depend on the vector. In finite dimension, there is a common exponent and the restriction is nilpotent. In any dimension, the kernel of each ambient shifted power lies inside this generalized eigenspace. Restriction therefore gives an isomorphism between the kernel of the restricted power and the ambient kernel, as used in the Lean `finrank` identity.

For endomorphisms of finite-dimensional spaces of equal dimension, assume the two specified scalars have equal algebraic multiplicities and ranks of the corresponding ambient shifted powers agree through the ambient dimension. Equal multiplicities give equal generalized eigenspace dimensions. Rank-nullity gives equality of the ambient kernel dimensions, hence equality of the restricted kernel dimensions. Applying rank-nullity inside the generalized eigenspaces gives equality of ranks of the nilpotent restrictions through their dimension. The nilpotent rank criterion supplies an isomorphism of the two generalized eigenspaces intertwining the restrictions.

In finite dimension over an algebraically closed field, the maximal generalized eigenspaces indexed by the distinct eigenvalues form an internal direct sum. The proof uses the independence and spanning of generalized eigenspaces and the fact that the space at any other scalar is zero. For any two supplied internal direct sums with a common index type, componentwise intertwining equivalences assemble to an ambient one. This follows by composing the direct-sum equivalences and verifying the intertwining relation on each summand.

These are internal results used to compare matrices whose eigenvalue labels vary.
