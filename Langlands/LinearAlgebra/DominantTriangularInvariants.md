# Dominant orbit representatives and triangular spanning of invariants

Worker 1800. Internal combinatorial support for the proof of
`Langlands.GroupScheme.ClassFunctions.TorusSeparationStatement` (task
`0c854e73-f39c-45cf-9dda-31e34e1711f6`). It supplies the weight-lattice half of
Steinberg's argument that the restrictions of characters of irreducible
representations span the Weyl-invariant functions on a maximal torus
(R. Steinberg, *Regular elements of semi-simple algebraic groups*, 1965,
source `steinberg-1965-regular-elements-semisimple-groups`, Lemma 6.3 and
Corollary 6.4, printed pp.62–63, respectively). The facts used are standard root-system
combinatorics: every Weyl orbit of weights meets the dominant chamber, and an
invariant function with highest weight `λ` is triangular with respect to the
dominance order. The Lean proofs below are written from scratch on top of
Mathlib's `RootPairing` API and do not follow a particular text line by line.

## Setting

Everything is stated for a root pairing `P : RootPairing ι ℤ M N` over the
integers with a base `b : P.Base`, in namespace
`Langlands.LinearAlgebra.DominantTriangular`.

- `reflectionGroup P` is the subgroup of `M ≃ₗ[ℤ] M` generated by all root
  reflections `P.reflection i` (the Weyl group acting on the weight lattice).
- `positiveCone P b` is the additive submonoid of `M` generated by the simple
  roots `P.root a`, `a ∈ b.support`: the nonnegative integral combinations of
  simple roots. The relation "`λ - μ ∈ positiveCone P b`" is the dominance order
  `μ ≤ λ`.
- `IsDominant P b x` means `0 ≤ ⟨x, α_a^∨⟩` for every simple coroot, the pairing
  being `P.toLinearMap x (P.coroot a)`.
- `IsInvariant W f`, for a subgroup `W` and a finitely supported function
  `f : M →₀ k`, means `f (w μ) = f μ` for all `w ∈ W` and all `μ`.
- `orbit μ` is the `reflectionGroup P`-orbit of `μ`, and `orbitSum k μ : M →₀ k`
  its indicator function (finitely supported once `ι` is finite).

## Results

- `eq_zero_of_mem_positiveCone_of_neg_mem`: the positive cone is pointed. The
  simple roots are linearly independent (`P.Base.linearIndepOn_root`), so a
  nonnegative combination whose negative is also nonnegative is zero.
- `finite_reflectionGroup` (instance, `[Finite ι]`): the reflection group is
  finite. By closure induction every element `w` permutes the roots by some `σ`
  with `P.coroot' (σ i) (w x) = P.coroot' i x`, and moves every vector by an
  element of the root span. An element fixing every root therefore moves each
  `x` by a vector `d` of the root span with all coroot values zero, so
  `P.RootForm d d = 0`, and Mathlib's
  `eq_zero_of_mem_rootSpan_of_rootForm_self_eq_zero` gives `d = 0`. Hence the
  action on the finite set of roots is faithful and the group is finite.
- `exists_dominant_mem_orbit` (`[Finite ι]`): every orbit contains a dominant
  element. The orbit is finite; take an element maximal for the strict
  dominance order. If its pairing `c` with some simple coroot were negative, the
  simple reflection would add `(-c) • α_a`, a nonzero element of the cone,
  contradicting maximality.
- `rootForm_lt_of_dominant` (`[Fintype ι]`): if `μ` is dominant, `λ - μ` lies
  in the positive cone and `μ ≠ λ`, then `B(μ, μ) < B(λ, λ)` for the root form
  `B = P.RootForm`. Writing `λ = μ + d`, one has `B(d, μ) ≥ 0` because
  `B(α_a, μ)` is a nonnegative multiple of `⟨μ, α_a^∨⟩`
  (`rootForm_self_smul_coroot`), and `B(d, d) > 0` for nonzero `d` in the root
  span (`posRootForm_posForm_pos_of_ne_zero`).
- `isInvariant_mem_of_orbitSum_mem`: an invariant function lies in a submodule
  `S` as soon as the orbit indicators of all points of its support do
  (induction on the size of the support).
- `isInvariant_mem_of_triangular` (`[Finite ι]`, `k` a ring): **triangular
  spanning.** Let `S` be a `k`-submodule of `M →₀ k`. Suppose every dominant
  `λ` admits an invariant `c ∈ S` with `c λ = 1` and `λ - μ` in the positive
  cone whenever `c μ ≠ 0`. Then every invariant `f : M →₀ k` lies in `S`. The
  proof shows `orbitSum k λ ∈ S` for dominant `λ` by strong induction on the
  natural number `B(λ, λ)`. The difference `c - orbitSum k λ` is invariant
  and vanishes on the orbit of `λ`. Each other point of its support has a
  dominant orbit representative `ν'` strictly below `λ`, so
  `B(ν', ν') < B(λ, λ)`. The general case follows from
  `isInvariant_mem_of_orbitSum_mem` and `exists_dominant_mem_orbit`.

## Scope

The file is pure lattice combinatorics. It does not mention group schemes,
representations or characters; the hypothesis of `isInvariant_mem_of_triangular`
is supplied elsewhere by the formal characters of highest-weight
representations. No finiteness of the Weyl group is imported: the instance
`finite_reflectionGroup` proves it from the root form. Dominance, the positive
cone and the reflection group are defined directly from the root pairing and its
base. They do not depend on the Library's `SplitPinning` structures, although they
agree with `SplitPinning.IsDominantWeight` and `SplitPinning.positiveRootMonoid`
when instantiated with a pinning's root datum.
