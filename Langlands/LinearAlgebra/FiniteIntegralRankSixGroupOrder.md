# A congruence bound for finite integral linear groups of rank six

`FiniteIntegralRankSixGroupOrderStatement` says that a finite group with a faithful representation in \(\operatorname{GL}_6(\mathbb Z)\) has order dividing \(2^{13}3^8\cdot 5\cdot 7\).

For each integer \(m\geq 3\), an element of the group whose matrix reduces to the identity acts as the identity on the free lattice modulo \(m\). It has finite order, so `finiteOrderCongruenceDetection` makes its action on the integral lattice trivial. Thus reduction preserves faithfulness. Taking \(m=3\) and \(m=7\) gives divisibility by both finite general linear group orders. The formula for those orders and the computed greatest common divisor give the bound.

The congruence input is the classical torsion-freeness fact recalled by B. Conrad, *Reductive group schemes* (2014), proof of Corollary B.3.6, printed p. 294. The bound gives a single possible factor of five for the rank-six root automorphism group in the torus-order calculation of Platonov and Rapinchuk, *Algebraic Groups and Number Theory* (1994), §6.8, Proposition 6.21.

`FiniteIntegralRankSixGroupOrderWithLineStatement` strengthens the bound for a representation whose reduction modulo three preserves the line generated by a supplied nonzero vector. Its order divides \(2^{13}3^8\cdot 5\). For an element of order seven, the induced action on the line scales the generator by an element of \(\mathbb F_3\) whose seventh power is one, so the generator is fixed. On the six-dimensional space the fixed subspace then has positive dimension \(j\), and its cardinality \(3^j\) is congruent to \(3^6\) modulo seven by the permutation count. The only such \(j\) in the range from one to six is six. The element thus acts as the identity after reduction, which contradicts faithfulness.
