# Coarse chambers and the affine vertex sum

Work in a finite-dimensional real inner product space with a root set \(R\), nested Levi flats \(a \subseteq a_1\), and a displacement \(\xi \in a_1 \cap a^\perp\). Write \(z\) for the central space of \(R\). Under `DescentPosition`, if a Levi flat \(L\) of \(a_1\) has nonzero descent coefficient, every point of \((\xi + (a \cap z^\perp)) \cap L \cap z^\perp\) is regular in \(L\).

A fine chamber containing the positive signs of a coarse chamber has the coarse chamber in its closure. A compatible ambient chamber can therefore be chosen for both. Its two simple-root projection formulas identify the nonzero projected fine simple roots with the coarse simple roots and give a bijection preserving the vectors. There is only one projected basis in such a fine chamber. It is feasible exactly when the offset of each zero projected normal is positive. In that case its Gram-normalized weight is the inverse coarse denominator.

A common strictly positive functional for the nonzero projected fine normals would give a coarse regular point in that fine chamber's closure. To see regularity, the normals vanishing at this point all annihilate the coarse flat, so its Levi flat is the coarse one. When the closure contains no coarse chamber, `vertexSum_eq_zero_of_no_coarse` applies offset invariance to give a zero vertex sum. This uses the hypothesis that the parameter \(\Lambda\) is nonzero on every nonzero projection of a root of \(R\) to \(a\).

For every coarse chamber the displacement determines a unique lift. A fine active root that vanishes on the coarse space must evaluate nontrivially on the displacement. Otherwise it would vanish at a transverse intersection and hence on its entire Levi flat by general position. Its fine restriction would then lie in the kernel of the bijective normal projection. A small positive displacement of a coarse regular point now gives a fine regular point. The signs of the coarse roots and the signs of the constant slice inequalities characterize its fine chamber.

The selected fine chamber meets the displaced coarse flat in a translate of the corresponding open coarse chamber. The recession cone of this slice cell is the closure of the coarse chamber. The translated chambers are described by Arthur, *The invariant trace formula. I. Local theory*, Appendix, printed p. 381.
