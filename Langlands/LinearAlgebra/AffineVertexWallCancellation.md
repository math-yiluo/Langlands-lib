# Wall cancellation for weighted affine vertices

Both results fix a real-linear map `param : V →ₗ[ℝ] ℂ` with `param (v i) ≠ 0` for every nonzero normal `v i`. At a regular offset all inequalities outside each basis have nonzero slack. The invariance theorem compares regular offsets with matching signs on the zero-normal inequalities. A finite hyperplane gallery connects them while avoiding the walls of those constant inequalities.

At a crossing, `changesAt` says the additional inequality is tight and all slacks outside the enlarged basis support are strictly positive. Every basis deletion of that support gives the same intersection and the same slacks outside the support. Each basis normal is nonzero, and avoidance of the constant walls makes the additional normal nonzero as well. The nonvanishing hypothesis on `param` thus makes the common product of the parameter evaluations nonzero.

The circuit coefficient of the omitted normal determines the sign of the feasibility change and the absolute volume ratio. Their product gives the signed coefficient. With the common denominator, the sum of the changes for one support is a scalar times the parameter evaluated on the linear dependence of the normals, hence zero. Summing over supports proves invariance. When no real linear functional is strictly positive on every nonzero normal, perturb an offset with strictly negative entries for those normals to a regular one, preserving those signs and the signs of the constant inequalities. A feasible basis there would yield such a functional by negating its interpolated offset function. The sum is therefore zero there, and invariance gives zero at the original offset.

The proof works with the affine chamber slice used in Arthur, *The invariant trace formula. I. Local theory*, Appendix, printed pp. 381–383.
