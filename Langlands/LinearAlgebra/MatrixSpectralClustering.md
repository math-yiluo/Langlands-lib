# Local spectral clusters of complex matrices

The characteristic-polynomial coefficients below the leading term are continuous on square matrices over a topological commutative ring. Each coefficient is the evaluation of the corresponding universal multivariate coefficient at the matrix entries.

For a complex matrix \(A\), enumerate the roots of its characteristic polynomial with multiplicity in any order. The local matching theorem for monic polynomials gives an enumeration for every sufficiently nearby \(B\) with each root arbitrarily close to the corresponding root of \(A\).

Choose disjoint open disks of the same positive radius around the distinct roots of \(A\). For all sufficiently nearby \(B\), each root of \(B\) belongs to exactly one of these disks. The number of roots of \(B\) in a disk, counted with multiplicity, equals the multiplicity of the root at its center. The proof filters the enumerated roots by disk membership, which agrees at each index with equality to the center in the enumeration for \(A\).

Each disk contains at least one root, so the number of distinct roots is lower semicontinuous. Where that number stays fixed, each disk contains a single distinct root, and the resulting bijection of distinct roots preserves their multiplicities. The disks can be chosen inside any prescribed positive radius. These are internal consequences of properness of the root-to-coefficient map and provide the spectral labels used in local matrix stratification.
