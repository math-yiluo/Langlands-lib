# Extension of isometries from a nondegenerate quadratic space

Over a field of characteristic different from two, two isometric linear embeddings of a finite-dimensional nondegenerate quadratic form into one ambient quadratic space differ by an isometry of that space. The ambient form may be degenerate. This is the nondegenerate-subspace case of Witt's extension theorem.

`reflection` sends a vector `x` to `x - (B(x,z) / Q(z)) z`, where `B` is the polar form and `Q(z)` is nonzero. The formula preserves the quadratic form and is an involution. To match two vectors of equal nonzero value, reflect in their difference if it has nonzero value. Otherwise reflect in their sum and then in the target vector. Both constructions fix the common orthogonal subspace. Induction on a finite orthogonal basis of the source matches its images under the two embeddings. Linearity extends this equality to the entire source.

The formulation is an elementary quadratic counterpart of the use of Witt transitivity in Kneser's *Lectures on Galois Cohomology of Classical Groups*, §5.8, pp. 92–93 (PDF pp. 98–99). Its proof uses the explicit reflection formula and the Library's orthogonal-basis theorem.
