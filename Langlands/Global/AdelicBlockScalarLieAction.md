# Lie differentiation on smooth compact-finite adelic block sections

`smoothCompactFiniteSections` consists of realized scalar sections whose fixed finite slices are smooth and whose infinite compact orbit spans are finite-dimensional. The ambient finite-smooth space already imposes an open finite stabilizer. On the compact-finite carrier, smoothness of every fixed finite slice is equivalent to smooth scalar evaluation of the normalized compact Levi slices. The finite and infinite compact subrepresentations act by right translation. Finite translation shifts the finite argument of a slice, and infinite compact translation right-translates its archimedean argument.

`sectionDifferential` differentiates each smooth slice in a real archimedean Lie direction. Left translation commutes with this differentiation, so the derivative remains left invariant under the adelic unipotent radical. The normalized compact slices of the derivative lie in the original Levi coefficient space by `normalizedSlice_derivative_mem`. An open finite subgroup that fixes the original section fixes its derivative because right translation by that subgroup equates the corresponding smooth slices before differentiation.

For compact finiteness, the compact orbit span of the original section inside the smooth carrier is finite-dimensional because the inclusion into the ambient finite-smooth carrier is an injective compact intertwiner. A compact translate of the derivative is the derivative of the translated section in the adjoint-conjugate direction. Expanding that direction in a finite real Lie basis puts the translated derivative in the sum of the images of the original orbit span under the basis differentials. This sum is a finite-dimensional complex subspace. The bundled differential is complex-linear in the section and restricts on every fixed finite slice to the Library's smooth right-translation differential.

This is an internal construction on the realized scalar model. Getz and Hahn (2024), §10.1, pp. 270–271 (PDF 282–283), describe the normalized scalar induced sections. Langlands (1979), proof of Proposition 2, pp. 204–205 (PDF 199–200), uses smooth compact-finite scalar sections with cuspidal Levi slices.
