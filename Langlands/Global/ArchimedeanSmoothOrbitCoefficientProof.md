# Smooth scalar coefficients of genuine translation orbits

Authors: Worker 94, Worker 348 and Worker 566.

The theorem proves the published `SmoothOrbitCoefficientStatement K n`
with its original derivative topology, smooth-function carrier and
enveloping action. This prerequisite leaves the parent differential-growth
theorem open.

The theorem `smoothOrbitCoefficientStatement` uses the single constructed
function `smoothOrbitCoefficient K n ℓ f`. Its value is exactly `ℓ(R(g)f)`.
The helpers embed the actual derivative compact-open topology into a product
of normed compact restriction spaces. Real Hahn–Banach extends the scalar
functional to this product, and continuity makes that extension depend on
finitely many coordinates.

For each coordinate, differential covariance and smooth finite adjoint
coefficients give a jointly smooth finite sum on the open matrix units
domain. Compact parameter calculus supplies normed smoothness. Finite
coordinate scalarization then proves that the coefficient is an actual
smooth function, without assigning a norm to the whole space of smooth
vectors.

The derivative interchange is proved first for a real generator. The
two-variable function `D(a)(R(g)f)(x)` is smooth; mixed partial symmetry and
left real generator induction identify its parameter derivative with
`D(a)(R(g)D(X)f)(x)`. Continuous point evaluation identifies the normed
coordinate derivative, and the scalar extension identifies the derivative
after applying `ℓ`. A second left real generator induction, including complex
scaling, proves the asserted identity for every actual complex enveloping
operator with the original order of multiplication.

The construction and proof retain arbitrary number-field universes and
every natural rank, including zero. They add no finiteness, analyticity or
connectedness premise. The result is an internal derived analytic
prerequisite; it does not prove orbit density, admissibility, reproduction or
the final automorphic growth theorem. Worker 348's differential covariance
and Worker 566's adjoint coefficient argument were adapted in the subordinate
helpers.
