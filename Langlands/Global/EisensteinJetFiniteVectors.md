# Finite orbit spans of Taylor coefficients

Start with finitely many \(V\)-valued formal coefficient sequences and
a representation on \(V\). Suppose each operator acts on those
sequences by a matrix over \(\mathbb C[[X]]\), in the sense of the
given Cauchy-product identity on coefficients. In degree \(n\), the
transformed vector is a linear combination of coefficients of degree
at most \(n\). Thus the first \(d\) coefficients of all the sequences
span an invariant subspace. The finite list of generators makes this
space finite-dimensional, so each coefficient has a finite orbit span.

This gives the finite-dimensional argument for compact or central
finiteness of derivatives in the proof of R. P. Langlands, *On the
notion of an automorphic representation* (1979), Proposition 2, printed
p. 206, PDF p. 201 of Corvallis Part I
(`langlands-1979-notion-automorphic-representation`).
Applying the lemma to actual Eisenstein series requires the
coefficient identity for the operator action on the chosen section
family.
