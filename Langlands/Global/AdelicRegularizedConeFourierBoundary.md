# Polynomial Fourier boundary values of the regularized pairing

The regularized term of Delorme's §7 uses a polynomial on a negative coroot orthant with a translated vertex. With nonzero real parts in every coordinate, its rational formula equals a signed, absolutely convergent Laplace integral over the corresponding oriented orthant. Reflection turns the phase into the negative phase on the spatial variable used in the Fourier test.

`translatedOrthant_polynomialLaplace` proves this comparison for the actual `negativeOrthantRegularizedIntegral` and `polynomialShift`. The proof expands the polynomial into monomials and factors each integral into half-line moments. Reflection and the one-dimensional moment formula determine the signs. Translation preserves product Lebesgue measure and contributes the displayed exponential and polynomial shifts.

The comparison is the coordinate form of the regularized cone calculation in Delorme, *On the spectral theorem of Langlands*, Advances in Mathematics 426 (2023), §7, (7.8)–(7.12) and Proposition 7.8.

`RegularizedOrthantFourierBoundaryStatement` is the tested limit on the imaginary axis. The displacement has a nonzero slope in each coordinate and the test is integrable for a supplied s-finite spectral measure. The reflected polynomial times the Fourier test is assumed integrable on the translated spatial orthant for product Lebesgue measure. For positive displacement, the absolute convergence of the polynomial Laplace integral and the unit norm of the Fourier kernel justify Fubini. After translating the vertex, the scalar exponential remaining outside the integral tends to one. Inside, the damping is bounded by one and tends to one, so dominated convergence proves the boundary formula. The orientation sign multiplies the regularized term on the spectral side.

`translatedOrthant_polynomialTest_tendsto` follows the translates with vertices determined by \(b+n v\). It assumes \(v_i>0\) and \(d_i\ne 0\) in every coordinate, and integrability of the polynomial Fourier test on the entire spatial coordinate space. Every fixed point eventually has \(x_i+b_i+n v_i>0\) in all coordinates. The translated orthant indicator therefore eventually equals one if all \(d_i>0\), and zero if some \(d_i<0\). Dominated convergence gives the full-space integral in the first case and zero in the second. The strictly dominant truncation ray is used in the proof of Lemma 7.11 on p. 35.
