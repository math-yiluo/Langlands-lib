# The truncated and the regularized Eisenstein pairings of Delorme 2023, §7

Module `Langlands.Global.AdelicTruncatedEisensteinPairing` defines the two sides of the
asymptotic identity of Theorem 7.1 of Delorme, *On the spectral theorem of Langlands* (2023):

- the region `𝒞_T^G ⊂ G(F) \ G(𝔸)^1` and the truncated pairing
  `Ω^T_{P_0}(E, Φ)^Z = ∫_{𝒞_T^G} E^Z conj Φ^Z`;
- the finite regularized sum `ω^T_{P_0} = ∑_{Q ∈ 𝒫_st} r^T_Q`, computed from finite unitary
  exponent and polynomial data of Delorme's extended pairings `p_Q` (the pairing (3.7) of the
  square-integrable part of the first argument, p.15) of the weak constant terms along each
  standard `Q`, together with the predicates relating those data to the actual pairings;
- fixed affine exponent labels, Delorme's `λ ↦ sλ − μ` of (7.12), and the generic parameter
  region where `θ_Q` vanishes at no label.

It also proves that the closed formula used for the regularized integrals is the convergent
integral where the latter converges, and that it satisfies the positive Fourier convention of
(7.10)–(7.11). The module does not state Theorem 7.1 (analyticity of the summands of
`ω^T_{P_0}` on its set `ℋ^c`, the analytic extension of `λ ↦ ω^T_{P_0}`, and the exponential
estimate of `Ω^T_{P_0} − ω^T_{P_0}`), and it does not prove that data satisfying the relation
predicates exist.

## The region `𝒞_T^G`

The definitions in namespace `ArthurTruncation` use the data of `Langlands.Global.ArthurSiegelSets`:
a connected reductive `G` over a number field `F`, a maximal split torus `i : T → G`, a generic
cocharacter `ν` (the minimal standard parabolic `P_0 = Standard.parabolicHom i ν ∅`), coroot data
`D`, a compact subgroup `K` with `P_0(𝔸) K = G(𝔸)` (`hK`), and the bijectivity `hρ` of
`X^*(P_0) ⊗ ℝ → X^*(T) ⊗ ℝ`. Here `𝔞_0 = V T`, `H_0 = height hi hK hρ` is the Iwasawa height of
`K` for `P_0` read in `V T`, and `𝔞_0^G = D.realCorootSpan`.

- `characteristicChamber ν hG hi D T₀ Tp` is the set of `X ∈ V T` with `X ∈ D.realCorootSpan`,
  `0 ≤ α(X − T_0)` and `ϖ_α(X − T) ≤ 0` for every `α ∈ Δ_0 = simpleFinset ν hG hi`, where
  `α(H) = eval T α H` and `ϖ_α = ArthurRelative.weight hG hi ν D Δ_0 α`, the fundamental weight of
  `α` in `Δ̂_0` (the coefficient of `α̌` in the `𝔞_0^G`-component). This is Delorme's `C_T^Q` for
  `Q = G`: then `Δ_0^Q = Δ_0`, `Δ̂_0^Q = Δ̂_0` and `Δ_Q` is empty, so the strict conditions
  `β(X − T) > 0` are absent. The chamber condition is the closed positive chamber starting at
  `T_0`, as printed.
- `leviHeightSet hi hK hρ C` is `M_0(C)`: the images in `G(𝔸)` of the points `m` of the standard
  minimal Levi `M_0 = Standard.leviScheme i ν ∅` whose image lies in `G(𝔸)^1 = normOneSubgroup F G`
  and has `H_0 ∈ C`. Delorme's definition has both conditions; for `m ∈ M_0(𝔸)` they are
  redundant, since `H_0(m) ∈ 𝔞_0^G` exactly when `m ∈ G(𝔸)^1`.
- `characteristicProduct hG hi D hK hρ C T₀ Tp` is the pointwise product
  `Ω_{N_0} · M_0(C_T^G) · K` in `G(𝔸)`, with `Ω_{N_0} = C.omegaN` mapped by the standard unipotent
  radical `N_0 = Standard.unipotentScheme i ν ∅`. `C : CompactFactors` is the compact data
  `Ω_0 = Ω_{N_0} Ω_{M_0^1}` of the Siegel domain (2.3) that Delorme fixes at the beginning of §7.
  The product uses the given `K` (the `K` of the height); in the statements of `ArthurSiegelSets`
  the compact subgroup `C.compactSubgroup` is taken equal to `K`, and it does not enter here.
- `characteristicRegion hG hi D hK hρ C T₀ Tp` is the image under `LeftCosetSpace.mk` of the
  norm-one points of the product in `NormOneRationalQuotient F G = G(F) \ G(𝔸)^1`, that is
  `G(F) Ω_{N_0} M_0(C_T^G) K` as a subset of `[G]^1 ⊂ [G]`. Every factor lies in `G(𝔸)^1`, the
  common kernel of the absolute values `|χ|_𝔸` of the rational characters `χ` of `G`: these
  absolute values are trivial on the unipotent group `N_0(𝔸)` and on the compact subgroup `K`
  (whose image is a compact subgroup of `ℝ_{>0}`), and `M_0(C_T^G) ⊂ G(𝔸)^1` by definition. So the
  norm-one slice is the whole product; it is stated as a slice so that the set lives on the
  norm-one quotient and its Haar measure.
- `IsRationalCovering C` is (7.1): every `n ∈ N_0(𝔸)` is `r ω` with `r ∈ N_0(F)` and
  `ω ∈ Ω_{N_0}`, and every `m ∈ M_0(𝔸)^1` is `r ω` with `r ∈ M_0(F)` and `ω ∈ Ω_{M_0^1}`.

Comparison with the forms of the source (not formalized here). `M_0(C_T^G) = M_0(𝔸)^1 exp(C_T^G)`
is right `M_0(𝔸)^1`-invariant. Under (7.1), every `n ∈ N_0(𝔸)` is `r ω` with `r ∈ N_0(F)`, so
`G(F) N_0(𝔸) M_0(C_T^G) K = G(F) Ω_{N_0} M_0(C_T^G) K`, Delorme's second description. Also
`M_0(𝔸)^1 = M_0(F) Ω_{M_0^1}` and `M_0(F)` normalizes `N_0(𝔸)`, so the region is also
`G(F) Ω_{N_0} Ω_{M_0^1} exp(C_T^G) K`, the image of the ordered factor product
`AdelicSiegelFactors.productSet` over the chamber `{a : H_0(a) ∈ C_T^G}`. Without (7.1) the set
`characteristicRegion` can be smaller than `G(F) N_0(𝔸) M_0(C_T^G) K`; the definition is the one
displayed by Delorme with his `Ω_{N_0}`.

Relation with `ArthurSiegelSets`. The height `H_0`, the weights `ϖ_α` and the inequality
`ϖ_α(H_0(x) − T) ≤ 0` are those of `ArthurTruncation.truncatedSiegelSet` for `P = G`, and `C` is the
same compact data. The chamber differs: `ArthurSiegelSets.siegelChamber` is the open chamber
`β(H_0(a) − T_1) > 0`, while `C_T^G` uses the closed chamber `α(X − T_0) ≥ 0` printed by Delorme,
and `𝒞_T^G` uses all of `M_0(𝔸)^1` and the norm-one condition. `𝒞_T^G` is a region; it is not
Arthur's alternating truncation operator `ArthurTruncation.truncation`.

## The truncated pairing `Ω^T_{P_0}`

The family-level definitions take a compatible family `𝓕 : StandardParabolicFamily F G`
(`F : Type`), a compact subgroup `K` with `hK : IsIwasawaCompact (𝓕.p 𝓕.bot) K`, as in
`WavePacketWeakConstantTermOrthogonalityStatement`.

- `isIwasawaCompact_standardBot` transfers `hK` to the canonical representative
  `Standard.parabolicHom 𝓕.i 𝓕.ν ∅`, whose all-test image is that of `𝓕.p 𝓕.bot` by
  compatibility; `bijective_restrictToTorus_bot` is the published
  `ArthurTruncation.bijective_restrictToTorus_minimal`. `minimalHeight 𝓕 hK` is `H_0`.
- `truncationRegion 𝓕 hK C T₀ Tp` is `characteristicRegion` for `𝓕.T`, `𝓕.i`, `𝓕.ν`, `𝓕.coroots`
  and `K`.
- `centralPoint 𝓕 z` is the image in `G(𝔸)` of `z ∈ 𝔄_G = 𝓕.positiveSplitComponent 𝓕.top`, the
  positive real points `A_G^∞` of the split centre. It is Delorme's `exp Z` with `Z = H_G(z)`;
  `H_G` identifies `𝔄_G` with `𝔞_G` (`standardPositiveLog`), so letting `z` range over `𝔄_G` is
  letting `Z` range over `𝔞_G`. `centralTranslate 𝓕 z f` is `f^Z = f(· exp Z)`.
- `truncatedPairingIntegrand 𝓕 z E Φ y = E(ẏ exp Z) · conj Φ(ẏ exp Z)` where
  `ẏ = Quotient.out y ∈ G(𝔸)^1` is the chosen representative of `y ∈ G(F) \ G(𝔸)^1`. This is the
  representative convention of the module: functions on `G(𝔸)` are evaluated at the chosen
  representative. For left `G(F)`-invariant `E` and `Φ`, such as an Eisenstein series and an
  automorphic form, the value does not depend on the choice.
- `truncatedPairing 𝓕 hK C T₀ Tp z E Φ = ∫_{y ∈ 𝒞_T^G} truncatedPairingIntegrand 𝓕 z E Φ y` for
  the fixed `normOneRationalQuotientHaarMeasure F G` (Bochner integral of the restricted measure).
  This is `Ω^T_{P_0}(E, Φ)^Z` of Theorem 7.1 for `E = E(·, λ)`. The functions are inputs; the
  definition makes no convergence claim. `𝒞_T^G` is compact and Delorme's functions are
  continuous, so the integral converges absolutely in his setting.

## Regularized integrals over cones

Delorme defines `∫*` (p.24) as the value at `λ ∈ i𝔞_Q^*` of the meromorphic continuation of an
integral that converges for `Re μ` strictly `Q`-dominant. In the coroot coordinates of the next
section the cone `𝔞^G_{Q,−}(T) − T_Q` is the negative orthant `{c ≤ 0}` and the integrals are
`∫_{c ≤ 0} e^{∑_α z_α c_α} P(c) dc` with `z_α = μ(α̌)`; strict `Q`-dominance of `Re μ` is
`Re z_α > 0`.

- `orthantFactor n s = (−1)^n n! / s^{n+1}`.
- `negativeOrthantRegularizedIntegral z P = ∑_{k ∈ supp P} P_k ∏_α orthantFactor (k_α) (z_α)` for
  `P = ∑_k P_k c^k ∈ ℂ[c_α]`. It is a rational function of `z`, holomorphic where every `z_α ≠ 0`.
- `negativeOrthantRegularizedIntegral_eq_integral`: for `Re z_α > 0` it equals
  `∫_{c ∈ (−∞,0]^σ} e^{∑_α z_α c_α} P(c) dc` (Lebesgue measure). Since the closed form is
  holomorphic off the hyperplanes `z_α = 0` and agrees with the integral on the convergence
  region, it is the meromorphic continuation, and its value at any `z` with all `z_α ≠ 0`
  (in particular at unitary `λ` with `θ_Q(λ) ≠ 0`) is Delorme's `∫*` in these coordinates.
- `negativeOrthantRegularizedIntegral_one`: `∫* e^{⟨z, c⟩} dc = (∏_α z_α)^{−1}`, which is (7.8)
  in coordinates: with `dX = C_Q dc` (below) and `θ_Q(λ) = ∏_α λ(α̌)`, it reads
  `∫*_{𝔞^G_{Q,−}(0)} e^{λ(X)} dX = C_Q θ_Q^{−1}(λ)`.
- `positiveFourierTransform f w = ∫ f(x) e^{∑_α w_α x_α} dx` is (7.10) in coordinates: for
  `X = ∑ x_α α̌` and `w_α = λ(α̌)`, `⟨λ, X⟩ = ∑ w_α x_α`, and Delorme's `ℱφ(λ)` is `C_Q` times
  `positiveFourierTransform (φ ∘ coordinates) w`. The kernel has the positive sign `e^{+⟨λ, X⟩}`.
  `negativeOrthantRegularizedIntegral_eq_positiveFourierTransform` restates the previous lemma as
  `∫* = ℱ_+(1_{c ≤ 0} · P)` on the convergence region.
- `coordinatePartial α h w` is the complex partial derivative of `h` in `w_α`;
  `monomialPartial k` composes them (`∂^k`); `polynomialSymbolOperator P h = ∑_k P_k ∂^k h` is
  `P(∂)`. On the imaginary axis `w = iξ` and for `h` holomorphic, `∂/∂w_α = (1/i) ∂/∂ξ_α`, the
  operator `D_α` of the positive convention.
- `hasDerivAt_negativeOrthantRegularizedIntegral` (and its `deriv` form
  `coordinatePartial_negativeOrthantRegularizedIntegral`): where `z_α ≠ 0`,
  `∂/∂z_α ∫* e^{⟨z,c⟩} P(c) dc = ∫* e^{⟨z,c⟩} c_α P(c) dc`. On the convergence region this is
  `ℱ_+(x_α f) = ∂_α ℱ_+(f)` for `f = 1_{c ≤ 0} P`, that is (7.11) for the coordinate symbol
  `x_α`, with the positive sign: the operator of the symbol `x_α` is `+∂_α = D_α`, not `−D_α`.
  Iterating it (not formalized as a separate statement) gives
  `negativeOrthantRegularizedIntegral z P = [P(∂) ∏_α w_α^{−1}](z)`, the form of (7.12).
- `polynomialShift t P` is `c ↦ P(t + c)`.

## Coroot coordinates on `𝔞_Q^G`

Standard parabolics are indexed by `Q : StandardIndex F G 𝓕.i 𝓕.ν`, with `Q.1 = Δ_0^Q`.

- `relativeSimpleRoots 𝓕 Q` is `Δ_0 ∖ Δ_0^Q`, which restricts bijectively to `Δ_Q`.
- `splitDual 𝓕 Q : V T → 𝔞_Q = RealCharacterDual F (𝓕.P Q)` sends `H` to `χ ↦ ⟨χ|_T, H⟩`; it is
  the transpose of restriction `𝓕.chamberEmbedding Q : X^*(P_Q) ⊗ ℝ → X^*(T) ⊗ ℝ`. For
  `x ∈ A_0^∞` it sends `H_0(x)` to `H_Q(x)`. Its kernel is `𝔞_0^Q`, so it is the projection
  `𝔞_0 → 𝔞_Q` along `𝔞_0^Q`, which is the orthogonal projection for a `W`-invariant scalar
  product.
- `relativeCoroot 𝓕 Q α = α̌_Q`, the projection to `𝔞_Q` of the coroot `α̌ = 𝓕.coroots.realCoroot α`
  of `α ∈ Δ_0 ∖ Δ_0^Q`: the simple coroot of `Δ̌_Q`. The `α̌_Q` form a basis of `𝔞_Q^G` and
  generate `L_Q`; the coordinates of `X ∈ 𝔞_Q^G` in this basis are the weights `ϖ_α(X)`,
  `α ∈ Δ_0 ∖ Δ_0^Q`, which restrict to the basis `Δ̂_Q` dual to `Δ̌_Q`. With these coordinates the
  Lebesgue measure `dX` of (2.2) is `C_Q dc`.
- `corootValue 𝓕 Q λ α = ⟨λ, α̌_Q⟩` for `λ ∈ 𝔞_{Q,ℂ}^* = ComplexCharacterParameter F (𝓕.P Q)`,
  so `θ_Q(λ) = ∏_α corootValue 𝓕 Q λ α`.
- `weightCoordinate 𝓕 Q T α = ϖ_α(T)` with the weight `ϖ_α ∈ Δ̂_0`. For `T ∈ 𝔞_0^G`,
  `T_Q = ∑_{α} ϖ_α(T) α̌_Q`, since `T − T_Q ∈ 𝔞_0^Q` and `ϖ_α` vanishes there.

In these coordinates `𝔞^G_{Q,−}(T) = {X ∈ 𝔞_Q^G | ϖ_α(X − T) ≤ 0, α ∈ Δ_Q}` is
`{∑_α (ϖ_α(T) + c_α) α̌_Q | c ≤ 0}`: the negative coroot cone of `Q` translated by `T_Q`.
The facts of the root datum used in this section (the kernel of `splitDual`, the basis `Δ̌_Q` of
`𝔞_Q^G` and its dual basis, the formula for `T_Q`) are standard and are not formalized in this
module; the definitions use them only to identify Delorme's objects.

## The data, their relation to the pairings, and `ω^T_{P_0}`

- `UnitaryPairingExpansion 𝓕 Q`: a finite set `exponents` of `λ ∈ 𝔞_{Q,ℂ}^*` with
  `Re λ = parameterRealPart λ = 0` (unitary exponents), and for each a polynomial
  `coefficient λ ∈ ℂ[c_α : α ∈ Δ_0 ∖ Δ_0^Q]` in the coroot coordinates. `eval e c` is the value
  `∑_λ e^{∑_α c_α ⟨λ, α̌_Q⟩} P_λ(c)` at `X = ∑_α c_α α̌_Q`, an exponential polynomial on
  `𝔞_Q^G` with unitary exponents. `IsRegular e` is the nonvanishing test for the supplied finite
  list: every coroot value `⟨λ, α̌_Q⟩` of every exponent listed in `e` is nonzero, so
  `θ_Q(λ) ≠ 0` there and the closed formula of the regularized integral divides by no zero. It
  sees only the exponents of this presentation of one slice (see the parameter region below).
- `normalizedPairing 𝓕 K hKc Q x f g = (x^{ρ_Q})^{−2} ∫ squareIntegrablePairingIntegrand` over
  `K × M_Q(F) \ M_Q(𝔸)^1` with `squareIntegrablePairingMeasure` (Haar probability on `K` times
  `normOneRationalQuotientHaarMeasure F (𝓕.M Q)`), at the chosen representatives of
  `M_Q(F) \ M_Q(𝔸)^1`. For `x = exp_Q X` it is Delorme's `(f, g)^X_Q` of (3.7):
  `𝓕.halfModulus Q x = x^{ρ_Q}`, so the prefactor is `e^{−2ρ_Q(X)}`. The published integrand and
  its representative convention are those of `AdelicTemperedVanishingReduction` (see its
  Markdown).
- `isIwasawaCompact_index 𝓕 hK Q` is `P_Q(𝔸) K = G(𝔸)`: the all-test image of `𝓕.p 𝓕.bot` lies
  in that of `𝓕.p Q` (`le_iff_range_le`). `isClosedImmersion_p 𝓕 Q`, from the compatibility of
  the family, is a local instance. With them, `(isIwasawaCompact_index 𝓕 hK Q).parameterTwist φ λ`
  is `φ_λ(g) = φ(g) e^{λ(H_Q(g))}` for the Iwasawa height `H_Q` of `K`, Delorme's `φ_λ` (p.9).

Delorme's extended pairing. In (3.7) the first argument of `p_Q(φ, ψ)` lies in `A^2_Q(G)`, and
the integral converges absolutely for `ψ ∈ A^temp_Q(G)`. For `φ = φ_1 + φ_2` with
`φ_1 ∈ A^{temp,c}_Q(G)` and `φ_2 ∈ A^2_Q(G)` Delorme sets `p_Q(φ, ψ) = p_Q(φ_2, ψ)` (p.15), and
(7.9) applies this to `φ_λ` for `φ ∈ A^2_Q(G) ⊕ A^{temp,c}_Q(G)`, extended linearly. The component
in `A^{temp,c}_Q(G)` contributes `0`, whether or not its product with `ψ` is integrable. For
instance, at `Q = G` an Eisenstein series induced from a proper parabolic lies in `A^{temp,c}(G)`
by (4.6) and contributes `0`, although its product with another such series is in general not
integrable over `[G]^1`.

- `IsSquareIntegrableTwistDecomposition 𝓕 rho hsmooth hcalc hKQ f s φ`: every `φ λ`, `λ ∈ s`,
  satisfies `IsSquareIntegrableInducedForm` for `P_Q = M_Q U_Q`, `K` and `rho` (the Library's
  `A^2_Q`, with Lapid's normalization `φ(a g) = a^{ρ_Q} φ(g)` for `a ∈ A_{M_Q}`), and the remainder
  `f − ∑_{λ ∈ s} (φ λ)_λ` satisfies `IsSquareIntegrableOrthogonal`, the condition defining
  `A^{temp,c}_Q(G)`: its pairing with every element of `A^2_Q` at every `x ∈ 𝔄_Q` converges
  absolutely and vanishes. Delorme's temperedness requirement on `A^{temp,c}_Q(G)` is not
  imposed. The orthogonality condition is preserved by sums and by twists `ψ ↦ ψ_λ`, since the
  twist is the constant `e^{λ(H_Q(x))}` on `x M_Q(𝔸)^1 K`. So one remainder collects all the
  twisted `A^{temp,c}_Q` components of Delorme's linear combinations.
- `IsNormalizedPairingExpansion 𝓕 K hKc Q s u g e`: for every `x ∈ 𝔄_Q` and `c` with
  `H_Q(x) = ∑_α c_α α̌_Q`, the pairing integrand of every `u i`, `i ∈ s`, against `g` at `x` is
  integrable, and `∑_{i ∈ s} normalizedPairing 𝓕 K hKc Q x (u i) g = eval e c`. As in the source,
  integrability is required for each piece entering (3.7), and nothing is required of the
  remainder. The relation therefore never holds through the value `0` of a divergent integral. By
  `standardPositiveLog`, these `x` are exactly `exp_Q X` for `X ∈ 𝔞_Q^G`.
  `isNormalizedPairingExpansion_empty`: with no pieces the relation holds for the zero expansion
  `UnitaryPairingExpansion.zero`.
- `IsWeakConstantTermPairingExpansion 𝓕 hsmooth hcalc rho hK z E Φ e`: for every standard `Q`
  there are `E_Q`, `Φ_Q`, `s` and `φ` with `IsWeakConstantTerm … Q E E_Q` and
  `IsWeakConstantTerm … Q Φ Φ_Q` (Delorme's Definition 3.7, for `K_∞ = K ∩ G(𝔸_∞)` as in
  `WavePacketWeakConstantTermOrthogonalityStatement`), a decomposition
  `IsSquareIntegrableTwistDecomposition … (isIwasawaCompact_index 𝓕 hK Q) E_Q s φ`, and
  `IsNormalizedPairingExpansion … Q s (λ ↦ ((φ λ)_λ)^Z) (Φ_Q)^Z (e Q)`. When `E = E(·, λ)`
  this says that `e Q` is the expansion on `𝔞_Q^G` of `X ↦ p_Q(E(λ)^w_Q, Φ^w_Q)(Z + X)` for
  Delorme's extended pairing. The central translation is applied to the pieces after the
  decomposition; it multiplies `(φ λ)_λ` by `e^{λ(Z)}`, since `exp Z` lies in `A_{M_Q}` and `ρ_Q`
  vanishes on `𝔞_G`.
- `UnitaryPairingExpansion.regularizedConeIntegral e T =
  ∑_λ e^{∑_α ϖ_α(T) ⟨λ, α̌_Q⟩} · negativeOrthantRegularizedIntegral (⟨λ, α̌_Q⟩)_α (P_λ(ϖ(T) + ·))`.
- `regularizedPairing 𝓕 vol e T = ∑ᶠ_Q vol Q · (e Q).regularizedConeIntegral T`, a finite sum
  over the standard indices.

What the relation determines (not formalized). Delorme states that the sum
`A^{temp,c}_Q(G) ⊕ A^2_Q(G)` is direct, so that his `p_Q(φ, ψ)` depends only on `φ`. This module
does not use the directness: the relation predicates quantify existentially over the weak
constant terms and over the decomposition. Given the pieces, the relation determines the function
`c ↦ eval e c` on `ℝ^{Δ_Q}`. Write
`ζ(λ) = (⟨λ, α̌_Q⟩)_α ∈ ℂ^{Δ_Q}` for the coroot values of an exponent. The functions
`c ↦ e^{⟨ζ, c⟩} P(c)` with distinct `ζ` and nonzero polynomials `P` are linearly independent, so
the function determines, for each `ζ`, the sum of the `P_λ` over the exponents `λ` with
`ζ(λ) = ζ`. It does not determine the exponents themselves, since `eval` sees only `ζ(λ)`. For
example, for `Q = G` the set `Δ_Q` is empty, and two expansions agree as functions exactly when
their total constant polynomials agree. `regularizedConeIntegral` depends on `e` only through
these sums, since each summand depends on `λ` only through `ζ(λ)` and is additive in `P_λ`. So,
for given pieces, `regularizedPairing` does not depend on the choice of data satisfying the
relation.

Correspondence with (7.9)–(7.12). By (4.3),
`E(λ)^w_Q = ∑_{s ∈ W(P,Q)} E^Q_{Q_s}(·, M(s, λ)φ, sλ)`. For `s ∈ W(P|Q)` (the associated terms)
the summand is `(M(s, λ)φ)_{sλ}`, the twist by `sλ ∈ i𝔞_Q^*` of an element of `A^2_Q(G)`: these
are the pieces `(φ λ)_λ`. For the other `s` the summand is an Eisenstein series on `M_Q` induced
from a proper standard parabolic of `M_Q`, twisted by the `𝔞_Q`-component of `sλ`; by (4.6) for
`M_Q` it satisfies the orthogonality condition and lies in the remainder. This is Delorme's remark
before Proposition 7.8 that only the terms with `s ∈ W(Q|P)` contribute. Let
`p(X) = p_Q(E(λ)^w_Q, Φ^w_Q)(X) = ∑_{s ∈ W(P|Q)} e^{sλ(X)} p_Q(M(s, λ)φ, Φ^w_Q)(X)` on `𝔞_Q`, the
extended pairing, which is the linear extension `r^T_Q(ψ, Ψ)` for `ψ = E(λ)^w_Q`, since
`p_Q(φ_λ, Ψ) = e^{λ} p_Q(φ, Ψ)`. As `exp Z` is central and `ρ_Q` vanishes on `𝔞_G`, the sum of
the pairings of the translated pieces at `exp_Q Y`, `Y ∈ 𝔞_Q^G`, is `p(Z + Y)`. This is the
restriction of `p` to `Z + 𝔞_Q^G` used in (7.9). With `Y = ∑_α (ϖ_α(T) + c_α) α̌_Q`, `c ≤ 0`,
and `dY = C_Q dc`,

`r^T_Q = ∫*_{Z + 𝔞^G_{Q,−}(T)} p(X) dX = C_Q ∑_λ e^{⟨λ, T_Q⟩} ∫*_{c ≤ 0} e^{∑ c_α ⟨λ, α̌_Q⟩}
P_λ(ϖ(T) + c) dc = C_Q · (e Q).regularizedConeIntegral T`,

so `ω^T_{P_0} = regularizedPairing 𝓕 vol e T` with `vol Q = C_Q`. By the derivative lemma and
`e^{−⟨t,w⟩} ∂_α e^{⟨t,w⟩} = ∂_α + t_α`, each term is
`C_Q [P_λ(∂)(e^{⟨·, T_Q⟩} θ_Q^{−1})](λ)`, the form of (7.12). In Delorme's notation the
exponents are `λ' = sλ − μ` and `P_λ'` is the coroot-coordinate form of
`e^{λ'(Z)} p_Q(M(s,λ)φ, Ψ_{0,μ})^Z`. The factor `e^{(λ−μ)(Z)}` comes from (7.9) through
`e^{λ(X)}` at `X ∈ Z + 𝔞^G_{Q,−}(T)`; the published display (7.12) omits it (the arXiv
version carries the factor `e^{(λ−μ)(T_Q+Z)}` outside the derivative). The definitions follow
(7.9).

The literal formula on p.24, `∫_{𝔞^G_{Q,−}(T)} e^{μ(X+Z)} p(X) dX`, evaluates `p` at `X` rather
than `X + Z`. The notation `∫*_{Z + 𝔞^G_{Q,−}(T)}`, the polynomial `p^Z(X) = p(X + Z)` of (7.12)
and the identity `Ω^T(E(λ), Φ)^Z = e^{(λ−ν_G)(Z)} Ω^T(E(λ), Φ)^0` for a form `Φ` with central
character `ν_G` require the integrand `e^{μ(X+Z)} p(X + Z)`, the integral of `e^{λ} p` over the
translated set `Z + 𝔞^G_{Q,−}(T)`, which is what the definitions use.

## The parameter region

Delorme's `ℋ^c` (Theorem 7.1 (i)) is the set of `λ ∈ i𝔞_P^*` at which the summands
`r^T_Q(E(λ)^w_Q, Φ^w_Q)^Z` of `ω^T_{P_0}` are analytic in `λ`, for all `Z`. Theorem 7.1 (i) says
that, by (7.12), it contains the complement of a finite union of hyperplanes, and that
`ω^T_{P_0}` on `ℋ^c` extends to an analytic function on `i𝔞_P^*`.

`IsRegular` of the data at one central translate does not describe `ℋ^c`. The relation at one `z`
admits presentations without the exponents whose coefficients vanish on that slice, and such a
coefficient can be nonzero at another translate. For instance, take split `GL_3` over `ℚ`, the
standard parabolic `P` of type `(2, 1)`, a nonzero `φ ∈ A^2_P(G)`, `λ_0 ∈ i𝔞_P^*` and
`Φ = log|det|_𝔸 · E_P(·, φ, λ_0)`, tempered in the sense of Definition 3.3 (the logarithmic
factor is bounded by a power of `σ`). At `Z = 0` the factor `log|det|_𝔸` vanishes on every slice
`exp_Q(X) M_Q(𝔸)^1 K`, `X ∈ 𝔞_Q^G`, so the zero data satisfy the relation for every `Q` and pass
`IsRegular`. For `Z` with `⟨det, Z⟩ ≠ 0` the `Q = P` summand is `C_P ⟨det, Z⟩ ‖φ‖²
e^{(λ−λ_0)(T_P+Z)} / (λ − λ_0)(α̌_P)` (here `W(P, P)` is trivial), which has a pole at `λ_0`.

The sufficient condition of Theorem 7.1 (i) uses the fixed labels of (7.12) instead. For every `Z`
the exponents of the `Q`-term are among the `sλ − μ` with `s ∈ W(P|Q)` and `μ ∈ ℰ_Q(Φ^w_Q)`: the
central translation multiplies the pieces `(M(s, λ)φ)_{sλ}` by `e^{sλ(Z)}` and replaces the
polynomial parts of the components of `Φ^w_Q` by translates, without changing the exponents.

- `AffineExponentLabels 𝓕 P Q`: finitely many affine maps `λ ↦ A_k λ + b_k` from
  `𝔞_{P,ℂ}^* = ComplexCharacterParameter F (𝓕.P P)` to `𝔞_{Q,ℂ}^*`, with `A_k` complex linear;
  `label L k λ = A_k λ + b_k`. Delorme's labels are `A_k = s` and `b_k = −μ`. They are supplied as
  data, like the expansions.
- `AffineExponentLabels.corootValue_label`: `⟨A_k λ + b_k, α̌_Q⟩ = ⟨A_k λ, α̌_Q⟩ + ⟨b_k, α̌_Q⟩`, an
  affine function of `λ`.
- `UnitaryPairingExpansion.IsLabelledBy e L λ`: every exponent of `e` is a label `A_k λ + b_k`.
- `genericParameterRegion 𝓕 P L`: the `λ` with `⟨A_k λ + b_k, α̌_Q⟩ ≠ 0` for every standard `Q`,
  every label `k` of `L Q` and every `α ∈ Δ_Q`, that is `θ_Q(A_k λ + b_k) ≠ 0`. Every label is
  tested, including labels whose coefficients vanish on a particular slice. For Delorme's labels it
  is the region `θ_Q(sλ − μ) ≠ 0` for all `Q`, `s ∈ W(P|Q)` and `μ ∈ ℰ_Q(Φ^w_Q)`. For
  `s ∈ W(P|Q)` the map `s : 𝔞_P → 𝔞_Q` is bijective, so each `λ ↦ (sλ − μ)(α̌_Q)` is a nonconstant
  affine function, and the region is the complement of finitely many affine hyperplanes. On it, by
  (7.12) and the analyticity of `M(s, λ)` on `i𝔞_P^*`, the summands are analytic for all `Z`, so its
  intersection with `i𝔞_P^*` lies in `ℋ^c`. That inclusion is part of Theorem 7.1 (i) and is not
  formalized here.
- `isRegular_of_mem_genericParameterRegion`: data labelled by `L Q` at a `λ` of the generic region
  pass `IsRegular`. So for data labelled by the fixed labels at every central translate, the closed
  formula computes Delorme's `∫*` at every `z`.

For data failing `IsRegular` the closed formula divides by zero (Lean's `x / 0 = 0`) and its value
has no meaning. At a point of `ℋ^c` outside the generic region Delorme's summands are their
limits in `λ`, and on the rest of `i𝔞_P^*` the value of `ω^T_{P_0}` is the analytic extension of
Theorem 7.1 (i). These values are the content of Theorem 7.1 (i); they are not defined in this
module.

## Normalization of the measures

Delorme normalizes the measures as in Mœglin–Waldspurger I.1.13 (p.8), with the Lebesgue
measures of (2.2) on `𝔞_Q^G`. (7.7) is the integration formula on `𝒞_T^Q` for these
normalizations, and (7.8) produces the constant `C_Q` from `dX`. The Library fixes the Haar
measures of `G(F) \ G(𝔸)^1` and of each `M_Q(F) \ M_Q(𝔸)^1` independently
(`normOneRationalQuotientHaarMeasure`, built from `Measure.haar`). `Ω^T_{P_0}` scales with the
first, while the pairing (3.7) of the `Q`-term scales with the second (already for `Q = G`, where
`C_G = 1` and the pairing uses the quotient of `𝓕.M 𝓕.top`, a group scheme different from `G`).
So no constants fixed independently of these measures can serve for every normalization. With the
Library's measures the integration formula holds in coroot coordinates with a positive constant
`c_Q` in place of `C_Q`, determined by the Haar measures, and Delorme's argument gives the
asymptotic identity with `vol Q = c_Q`. The constants are therefore an input `vol` of
`regularizedPairing`; Delorme's `ω^T_{P_0}` is its value at the constants for which the
integration formula (7.7) holds, which are `C_Q` when the measures are normalized compatibly as
in the source. Rescaling the Haar measure of `[G]^1` rescales `Ω^T_{P_0}` and all `c_Q` by the
same factor.

## The compact subgroup

Delorme takes `K` in good position with respect to `M_0`. The definitions only use
`P_0(𝔸) K = G(𝔸)` (through `IsIwasawaCompact`, which defines `H_0`, and through `K` in the product
and in the pairings), so they make sense for every compact `K` with this property, as in the
target `WavePacketWeakConstantTermOrthogonalityStatement`. The good position enters Delorme's
proof of Theorem 7.1 (the integration formulas and Arthur's partition (7.6)), not the definitions;
whether Theorem 7.1 holds for such `K` outside good position is a question about that theorem.

- `norm_pow_mul_cexp`: `‖x^k e^{ax}‖ = |x|^k e^{Re(a) x}` by `Complex.norm_exp` and

  (`IntegrableOn.comp_neg_Iic`) of `u^k e^{−Re(a) u}` on `[0, ∞)`, integrable by
