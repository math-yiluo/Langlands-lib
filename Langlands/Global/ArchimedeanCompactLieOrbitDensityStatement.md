# Density of compact translates of the enveloping orbit

This statement-only pair, formulated by Worker 94, defines
`Langlands.Global.ArchimedeanGL.CompactLieOrbitDensityStatement K n`. It is a
proposition, not a theorem. The pair proves nothing about it.

## Statement

Fix a universe `u`, a number field `K : Type u` and a natural number `n`.
Rank zero is allowed. Write `G` for the actual archimedean group
`Matrix.GeneralLinearGroup (Fin n) (NumberField.InfiniteAdeleRing K)`.
The proposition says the following.

Let `f : DerivativeSmoothFunctions K n`, and suppose
`IsJointFinite K n (DerivativeSmoothFunctions.toSmooth K n f)`. Then for every
`g : G`,

```
R(g) f  ∈  closure ( span_ℂ { R(k) D(a) f  :  k ∈ standardInfiniteCompact K n,
                                              a ∈ EnvelopingAlgebra K n } ).
```

Here `R` is `DerivativeSmoothFunctions.rightAction K n`, and `D(a)` is
`DerivativeSmoothFunctions.operator K n a`. The span is `Submodule.span ℂ` of the
range of the map `(k, a) ↦ R(k.val) (D(a) f)` on the product type
`standardInfiniteCompact K n × EnvelopingAlgebra K n`. The closure is
`Submodule.topologicalClosure` in the topology of `DerivativeSmoothFunctions K n`.
The order matters: the enveloping derivative `D(a)` is applied first, and the
compact translation `R(k)` second.

## The published ingredients

All carriers and operators are the published concrete ones. The statement does
not introduce any new structure.

- `DerivativeSmoothFunctions K n` is a type synonym for the published smooth
  functions `SmoothFunctions K n` on `G`. Its topology is induced by
  `derivativeCoordinates`, which sends `f` to the family `a ↦ D(a) f`. Each
  `D(a) f` is a continuous function on `G` with the compact-open topology. So a
  net converges exactly when every enveloping derivative converges uniformly on
  compact sets. The space is Hausdorff and locally convex over `ℝ`. The
  closure in the statement uses this full derivative topology. It does not use
  the topology of `C(G, ℂ)` alone.
- `DerivativeSmoothFunctions.toSmooth K n` is the identity linear equivalence
  back to `SmoothFunctions K n`. It only forgets the topology.
- `rightAction K n g` is the published right regular representation
  `smoothRightAction`, transported along `toSmooth`. Pointwise,
  `(R(g) f)(x) = f(x g)` (`toSmooth_rightAction_val`).
- `EnvelopingAlgebra K n` is `UniversalEnvelopingAlgebra ℂ (ComplexLieAlgebra K n)`.
  This is the complex universal enveloping algebra of the complexified real
  Lie algebra of `G`. `operator K n a` is the published algebra map
  `envelopingAction K n a`, transported along `toSmooth`. A real Lie algebra
  element `X` acts by differentiation along right multiplication,
  `f ↦ (x ↦ d/dt f(x exp(tX)) at t = 0)`. In Lean this is the `fderiv` of the
  coordinate extension of `f` at the coordinates of `x`, in the direction
  `x X` (`envelopingAction_real_generator_apply`).
- `standardInfiniteCompact K n` is the image of
  `ArchimedeanCompactFactors.toInfiniteAdelicGL`. That is, it is the product of
  the orthogonal groups `O(n)` at the real places and the unitary groups `U(n)`
  at the complex places, placed in `G` through completion coordinates.
- `IsJointFinite K n f` means `Module.Finite ℂ (jointOrbitSpan K n f)`. The
  joint orbit span is the complex span of all `envelopingAction (z * b) f`, as
  `z` ranges over the centre `EnvelopingCenter K n` of the enveloping algebra
  and `b` ranges over `compactEnvelopingSubalgebra K n`. The latter is the
  subalgebra generated by the complexified compact Lie algebra. This is a
  finiteness condition on the orbit of this one function. It is not a
  condition on the ambient space.

## Scope

The proposition is quantified over every universe, every number field and
every rank `n`, including `n = 0`. Its only hypothesis is the joint finiteness
of `f`. It does not assume any of the following:

- analyticity of `f`;
- that `standardInfiniteCompact K n` meets every connected component of `G`;
- finite dimensionality of compact-type spaces, or admissibility;
- a reproducing kernel or approximate identity;
- growth bounds;
- positive rank.

It also asserts none of the following:

- density of the enveloping orbit `{D(a) f}` alone;
- finite dimensionality of the displayed span;
- an identification of the closure with the closed `G`-stable subspace
  generated by `f`.

It states only that each single translate `R(g) f` lies in the closure.

## Why compact translates are included

The following remark is informal. It is not formalized in this pair.

`G` is not connected when `K` has a real place and `n ≥ 1`. The real factor
`GL_n(ℝ)` has two components, distinguished by the sign of the determinant.
An enveloping orbit cannot see the other components. As an example, take
`K = ℚ` and `n ≥ 1`, so `G` is `GL_n(ℝ)`. Let
`f = 1 + sign(det)`, which equals `2` on `det > 0` and `0` on `det < 0`.

- `f` is locally constant, so it is smooth, and every real Lie algebra element
  annihilates it.
- Therefore `D(a) f = ε(a) f`, where `ε` is the augmentation of the enveloping
  algebra. The joint orbit span is `ℂ f`, so `f` is joint finite.
- The span of all `D(a) f` is the line `ℂ f`. This line is closed, because it
  is finite dimensional in a Hausdorff space.
- Now take `g` with `det g < 0`. Then `R(g) f = 1 - sign(det)`, which is not a
  multiple of `f`.

So the version without compact translates fails for disconnected real general
linear groups. Translating by `k = diag(-1, 1, …, 1) ∈ O(n)` recovers this
function. For this reason the statement uses `R(k) D(a) f` rather than
`D(a) f`.

## Provenance

This is an internal derived prerequisite. It belongs to the automorphic
differential-growth route recorded in the public note
`notes/automorphic/differential-growth-source-map.md`. The parent task is
`e0d3fab3-d5c8-47ae-bd65-d85037d6fd80`. The proposition is formulated directly
on the published concrete carriers. It is not a transcription of a printed
theorem.

Its classical model is the first step of the proof of Theorem 1 in
Harish-Chandra, *Discrete series for semisimple Lie groups II*, Acta Math. 116
(1966), §8, printed page 18. The registered source ID is
`harish-chandra-1966-discrete-series-ii`. In that source:

- `G` is a connected reductive group with a compact `K`.
- `f` is a smooth vector-valued function. Its orbit under the algebra
  generated by the centre and the complexified compact Lie algebra is finite
  dimensional.
- The proof shows that the smallest closed `G`-stable subspace containing `f`
  is the closure of the enveloping orbit of `f`. The argument uses a
  Hahn–Banach separator and analyticity.

The present formulation differs from the source in three ways:

- It uses the actual group `GL_n` over the archimedean completions of `K`.
  For `n ≥ 1` this group has non-compact centre, and it is disconnected when
  `K` has a real place.
- It adds standard compact translates to reach every component.
- It uses the derivative compact-open topology defined above.

## Remaining proof obligations

The intended proof route is listed below. None of these steps is a hypothesis
of the proposition, and this pair proves none of them.

1. **Separation.** If `R(g) f` were outside the closure, there would be a
   continuous complex linear functional `ℓ` on `DerivativeSmoothFunctions K n`.
   It would vanish on the displayed span, and `ℓ(R(g) f) ≠ 0`. This is a
   Hahn–Banach argument in this locally convex Hausdorff space.
2. **Smooth orbit coefficient.** `F(x) = ℓ(R(x) f)` is an actual smooth
   function, with `D(a) F(x) = ℓ(R(x) D(a) f)`. This is the published
   statement `SmoothOrbitCoefficientStatement`, which is not yet proved in
   the Library.
3. **Transfer of joint finiteness.** The coefficient map intertwines the
   enveloping actions. It therefore carries the joint orbit span of `f` onto
   that of `F`, so `F` is joint finite.
4. **Analyticity.** A joint-finite smooth function is real analytic. This is
   the published statement `JointFiniteAnalyticityStatement`, which is not
   yet proved in the Library.
5. **Jets and component uniqueness.** At each `k ∈ standardInfiniteCompact K n`,
   every `D(a) F(k) = ℓ(R(k) D(a) f)` vanishes, because `ℓ` vanishes on the
   displayed span. By the published theorem
   `envelopingZeroJet`, which proves `EnvelopingZeroJetStatement`, the whole
   ambient jet of `F` vanishes at `k`. By the published theorem
   `analyticMatrixJetUniqueness`, which proves
   `AnalyticMatrixJetUniquenessStatement`, `F` then vanishes on the connected
   component of `k`.
6. **Component coverage.** Every point of `G` lies in the connected component
   of some element of `standardInfiniteCompact K n`. This is the published
   statement `CompactComponentCoverageStatement`, which is not yet proved in
   the Library.

Together these give `F = 0`, and so `ℓ(R(g) f) = F(g) = 0`, which is a
contradiction.
