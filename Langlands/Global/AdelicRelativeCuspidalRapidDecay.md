# Relative cuspidal rapid decay with bounded complementary coordinates

`AdelicRelativeCuspidalRapidDecayStatement` uses a compatible standard parabolic family for a connected reductive group \(G\) over a number field \(F\). Fix a standard parabolic \(P\) with Levi simple-root set \(J\), a smooth relative dimension for \(G\), and a positive-rank matrix representation \(\rho\) with adelic height \(H\). Choose a split torus \(T_0\) mapping to the maximal split torus and a set of auxiliary characters of \(T_0\). The auxiliary characters together with the restricted simple roots must span the rational character space of \(T_0\).

Consider points \(x=p a_G k\), where \(p\) lies in a compact subset of the adelic minimal parabolic, \(a\) lies in the intrinsic positive torus and the weak simple-root chamber with a common positive threshold, and \(k\) lies in a compact set of right factors. Require \(x_f\) to lie in a prescribed compact finite slice. The absolute log norms of the auxiliary characters and the restricted simple roots outside \(J\) are at most a fixed bound.

Let \(f_q\) be a family of continuous complex functions, smooth on the archimedean slices, left invariant under \(P_0(F)\) and \(U_P(\mathbb A)\), right invariant under a fixed open finite level, and cuspidal along \(P\). Suppose every word \(D\) of right-orbit derivatives satisfies

\[
  |D f_q(x)| \le C_D H(x)^R s(q)
\]

on the ambient adelic group, with one exponent \(R\ge 0\), nonnegative \(s(q)\), and constants independent of \(q,x\). For every integer \(m\ge 0\), the theorem gives a constant \(C_m\) such that

\[
  |f_q(x)| H(x)^m \le C_m s(q)
\]

on the specified region.

The proof uses the largest of zero and the log norms for roots in \(J\). A maximum of zero gives a bounded height and uses the order-zero estimate. A maximizing root in \(J\) has a vanishing maximal-parabolic constant term by relative cuspidality. Its iterated estimate bounds the function using finitely many derivative words on enlarged compact right factors. The simple-root lower bounds and the bounded complementary log coordinates bound the torus height exponentially in the maximizing log norm. The derivative order is chosen to absorb this exponential growth and the requested power of height. The finite set of roots gives a uniform constant.

This proves the relative decay estimate used in the Hilbert–Schmidt argument of Bernstein–Lapid, §7.4, p. 217, on the specified regions with bounded complementary log coordinates.
