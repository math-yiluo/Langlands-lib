# Central covariance of the characteristic pairing and weak constant terms

For the logarithmically square-integrable tested form and the continued Eisenstein family, `truncationCentralCovariance` expresses the characteristic pairing at a central translate as the pairing at the identity times a scalar of absolute value one on the imaginary axis. It gives the same pointwise identity for the characteristic integrand. The scalar is holomorphic in the complex inducing parameter in every linear chart.

The top positive split subgroup maps into the canonical ambient split centre because the full Levi maps isomorphically onto the group. Its ambient image commutes with every adelic point. A central point lifts to the positive split component of each inducing parabolic, where its half modulus equals the full-group half modulus, which is one. Hence it acts trivially on the inducing form. Scalar meromorphic uniqueness continues the Eisenstein covariance from the regular chamber. The multiplying factor is the inducing parameter power at the lifted point, given by an exponential of a complex linear form. At imaginary parameters it has unit modulus. The prescribed unitary character of the tested form supplies the conjugate factor in the pairing. Both factors are constant along the quotient integration variable.

The file also proves that every normalized exponent component of a constant term inherits the scalar by which the original form transforms. Central translation commutes with taking the ordinary constant term. Applying the group-algebra element given by translation minus the scalar therefore gives zero on the sum of the components. This operator preserves each normalized exponent. Directness forces the result to be zero on every component, and summing over the unitary labels yields the covariance for the weak constant term.

This supplies the central translation calculation for the characteristic pairing and weak terms in Delorme, *On the spectral theorem of Langlands*, Advances in Mathematics 426 (2023), 109107, Lemma 3.8 and Theorem 7.1.
