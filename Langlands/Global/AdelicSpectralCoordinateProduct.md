# Concatenation of spectral coordinates and product coefficients

`spectralCoordinateProduct m n` is the linear coordinate concatenation from `ℝᵐ × ℝⁿ` to `ℝᵐ⁺ⁿ`, with the first block before the second. It is not a comparison for arbitrary linear coordinates. By the finite-product and reindexing measure identities it transports product Lebesgue measure to Lebesgue measure. The same map transports `(spectralMeasure m).prod (spectralMeasure n)` to `spectralMeasure (m+n)`: the two scaling factors multiply to the factor of dimension `m+n`. This includes the dimension-zero convention. The spectral measure is the existing `(2π)^{-n}` times Lebesgue measure in these coordinates, and is s-finite.

`spectralProductCoefficient c d` multiplies the two scalar coefficients after extracting their respective coordinate blocks. The coordinate maps are continuous linear, so the new coefficient has the same differentiability order if both factors do. Its support lies in the image of the product of the compact supports, which is compact and closed. The integral law is change of variables by the measurable equivalence followed by Mathlib's `integral_prod_mul` for the total Bochner integral. It is not a claim of integrability for arbitrary coefficients.

This is an internal construction for the product-coordinate use of wave-packet synthesis in Getz–Hahn (2024), §10.5, pp. 281–282 (PDF pp. 293–294), (10.22)–(10.24). The exact coordinate normalization `(2π)^{-n}` is the Library's `spectralMeasure`, dual to the exponential pairing in `AdelicInducedInnerProduct`, not an additional normalization theorem attributed to that source. The construction does not identify the coordinate-independent measures of two unrelated charts, and does not by itself establish wave-packet synthesis, orthogonality or completeness.
