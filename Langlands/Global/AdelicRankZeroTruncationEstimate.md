# The characteristic truncation estimate in relative rank zero

With no simple relative roots, there is a single standard parabolic, namely the full group. The compact and simultaneous Levi compacts are chosen from the local standard-Levi compacts. The maximal split torus is central because the minimal Levi is the whole group, which gives positive-torus compatibility. The characteristic inequalities are independent of the upper truncation parameter. Exhaustion therefore identifies each characteristic region with the whole norm-one rational quotient, and compactness of the characteristic region gives compactness of that quotient.

The continued series is the inducing form multiplied by an ambient character power. On a representative in the norm-one group this character is one. At a fixed central translate the spectral dependence of the full quotient integral is thus an exponential of a complex linear functional times a fixed integral. Continuity of the descended integrands and compactness of the quotient give integrability. The exponential gives the entire regularization.

Both functions equal their full-group weak constant terms at imaginary parameters. The inducing form supplies a singleton square-integrable twist decomposition with zero orthogonal remainder. The relevant full-group split logarithm is zero, so the two functions are invariant under the split shift in the normalized pairing. The full Levi isomorphism transports its norm-one quotient Haar measure to a positive scalar multiple of the ambient one. Right translation by each compact point preserves the ambient norm-one quotient Haar measure, and the compact probability Haar factor integrates to one.

The pairing expansion is a constant polynomial with exponent zero in zero relative coroot variables. The generic set is the whole imaginary parameter space. The zero-dimensional regularized cone integral evaluates this constant, and the reciprocal of the Haar scalar gives the characteristic pairing itself. Consequently the error in the relative-rank-zero case of Delorme, *On the spectral theorem of Langlands*, Advances in Mathematics 426 (2023), 109107, Theorem 7.1, is zero at every truncation parameter and central shift.
