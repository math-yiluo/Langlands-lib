# The closed stabilizer of the actual adelic intertwining integral

Let \(G\) be an affine group over a number field \(F\). Suppose the source radical embedding \(p\circ i_U:U\to G\) and target radical embedding \(j:V\to G\) are closed immersions, with \(V\) affine, unipotent, and locally of finite type over \(F\). The scheme-theoretic stabilizer \(H\) is the intersection of the target radical with the rational conjugate of the source radical. Its adelic points give the subgroup of \(V(\mathbb A_F)\) defined by the conjugation condition in the intertwining integral. Schematic dominance of the diagonal coefficient map detects membership of a rational point over \(F\) itself. Thus the lattice from the defining integral restricts to the intrinsic principal image of \(H(F)\) under the continuous group equivalence induced by inclusion.

The first projection of the intersection is a closed immersion. This makes the adelic stabilizer closed in the topology induced from \(G(\mathbb A_F)\). The stabilizer is unipotent, so characteristic zero gives smoothness and connectedness. The adelic compactness theorem gives compactness of the left quotient \(H(F)\backslash H(\mathbb A_F)\). This is the stabilizer in the integral of Bernstein–Lapid, §2.2.
