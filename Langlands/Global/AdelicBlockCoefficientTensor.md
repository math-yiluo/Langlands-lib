# The coefficient tensor of scalar adelic block sections

`indexedEvaluation` sends the pure tensor of \(v_i\) to the function on the product of the evaluation sets with value \(\prod_i e_i(v_i)(x_i)\). It is constructed from the product multilinear map by the universal property of the indexed tensor product. A coordinatewise pullback of these functions is compatible with the tensor of the corresponding coefficient maps. The equality follows by evaluating the two linear maps on pure tensors.

The evaluation is injective when all the coordinate evaluations are injective. The empty tensor is the scalar field and its evaluation at the empty tuple is the scalar itself. For a nonempty family, `FiniteTensor.headTailLinearEquiv` separates the head and tail tensors. The tail evaluation is injective by induction. The published binary `tensorEval_injective` therefore identifies the resulting tensor with a function of the tail arguments valued in the head coefficient space. The head evaluation is injective, so scalar evaluation at all product arguments distinguishes these functions.

For an actual family \(S_i\) of operator realizations, the coordinate maps send each \(S_i\)-vector to its scalar function on the actual adelic block. They are injective by the faithful continuous-function realization. `coefficientEvaluation` consequently embeds the tensor in functions on the adelic Levi. Pure tensors span the source, and their images are exactly the products defining `coefficientValues`. The range is that submodule, giving `coefficientEquiv`. The finite and infinite compact right translation identities follow from the coordinate pullback formula and the published evaluation identities for each \(S_i\).

This is an internal algebraic realization of the inducing block tensor. The role of this tensor in adelic induction is visible in Getz and Hahn (2024), §10.7, p. 288 (PDF 300), immediately before Theorem 10.7.5. The induced functions have Levi slices in the inducing representation in §10.1, p. 271 (PDF 283). The Lean equivalence concerns the coefficient space of those slices. Factorization of the induced sections into their place modules requires the additional comparison of induction itself.
