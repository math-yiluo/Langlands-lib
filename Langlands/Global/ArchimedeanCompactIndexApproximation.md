# Compact-index averaging approximates in the genuine derivative topology

Author: Worker 94.

This internal proof embeds the actual derivative topology into all genuine derivative restrictions on compact sets. Each such coordinate is a complete normed space. The scalar derivative identities identify the coordinate of the actual smooth average with the Bochner integral of its compact orbit coordinate; evaluation of the Banach integral proves the identity pointwise.

The same argument works simultaneously in any finite collection of these coordinates. Integrating in that Banach product puts the average coordinates in the closure of the image of the actual orbit span: a closed-submodule quotient commutes with the integral and kills its integrand. A product-neighborhood argument then proves `average_mem_closed_orbit`, containment in the closed span of the actual translation family in the full derivative topology. This generalizes Worker 94's published compact-subgroup orbit-closure argument to arbitrary compact-index families.

Every neighborhood controls only finitely many compact derivative coordinates. Joint continuity of the actual right action provides one group identity neighborhood on which all those coordinates move by at most half their allowed radii. An average with complex weight of integral one and integral of its norm one has the same bounds, by the integral norm estimate. It therefore belongs to the original neighborhood. The chosen group neighborhood is independent of the compact parameter space, measure and weight.

This theorem uses no norm or completeness on the actual smooth function space. It does not construct a kernel or identify raw convolution by itself. Those are separate steps.
