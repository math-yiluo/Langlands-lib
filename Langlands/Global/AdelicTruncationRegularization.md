# Regularizing the pairing across the imaginary hyperplanes

Delorme obtains the holomorphic continuation in Theorem 7.1(i) from a bound on the generic imaginary parameters for the difference between the characteristic and regularized pairings. `HasImaginaryHyperplanePresentation` expresses the local denominator condition. Near an imaginary point, the generic pairing times a finite product of centered linear forms extends holomorphically. The linear forms are real on real vectors and nonzero, and the product can repeat a factor. In (7.12) the denominators arise from `θ_Q` and its derivatives. At a given point, the factors with nonzero value can be absorbed into the numerator on a smaller neighbourhood.

`range_imaginarySlice_subset_extensionDomain_of_locally_bounded` proves the local continuation criterion. Multiplication by the denominator gives a norm bound for the holomorphic numerator on a dense set of imaginary points. Continuity extends that bound to the full local imaginary slice. `exists_differentiableOn_mul_eq_of_norm_le_on_imaginary` divides the numerator by the product. Cancellation on the dense complex nonvanishing locus and continuity give agreement on the intersection with the original domain.

`exists_holomorphic_extension_of_generic_comparison` derives the needed local bounds from the difference estimate and continuity of the characteristic pairing on the imaginary slice. It bounds the continuous function on a compact real coordinate ball and applies the difference estimate there. The maximal holomorphic extension from `ImaginaryHolomorphicExtension` gives one open domain containing the entire imaginary slice. `norm_sub_le_of_dense_imaginary` passes the bound to any subset of an open ball on which the generic bound holds. In particular, a bounded set contained in the exceptional locus can be placed in a larger open ball before taking limits.

The source is P. Delorme, *On the spectral theorem of Langlands*, Advances in Mathematics 426 (2023), 109107, (7.12) and the final paragraph of the proof of Theorem 7.1, pp. 25 and 32.
