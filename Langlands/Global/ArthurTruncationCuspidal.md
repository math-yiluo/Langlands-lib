# Arthur truncation on cuspidal functions

Assume that the function is left rational invariant and its actual constant terms along the unipotent radicals of all proper parabolics vanish. In Arthur's published truncation formula, every proper-index summand is then zero. The order theorem for standard parabolics shows that each proper subset of the simple roots represents a proper parabolic. At the full index, the radical is trivial and its normalized quotient measure has mass one. The parabolic contains all principal rational points, so its coset space has one element. The sign and the chamber factor are both one. Rational invariance identifies the resulting summand with the original value.

This identity applies to Arthur's formula (13.1) in *An introduction to the trace formula* and permits applying the rapid decrease of truncated functions to the cuspidal test functions used for the Eisenstein functional equation.
