# The packets of the whole group: reduction to square-integrable automorphic forms

Module `Langlands.Global.AdelicGroupParabolicSpectrumReduction` reduces the published
`Langlands.Global.GroupParabolicSpectrumStatement` (module
`Langlands.Global.AdelicSpectralDecompositionReduction`) to a new proposition,
`GroupSquareIntegrableFormSpectrumStatement`. The new proposition is Lapid's density of `A²_G`
in the discrete spectrum. The module proves the implication
`groupParabolicSpectrum_of_squareIntegrableFormSpectrum`. It proves neither statement.

The reduction removes the wave-packet formalism. For a presented standard parabolic `p : P → G`
that is an isomorphism, the wave packets of `IsRelativeWavePacket` are exactly the finite linear
combinations `∑ j, c j · v j` of elements `v j` of Lapid's `A²_P`
(`IsSquareIntegrableInducedForm`). So `parabolicSpectrum` is the closed span of the classes of
these combinations. What remains is the representation-theoretic comparison of that closed span
with `discreteSpectrum`. This is the "second comparison" in the context of Task 7949.

## Sources

- E. Lapid, *On Arthur's asymptotic inner product formula of truncated Eisenstein series*,
  Clay Math. Proc. 13 (2011), registered as
  `lapid-2011-asymptotic-inner-product-truncated-eisenstein`. §2, printed p.311 (volume PDF
  p.320) defines `A_P`, `A^n_P` and
  `A²_P = {φ ∈ A^n_P : ∫_{A_M P U(𝔸) \ G(𝔸)} |φ(g)|² dg < ∞}`. It then states: "We can view
  `A²_P` as a dense subspace of `Ind_{P(𝔸)}^{G(𝔸)} L²_disc(A_M M \ M(𝔸))`, where
  `L²_disc(A_M M \ M(𝔸))` is the direct sum of all irreducible subrepresentations of the regular
  representation of `M(𝔸)` on `L²(A_M M \ M(𝔸))`." Lapid gives no proof; it is classical.
- Getz–Hahn, *An introduction to automorphic representations* (2024), registered as
  `getz-hahn-2024-automorphic-representations`, §9.1 (9.6), printed p.242: `L²_disc([G])` is the
  largest closed subspace of `L²([G])` that is a Hilbert direct sum of irreducible
  subrepresentations. §10.5, printed pp.281–282: for `M = G`, `(𝔞_G^G)^* = {0}` and
  `L²_G([G]) = L²_disc([G])`.
- A. Borel and H. Jacquet, *Automorphic forms and automorphic representations*, Proc. Sympos.
  Pure Math. 33, part 1 (1979), registered as `borel-jacquet-1979-automorphic`. This is
  background for why the statement holds; it is not the statement's source. §4.2, printed
  p.195, defines automorphic forms of a type `(ξ, J, K_∞)`: `f * ξ = f` for a simple element `ξ`
  of the Hecke algebra. It notes that `ξ_f * ξ_L = ξ_f` for some compact open subgroup `L` of
  `G(𝔸_f)`, so these forms are right invariant under `L`.
  §4.3(i), p.195, says that the space of automorphic forms of a fixed type is finite-dimensional
  (after Harish-Chandra). §4.5, p.196, deduces that the Hecke module generated by an automorphic
  form is admissible. §4.6, p.196, calls a topological `G(𝔸)`-module `E` automorphic "if the
  subspace of admissible vectors in `E` is an automorphic representation" of the Hecke algebra.
  It then says that, for a character `χ` of `Z(𝔸)/Z(F)`, "any `G`-invariant irreducible closed
  subspace of `L²(G(F)\G(𝔸))_χ` … is automorphic in this sense, in view of [4, Theorem 4]". By
  the bibliography on p.202, [4] is Flath's article below, not a paper of Langlands. That
  statement concerns `L²` with a fixed central character and the automorphy of admissible
  vectors. It is not the comparison on `[G] = A_G G(F) \ G(𝔸)` made here.
- D. Flath, *Decomposition of representations into tensor products*, in the same volume
  (1979), pp.179–183, registered as `flath-1979-tensor-products`. Theorem 4, printed p.182, deals
  with a continuous irreducible unitary representation `π` of `G(𝔸)`. Part (2) says that its
  isotypic components for the full adelic maximal compact subgroup `K = ∏_v K_v` are
  finite-dimensional. Part (3) says that its `K`-finite vectors form an admissible irreducible
  `G(𝔸)`-module. In Flath's definition on the same page, an admissible module is smooth for
  `G(𝔸_f)`. So every `K`-finite vector is fixed by a compact open subgroup of `G(𝔸_f)`, not
  only `K_∞`-finite.

## Definitions and statement

### `squareIntegrableFormSpectrum`

`squareIntegrableFormSpectrum K hKc rho hsmooth hcalc p iU l` is the topological closure in
`L²([G]) = Lp ℂ 2 (RestrictionSplitCentre.canonicalMeasure F G)` of the span of the classes `f`
with

`f =ᵐ fun y => ∑ j, c j * v j y.out`

for some `k : ℕ`, `v : Fin k → G(𝔸) → ℂ` and `c : Fin k → ℂ` with
`IsSquareIntegrableInducedForm K hKc rho hsmooth hcalc p iU l (v j)` for every `j`.

Correspondence and provenance. `IsSquareIntegrableInducedForm` is the Library's formalization of
Lapid's `A²_P` (module `Langlands.Global.AdelicSquareIntegrableInducedForms`). Lapid's `A²_P` is
a vector space, so this is the closure of the set of `L²` classes of elements of `A²_P`. The
definition uses finite combinations instead of single elements. This means that no proof is
needed that the Library's predicate is closed under linear combinations, and that a combination
can be square integrable on `[G]` even when its terms are not known to be. The definition is
written for any `p`, but it is used, and is meaningful as a subspace of `L²([G])`, only for
`P = G`. There the elements of `A²_G` are the square-integrable automorphic forms on
`[G] = A_G G(F) \ G(𝔸)`.

### `GroupSquareIntegrableFormSpectrumStatement`

For a number field `F : Type`, `G` with `IsAffineHom G.X.hom`, `IsAffine G.X.left`,
`LocallyOfFiniteType G.X.hom` and `hG : IsConnectedReductive G`, a subgroup `K` of `G(𝔸)` with
`IsMaximalCompactSubgroup F G K`, `rho` with `IsClosedSubgroup rho`, `hsmooth` and `hcalc`, and
data `P M U A`, `p : P → G` (a closed immersion), `iU : U → P`, `l : M → P` and `a : A → M`
satisfying:

- the instance hypotheses `IsAffine P.X.left`, `IsAffine M.X.left`,
  `LocallyOfFiniteType M.X.hom` and `IsAffine U.X.left`;
- there is a `ParabolicRootPresentation p l a`;
- `LeviSubgroup.IsGeometricUnipotentRadical iU`;
- `hK : IsIwasawaCompact p K`;
- `IsIso p`.

The conclusion is

`squareIntegrableFormSpectrum K hK.isCompact rho hsmooth hcalc p iU l = discreteSpectrum F G hG`.

Correspondence. This is Lapid's sentence for `P = G` (so `M = G`, `U = 1`, `A_M = A_G`, and
`Ind` is the identity). `A²_G` is dense in `L²_disc(A_G G \ G(𝔸)) = L²_disc([G])`, that is, the
closure of the set of classes of elements of `A²_G` is `L²_disc([G])`. On the Lean side:

- `p` is an isomorphism, the parabolic `P = G` with its presentation;
- `squareIntegrableFormSpectrum` is that closure;
- `discreteSpectrum F G hG` is `Langlands.Analysis.discretePart` of the right regular
  representation `canonicalL2RightAction` on `L²([G])`. This is the closure of the sum of all
  irreducible closed invariant subspaces, Lapid's "direct sum of all irreducible
  subrepresentations" and Getz–Hahn's (9.6).

The hypotheses on `P`, `M`, `U`, `iU` and `l` say that `A²_P` is formed with the parabolic
`P = G`, its trivial unipotent radical and its Levi `M ≅ G`. The statement keeps them in the form
used by `IsSquareIntegrableInducedForm` and by `GroupParabolicSpectrumStatement`. It drops the
data of the target that do not enter `A²_P`: the minimal parabolic `P₀`, the instance arguments
of the presentation, `hA` and `hT`. This makes it stronger than the reduction needs only by
removing unused hypotheses.

Two differences from Lapid's setting do not change the content:

- Lapid fixes `K` in good position with respect to a minimal Levi subgroup. Here `K` is any
  maximal compact subgroup with `P(𝔸)K = G(𝔸)`, which is automatic for `P = G`. `K` enters only
  through the `K_∞`-finiteness of automorphic forms, with `K_∞` the archimedean component
  `K.comap (ofInfinite F G)`, and through the Iwasawa form of the integrability condition.
- `IsSquareIntegrableInducedForm` writes Lapid's integral in Iwasawa coordinates, as
  integrability of `(k, m) ↦ δ_P(m)⁻¹ |φ(m k)|²` on `K × [M]`. For `P = G` this is
  square integrability on `[G]`: here `δ_P = 1`, `[M] ≅ [G]` and the measure on `[G]` is right
  `K`-invariant. This explains the reading; it is not proved here.

Why it holds (explanatory, not proved here). The predicate `IsSquareIntegrableInducedForm`
contains `IsAutomorphicFunctionOn`. That predicate includes `IsAdelicSmoothFunction`, so
every `φ ∈ A²_G` is right invariant under an open subgroup `L` of `G(𝔸_f)`. It is also
`K_∞`-finite and annihilated by an ideal `J` of finite codimension in `𝔷`.

- Forward. Let `H_φ` be the closed invariant subspace of `L²([G])` generated by `φ`. Take any
  orthogonal decomposition of `H_φ` into nonzero closed invariant subspaces. If the projection
  of `φ` to a summand were zero, then `φ`, and with it all of `H_φ`, would be orthogonal to
  that summand. So the projections of `φ` to the summands are nonzero and mutually orthogonal.
  They keep the right `L`-invariance, the finitely many `K_∞`-types of `φ` and the
  annihilation by `J`. A proof must show that they are automorphic forms of one fixed type. For
  growth, compare Borel–Jacquet §4.3(ii), which is stated with a fixed central character. By
  the finiteness theorem (Borel–Jacquet §4.3(i)), these forms lie in a finite-dimensional
  space. So the number of summands is bounded by its dimension. A decomposition with the most
  summands then has irreducible summands, so `H_φ` is a finite orthogonal sum of irreducibles
  and the class of `φ` lies in the discrete part. The finiteness comes from the fixed compact
  types (`L`, the `K_∞`-types of `φ`) and `J` of the cyclic generator, together with the
  finite dimension of a fixed type.
- Converse. Let `H` be an irreducible closed invariant subspace of `L²([G])`. Let
  `K = ∏_v K_v` be a maximal compact subgroup of `G(𝔸)` as in Flath §3. By Flath's
  Theorem 4(2)–(3), the `K`-finite vectors of `H` form an admissible module with
  finite-dimensional `K`-isotypic components. They are dense in `H`, as `K`-finite vectors of
  any unitary representation are (Peter–Weyl). Each such vector is fixed by a compact open
  subgroup of `G(𝔸_f)` and is `K_∞`-finite. `K_∞`-finiteness and `𝔷`-finiteness alone are not
  enough: a vector of `H` with both properties need not have an open stabilizer in `G(𝔸_f)`.
  The dense space must be the full adelic `K`-finite vectors. Borel–Jacquet §4.6, citing Flath
  [4, Theorem 4], states that such admissible vectors form an automorphic representation, for
  `L²` with a fixed central character. A proof must still show that each `K`-finite vector of
  `H` is a.e. equal to `y ↦ φ (y.out)` for an actual function `φ` satisfying
  `IsSquareIntegrableInducedForm`. Concretely, `φ` must be smooth, `𝔷`-finite, of moderate
  growth and left invariant under `G(F)`. It must also satisfy the `A^n_P` condition
  `IsSplitComponentEquivariant` and the Iwasawa-form integrability condition.

## Proved lemmas

- `ParabolicRootPresentation.isCentral_of_isIso`: if `p` is an isomorphism, then
  `IsCentral (a ≫ l ≫ p)`. Proof: `l` is an isomorphism by
  `IsRationalLeviSubgroup.isIso_of_isIso_parabolic`. The split component `a` is central in `M`
  (`isCentralSplitTorus.isCentral`), and centrality is transported along the isomorphism
  `l ≫ p` (`IsCentral.comp_iso`).
- `ParabolicRootPresentation.unipotentRoots_eq_empty_of_isIso`: if `p` is an isomorphism, then
  `r.unipotentRoots = ∅`. Proof: an element is `f ≫ χ ≠ 0` with `χ` a positive relative root of
  the presentation torus. Since `groupHom f ≫ i = a ≫ l ≫ p` is central,
  `root_restrict_eq_zero_of_isCentral` gives `f ≫ χ = 0`.
- `AdelicGroupPoints.map_rational_inv_of_isIso`: if `p` is an isomorphism, then for every
  rational point `w` of `G`, the image under `p` of the rational point `inv p ∘ w` of `P` is
  `w`. Proof: `rational_naturality` and `inv p ≫ p = 𝟙`.
- `AdelicGroupPoints.subsingleton_parabolicRationalCosets_of_isIso`: if `p` is an isomorphism,
  `P(F) \ G(F)` has at most one element, since every `y x⁻¹` lies in the image of `P(F)`.
- `AdelicGroupPoints.IsIwasawaCompact.eisensteinSeries_of_isIso`: let `p` be an isomorphism,
  `l` a rational Levi, `iU` a geometric unipotent radical and `φ` left invariant under
  `U(𝔸) M(F)`. Then `hK.eisensteinSeries φ λ g = φ g * hK.heightPower λ g`. Proof: the `tsum`
  over the subsingleton `P(F) \ G(F)` is its value at the coset of `1`
  (`tsum_eq_single`), and the published `eisensteinTerm_eq_of_representative` evaluates that
  term at the representative `1`.
- `AdelicGroupPoints.IsIwasawaCompact.eisensteinSeries_zero_of_isIso`: in the same situation,
  `hK.eisensteinSeries φ 0 g = φ g`, because `heightPower 0 = exp 0 = 1`.
- `AdelicGroupPoints.IsIwasawaCompact.isMeromorphicInCoordinatesOn_eisensteinSeries_of_isIso`:
  in the same situation, and with `X^*(P) ⊗ ℂ` finite-dimensional, `λ ↦ E(g, φ, λ)` is
  meromorphic in linear coordinates with domain of holomorphy `Set.univ`. Proof: in coordinates
  `e`, the function is `s ↦ φ g · exp(L s)` with `L` the `ℂ`-linear form
  `s ↦ ⟨e s, H_P(g)⟩`. So it is differentiable, hence holomorphic on every set; the local
  meromorphic data are `W = univ`, `g = 1` and `h` the function itself.
- `realParameterPairing_left_eq_zero`: if `X^*(P)` is a free `ℤ`-module and
  `⟨w, H⟩ = 0` for every `H ∈ Hom(X^*(P), ℝ)`, then `w = 0` in `X^*(P) ⊗ ℝ`. Proof: for a basis
  `b`, the pairing of `w` with `Int.cast ∘ b.coord i` is the `i`-th coordinate of `w` in the
  base-changed basis `b.baseChange ℝ` (induction on tensors, `Module.Basis.baseChange_repr_tmul`).
- `exists_characterLog_of_isIso`: if `p` is an isomorphism, then every
  `H ∈ Hom(X^*(P), ℝ)` is `characterLog F P x` for some `x ∈ P(𝔸)` with `p x ∈ A_G`. Proof:
  take `y = logSection(H ∘ p^*)` in the canonical split centre. The published
  `PositiveRealTorus.logSection` applies to the canonical split component, using
  `canonical_characterRestrict_injective` (with the proved `centralSplitTorusCharacter`),
  `canonical_finiteIndex` and a splitting of the canonical torus. Then `y ∈ A_G` by
  `logSection_range` and `canonicalSubgroup_eq_imageSubgroup`, and `x = (inv p)(y)`.
  `characterLog_map` and `characterRestrict_comp` give `characterLog F P x = H`.
- `relativeParameterSpace_eq_bot_of_isIso`: for a presented `p` that is an isomorphism,
  `relativeParameterSpace p = ⊥`, that is `(𝔞_G^G)^* = 0`. Proof: `X^*(P)` is free
  (`ParabolicRootPresentation.rationalCharacters_free_finite`). A `w` in the space pairs to zero
  with every `characterLog F P x` with `p x ∈ A_G`, hence with every `H` by the previous lemma.
  So `w = 0` by `realParameterPairing_left_eq_zero`.
- `subsingleton_relativeParameterSpace_of_isIso`: the same space is a subsingleton.
- `integral_spectralMeasure_zero`: `∫ x, f x ∂(spectralMeasure 0) = f 0` for
  `f : (Fin 0 → ℝ) → ℂ`. Proof: `f` is constant on the one-point space. `integral_const` and
  `spectralMeasure 0 = volume = dirac 0` (`Measure.volume_pi_eq_dirac`) give the value.

## The comparison of spaces

`parabolicSpectrum_eq_squareIntegrableFormSpectrum_of_isIso`: for a presented `p` that is an
isomorphism, with `hK`, the instance arguments of `r`, `hA`, `hT`, `hG` and
`LeviSubgroup.IsGeometricUnipotentRadical iU`,

`parabolicSpectrum hK rho hsmooth hcalc iU l r hA hT = squareIntegrableFormSpectrum K hK.isCompact rho hsmooth hcalc p iU l`.

Proof (as implemented). Both sides are closures of spans, so it suffices that the generating sets
are equal (`congrArg` on `Set.ext`).

- From packets to combinations: let `Θ` be a wave packet with data `n, e, k, v, c, E, D`.
  `e : ℝⁿ ≃ relativeParameterSpace p` and the target is a subsingleton, so
  `n = finrank ℝⁿ = 0`. Every parameter is regular because `r.unipotentRoots = ∅`, so the
  regularity clause gives `E j = hK.eisensteinSeries (v j)` everywhere. Every `e x` is `0`, and
  `imaginaryParameter 0 = 0`. By `integral_spectralMeasure_zero` and
  `eisensteinSeries_zero_of_isIso` (with the left invariance contained in
  `IsSquareIntegrableInducedForm`), `Θ = ∑ j, c j 0 · v j`. So the class of `Θ` is in the second
  generating set, with coefficients `c j 0`.
- From combinations to packets: given `∑ j, c j · v j`, take `n = 0`, `e` the unique linear
  equivalence of the two zero spaces (`LinearEquiv.ofSubsingleton`), the constant coefficients
  `fun _ => c j` and `E j = hK.eisensteinSeries (v j)` on `D = univ`. The conditions are:
  - constant functions on `ℝ⁰` are smooth, and have compact support because `ℝ⁰` is finite;
  - meromorphy is `isMeromorphicInCoordinatesOn_eisensteinSeries_of_isIso`, with
    finite-dimensionality from `finiteDimensional_complexCharacterParameter`;
  - the regularity clause holds by `rfl` for any `R`;
  - the integral formula follows from `integral_spectralMeasure_zero` and
    `eisensteinSeries_zero_of_isIso`.

## The reduction theorem

`groupParabolicSpectrum_of_squareIntegrableFormSpectrum` takes
`GroupSquareIntegrableFormSpectrumStatement` and proves `GroupParabolicSpectrumStatement`.

Proof (as implemented). Introduce the data of the target. Install `IsIso p` and the instance
`LocallyOfFiniteType P.X.hom` (from `IsClosedSubgroup.locallyOfFiniteType`, as in the target).
Rewrite `parabolicSpectrum` with `parabolicSpectrum_eq_squareIntegrableFormSpectrum_of_isIso`.
Then apply the premise to the same data, without `P₀` and the presentation's instance
arguments.

The module uses only the axioms `propext`, `Classical.choice` and `Quot.sound`.

## Scope

- Proved: the identification of the packet space of a full presented parabolic with the closed
  span of the `L²([G])` classes of finite combinations of elements of `A²_G`, and the implication
  from `GroupSquareIntegrableFormSpectrumStatement` to `GroupParabolicSpectrumStatement`.
- Not proved: `GroupSquareIntegrableFormSpectrumStatement` itself. A proof needs:
  - the finite discrete content of a square-integrable automorphic form: the projections of a
    cyclic generator have fixed compact types and lie in a finite-dimensional space of
    automorphic forms of one type (Harish-Chandra's finiteness theorem);
  - the density of the full adelic `K`-finite vectors (Flath's Theorem 4) in the irreducible
    closed invariant subspaces of `L²([G])`. Each such vector is right invariant under a compact
    open subgroup of `G(𝔸_f)` in addition to its archimedean finiteness;
  - the representative automorphy of those vectors: each is a.e. equal on `[G]` to an actual
    function satisfying `IsAutomorphicFunctionOn` and the `A^n_P` condition;
  - the comparison of Lapid's integrability condition, in its Iwasawa form, with square
    integrability on `[G]`.
- Not proved or used: the remaining premises of `spectralDecomposition_of_reduction`.
