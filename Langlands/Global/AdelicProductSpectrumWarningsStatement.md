# Support warnings for spectra of products and adelic levels

`AdelicProductSpectrumWarningsStatement` states the warnings about factor
spectra of an infinite product ring in Brian Conrad, *Weil and Grothendieck
approaches to adelic points* (2011), printed pp.7–8, proof of Theorem 3.6. The
registered source is `conrad-2011-weil-grothendieck-adelic-points`, SHA256
`fe4a9193aff4ef575665b40971f3da32f850a74d87b3f2d0fa28bba26b9cbabb`.

On p.7 Conrad notes that the quasi-compact `Spec A_{F,S}` "is not the disjoint
union of these infinitely many pairwise disjoint non-empty affine open
subschemes". For an arbitrary product `R = ∏ R_i` he notes that the `Spec(R_i)`
are disjoint open subschemes of `Spec(R)`, "so this is not a cover of `Spec(R)`
if infinitely many of the `R_i` are nonzero". He writes `U` for their union. In
the injectivity proof he remarks that the quasi-compactness of `V` is essential,
"as otherwise we could take `V = U`". He also says it is easy to construct ideals
in `A_{F,S}` that are not finitely generated but generate the unit ideal in each
standard factor ring: "consider the ideal generated by elements that have a
uniformizer component in all but finitely many places". On p.8 he says the
argument in the closed case shows that `U` is scheme-theoretically dense in
`Spec(R)`, but need not be topologically dense. He then gives Moret-Bailly's
example: `C = k` a field, `R_n = k[t]/(t^(n+1))` for `n ≥ 0`, and the closed
subscheme `V = Spec(R/(r))` cut out by the diagonal element `r = (t, t, ...)`.
This `V` contains every point of `U`. It does not contain `Spec(R_n)`
scheme-theoretically for any `n ≥ 1`. Its underlying space is not all of
`Spec(R)`, because `r` is not nilpotent.

Throughout, the factor spectrum of a ring map `p : A →+* B` is the actual scheme
morphism `Spec.map (CommRingCat.ofHom p) : Spec B ⟶ Spec A`, and `U` is the union
of the set-theoretic ranges of these morphisms.

## The predicates

`ProductFactorSpectrumStatement` concerns an arbitrary family `R : ι → Type u`
of commutative rings and the evaluation maps `Pi.evalRingHom R i`. It states:

1. each factor morphism `Spec (R i) ⟶ Spec (∏ R i)` is an open immersion;
2. the ranges of two different factor morphisms are disjoint;
3. the union `U` of the ranges is all of `Spec (∏ R i)` if and only if only
   finitely many `R i` are nontrivial;
4. if infinitely many `R i` are nontrivial, then `U` is not compact;
5. if every factor morphism factors through the closed immersion
   `Spec ((∏ R i) ⧸ I) ⟶ Spec (∏ R i)`, then `I = ⊥`.

Clause 5 is the scheme-theoretic density of `U`. Clause 4, with `U` open and
`U ≠ Spec (∏ R i)` from clause 3, is Conrad's counterexample `V = U`.

`LocalFactorSpectrumWarnings A B p` is a predicate on a ring `A` with ring maps
`p i : A →+* B i` to local rings. It asserts clauses 1, 2 and 5 for the maps
`p i`. It also asserts the following:

- each range is nonempty;
- `U` is not all of `Spec A`;
- `U` is not compact;
- for the ideal `I` spanned by the elements `b` such that `p j b` is irreducible
  for all but finitely many `j`:
  - an element `a` lies in `I` exactly when `p j a` lies in the maximal ideal of
    `B j` for all but finitely many `j`;
  - `I` is not finitely generated;
  - `I` maps onto the unit ideal under every `p i`.

In a discrete valuation ring the irreducible elements are the uniformizers, so
`I` is Conrad's ideal. The predicate only names these properties and assumes
none of them.

`LocalProductSpectrumWarningsStatement` asserts `LocalFactorSpectrumWarnings`
for `∏ R i` and its evaluation maps. Here the index type is infinite, every
`R i` is local, and for all but finitely many `i` the maximal ideal of `R i` is
spanned by one irreducible element.

`ClosedSubschemeSupportWarning R r N` is a predicate on a family indexed by a
type in universe `0`, an element `r` of the product and a set `N` of indices,
with `V` the closed subscheme `Spec ((∏ R i) ⧸ (r))`. It asserts:

1. every factor range lies in the range of `V`;
2. for `i ∈ N`, the factor morphism `Spec (R i) ⟶ Spec (∏ R i)` does not factor
   through `V`;
3. `r` is not nilpotent;
4. the range of `V` is not all of `Spec (∏ R i)`;
5. the closure of `U` is not all of `Spec (∏ R i)`.

`MoretBaillyDiagonalStatement` asserts this predicate for every field `k`, the
family `n ↦ k[X] ⧸ (X ^ (n + 1))` over `n : ℕ`, the diagonal element whose
`n`-th component is the class of `X`, and `N = {n | 1 ≤ n}`.

## Adelic levels

`NumberFieldLevelSpectrumWarningsStatement` asserts `LocalFactorSpectrumWarnings`
for the published full level `numberFieldAdeleLevel K S` of every number field
`K` and finite set `S` of finite places. Its factors are the published
`NumberFieldLevelFactor K S`: every infinite completion, the finite completions
at places in `S`, and the completed integers at finite places outside `S`. Its
maps are the published `numberFieldLevelFactorProjection K S`.

`CurvePlaceLevelSpectrumWarningsStatement` asserts the same for the published
curve-place level `curvePlaceAdeleLevel k F S`, its factors
`CurvePlaceLevelFactor k F S` and its maps `curvePlaceLevelFactorProjection k F S`.
Here `F` is any finite extension of `RatFunc k` with the specified compatible
actions, `k` is an arbitrary field and `S` is any finite set of places. No
finiteness or perfectness of `k` and no separability of `F` are assumed.

`AdelicProductSpectrumWarningsStatement` is the conjunction of
`ProductFactorSpectrumStatement`, `LocalProductSpectrumWarningsStatement`,
`MoretBaillyDiagonalStatement` and the two level statements, in one universe `u`.

## Provenance

Conrad states these facts in prose and parentheses, with brief or no proof.
The following are this formalization's precise reading. Conrad gives only the
direction "not a cover if infinitely many are nonzero"; the converse in clause 3
is added. Non-compactness of `U` is the reason his counterexample `V = U` works.
The general local-product hypothesis, cofinitely many principal maximal ideals
spanned by irreducibles, isolates what his `A_{F,S}` example uses. Conrad
describes the ideal, but not its membership characterization. The topological
non-density clause follows from the closed `V` he constructs. The number-field
level includes the archimedean factors, and the function-field level is added
to treat both characteristics.

Conrad's main assertions here, that a finitely generated ideal that is the unit
ideal in every factor is the unit ideal and the bijectivity of (3.6.2), are not
restated. Mathlib's `AlgebraicGeometry.Ideal.span_eq_top_of_span_image_evalRingHom`,
`pointsPi_injective` and `pointsPi_surjective` already prove them.
