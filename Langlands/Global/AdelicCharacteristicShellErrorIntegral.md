# Integrating a minimal-height majorant on a characteristic shell

`characteristicShellErrorIntegral` integrates a pointwise error estimate on the actual rational parabolic shell. The pointwise hypothesis, evaluated on the minimal Iwasawa representatives whose heights lie in the shell, is a nonnegative scalar times the norm of the squared minimal-parabolic half modulus. The conclusion gives integrability and bounds the integral by that scalar times a polynomial in the norm of the upper cutoff shifted by a fixed lower point. The degree is the relative rank.

The proof lifts the shell to the minimal rational parabolic quotient. The pushforward of its restricted measure dominates the restricted measure on the target parabolic quotient. To see the inequality, intersect the inverse image of a measurable test set with the fixed fundamental domain of the smaller rational subgroup. Its image in the larger quotient has measure at most the measure of the intersection. This uses the fundamental-domain measure formula and countable subadditivity, so possible multiple fibres only enlarge the upper bound.

The minimal Iwasawa height is invariant under the minimal rational parabolic. On a minimal Iwasawa representative, the radical and compact factors leave the height unchanged, and the intrinsic norm-one minimal Levi factor has height zero. Thus the restriction of the coordinate measure imposes the actual height constraint on the positive split factor alone. In the majorant integral the squared minimal half modulus cancels the inverse split density. The normalized radical and compact factors have mass one, and the intrinsic norm-one minimal Levi quotient has finite mass. Tonelli leaves the split Haar volume times the error scalar. A fixed strict lower cutoff puts the split points in the preceding coroot-coordinate box estimate. The resulting finite bound gives both the integrability assertion and the estimate for the complex integral.

This supplies the measure and volume step from a pointwise error bound to the integrated shell estimate in Delorme, *On the spectral theorem of Langlands*, Advances in Mathematics 426 (2023), 109107, (7.17)–(7.22).
