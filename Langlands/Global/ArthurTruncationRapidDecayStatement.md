# Arthur's norm-one truncation estimates

**Sources.** J. Arthur, *An introduction to the trace formula* (2005), §13, printed pp. 69–71 (PDF pp. 81–83), in `arthur-ellwood-kottwitz-2005-harmonic-analysis-shimura`: height and uniform temperedness, Proposition 13.2(a),(b), (13.5)–(13.6). The earlier estimate is Arthur, *A trace formula for reductive groups II* (1980), §1, pp. 95–97 (PDF pp. 10–12), Lemma 1.4 and its proof, `arthur-1980-trace-formula-reductive-groups-II`. There `T` is fixed and suitably regular (p. 89, PDF p. 4); the chosen differentiability order can exceed the degrees of the finitely many operators (p. 97).

**Exact target.** `Langlands.Global.ArthurTruncation.TruncationRapidDecayStatement`. For the actual connected reductive group over a number field, the statement produces an embedded smooth Lie atlas on `G(F∞)¹` in the ambient [infinite adelic manifold](ReductiveInfiniteAdelicManifold.md). In particular, the atlas is *not* a premise of this closed proposition. It also chooses a single suitable maximal compact `K`, as in [ArthurTruncationStatement](ArthurTruncationStatement.md): `P₀(𝔸_F) K = G(𝔸_F)`, with the L1 positive-torus compatibility. For reflection data `D` and the character comparison `hρ`, and for any closed matrix realization in `GL_N` with `N > 0`, there is a threshold `c` such that (a) and (b) hold for all `T` with `β(T) > c` for every simple relative root `β`. The formula is [normOneTruncation](ArthurNormOneTruncation.md) on the actual norm-one group. The height `H(x)` in the estimates denotes the positive real `RepresentationGrowth.adelicSourceNorm F G rho x`, the Library's normalized closed-representation height, not the vector-valued Iwasawa height.

**Part (a).** `IsNormOneUniformlyTempered` requires smoothness at some open compact finite level and one nonnegative exponent `p`, with `|X f(x)| ≤ B_X H(x)^p` on all of `G(𝔸_F)¹` for each invariant differential operator `X`. The constant may depend on `X`, the exponent may not. The operators are represented by words with complex coefficients in an identity tangent basis; see [GroupSchemeNormOneSmooth](../Automorphic/GroupSchemeNormOneSmooth.md). For each such `f`, the `IsRapidlyDecreasing` predicate of [ArthurSiegelSets](ArthurSiegelSets.md) gives the negative-power bound for every order on every `S ∩ G(𝔸_F)¹` for the chosen `K`. The zero extension in that predicate only lets it be applied to a function defined on the norm-one group; its tested values are on that group. This does not restrict the input `f` to restrictions of smooth full-adelic functions as in the earlier `IsTruncationRapidDecay` formulation.

**Part (b).** Fix `T`, a Siegel set for `K` (compact factors and `T₁`), positive integers `n,n₀`, and an open compact finite level `K₀`. The statement chooses one finite set of left-invariant operators `X`, an order `r > 0` bounding their word degrees, and a finite nonnegative constant `B`, independently of the family and its measure space. For any measure space `(Ω,m)` and fixed-level `C^r` family with joint measurable evaluation `(o,x) ↦ φ(o,x)`, it asserts measurability of the truncated values in `o` for `x ∈ S ∩ G(𝔸_F)¹` and the pointwise bound there

```
H(x)^n ∫_Ω |Λ^T φ(o,x)| dm(o)
  ≤ B sup_{y ∈ G(𝔸_F)¹} [ H(y)^(−n₀) ∑_X ∫_Ω |(Xφ(o))(y)| dm(o) ].
```

Taking the supremum in `x` is (13.5)–(13.6). The nonnegative integrals and supremum in Lean are `lintegral` and `iSup` in `ℝ≥0∞`, so the inequality has content whenever the right side is finite without requiring global integrability of every family. Joint measurable evaluation is the form of the measurable-family hypothesis used here: in the usual compact-open `C^r` topology at fixed level evaluation is jointly continuous. No topology on a space of families is a parameter. Arthur 1980 displays a sum of suprema and the proof produces fixed constants; for finitely many nonnegative terms a sum of suprema is bounded by the cardinality times the supremum of the sum. The allowance of `B` records this harmless constant without hiding it in the normalization of the chosen operators.

This is the proposition, not a proof of it. The embedded atlas existence and the estimates still require proofs. The fixed-level family estimate does not make any claim of a bound uniform in `T` or in `K₀`.
