# Completeness on the relative quotient from full-quotient completeness

`RelativeWavePacketCompletenessFromFullStatement` takes `WavePacketCompletenessStatement` on `G(F) \ G(𝔸)` and `WavePacketRapidDecayStatement`. Its conclusion is `RelativeWavePacketCompletenessStatement` on the canonical quotient `[G]`, with the prescribed minimal parabolic and square-integrable relative tests.

`standardWavePacketCompleteness_of_full` applies full completeness using the specified minimal Iwasawa parabolic. For each test in that application, it packages the given data as `IsWavePacket`. The theorem `standardWavePacketTransport` exhibits the same function with a presentation above the specified minimal parabolic and with the same compact subgroup. Orthogonality for that presentation gives the pairing required by full completeness.

`relativeWavePacketCompleteness_of_full` supplies standard-family completeness to `relativeWavePacketCompleteness_of_standard`. It obtains the shift of the continued Eisenstein series on imaginary parameters from `eisensteinContinuationAmbientShift_of_regularParameter`, using `ParabolicRootPresentation.regularParameter_nonempty` for each regularity threshold. The split-centre transfer uses rapid decay at exponent zero to obtain square integrability of the full packets before taking their central averages.

Delorme's *On the spectral theorem of Langlands*, Theorem 7.9, pp. 33–35, supplies the mathematical full-quotient completeness input and Proposition 5.4, p. 20, gives the wave-packet decay used here. The proof of Theorem 7.9 splits the full imaginary parameter into relative and ambient factors. Getz–Hahn's *An Introduction to Automorphic Representations*, §2.6, Lemma 2.6.2 and (2.19)–(2.22), describes the canonical quotient, and Theorem 10.5.1, pp. 281–282, gives density for relative parameters. The Lean implication composes the standard-parabolic transport and the split-centre transfer to pass between these settings.
