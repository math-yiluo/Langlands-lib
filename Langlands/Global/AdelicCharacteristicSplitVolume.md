# Haar volume of a characteristic split shell

`characteristicSplitVolume` proves that the relative positive split factor of a characteristic shell is Borel. Its Haar volume is at most a fixed positive multiple of the product of the positive parts of the outside fundamental weights of the cutoff increment. In any real linear chart of the cocharacter space this is bounded by a polynomial in one plus the norm of the increment, of degree the number of outside simple roots. The constants are independent of both cutoffs.

Use the relative logarithm with the projected simple coroots as its coordinate basis. Evaluation of an outside fundamental weight at the minimal height of a split point recovers the corresponding coordinate. This follows from the simple coroot pairing and the vanishing of the weight on the Levi coroot span. The defining split-shell inequalities are continuous root and weight inequalities in these coordinates, which gives Borel measurability.

For the lower bound on each coordinate, the simple roots of the difference between a shell point and the split projection of the first cutoff are nonnegative. Inside the Levi they are zero, and the defining split chamber gives strict positivity outside. The nonnegative inverse Cartan coefficients imply that each outside weight is nonnegative on this difference. The upper weight inequalities come directly from the shell definition. Since projection leaves the outside weights unchanged, the shell lies in the coordinate box between the weights of the two cutoffs. The Haar measure in these coordinates is a scalar multiple of Lebesgue measure. The finite product formula for the box and the operator norm bounds for its side lengths give the two volume estimates. The empty product has value one when there are no outside roots.

The split shell is the set occurring in Delorme, *On the spectral theorem of Langlands*, Advances in Mathematics 426 (2023), 109107, (7.23). This volume estimate controls integration over that factor in the shell increment argument.
