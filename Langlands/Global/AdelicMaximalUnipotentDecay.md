# Iterated decay from a vanishing maximal-parabolic constant term

`AdelicMaximalUnipotentDecayStatement` uses a connected reductive group \(G\) over a number field \(F\), its maximal split torus \(T\), a generic cocharacter, and a simple relative root \(\alpha\). Let \(U\) be an affine smooth connected unipotent closed model of the radical of the maximal standard parabolic omitting \(\alpha\). The data specify smooth relative dimensions for \(G\) and \(U\), a torus morphism \(T_0\to T\), and a weak simple-root chamber in the intrinsic positive subgroup of \(T_0\) with a common positive threshold.

Fix a compact set \(\Omega\) of left factors normalizing \(U(\mathbb A)\), a compact finite slice \(Y_f\), an open right level \(L\), and a compact set \(K\) of right factors. For every \(n\ge 0\), the theorem chooses a compact enlargement \(K'\supseteq K\) and a constant \(C\ge 0\). Consider \(x=p a_G k\) with \(p\in\Omega\), \(a\) in the chamber, \(k\in K\), and \(x_f\in Y_f\). Suppose \(f\) is continuous, smooth on each infinite slice, left \(U(F)\)-invariant, right \(L\)-invariant, and has identically vanishing normalized \(U\)-constant term. If every word of \(n\) basis right-orbit derivatives has norm at most \(M\ge 0\) at \(p a_G k'\) for \(k'\in K'\) with \(k'_f=k_f\), then

\[
  |f(x)|\le C \bigl(|\alpha(a)|_{\mathbb A}^{-1/[F:\mathbb Q]}\bigr)^n M.
\]

The bound and compact enlargement are uniform in the evaluation factors and the function. At order zero the sole derivative word is the original function.

The proof iterates the first-order estimate for the deviation from the constant term. Differentiation preserves smoothness on slices, continuity, the two invariances, and vanishing of the normalized constant term. At each stage all derivative evaluation points retain the original finite coordinate, so the same compact finite slice controls the unipotent quotient representatives.

This gives the arbitrary power of root contraction in the decay argument of Mœglin–Waldspurger, §I.2.10, pp. 30–33, and the relative cuspidal estimate used in Bernstein–Lapid, §7.4, pp. 215–217, once the corresponding maximal-parabolic constant term is known to vanish.
