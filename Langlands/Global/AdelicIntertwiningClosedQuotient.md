# The closed-quotient measure of the adelic intertwining integral

The unipotent target radical and its closed stabilizer both have compact rational quotients. Their intrinsic normalized Haar measures are regular and two-sided. Transport the stabilizer's Haar measure to the stabilizer inside the radical through the continuous group equivalence. The lattice comparison identifies the transported discrete quotient measure. The mass-one normalization then gives a fundamental domain of mass one on the stabilizer inside the radical.

The compatible closed quotient measure exists for these two Haar measures. The projection from the quotient by the original discrete intertwining subgroup is measure preserving by the subgroup slice formula. An automorphism of the target radical preserving the closed stabilizer now scales the integral of an inverse pullback by the ratio of the radical and stabilizer Haar moduli.

This internal reduction applies to the original adelic measure in the intertwining operator of Bernstein–Lapid §2.2.

Continuity and adelic source-radical invariance allow both translated integrands to descend to the closed quotient. When an adelic element conjugates the target radical and preserves the stabilizer, the inverse quotient automorphism moves that element from the external argument to the twisted input. The automorphism scales the quotient measure by a finite nonzero factor, so both expressions have the same integrability condition.
