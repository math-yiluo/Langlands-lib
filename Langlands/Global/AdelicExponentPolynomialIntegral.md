# Integrating bounded-degree exponent polynomials

A linear map of real logarithm spaces preserves the degree bound under pullback. In finite dimension the products of basis coordinate functions of length at most the bound give a finite spanning family for the space of exponent polynomials.

For any finite-dimensional subspace of scalar functions, the point evaluations span its dual because their common kernel is zero. Thus each basis coefficient of a family in that subspace is a finite linear combination of evaluations and is integrable when every evaluation is integrable. Integrating the finite basis expansion gives an element of the same subspace. Applied to exponent polynomials, this keeps the same degree bound when integrating their scalar values.
