# Directness of the unnormalized exponent decomposition

Terms with the same exponent can be collected in a finite sum.
The polynomial bounds for two terms fit in the larger degree bound,
and addition stays in that polynomial submodule. Twisting by an
Iwasawa height character adds the inducing parameter to the exponent.
The left translation law for the height cancels the new character
factor in the orbit formula and scales its polynomial by a constant.

For a function with unnormalized exponent `λ`, fix a positive split
component point `b` and a test point `g`. On the ray of nonnegative
integer powers of `b`, its polynomial orbit formula writes the translated
values as `μ^m q(m)`, with `μ = b^λ`. The polynomial `q` has degree bounded
by the degree bound of the orbit formula. The proof restricts each product
of linear forms to this ray and extends by the linear span.

The operator for translation by `b` minus `μ` acts by a forward difference
on `q`, together with a factor of `μ`. Translating a polynomial by one
preserves its leading coefficient and degree, so subtracting the original
polynomial lowers its degree. Iteration through the degree bound therefore
annihilates the function. The function belongs to the joint generalized
eigenspace of the actual split component action.

The positive split component powers distinguish distinct complex
parameters. The published independence of joint generalized eigenspaces
then forces every component of a vanishing finite sum to vanish. This
gives the directness in Bernstein–Lapid (6.10) for the Library's
polynomial orbit condition.

For the comparison of leading sums, extend all three families by zero
to the union of their index sets. Independence makes the equality of
the full sums an equality of components at each exponent. At a leading
exponent the error vanishes, so summing these component equalities
gives the claimed agreement. Applied to supplied cuspidal projections
and decompositions along every standard parabolic, this constructs
one family that satisfies the actual global leading-component relation
for both functions.
