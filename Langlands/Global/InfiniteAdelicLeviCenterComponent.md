# The Levi component of a central element of the archimedean enveloping algebra: proof

Module `Langlands.Global.InfiniteAdelicLeviCenterComponent`, namespace `Langlands.Global`. It
proves `LeviCenterComponentStatement`
(`Langlands.Global.InfiniteAdelicLeviCenterComponentStatement`) from
`LeviTangentWeightBasisStatement` (`Langlands.Global.InfiniteAdelicLeviWeightBasisStatement`),
and then unconditionally with the proof `leviTangentWeightBasis` of the latter
(`Langlands.Global.InfiniteAdelicLeviWeightBasis`).

## Contents

- `range_le_of_ι_mem`: an algebra map out of a complex enveloping algebra has range in a
  subalgebra `S` as soon as it maps every generator `ι x` into `S`, because the enveloping
  algebra is generated by the generators (`Langlands.Algebra.LieCasimir.enveloping_adjoin_range`).
- `extendLieHom_injective`: the complexification `extendLieHom f` of an injective real Lie algebra
  map `f` is injective, since `ℂ` is flat over `ℝ`
  (`Module.Flat.lTensor_preserves_injective_linearMap`).
- `leviCenterComponent_of_weightBasis`: `LeviTangentWeightBasisStatement` implies
  `LeviCenterComponentStatement`.
- `leviCenterComponent`: the proof of `LeviCenterComponentStatement`, applying
  `leviCenterComponent_of_weightBasis` to `leviTangentWeightBasis`.

## Proof of `leviCenterComponent_of_weightBasis`

The weight-basis statement gives `t ∈ M'(F_∞)`, a real basis `b` of the Lie algebra `𝔤` of
`G(F_∞)` indexed by `Fin n` and integer weights `w`. Its complexification
`bC = Algebra.TensorProduct.basis ℂ b` is a complex basis of `ℂ ⊗ 𝔤` with `bC a = 1 ⊗ b a`. Let
`T` be the complexification of the tangent conjugation by `ι t`
(`groupTangentConjugationLieEquiv`). Then `T (bC a) = 2 ^ (w a) • bC a`, and the unit `c = 2` of
`ℂ` is not a root of unity (its powers have norm `2 ^ m`). The enveloping decomposition
`envelopingFixedWeightDecomposition`
(`Langlands.Algebra.EnvelopingFixedWeightDecomposition`) for `L = ℂ ⊗ 𝔤`, `bC`, `w`, `c` and
`T` gives two facts. Here `U(𝔤)` denotes `AdelicEnvelopingAlgebra F G hsmooth`, the enveloping
algebra over `ℂ` of the complexification `ℂ ⊗ 𝔤`:

- every element of `U(𝔤)` fixed by the algebra map induced by `T` lies in
  `P + Z`, where `P = positiveWeightGeneratorSpan bC w` is the span of the products
  `ι(bC a) · v` with `w a > 0` and `Z = zeroWeightGeneratorSubalgebra bC w` is the subalgebra
  generated by the `ι(bC a)` with `w a = 0`;
- `P` and `Z` meet only in `0`.

The algebra map induced by `T` is `adelicEnvelopingConjugation F G hsmooth (ι t)`, which fixes
the center of `U(𝔤)` by `adelicEnvelopingCenterConjugation`. Four comparisons, each from one
clause of the weight-basis statement, relate `P` and `Z` to the curve ideal `I_V` and the image
of `j`:

1. `P ⊆ I_V`: a positive-weight `b a` is the velocity of a smooth curve through `1` in `S_V`,
   so `ι(bC a) · v = ι(1 ⊗ b a) · v` is a generator of `I_V`.
2. `I_V ⊆ P`: the velocity `X` of such a curve lies in the real span of the positive-weight
   `b a`, and `ι(z ⊗ X) · v ∈ P` for every `z ∈ ℂ` by induction on that span. Here
   `z ⊗ b a = z • bC a`, and real scalars are moved into `z` by `TensorProduct.smul_tmul`.
3. `Z ⊆ range j`: a weight-zero `b a` is `dι Y` for a tangent vector `Y` of `M'(F_∞)`, so
   `ι(bC a) = j (ι(1 ⊗ Y))` (`pointEnvelopingMap_ι_tmul`).
4. `range j ⊆ Z`: on generators, `j (ι(z ⊗ Y)) = ι(z ⊗ dι Y)`, and `dι Y` lies in the real span
   of the weight-zero `b a`; induction on that span and `range_le_of_ι_mem` conclude.

Moreover `j` is injective. It is induced by the complexification of the tangent map of `ι`,
which is injective by the weight-basis statement and `extendLieHom_injective`.
`lift_comp_injective` (`Langlands.Algebra.EnvelopingMapInjective`) then shows that the induced
map of enveloping algebras is injective; the Lie algebras are finite-dimensional.

For central `z`, write `z = p + r` with `p ∈ P` and `r ∈ Z`. By 3, `r = j y` for some `y`, and
`z - j y = p ∈ I_V` by 1. If `j y ∈ I_V`, then `j y ∈ P` by 2 and `j y ∈ Z` by 4, so `j y = 0`
and `y = 0` by injectivity.

This is the weight argument of Knapp–Vogan, *Cohomological Induction and Unitary
Representations* (1995), Lemma 4.123. The decomposition is taken with respect to the eigenvalues
of `Ad(ι t)` rather than the roots of a Cartan subalgebra, and in the mirror form with right
ideals.
