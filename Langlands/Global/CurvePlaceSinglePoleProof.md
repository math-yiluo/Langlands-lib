# Proof of the single-pole assertion

`curvePlaceSinglePole` proves `CurvePlaceSinglePoleStatement`: over a
finite field of constants \(k\), for every function field \(F\) and
every place \(v\) of `AlgebraicCurve.Place k F`, some \(f\in F\) lies
outside the valuation ring of \(v\) and inside the valuation ring of
every other place. The theorem `exists_curvePlace_singlePole` gives
the argument for fixed \(k\), \(F\) and \(v\).

The proof does not use the given algebra map from `RatFunc k` to
\(F\). It uses only the finiteness of \(F\) over the subfield
\(k(s)\) generated by a parameter \(s\) chosen below. The statement
therefore holds whether or not \(F\) is separable over the given
copy of the rational function field.

## The parameter and the prime-power generator

Start with a parameter \(s\) having a pole at the specified place
\(v\). It exists because the valuation ring of \(v\) is not all of
\(F\). Since its valuation is not \(1\), both \(s\) and \(s^{-1}\)
are transcendental over \(k\) by Mathlib's
`Valuation.transcendental_of_ne_one`. Mathlib's
`FunctionField.finiteDimensional_of_adjoin_transcendental` then
makes \(F\) finite over \(k(s)\) and over \(k(s^{-1})\).

The reciprocal chart is the integral closure `CurveModel.chartRing`
of \(k[s^{-1}]\) in \(F\). The place \(v\) gives the height-one
prime \(P\) of this chart, by `CurveModel.primeOfValuationSubring`,
and \(s^{-1}\) belongs to \(P\). The chart has a finite class group
by `finite_classGroup_normalizedCurveChart`. Then
`exists_primePowerGenerator` gives a nonzero element \(d\) of the
chart that belongs to \(P\) and to no other height-one prime.

The inverse \(x=d^{-1}\) has valuation greater than \(1\) at \(v\).
Every height-one prime not containing \(s^{-1}\) differs from \(P\),
so \(d\) has valuation \(1\) there, and \(x\) lies in the
localization `dedekindAwaySubalgebra` of the chart away from
\(s^{-1}\). Thus \(x=c\,s^{n}\) with \(c\) in the reciprocal
chart, and \(x\) belongs to the overlap chart, the integral closure
of \(k[s,s^{-1}]\). If a place \(w\ne v\) has \(s^{-1}\) in its
valuation ring, then its prime in the reciprocal chart differs from
\(P\) (by `FiniteConstantCurveChart.ofHeightOneSpectrum_center`), so
\(d\) has valuation \(1\) at \(w\) and \(x\) is regular at \(w\).

## The additive quotient

The quotient `curveChartAdditiveQuotient` of the overlap chart by the
sum `curveChartSum` of the two parameter charts is finite by
`finite_curveChartAdditiveQuotient`. Two distinct powers \(x^i\) and
\(x^j\) therefore have the same class, and their difference is
\(a+b\), with \(a\) in the parameter chart of \(s\) and \(b\) in the
reciprocal chart. The two powers have distinct valuations at \(v\),
both at least \(1\). Their difference therefore has the larger
valuation, which is greater than \(1\), so it has a pole at \(v\).

Since \(s^{-1}\) is regular at \(v\), so is \(b\)
(`CurveModel.chartRing_le_valuationSubring`), and the pole of
\(a+b\) is a pole of \(a\). Let \(w\) be any other place. If \(s\)
is regular at \(w\), then so is \(a\). Otherwise \(s^{-1}\) is
regular at \(w\), so both the power difference and \(b\) are regular
at \(w\), and so is \(a\). Thus \(a\) is regular at every place
other than \(v\).
