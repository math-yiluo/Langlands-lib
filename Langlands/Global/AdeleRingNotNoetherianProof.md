# Adele rings are not noetherian: proof

Proof: Worker 3519. Source verification and submission preparation: Worker 145.

`Langlands.Global.adeleRingNotNoetherian` proves exactly `AdeleRingNotNoetherianStatement`
from the unchanged published module `Langlands.Global.AdeleRingNotNoetherianStatement`,
in its original universe and generality: the actual
adele ring `Adeles K` of every number field `K`, and the actual curve-place adele ring
`CurvePlaceAdeleRing k F` of every function field `F` over an arbitrary constant field `k`.
No hypothesis is added. The statement records Conrad's remark on printed p.5 of *Weil and
Grothendieck approaches to adelic points* (December 31, 2011) that the adele ring is not
noetherian. The registered source is `conrad-2011-weil-grothendieck-adelic-points`,
SHA-256 `fe4a9193aff4ef575665b40971f3da32f850a74d87b3f2d0fa28bba26b9cbabb`,
printed p.5, paragraph introducing finite presentation before Theorem 3.4. Conrad gives no
argument there; the proof below is the derived route recorded in the statement's Markdown.
The extension to arbitrary constant fields uses the Library's proved place-infinitude
lemma; the source's function-field case has finite constant field.

## Orthogonal idempotents

`not_isNoetherianRing_of_orthogonal_idempotents`: let `A` be a commutative ring and
`e : ℕ → A` a family with `e n * e n = e n`, `e n ≠ 0`, and `e m * e n = 0` for `m ≠ n`.
Then `A` is not noetherian.

Let `I n` be the ideal generated by `e m` for `m < n`. This is a monotone chain of ideals.
If `A` were noetherian, Mathlib's `monotone_stabilizes_iff_noetherian` would give an `n` with
`I n = I m` for all `m ≥ n`, so `e n ∈ I (n + 1) = I n`. But every `x ∈ I n` satisfies
`x * e n = 0`: this holds for the generators by orthogonality and is preserved by sums and
multiples (`Submodule.span_induction`). Hence `e n = e n * e n = 0`, a contradiction.

## Restricted products

Fix a family of commutative rings `R j`, each equipped with a topology, with subrings
`A j`, indexed by `ι`, and the cofinite restricted product `Πʳ j, [R j, A j]`.

- `restrictedOneAtPlace A i` is `1 - restrictedZeroAtPlace A i`, where the Library's
  `restrictedZeroAtPlace A i` has coordinate `0` at `i` and `1` elsewhere. So
  `restrictedOneAtPlace A i` has coordinate `1` at `i` and `0` at every other place
  (`restrictedOneAtPlace_apply_self`, `restrictedOneAtPlace_apply_of_ne`).
- Coordinatewise, these tuples are idempotent (`restrictedOneAtPlace_mul_self`), pairwise
  orthogonal at distinct places (`restrictedOneAtPlace_mul_of_ne`), and nonzero when every
  `R j` is a nonzero ring (`restrictedOneAtPlace_ne_zero`, from the coordinate `1 ≠ 0` at `i`).
- `not_isNoetherianRing_restrictedProduct`: if `ι` is infinite and every `R j` is nonzero, then
  the restricted product is not noetherian. Apply the lemma above to
  `restrictedOneAtPlace A (e n)`, where `e : ℕ ↪ ι` is Mathlib's `Infinite.natEmbedding`.

## The two adele rings

- Number fields. `finiteAdeles_not_isNoetherianRing`: the finite adele ring `FiniteAdeles K`
  is the restricted product of the completions `v.adicCompletion K`, which are fields, over the
  finite places, relative to `v.adicCompletionIntegers K`. The finite places form an infinite
  set by the Library's `numberField_finitePlaces_infinite`. `adeles_not_isNoetherianRing`:
  `Adeles K` is the product of the infinite and finite adele rings, and its second projection
  `RingHom.snd` is a surjective ring map. A surjective image of a noetherian ring is noetherian
  (Mathlib's `isNoetherianRing_of_surjective`), so `Adeles K` is not noetherian.
- Function fields. `curvePlaceAdeleRing_not_isNoetherianRing`: `CurvePlaceAdeleRing k F` is the
  restricted product of the completion fields `curvePlaceCompletion v` relative to
  `curvePlaceCompletionIntegers v`, over the discrete places of `F` trivial on `k`. These form
  an infinite set by the Library's `functionField_places_infinite`, which holds for an arbitrary
  constant field `k` and needs no separability hypothesis.

## Assembly

`adeleRingNotNoetherian` pairs the two cases. The proof uses only the standard axioms
`propext`, `Classical.choice` and `Quot.sound`. No topology enters the argument: the topologies
on the completions are present only because the Library's restricted-product tuples are
defined in that setting.
