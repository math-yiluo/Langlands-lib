# Injectivity from a distinct-character constant-term expansion

Fix a parameter and a subspace of sections. Suppose the actual
constant term of the Eisenstein series is a finite sum of components,
each transforming under translation by its own character of the
exponent group. If the characters are distinct, the sum determines
each component. Indeed, for each evaluation point, translate that
point through the exponent group. The scalar relation obtained from
vanishing of the sum is a linear combination of distinct multiplicative
characters. Dedekind independence makes every coefficient zero.

If one component is the original section times the height power,
that component is injective since the height power is nonzero
everywhere. Equality of two actual Eisenstein series then implies
equality of their constant terms, of this component, and of the sections.

This supplies the character-separation implication used for generic
injectivity in R. P. Langlands, *On the notion of an automorphic
representation* (1979), proof of Proposition 2, printed pp. 206–207,
PDF pp. 201–202 of Corvallis Part I
(`langlands-1979-notion-automorphic-representation`). The expected
geometric input is the expansion in J. Bernstein and E. Lapid,
*On the meromorphic continuation of Eisenstein series* (2024),
§6.10, formula (6.17), journal p. 208, PDF p. 22
(`bernstein-lapid-2024-meromorphic-continuation-eisenstein`).
The expansion, the identity component and the distinctness of the
characters are hypotheses of this implication.
