# Arthur truncation for every compact Iwasawa subgroup

`Langlands.Global.ArthurTruncation.iwasawaTruncationIdempotent` proves the universe-polymorphic `IwasawaTruncationIdempotentStatement`. The compact subgroup `K` is any subgroup satisfying `P₀(𝔸) K = G(𝔸)` and compactness. There is a common regularity constant for the given data. For each parameter above it, each measurable locally bounded rational-invariant input, and each standard testing parabolic, the constant term of the truncation vanishes at every point where one of the testing weights is strictly positive. Applying truncation twice gives the first truncation pointwise.

The proof expands the constant term in Bruhat cells using the integration identity of the geometric lemma. Uniform finiteness on compact sets gives an absolute majorant on each full radical orbit, and measurable nested averaging identifies the inner terms. The finite parabolic sum is then grouped by Weyl element and by the smaller Levi index. The rational coset space and radical measure agree within each group of terms. The regularity bound and the uniform Weyl height comparison make the transported testing weight strictly positive. Finite cutoff alternation then gives zero inside each common integral.

The Weyl comparison uses an actual maximal-parabolic eigenline and the compact-factor bounds for primitive adelic heights. Its additive error is absorbed by the regularity of the truncation parameter, with exact covariance in the stabilizing Weyl subgroup. Rational invariance of the first truncation allows a second application. Every proper-parabolic term then vanishes on its active cutoff and the full-group term gives back the first truncation.

The source statement is James Arthur, *An introduction to the trace formula* (2005), Proposition 13.1(a),(b), pp. 68–69, in `arthur-ellwood-kottwitz-2005-harmonic-analysis-shimura`. The cancellation follows Arthur, *A trace formula for reductive groups II* (1980), Lemma 1.1 and Corollary 1.2, pp. 89–91, in `arthur-1980-trace-formula-reductive-groups-II`. The implemented unfolding uses Bernstein–Lapid, *On the meromorphic continuation of Eisenstein series* (2024), §6.10 and equation (6.17), pp. 208–209, in `bernstein-lapid-2024-meromorphic-continuation-eisenstein-published`.
