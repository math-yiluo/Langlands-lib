# Cuspidal tests of convergent Eisenstein series with a uniform exponent degree

Module `Langlands.Global.AdelicEisensteinUniformCuspidalExpansionStatement`. It contains one statement, `Langlands.Global.EisensteinUniformCuspidalExpansionStatement`, and no proof.

## Sources

- J. Bernstein and E. Lapid, *On the meromorphic continuation of Eisenstein series*, J. Amer. Math. Soc. 37 (2024), 187–234 (literature id `bernstein-lapid-2024-meromorphic-continuation-eisenstein`): §6.10, the constant-term formulas (6.17) and (6.18) and their consequence (6.20), pp. 207–209; the norm-one cuspidal tests (6.8) of §6.2, p. 202; Corollary 6.10 and its use in the proof of Proposition 8.4, §8.4, pp. 223–224.

Provenance: derived. The statement is the case of the identity Weyl element of the published `ConvergentCuspidalTestExpansionStatement` (module `AdelicConvergentCuspidalTestExpansionStatement`), with three changes explained below: the subtracted term is written as the twist, the statement is uniform over finite linear combinations of fixed inducing functions, and the polynomial degree is bounded uniformly.

## Statement

The binders are those of `ConvergentCuspidalTestExpansionStatement`, without the second index, the Weyl representative and the Levi conjugation. That is: a number field \(F\); an affine group scheme \(G\) of finite type; a compatible standard family `fam`; a standard index \(I\), the inducing parabolic \(P_I\); a maximal compact \(K\) with `hK : IsIwasawaCompact (fam.p I) K`, Iwasawa coverage for the minimal standard parabolic, and maximal compact intersections with the standard Levis; a closed matrix embedding `rho`; and the smooth structure `hsmooth`. Here `r` is the presentation of \(P_I\) given by the family.

Fix finitely many functions \(\varphi_0,\dots,\varphi_{m-1}\) automorphic on \(U_I(\mathbb A)M_I(F)\backslash G(\mathbb A)\) (`fam.IsAutomorphicOn hsmooth Kinf rho I (φ i)`). For every standard index \(J\) there are a threshold \(R\), competing Weyl data `data : CompetingWeylExponents fam I J 1` for the identity element, and a natural number \(n\) with the following property. Let \(\lambda\) be \(R\)-regular (`r.IsRegularParameter hA hT R λ`) and let \(a_0,\dots,a_{m-1}\in\mathbb C\) be arbitrary. Then there are functions `terms k`, \(k<\) `data.size`, such that:

1. **Uniform degree.** For every \(k\), `fam.HasPolynomialOrbit J (data.label k λ) n (terms k)` (module `AdelicExponentPolynomials`): for every \(g\in G(\mathbb A)\), the function \(a\mapsto (\texttt{terms}\ k)(a g)\,a^{-\mathrm{label}_k(\lambda)}\) on the positive split component \(\mathfrak A_J\) is a polynomial of degree at most \(n\) in \(H_J(a)\). Here \(\mathrm{label}_k(\lambda)=\) `data.label k λ` is the affine label of the \(k\)-th competing term. This is the condition of `fam.HasUnnormalizedExponent J (data.label k λ) (terms k)` with the degree fixed to \(n\).
2. **Tested identity.** For every Levi cusp form `test` on \(M_J\) (`fam.IsLeviCuspForm Kinf rho J test`) and every \(g\), each tested term is absolutely integrable on the norm-one rational quotient of \(M_J\), and
   \[
   \Bigl\langle C_J\sum_ia_iE(\varphi_i,\lambda),\texttt{test}\Bigr\rangle_g-\Bigl\langle C_{I,J}\sum_ia_i\varphi_{i,\lambda},\texttt{test}\Bigr\rangle_g=\sum_k\langle \texttt{terms}\ k,\texttt{test}\rangle_g .
   \]
   Here \(\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_g\) is `fam.leviPairing J · g test`, \(C_J\) is `fam.constantTerm J`, \(C_{I,J}\) is `fam.parabolicConstantTerm I J` (zero unless \(J\le I\)), \(E(\varphi_i,\lambda)\) is `hK.eisensteinSeries (φ i) λ` and \(\varphi_{i,\lambda}\) is `hK.parameterTwist (φ i) λ`. The sums are taken pointwise on \(G(\mathbb A)\).

**Uniformity.** The threshold \(R\), the data and the bound \(n\) depend on the family \((\varphi_i)\) and on \(J\), but neither on \(\lambda\) nor on the coefficients \(a_i\). The terms depend on \(\lambda\) and on the \(a_i\). For \(m=1\) and \(a_0=1\) this is Corollary 6.10 for \(\varphi=\varphi_0\) with a uniform degree.

## Relation to the published (6.20) statement

- **The identity Weyl element.** Take the second index \(Q=I\), the Weyl representative \(1\) and the identity Levi conjugation (`isWeylConjugation_one`, module `AdelicSelfWeylParameters`) in `ConvergentCuspidalTestExpansionStatement`. Its subtracted term is the \(J\)-constant term of the intertwining integral `hK.intertwiningIntegral (fam.iU I) (fam.iU I ≫ fam.p I) 1 φ λ`. By `IsIwasawaCompact.intertwiningIntegral_one_self` (module `AdelicIntertwiningIdentity`), that integral equals the twist \(\varphi_\lambda\) at every point, for every \(\varphi\) invariant under \(U_I(\mathbb A)\). The statement here writes \(\varphi_\lambda\) directly. Its competing labels are those of Weyl elements \(w\neq1\) (field `competing`), which are the factors \(w\neq e\) of (8.2b).
- **Finite families.** Put \(\varphi=\sum_ia_i\varphi_i\). It is automorphic on \(U_I(\mathbb A)M_I(F)\backslash G(\mathbb A)\), and \(\varphi_\lambda=\sum_ia_i\varphi_{i,\lambda}\). In the region of absolute convergence, \(E(\varphi,\lambda)=\sum_ia_iE(\varphi_i,\lambda)\). By (6.18), the competing terms of \(\varphi\) come from the cuspidal components of the constant terms of \(\varphi\). These are the corresponding combinations of the cuspidal components for the \(\varphi_i\), so their exponents lie in the finite union of the exponent lists of the \(\varphi_i\), with multiplicities bounded by the largest multiplicity among the \(\varphi_i\). Hence one list of competing labels and one degree bound serve all coefficients.
- **The uniform degree.** In the published statement the polynomial degree of each competing term exists separately for each \(\lambda\). Here one \(n\) serves all \(R\)-regular \(\lambda\). Bernstein–Lapid's equations (8.2b) need this: they apply products of difference operators of a fixed total degree, with the multiplicities of the exponent lists \(\underline\mu_{P_w}\) of the cuspidal components of \(\varphi\), to all \(\lambda\) at once. In (6.18) each competing term is an Eisenstein-type series along \(J\) of an intertwining operator \(M(w,\lambda)\) applied to the cuspidal component of a constant term of \(\varphi\). Its exponent along \(\mathfrak A_J\) is \(w(\mu+\lambda)\), with \(\mu\) an exponent of that fixed cuspidal component. The polynomial part in \(H_J\) comes from the generalized-eigenspace structure of that component, which does not depend on \(\lambda\), so its degree is bounded independently of \(\lambda\).

## Use

The statement is intended as the source of clause 3 of `EisensteinCuspidalTestSystemStatement` (module `AdelicEisensteinCuspidalTestSystemStatement`): at regular parameters, the Eisenstein series satisfies the cuspidal tests. That is Bernstein–Lapid Corollary 6.10 as used in the proof of Proposition 8.4. The difference operators are formed as follows, with the raw labels evaluated at the lift of the translation point to the split component of the tested parabolic.

- **(8.2b).** Raise the raw linear factor of each competing label to the power \(n+1\) and apply the product to the tested difference of the statement. The terms are annihilated, so the tested constant term of the Eisenstein series and that of the identity term satisfy the same non-homogeneous equations. The degree of the product does not depend on the parameter.
- **(8.3).** The homogeneous equations of clause 2 of that statement apply a polynomial to the constant term itself, not to the difference. When the tested parabolic is contained in the inducing parabolic, the product must therefore also contain the factors of the cuspidal projection of the identity term \(C_{I,J}\varphi_\lambda\), with the degree bounds of its exponent decomposition. These do not come from this statement: they come from the exponent decompositions of the cuspidal projections of the constant terms of the fixed \(\varphi_i\), shifted by the restriction of \(\lambda\). Without them the product can fail: for \(G=\mathbb G_m\) over \(\mathbb Q\), \(I=J=G\), the inducing function \(1\) and \(\lambda=0\), there are no competing labels, while the constant term \(1\) has a nonzero pairing with the cusp test \(1\). The uniformity in the coefficients is needed when the inducing function itself depends on \(\lambda\) through finitely many entire coefficients. For example, `EisensteinFiniteCompactChangeStatement` (module `AdelicEisensteinFiniteCompactChangeStatement`) writes the twist for one Iwasawa compact as \(\sum_v e^{\langle\lambda,v\rangle}\) times the twists of finitely many fixed functions for another compact.
