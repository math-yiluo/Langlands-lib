# Joint center and compact enveloping orbits

Author: Worker 94. Module: `Langlands.Global.ArchimedeanJointFinite`.
All declarations are in `Langlands.Global.ArchimedeanGL`.

This module constructs the actual joint differential orbit of one complex-valued
smooth function on the archimedean general linear group, for every
`K : Type u` with `Field K` and `NumberField K`, and every natural rank `n`,
including zero. It reuses the published smooth functions, enveloping action,
enveloping center, and compact-generated enveloping subalgebra.

## The concrete product action

Write `Z = EnvelopingCenter K n` and
`B = compactEnvelopingSubalgebra K n`. Here `B` is the complex unital subalgebra
of the actual enveloping algebra generated by the complexified skew-adjoint
matrix Lie directions. It is not an independently chosen algebra of operators.
The imported `envelopingAction` acts by the actual smooth differential operators.

`compactEnvelopingAction` restricts this algebra homomorphism along the subtype
inclusion of `B`; `compactEnvelopingRepresentation` is its underlying monoid
representation. Their `_apply` lemmas give the unchanged ambient action.
`compactOrbitSpan f` is the complex span of all `compactEnvelopingAction b f`.
`IsCompactLieFinite f` means `Module.Finite ℂ` of that span.

`jointRepresentation` is a representation of the **product monoid** `Z × B`:

```text
(z,b) · f = envelopingAction (centerInclusion z * b.val) f
         = centerAction z (compactEnvelopingAction b f).
```

These formulas are `jointRepresentation_apply` and
`jointRepresentation_apply_eq`. Multiplication in the product is componentwise.
To prove its action law, the implementation moves the second central factor
past the first compact factor with the published `centerInclusion_commute`,
reassociates the products, and applies multiplicativity of `envelopingAction`.
Thus no commutation or invariance assumption is added.
`jointRepresentation_center` and `jointRepresentation_compact` recover the
existing center action at `(z,1)` and the restricted compact action at `(1,b)`.
The construction does not claim that multiplication `Z × B → U` is a
homomorphism of product algebras; the required structure is a monoid
representation.

## Orbit and finite vectors

`jointOrbitSpan f` is `FiniteVectors.orbitSpan` for this product representation.
In particular, it is precisely the complex linear span of
`envelopingAction (centerInclusion z * b.val) f` over all pairs `(z,b)`.
This uses individual products as generators and then takes their linear span.

`jointAction_mem_jointOrbitSpan` and `self_mem_jointOrbitSpan` give membership
of every such translate and of `f`. `jointOrbitSubrepresentation` bundles the
same span with invariance under every joint action.
`jointOrbitSpan_le` states minimality: every invariant subrepresentation
containing `f` contains its joint orbit span. These constructions and laws
specialize the published general `Langlands.FiniteVectors` API.

`IsJointFinite f` is the proposition
`Module.Finite ℂ (jointOrbitSpan f)`. Since the scalar ring is the complex
field, this says that this orbit span has finite dimension.
`isJointFinite_iff` characterizes it by existence of a finite-dimensional
joint subrepresentation containing `f`.
`jointFiniteFunctions` is the actual complex submodule of such functions,
obtained from the generic finite-vector subrepresentation;
`mem_jointFiniteFunctions` gives its membership predicate.
`isJointFinite_jointAction`, `isJointFinite_centerAction`, and
`isJointFinite_compactEnvelopingAction` prove stability of this condition under
the indicated actual actions.

`centerOrbitSpan_le_jointOrbitSpan` and
`compactOrbitSpan_le_jointOrbitSpan` insert the missing factor as the unit.
The resulting subspace inclusions give `isCenterFinite_of_isJointFinite`
and `isCompactLieFinite_of_isJointFinite`, by finite-dimensionality of
subspaces. No reverse implication is proved here.

## Original source and derived provenance

Harish-Chandra, *Discrete series for semisimple Lie groups II: Explicit
determination of the characters*, Acta Mathematica **116** (1966), section 8,
Theorem 1, printed and one-based PDF pp.18–19, motivates this prerequisite.
On p.18 the source defines the compact-direction enveloping subalgebra as
the one generated by the unit and the complexified compact Lie algebra.
Its hypothesis says that the differential translates by products from the
ambient enveloping center and this compact subalgebra span a finite-dimensional
space. The theorem then constructs a smooth compactly supported reproducing
kernel with compact-conjugation invariance.

The Lean module isolates the algebraic orbit in that hypothesis, specializing
to scalar-valued functions and to the already constructed archimedean matrix
objects. Its product-monoid packaging and generic finite-vector API are internal
formalization choices. The construction laws above use only the actual
algebra action, central commutation, and linear span; they do not invoke the
source's reproduction theorem or its analytic proof.

## Scope

No finite-dimensionality is imposed on the entire smooth-function space.
This module does not assert that any given automorphic form or compact-group
finite function is joint finite. In particular, compact Lie finiteness here
is not identified with finiteness under `standardInfiniteCompact`; that
identification requires an analytic group-to-differential bridge.
The source's connected reductive and compact subgroup setup is not asserted
for the actual general linear group by these definitions. No topology,
admissibility, elliptic regularity, reproduction, convolution differentiation,
or polynomial derivative-growth theorem is proved. The parent
`AutomorphicDifferentialGrowthStatement` remains unchanged and open.
