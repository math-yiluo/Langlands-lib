# Minimal Iwasawa height in relative split coordinates

The ambient minimal Iwasawa height of a product \(n b m k\), with \(n\) in the radical of a standard parabolic, \(b\) in its ambient norm-one positive split component, \(m\) in the intrinsic norm-one Levi, and \(k\) in the Iwasawa compact, is the sum of the heights of \(b\) and \(m\). The first summand lies in the ambient coroot span and annihilates the Levi simple roots. The second lies in the Levi coroot span.

To identify the second summand, factor the canonical minimal parabolic into the supplied standard parabolic. For a common compact, restriction of characters along this factor gives the parabolic height from the minimal height. In the maximal-torus coordinates the restriction is `splitDual`. Its kernel is the Levi coroot span because the real characters of the Levi restrict to the perpendicular space of that span and lift to the parabolic by the Levi retraction. The parabolic height of an intrinsic norm-one Levi point vanishes by functoriality of the character logarithm.

The split height follows from the projected simple-coroot coordinates of the relative positive group. The relative radical factors into the minimal parabolic and has trivial character logarithm there. The positive split point also belongs to the minimal parabolic, so its height adds by left multiplication. Right multiplication by the compact leaves the height unchanged. These are the height identities underlying the split and Levi factors of Delorme, *On the spectral theorem of Langlands*, Advances in Mathematics 426 (2023), 109107, (7.2), (7.7) and (7.23).
