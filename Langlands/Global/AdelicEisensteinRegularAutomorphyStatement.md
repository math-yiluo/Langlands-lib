## Statement

The binders are those of `EisensteinRegularUniquenessStatement`, without its finite family of inducing functions and its competing exponent data:

- a number field \(F\) and an affine group scheme \(G\) of finite type over \(F\);
- a standard parabolic family `fam` (module `AdelicLeadingCuspidalComponents`), which includes the connected reductivity of \(G\), and a standard index \(I\), with the parabolic \(P_I\) embedded by the closed immersion `fam.p I`, finiteness of the relative Weyl group and the monomorphism instance for `fam.f I`;
- a subgroup \(K\) of \(G(\mathbb A)\) with `hK : IsIwasawaCompact (fam.p I) K`, that is \(P_I(\mathbb A)K=G(\mathbb A)\);
- a closed matrix embedding `rho` (`IsClosedSubgroup rho`) of any size and the smooth structure `hsmooth`.

The hypotheses on \(K\) are:

- it is a maximal compact subgroup of \(G(\mathbb A)\) (`IsMaximalCompactSubgroup`);
- it satisfies Iwasawa coverage for the minimal standard parabolic (index \(\emptyset\));
- it meets every standard Levi \(M_J(\mathbb A)\) in a maximal compact subgroup of \(M_J(\mathbb A)\), the comap along `fam.l J ≫ fam.p J`.

**Assertion.** Let \(\varphi\) be automorphic on \(X_{P_I}=U_I(\mathbb A)M_I(F)\backslash G(\mathbb A)\) for the archimedean part \(K_\infty\) of \(K\) (the comap of \(K\) along `ofInfinite`) and for `rho`: `fam.IsAutomorphicOn hsmooth Kinf rho I φ`, which uses the canonical commutator datum `adelicDifferentialCommutator_of_smooth`. Then there is a real number \(R\) such that for every \(\lambda\in X^*(P_I)\otimes\mathbb C\) (`ComplexCharacterParameter F (fam.P I)`) with `r.IsRegularParameter hA hT R λ`, the Eisenstein series `hK.eisensteinSeries φ λ` is automorphic on \(G(F)\backslash G(\mathbb A)\): `IsAutomorphicFunctionOn` for the rational subgroup `rationalSubgroup F G`, the same \(K_\infty\) and `rho`, and the canonical commutator datum.

Here `r` is the root presentation `⟨⟨fam.T, fam.i, fam.ν, I.val, fam.f I⟩, fam.isCompatible I⟩` of \(P_I\), and `hA`, `hT` are the split-torus witnesses of the family, as in `EisensteinRegularUniquenessStatement`. The predicate `r.IsRegularParameter hA hT R λ` (module `AdelicEisensteinConvergenceStatement`) says that \(R<B(\operatorname{Re}\lambda|_{A_I},\alpha)\) for every root \(\alpha\) of the split component \(A_I\) in the unipotent radical, where \(B\) is the positive definite Weyl-induced form. The series `hK.eisensteinSeries φ λ` (module `AdelicEisensteinSeries`) is the unconditional sum over \(P_I(F)\backslash G(F)\) of the terms \(\varphi_\lambda(\gamma g)\), with \(\varphi_\lambda(g)=\varphi(g)\exp\langle\lambda,H_{P_I}(g)\rangle\) (`hK.parameterTwist φ λ`) and \(H_{P_I}\) the Iwasawa height of \(K\).

## Correspondence with the source

- **Setting.** Bernstein–Lapid fix one minimal parabolic, work with the standard parabolics \(P\in\mathcal P\), and fix \(K\) in good position. The family `fam` gives the standard parabolics \(P_J\) of one minimal parabolic \(P_\emptyset\). The hypotheses on \(K\) are the properties of good position that §2.1 states: Iwasawa coverage \(G(\mathbb A)=P_J(\mathbb A)K\) for every standard \(J\), which follows from the coverage for \(P_\emptyset\subseteq P_J\), and maximality of \(K\cap M_J(\mathbb A)\) in \(M_J(\mathbb A)\). The statement does not assume the remaining conditions of good position in Mœglin–Waldspurger I.1.4. The explanation below uses only \(P_I(\mathbb A)K=G(\mathbb A)\), the maximality of \(K\) and the twist statement `ParameterTwistAutomorphyStatement`, which has the same hypotheses on \(K\).
- **Inducing data.** Bernstein–Lapid's Theorem 2.3 (1) and §2.2 concern every \(\varphi\in\mathcal A_P\). Mœglin–Waldspurger II.1.5 concerns an isotypic \(\varphi_\pi\). Its majorant uses a central character: at the end of printed p. 86 the centre of \(M\) acts on \(\lambda\varphi_\pi\) through the central character of \(\pi\otimes\lambda\otimes\rho_P\), in the book's normalization, which gives on p. 87 a bound by \(c\,m_P(g)^{\operatorname{Re}\pi+\operatorname{Re}\lambda}E^M(g)\) with a single exponent. A general automorphic \(\varphi\) need not transform by a character of the centre, so II.1.5 alone does not give the general case. The general case is Bernstein–Lapid's assertion.
- **Regularity.** The source's region, \(\langle\operatorname{Re}\lambda,\alpha^\vee\rangle\gg0\) for the simple roots \(\alpha\in\Delta_P\), is expressed by `IsRegularParameter`: large values of \(B(\operatorname{Re}\lambda|_{A_I},\alpha)\) for all roots \(\alpha\) of \(A_I\) in the unipotent radical, as in `EisensteinAbsoluteConvergenceStatement`. Both describe parameters sufficiently regular in the positive chamber. The threshold is existential, so this normalization does not matter.
- **Automorphy.** The Library's `IsAutomorphicFunctionOn` (module `GroupSchemeInvariantForms`, after Mœglin–Waldspurger I.2.17) consists of left invariance under the given subgroup, smoothness with an open subgroup of \(G(\mathbb A_f)\) fixing the function, finiteness under the given archimedean compact subgroup, finiteness under the center of the enveloping algebra, and moderate growth for the norm of `rho`. Membership in \(\mathcal A_G\) is this predicate for `rationalSubgroup F G` and \(K_\infty\).

## Why it holds

This explains the truth of the statement; the module contains no proof. For \(N=0\) a closed immersion of \(G\) into \(\mathrm{GL}_0\), the trivial group, makes \(G\) trivial, and the assertion is immediate. Let \(N>0\).

- **Growth, smoothness and derivatives.** The Library's `eisensteinCommonGrowth` (module `AdelicEisensteinCommonGrowthProof`) applies to every maximal compact \(K\) with \(P_I(\mathbb A)K=G(\mathbb A)\) and every automorphic inducing function. It gives a threshold \(R_0\) such that for \(R_0\)-regular \(\lambda\) the series \(E(\varphi,\lambda)\) is the function of an element of the space `UniformGrowthFrechetSpace`. That element is a smooth function on \(G(F)\backslash G(\mathbb A)\), invariant on the right under a compact open subgroup of \(G(\mathbb A_f)\), with uniform moderate growth. Its proof bounds every enveloping-algebra derivative of the terms \(\varphi_\lambda(\gamma g)\) by a summable majorant at that threshold, so the derivative series converge locally uniformly on every infinite slice and the derivatives of \(E(\varphi,\lambda)\) are the sums of the derivatives of the terms. This gives left \(G(F)\)-invariance, smoothness and moderate growth.
- **\(K_\infty\)-finiteness.** The height \(H_{P_I}\) is right \(K\)-invariant (`IsIwasawaCompact.height_mul_right`). So for \(k\in K_\infty\) the right translate of \(E(\varphi,\lambda)\) by \(k\) is \(E(R(k)\varphi,\lambda)\), the Eisenstein series of the right translate of \(\varphi\), whose series converges at the same \(\lambda\). The translates \(R(k)\varphi\) span a finite-dimensional space, and the convergent series is linear in the inducing function. So the translates of \(E(\varphi,\lambda)\) span a finite-dimensional space.
- **Finiteness under the center.** By `ParameterTwistAutomorphyStatement` (Bernstein–Lapid §2.2, \(\varphi_\lambda\in\mathcal A_P\)), one ideal \(\mathcal J\) of finite codimension in the center of the enveloping algebra annihilates every infinite slice of \(\varphi_\lambda\). The infinite slice of the term \(g\mapsto\varphi_\lambda(\gamma g)\) at a finite adelic point \(b\) is the left translate by \(\gamma_\infty\) of the slice of \(\varphi_\lambda\) at \(\gamma_f b\). Central elements act by bi-invariant differential operators, which commute with left translation. So \(\mathcal J\) annihilates the slices of every term, and, the derivatives of the sum being the sums of the derivatives, also the slices of \(E(\varphi,\lambda)\).

## Use

Bernstein–Lapid's (6.22) characterizes \(E(\varphi,\lambda)\), at sufficiently regular \(\lambda\), as the unique automorphic form on \(G(F)\backslash G(\mathbb A)\) satisfying (6.21). The comparison of a given automorphic solution \(v\) with \(E(\varphi,\lambda)\) applies Theorem 6.3 (`LeadingCuspidalInjectivityStatement`, module `AdelicLeadingCuspidalInjectivityStatement`) to the two functions, and that statement concerns automorphic forms on \(G(F)\backslash G(\mathbb A)\). The present statement supplies the automorphy of \(E(\varphi,\lambda)\) in the setting of `EisensteinRegularUniquenessStatement`. For a finite family of inducing functions, the maximum of the thresholds serves all of them, and automorphic forms are closed under finite linear combinations.
