# Transitivity of normalized adelic constant terms

Mœglin–Waldspurger, *Spectral Decomposition and Eisenstein Series* (1995), I.3.4, p. 45, use compatibility of the cuspidal components of a function and of its constant term along a larger parabolic. Bernstein–Lapid state the constant-term identity explicitly in §6.2, after (6.3) and before (6.4). This module proves it for the Library's compatible standard family:

`constantTerm_constantTerm_of_le`: if \(Q\subseteq P\), then \(C_Q(C_P f)=C_Q f\) for every continuous left \(G(F)\)-invariant complex function \(f\) on \(G(\mathbb A_F)\).

The identity is on actual adelic points, uses exactly the measures in `subgroupConstantTerm` (Bernstein–Lapid, §6.2, p. 201, with the mass normalization of §2.2, p. 191 n. 1), and allows \(Q=P\). It does not require any growth hypothesis. This is a derived part of the transitivity argument, not the cuspidal projection or the vanishing criterion in I.3.4. It also does not identify the outer average with a single average over the extra radical in the Levi of \(P\).

## Adelic subgroup functors and rational change of variables

`adelicInclusion` is the inclusion of point groups associated with a containment of subgroup functors \(U\le V\). It maps the principal image of \(U(F)\) into the principal image of \(V(F)\) by restricting the same ambient principal map. If \(V\) normalizes \(U\) on every test scheme, `adelicNormalizerConj` restricts ambient conjugation by a point of \(V(\mathbb A_F)\) to a continuous group automorphism of \(U(\mathbb A_F)\). The inverse is conjugation by the inverse point. For the principal image of a rational point of \(V\), conjugation preserves the actual rational subgroup of \(U\): one conjugates before applying the principal map, and the equality is functorial. This last assertion is not made for arbitrary adelic conjugators.

For a closed adelic subgroup with compact rational quotient, `measurePreserving_rationalQuotientAutomorphism` uses the generic uniqueness of normalized right-invariant probability from `CompactQuotientSubgroupAverage`. The rational subgroup is discrete, and its counting measure is Haar and regular. The intrinsic quotient measure is regular, a probability, and right invariant by the compact-quotient Weil theorem. Thus an automorphism respecting the rational subgroup preserves the exact measure.

## Constant-term proof

`normalizedConstantTerm` applies the published descended integral to the intrinsic normalized measure on the rational quotient. At a represented subgroup the value agrees with the chosen-representative definition `subgroupConstantTerm`, since the descended function evaluated at the class of its chosen representative has the same value. For compact rational quotient the integrand is jointly continuous in the ambient argument and the quotient variable (by an open quotient map), so the integral is continuous. Right invariance of the quotient probability gives left invariance of the constant term by the adelic subgroup itself. For the principal image of a normalizing rational point, left invariance instead uses the inverse conjugation change of variables and the left rational invariance of the input.

For \(U\le V\) with \(U\) normalized by \(V\), both adelic image groups closed and both rational quotients compact, take the continuous descended function \([v]\mapsto f(vg)\) on the outer quotient. The inner fiber average of that function evaluated at \([v]\) is the value of the normalized constant term along \(U\) at \(vg\), by associativity. The normalization and conjugation conditions of the compact averaging identity hold by the preceding argument. Integrating this fiber average over the outer quotient leaves its integral unchanged. This gives `integral_normalizedConstantTerm_eq_of_isNormalizedBy`, with the outer integral over the actual rational quotient of \(V\) even though the intermediate function need not descend by all of \(G(F)\).

For the standard-family specialization, identify the supplied radical with the standard unipotent subgroup functor by the dynamic comparison for a standard cocharacter. Standard parabolic order then gives \(U_P\le U_Q\); it also gives \(U_Q\le Q\le P\), so \(U_Q\) normalizes \(U_P\). The represented smooth connected unipotent quotient theorem gives compactness for both radicals. These facts discharge the generic hypotheses, and the two represented comparisons recover the original `StandardParabolicFamily.constantTerm` without a separate choice of measures.
