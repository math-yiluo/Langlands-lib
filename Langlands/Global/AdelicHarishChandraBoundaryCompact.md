# Compact Harish–Chandra restriction to polynomial boundary slabs

`AdelicHarishChandraBoundaryCompactStatement` takes an actual compatible
standard parabolic family, its Siegel setup, an Iwasawa compact for the
minimal parabolic and linear height coordinates of the local-finiteness
system. The simple-root coordinates are scaled by positive steps and the
central zero subspace maps to zero in the ambient character dual. The map
from the torus dual to the minimal-parabolic dual is the one induced by
inclusion. All components use the indicated positive-rank closed matrix
representation and archimedean differential data.

There is a common positive lower bound on the rates in the log weight. For
rates at least that large, the statement applies to any measurable subset
of a slab with fixed lower thresholds on all the roots, upper thresholds on
the roots outside the Levi of the component, and bounds on the absolute
central coordinates. For every nonnegative uniform growth exponent and open
finite right level, each continuous linear family in the corresponding
Fréchet space has a continuous linear Harish–Chandra restriction into the
slab's weighted L² space on the rational minimal-parabolic quotient. The
operator is Hilbert–Schmidt and compact. Its values agree almost everywhere
with the actual component evaluated at a representative.

The proof compares the norm of split-torus log coordinates with the norm in
the given parabolic coordinates using a continuous linear map. The chamber
weight dominates that norm up to a constant when the rates are large enough.
The slab inequalities also give uniform bounds on the auxiliary character
logs and on the roots outside the Levi. Exponentiating the common lower log
bound supplies the simple-root chamber threshold. The uniform directional
growth of the actual Harish–Chandra component then supplies the family
for the weighted relatively cuspidal restriction theorem.

This proves the compact boundary-restriction step of Bernstein–Lapid,
*On the meromorphic continuation of Eisenstein series*, §7.4 and its use in
§§7.6–7.7, for this supplied Iwasawa geometry.
