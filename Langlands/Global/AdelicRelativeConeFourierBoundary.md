# The finite cone Fourier boundary on a relative spectral plane

`RelativeConeFourierBoundaryStatement` is a coordinate form of the compact-test boundary argument in Proposition 7.8 of Delorme's spectral theorem. It considers a holomorphic representative of a finite regularized cone sum, specified by its agreement on a dense regular subset of the full imaginary plane. A smooth chart of a possibly smaller relative plane has imaginary values at zero displacement. In each term the chart gives coroot evaluations of the form \(t d_i + i((e x)_i-\xi_i)\), with \(d_i \ne 0\) and a real linear equivalence \(e\) from the relative spectral coordinates to that term's coroot coordinates.

The conclusion gives the absolute integrability of the representative against a smooth compactly supported test on the relative plane. Its integral is the finite sum of signed translated-orthant integrals of the corresponding polynomial Fourier tests. Each summand uses the pulled-back product of the original test with its holomorphic coefficient at zero displacement, translated by the corresponding frequency \(\xi\). The unshifted pulled-back test is smooth and compactly supported. The positive factors compare the original spectral Haar measure with normalized coroot spectral Lebesgue measure. They depend only on the original spectral measure and the coroot equivalences.

The proof identifies the representative with the finite formula at nonzero denominators in a neighborhood of the full imaginary plane. Compactness then permits a common interval of positive displacements on the support of a given test. Cutoffs in the displacement variable extend the coefficient-weighted tests to globally smooth families with common compact supports. The affine substitution \(y=e x-\xi\) changes the measure by the Haar factor of the linear part, since translation preserves additive Haar measure. The varying-test Fourier boundary gives the signed spatial integral of each term. The integrable terms can be summed before taking the limit, and continuity of the tested representative identifies the limit with the integral at zero.

`relativeConeFourierBoundary_withHaar` also gives the unshifted measure identity \(e_{l*}\mu=c_l\,(2\pi)^{-\dim}\,dy\). The normalization `coordinateSpectralMeasure` is an additive Haar measure, so the identity characterizes each positive factor.

Patrick Delorme, *On the spectral theorem of Langlands*, Advances in Mathematics **426** (2023), 109107, (7.8)–(7.12), pp. 24–25, and Proposition 7.8, p. 33.
