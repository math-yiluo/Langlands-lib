# Split-component shifts and nested constant terms

This module proves the commutation step used to pass from the cuspidal tests to the product of differences in Bernstein–Lapid, *On the meromorphic continuation of Eisenstein series* (2024), §8.1, proof of Lemma 8.1, p. 222. The constant terms are the normalized integrals of §6.2. The module uses **raw left shifts**, without the factor \(\delta_P(a)^{-1/2}\) in (6.2). The passage from normalized characters to the roots for the raw shifts is in `AdelicExponentPolynomials`; no new identification of characters across parabolics or cuspidal separation result is asserted here.

## Integral comparison without conjugating a rational quotient

For a standard parabolic \(P\), `IsRadicalInvariant P f` means left invariance under every actual point of \(U_P(\mathbb A_F)\). For \(Q\subseteq P\), take \(b\) in the image of the positive split component of \(P\) and \(n\in U_Q(\mathbb A_F)\). Then \(n b n^{-1} b^{-1}\in U_P(\mathbb A_F)\). The group-scheme lemma proves the stronger all-test-schemes assertion with any point in the image of \(A_P\) and any point of \(P\). It uses the actual dynamic Levi projection for a standard cocharacter: the image of \(A_P\) is central in the Levi, so the projection of the commutator is one; the kernel is the supplied radical by the standard-cocharacter comparison. For the adelic application \(U_Q\subseteq Q\subseteq P\).

Thus \(n b g=(n b n^{-1}b^{-1})(b n g)\), so \(f(n b g)=f(b n g)\) for a radical-invariant \(f\). This holds pointwise in the chosen representative, proving `constantTerm_unnormalizedAction_of_radicalInvariant` without assuming that \(b\) conjugates any rational subgroup to itself. Raw split-component shifts preserve `IsRadicalInvariant` since \(P\) normalizes its radical, and hence their differences with scalar multiples of the identity preserve it too.

## Differences and products

`HasIntegrableConstantTerm Q f` requires Bochner integrability of the chosen-representative integrand at every ambient argument, for the same quotient measure as `constantTerm`. This extra premise is needed for the linearity of that integral. The pointwise commutation formula identifies the integrand of the translate at \(g\) with the original integrand at \(bg\), so integrability is preserved by a raw difference. Subtracting the integrals gives the intertwining identity for each difference, and induction on a finite list (applying the last factor first) gives `constantTerm_list_prod_unnormalizedExponentDifference`. The shifts and the complex parameters in the list are from \(P\); commutativity of the factors is not needed for this ordered identity.

The module also supplies the input hypotheses for \(f=C_P f_0\) with \(f_0\) continuous and left \(G(F)\)-invariant. Radical invariance is right invariance of the normalized quotient measure. For every \(Q\subseteq P\), the \(Q\)-integrand for \(f\) descends continuously: \(U_Q(F)\) normalizes \(U_P\) on the rational and adelic test schemes, so its principal points preserve the \(P\) constant term by the rational-normalizer change of variables. The outer quotient is compact with its probability measure, giving `hasIntegrableConstantTerm_constantTerm_of_le`. In this application `AdelicConstantTermTransitivity` identifies \(C_Q f\) with \(C_Q f_0\). This allows insertion of the product of split-component differences before the remaining cuspidal tests.
