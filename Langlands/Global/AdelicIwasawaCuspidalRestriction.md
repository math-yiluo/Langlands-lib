# Hilbert–Schmidt cuspidal restrictions on a weighted Iwasawa domain

`AdelicIwasawaCuspidalRestrictionStatement` uses a compatible standard parabolic family over a number field, a Siegel setup for its maximal split torus, and a compact Iwasawa subgroup for the canonical minimal parabolic \(P_0\). There is a continuous full positive torus section of the \(P_0\) logarithm from which to read root and auxiliary character coordinates. Fix a standard parabolic \(P\), auxiliary characters spanning with the simple roots, a weak simple-root threshold, and a fixed open right finite level. Consider a measurable region of \(P_0(F)\backslash G(\mathbb A)\) lying in the weak chamber, with bounded absolute logs for the auxiliary characters and the simple roots outside the Levi root set of \(P\). The log weight on the region must dominate a fixed positive multiple of the torus coordinate norm, up to an additive constant.

A complex Hilbert-space family of point evaluations gives a continuous linear restriction to the actual weighted \(L^2\) space of this region, and this map is Hilbert–Schmidt and compact. The assumed scalar functions are continuous, smooth on archimedean slices, left invariant by \(P_0(F)\) and \(U_P(\mathbb A)\), invariant at the given right level, and relatively cuspidal along \(P\). Every right-orbit derivative word satisfies a bound by a word-dependent constant times the representation height to a common nonnegative exponent times the Hilbert input norm.

The compact-factor formula supplies representatives on the rational-parabolic quotient. The finite slice is compact since intrinsic positive torus points have finite coordinate one. Scalar evaluation agrees with the continuous function descended from the ambient group, so the choice of representative preserves almost-everywhere measurability. The canonical minimal parabolic and the one in the standard family have the same all-test image and the same rational image. The weighted measure is finite by the exponential height estimate. The relative cuspidal restriction theorem therefore applies with inverse height power zero, yielding the actual almost-everywhere scalar evaluations in the target \(L^2\) space.

This gives the boundary restriction step of Bernstein–Lapid, §7.4, pp. 216–217, on the specified weighted Iwasawa region.
