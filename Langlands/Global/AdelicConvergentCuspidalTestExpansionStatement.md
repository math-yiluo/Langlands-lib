# The convergent Eisenstein comparison in Levi cusp tests

This proposition states the scalar version of Bernstein–Lapid (6.20) needed for the functional equation. Fix compatible standard parabolics, a maximal compact with minimal-parabolic Iwasawa coverage and maximal intersections in the standard Levis, and a Weyl representative carrying the two inducing simple-root sets to each other.

At each standard index \(J\), and for sufficiently regular source parameters, take the norm-one Levi pairing of the Eisenstein constant term. Subtract the same pairing of the \(J\)-constant term of the selected twisted intertwining operator. The Library's intertwining integral is the selected operator with its target twist included. The parabolic constant term of this inducing expression is zero for an index outside the target parabolic.

The difference is a finite sum of absolutely convergent pairings. Its labels are the affine unnormalized exponents coming from the competing Weyl terms. Each label's ambient real linear part is the corresponding Weyl transform of the source parameter. The label's constant offset includes the change from normalized to unnormalized exponents. The Weyl elements send the source Levi simple roots to positive roots and exclude the selected representative. The terms may vary with the regular parameter, while the finite affine label data stay fixed.

Bernstein–Lapid §6.10, (6.18) and (6.20), gives the cuspidal constant-term identity and its exponent consequence before taking the norm-one tests of §6.2, (6.8).
