# Proof of the finite change of Iwasawa compact

Maximal compactness identifies the full adelic compact with the product of its infinite and finite projections. Its finite projection is maximal compact in the finite adelic group. The intersection of the conjugates of a compact open subgroup under a compact subgroup is compact, and the tube lemma makes it open. Multiplying by this intersection gives a compact overgroup of the finite projection, so maximality gives openness of the projection. Two maximal compacts with the same infinite projection therefore have an intersection that is relatively open in either compact.

The height difference is invariant under left parabolic translation and under right translation by the compact intersection. Its range is attained on the first compact, where local constancy gives finite range. Continuity of the heights gives clopen fibers. Projecting an Iwasawa factorization to the infinite factor expresses the height difference as a function of the finite coordinate alone.

The inducing functions are the original function cut along these fibers. Each cut respects left invariance and an open finite level. It keeps each infinite slice or makes it zero, preserving smoothness and the same central annihilator. The cutoff commutes with right translation by the infinite subgroup, so the span of the new orbit is contained in the image of the original finite-dimensional span. The same pointwise moderate growth estimate bounds each cut. Finally, on every fiber, the difference of heights yields the corresponding exponential scalar in the parameter twist.
