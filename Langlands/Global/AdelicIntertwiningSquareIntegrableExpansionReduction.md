# Reduction of square-integrable intertwining expansions

`intertwiningSquareIntegrableExpansionReduction` derives the published square-integrable expansion target from three analytic inputs. They are preservation of the exact square-integrable predicate by the integral on a regular region, finite meromorphic continuation in automorphic forms, and the published imaginary unitarity statement.

The regular square-integrability premise uses the same data as the target. Its conclusion is membership in the target square-integrable submodule for every regular parameter, at a threshold allowed to depend on the source form and data.

Intersect the span from the continuation with the published square-integrable submodule and choose a finite basis of the intersection. A continuous linear left inverse to basis synthesis gives each new coefficient as a finite linear combination of the old coefficients. It therefore preserves the common meromorphic domain. At every parameter regular for both inputs, the actual integral belongs to the intersection, so this new expansion equals it there.

The two compatible Haar measures come from the Iwasawa measure existence proof. Character restriction to a rational Levi is bijective, the Weyl conjugation is an isomorphism, and the target Levi retraction gives the inverse character restriction. Tensoring the resulting character isomorphism with the reals gives transported coordinates. A normalized compact weight on the common coordinate space suffices for both measures. Applying imaginary unitarity to one source form produces a domain containing the imaginary axis.

In a complex linear chart, the actual integral and the improved expansion are continuous on the intersection of the regular region and the improved holomorphy domain. They agree on its dense intersection with the original domain, so they agree throughout. Outside the improved domain use the original coefficients. This keeps meromorphy on the improved domain and gives equality at every regular parameter, including when the regularity condition has no roots. For an empty spanning family the expansion is zero wherever the original expansion is valid.

This is a derived reduction for Lapid's working hypothesis in §2, pp. 311–312. The three input propositions remain premises. The proof uses the asserted continuations at exactly the presented parabolics and the existing holomorphy theorem for the actual integral.
