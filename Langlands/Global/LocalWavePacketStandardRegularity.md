# Standard regularity and regularity for a standard root presentation

Module `Langlands.Global.LocalWavePacketStandardRegularity`, namespace
`Langlands.Global.StandardParabolicFamily`. It is a helper module for the proof
`Langlands.Global.truncatedWavePacketStandardization` and contains no statement of its own.

## Result

`exists_isRegularParameter_of_coroot_lt`: let `fam` be a standard parabolic family with maximal
split torus `T`, generic cocharacter `ν` and simple roots `Δ`, let `D` be reflection data for `T`,
`J ⊆ Δ` and `R₁ ∈ ℝ`. There is `R` such that every parameter `λ` of `P_J` with
`⟨Re λ|_T, α^∨⟩ > R` for all `α ∈ Δ ∖ J` (coroots `α^∨ = corootVector D α`) is `R₁`-regular for
the root presentation `fam.rootPresentation J`: `B(Re λ|_{A_J}, α) > R₁` for every root `α` of
`A_J` in the unipotent radical (`ParabolicRootPresentation.IsRegularParameter`).

`IsStandardRegularParameter D J R λ` (module
`Langlands.Global.AdelicTruncatedEisensteinAsymptoticsStatement`) is exactly the hypothesis, with
`restrictToTorus` in place of `fam.torusRestrictReal`; the two restrictions agree for the family
`StandardParabolicFamily.ofMaximalSplitTorus`.

## Proof

Write `y = Re λ|_T` and `B` for the Weyl-invariant form on `X^*(T) ⊗ ℝ`.

1. Root forms: for a positive relative root `χ`, `B(Re λ|_{A_J}, χ|_{A_J}) = B(y, χ)`
   (`StandardParabolicFamily.realRootFunctional_eq`).
2. Reflection formula: `2 B(x, α) = ⟨x, α^∨⟩ B(α, α)` for every relative root `α` and any reflection
   data (`RelativeRootReflectionData.two_mul_invariantForm_root`); `B(α, α) > 0` because
   `⟨α, α^∨⟩ = 2`.
3. `y` annihilates the coroots of `J` (`torusRestrictReal_mem_corootAnnihilator`), so `B(y, x) = 0`
   for `x` in the additive monoid generated by `J` (`invariantForm_root_eq_zero`).
4. Let `c = 1 / (1 + ∑_{β ∈ Δ} 2 / B(β, β))`, so `0 < c ≤ B(β, β)/2` for all simple `β`, and
   `R = max(0, R₁) / c`. For simple `β ∉ J`, step 2 gives `B(y, β) > R B(β, β)/2 ≥ max(0, R₁)`.
   Induction over the additive monoid generated by `Δ` shows that every element either lies in the
   monoid generated by `J` or has `B(y, x) > max(0, R₁)`.
5. Positive roots are sums of simple roots (`DominantDisplacement.positive_mem_closure`). A
   positive root in the monoid generated by `J` restricts to `0` on `A_J`
   (`ParabolicRootPresentation.restrict_positiveRoot_eq_zero_iff`), so it is not a root of `A_J`
   in the unipotent radical. Hence `B(Re λ|_{A_J}, α) > max(0, R₁) ≥ R₁` for every such root.

## Provenance

Lapid, *On Arthur's asymptotic inner product formula of truncated Eisenstein series* (2011), §2
(pp. 311–312), requires `Re λ` to be sufficiently regular in the positive Weyl chamber for the
convergence of Eisenstein series and intertwining operators. Bernstein–Lapid, *On the meromorphic
continuation of Eisenstein series* (2024), §2.3 (p. 191), makes this explicit as
`⟨Re λ, α^∨⟩ ≫ 0` for all simple roots `α ∈ Δ_P`, which is the form of
`IsStandardRegularParameter`. The root–coroot formula `⟨α^∨, λ⟩ = 2(α, λ)/(α, α)` for a
Weyl-invariant scalar product is Renard, *Représentations des groupes réductifs p-adiques*
(French version, 2026), V.3.7 (p. 111); the Lean proof obtains it from the reflection data
(`RelativeRootReflectionData.two_mul_invariantForm_root`). The comparison of the two regularity
conditions, with the explicit constant `R`, is derived here from these facts and from the
expression of positive roots as sums of simple roots.
