# Central translation of an unshifted weak pairing expansion

`WeakPairingCentralTransferStatement` takes an actual weak constant-term pairing expansion at the identity of the positive split centre. If each of the two original forms transforms under a scalar at a given central point, the translated pairing expansion uses the same exponents and multiplies its polynomial coefficients by the first scalar times the conjugate of the second. This describes the dependence on the central shift in (7.9) for such forms. The aggregate regularized pairing retains the same Haar factors.

The actual weak terms inherit their central scalars by the directness of normalized exponent expansions. In the square-integrable twist decomposition of the first weak term, apply central translation minus its scalar to the orthogonal remainder. Orthogonality is preserved because a central translate moves the test slice by a point of the same positive split component, and the normalization of the test gives a scalar independent of the compact and Levi variables. The resulting orthogonal function is also the sum of the normalized height twists with coefficients equal to the differences between their individual central factors and the scalar of the weak term, up to the sign from subtracting the twists. Such a square-integrable height expansion vanishes by testing on every slice. The twisted pieces have the distinct labels of the decomposition, so exponent directness forces each piece to carry the original scalar.

The proof then scales the normalized pairing and its coroot-coordinate polynomial expansion by the sesquilinear product of the two scalars. This uses the actual slice integrability in the unshifted expansion. It applies to each standard parabolic separately and uses the same square-integrable decomposition and test weak term after central translation.

See P. Delorme, *On the spectral theorem of Langlands*, Advances in Mathematics **426** (2023), 109107, §3.4, pp. 14–15, (3.7) and the extension of the pairing, and §7, pp. 24–25, (7.8)–(7.12).
