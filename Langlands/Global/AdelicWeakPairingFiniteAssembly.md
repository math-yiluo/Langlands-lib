# Finite Weyl labels and the normalized weak pairing

Delorme's normalized pairing (3.7) uses the square-integrable part of the first argument, as described before Remark 3.9. When this part is a sum of twists with parameters \(A_i\lambda\), and the tested weak term has exponents \(\mu_j\), the labels in (7.12) are \(A_i\lambda-\mu_j\). The result here gives the finite assembly of that pairing and its regularized integral in coroot coordinates. Delorme's Haar normalization (2.2) multiplies this contribution by the covolume \(C_Q\) in (7.8)–(7.9). In the aggregate `regularizedPairing`, this measure factor is the corresponding `vol Q`.

`WeakPairingFiniteAssemblyStatement` takes fixed square-integrable inducing vectors \(v_i\), complex linear parameter maps \(A_i\), spectral scalar coefficients and finitely many unitary exponent components \(w_j\) for the tested term. It assumes slice integrability of each untwisted pairing. For any parameter at which the twists are unitary and the difference between the first argument and the prescribed sum is square-integrably orthogonal, the statement supplies an `IsSquareIntegrableTwistDecomposition` and an `IsNormalizedPairingExpansion` against \(\sum_j w_j\). Its fixed `AffineExponentLabels` enumerate precisely the maps \(\lambda\mapsto A_i\lambda-\mu_j\), with repetitions allowed.

The proof chooses the coroot polynomial of each pair \((v_i,w_j)\) using `normalizedPairingCorootPolynomial`. It then groups the inducing vectors by equal values of \(A_i\lambda\). The complex submodule of square-integrable inducing forms is closed under these weighted sums. Regrouping the height twists recovers the original finite sum and hence the supplied orthogonal remainder. Linearity in the first argument and additivity in the conjugated second argument preserve integrability and give the sum of the individual normalized pairings. A second regrouping, this time by \(A_i\lambda-\mu_j\), constructs the unitary exponent expansion.

Polynomial evaluation and the regularized orthant formula are complex linear in the polynomial. For the orthant formula the proof uses the finite-support linear combination of the products of one-variable factors (7.8). Polynomial translation is also complex linear. Applying these linear maps to the grouped polynomials recovers both indexed formulas, including at collisions of labels. Both formulas use a fixed polynomial for each pair \((i,j)\). They use the supplied pairing pieces, so central translates can enter through those pieces. Orthogonality and slice integrability are hypotheses. The identity for `regularizedConeIntegral` uses Lean's totalized division. Its interpretation as the coroot-coordinate meromorphic value applies where the displayed coroot denominators are nonzero.

See P. Delorme, *On the spectral theorem of Langlands*, Advances in Mathematics **426** (2023), 109107, (2.2), §3.4, p. 15, (3.7) and Remark 3.9, and §7, pp. 24–25, (7.8)–(7.12).
