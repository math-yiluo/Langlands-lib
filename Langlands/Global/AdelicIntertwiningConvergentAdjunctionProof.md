# Adjunction of intertwining integrals in the region of convergence

`Langlands.Global.intertwiningConvergentAdjunction` proves `IntertwiningConvergentAdjunctionStatement`. The adjunction identity comes from Mœglin–Waldspurger, *Spectral Decomposition and Eisenstein Series* (1995), II.1.8, pp. 93–94. The proof uses the square-integrability and measure hypotheses of the adelic formulation in the Library.

The product of the two inducing functions with their parameter factors descends to the left quotient of the ambient adelic group by the rational points common to the target parabolic and the Weyl conjugate of the source parabolic. Its intersection with the target radical is the subgroup defining the original intertwining quotient. Iwasawa unfolding gives the measure on that quotient, the supplied Levi Haar measure on a rational fundamental domain, and compact Haar probability, with the inverse parabolic modulus as density.

Moving the target Levi variable past the radical changes the radical quotient measure. The Haar balance combines that factor with the inverse modulus to give the inverse half moduli in the two normalized Levi values. Iwasawa decomposition in the source bounds the first normalized value uniformly in squared norm on compact times canonical Levi quotient. Together with the second squared norm and the shifted scalar kernel in the source regular region, this gives absolute integrability of the normalized product. The target and source cutoffs differ by a translation in the positive split-component coordinates after the change of variables. Each has the same integral in that component by its Lebesgue normalization. Compact support justifies Fubini for each cutoff, including when it changes sign.

Translation by the inverse Weyl point preserves the counting-normalized measure between the two common quotients. Complex conjugation after that translation matches the reflected-parameter product and swaps the pairing arguments. Refolding in each parabolic gives the two integrable pairing integrands and the claimed equality.
