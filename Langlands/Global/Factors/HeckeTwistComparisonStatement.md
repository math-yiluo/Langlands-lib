# Conditional comparison using actual Hecke twists

This derived target adapts the generic finite functional-equation comparison to the Library's actual idele-class characters. Its source route is Henniart (1986), §4.3, formulas (2), (4), (5), printed pp.157–159, and the character-prescription interface already stated in `Langlands.Global.Arithmetic.CharacterPrescriptionStatement`. It is a conditional adapter, not a global analytic or local-stability theorem.

`AllPlaces K` is the disjoint union of all actual infinite and finite places. `archimedeanBlock K` contains every infinite place. `exceptionalPlaces K B` contains that entire block and every member of the finite set B. The original bad finite set T contains the chosen finite place w. `TwoHeckeTwists` records two actual finite-order Hecke characters: both are trivial on the whole archimedean idele factor, the first is sufficiently ramified at every T-place, and the second is trivial at w and sufficiently ramified at every other T-place. Ramification is the existing nontriviality predicate on the actual principal-unit filtration.

The given `RamifiedTwistIsolationStatement` selects these characters. It does not constrain ramification at other finite places. Consequently `CommonTwistEulerInputs` selects a single enlargement B only after both twists have been chosen. B contains T and all ramification of both twists, every infinite place is included in the comparison set, and original and dual local L-factors match at every place outside it. Four separate `Admissible` conditions retain meromorphy and nonzero germs for both sides of both twists; four separate `FunctionalEquation` conditions retain the full equations; two separate half-plane comparisons retain actual `HasProd` convergence and local L/dual-L matching. The placement of every epsilon contribution in the finite set is part of those full-equation inputs, not inferred from local L matching. In an actual application the original bad finite set must also contain the ramification of the fixed additive character; the actual-certificate adapter records this condition explicitly.

`HeckeEulerFamily K` keeps `localFactors χ` fixed for each character, while `partialL χ B` and `partialDualL χ B` depend on the omitted finite set B. Its `eulerData χ B` supplies the generic engine with exactly these tails and the unchanged local factors. Every admissibility, functional-equation and Euler-product premise above uses the same selected B. Enlarging B can therefore remove additional Euler factors from the tails without changing any local gamma factor. This dependence is essential: even with χ₁ trivial, the ramification of χ₂ can require B to grow. In the trivial GL₁ case over the rationals, for example, the original partial L-function is ζ(s) times the product of `(1-p^(-s))` over the primes in B, so its value depends on B. Preservation, stability, the original archimedean product and the selected-place conclusion all use the fixed local factors and have no B dependence.

The remaining hypotheses are individual factor-preservation and local-stability inputs. Triviality at infinity preserves each side's original archimedean factors, with no comparison between the sides at an individual infinite place. Triviality at w preserves each side's original w-factor. At each member of T the supplied depth condition implies gamma stability. At every finite place outside T the two twisted gamma factors match for every supplied finite-order twist. This last input accounts explicitly for every new auxiliary place in B outside T, including additional ramification of the chosen twists. Its proof from actual unramified factor formulae and determinant/central-character matching remains with the global-factor and local-factor owners.

The conclusion supplies the two actual characters and their common finite enlargement, equality of the entire original archimedean gamma product, and equality of the original gamma germs at w. The proof first compares the archimedean block using the first twist, then retains that block together with w using the second twist and cancels the common nonzero archimedean product. There is no infinite gamma product.

The data families D and E are not yet certificates of particular representations. The later actual-factor adapter must link their original local gamma functions to certified automorphic and arithmetic rational factors, while retaining arithmetic fundamental classes, local Weil maps, cuspidal realizations, spectral presentations, additive characters, self-dual measures, factor existence and independent universes. The present target claims only its actual-character specialization. Parent H1 and the common global library retain the global functional equations and analytic inputs; the character-prescription owners retain twist existence; Workers 35/44 and the common local-factor branches retain the actual factor and stability discharges.

The set-dependent tail formulation incorporates Reviewer 8775's correction of the earlier fixed-tail interface. It is an internal formulation of the cited finite-set comparison, with no additional assertion of Euler-product existence.
