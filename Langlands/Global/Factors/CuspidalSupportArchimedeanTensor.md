# Standard constituents of archimedean tensor subquotients

`ArchimedeanPlaceStandardConstituentOfTensorStatement` treats both real and complex places of an operator subquotient `Q`. At either place, let `V` be the space of compact-finite functions in the standard module for the given parameter list. The list must have total rank equal to the rank of `Q`. The statement takes a complex vector space `M`, a subspace `S` of `V ⊗ M`, and a linear map `q : S → Q.Space`. The hypothesis `IsPlaceTensorIntertwining` says that the compact operators and the derivatives along real matrix directions act on the `V` factor, preserve `S`, and intertwine with the actions of `Q` through `q`. If the image of `q` contains an irreducible subspace `J` for the place actions, the corresponding `HasRealPlaceStandardConstituent` or `HasComplexPlaceStandardConstituent` predicate holds.

`archimedeanPlaceStandardConstituent_of_tensor` indexes both operator families by the disjoint union of the compact group and the real matrix directions. It applies `exists_subquotient_of_tensorProduct` to get a subspace of `V` and an equivariant map with image `J`. Stability under the real directions suffices for irreducibility because each pure tensor `c ⊗ X` in the complexified Lie algebra is `c` times `1 ⊗ X`. Tensor induction extends stability to the whole Lie algebra. The proof maps the extracted subspace into the ambient function space along the inclusion of `V`, then transports the map along the induced linear equivalence. The formulas for the standard-module actions give the right translations and right derivatives required by the constituent predicates. The compact matrix representing a given element of the compact subgroup of `GL_n` yields that same element via `Unitary.toUnits`.

For irreducible jointly admissible `Q` with nontrivial carrier, `hasRealPlaceStandardConstituent_of_surjective_tensor` and `hasComplexPlaceStandardConstituent_of_surjective_tensor` obtain `J` from the corresponding place-component existence theorem. Surjectivity puts that entire subspace in the image of `q`.

This is a derived single-place application of the finite-stage argument of R. P. Langlands, *On the notion of an automorphic representation* (1979), Lemma 1 and its proof, printed p. 203 (PDF p. 198 of the registered Corvallis Part I, `langlands-1979-notion-automorphic-representation`). It supplies the extraction after the local tensor description of the global inducing representation has been given. The identification with that global space and the existence of inducing parameters are separate inputs to the every-place theorem.
