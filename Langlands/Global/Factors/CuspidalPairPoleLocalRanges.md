# Specified local Whittaker ranges from actual restriction surjectivity

`RealPlaceWhittakerRestrictionSurjectivityStatement` and `ComplexPlaceWhittakerRestrictionSurjectivityStatement` give the converse direction of the place comparison for actual cuspidal realizations trivial on the split centre. They take a nontrivial unitary adelic additive character trivial on the diagonal number field, a supplied standard-module parameter, and the scale `a` of the character at the chosen place. Every function of the specified local model at scale `a` is a restriction of an actual global Whittaker coefficient for that character. The comparison at scale `-a` uses its inverse.

`SpecifiedWhittakerSliceComparisonAt` uses the same split-centre and character hypotheses over a fixed number field. It takes a spectral presentation with irreducible admissible finite components and the supplied archimedean scale and parameter families. For the same realization and presentation, it supplies two normalized infinite tensor slice models whose ranges equal the specified ordinary local models for the character and its inverse.

`specifiedWhittakerSliceComparisonAt_of_local` assumes `InfiniteWhittakerSliceModelStatement` and the two converse restriction propositions. Split-centre triviality supplies the unitary central character used for finite genericity. On the union of the exceptional places and the additive conductor support, the proof chooses canonical local characters and finite reference vectors on which the corresponding functionals have value one. The normalized slice comparison and these references identify each model range with the actual restrictions. The published forward real comparison passes through the uniform model and its inclusion in the ordinary model. The published forward complex comparison gives membership in the ordinary complex model. The two converse premises provide the reverse inclusions for both characters.

This is an internal derivation. The local uniqueness and model comparison relevant to the converse premises concern Shalika, *The Multiplicity One Theorem for GL(n)* (1974), Theorem 3.1, printed pp. 183–185, and Jacquet, *Archimedean Rankin–Selberg Integrals* (2009), §2, author PDF pp. 3–10.
