# The prescribed induction tensor in smooth cuspidal block sections

Fix a number field with compact additive adele quotient, cuspidal realizations of positive ranks summing to `r + 1`, and their spectral presentations. The number field and finite carriers have universe `u`, and the presentation factors have universe `uQ`. The embedding theorem assumes that the supplied finite components are irreducible and admissible and that the real and complex parameters come from the given realizations. The source uses the tensor of the standard modules in the original block order at the infinite places.

`HasCuspidalReferenceTensorSectionEmbedding` is the full operator embedding into smooth compact-finite scalar block sections at parameter zero. For any reference family fixed by the finite integral subgroups outside a finite exceptional set, `cuspidalReferenceTensorSectionEmbedding` proves this embedding when the reference vectors are nonzero outside that set. `finiteReferencesEquiv` gives the actual finite adelic equivalence from the presentation-normalized restricted tensor. Precomposing the evaluation with the inverse equivalence preserves right translation, normalized vector covariance, and separation at finite compact arguments. Smoothness transports along the same equivalence. The linear pairing of the finite vector law with the infinite coefficients gives the scalar parabolic law used by `cuspidalBlockOperatorTensorEmbedding`.

`cuspidalIsobaricBlockSectionEmbedding` applies this construction to the source references, invariance and nonvanishing fields of `CuspidalInducedFiniteTensor.assemble`. For the infinite factors it takes the original-order blocks and actual place parameters of the supplied real and complex data. The resulting map intertwines the whole common free operator action and proves `CuspidalIsobaricBlockSectionEmbeddingStatement` at both universes.

`cuspidalIsobaricAutomorphy_of_eisensteinContinuation` applies the block section reduction to this embedding. Its premise is `EisensteinMeromorphicContinuationStatement`. The reduction uses convergence-region injectivity on finite-dimensional compact projection ranges and the existing specialization argument to realize the prescribed constituent tensor.

The compact model follows Getz–Hahn (2024), §10.1, printed pp. 270–271 (PDF pp. 282–283). The all-places construction uses their Theorems 10.7.1 and 10.7.5, printed pp. 286 and 288 (PDF pp. 298 and 300), and the restricted place tensor and specialization of Langlands (1979), Lemma 1 and Proposition 2 with Lemmas 7–9, printed pp. 203–207 (PDF pp. 198–202).
