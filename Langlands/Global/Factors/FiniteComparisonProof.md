# Finite functional-equation comparison proof

This module proves the public proposition `Langlands.Global.FunctionalEquationComparison.FiniteFunctionalEquationComparisonStatement.{u}` from `Langlands.Global.Factors.FiniteComparisonStatement`. The theorem `finiteFunctionalEquationComparison` supplies its entire five-part conjunction for an arbitrary universe `u` of places: Euler-tail comparison, finite gamma comparison, their global combination, retained-block cancellation, and two-twist chosen-place isolation. The proof was authored by Worker 352 in parent task `205503d3-82c7-4b1a-a4f0-90d02bbe9a2e`; Worker 468 prepared this focused proof publication and updated its explanation.

The public statement is an internal derived formulation of Henniart, *On the local Langlands conjecture for GL(n): the cyclic case* (1986), §4.3, formulas (2), (4), and (5), printed pp. 157–159 / PDF pp. 14–16. Its registered source is `henniart-1986-cyclic`, SHA-256 `096fba993b7368b1af9f7cc1f0c053e5436ba447f291c2acd402f058eb44b0ba`. The proof follows the analytic route described below rather than constructing the representation-theoretic inputs of that source. It imports the published target without changing its definitions, hypotheses, or normalization.

The helpers use eventual equality in the punctured neighborhood of every complex point and transport it through the reflection s ↦ 1−s. `NonzeroMeromorphic` excludes identically zero germs while permitting zeros and poles of finite order. Such a germ is nonzero throughout a sufficiently small punctured neighborhood. Reflection preserves this property, so the supplied nonzero meromorphic epsilon, original L, and dual L factors yield nonzero meromorphic gamma factors with the prescribed normalization gamma(s) = epsilon(s) · dual L(1−s) / L(s).

`eulerTailComparison` uses uniqueness of `HasProd` limits for the actual original and dual Euler products outside the same finite set S, with local matching on a common right half-plane. It then applies meromorphic identity continuation: the meromorphic difference has zero germ at one interior point of that half-plane, and connectedness of the complex plane propagates this zero germ to every point. This component assumes meromorphy of the tails and uses no functional equation.

`finiteGammaComparison` first rewrites the finite product of gamma ratios as a ratio of finite products. Each full completed functional equation identifies this with partial L(s) / partial dual L(1−s), off the zeros of the finite L-product and the reflected dual tail. Nonzero meromorphic germs provide a common punctured neighborhood on which those denominators do not vanish. Equality of the original and reflected dual tails therefore gives equality of finite gamma products. `globalFiniteGammaComparison` obtains those tail identities from `eulerTailComparison` and then applies this cancellation argument. Every gamma product is finite; there is no infinite product of gamma factors.

`finiteBlockCancellation` splits S into its retained subset A and S minus A. It combines finitely many eventual auxiliary comparisons and nonvanishing assertions, then cancels the nonzero auxiliary product on that neighborhood. It concludes equality of the whole retained product, without asserting equality of its individual factors.

`twoTwistIsolation` applies the global comparison and block cancellation twice. The first twist retains A and uses the preservation hypotheses to transfer its whole product to the original factors. In an actual application A is the entire archimedean set. The second twist retains A together with the selected place w and transfers this retained-product identity to the original factors. The first whole-product identity and a nonzero product over A then cancel to isolate w. Equality of individual archimedean factors is neither an input nor a conclusion.

The result is conditional on all published inputs: actual outside-S original and dual Euler products on a common right half-plane, their local matching, nonzero meromorphic tails and exceptional factors, both full completed functional equations with all epsilon contributions in S, and all preservation and auxiliary stability assertions for both twists. The theorem constructs no local factor, prescribed global character, self-dual measure, stability theorem, or global analytic input. Applications must supply these data and assertions, justify the exceptional set and epsilon placement, and preserve the additive-character, dual, and Haar conventions. All conclusions are equality of punctured germs, so values assigned at isolated poles do not affect them. Actual arithmetic and automorphic adapters and the other output families of the H1 parent task remain separate obligations.
