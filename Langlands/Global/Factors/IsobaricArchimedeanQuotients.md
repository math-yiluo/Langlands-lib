# Archimedean ordering and quotient embeddings for isobaric sums

The ordering lemmas concatenate the parameters and sort by weakly decreasing real exponent. `List.mergeSort_perm` preserves every block with its multiplicity. The real rank is the sum of the block ranks, and the complex rank is the list length. The rank of the concatenation is therefore the sum of the original ranks. The two real order predicates agree because `RealBlock.exponent.re` and `realBlockExponent` give the same value on each constructor. These list operations implement the ordering of the archimedean parameters in Getz and Hahn (2024), (10.30), p. 283 (PDF 295), and (10.33), p. 284 (PDF 296).

`StrictAdjacentSwap` is an internal relation on lists with weights. One step interchanges adjacent elements whose weights are strictly increasing, with the prefix and suffix fixed. `strictAdjacentSwaps_mergeSort` reaches the same output as `List.mergeSort` by a finite sequence of these steps. The proof sorts the tail with the head held fixed, then uses `List.mergeSort_cons` to move the head past the strictly greater weights in the sorted tail. Each step preserves the order of equal weights. The real and complex versions take the real part of the exponent as the weight. They give a list argument for reducing a comparison with the original block order to comparisons for one strict adjacent swap at a time.

The quotient lemmas use `Representation.quotient` for the compact action and `RealLeviDuality.quotLie` for the Lie action. The quotient projection intertwines both actions by definition. A stable subspace of the quotient pulls back to a stable subspace containing the denominator. If the pullback is proper, the hypothesis that the denominator is the largest proper stable subspace makes the pullback equal to the denominator, so the subspace in the quotient is zero. A full pullback gives the whole quotient. The properness of the denominator also gives a nonzero quotient. These algebraic lemmas start with a supplied largest proper stable subspace. Their intended applications are to the ordered standard modules with Langlands quotients in Knapp (1994), Theorem 1(a), p. 400 (PDF 410), and Theorem 4(a), p. 405 (PDF 415).

For either place predicate, compose the supplied quotient map with a nonzero equivariant map from the irreducible quotient into the specified operator subquotient. Surjectivity of the quotient map identifies the image of the composite with the image of the second map. A stable subspace of that image pulls back to a stable subspace of the irreducible module, so it is zero or the full image. The identities for the standard compact and Lie actions identify the composite as an intertwiner for the right translations and derivatives used by the predicates. Stability under the real Lie directions implies stability under the complexified Lie action by linearity on pure tensors. These internal algebraic lemmas implement the final local map in the place-by-place construction of Getz and Hahn (2024), Theorem 10.7.5 and (10.35), p. 288 (PDF 300). Each lemma takes both the local quotient and its map to the specified operator subquotient as inputs.
