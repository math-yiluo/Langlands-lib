# Common split-centre normalization for cuspidal realizations

This module implements the common determinant twist for the proof of strong multiplicity one from Getz–Hahn, *An Introduction to Automorphic Representations* (2024), Theorem 11.7.2, p. 309 (PDF p. 320), and Exercise 11.6, p. 314 (PDF p. 325). The passage before Theorem 11.7.1 on p. 309 normalizes the split-centre action by determinant twists. Section 2.6, equations (2.18) and (2.20) and the example following (2.20), p. 68 (PDF p. 85), supplies the idelic norm and the positive scalars that form the split centre of the general linear group.

An equivalence of two finite components carries the scalar action of the first central character to a nonzero vector of the second realization. Irreducibility then makes that scalar action hold on the whole second realization. The closed subgroup on which the first character acts by scalars contains all principal ideles and all local groups outside the finite exception set. The published idele-density theorem makes this subgroup the whole idele group. Both realizations therefore have the same central character.

Write `n = r + 1` and `d = [F : ℚ]`. The definition `splitCentreNormalizingCharacter` sends an idele class of norm `m` to the inverse of the common central character at the positive scalar `m^(1/(dn))`. This formula is derived from the norm and split-centre description in §2.6. At the positive scalar `t`, the idelic norm is `t^d`. Taking the `n`th power of the normalizing character there gives the inverse of the original central character at `t`. The determinant twist consequently makes the split centre act trivially on each realization with the given central character.

A common determinant twist preserves the finite local equivalences by the published presentation twist and ordinary twisting of intertwiners. A full operator equivalence of the twisted realizations transports back via the two determinant multiplier maps. Both maps conjugate the operators using the same operator algebra automorphism, so the transported equivalence intertwines the original actions.
