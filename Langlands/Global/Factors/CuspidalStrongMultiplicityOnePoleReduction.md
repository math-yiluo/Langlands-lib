# Strong multiplicity one from the completed pair pole theorem

`Langlands.Global.CuspidalStrongMultiplicityOneFromPairPole` is the implication from `CuspidalPairPoleStatement` to `CuspidalStrongMultiplicityOneStatement`. Its proof, `Langlands.Global.cuspidalStrongMultiplicityOne_of_pairPole`, factors through `Langlands.Global.CuspidalNondualPartialEulerContinuationStatement`. This intermediate proposition concerns equal-rank cuspidal realizations with trivial split-centre action and irreducible admissible finite components. When the first realization fails to be contragredient to the second, it asks for an entire function agreeing on some right half-plane with the partial Euler product outside any finite set that contains both presentations' exceptional sets. All three propositions retain the field and presentation universes of the strong multiplicity one statement.

`cuspidalNondualPartialEulerContinuation_of_pairPole` is proved in `CuspidalNondualPartialEulerContinuation`. It uses the assumed pole theorem, Getz–Hahn, *An Introduction to Automorphic Representations* (2024), Theorem 11.7.1, p. 309 (PDF p. 320), to obtain an entire completion in the nondual case. Clearing the omitted finite Euler denominators and all archimedean denominators gives the required continuation of the partial product.

`cuspidalStrongMultiplicityOne_of_nondualPartialEulerContinuation` follows the comparison of mixed and self-pair factors in Getz–Hahn §11.7, p. 309 (PDF p. 320), and Exercise 11.6, p. 314 (PDF p. 325). Equality of the local classes outside the given finite set gives equality of the central characters. The common determinant twist makes the split-centre action trivial on both realizations and preserves the local class equalities. The conjugate of the second normalized realization supplies its contragredient, with a spectral presentation furnished by `cuspidalContragredientPresentationExistence`.

If the two normalized realizations are inequivalent, uniqueness of the contragredient puts the first realization and the conjugate of the second in the nondual case. The proof enlarges the omitted set by the exceptional sets of both normalized presentations and of the contragredient presentation. At each remaining place, local class equality provides an equivalence of the first two components. The equivalence transports the spherical spectrum, and spherical spectrum uniqueness gives equality of the eigenvalue multisets used in the mixed and self-pair Euler factors.

Entire continuation of the mixed partial product would therefore give entire continuation of the self-pair partial product. The proof rules this out by `CuspidalSelfPairAssembly.not_exists_entire_selfPair`, which combines Landau's theorem with the nonnegative logarithmic series in Jacquet–Shalika, *On Euler Products and the Classification of Automorphic Representations I* (1981), Theorem (5.3), equations (1)–(3), p. 556 (PDF p. 58). Cancelling the common determinant twist then gives the original operator equivalence of Getz–Hahn Theorem 11.7.2, p. 309 (PDF p. 320).
