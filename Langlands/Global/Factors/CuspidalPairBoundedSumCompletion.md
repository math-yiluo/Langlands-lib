# Bounded completions from realized finite normalized sums

`equalBoundedSumCompletion` and `unequalBoundedSumCompletion` take the existing families of entire normalized partial ordinary integrals and an indexed family of normalized values realized by their actual global tests. The indexed values have a finite weighted sum equal to one away from 0 and 1, with weights bounded in strips away from these points. The finite additive support and both spherical exceptional sets are contained in the specified set of finite places. The common additive character is unitary and trivial on the diagonal field. The initial completed Euler product is assumed continuous on a right half-plane. The equal-rank theorem also uses the two split-centre and central-character hypotheses of the equal-rank pole calculation.

The existing continuation arguments supply the completed Euler product and a bounded global test integral for each index, with each test equal to its normalized factor times the completion away from 0 and 1. The weighted sum then gives the vertical-strip bound of the completion. Keeping the original indices allows several indices to realize the same global test. The equal-rank conclusion retains the determinant-slab period pole criterion, and the unequal-rank completion is holomorphic off 0 and 1 with nonnegative meromorphic order at both points. Its singularities there are removable, with assigned values allowed by `HasSimplePoleProfile`. The arguments use the global integral bounds and the realized finite sum directly.

This is a derived form of the argument of J. W. Cogdell, *Analytic theory of L-functions for GL_n*, chapter 9 of Bernstein and Gelbart (eds.), *An Introduction to the Langlands Program*, Birkhäuser, 2004, §4.1, printed pp. 219–221 (PDF pp. 222–224), with the equal-rank pole criterion of Theorem 4.2, printed p. 222 (PDF p. 225). The local finite combinations and their realization as global tests enter as hypotheses.
