# Equal-rank continuation from normalized partial integrals

`EqualPoleTest` records two cuspidal vectors spherical outside \(S\), and a Schwartz–Bruhat row test standard outside \(S\). `EqualNormalizedTests` gives an entire function for every such test, and requires at least one nonzero value at each spectral point. On a right half-plane the partial integral must be the normalized function times the specified archimedean product and the finite factors at \(S\).

`EqualNormalizedTests.exists_completion` uses the equal-rank unfolding and Euler factorization (Cogdell 2004, §2.3, printed pp. 210–211) to express the global integral as the matching normalized test times the full Euler product on a right half-plane. The split-centre residue formula gives an entire pole-cleared integral for every test and bounds the original continuation in strips away from \(0\) and \(1\). The normalized tests with no common zero glue the completed function as in the argument of Cogdell's Theorem 4.1, printed pp. 219–220.

`HasEqualTestPeriod` requires a trivial product central character and a nonzero period for a pair of vectors spherical outside \(S\). The explicit residue formula implies that any pole of a test integral requires this condition. When the condition holds, a Schwartz–Bruhat function can be chosen standard outside \(S\) with both its value and its Fourier transform nonzero at the origin. Thus the condition gives a test with a pole at each of the two points. The glued completed function has exactly this pole criterion, and the continuation of each test retains its strip bounds.

`HasEqualTestPeriod.isContragredientOf` uses the invariant residue pairing to identify the necessary contragredience in the order of the pole statement. `hasEqualTestPeriod_conjugate` uses a nonzero spherical vector from the given spectral presentation, pairs it with its pointwise conjugate and uses the positivity of the determinant-slab period. The existence of the normalized tests and transfer from the conjugate pair to any equivalent realization remain separate inputs to the full pole theorem.
