# Nondual partial Euler continuation from covering equal-rank tests

`CuspidalNondualPartialEulerFromCoveringTests` derives the existing nondual partial Euler continuation statement from `CuspidalNondualCoveringEqualTestsStatement`. For each nondual equal-rank pair with trivial split-centre actions, the premise selects a nontrivial unitary additive character trivial on the number field, a finite set containing both exceptional sets and the finite additive support, certified finite factors, and one product of archimedean pair factors. It asks for actual tests with the prescribed spherical data outside that set whose normalized partial integrals are entire and have no common zero. Any supplied archimedean lists work if they give these normalizations. Pointwise normalized local generation with the specified slice comparisons is one way to obtain the covering property.

The proof uses `EqualNormalizedTestFamily.exists_nondual_completion`. Unfolding each global integral and factoring it outside the selected finite set identifies its value on a right half-plane with its entire normalization times the completed Euler product. Any pole of a global integral at zero or one would make the determinant-slab residue period nonzero with trivial product central character. The invariant pairing from that period would give contragredience. The quotient gluing theorem therefore gives the completed product with a false pole condition. Filling removable singularities and multiplying by the archimedean and omitted finite denominators continues the partial product for any allowed omitted finite set.

This intermediate implication uses the equal-rank unfolding and Eisenstein residue argument in Cogdell, *Analytic Theory of L-functions for GLₙ* (2004), §2.3, pp. 208–211 (PDF pp. 211–214), and Theorems 4.1–4.2, pp. 219–222 (PDF pp. 222–225). The conclusion is the analytic premise of the Library's strong multiplicity one reduction for Getz–Hahn (2024), Theorem 11.7.2, p. 309 (PDF p. 320), and Exercise 11.6, p. 314 (PDF p. 325).
