# Arithmetic adapters for finite Hecke divisors

Worker 1692 derives these internal comparisons from the Library's existing
`ClassField.Global.IdeleDivisor`, `FiniteDivisorFractionalIdeal` and
`PrincipalIdeleDivisor` modules and Mathlib's fractional-ideal norm. No new
idele, divisor or valuation is chosen. The number field has unrestricted
universe `u`.

The actual local order of `CompletionPadic.topologicalData v` equals the negative
integer exponent of the completion valuation. Exact equality of the two
normalized discrete valuations proves this, using their logarithms. Thus each
coefficient of the existing idele divisor is exactly the order used by the
local Hecke shell formulas.

`principalDivisor a` applies the existing idele divisor to the actual principal
idele. Its prime multiplicity is the existing principal fractional-ideal count.
Injectivity of prime multiplicities identifies its prime-product fractional
ideal with the principal fractional ideal generated by `a`.

`norm d` is the product of `N(v) ^ d(v)` over the finite support of the integer
divisor. Mapping the prime-product fractional ideal through Mathlib's absolute
norm identifies this real number with its fractional-ideal norm. Mathlib's
principal-ideal norm formula gives `norm (principalDivisor a) = |N_{K/ℚ}(a)|`.

A principal divisor vanishes if and only if `a` is the image of a unit of the
ring of integers. For the forward implication its completion valuations are
all one, so both `a` and `a⁻¹` lie in the ring of integers by the intersection
of the finite valuation rings. The two preimages multiply to one by fraction
field injectivity. Conversely, an integral unit has valuation one at every
finite place, so its idele divisor vanishes.

These comparisons do not establish a class representative decomposition, an
adelic integral identity, analytic continuation or the global functional equation.
