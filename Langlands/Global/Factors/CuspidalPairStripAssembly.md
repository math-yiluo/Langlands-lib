# Transfer of vertical-strip bounds to a completed pair function

`CompletionStripBoundsFromTest` takes a completed function holomorphic off \(\{0,1\}\) with at most simple poles there. The same proposition characterizes negative meromorphic order at each of the two points. The criterion also takes a single entire normalized factor \(R\) with exponential growth on each strip and a nonzero value somewhere, and a test integral \(I\) bounded in strips away from the two points. Away from the points, the matching identity is \(I=R\Lambda\). Given bounds for \(\Lambda\) on arbitrarily far left and right vertical lines, the criterion proves `IsBoundedInVerticalStrips Λ`.

Clearing the possible poles gives the entire function \(H\) with \(H(s)=s(s-1)\Lambda(s)\) off \(\{0,1\}\). The product \(RH\) has exponential strip growth because it equals \(s(s-1)I(s)\) there and the strip estimate for \(I\) applies when \(|\operatorname{Im}s|\ge1\). The whole-plane quotient estimate gives \(H\) double-exponential growth with arbitrarily small rate on every fixed strip.

The proof encloses the target strip within two bounded lines of \(\Lambda\), with the left line strictly below zero and the right line strictly above one. It applies Phragmén–Lindelöf to \(H(s)/(s-d)^2\) for \(d\) one unit to the right of the right boundary. This quotient is holomorphic on a neighborhood of the enclosing strip and has a bounded norm on its edges because \(|s-d|\ge1\) there. On the target strip away from the two discs, the ratios \(|s-d|/|s|\) and \(|s-d|/|s-1|\) are bounded in terms of \(d\) and the disc radius. This transfers the bound back to \(\Lambda\).

`isBoundedInVerticalStrips_of_completed_test` supplies the right-line hypothesis from `completedPairRightStripBounds` for the actual initial product. It still assumes the matching normalized test, its growth estimate and the left-line bounds. The analytic argument is a derived application of the pole regularization, the wide-strip quotient estimate and Mathlib's `PhragmenLindelof.vertical_strip`.
