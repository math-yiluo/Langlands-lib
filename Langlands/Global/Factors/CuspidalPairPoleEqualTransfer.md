# The equal-rank pole criterion after transport to the conjugate pair

`EqualNormalizedProductStatement` is the normalization input for equal ranks. Each realization is trivial on the split component of its centre. The additive character of the adeles is nontrivial, continuous and unitary, and is trivial on the diagonal copy of the number field. For the specified finite data and standard-module parameters, the input asks for normalized partial Whittaker integrals at a finite set containing the exceptional places and the additive ramification. The normalized family is indexed by all standard-outside tests, has no common zero, and contains one member that is nonzero somewhere and has exponential strip growth.

`exists_equal_completion_of_normalized_products` proves, from this input, that the completed Euler product for such a pair is holomorphic away from \(0\) and \(1\) and has at most simple poles there. It has poles at both points exactly when the first realization is the contragredient of the second. The two split-centre conditions force the norm-shift exponent in the residue calculation to vanish, giving the fixed locations \(0\) and \(1\). The theorem also gives a matching integral and nonzero normalized factor for use in the strip argument.

When the contragredient condition fails, the equal-rank assembly identifies the poles with nonzero determinant-slab periods, and invariance of the pairing excludes any such period. When the condition holds, uniqueness of the contragredient identifies the first realization with the pointwise conjugate of the second by a full-operator equivalence. Transport preserves the actual finite local classes, including irreducibility and admissibility, so the same finite polynomials and self-dual measures serve for the conjugate pair. The equivalence also transports the specified archimedean parameter lists. The completion constructed for the conjugate pair thus matches the original Euler product. Its determinant-slab period is positive on a nonzero spherical vector paired with its conjugate, giving the two poles.

This combines the standard continuation and period argument of Cogdell 2004, §2.3, printed pp. 208–211, and Theorems 4.1–4.2, printed pp. 219–222, with transport at the level of actual realizations and local factor data. The norm shift appears in Cogdell's Theorem 4.2, printed p. 222, and the split-centre normalization is used in Getz–Hahn 2024, Theorem 11.7.1 and its preceding twist formula, printed p. 309. The existence and growth of the normalized families in `EqualNormalizedProductStatement` enter as hypotheses.
