# Finite functional-equation comparison

This is an internal derived formulation of the finite-exceptional-set argument in Henniart, *On the local Langlands conjecture for GL(n): the cyclic case* (1986), §4.3, especially formulas (2), (4), and (5), printed pp. 157–159. The registered source is `henniart-1986-cyclic`, SHA-256 `096fba993b7368b1af9f7cc1f0c053e5436ba447f291c2acd402f058eb44b0ba`. It supplies conditional targets for H1; it does not assert any global Rankin–Selberg functional equation, local stability theorem, or factor existence theorem. The file contains constructions and proposition definitions. Proofs require separate exact-target proof tasks after publication.

`LocalFactors` consists of three actual functions from the complex numbers to the complex numbers. Its dual L-function is supplied for the chosen contragredient convention. Gamma is defined as epsilon(s) times dual L(1-s), divided by L(s). Complex division assigns a value even at a zero denominator; all conclusions concern punctured germs, so a value assigned at an individual pole is immaterial. `MeromorphicEq` requires eventual equality in the punctured neighborhood of every complex point. `NonzeroMeromorphic` requires meromorphy and excludes an identically zero germ at every point; zeros and poles of finite order are allowed.

`FiniteEulerData` contains actual local factors at every place and two partial L-functions. For a finite set S its completed L-function is partial L(s) times the finite product of local L(s) over S; the dual completion uses the same S. Its finite epsilon and gamma products use S. No infinite product of gamma factors is defined. The type of places has an independent universe; the abstract engine does not impose an arithmetic realization on it.

`HasEulerProductsOutside` asserts convergence, through `HasProd`, of the two actual local L-products indexed by the complement of S to the supplied partial L-functions on a common right half-plane. `MatchesOutside` asserts local equality on that half-plane for both original and dual L-factors at every place outside S. Here almost-everywhere matching means matching away from finitely many places. A common half-plane makes the quantifiers explicit and may be obtained by increasing individual convergence bounds. `EulerTailComparisonStatement` derives equality of both continued tails as punctured germs, using uniqueness of product limits and meromorphic identity continuation. It assumes meromorphy of the tails but no functional equation or nonzero condition.

`FunctionalEquation` asserts the full completed equation as equality of punctured germs: completed L(s) equals the finite epsilon product times completed dual L(1-s). Every global epsilon contribution must be included in S. In an actual adapter, S must contain all archimedean and exceptional places and the adapter must establish triviality of epsilon outside S or otherwise justify that the displayed finite epsilon is the full global epsilon. Equality of the L-factors outside S does not imply this fact. The analytic inputs `Admissible` assert nonzero meromorphic tails and nonzero meromorphic original L, dual L and epsilon factors separately at every member of S. These conditions permit finite cancellation and exclude a missing or zero factor. No desired gamma comparison is a field of the data.

`FiniteGammaComparisonStatement` derives equality of the finite gamma products from two full functional equations and equal original and dual tails, under those analytic inputs. `ComparisonInputs` groups the explicit inputs with the existence of a common half-plane. `GlobalFiniteGammaComparisonStatement` combines tail comparison with finite cancellation. It is the conditional form of §4.3(2).

`FiniteBlockCancellationStatement` cancels each auxiliary factor outside a retained finite block A contained in S. It requires a nonzero meromorphic germ for each factor and equality at each removed place. It concludes equality of the entire retained product, without asserting equality of its individual factors. The nonzero hypotheses are sufficient even if some individual factors have a zero or pole at the point under consideration, because cancellation takes place on a punctured neighborhood.

`TwoTwistIsolationStatement` implements both source twists. Its first pair of Euler data satisfies all global comparison inputs; `PreservesGamma` identifies its archimedean factors with the original factors, and `StableGamma` compares its factors at every finite member of S. This yields equality of the whole original archimedean gamma product, as in §4.3(4). Its second pair again satisfies all global comparison inputs, preserves both the archimedean factors and the chosen finite factor w, and has local stability at every other finite member of S. Using the first archimedean-product identity and nonzero germs then gives the chosen-place identity of §4.3(5). Neither per-archimedean equality nor the desired chosen-place identity is assumed.

These generic preservation and stability predicates are factor-level hypotheses. They do not construct a Hecke character or assert that highly ramified stability holds. The actual adapter must name the two characters, prove their prescribed local triviality and ramification, supply the individual stability and central-character inputs, and preserve the local additive-character and Haar conventions of both sides. Actual factor and self-dual measure existence, arithmetic fundamental classes and local Weil maps, and automorphic realizations/presentations remain obligations of the separate adapter and their owning tasks. Workers 35 and 44 own the global arithmetic/automorphic statements, Worker 2 their actual construction interfaces, and the parent global-library track owns discharging the analytic and functional-equation inputs. This unit supplies no unconditional proof of the associated-factor statement, pole-region theorem, or local correspondence.

`FiniteFunctionalEquationComparisonStatement` is the conjunction of these five derived targets. The split permits focused proof work while preserving the common normalization and analytic assumptions.
