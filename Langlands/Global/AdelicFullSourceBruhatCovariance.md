# The Weyl character of a full-source Bruhat summand

Consider a standard Bruhat summand whose source intersection is the entire inducing parabolic. Use a minimal-parabolic Iwasawa compact and a continuous inducing function invariant under the source adelic radical and rational Levi. Assume its positive split equivariance is \(\phi(ag)=\delta_P(a)^{1/2}\phi(g)\). Under these hypotheses the relative Eisenstein summand transforms under the normalized Weyl character of the positive split component of the full target parabolic.

The source constant term equals the inducing function because the source radical acts trivially and its rational quotient has mass one. The source inclusion is the identity, so the restricted parameter equals the original one. The standard representations identify the resulting intertwining integral with the integral for the two actual intersection parabolics.

Haar balance identifies the quotient conjugation factor times the source half-modulus with the target half-modulus. This transports the given equivariance to the normalized intertwining operator. Restoring the target Weyl twist gives the raw split character of the intertwining integral. This integral is also invariant under the adelic radical and the rational Levi, by invariance of the normalized operator and its height twist.

Relative summation commutes with the split translation of the full target, calculated via its lift into the target intersection. The two parabolic half-moduli agree on this lift. Canceling the full target half-modulus gives the claimed Weyl character. For a pointwise meromorphic continuation agreeing with the summand in the regular region, both sides of the character identity are meromorphic in the spectral parameter. The Weyl character at a fixed split point is the exponential of a complex linear functional. Meromorphic uniqueness extends the identity throughout the continuation's holomorphy domain.

This is the covariance of the full-source terms in Delorme, *On the spectral theorem of Langlands*, Advances in Mathematics 426 (2023), 109107, (4.3). The continuation clause uses a supplied continuation of the individual summand.
