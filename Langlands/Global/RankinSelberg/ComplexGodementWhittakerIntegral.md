# Spectral holomorphy of the partially transformed complex Godement integral

Fix a complex Whittaker model vector \(W\) on \(GL_m(\mathbb C)\), a
complex character \(d\), and a Schwartz test \(\Phi\) on \(m\)-by-\((m+1)\)
matrices. For every complex \(s\), the integral of
\[
  \Phi([h,\ h^{-T}e_m])\,W(h^{-1})\,d(\det h)\,|\det h|_{\mathbb C}^{s}
\]
against Haar measure on \(GL_m(\mathbb C)\) converges absolutely. Its
value is entire in \(s\). The test can be the partially Fourier-transformed
test at a fixed larger-rank argument in Jacquet's formula (7.7).

The proof factors \(h^{-1}\) in upper-unipotent, positive-diagonal and
unitary order. The entries of \(h\) bound the negative diagonal
logarithms of \(h^{-1}\) via the inverse triangular factor. The last row
of \(h^{-1}\), placed in the appended column, bounds the final
logarithm. Telescoping the positive simple roots controls the remaining
positive logarithms. The sum of absolute diagonal logarithms is thus
bounded by a constant times the sum of the logarithmic size of the
appended matrix and the positive simple-root sum.

Left unipotent covariance and the Whittaker estimate supply arbitrary
simple-root decay at fixed toral growth. The real part of the spectral
parameter and the exponent of \(d\) contribute a bounded multiple of the
toral length on a bounded real interval. The proof also accounts for
the determinant weight of Haar measure on the invertible matrix locus.
Choosing the root decay order absorbs the simple-root term. Schwartz
decay then bounds the weighted integrand by any negative power of one
plus the norm of \(h\) in the additive matrix space. A power exceeding
the real dimension is integrable there. Haar uniqueness transfers the
bound to the supplied Haar measure. The resulting real exponential
moments let the proof differentiate under the integral in \(s\).

This is the fixed-vector convergence and single-parameter holomorphy
in Jacquet, *Archimedean Rankin–Selberg integrals* (2009), formula (7.7)
and Proposition 7.2, pp. 48–49. The formula's identification with a
larger-rank Whittaker functional and estimates for a varying
smaller-rank Whittaker family are separate inputs.
