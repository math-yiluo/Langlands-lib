# Local complex Rankin–Selberg functional equation in equal rank

`complexLocalEqualFunctionalEquation` proves the published `ComplexLocalEqualFunctionalEquationStatement` for every rank, every pair of inducing character lists of the specified length, every nonzero additive parameter, and every pair in the exact compact-finite Whittaker models. It supplies a common right half-plane of absolute convergence and holomorphy, meromorphic continuations of both integrals, and the reflected equation on the codiscrete filter with the prescribed epsilon factor and central sign.

## Induction at the standard character

`complexLocalEqualStandardGrowth` proves that multiplication by the corresponding reciprocal Gamma products gives two entire functions of exponential strip growth. These functions agree with the normalized ordinary and dual integrals on a common right half-plane and obey the normalized reflection identity everywhere. For size one the model coefficients are scalar multiples of the inducing characters. The integral comparison and the one-coordinate Tate equation give the two normalizations and their strip bounds with a common Haar scalar.

Whittaker multiplicity one identifies each exact model with the range of canonical continued Jacquet evaluation. The smooth extension of a compact-finite intertwiner has the required unipotent character. On the long Bruhat chart a finite-order Whittaker functional is a scalar multiple of the character-weighted Haar integral, and restriction to that chart is injective by the vanishing of the transverse jets on the boundary cells. The canonical evaluation has a nonzero value at the identity. Proportionality of the exact intertwiners supplies the identity comparison used by the equal-rank successor.

From the equal-rank equation at size \(n\), the adjacent step proves the normalized equation at sizes \(n+1\) and \(n\). In a strong chamber of inducing exponents, polynomial Gaussian Godement sections span the larger compact-finite module. Each monomial test splits into a square matrix test and a column test. The adjacent unfolding uses the lower equal-rank integrals on the partial row Fourier test and the square matrix equation. Linearity extends the normalized equation and strip growth from the split generators to the canonical range. Continuation along two lines in the larger inducing exponents removes the chamber inequalities. Uniform bounds on the ordinary traces and a common reciprocal Gamma rate on vertical strips preserve strip growth in this continuation.

The equal-rank successor uses the adjacent result at the opposite additive character, obtained by applying the standard-character result to the dual coefficients. Compact rectangular Schwartz tests supported on full-row-rank matrices give the quotient recursion generators for the first canonical coefficient. For such a generator and the prescribed last-row Schwartz test, their product is a square matrix Schwartz test. For tests with finite left-unitary orbit, compact insertion gives finite sums of products of adjacent normalized integrals and normalized matrix factors. Take a sequence of these tests converging in Schwartz space, with the Fourier transforms converging as well. The primal and dual unfolded integrals are controlled in separate chambers of the adjoining exponent. On affine lines where the adjoining exponent varies with the spectral parameter, the two controls give bounds at opposite vertical boundaries. The strip extension theorem yields a locally uniform limit that is holomorphic along complex curves and has strip growth at each fixed adjoining exponent.

The two unfolded traces have the same positive Haar scalar. On the dual side the recursion sign and the mixed Fourier signs identify the double-integral phase with the full epsilon factor multiplied by the required central sign. The ordinary integrals are jointly holomorphic in the adjoining exponent and the spectral variable on a right half-plane over each bounded exponent disk. The identity theorem in the adjoining exponent therefore identifies the square limit with both ordinary normalized traces at the prescribed value. Cancellation of the Haar scalar gives the witnesses for the compact generator. The span of these generators gives the conclusion for the first canonical coefficient, since absolutely convergent normalized pairs with strip growth are closed under addition and scalar multiplication.

## Meromorphic continuations and change of character

The endpoint convergence theorem supplies absolute convergence for the two ordinary integrands. Their integral holomorphy holds there. Far enough to the right, both entire witnesses agree with the normalized integrals and both reciprocal Gamma products are nonzero. Dividing by those products gives the meromorphic continuations with the prescribed ordinary values. The normalized reflection identity then gives the equation with the two pair \(L\)-factors on the codiscrete filter. Conjugation by the change-of-character diagonal transports the Whittaker coefficients and compatible quotient measures. The two integral multipliers satisfy the scaling identity for the pair epsilon factor, which transfers the equation and all convergence and continuation clauses to the requested additive parameter.
