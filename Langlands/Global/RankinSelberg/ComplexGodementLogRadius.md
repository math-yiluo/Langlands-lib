# Logarithmic radius for complex multiplicative integration

The complex measure `multiplicativeComplexLebesgue` is Lebesgue measure
weighted by the inverse squared norm. The proof uses polar coordinates
and integrates the angle over an interval of length `2π`. The polar area
factor leaves the inverse radius, which becomes Lebesgue measure after
substituting the exponential of the logarithmic radius. The pushforward
measure on the logarithmic radius is therefore `2π` times Lebesgue measure.

On a finite complex diagonal the coordinatewise pushforward is the product
of those measures. This supplies the measure conversion from positive
logarithmic coordinates to complex multiplicative coordinates for tests
whose compact and strictly upper integrals are invariant under phases.

[Jacquet, *Archimedean Rankin–Selberg integrals*, §7.1, proof of Proposition 7.1, pp. 43–44](https://www.math.columbia.edu/~hj/PerfectRankinSelberg.pdf).
