# Matrix derivatives of left-translated complex coefficients

In the open chart of invertible matrices, a left-translated function is
written \(u(h)=W(gh)\). The right derivative in direction \(X\) becomes
the matrix derivative \(Du(h)(hX)\). This uses associativity to commute
the right derivative with the left translation. Equality of the chart
expressions holds in a neighborhood of each invertible matrix, so it
can be differentiated once more.

The product rule gives \(D^2u(h)(hX,hX)+Du(h)(hX^2)\) for the repeated
direction. When the direction and its imaginary multiple are paired,
the first-order terms cancel. Thus for a coefficient of an intertwiner
the coordinate square operator in this chart is the Hessian sum over
the real matrix basis, and its value is the coefficient of the square
operator on the inducing vector.

The principal symbol of the Hessian sum on a real covector \(\xi\) is
\(\sum_X \xi(hX)^2\). Left multiplication by \(h\) is invertible and the
directions \(X\) form a real basis, so this symbol is positive whenever
\(\xi\ne0\). This proves ellipticity in the actual matrix chart.
