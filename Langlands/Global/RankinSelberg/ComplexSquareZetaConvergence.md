# Absolute convergence of complex square matrix zeta integrals

`ComplexSquareZetaConvergenceStatement` gives a right half-plane of absolute convergence for the square zeta integral of a complex standard vector and a Schwartz test. The matrix height bound supplies an exponent `M` depending on the inducing characters and a constant depending on the vector. If the real part of `s + (n−1)/2` is at least `n + M`, the determinant-density estimate makes the product of the height power, test, and determinant weight integrable. Haar uniqueness transfers that estimate to the supplied group measure. The norm of the zeta kernel is bounded by a constant multiple of this integrable function, and continuity gives measurability.

This is the absolute convergence used in the square zeta equation. [Jacquet, *Archimedean Rankin–Selberg integrals*, Lemma 3.3 and §4.7](https://doi.org/10.1090/conm/489/09547).
