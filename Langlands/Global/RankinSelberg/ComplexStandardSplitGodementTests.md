# Compact finiteness and Fourier inversion for standard split Godement tests

A square polynomial Gaussian and a polynomial Gaussian on the last column, both of scale \(2\pi\), give a rectangular Schwartz product with finite-dimensional right unitary translate span. The product has a polynomial multiplier in the real and imaginary coordinates of the full rectangle, since the sums of squared entries in the two blocks add. Inverting the fixed real dilation in the polynomial multiplier identifies the product with a dilated matrix Gaussian. The dilation is equivariant for unitary translation, so finiteness follows from the finite polynomial span at scale one.

At every unit-modulus trace scale the inverse split row transform of a standard polynomial Gaussian is again of the same class. The trace transform of the opposite sign is the inverse by Fourier inversion for the self-dual measure. Jacquet introduces the Fourier convention and the standard complex Gaussian in §2, pp. 3 and 10. The square-plus-last-column split and the transformed column appear in §8.3, equations (8.3)–(8.4), pp. 58–59. [Jacquet, *Archimedean Rankin–Selberg Integrals*](https://www.math.columbia.edu/~hj/PerfectRankinSelberg.pdf#page=58)
