# Exponential strip growth of normalized complex square zeta integrals

`complexSquareZetaStripGrowth` gives an entire continuation of a complex square matrix zeta integral multiplied by the reciprocal L-denominator of its inducing characters. The initial identity holds on a right half-plane of absolute convergence, for the given Haar measures, Schwartz function and complex standard vector. The normalized continuation has exponential growth in vertical strips.

The proof takes the published square zeta normalization through the compact average and upper triangular marginal for the standard trace character. Its entire function is a constant multiple of an entire product Tate normalization along the spectral line with slope one in each coordinate. The product Tate strip theorem bounds this specialization. The square convergence theorem and the real parts of the inducing exponents give a common right half-plane for the integral and the product Tate identity. A separate corollary transfers the same bound to any entire square normalization with the prescribed identity on the right, by the identity theorem.

The square matrix zeta integral and its normalized continuation occur in Hervé Jacquet, *Archimedean Rankin–Selberg Integrals* (2009), §4.7, equations (4.3)–(4.4) and Proposition 4.4, author-version pp. 30–31. The diagonal product Tate integration used by the Library’s square normalization parallels the calculation for rectangular Godement sections in §7.1, author-version p. 44. The square integrals supply matrix factors in Jacquet’s local Rankin–Selberg induction.
