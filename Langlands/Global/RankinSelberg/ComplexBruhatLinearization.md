# Linearization of a stabilizing right direction in the Gauss chart

Suppose the right matrix direction `X` at a chart representative agrees with left multiplication by a strictly upper matrix `A`. Both the coordinate and inducing scalar terms of the right derivative vanish at that point. If both matrices are square-zero, the linearization of the coordinate field at that point is cube-zero.

Left upper triangular multiplication leaves the chart coordinates fixed. For a strictly upper direction it also leaves the inducing multiplier fixed at the representative. This is proved by differentiating the affine left path in the chart. Subtracting the left direction in the coordinate field leaves the ambient linear operator `Y ↦ YX − AY`. At the representative this operator kills the base matrix. The product rule therefore expresses the derivative of the field by applying this operator between the derivative of the chart section and the derivative of the coordinates. Differentiating Gauss factorization shows that the remaining directions are upper triangular left multiples of the representative. The ambient operator preserves these directions and its third power is zero, so the induced coordinate operator has third power zero as well.

The affine path lemma also records that the same matrix line defines a Borel element for parameters near zero.
