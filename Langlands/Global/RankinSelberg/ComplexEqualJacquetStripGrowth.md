# Exponential strip growth through the complex equal-rank Jacquet expansion

`complexEqualJacquetStripGrowth` takes a finite expansion on a right half-plane of the ordinary equal-rank complex integral as a sum of adjacent-rank integrals times square matrix zeta integrals. Each square integral uses an actual Schwartz matrix test and a standard vector with the character list obtained by multiplying every character on the larger side by the removed inducing character. Each adjacent-rank integral is assumed to have an entire normalization of exponential strip growth by its prescribed pair L-factor. The conclusion gives the corresponding entire normalization and strip growth for the equal-rank integral by its full pair L-factor.

The proof uses the checked complex square-zeta strip theorem for the matrix terms. The reciprocal pair denominator of the equal-rank integral is the product of the adjacent pair denominator and the square denominator, using symmetry of the pair and multiplication of the inducing lists. Right-half-plane nonvanishing of the square denominator gives the ordinary identity for every square normalization. Multiplication and finite summation of the normalized functions preserve entireness and exponential strip growth. The exact expansion on the right identifies this sum with the required normalization there.

Jacquet’s square terms use matrix coefficients of the second representation twisted by the removed character. `ComplexSquareZetaCoefficientAverage` gives the change of variables that moves the compact coefficient average into the Schwartz test, producing the standard-vector square integral used here. The second inducing list describes the full rank-\(n\) representation, as in the pair-factor products on author-version p. 56.

This is the growth step for the right-half-plane expansion in Hervé Jacquet, *Archimedean Rankin–Selberg Integrals* (2009), §8.2, equation (8.2), author-version pp. 55–57. The square matrix integral and its normalization occur in §4.7, equations (4.3)–(4.4) and Proposition 4.4, pp. 30–31. The existence of the finite expansion and the lower-rank ordinary growth enter as hypotheses.
