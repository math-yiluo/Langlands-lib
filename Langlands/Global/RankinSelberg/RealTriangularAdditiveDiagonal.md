# Additive Tate weights on the full real diagonal

**Provenance.** This is an internal measure and character calculation for the real matrix zeta integrals in Hervé Jacquet, *Archimedean Rankin–Selberg integrals* (2009), §4.7, pp. 30–31, using the one-dimensional Tate normalization of §4.6. The finite-product measure step adapts the Library's `ComplexGodementAdditiveDiagonal` construction to the real density `RealRankZero.invAbs`.

For a finite real diagonal with entries in `ℝˣ`, use the product of the fixed multiplicative measures `RealRankZero.unitsHaar`. Given parities `eᵢ`, complex exponents `uᵢ` and a measurable complex-valued function `F` on the additive coordinate space, `RealDiagonalTateIntegrationStatement` compares the multiplicative integrand `F(d) ∏ᵢ sgn(dᵢ)^eᵢ |dᵢ|^uᵢ` and the additive integrand `F(x) ∏ᵢ RealTate.integrand (fun _ => 1) eᵢ uᵢ xᵢ`. Integrability is equivalent and the Bochner integrals are equal. Each Tate density is set to zero at its coordinate origin. The test function need not factor into one-variable functions. There is no comparison of different Haar normalizations or assertion of a functional equation here.

**Lean proof.** Push each multiplicative measure to `ℝ` by `Units.val` using `map_val_unitsHaar`. In finite products the pushforward commutes with `Measure.pi`. To identify the product density, split off coordinate zero using the measurable equivalence `piFinSuccAbove` and apply `prod_withDensity` to the scalar density and the inductive product. The empty product has density one. Multiplying by the density turns `charFun e u` into the additive Tate factor: for nonzero input this is `cpow_sub` at exponent `u - 1`, and at zero the density and the additive Tate integrand both vanish. Apply the Bochner map and density lemmas to get the two claimed comparisons. The helper `realCharFun_inv` computes the inverted character by retaining its parity and negating its exponent, using the unchanged sign of a nonzero real reciprocal.
