# Ordinary complex polynomial normalizations in equal and adjacent ranks

Assume polynomial normalization of every adjacent ordinary integral for the larger \(GL(r+2,\mathbb C)\) coefficient at scale \(-a\) and the smaller \(GL(r+1,\mathbb C)\) coefficient at scale \(a\). `complexOrdinaryEqualPolynomialStep` gives polynomial normalization in equal rank \(r+2\) for the first coefficient at scale \(a\), the second at scale \(-a\), and last-row tests that are polynomials times the Gaussian of scale \(2\pi\). It uses the supplied inducing lists of length \(r+2\) and compatible quotient measure. Each test has an entire normalization which equals the evaluation of a polynomial in the spectral variable.

With the smaller inducing data fixed, the ordinary polynomial trace of a continued standard Gaussian recursion test extends from the positive adjoining chamber to a disk containing the desired point and an open subset of that chamber. Multiplication by the reciprocal pair \(L\)-factor gives a jointly holomorphic function on the disk and a common right spectral half-plane. Polynomial interpolation and holomorphic uniqueness continue the polynomial fibers. In the strong chamber, canonical Jacquet evaluation of scale-one Gaussian sections and the nonzero dilation multiplier compare these sections to the standard Gaussian recursion tests. Their span gives every compact-finite section there. Continuing the fixed compact profile first in the adjoining exponent and then in the retained exponents gives arbitrary inducing parameters. The canonical model range identifies these values with the supplied Whittaker model.

Starting with the scalar Tate polynomial normalization and alternating the adjacent and equal steps proves `complexOrdinaryPolynomial`. For every trace scale of unit modulus, it covers equal-rank tests with last-row Gaussian scale \(2\pi\) and every ordinary adjacent-rank test at the given trace scale. In both cases the members of the two Whittaker models have opposite scales.

This formalizes Jacquet, *Archimedean Rankin–Selberg Integrals* (2009), Proposition 8.1, author-posted pp. 60–61.
