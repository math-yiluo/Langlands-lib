# Strip growth in the continuation of complex pair normalizations

For fixed angular weights and holomorphic families of inducing exponents, suppose the primal and dual ordinary integrals are jointly holomorphic on a common right half-plane over the parameter disk of radius three. Suppose both ordinary integrals are bounded uniformly in the disk on each closed strip of that half-plane. Entire normalized witnesses with strip exponential growth near parameter zero then give entire normalized witnesses with strip exponential growth at parameter one, with the same reflection equation.

The proof multiplies the two ordinary integrals by their reciprocal Gamma products. It reflects the dual function in the spectral argument and divides by the nonzero epsilon factor with the prescribed scalar. The reciprocal Gamma products have a common exponential rate on every strip. The modulus of the scalar times the epsilon factor depends on the real part of the spectral argument, so both this modulus and its reciprocal are bounded on each strip by continuity on a compact set of parameters and real parts. Both resulting half-plane traces therefore satisfy the fixed-rate hypothesis of the compact parameter continuation theorem. On a small parameter disk the one-variable identity theorem extends the agreements to the whole common convergence half-planes, and the initial reflection equation identifies the left trace. After continuation, reflecting the entire witness and restoring the epsilon factor gives the dual witness with the required strip growth.

This is an internal quantitative form of the continuation in the inducing exponents used by Jacquet in *Archimedean Rankin–Selberg Integrals*, §8.1, printed and PDF pp. 53–54.
