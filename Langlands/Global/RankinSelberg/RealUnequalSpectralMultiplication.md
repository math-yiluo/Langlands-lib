# Spectral multiplication for ordinary real unequal-rank integrals

Simultaneous right shifts of the two Whittaker coefficients by a positive
first diagonal dilation give a corresponding shift on the unipotent
quotient of the smaller group. Quotient measure invariance makes the
integral acquire an exponential of the shifted spectral parameter.
The derivative of the product along this path is the sum of the two tests
with one Whittaker coefficient differentiated in each summand.

For irreducible real standard modules the ordinary and uniform coefficient
models coincide. The real convergence theorem applies to the three
ordinary kernels in the formula. The translated derivative kernels have
integrable absolute-value integrals on each bounded dilation interval by
the covariance identity. Fubini and the fundamental theorem of calculus
therefore give the spectral multiplier.

If all ordinary unequal tests of an irreducible pair have polynomial
normalizations, comparison on a common right half-plane identifies the
normalized sum of the two differentiated integrals with
\(-\bigl(s-(r-t)/2\bigr)\) times the original normalized polynomial.
Scaling the larger Whittaker coefficient realizes scalar multiples of
the polynomials. Their linear span is therefore an ideal of
\(\mathbb C[s]\). If the normalized values cover every spectral point,
the ideal has no common root and contains one. Expressing one as a finite
linear combination yields the finite sum of actual ordinary tests.

This is the unequal-rank differentiation identity used to obtain the
spectral ideal in H. Jacquet, *Archimedean Rankin–Selberg Integrals*
(2009), §15, pp. 108–109 of the author-posted version.
