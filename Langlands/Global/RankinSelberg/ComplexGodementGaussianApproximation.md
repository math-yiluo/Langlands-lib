# Compact tests with a Gaussian approximation bound

Every compactly supported continuous complex test on a finite complex matrix
space can be approximated by a finite sum of coordinate polynomials times
positive integer powers of the fixed Gaussian. The pointwise error is at most
any prescribed positive constant times the fixed Gaussian.

The proof extends the Gaussian and each real coordinate times the Gaussian by
zero to the one-point compactification. These real-valued functions separate
points. The Gaussian separates every matrix from infinity. At two matrices
with the same Gaussian value, equality of all the other generators implies
equality of every real and imaginary coordinate. The evaluated polynomial
algebra is closed under conjugation. Stone–Weierstrass gives uniform
approximation of the continuous extension of the compact test divided by the
Gaussian. Multiplying back by the Gaussian gives the pointwise error bound
and makes each polynomial term have a positive Gaussian power.

Each term is a dilated Gaussian-polynomial Schwartz test. The dilation is
the positive square root of the Gaussian power, and the polynomial is
composed with the inverse dilation on coordinates.

This is an internal approximation result for the standard-test spanning
argument of H. Jacquet, *Archimedean Rankin–Selberg Integrals* (2009),
§7.1, Proposition 7.1(vi) and the proof on author pp. 46–47. The source
describes its standard Schwartz family on p. 10.
