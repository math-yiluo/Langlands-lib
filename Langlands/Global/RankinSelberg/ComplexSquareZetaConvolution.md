# Polynomial convolution by complex square Gaussian tests

For a fixed complex square polynomial Gaussian, a determinant character and a compact-finite inducing vector, the convolution has a continuation with values in the same compact-finite standard module. The continuation is a finite sum of standard vectors with polynomial coefficients. At the identity its inducing value and at every group element its Whittaker value are the corresponding convergent convolutions times the reciprocal singleton-pair \(L\)-factor on right half-planes.

The reproducing compact weight inserts a projection into the group integral. Compact projection commutes with Whittaker evaluation. The projection has a finite expansion whose scalar coefficients are compact averages of the inducing vector. The square polynomial theorem normalizes their integrals. Integrating the finite expansion gives the vector-valued continuation. Left Haar invariance and the reproducing property recover each ordinary convolution from the projected one. The polynomial height estimate gives a common half-plane for each input vector.

This is a vector-valued form of the compact expansion in Jacquet, *Archimedean Rankin–Selberg Integrals* (2009), §8.2 and Proposition 8.1.
