# Compact convolution in the Jacquet expansions

Jacquet, *Archimedean Rankin–Selberg integrals* (2009), §8.2 and §8.3, pp. 56–60, expresses a compact projection of the right-translated inducing vector in a finite basis. These internal lemmas give the coordinate part of that construction. The compact convolution kernel has finite left-translate span. A basis of that span expresses the two-variable kernel as a sum of products, with continuous factors in the integration variable.

Given a family of projected vectors with an injective restriction to the compact group and the stated convolution formula there, their restrictions lie in a single finite-dimensional space. The proof transports that finite dimensionality to the span of the projected vectors. Evaluation on the compact group separates this span. Therefore every coordinate functional is a finite linear combination of evaluations. Haar translation in the convolution and the interchange of a finite sum and integral express each coordinate as a pairing of the original right-translated vector with a continuous compact test. This is the form needed for the matrix zeta equation.

The complex specialization uses Iwasawa decomposition through `ComplexPrincipalSeriesExtension.eq_of_eqOn_unitary` to make restriction injective. Applying the Whittaker intertwiner to the finite sum gives coefficients in the Library Whittaker model. Applying Weyl inverse transpose in the first variable and inverse transpose in the matrix variable gives the paired dual identity with the same compact tests. Both identities in `ComplexLocalCompactProjectionReductionStatement` use the original compact Haar measure. The dual identity parametrizes the compact translate by `k ↦ ᵗk⁻¹`.

The statement requires a continuous kernel with finite left-translate span, projected vectors in the `U(r + 1)`-finite inducing space with the prescribed compact convolution formula, and compatibility of those vectors under the Whittaker map with convolution of its coefficient. Constructing a kernel that fixes the test in the unfolded integral and obtaining the two projection identities for the Library intertwiners remain separate steps.
