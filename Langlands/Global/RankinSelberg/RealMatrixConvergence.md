# Initial convergence of the real square matrix integral

**Provenance.** This is a quantitative starting-domain result for the real form of Jacquet, *Archimedean Rankin–Selberg integrals* (2009), §4.7, pp. 30–31. In the proof of Proposition 4.4 Jacquet invokes Lemma 3.3 to pass from polynomial coefficient growth to absolute convergence. The Lean proof uses the Library's real matrix Haar density and an adjugate estimate, following the polynomial estimate in `ComplexMatrixConvergence` with the real determinant modulus. The explicit sufficient bound is internal to this implementation.

Fix `n = r + 1`. Let `μ` be Haar measure on `GL_n(ℝ)`, `Φ` a Schwartz function on the additive space of real `n` by `n` matrices with the row-sum operator norm, and `f` a measurable complex-valued function on `GL_n(ℝ)` such that `‖f(g)‖ ≤ C H(g)^N` for `C ≥ 0` and `N : ℕ`. The height `H` is the sum of squares of entries of `g` and `g⁻¹`. `RealMatrixInitialConvergenceStatement` gives integrability of `‖Φ(g)‖ ‖f(g)‖ |det g|^q` for `q ≥ n + 2N`. It gives absolute convergence of the corresponding complex matrix zeta integral for `Re(s) ≥ n + 2N` and holomorphy for the strict inequality. The `s` in this statement is the **total** exponent of `|det g|`, before writing it as `s₀ + r/2` in the normalized zeta integral. This bound is sufficient, not optimal. There is no continuation or functional equation in this unit.

**Lean proof.** Each entry norm is at most the row-sum operator norm. The determinant and all adjugate entries are bounded by a factorial times a power of `1 + ‖g‖`, by a finite determinant estimate (using the row replacement formula for adjugate entries). Multiplying an inverse entry by the determinant clears its denominator. Thus `H(g) |det g|²` has polynomial growth in the forward matrix norm. Raising to the `N`th power, the remaining exponent `q - n - 2N` is nonnegative; another determinant estimate and a natural ceiling bound this leftover factor by a polynomial. This bounds `H(g)^N |det g|^(q-n)` by forward matrix growth. A Schwartz seminorm absorbs that polynomial and supplies the additional decay in the integrable forward-density majorant `RealGodement.exists_integrable_matrixDecay` on `μ`. For the complex kernel `norm_realDetPower` gives the norm identity. Finally `differentiableOn_integral_mul_cexp` gives holomorphy from the absolute convergence throughout the open half-plane, using `log |det g|` as the real parameter.
