# Complex Bruhat cells in Gauss coordinates

For a permutation w, apply the lower column solve to the matrix after
right multiplication by the inverse permutation matrix. Its pivot
conditions define an open chart and the lower entries are smooth
coordinates on it. The Gauss upper factor and the chosen representative
from the lower coordinates multiply to the original group element.
Recovery of the Gauss solve also shows that left multiplication by an
upper triangular unit leaves the coordinates fixed and multiplies the
upper factor by that unit.

On the actual upper Bruhat cell, use its upper-by-unipotent coordinates.
The lower part of the Gauss decomposition is exactly the conjugated
unipotent coordinate. Its inverse has nonzero lower entries only in
positions whose indices become strictly upper after applying the
inverse permutation. Conversely, a lower matrix with this entry
condition conjugates to an element of the prescribed unipotent
subgroup. Gauss factorization then puts the original matrix in the
actual Bruhat cell. Thus within its open chart the cell is given by
vanishing of specified coordinates.
