# Residue coefficients of the split-centre equal-rank integral

`HasSplitCentreResidueProfile` describes a function holomorphic off 0 and 1 and meromorphic with order at least −1 at both points. Its order at either point is negative exactly when the product central character is trivial, the period is nonzero, and the corresponding test value is nonzero. The test values are the values of the Schwartz function at 0 and its Fourier transform at 0, respectively. This predicate packages the two residue tests of the implemented continuation.

`equalIntegral_splitCentre_continuation` applies the profile to the equal-rank global Rankin–Selberg integral of a pair of cuspidal vectors, with supplied central characters, a standard additive character and a Schwartz–Bruhat function. Both realizations are trivial on the split centre. The period in the profile is `residueConstant` at exponent 0 for the product of the two cusp functions. The integral converges absolutely and agrees with the continuation in a right half-plane. The continuation is bounded in every vertical strip away from positive-radius discs about 0 and 1.

The norm section is a positive archimedean scalar. Evaluation on a nonzero cuspidal vector shows that each central character is trivial on this section. In the norm-power branch of the central-character dichotomy, differentiating the character on the section at the origin forces its exponent to be zero. The explicit theta-kernel continuation from `EqualAnalyticMain` then adds the two fractions with coefficients `−Φ(0) C/n` and `Φ̂(0) C/n` to an entire function, where `C` is the period and `n` is the matrix rank. The coefficient of each fraction determines the meromorphic order test because the other fraction is analytic at the point in question. In the other branch `EqualAnalyticResidue` makes the central averages vanish. The strip bounds follow from the entire part and the bounds on the fractions away from their poles. `equalIntegral_unfold` identifies the theta-kernel integral with the global integral on the half-plane of convergence.

Cogdell (2004), *Analytic Theory of L-functions for GLₙ*, §2.3, pp. 208–211, especially p. 210 (registered as `cogdell-2004-analytic-theory-gln`, PDF pp. 211–214), obtains the two residue terms by Poisson summation. Getz and Hahn (2024), (2.20), p. 68 and Theorem 11.7.1, p. 309 (registered as `getz-hahn-2024-automorphic-representations`, PDF pp. 85 and 320), give the split-centre normalization used here. This is the integral-side step toward `CuspidalPairPoleStatement`. The period criterion for contragredience and the comparison to the completed Euler product enter after this step.
