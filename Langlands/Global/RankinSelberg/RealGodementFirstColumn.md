# Fourier transformation in the free first Godement column

Let `Φ` be a complex-valued Schwartz function on the real `m × (m + 1)` matrix space. Splitting a rectangle into its first column and trailing square block gives a linear coordinate isomorphism. For each nonzero real parameter `a`, the first-column transform integrates `Φ([x,A])` against `exp(2πia x·ξ)`, with the product of the scalar self-dual measures `√|a| dx`. The result is continuous in `(ξ,A)`. For every natural order `N`, its norm times `(1 + ‖ξ‖)^N (1 + ‖A‖)^N` is bounded uniformly in the frequency `ξ` and trailing block `A`, with Euclidean entry norms.

`firstColumnFourier_decay_seminorm` bounds these weighted values by a continuous real seminorm of the original test. It composes the partial Fourier seminorm with the continuous pullback that splits off the first column.

`firstColumnFourierLinear` bundles the integral at fixed frequency and trailing block as a complex-linear form. The integrability of the original affine slice justifies addition under the integral, and scalar multiplication commutes with the Fourier character.

Left multiplication by `h` acts on the free column as `x ↦ h x` and multiplies the trailing block by `h`. The entry pairing identifies the new frequency as `h⁻ᵀ ξ`. The real linear change of variables on the column contributes `|det h|⁻¹`. This gives the column substitution from equation (7.6) to (7.7) of Hervé Jacquet, *Archimedean Rankin–Selberg Integrals*, §7.2, p. 48. For `m ≥ 1`, reversing rows and columns takes `[I,X]` to `[w_m X,I]` and takes Jacquet's last basis vector to the first. This reversal preserves the pairing. Jacquet integrates against the inverse character, so for his parameter `b` the Lean specialization at the first basis vector uses `a = −b`. Both parameters give the same self-dual measures.
