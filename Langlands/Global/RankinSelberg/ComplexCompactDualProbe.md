# The dual compact profile of an inverse-translation coefficient

For a compact-finite complex standard section f and a unitary point x,
average `k ↦ f(k⁻¹ x)` against the inverse of the inducing character on the
compact diagonal torus. Left translation of k by a torus element changes
the resulting compact profile by the inverse inducing character. The
value at k = 1 is f(x), by the original torus covariance and the Haar
probability normalization.

The torus weights have norm one. Integrating the pointwise norm bounds
therefore preserves both uniform value bounds and uniform bounds for the
compact translation difference from the identity value.

The restriction of f to the unitary group is polynomial in the entries
and their conjugates. The inverse-product polynomial has separated
variables by `UnitaryFinitePolynomial`. Integration against the torus
character acts on the compact coefficient in each separated term and gives
a polynomial in k. The principal-series extension of that compact profile
is therefore smooth and carries the inverse inducing characters with the
same normalized modulus factor.

The compact integral of the dual extension against a right translate of
another section equals the compact integral of the original inverse
coefficient against that translate. Fubini reduces the calculation to a
fixed torus element. Left invariance of compact Haar and the covariance of
the translated section cancel the torus character. This equality supplies
the product with modulus covariance used in the opposite-cell change of
variables for compact averages.

This internal construction uses the complex compact picture and compact
integration setting of Jacquet, *Archimedean Rankin–Selberg Integrals*
(2009), §4.
