# The split partial Fourier test in two-block coordinates

A Schwartz test given as a leading block factor times separate column factors has the leading, trailing, and frequency factors required by the joint convergence estimate. The leading factor is unchanged. The trailing factor is the product of the original column functions preceding the last column. The frequency factor is the positive Fourier transform of the last column function.

The proof first builds the Schwartz product by induction on the number of trailing columns. Restriction to the preceding columns and projection to the last column are continuous real-linear maps. The entrywise norm of the whole matrix is at most the sum of the norms of the two parts, so the Schwartz product estimate applies at each step. The partial Fourier identity for the split test and reindexing of the trailing columns then give the exact factorization. This is the split-test case of the partial Fourier transform used in Jacquet’s Section 10.4.
