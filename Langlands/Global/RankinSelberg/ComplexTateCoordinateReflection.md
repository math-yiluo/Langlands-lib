# Reflection of one coordinate in a normalized product Tate integral

The normalization multiplies the product Tate integral by the reciprocal complex Gamma factor at `u j + |m j| / 2` in every coordinate. Assume every coordinate parameter has positive real part and the selected coordinate has real part less than one. The positive trace Fourier transform of the test in that coordinate, together with reversal of its angular weight and reflection of its parameter about `1/2`, multiplies the normalized product integral by `I ^ |m i|`.

For a single coordinate, insert the adapted angular Gaussian in the scalar Tate integral identity. Its Fourier transform and Gamma integral supply the phase and the two Gamma factors. The normalized identity is valid with additive area on both sides because the scalar twice-area measures cancel. A measure-preserving split at the selected coordinate and absolute convergence give the two product integrals as iterated integrals. The coordinate Fourier theorem identifies their inner Schwartz slices, so the scalar normalized reflection can be applied for every value of the other coordinates.

This is the common-strip step for the diagonal Tate integrals in Jacquet, *Archimedean Rankin–Selberg Integrals* (2009), §7.1, proof of Proposition 7.1, p. 44.
