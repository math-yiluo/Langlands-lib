# Absolute convergence of the unfolded adjacent-rank complex kernel

`ComplexAdjacentConvergenceStatement` proves absolute integrability of the unfolded product in equation (8.3) on a right half-plane for complex Whittaker vectors and square and row Schwartz tests. The threshold can depend on the character and vectors.

Submultiplicativity of the matrix height bounds the translated smaller Whittaker vector by a product of heights. The determinant density and the square Schwartz estimate then bound the general linear group factor. On the quotient, the first Whittaker vector supplies arbitrary decay in positive simple-root logarithms, and the row test supplies arbitrary decay in the positive last logarithmic coordinate. The toral exponent estimate controls the sum of absolute logarithmic coordinates after including the compatible quotient measure's density. These two factors give an integrable product majorant. The paired characters make the quotient kernel independent of the representative, and its lift is jointly continuous.

This is the convergence argument used to justify the adjacent-rank unfolding. [Jacquet, *Archimedean Rankin–Selberg integrals*, §8.3, equation (8.3)](https://doi.org/10.1090/conm/489/09547).
