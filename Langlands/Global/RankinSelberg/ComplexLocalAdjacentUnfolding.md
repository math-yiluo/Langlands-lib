# Change of variables for the complex adjacent-rank integral

This internal comparison implements the changes of variables leading to Jacquet, *Archimedean Rankin–Selberg integrals* (2009), §8.3, equation (8.3). Write `H = GL_(r+1)(ℂ)` and `Q = N_(r+1)(ℂ) \ H`. Its recursion premise is the smaller-group integral for the restriction of the larger Whittaker function to `diag(g,1)`. Here `Φ₁` is the square-matrix part of the rectangle test and `Φ₂` denotes the last-column test after the partial Fourier transform. The hypothesis on the product of `U` and the right translate of `V` expresses its left-unipotent invariance. Absolute integrability is required for the final joint kernel on `Q × H`.

In the integral over `k ∈ H`, put `k = g z⁻¹`, so that `k⁻¹g = z`. The determinant characters at `k⁻¹` and `g` combine to the single character at `z`. The determinant powers combine to `|det k|^s |det z|^(s+r/2)`. Inversion preserves the group Haar measure because the modular character of `H` is trivial. Right translation preserves the compatible quotient measure, giving the measure-preserving shear from `(N g,z)` to `(N (g z⁻¹),z)`. Fubini then expresses the original unequal-rank integral as the matrix integral of smaller equal-rank integrals, with the same Haar and quotient measures. The comparison retains both the recursion identity and convergence of the joint kernel as explicit inputs.

Their relation to the Schwartz tests in the recursive Whittaker function is part of the proposed application. The corank-one dual integral is first expressed as the ordinary integral of the duals using `ComplexLocalAdjacentIntegrals.lean`. The same change of variables then unfolds it with the reflected matrix test.

Replacing the matrix variable `z` by `(-I)z` removes the reflection in that test. The normalized determinant norm is unchanged and the inverse determinant character contributes its value at `-1` to the power `r + 1`. This cancels the sign of the dual recursion because the square of that power is `1`. The shift of the right-translated dual coefficient contributes its central scalar `c`. The paired statement retains the primal and dual recursion identities, the two joint integrability hypotheses, and the left-unipotent and central covariance identities. In the intended Whittaker-model application `c` is `complexCentralSign es` for the inducing list `es` of the smaller second vector.
