# Ordinary real normalized continuation through rank two

`realOrdinaryLowRankContinuation` proves `RealOrdinaryLowRankContinuationStatement`, the real ordinary normalization for the equal-rank pairs of total rank one or two and the unequal-rank pair of total ranks two and one. For all allowed real block lists with arbitrary complex exponents, the test integral has an entire normalization by its original pair \(L\)-factor. The tests use the prescribed opposite additive scales and supplied compatible quotient measure. The equal-rank tests include every Schwartz function of the last row. The entire normalizations include values at the reciprocal-denominator zeros.

`realTemperedWhittakerBoundAtRank_one` proves the scalar bound required for total rank two. Lists with only character or weight-zero blocks use the tempered character estimate. Any list of total rank two with a nonzero-weight induced block consists of that single block. Its real determinant twist disappears for purely imaginary exponents, and the discrete-block bound supplies the exact half-modulus in that case. The rank-one scalar and ordinary normalization statements give the corresponding facts in total rank one.

The canonical range and evaluation give equal character growth, and the adjacent growth step supplies the character pair of total ranks two and one. The canonical Jacquet families have uniform ascending membership on the parameter disk. In rank one the expansion is the original character list. In rank two the exact ascending model equals the exact original model, so the same families have the original block membership used on the imaginary axis. The fixed-rank block assembly compares the expanded and block factors, using the scalar bounds at these ranks to glue the primal and reflected convergent normalizations. Holomorphic division in the parameter then specializes to the original exponents. The proof preserves the full test spaces, including their zero elements.

This is the holomorphic-multiple conclusion of Theorem 2.1(ii) for the ordinary real integrals (2.4) and (2.6) in these ranks. It uses the principal-series comparison and the imaginary-exponent argument of §11.
