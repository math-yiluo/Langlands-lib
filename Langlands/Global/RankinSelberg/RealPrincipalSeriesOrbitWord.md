# Joint continuity and height bounds of translated real derivative words

**Provenance.** This is an internal analytic input for carrying the real matrix identity of Jacquet, *Archimedean Rankin–Selberg integrals* (2009), §4.7, from evaluation coefficients to compact-derivative-bounded linear forms. Jacquet gives a moderate-growth estimate in the introduction to Proposition 4.4 (p. 31); the joint continuity and explicit separate height powers below are implemented estimates, not a quotation of that proposition. The argument extends `RealPrincipalSeriesCoefficientGrowth` from compact evaluation points to arbitrary group points. Submultiplicativity of the real height follows by the same Frobenius norm argument as the Library's complex height bound.

Fix a rank `n = r + 1` and the smooth real principal series of the position characters of an inducing list. Given a section `f` and a finite list `Xs` of Lie-algebra matrices, `orbitWord r ds Xs f g x` evaluates the corresponding iterated right derivative of the right translate `y ↦ f(y g)` at `x`. `RealPrincipalSeriesOrbitWordStatement` gives joint continuity in `(g, x)` and a natural `N` depending only on the inducing list and rank such that, for every word and section, some `C ≥ 0` gives

`‖orbitWord r ds Xs f g x‖ ≤ C * H(x)^N * H(g)^(N + Xs.length)`

on the whole group product. Here `H` is the sum of squares of forward and inverse entries. The evaluation-point dependence can thus be bounded separately on a fixed compact set. The factor at `g` grows with word length; neither this statement nor its helpers assert a theta identity.

**Lean proof.** The Frobenius expression for `H` gives its continuity and the bound `H(xg) ≤ H(x)H(g)`. A right derivative of a right translate is computed by conjugating the direction by `g⁻¹` and taking the derivative at `xg`. For a list of directions this conjugates every entry. Expand the last conjugated direction into the finite matrix-unit basis. The coefficients of that expansion are continuous functions of `g` and each is bounded by a constant times `H(g)` by the Frobenius norm estimate. The resulting finite sum proves joint continuity by reverse induction on the word. The published growth bound for the empty derivative word, composed with `xg` and the submultiplicativity estimate, starts the height induction. The same expansion adds one factor of `H(g)` per direction, with no additional `x` factor. Smoothness in `x` for fixed `g` follows because the iterated derivative is still a section of the principal series.
