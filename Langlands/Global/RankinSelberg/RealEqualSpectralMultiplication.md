# Spectral multiplication for ordinary real equal-rank integrals

Radial differentiation of the last-row Schwartz function implements the affine
spectral multiplier in the real equal-rank Rankin–Selberg integral. In rank
\(n\), the multiplier is \(-ns-\lambda_d-\lambda_e\), where the two central
exponents are the sums of the unshifted position exponents.

The proof integrates the scalar Lie action on each real Whittaker coefficient
along the positive scalar subgroup. Invariance of the quotient measure under
right translation then relates the integral with row dilation by \(e^x\) to the
original integral by an exponential factor. The maximum over \(|x|\leq 1\) of
the norm of a dilated Schwartz function is continuous and still rapidly
decreasing. The real convergence theorem applied to these majorants permits
parameter differentiation at zero. The gauge bounds on the coefficients are
hypotheses of the result. They hold for the uniform derivative model by the
Library's real Whittaker gauge bound.

This is the half-plane integral identity underlying the equal-rank
polynomial-ideal argument in H. Jacquet, *Archimedean Rankin–Selberg Integrals*
(2009), §15, pp. 108–109 of the author-posted version. The proof uses the real
quotient covariance and differentiates the row rather than the Whittaker
coefficients.
