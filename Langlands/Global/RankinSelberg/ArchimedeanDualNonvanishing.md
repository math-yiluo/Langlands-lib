# Nonvanishing through the local functional equation

The dual integral for the same test as a nonzero ordinary integral is holomorphic and nonzero somewhere on a right half-plane, provided the local functional equation supplies its meromorphic continuation data. The real and complex statements take the corresponding Library functional equation as a premise.

The proof finds a nonzero value of the ordinary integral inside the half-plane where it agrees with its meromorphic continuation. This makes the continuation nonzero away from a discrete set. The reflected reciprocal of the dual \(L\)-factor denominator is also nonzero away from a discrete set, since the denominator is entire and has a nonzero value. Choose a point \(s\) where both factors are nonzero, the functional equation holds, and \(1-s\) belongs to the half-plane of the dual integral. Such points exist in a punctured neighborhood inside the reflected half-plane. The epsilon factor is a unit, so the functional equation forces the dual continuation and hence the dual integral to be nonzero at \(1-s\).

At a real or complex place, the prescribed central sign is a power of a central character value in \(\mathbb C^\times\), so it supplies the nonzero constant required in this argument. The specializations keep the original Whittaker functions, Schwartz function, and quotient measure throughout.
