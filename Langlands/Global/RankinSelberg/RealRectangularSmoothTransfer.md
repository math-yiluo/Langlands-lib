# Smooth vector factorization along real rectangular rows

`RealRectangularSmoothTransferStatement` compares a real rectangular prefix with the maximal rectangle for a smooth vector of a jointly continuous complex Banach representation. The larger group acts through a supplied continuous monoid homomorphism into the units of a finite-dimensional real normed algebra. The continuous complex-linear evaluation forms on the word-topology smooth space satisfy Whittaker covariance. The hypotheses give commuting lower-row exponential lifts and a height bound for the mapped upper columns that is linear in the maximum of one and the column norm. They also give a polynomial bound in the height for the representation operator norm on all ambient units.

Smooth vector factorization along the lower row inserts one row integral with a compactly supported smooth weight. The upper-column character formula evaluates at the pairing with that row, so Schwartz averaging absorbs the weight into a genuine smooth vector. This averaging uses a single vector for all evaluation forms in the quotient integral. Changing Lebesgue measure to the supplied row Haar measure gives the corresponding inverse scalar in the profiles. The fixed weight depends only on the spectral parameter and the quotient variable. The Rankin–Selberg specialization uses the determinant power. The terminal family is chosen independently of the spectral parameter. At each parameter for which every terminal kernel is absolutely integrable under the quotient-times-rectangle measure, Fubini gives absolute integrability of the prefix and equality of its integral with the terminal sum.

The rectangular integrals use equation (2.5) in Jacquet, *Archimedean Rankin–Selberg integrals* (2009), p. 6. The smooth factorization is Lemma 6.1 on p. 37. The column averaging and the one-row comparison use Lemma 6.2 and its proof on pp. 38–39 in §6.1. [Author-posted edition](https://www.math.columbia.edu/~hj/PerfectRankinSelberg.pdf#page=37)
