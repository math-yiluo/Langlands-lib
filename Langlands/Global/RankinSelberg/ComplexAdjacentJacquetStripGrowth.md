# Complex adjacent-rank normalizations for split tests

`ComplexAdjacentJacquetStripGrowthStatement` uses the continued Godement Whittaker value of a column product on `GL_(r+2)(ℂ)`, a smaller Whittaker vector, and a second vector given by a Whittaker intertwiner on `GL_(r+1)(ℂ)`. The square test has a finite-dimensional inverse left unitary translate span. Entire normalizations of exponential strip growth for the smaller equal-rank tests with the fixed transformed column give the same conclusion for the adjacent ordinary integral, with the factor of the enlarged inducing list. The continued value is evaluated at zero added shift, so the adjoining character includes its exponent.

The proof inserts a reproducing compact weight into the unfolded integral. Compact projection replaces the translated Whittaker factor by finitely many Whittaker vectors times compact matrix coefficients. The Borel sigma algebra of the unitary group is the pullback of the ambient Borel sigma algebra, so transporting its Haar measure between these two descriptions gives the same coefficient integrals. Each determinant-twisted square zeta integral absorbs the compact coefficient into a left average of the square Schwartz test. On a common right half-plane the product of the two smaller denominators gives the prescribed adjacent-rank normalization. The finite sum of normalized products, multiplied by the sign of the adjoining character in the split restriction, gives the entire function and the exponential strip bound.

`ComplexAdjacentJacquetPolynomialStatement` specializes the square factor to a polynomial Gaussian of scale \(2\pi\). The left compact average preserves this class. The square zeta polynomial theorem supplies a polynomial for each normalized square factor. If the smaller equal-rank normalizations for the fixed transformed row are polynomial, the same product expansion makes the continued split test's ordinary normalization a polynomial.

This implements the ordinary split-test part of H. Jacquet, *Archimedean Rankin–Selberg Integrals* (2009), §8.3, author pp. 58–60, especially the compact insertion and finite sum following (8.3). The square integral is the one in §4.7, Proposition 4.4, author pp. 30–31. [Registered author edition](https://www.math.columbia.edu/~hj/PerfectRankinSelberg.pdf)
