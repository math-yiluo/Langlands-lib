# The local complex Rankin–Selberg functional equation for unequal ranks

`complexLocalUnequalFunctionalEquation` proves `ComplexLocalUnequalFunctionalEquationStatement` in `Langlands.Global.RankinSelbergGlobal`. It covers the two integrands, holomorphy in a right half-plane, meromorphic continuation, and the functional equation off a discrete set for the exact Whittaker models and measures of the Library statement. This is the complex induced-representation case of Theorem 2.1(i)–(iii). The proof uses Jacquet’s adjacent-rank and larger-gap recurrences in Sections 8–10. [Jacquet, *Archimedean Rankin–Selberg integrals*](https://doi.org/10.1090/conm/489/09547)

## Rank induction and test functions

The induction works with both zeta integrals normalized by reciprocal local factors. The normalized continuations are entire and have exponential growth on vertical strips. The rank-one complex Tate computation starts the equal-rank growth induction. The adjacent-rank step uses the equal-rank result and square Godement–Jacquet reflection. It supplies the next equal-rank step and the base case of the unequal-rank induction. Change of trace character transports the standard-character equal-rank result to the character used in the adjacent step. [Unequal-rank induction](ComplexUnequalGrowthInduction.md)

For a larger gap, the induction hypothesis concerns `GL_(t+q+2)(ℂ) × GL_(t+1)(ℂ)` and the step adjoins a character `d` to the larger list. The proof first takes a rotated Gaussian monomial Godement generator in the enlarged model. Its test splits into a square leading factor and factors on the remaining blocks. The leading square test has a finite left unitary orbit span. Multiplicity one expresses the retained larger Whittaker function as the value of the continued Jacquet intertwiner, so the smaller-rank reflection can be applied to its translates. [Monomial reduction](ComplexUnequalFromMonomialGrowth.md)

## Two determinant variables

The ordinary and dual restriction formulas use the same positive Haar constant `cH`. The ordinary chart carries an additional Weyl character. The ordinary and dual Mellin lemmas identify the original integrals with the chart integrals on a common right half-plane and give absolute moments for the product of the quotient and leading determinants. The dual calculation uses the exact rectangular argument of the dual Whittaker function, the inverse-transpose quotient shear, and integration over the Fourier-transformed rectangle. [Ordinary Mellin formula](ComplexGapOrdinaryMellin.md), [dual Mellin formula](ComplexGapDualMellin.md)

At two independent positive scales the proof uses the rapid ordinary and reflected weights of Gaussian rate `1/2`. Joint integrability permits the changes in order of integration. The first comparison reflects the square leading integral while the retained coordinates are fixed. The second reflects the translated smaller pair using the induction hypothesis. The Weyl character identity `adjoiningWeylSigns`, together with the fact that the character value at the relevant power of `-1` squares to one, turns the resulting tail Weyl character into the ordinary character with the adjoining phase. Both sides then carry the same Haar constant. [Scaled comparisons](ComplexGapScaleComparison.md)

The two-scale identity is integrated in the relative logarithmic scale. Absolute product moments justify the Mellin convolution, whose transform multiplies the two lower local factors and combines the two half-rate Gaussians. The resulting one-scale identity gives the entire normalized continuations and their strip bounds. Agreement on the right half-plane identifies them with the normalized original integrals. [Continuation from the separated identity](ComplexGapGrowthFromSeparatedTheta.md)

## Passage to the published proposition

Gaussian spanning first gives the normalized equation in the strict inducing chamber. Holomorphic continuation along affine parameter lines removes the chamber and the constraint on the adjoined exponent. The canonical Jacquet range identifies the resulting functions with the full finite Whittaker model. The unequal-rank induction now applies at every strict rank inequality. Finally, the analytic reduction combines the normalized reflection with the convergence and holomorphy results for the original integrals. Dividing by the reciprocal local factors gives meromorphic continuations and yields the stated equation away from their discrete zero sets. [Canonical growth](ComplexGapCanonicalGrowth.md), [removal of normalization](ComplexLocalUnequalFromNormalized.md)
