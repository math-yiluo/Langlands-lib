# Initial convergence of ordinary integrals for genuine smooth real pairs

`RealPairedOrdinaryConvergenceStatement` gives a common right half-plane of
absolute convergence and holomorphy for padded paired Whittaker coefficients
on a real unipotent quotient. The scalar form on the genuine smooth product
representation has opposite Whittaker scales in the two independent groups.
It is invariant under the common upper unipotents acting by simultaneous
leading padding. The comparison rank is at most the rank of each group.
A Schwartz test of the common last row is allowed at every comparison rank.
The integral without a row test is covered when at least one of the two
paddings has a positive identity block. The abscissa is uniform over all
smooth vectors and all allowed rows. The determinant exponent has the
ordinary half-sum shift of the two padding gaps.

The simultaneous embedding of `torus y * k` splits as the two commuting
slot embeddings. Their matrices are torus points with logarithmic
coordinates padded by zeros, times orthogonal matrices. Apply the paired
Whittaker gauge in one slot and bound the height power of the other slot
using its toral coordinates. The sum of absolute logarithmic coordinates
is unchanged by padding, so the resulting toral growth allowance is
uniform. A Schwartz row supplies arbitrary decay in the positive part of
the last coordinate. With a strict padding, the simple root connecting the
last comparison coordinate with the first padding coordinate supplies the
same decay. Continuity of the paired scalar orbit and invariance of the
last row under upper unipotents give a continuous invariant kernel.
The common toral convergence theorem applies with the real determinant
shift to obtain the bound and holomorphy.

Jacquet uses the last-row decay and the additional simple root of a strict
padding in the ordinary convergence estimates in §5.1, p. 32. Here the
single scalar coefficient comes from the product group used in the rank
comparison in Lemma 14.1, pp. 105–107.
[H. Jacquet, *Archimedean Rankin–Selberg Integrals* (2009)](https://www.math.columbia.edu/~hj/PerfectRankinSelberg.pdf#page=32).
