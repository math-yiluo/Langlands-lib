# A compact-finite standard section with nonzero opposite-cell mass

For any complex inducing characters and any fixed polynomial moment, the
section `momentSection` has that moment once the shift of its minor powers
is large enough. This section equals one at the identity. Adding j to the
shift multiplies its opposite-cell restriction by the inverse Gram product
to the j-th power.

The inverse Gram product is continuous, is one at the identity, and lies
between zero and one. Off each identity neighborhood it is bounded
uniformly below one. Indeed the Gram product is at least one plus the
squared norm in upper-entry coordinates. The proof applies
`exists_pos_integral_pow_of_peak`, factored from the chamber nonvanishing
argument. On a sufficiently small compact neighborhood of the identity
the real part of the original section is positive. The weighted integral
there decays more slowly than the bound for negative contributions outside
the original neighborhood. At a sufficiently high power the full integral
therefore has strictly positive real part. The enlarged shift retains the
prescribed moment and the exact inducing character.

This is an internal construction of the nonzero mass needed to normalize
compact averages in the proof of the complex local functional equation.
