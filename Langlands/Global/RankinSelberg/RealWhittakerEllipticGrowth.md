# A common growth exponent for real Whittaker intertwiners

`realWhittakerUniformGrowth` proves `RealWhittakerUniformGrowthStatement`. On an irreducible real standard module, every ordinary Whittaker intertwiner satisfies the uniform Whittaker intertwiner condition. One polynomial exponent in the real matrix height works for every source vector. Each vector can have its own bounding constant.

Choose the adapted Cartan basis of real matrices by transporting the Library's rational archimedean basis to its unique real place. The sum of its squared directions in the enveloping algebra is the projected central Casimir plus twice the projected compact square sum. The infinitesimal character makes the first operator scalar. Each skew direction preserves the finite-dimensional compact orbit span, by differentiating the orthogonal exponential curve in that span. Consequently the full square sum preserves this span, and a monic polynomial in the sum annihilates its original vector.

The intertwining identity in real Lie directions transfers this polynomial equation to the coefficient and its left translates. The highest-order symbol is a power of a sum of squares of the covector on the real basis, so it is nonzero on every nonzero real covector. The interior estimate for elliptic left-invariant operators bounds each first derivative at the identity of a left translate by the supremum of the translated coefficient on a compact set. Submultiplicativity of the matrix height preserves the exponent in this compact bound. The resulting estimates retain the exponent under each basis direction and, by linearity, all complexified Lie directions.

Fix the exponent of any nonzero source vector. The source vectors whose coefficients satisfy this exponent form a subspace. The compact translation law and submultiplicativity give compact stability, and the elliptic estimate gives Lie stability. The nonzero vector belongs to the subspace, so irreducibility makes it the whole source.

This is an internal consequence of the Library's Cartan square-sum decomposition, infinitesimal character and elliptic interior estimate.
