# Localization by the observed tail columns

`tailObservation` records the first tail column of the retained matrix and the tail columns of its inverse transpose. These are the arguments of the split tail tests. As a sequence of these observations approaches the identity observation, `complexGapTailObservation` gives a subsequence of retained points converging to a point whose matrix is an embedded leading unitary. The identity observation lies in the image. `exists_tailObservation_neighborhood` gives the corresponding open-neighborhood statement.

The block product expresses the inverse tail rows times the compact matrix as the inverse triangular tail block followed by the fixed tail projection. Along a convergent compact subsequence, the triangular block therefore converges. Its Gram matrix tends to the identity. The limit is upper triangular and unitary with nonnegative real diagonal entries, so it is the identity. Taking logarithms of the diagonal entries gives convergence of the logarithmic tail to zero. The triangular factor tends to the identity, and the first observed column gives convergence of the remaining head to zero. These coordinate arguments use the tail chart associated with [Jacquet, *Archimedean Rankin–Selberg Integrals*, §10.2](https://www.math.columbia.edu/~hj/PerfectRankinSelberg.pdf).
