# Strip growth at the base of the equal-rank induction

For \(GL_1(\mathbb C)\times GL_1(\mathbb C)\) the model coefficients are scalar multiples of their characters. The rank-zero integral comparison identifies each ordinary trace with a scalar times a one-coordinate Tate integral at the spectral exponent of the product character. The dual trace uses the inverse characters and the row Fourier test. The Haar comparison supplies the same scalar for a trace before and after taking the dual.

The normalized product Tate integral is entire and has exponential vertical-strip growth along affine spectral lines. It gives the two entire witnesses, with their right-half-plane agreements by the rank-zero Tate comparison. The rank-zero local equation and the normalized meromorphic comparison give their reflection identity everywhere. This is the growth-strengthened rank-zero case of Jacquet (2009), Theorem 2.1.
