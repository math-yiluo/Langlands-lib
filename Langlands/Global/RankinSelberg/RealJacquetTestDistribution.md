# The real Jacquet distribution on the strict chamber and the reversal cell

`RealJacquetTestDistributionStatement` concerns the distribution obtained by Borel-averaging a real matrix test and applying the convergent Jacquet functional. Strict dominance refers to the position exponents of the normalized character list. The distribution has the left upper-unipotent invariance and the right Whittaker law for the inverse additive character. It has order zero on every compact support in the invertible matrices.

To prove the bound, the Jacquet functional is dominated by the integral for the positive principal-series section obtained from the real parts of the exponents. Its value on the orthogonal subgroup is one. The absolute integral of that section is finite in the chamber, which bounds the functional by the supremum of the original section on the orthogonal subgroup. The Borel average on that subgroup has a common compact integration set determined by the test support. Its supremum is bounded by the order-zero seminorm of the test.

On the reversal cell the orbit coordinates are `b w₀ u`. A compact test on the cell pulls back to compact support in the Borel and unipotent variables. Fubini identifies the dominant distribution on this test with the product-measure integral of the inverse inducing weight and the positive Jacquet phase. Each diagonal inducing weight is a fixed sign factor times an exponential linear in the exponents. Holomorphic integration on the common compact support gives an entire scalar function of all the exponents for every test on the reversal cell.

This is an internal seed for extension through the lower Bruhat strata in the real Rankin–Selberg proof. The order-zero estimate on the full invertible domain uses strict dominance.
