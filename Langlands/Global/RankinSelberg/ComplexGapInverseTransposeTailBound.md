# Domination of the complex inverse-transpose tail

`complexGapInverseTransposeTailBound` gives a pointwise majorant in the two-block
right-tail chart. The first test evaluates the physical first tail column.
The second evaluates the trailing columns of the inverse transpose. Extra
fixed powers of their norms are allowed. For a sufficiently large leading
determinant exponent, a choice of simple-root decay gives an integrable
exponential in the leading torus and arbitrary decay orders in the first
rectangle column, the inverse unipotent tail, and the logarithmic tail.

Removing the unitary matrix bounds the first rectangle column by the
physical test argument and bounds the inverse tail block by the
inverse-transpose test argument. In triangular coordinates the inverse
unipotent matrix is obtained from the inverse tail block by multiplying
by the diagonal. Its norm costs an exponential in the sum of absolute
logarithmic coordinates. The first physical column and the last inverse
row control the two endpoint directions. The simple-root estimate absorbs
this cost and the extra tail exponential. The Schwartz seminorms supply
the powers needed for the remaining coordinate bounds.

This is a derived pointwise estimate for the alternate column arrangement
of H. Jacquet, *Archimedean Rankin–Selberg Integrals* (2009), §10.2,
formula (10.5), author pp. 72–73, and for the inverse-transpose Schwartz
bounds in §10.4, Lemma 10.7, pp. 79–81.
