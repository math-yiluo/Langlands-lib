# Localization of the compact real Godement kernel

Fix a smooth upper position principal-series vector with leading character `c` and trailing position data `ds`. The lower compact-picture cutoff is formed with those same trailing position characters. On the upper orthogonal subgroup, the section formed with the Gram and angular cutoffs has a kernel with two scalar cutoff factors. The remaining kernel is the upper vector at `diag(1,h) * k`, multiplied by the lower section with constant scalar cutoff at `h⁻¹` and by `|det h|^(1/2)`.

A compact invertible Gram window keeps the lower integration variable in a compact set. When the lower Gram matrix is the identity, the lower matrix is orthogonal. Its signed orthogonal factor at the inverse is then the inverse itself. If the angular sum of squares also vanishes, the lower matrix is diagonal with signs. The lower section is evaluated at the inverse lower matrix. Its character on the diagonal signs is the inverse of the upper vector’s character on the embedded signs because their trailing position characters agree. The determinant factor is one and the characters cancel. The remaining kernel takes the value of the original upper vector at `k`.

The kernel is continuous. Inside a fixed compact lower Gram window times the upper orthogonal subgroup, a closed set on which the kernel differs from the original vector by at least a given positive number is compact. The sum of the Gram distance and the angular sum is strictly positive there. Its positive minimum supplies a single localization radius for all upper orthogonal matrices. This estimate is derived for the Library’s compact-approximation proof of the K-finite case of the generation result in Hervé Jacquet, *Archimedean Rankin–Selberg Integrals*, Section 7.1, Proposition 7.1(v).
