# Unequal-rank pointwise factorization from normalized infinite Whittaker tests

`unequalInfiniteWhittakerReduction` proves `UnequalInfiniteWhittakerReductionStatement.{u,uQ}`: the input `UnequalInfiniteWhittakerTestStatement.{u,uQ}` implies the existing Library proposition `UnequalPointwiseFactorizationStatement.{u,uQ}`. The two universes remain independent. The implication includes the adjacent-rank case, where the rectangle has no rows, and permits an empty retained finite set.

## Retained input

Use the target's number field, compactness, unitary adelic character trivial on the number field, ranks, cuspidal realizations and contragredients, spectral presentations, finite exceptional and additive support conditions, infinite place parameters, and canonical finite additive components at the retained places. For the two presentations take supplied standard and inverse-character Whittaker functionals on their retained factors, with reference tuples on which those functionals take value one.

`UnequalInfiniteWhittakerTestStatement` asks for two tensors in the actual infinite source spaces, and for actual real and complex place restrictions in the two ranks, for the character and its inverse. With the normalized references, each infinite residual is the product of its place restrictions times a nonzero scalar. The two identities are separate, and are stated on all arguments: the residual only evaluates at the point with the given infinite components and with identity finite components. The input also supplies quotient measures and ordinary and dual products of archimedean integrals nonzero somewhere in each right half-plane. The nonzero parameters need not be the same for the two products. No finite integral nonvanishing or rectangular support is assumed by the implication.

## Implemented proof

1. At each retained finite place, `UnequalFiniteWhittakerTests.exists_tests` uses the p-adic local functional equation and rational continuation to select one pair of test vectors with both right-half-plane nonvanishing properties. The local model functions are transported to the exact presentation factors, with normalized finite reference vectors. The ordinary and dual kernels of these functions integrate to the prescribed local test integrals for every spectral parameter.
2. Apply the archimedean input to those references. Tensor the chosen infinite vectors with the transported finite vectors to obtain the actual two global vectors. The restricted tensor construction makes them spherical outside `S`. At points integral outside `S`, finite separation and the two residual identities give single-function Whittaker products with the respective scalars `I.κ` and `I.κ'`.
3. For each class in the integral domain, choose an integral-outside representative. Evaluate the ordinary product at its padded and smaller-rank arguments. For an integral rectangle evaluate the two products at the dual point of the translated rectangular argument and at the smaller-rank dual point. The dual operation only changes the arguments of the functions; the product of the two scalars is therefore the same in both formulas. Incorporate the determinant products and use local unipotent equivariance to identify the resulting functions on the local quotient classes. For a nonintegral rectangle the first dual factor vanishes at the representative, by the conductor-zero rectangular support argument. This is precisely the target's indicator factor. Supply the two archimedean and two finite nonvanishing clauses.

## Scope and provenance

This is an internal reduction of the pointwise step in J. W. Cogdell, *Analytic theory of L-functions for GL_n* (2004), proof of Theorem 4.1, printed p. 219, with the Whittaker product discussed on printed p. 206. It adapts the Library's normalized infinite residual method of `EqualInfiniteWhittakerReduction` to the different ranks and the translated rectangular kernel. The archimedean residual product and the archimedean nonvanishing assertions remain open inputs here; the latter are motivated by H. Jacquet, *Archimedean Rankin–Selberg integrals* (2009), Theorems 2.3, 2.6 and 2.7, not formalized by this module. The implication is not an unconditional proof of `UnequalPointwiseFactorizationStatement` or a proof of a global functional equation.
