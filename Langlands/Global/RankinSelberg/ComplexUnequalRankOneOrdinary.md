# Ordinary nonvanishing for a complex unequal-rank pair with smaller group GL₁

`ComplexUnequalRankOne.exists_complexUnequalIntegral_rankOne_ne_zero_above` fixes a compatible quotient measure, a nonzero complex additive scale, and a smaller compact-finite Whittaker coefficient whose value at the identity is nonzero. The larger coefficient comes from a supplied Whittaker intertwiner for the opposite character and is also nonzero at the identity. The theorem gives a larger compact-finite model coefficient for which the ordinary integral, paired with the fixed smaller coefficient, is nonzero somewhere in every right half-plane.

On the smaller quotient \(N_1(\mathbb C)\backslash\mathrm{GL}_1(\mathbb C)\), write \(z\) for the determinant. Right translation of the larger coefficient by \(1+bE_{12}\) introduces the factor \(\psi_a(zb)\) in the ordinary integrand. The matrix identity moves the transvection to the left of the padded point and multiplies its parameter by \(z\). Left Whittaker equivariance then gives the factor. Since the character is unitary, one half-plane domination bound gives holomorphy for all these translated ordinary integrals.

Suppose every compact-finite test with the fixed smaller coefficient vanishes on some right half-plane. The compact-finite approximation theorem `complexUnequalTranslatedNormalization` gives entire normalizations for the approximating tests and a locally uniform limit for each translate. The identity theorem makes each approximating normalization zero, so its limit is zero. The reciprocal pair denominator is nonzero sufficiently far to the right. Thus every translated ordinary integral vanishes there, and holomorphy gives vanishing at a common spectral point for all \(b\).

Transport the compatible quotient measure to a positive multiple of \(\lVert z\rVert^{-2}\,dz\) on \(\mathbb C^\times\). At the common spectral point the unshifted integrand times \(\lVert z\rVert^{-2}\), extended by zero, is integrable on \(\mathbb C\) and continuous at \(1\). Varying \(b\) in the scaled trace character gives all Euclidean Fourier frequencies. The translated vanishing therefore makes its Fourier transform zero. Fourier inversion at \(1\) contradicts the two nonzero identity evaluations and the nonzero determinant weight.

The character insertion is the scalar case of the calculation in H. Jacquet, *Archimedean Rankin–Selberg integrals*, §6.2, Proposition 6.1, author pp. 41–42. The implemented proof applies Fourier inversion to the quotient integrand and transfers vanishing through compact-finite approximation. The general existence of a test is discussed in Theorems 2.3 and 2.6, author pp. 8–10.
