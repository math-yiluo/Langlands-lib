# The complex equal-rank equation from Jacquet’s finite expansion

This is an internal reduction based on Jacquet, *Archimedean Rankin–Selberg integrals* (2009), §8.2, especially (8.2) and the two finite expansions on pp. 57–58. The reduction is for the Library’s upper-Borel order and positive Fourier transform. Write `r + 1` for the small group size, `d :: ds` for the first inducing list and `es` for the second inducing list. The character removed from the first inducing list is `d`.

`HasEqualJacquetExpansion` supplies paired finite sums on a right half-plane. Each primal summand uses a corank-one Rankin–Selberg integral with inducing lists `es, ds` and a meromorphic matrix continuation with the pair factors of `[d], es`. The dual sum uses the corresponding corank-one dual integral and dual matrix continuation, with the common multiplier `complexCentralSign ds`. In the paper’s derivation the two parts of the square test inherit opposite Fourier signs in the Library convention. Negating the first `r + 1` rows makes the Fourier sign positive on the full matrix. Moving the same diagonal sign into the small group contributes `complexCentralSign ds`. The determinant twist from that change cancels the sign of the removed character from the dual Whittaker recursion. The finite expansion premise retains this signed dual identity and the matrix equations explicitly.

`pairMeromorphicEquation_equal_sum_products` multiplies the two equations for each summand after commuting the corank-one pair factors. The corank-one additive parameter is `-a` because its larger Whittaker vector comes from the second equal-rank vector. Changing the epsilon parameter to `a` contributes `complexCentralSign ds ^ (r + 2) * complexCentralSign es ^ (r + 1)`. Together with the adjacent-rank sign `complexCentralSign ds ^ (r + 1)` and the sign of the dual expansion, the even power of `complexCentralSign ds` cancels. The two Euler factors and the two epsilon factors multiply by splitting the pair products along `d :: ds`. Finiteness gives one codiscrete set on which the component equations hold and the sum satisfies the equation.

`complexLocalEqualJacquetExpansionReduction` applies the adjacent-rank theorem to obtain continuations of each corank-one term. It then forms the meromorphic sums, enlarges the common lower bound on the real part to include every component and the supplied convergence bound, and proves the exact equal-rank pair assertion. This conditional argument retains absolute convergence for the two equal-rank integrals, the adjacent-rank equation, and the paired integral expansion with the meromorphic matrix equations as premises.
