# Spectral multiplication for ordinary complex equal-rank integrals

A positive central scalar acts on the two Whittaker vectors by their central
characters and dilates the last row. Substituting that scalar on the right
of the unipotent quotient gives the identity between the integral with
dilated row test and the original integral multiplied by an exponential.
The exponent includes the determinant contribution and the two central
character exponents. The radial derivative of the test is the differential
of the Schwartz function applied to its argument, which is still Schwartz.

The dilated tests have uniform last-row decay on the compact real dilation
interval, as do their radial derivatives. Equal-rank half-plane domination
therefore permits differentiation under the quotient integral. Differentiation
at zero gives the spectral multiplier in `ComplexEqualSpectralMultiplicationStatement`.

This is a derived identity for Jacquet's ordinary equal-rank integral (2.6),
*Archimedean Rankin–Selberg integrals* (2009), author p. 6. The identity gives
the spectral multiplication needed for the polynomial-span argument in §15,
author pp. 108–109.
