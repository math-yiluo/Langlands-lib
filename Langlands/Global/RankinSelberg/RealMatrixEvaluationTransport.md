# Changing the evaluation point in the real matrix theta identity

**Provenance.** This is an internal covariance calculation for Jacquet, *Archimedean Rankin–Selberg integrals* (2009), §4.7, pp. 30–31, for the positive matrix Fourier transform with parameter one and the normalized rank shift. It supplies the starting identity at all evaluation points of the right-translated section before applying a compact derivative functional.

Write `ι g = g^-T`, `D(g) = |det g|`, and take rank `n = r + 1`. Suppose the matrix theta reflection holds with determinant kernels `h, k` and the coefficients `f(g), f(ιg)` for every Schwartz square matrix test and positive scale. `realMatrix_theta_of_left_evaluation` gives the same theta reflection for `f(xg), f(x ιg)` at every fixed `x ∈ GL_n(ℝ)`. The theorem is an identity of Bochner integrals; by itself it does not supply their integrability. `realMatrix_position_theta_left_evaluation_of_pair` instantiates the hypothesis for signed-power diagonal characters and a polynomially height-bounded principal-series section. It works for any measurable, rapidly decreasing determinant kernel pair `(Hk, Kk)` that satisfies the torus theta identity at every positive scale for all Schwartz tests on `ℝⁿ`. Integrability comes from the rapid decay of the kernels and the height bound, and the theta identity at the identity from `realMatrix_theta_at_one_of_pair`. `realMatrix_position_theta_left_evaluation` is the instance for the explicit convolved Tate pair, whose torus identity is `torus_theta_of_schwartz`.

**Lean proof.** On the primal side use left translation by `x⁻¹`; this sends the test to `Φ(x⁻¹·)` and the scale to `a / D(x)`, with a scalar from the rank shift. On the dual side use left translation by `(ιx)⁻¹` and the left-right covariance of the Fourier transform of the same translated test. The determinant scale changes by `D(x)` and the Fourier Jacobian contributes the inverse `n`th power. The two scalar factors agree after the reflection because the square of the rank-shift factor times `D(x)` is `D(x)^n`. The principal-series instance uses polynomial initial integrability and rapid decay to justify the theta identity at the identity for every translated test.
