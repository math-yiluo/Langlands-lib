# The last-column chart of the real Godement open cell

For adjacent ranks \(m\) and \(m+1\), with \(m=t+1\), `lastColumnChart` joins a real column \(x\) and an upper unipotent matrix \(v\) of rank \(m\) into an upper unipotent matrix \(u\) of rank \(m+1\). The upper-left block of \(u\) is \(v\), and the entries above the diagonal in the final column read \(x\) in reversed order. The chart carries the product of the column and lower unipotent entry measures to the upper unipotent entry measure, using the same real self-dual scalar measure in every coordinate.

`RealGodementLastColumnStatement` also records that the standard phase of \(u\) is \(\psi_a(x_0)\theta_{a,m}(v)\). The last \(m\) rows of \(w_{m+1}ug\) are \([x,w_mvw_m]w_{m+1}g\), where the brackets put \(x\) in the first column. This is the upper-Borel change of matrix coordinates for the split of the unipotent integral in Jacquet, *Archimedean Rankin–Selberg Integrals* (2009), §7.2, pp. 47–48, from (7.5) to (7.6). The long Weyl element converts the leading rectangle in Jacquet's lower-Borel convention to the trailing rectangle here. The inverse phase for Jacquet's additive parameter \(b\) is the positive phase with \(a=-b\), which has the same self-dual measure.

The Lean proof partitions the strict upper entries by whether their second index is last. It transports the finite product measures through that index equivalence and the actual upper entry coordinates. Splitting the first-superdiagonal sum isolates the last term, which becomes \(x_0\). Conjugating by the long Weyl element reverses both indices, and the trailing-row identity follows entrywise before multiplying by \(w_{m+1}g\).
