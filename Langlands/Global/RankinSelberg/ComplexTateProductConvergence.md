# Absolute convergence of the product of complex Tate weights

Let the test function be Schwartz on a finite product of copies of the complex plane. Give each coordinate its own integral angular weight and complex spectral parameter. The product of the scalar Tate weights times the test function is absolutely integrable against additive Lebesgue measure if the real part of every spectral parameter is positive. The weights are assigned value zero at the origin of each coordinate.

Off those coordinate hyperplanes the angular factors have norm one. The Schwartz seminorms of orders zero and the chosen decay degree bound the norm of the test by a constant times a product of inverse powers of `max 1 ‖z j‖`. This uses the inequality between each coordinate norm and the supremum norm on the product. In each complex coordinate the radial Jacobian contributes one power of the radius. Below radius one the resulting exponent is greater than −1, and beyond radius one the chosen decay degree makes the exponent less than −1. The product of the one-dimensional radial majorants is integrable by the finite product measure theorem.

This is the convergence estimate for the diagonal Tate integrals in the proof of Proposition 7.1 of Jacquet, *Archimedean Rankin–Selberg Integrals* (2009), §7.1, p. 44.
