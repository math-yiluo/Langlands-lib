# Simple-root stabilizers along a complex Bruhat cell

The Gauss coordinate space splits into the entries of the cell and the complementary entries. For a permutation with an adjacent ascent, a simple upper root gives a smooth family of stabilizing matrices along the cell. Their exact Whittaker frequency is `4πi`. The associated coordinate field and inducing scalar vanish on the cell, and the derivative of each fixed-direction coordinate field at its point of stabilization is cube-zero.

The ascending pair is carried by the permutation to an upper root. At each cell point the permutation conjugate of the lower Gauss factor lies in the original upper unipotent. Conjugating the simple-root matrix by this unit gives the right stabilizer. Conjugation preserves its square-zero property. The first-superdiagonal addition formula on upper unipotents shows that the conjugated transvection has the same adjacent entries as the original one. Smoothness follows from the Gauss section and smooth inversion. The stabilizing matrix equation identifies the corresponding left direction with the permuted root, so the checked Gauss linearization proves the three vanishing conclusions.
