# The elliptic matrix square sum for real Whittaker coefficients

The sum of squares in the real matrix-unit directions equals the quadratic
central trace operator plus one half the sum of squares in the antisymmetric
matrix-unit differences. The central term acts by a scalar on the standard
module. The antisymmetric directions preserve the finite-dimensional span of
the orthogonal orbit of each compact-finite vector. Thus each such span
carries a finite system for the square operator.

On the chart of invertible matrices the operator on a left-translated
coefficient has second-order fields given by left multiplication of the
matrix units by the chart point. Its first-order drift is the chart point
itself, since the sum of the squares of the matrix units is the identity.
The principal symbol is a sum of squares in a basis of tangent directions,
and is therefore elliptic. The coefficient intertwining relations identify
the chart operator with the finite system on the compact-finite vectors.
These three facts are collected in `RealWhittakerEllipticOperatorStatement`.

This is an internal real-matrix version of the elliptic square-sum
calculation used for complex Whittaker coefficients. It supplies the local
elliptic equation for comparing the real moderate-growth model with the
uniform growth in H. Jacquet, *Archimedean Rankin–Selberg integrals* (2009),
§2, pp. 3–4 of the author-posted version.
