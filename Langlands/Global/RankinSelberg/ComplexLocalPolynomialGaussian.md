# Finite unitary translates of complex polynomial Gaussians

Jacquet defines standard complex Schwartz functions as polynomials in the matrix entries and their conjugates times a Gaussian. The compact expansion in §8.2 uses finite-dimensional unitary translates of the square test. This lemma proves continuity and the left-translate finiteness for the polynomial-Gaussian expression on rectangular matrices, including square matrices.

The sum of squared entry norms equals the real trace of the adjoint times the matrix. It is unchanged by unitary left multiplication. Left multiplication replaces the entry variables by linear combinations of entries and the conjugate variables by linear combinations of conjugates. Expanding each monomial shows that the substitution preserves the original total-degree bound. All translates are therefore in the image of one finite-dimensional polynomial space under evaluation and multiplication by the Gaussian.

For positive Gaussian scale the expression is also an actual Schwartz map. The Gaussian exponent and the polynomial factor have temperate growth. The sum of squared entry norms dominates the square of the maximum entry norm. The chain-rule bound for the derivatives of the exponential, together with Gaussian decay in that maximum norm, gives the Schwartz seminorm bounds. Multiplication by the polynomial then preserves the Schwartz space.
