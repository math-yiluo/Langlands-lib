# A boundary estimate for the tempered bound of real block coefficients

`realTemperedWhittakerBoundAtRank_of_descendingCanonicalLines` proves `RealTemperedDescendingBoundaryStatement`. It derives the scalar exact-model tempered bound at a given rank from `RealDescendingCanonicalLineBoundAtRank`. This hypothesis concerns the canonical Jacquet family attached to an original compact-finite block vector in the descending character presentation. Its exponent path \(q(t)\) is holomorphic and starts at the descending vector \(q(0)=z\). For \(0<\delta<1\) the hypothesis bounds the coefficient on \(\operatorname{Re}t=\delta\), \(|\operatorname{Im}t|\leq 1\), by the upper Borel half-modulus times \(A_\delta\exp(D\delta\sum_i|y_i|)\). The nonnegative \(D\) is chosen before \(\delta\), and \(A_\delta\) works for all points on that segment and all diagonal and orthogonal arguments.

The descending range equality represents every exact coefficient at scale \(a\) as a canonical value at parameter \(-a\) on an original block vector. The canonical family uses its fixed compact restriction. The general interpolation lemma `canonicalTemperedBound_of_rightBoundary` bounds this family at \(q(0)\) with any positive exponential loss. Its proof uses locally uniform polynomial height growth on the compact image of the parameter disk, divides out the half-modulus, and applies the small exponential estimate from nearby vertical segments. Substituting \(q(0)=z\) gives the bound on the given coefficient itself.

The scalar target is a consequence of the tempered majorization in Jacquet, *Archimedean Rankin–Selberg Integrals* (2009), Proposition 3.4. The canonical families use continuation of the Jacquet integrals of §7.2. [Author-posted edition, pp. 21 and 47](https://www.math.columbia.edu/~hj/PerfectRankinSelberg.pdf)
