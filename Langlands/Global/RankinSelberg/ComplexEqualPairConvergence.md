# Convergence of the complex equal-rank test integrals

The published simple-root estimate applies to the empty derivative word of a
complex Whittaker coefficient. The positive diagonal matrices used for this
estimate and for the convergence theorem have the same entries. Splitting
each absolute logarithm into its positive and negative parts matches the growth
term in the kernel bound. A natural decay order at least as large as the
requested real order suffices because the simple-root sum is nonnegative.

The product of coefficients for opposite characters is invariant under the
upper unipotent subgroup. The kernel formed with the Schwartz function of the
last row is continuous, and the simple-root bounds give the logarithmic kernel
bound. The published convergence and holomorphy lemmas give a right half-plane
for the ordinary integral. Inverse transpose with the long Weyl element
transports each coefficient to the model with the reversed inverse parameters
and the opposite character. The Fourier transform is Schwartz, so the same
convergence argument applies to the dual integral. The maximum of the two
thresholds gives a common half-plane.

The final lemma uses the published analytic bridge to put actual
complex-place restrictions of the two cuspidal realizations in these models.
It supplies convergence for arbitrary test data of the kind used in the
equal-rank factorization. This is an internal application of the Library's
analytic lemmas for the local integrals in Jacquet 2009, Theorem 2.1.
