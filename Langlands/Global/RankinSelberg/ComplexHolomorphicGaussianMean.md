# The Gaussian mean of a holomorphic polynomial

On a finite product of complex coordinate spaces with the given product additive Haar measure, the Gaussian of scale \(2\pi\) has nonzero mass. Every holomorphic polynomial is integrable against this Gaussian. Its weighted integral equals the Gaussian mass times the polynomial's value at the origin.

A nonconstant monomial in one variable integrates to zero by rotating the variable through an angle that changes the sign of its power. The rotation preserves the measure and the Gaussian. The integral of the constant monomial against the Gaussian is positive. The product integral factors into the one-variable moments, and linearity gives the result for polynomials. In triangular matrix coordinates this removes the strict upper entries whenever the polynomial dependence on those entries is holomorphic.
