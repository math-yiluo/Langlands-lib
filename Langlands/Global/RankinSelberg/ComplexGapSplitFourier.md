# Fourier transformation of the split Godement test

`complexGapSplitFourier` proves that the full positive trace Fourier transform acts separately on the leading rectangular Schwartz factor and each tail column factor. Its rectangular transform agrees with the one in the Godement reflection theorem. Transforming a column with the opposite character after its positive transform recovers the original column.

The proof separates the column variables by transposition and reindexing, which preserve the additive product measures. The trace pairing becomes the sum of the leading pairing and the tail column pairings. The additive character splits that sum into a product, and the product measure integral gives the independently transformed factors. The column inversion uses the same self-dual measure for opposite characters. [Jacquet, *Archimedean Rankin–Selberg integrals*, Sections 10.2–10.3, split tests and (10.7)](https://www.math.columbia.edu/~hj/PerfectRankinSelberg.pdf)
