# Square reflection for the leading test

At each fixed Whittaker evaluation point, the ordinary and inverse-transpose square coefficients satisfy the adjoining-character functional equation. The theorem includes right half-plane convergence and exponential growth of both normalized entire functions on vertical strips. It is a derived step in the square comparison of Jacquet’s Section 10.2.

A finite left unitary span gives a compact weight that reproduces the ordinary test and its positive Fourier transform. Lean moves this average to the Whittaker coefficient in both square integrals. Compact projection expresses the averaged coefficients as finite sums of standard matrix coefficients with the same scalar multipliers. The square coefficient equation and the finite sum preserve the pair normalization and its growth bounds.

For the leading test on a retained matrix, the square restriction reads the first block columns. The trace pairing and the self-dual product measure identify its positive Fourier transform with the leading Fourier test obtained by integrating the complementary rectangle. The finite span for a leading polynomial Gaussian applies after changing the matrix norm used to present the Schwartz function.
