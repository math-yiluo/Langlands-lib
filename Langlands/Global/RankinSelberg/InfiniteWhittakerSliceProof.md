# Archimedean Whittaker slice comparison for a cuspidal tensor presentation

`infiniteWhittakerSliceModel` proves `InfiniteWhittakerSliceModelStatement.{u,uQ}` on the supplied cuspidal realization and spectral presentation. It gives a linear coefficient map at each infinite place, normalized to have identity value one on a reference vector. For each finite tensor and each choice of the other infinite vectors and group arguments, one scalar relates the resulting global Whittaker slice to that map on every vector and group argument in the varying place.

The proof chooses a pure tensor with nonzero global Whittaker value at the identity. The comparison first uses only values at this identity. At a fixed place, the reference and any other tensor insertion intertwine the same local compact and Lie actions. To use the unitary local theorem, the proof twists the global realization by the determinant character obtained from the modulus of its central character. The multiplier is one on the upper unipotent subgroup, so the original and twisted identity integrals agree. The twist is trivial on the compact subgroup, adds a scalar to each Lie generator and preserves a common finite level for each slice after intersecting with the finite determinant kernel. The compact-finite local module retains its admissibility and irreducibility under this shift. The two twisted insertions therefore satisfy `proportional_cuspWhittaker_one_of_placeEmbeddings`, which gives proportionality at identity for the original insertions. The construction of the place modules uses the actual tensor presentation and applies in both kinds of infinite place and in every rank covered by the statement.

`nonempty_modelData_of_eval_one` normalizes the maps using the reference tensor and extends the identity comparison to arbitrary frozen group arguments. It uses compact and Lie covariance and analytic uniqueness to propagate the comparison one group coordinate at a time. The local uniqueness step is the Library's formulation of Shalika, *The Multiplicity One Theorem for GL(n)*, Theorem 3.1, pp. 183–185, registered as `shalika-1974-multiplicity-one` with SHA-256 `788c5d124024c768aa47c4e8b179475fbcf5b0882a518ae60b19cdc88089f087`.
