# Positive trace Fourier transformation in one complex coordinate

For a Schwartz function on a finite product of complex planes, retain all coordinates except the selected one as parameters. Fourier transformation in the selected coordinate for the positive trace character and twice additive Lebesgue area again gives a Schwartz function on the product. For every fixed value of the retained coordinates, its slice is the scalar positive trace Fourier transform used in the complex Tate integral.

The proof splits the selected coordinate off as the last factor of a continuous linear equivalence. The real partial Fourier transform on this product gives a Schwartz function. In the selected output coordinate, substitution by the conjugate of `−2` times the frequency changes the negative real inner product pairing to the positive complex trace pairing. Multiplication by `2` changes area to twice area. The slice is a Schwartz function by restriction to the affine embedding with the retained coordinates fixed.

This construction supplies the coordinate Fourier transforms for the diagonal Tate integrals in Jacquet, *Archimedean Rankin–Selberg Integrals* (2009), §7.1, proof of Proposition 7.1, p. 44.
