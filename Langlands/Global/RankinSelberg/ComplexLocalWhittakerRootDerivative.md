# The simple-root differential equation for complex Whittaker coefficients

Let \(u_i(z)=1+z E_{i,i+1}\). The matrix lies in the upper unitriangular
subgroup and its superdiagonal sum is \(z\). For the Library's positive
trace character with parameter \(a\), take the real path
\(u_i(t/a)\). The additive character along it is
\(\exp(4\pi\mathrm{i}\,t)\). Its derivative is the nonzero scalar
\(\gamma=4\pi\mathrm{i}\).

Differentiate the left equivariance at \(t=0\) in matrix coordinates.
The resulting right differential at \(g\) is in direction
\(g^{-1} (a^{-1}E_{i,i+1}) g\) and gives \(\gamma W(g)\).
At \(g=\operatorname{diag}(e^{x_i})k\), diagonal conjugation
contributes the reciprocal of \(e^{x_i-x_{i+1}}\). Consequently the
right differential in direction \(k^{-1}(a^{-1}E_{i,i+1})k\)
multiplies \(W(g)\) by \(\gamma e^{x_i-x_{i+1}}\).

The right derivatives of the inducing vector belong to the
unitary-finite standard module. Using preservation of these derivatives
by the intertwiner, induction on the derivative word proves the product
of the corresponding multipliers at the same \(g\). This is the
simple-root calculation in Jacquet's *Archimedean Rankin–Selberg
integrals* (2009), printed and PDF pp. 15–16, with the Library's
choice of character and the actual model intertwiners.
