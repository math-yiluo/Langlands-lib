# Fourier reflection of the real Godement outer integrand

For a real Schwartz test on `m × (m+1)` matrices, the inner Godement integral over the lower-unitriangular and free-column coordinates is absolutely integrable for every pair of invertible translators. The outer integrand uses the right lower-unipotent representative `h = k⁻¹ w_m` and a coefficient function evaluated at `k`. It has the determinant-character weight with shift `m/2` in the upper variable and with shift `(m+1)/2` in `h`.

At the reflected upper argument `w_(m+1) g⁻ᵀ`, apply the two Weyl slice identity to the pullback of the test by `Y ↦ h Y g⁻ᵀ`. Real rectangular Fourier covariance contributes `|det g|^m |det h|^(-(m+1))`. The two determinant weights absorb those powers and invert the inducing character. The remaining Weyl determinants have product `(-1)^m`. The lower argument of the reflected coefficient is `w_m k⁻ᵀ`, and its corresponding right representative is `h⁻ᵀ w_m`. The change of sign in the slice phase uses the same real self-dual measure.

This is the pointwise calculation in the proof of Jacquet's Proposition 7.3 for the upper inducing Borel and positive Fourier transform. The coefficient input is an arbitrary function on the lower general linear group. Passage to a quotient integral uses the coefficient's unipotent law and compatible measures.
