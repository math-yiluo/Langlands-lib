# A fixed finite-dimensional span of complex Godement compact profiles

`ComplexGodementFiniteProfilesStatement` starts with an admissible compact profile on the larger unitary group, fixed angular weights, and right-invariant Haar measure on the smaller group. It produces a finite-dimensional space of admissible larger profiles containing the given one, together with an indexed family of fixed Schwartz tests and fixed smaller profiles. Each test has compact full-row-rank support and a finite-dimensional orbit under right unitary translation. At every radial exponent tuple, the compact restrictions of the corresponding Godement sections span the same larger profile space. The indexing set can depend on the given profile. At each tuple, finite-dimensionality permits a finite choice from that family as a basis.

The larger space is the subspace with the prescribed phase covariance in the two-sided translate span of the given profile. The latter is finite-dimensional and has continuous functions. Right translation preserves the covariant subspace, so all its vectors are admissible compact profiles. To make each test, the proof takes the cofactor lift of a vector in this subspace extended at a fixed radial tuple, and multiplies it by an invariant cutoff. The smaller profile is extended at the current retained radial exponents. The cutoff gives compact full-row-rank support, and the cofactor translation law gives the finite-dimensional orbit of the test.

For any current tuple the cofactor translation law identifies the compact restriction of the resulting section with a matrix coefficient of the fixed larger profile. The determinant factor on a unitary element has the same value at the frozen and current tuples because its norm is one. Evaluation of the Godement section at the identity is therefore a linear functional on the fixed covariant space. Extending this functional to the two-sided space and using `FiniteTranslationSpan.coefficient_mem` places the coefficient in that space. The section's Borel covariance gives the required angular condition.

For spanning at a given tuple, choose the frozen tuple equal to it. The compact Godement separation theorem then shows that a vector with nonzero identity evaluation has some cofactor section with nonzero identity value. Finite-dimensional annihilator duality expresses the identity evaluation as a linear combination of these section evaluations. Applying the coefficient map to that relation expresses every admissible profile in the finite space as a linear combination of the stated Godement compact restrictions.

This strengthens the compact Schwartz spanning argument for Jacquet, *Archimedean Rankin–Selberg integrals* (2009), §7.1, Proposition 7.1(v), pp. 44–46, in the parameter direction needed for the Jacquet continuation. The proof uses Worker 273's cofactor constructions and compact separation. It keeps the profile space and generator data fixed while allowing the section exponents to vary.
