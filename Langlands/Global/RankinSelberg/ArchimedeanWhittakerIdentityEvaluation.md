# Nonzero evaluation at the identity for actual global Whittaker functions

For an actual cuspidal realization and a specified standard additive character, some vector has a nonzero global Whittaker coefficient at the adelic identity. The rank is arbitrary.

Fix a finite adelic coordinate. The matrix extension of the coefficient gives a smooth function on the infinite group. Right compact translation acts on the vector by the actual compact representation. The derivative of the matrix extension in a genuine Lie direction comes from the corresponding Lie operator on the vector, and induction gives covariance for the enveloping algebra.

A finite-codimensional ideal of the enveloping center kills the vector and hence the coefficient. Compact finiteness passes to the coefficient through its compact intertwiner. These two facts give the published joint-finiteness criterion. If every vector has zero evaluation at the identity of the infinite group, the compact translates of all its enveloping derivatives have zero value there as well. The analytic jet theorem forces the coefficient to vanish on every connected component.

If every vector had zero coefficient at the adelic identity, finite right translation would give zero at the infinite identity for every finite coordinate. The resulting vanishing of the entire coefficient contradicts the cuspidal Whittaker nonvanishing theorem for any nonzero vector. Using such a vector with finite offset one gives an actual restriction with nonzero identity value at each real or complex place.

These results are internal consequences of the Library's actual compact and differential operator actions and its Whittaker expansion.
