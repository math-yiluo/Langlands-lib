# Adelic arguments for unequal-rank Whittaker products

These internal lemmas concern the product and support steps in Cogdell, *Analytic theory of L-functions for GL_n* (2004), proof of Theorem 4.1, p. 219. They use the Library's adelic matrix conventions and make no archimedean product assumption.

* The adelic padding, rectangular point and lower-block Weyl element project to their local analogues. This follows from the entrywise formulas for the blocks and reindexing; the place projections are entrywise ring homomorphisms.
* `exists_integral_rep` uses `UnipotentClass.exists_semilocal_of_mem_unipotentIntegralOutside` to choose a representative with integral components off the retained set. The ordinary and dual class kernels can both be evaluated there: their character multipliers under a change of representative are reciprocal, and the determinant factor is unchanged.
* `coefficient_eq_product` writes an integral-outside point as its infinite part times the embedding of its finite part. `translated_coefficient_eq_product` separates the retained finite factors and leaves the normalized `infiniteResidual` unchanged. This is a single-coefficient statement and applies separately in the two ranks.
* The padded and translated rectangular arguments remain integral off the retained set when the rectangle is integral there; the dual argument also preserves this integrality. For the complementary case, `dualWhittaker_eq_zero_of_not_rectIntegral` freezes the components outside one finite place. The resulting local function, with the lower-block Weyl translation included, is left equivariant for the inverse local standard character and right invariant under the local integral group. For the latter, the right multiplier is conjugated by the integral Weyl element and carried through the global dual transform by inverse transpose, so sphericity of the original vector applies. Conductor zero supplies the nontrivial character test for each nonintegral entry. `RectangularSupport.apply_rectGL` then gives zero. This proves support directly on the transformed global coefficient, without supplying a contragredient realization of that coefficient.
