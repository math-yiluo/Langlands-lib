# Spectral holomorphy and locally uniform bounds for upper right derivatives

The spectral domain is an open subset of a finite-dimensional complex parameter space. The lower family at each fixed group element and the varying leading exponent are holomorphic on that domain. Every lower right derivative is regular for the fixed opposite additive parameter and has a polynomial growth bound with one locally common exponent. For a Schwartz test supported in a compact set of full-row-rank matrices, the upper column is smooth in its group variable. Every upper iterated right derivative is holomorphic at each fixed group argument and has a polynomial height bound uniform near each spectral parameter. The exponent on the neighborhood is common to every derivative word.

The Lean proof bounds the leading exponent on a small spectral ball, then applies the common Haar majorant and the uniform determinant power bound to the upper columns. Differentiation along a real matrix direction replaces a compact test by its first variation and adds a determinant derivative times the original column. The variation is supported in the same chosen compact set. The determinant derivative is linear in the shifted leading exponent. Induction starting from the last direction in the derivative word expresses a new derivative as a sum of two columns or shorter derivatives on these tests. This proves the common growth bound and propagates the pointwise spectral holomorphy of the column.
