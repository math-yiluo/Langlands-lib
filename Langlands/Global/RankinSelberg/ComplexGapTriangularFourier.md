# The triangular column Fourier identity

Let the ambient column have `k + q + 1` entries. The leading block has size `k`, and the tail has size `q + 1`. On the ordinary side `directColumn` takes the last `q` columns of the upper block matrix whose leading block is the identity, whose first tail column has zeros in the leading rows, and whose remaining upper right entries form a free rectangle. The lower right block is upper unipotent. On the Fourier side `transposeColumn` takes the first `q` columns of the transposed tail block, with zeros in the leading rows. In `triangularFourier` the tail matrix is inverted before transposition.

`complexGapTriangularFourier` proves the identity in Lemma 10.2 for the positive Fourier transform and the self-dual measure of `ψ_a`. Both kernels are absolutely integrable. An invertible matrix `g` acts on the ordinary columns by multiplication and on the frequencies by inverse transpose. The scalar in the identity is the inverse of its normalized determinant raised to `q`.

The proof uses the measure-preserving prefix and suffix coordinates. The fixed column and frequency entries are both unit basis vectors at the respective pivots. Each pairing extracts the corresponding simple-root entry of the tail, so the product of the column characters is the standard unipotent character. The column Fourier exchange initially uses the transposed tail and `ψ_{-a}`. Transpose followed by conjugation by the long Weyl element preserves the entry product and exchanges left and right translations. Thus the entry Haar measure is invariant under inversion. Inverting the tail matrix negates the superdiagonal sum and restores `ψ_a` in the Fourier-side kernel. [Jacquet, *Archimedean Rankin–Selberg integrals*, Section 10.2, Lemma 10.2](https://www.math.columbia.edu/~hj/PerfectRankinSelberg.pdf).
