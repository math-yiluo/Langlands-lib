# Transport of the compact weight to the dual square test

Jacquet transports the reproducing compact average of the matrix test through Fourier transform in *Archimedean Rankin–Selberg integrals* (2009), §8.2, printed pp. 57–58. The square test is integrable for the self-dual matrix measure. The weight and determinant character are continuous, and the supplied compact measure is Haar.

The integral of the determinant-twisted test against the compact weight reproduces the test pointwise. In the joint integral, the shear `(k,X) ↦ (k,k⁻¹X)` preserves the product of compact Haar and the additive measure. The compact factor is integrable by continuity, and the square test is integrable by assumption. Multiplication by the unit-norm Fourier character preserves integrability. Fubini therefore takes the compact integral outside the Fourier integral.

For each compact element, the square Fourier covariance replaces multiplication by `k⁻¹` by multiplication by its inverse transpose. The determinant of the inverse transpose is the inverse determinant. This gives reproduction of the Fourier transform with the opposite determinant twist. The compact integration is still parametrized by the original `k`, so the identity uses the same measure and weight on both sides.
