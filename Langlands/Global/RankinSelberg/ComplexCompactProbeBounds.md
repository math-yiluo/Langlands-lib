# Uniform value and first-order bounds for the complex compact probe

The dual compact extension is the scalar extension factor times the torus
average of the inverse compact coefficient. The torus weights have norm
one, so the compact value bound and first translation difference bound
pass to the averaged profile. Left translation by k⁻¹ can be expressed
as right translation by the conjugate of k⁻¹. Compactness bounds the
matrix norms of the multiplying unitary factors, giving a uniform
comparison of the two displacements.

The polynomial growth of the scalar factor gives a global bound for the
probe by the compact uniform value bound. Near the identity, the scalar
and compact-factor Lipschitz bounds control the difference from identity
evaluation by one power of the upper-entry norm. Outside that neighborhood
the same coordinate norm is bounded below by the neighborhood radius.
The global value estimate then supplies a polynomial majorant for the
first-order error. The constants depend on the rank and inducing data and
are uniform in the input compact-finite section and its compact evaluation
point.
