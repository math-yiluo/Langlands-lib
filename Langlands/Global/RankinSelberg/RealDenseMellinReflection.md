# Extension of real normalized Mellin reflection from dense generators

`RealDenseMellinReflectionStatement` concerns a pair of determinant Mellin
integrals whose kernels evaluate continuous linear scalar forms on a
sequential barrelled complex topological vector space. It assumes positive
measurable determinant scales and a common range of finite moments for the
two kernels on all source vectors. If the entire real normalized reflection
with exponential strip growth holds on a set of vectors with dense complex
span, it holds on the full space, for the same paired factors, additive data
and nonzero reflection sign.

Take the real determinant weights of unit Gaussian rate and zero linear
shift. At each positive dilation the weights are bounded by a constant times
a fixed power of the determinant scale. The moment assumptions make both
weighted kernels integrable on every vector. The continuity theorem for
integrable linear kernels then gives two continuous linear theta evaluations.
The normalized reflection on the generators implies equality of these
weighted values by inverse Mellin reflection. Linearity gives equality on
the span, and continuity gives the same theta identity on the full space.

The Gaussian Mellin theorem now gives an entire function of exponential
strip growth with the ordinary normalized value on its right half-plane and
the reflected normalized value divided by the signed epsilon factor on its
left half-plane. Reflect the variable and multiply back by the signed
epsilon factor to obtain the second entire function. The two half-plane
identities and this construction give the normalized equation everywhere.

Jacquet extends the equation from pure tensors by continuity of the two
weighted integrals in §12.3, pp. 89–90, using the Mellin argument of
Propositions 4.1–4.2, pp. 23–28. The density step here uses the continuous
scalar forms and the Gaussian weights explicitly, so both integrals may be
on different measure spaces.
[H. Jacquet, *Archimedean Rankin–Selberg Integrals* (2009)](https://www.math.columbia.edu/~hj/PerfectRankinSelberg.pdf#page=89).
