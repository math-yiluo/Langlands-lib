# Entire ordinary normalizations for genuine smooth real pairs

`RealPairedUnequalOrdinaryReflectionStatement` extends the normalized
ordinary and rectangular reflected equation from pure tensors to all
smooth vectors in the completed projective product of two jointly continuous
complex Banach representations. The two
individual smooth sources may be any dense ranges. The continuous form
on the joint word-topology space has independent opposite nonzero Whittaker covariance and
invariance under the common padded upper unipotents. The paired normalized
equation with exponential strip growth is assumed on the pure vectors
from the dense sources. The conclusion gives that same equation and strip
growth at every genuine smooth vector. The ordinary and reflected kernels
use the original determinant shift and compatible quotient and rectangle measures.

`RealPairedEqualOrdinaryReflectionStatement` gives the same extension for
equal ranks at each fixed Schwartz row. The covariance uses the two scales
\(a\) and \(-a\). The reflected integral uses a Fourier transform at the
independently supplied scale \(b\). Both statements assume the normalized
equations with exponential strip growth for the joint kernels on the pure
source tensors.

If the joint form factors on pure tensors as the product of two fixed
continuous Whittaker evaluations, `realPairedUnequalOrdinaryKernels` and
`realPairedEqualOrdinaryKernel` identify the ordinary kernels with the
scalar ones. The reflected comparisons are `unequalPairedDualKernel_pure`
and `equalPairedDualKernel_pure`. In unequal rank, absolute integrability
on the initial half-plane permits Fubini to identify the product integral
with the iterated scalar integral.

For each quotient or rectangle argument, the coefficient of the vector
is a continuous linear form, with the determinant power factored out.
The common initial convergence of the ordinary and reflected kernels
supplies all large real determinant moments. Multiplying by the inverse
determinant power at one convergent point gives measurability of the
underlying coefficient. Completeness and metrizability of the genuine
word topology make the source Baire and hence barrelled. The pure tensors
from the given dense individual sources have dense span in that topology.
Apply the dense normalized Mellin reflection theorem to these kernels.

Jacquet illustrates the tensor-product density argument in adjacent ranks
in §12.3, pp. 89–90. The ordinary and reflected integrals in the
corresponding ranks use the determinant and row conventions of
(2.4)–(2.6) and Theorem 2.1(iii), p. 6. The Library's real Fourier kernel
uses the positive sign. In equal rank the specialization \(b=-a\) gives
Jacquet's negative Fourier kernel for the first Whittaker character.
[H. Jacquet, *Archimedean Rankin–Selberg Integrals* (2009)](https://www.math.columbia.edu/~hj/PerfectRankinSelberg.pdf#page=89).
