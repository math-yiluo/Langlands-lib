# Ordinary tests in the span of reflected real rectangular integrals

`RealPairedReflectedOrdinaryTransferStatement` takes two jointly continuous complex Banach representations and a continuous complex-linear form on the joint smooth vectors of their completed projective product in the word-derivative topology. The form has upper-unipotent covariance with scales `a` and `-a` in the two slots. For each smooth vector, the ordinary integral of the Weyl inverse-transpose coefficients is a finite sum of the reflected rectangular integrals with vectors chosen independently of the spectral parameter. The rectangular convergence theorem `realPairedUnequalDualConvergence` supplies a shared right half-plane on which all the integrals in the comparison are absolutely convergent. The quotient measure, spectral shift and self-dual rectangular measure are those of the real unequal-rank functional equation.

The action of the larger group is its inverse transpose in the first slot. The derivative of this action on a lower row is the negative transpose of its row matrix, and the independent slot exponential realizes that action. Inverse transposed upper columns and their inverses are affine linear in the column, which gives the required height bound. The two Weyl factors give the opposite-character evaluation for the rectangular row comparison. The fixed right tail Weyl in the reflected kernel is absorbed by an inverse translation of the resulting smooth vectors. The empty rectangle has unit Dirac mass, giving the ordinary integral.

The ordinary and rectangular kernels use equations (2.4)–(2.5) and the functional equation in Theorem 2.1(iii) of Jacquet, *Archimedean Rankin–Selberg integrals* (2009), p. 6. The smooth comparison applies the compact factorization of Lemma 6.1, pp. 37–38, and the column modulation of Lemma 6.2 and its proof, pp. 38–39. The reflected comparison is in §6.1, pp. 39–41.
