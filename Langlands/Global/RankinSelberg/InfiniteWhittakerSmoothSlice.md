# Determination of a one-place Whittaker slice by identity evaluation

The smooth slice first inserts the variable local vector into the actual pure tensor of the supplied spectral presentation. It takes the global Whittaker function at finite offset one and freezes all infinite group coordinates except the selected place. The result is linear in that local vector. The global coefficient is jointly finite, so its matrix expression is real analytic. The affine freezing map preserves this analyticity.

The tensor equivalence transfers the supported Lie action to the selected source factor. At a given compact point, the evaluation of every frozen slice is the identity evaluation on one and the same compact translate of the local vector. The enveloping induction uses the Lie covariance to replace any enveloping derivative by evaluation on a single new vector shared by all frozen slices. If the identity evaluations of two slices are proportional on all source vectors, their difference with that scalar has a zero enveloping jet at each compact point. Analytic uniqueness and compact component coverage give equality of the coefficient functions. A reference tensor with a nonzero identity value normalizes the local models. At the full adelic identity, replacing the local vectors one at a time gives the product of their normalized evaluations. A second finite induction changes the group coordinates from identity to the desired tuple. At each step the product formula for the previous tuple holds on every vector in the new slot, so analytic uniqueness extends it to that slot’s arbitrary group argument. Splitting the resulting product at the selected place gives the normalized model data.

This is an internal reduction for the pure-tensor Whittaker separation used by Cogdell, *Analytic theory of L-functions for GL_n* (2004), §1.2 and the proof of Theorem 4.1 on pp. 219–220. The identity proportionality of the actual slices is a premise.
