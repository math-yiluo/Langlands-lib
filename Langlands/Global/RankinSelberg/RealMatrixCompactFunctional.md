# Passing real evaluation identities through compact derivative functionals

**Provenance.** This is the internal functional-analytic step used for Jacquet, *Archimedean Rankin–Selberg integrals* (2009), §4.7, Proposition 4.4, p. 31. It adapts the Library's compact scalarization argument in `RealPrincipalSeriesSmoothCoefficient`. Here the integration argument uses finite compact derivative coordinates and dominated differentiation rather than the compact idempotents of the source.

On the smooth real position principal series, a linear form `ℓ` is assumed to have the Library's `HasCompactDerivativeBound`: its norm is bounded by a fixed multiple of a finite sum of compact right derivative supremum bounds. `continuous_compactFunctionalCoefficient` shows that the coefficient `g ↦ ℓ(translate g f)` is continuous. If the weighted integrals of `f` translated by two continuous height-preserving maps agree at every evaluation point, `integral_compactFunctional_eq_of_evaluation_eq` gives their equality with `ℓ` applied to the translated sections instead. Each weight is assumed a.e. strongly measurable and to have all nonnegative integer height moments, just as in `RealMatrixOrbitIntegral`. No comparison with continuous forms defined through a different topology is asserted.

**Lean proof.** Collect the finite compact derivative coordinates of a section into `Fin m → C(O(n),ℂ)`. The derivative bound makes `ℓ` factor continuously through the image of this map. Extend that factor to the ambient normed space by Hahn–Banach. The coordinate orbit is continuous by joint continuity and the compact-open topology. Its norm is controlled by a fixed polynomial of the group height using compactness of `O(n)`, so the two weighted coordinate maps are Bochner integrable. The derivative transport proves equality of their integrals after every coordinate evaluation. Coordinate evaluation is a continuous linear map and commutes with the integral; these evaluations separate the integral vectors. Apply the extended linear form to the vector equality and commute it with the integral. This yields the claimed scalar equality.
