# Real general-linear Haar measure and integrable height decay

On the invertible real matrices, the measure with density `|det A|⁻ⁿ` against additive matrix Lebesgue measure is a positive multiple of any given left Haar measure. The determinant Jacobian for left multiplication and the general determinant-density theorem give the comparison. The modular character is trivial on scalar matrices, and the algebraic result on general-linear characters gives triviality on the group. The right translation and inversion formulas then give invariance under both maps.

For positive rank, some negative power of the matrix height is integrable. Negative determinant powers are bounded by powers of the height. The height also controls the Frobenius norm. After multiplying by a sufficiently large negative height power, the resulting majorant is a positive determinant power times an integrable negative power of `1 + ‖A‖`. The determinant power cancels the Haar density, so the remaining integral is bounded by a Lebesgue integral on the full matrix space.

These measure facts support the real convergence argument in Hervé Jacquet, *Archimedean Rankin–Selberg Integrals*, Section 7.2, Proposition 7.2. The integrable height bound uses the determinant density to integrate over the real group directly.
