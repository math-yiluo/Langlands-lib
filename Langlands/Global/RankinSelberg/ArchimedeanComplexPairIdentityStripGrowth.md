# Complex ordinary local normalizations from Jacquet’s pair identity

`complexPairIdentityNormalizedStripGrowth` gives an entire normalization of exponential strip growth for the ordinary complex Rankin–Selberg integral in unequal and equal ranks. The determinant pair identity is a premise for each pair of tests and factors. In unequal ranks the inputs are complex Whittaker model vectors for opposite additive characters, and the proof uses the published common right-half-plane convergence of their ordinary and dual integrals. In equal ranks the inputs are two functions and a Schwartz test, with common right convergence of the ordinary and Fourier-dual kernels given explicitly. Both clauses take a nonzero sign and the indicated original and dual character lists.

On the unipotent quotient the weight is \(\lvert\det g\rvert_{\mathbb C}=\lVert\det g\rVert^2\). The proof recovers measurability by exponentiating the continuous descended logarithm and uses the exact complex power of this weight to move the spectral parameter out of the zero-parameter kernel. The compatible quotient measures are sigma finite by regularity and sigma compactness. In the unequal dual integral the rectangular self-dual measure is a finite product of sigma-finite Haar measures. Each complex reciprocal L-denominator is a product of shifted reciprocal Gamma factors of exponential type on strips. The closed epsilon formula gives a nonzero entire factor whose norm depends only on the real part. These inputs satisfy `pairIdentityNormalizedStripGrowth` with the exact ordinary and dual kernels.

The shapes of the local integrals and their duals come from Hervé Jacquet, *Archimedean Rankin–Selberg Integrals* (2009), §2.1 and Theorem 2.1(i)–(iii), author-version pp. 6–7, and the pair identity and its use in §4.2 and Proposition 4.1, pp. 22–25. The unequal-rank dual uses the Weyl translate and rectangular measure of (2.5). Its epsilon parameter can be chosen separately from the character determining the measure to accommodate the sign convention of `ComplexUnequalJacquetConventionReduction`.
