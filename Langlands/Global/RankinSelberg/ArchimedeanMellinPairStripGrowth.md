# Strip growth of normalizations from a Mellin pair

`MellinPairEntireValueStatement` starts with two absolutely convergent determinant Mellin integrals on right half-planes and one rapidly decreasing Mellin pair satisfying the theta identity. The pair's transforms are the reciprocal \(L\)-denominators multiplied by a Gaussian, with the signed epsilon factor on the original side. The proof uses the choice \(P(s)=s^2\) from Jacquet's family of quadratic polynomials. The inverse signed epsilon factor is entire and its norm depends only on the real part. Both reciprocal \(L\)-denominators have exponential strip growth.

The implemented normalization multiplies the theta Mellin transform by \(\exp(-s^2)\) and the inverse signed epsilon factor. The two half-plane identities in `mellin_theta_entire` identify this normalization with the original integral times its reciprocal \(L\)-denominator on the right and the reflected dual integral times the dual denominator and inverse signed epsilon factor on the left. Right convergence bounds each raw integral on the corresponding line. The denominator estimates then bound the normalized value exponentially on both lines. Rapid decrease bounds the theta Mellin transform on the full strip between them, so the normalization has at most Gaussian growth there.

`mellinPairEntireValue` gives the entire function with both half-plane identities. If the original denominator is nonzero sufficiently far to the right, `mellinPairNormalizedStripGrowth` uses the right identity to construct an `OrdinaryIntegralNormalization`.

`norm_le_exp_of_gaussian_on_strip` propagates exponential edge bounds for an entire function with Gaussian growth on a strip. At a point \(z\) with \(m=|\operatorname{Im} z|\ge1\), it multiplies the function by \(\exp(s^2/m)\). Completing the square gives a constant bound on each edge. The multiplier preserves the Gaussian estimate in the interior, which meets Mathlib's growth hypothesis for Phragmén–Lindelöf. Removing the multiplier at \(z\) gives a bound exponential in \(m\).

This derived estimate uses Jacquet, *Archimedean Rankin–Selberg integrals* (2009), §4.2 and Proposition 4.1, author-version pp. 22–25 (published pp. 78–81). Jacquet applies Phragmén–Lindelöf to the raw continuation, using the Stirling ratio bound of Lemma 4.2. The Lean proof applies it directly to the normalized integral and uses the exponential estimates for the reciprocal denominators on the boundary. The theta identity and pair transform formulas are inputs.
