# Schwartz seminorm bounds and convergence of the real Godement column integral

`RealGodementColumnSeminormStatement` fixes a nonzero real additive parameter, a lower Whittaker function and a lower-group Haar measure. The coefficient and all its right derivatives satisfy the regular Whittaker conditions for the opposite additive parameter, and the derivatives have a common growth exponent. On any strip `|Re s − m| ≤ radius` with `m` the lower rank and `radius ≥ 0`, the statement bounds the norm of the first-column kernel pointwise by `q Φ * B h`. The continuous real Schwartz seminorm `q` and the nonnegative Haar-integrable function `B` work for every rectangular Schwartz test `Φ`, determinant parity and exponent in the strip.

After including the real Haar determinant density, the determinant weight contributes the real exponent `Re s − m`. The coefficient gauge estimate gives a fixed exponential growth term in the absolute logarithmic Iwasawa coordinates and arbitrarily fast decay in the positive simple-root sum. The determinant logarithm is bounded in absolute value by the sum of the absolute coordinates, so the strip radius controls its contribution to the exponent. The real Iwasawa bound controls that coordinate sum by the positive simple-root sum and logarithms of the two Fourier argument norms. The gauge decay absorbs the root term. The first-column seminorm estimate absorbs the remaining norm powers and leaves a negative power of the matrix norm of an order integrable against additive matrix Lebesgue measure. Multiplying by `|det h|^m` transfers the bound to the specified Haar measure.

Specializing the bound to each real exponent gives absolute convergence for all complex exponents. Differentiation under the integral on a right half-plane containing each chosen exponent gives an entire integral for a fixed test. The Library’s uniform real Whittaker model supplies the derivative hypotheses for these conclusions.

`exists_columnMellin_uniform_seminorm` chooses the continuous seminorm before the lower coefficient once its derivative growth exponent is specified. The exponent and strip radius determine the gauge and Fourier decay orders. The constant in the gauge estimate depends on the lower coefficient and is included in the Haar majorant.

The seminorm bound is derived from the fixed-lower-coefficient argument for equation (7.7) in Hervé Jacquet, *Archimedean Rankin–Selberg Integrals*, §7.2, Proposition 7.2, pp. 48–49. It applies with the two column conventions related by the long Weyl reversal and at the identity in the upper group.

For a family sharing the lower derivative constants as well as the exponent, the bound has one integrable lower Haar majorant for all members. The simple-root gauge bound supplies a common constant, and the Mellin strip and Schwartz seminorm estimates then apply over the entire family.
