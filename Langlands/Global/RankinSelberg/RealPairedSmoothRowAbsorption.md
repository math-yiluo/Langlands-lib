# Absorbing a row test in a smooth real Whittaker pair

Take two jointly continuous complex Banach representations of real general
linear groups of positive ranks. The continuous complex-linear form on the
genuine smooth part of their completed Banach projective product is
assumed to obey the independent upper-unipotent covariance with opposite
nonzero additive scales. Here smoothness uses the product-group action.
Suppose a common leading block has positive rank and one of the factors
has a strictly larger rank. For every genuine smooth vector and Schwartz
test of the full last row in the common group, there is a genuine smooth
vector whose paired coefficient on the common group is the original
paired coefficient multiplied by that test.

Insert the column unipotent immediately after the common block in the
chosen larger factor, with identity in the other factor. The matrix of
this product-group element and its inverse are identity plus and minus
one continuous linear function of the column. This gives the linear
height bound. Moderate growth of the two Banach actions and the
projective operator norm inequality give the required polynomial bound
on the product action.

Move the column past the simultaneously padded argument. In its own
factor it becomes the column obtained by multiplying by the common
matrix. In the other factor the insertion stays identity. Independent
upper-unipotent covariance therefore gives the additive character of
the pairing of the column with the common last row, at the scale of the
chosen factor. The additive row absorption theorem averages that orbit to
realize the given test.

The paired absorption is the use of Proposition 6.2 on p. 106 in the
proof of Lemma 14.1, with the row multiplication proved in Propositions
6.1–6.2, pp. 41–42 of the author-posted edition.
[H. Jacquet, *Archimedean Rankin–Selberg Integrals* (2009)](https://www.math.columbia.edu/~hj/PerfectRankinSelberg.pdf#page=106).

The map `slot` embeds one group into the two-factor group with identity
in the other coordinate. Its projection formulas also identify
independent translates of paired coefficients. The lemma
`exists_productNorm_bound` gives the product Banach operator estimate for
all product-group elements.
