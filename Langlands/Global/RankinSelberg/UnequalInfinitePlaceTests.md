# Unequal-rank Rankin–Selberg tests at individual infinite places

`UnequalInfinitePlaceTestsStatement` collects the two independent open inputs `RealUnequalPlaceTestsStatement` and `ComplexUnequalPlaceTestsStatement`. Each uses the data of the corresponding published unequal-rank local functional-equation statement, up to (and excluding) the test functions and quotient measure. In particular, the additive character is trivial on the number field and has the specified nontrivial local component at the chosen place. The original and contragredient realizations have the supplied parameters there.

`HasRealUnequalPlaceTest` and `HasComplexUnequalPlaceTest` express the conclusion at one place. For ranks `r + 1 > t + 1`, choose restrictions of actual global Whittaker coefficients in those ranks, for the original additive character and its inverse respectively. Choose compatible quotient measure data on the smaller-rank unipotent quotient. For that **same** pair of restrictions and quotient measure, the block-zero integral and the translated complementary-block dual integral are each holomorphic on a right half-plane and nonzero at some point there. The two half-planes and points may differ. The dual uses the self-dual rectangular measure of the specified local additive component. There is no Schwartz argument; adjacent ranks are included with a zero-row rectangle. The fixed-place conclusion does not require tests at any other place, so the complex version can also be used with a totally complex field.

## Implemented product lemma

`HolomorphicNonzeroOnRightHalfPlane.finset_prod` takes a finite product of holomorphic, non-identically-zero tests. Choose a common right half-plane by bounding the finitely many original thresholds. Each factor is still nonzero somewhere on the common half-plane by `exists_ne_zero_above`. Isolated zeros on this connected half-plane give eventual nonvanishing along the punctured neighborhood of any chosen point in it. Intersect the finitely many conditions and choose a point still in the half-plane; there the entire product is nonzero. Differentiability follows from the finite product rule. This also works for an empty family, with product one. The shared Lean lemma in `EqualInfinitePlaceTests` proves the product formula by finite-set induction using the two-factor result.

## Scope and provenance

These are internal test-existence targets for the pointwise step in Cogdell 2004, proof of Theorem 4.1, printed p. 219. The existence conclusions are not proved here. Jacquet 2009, author-posted pp. 8–10, gives a route: Theorem 2.6(i) supplies an ordinary test in a completed tensor product of ordered smooth inductions, and Theorem 2.3 gives continuity. One still has to obtain an ordinary nonzero value for an actual pair of restrictions. Theorem 2.7(i) supplies an algebraic, compact-finite tensor only in adjacent ranks for irreducible inductions, and does not itself identify the Library's actual slices. The local convergence and functional equation (Theorem 2.1) provide a way to transfer nonvanishing to the dual once an ordinary test is available. The statements here require only nonzero values, not a test or finite sum equal to the entire local L-factor.
