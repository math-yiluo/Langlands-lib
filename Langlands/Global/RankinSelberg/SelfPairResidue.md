# Nonzero residues for the equal-rank conjugate pair

The period of a nonzero cusp form paired with its complex conjugate is the integral of its squared absolute value over the determinant slab. A scalar moves a point of nonzero evaluation into the interior of the slab. The central character preserves nonvanishing there, and continuity and the full support of quotient Haar measure make the integral strictly positive. The argument implements the Petersson pairing in the equal-rank residue formula of Cogdell (2004), §2.3, p. 210 (PDF p. 213, `cogdell-2004-analytic-theory-gln`).

`exists_schwartzBruhat_origin_ne_zero` uses the Library's nonnegative standard Schwartz test with value one at the origin. It works outside any prescribed finite set of places. The Fourier transform at zero integrates the function, which is continuous, integrable, nonnegative and positive at zero. The row Haar measure has full support, so this integral is positive. The Fourier convention is that of Getz and Hahn (2024), Appendix C.3, pp. 553–554 (`getz-hahn-2024-automorphic-representations`).

A realization trivial on the split centre has unitary central character, and its conjugate realization is also trivial on the split centre. Their product central character is trivial. The positive period then shows that the conjugate realization is a full-operator contragredient. `SelfPairIntegralPoleStatement` and `selfPairIntegralPole` apply the equal-rank continuation to this conjugate self-pair. For a nonzero vector and a test with both origin values nonzero, the order is negative at each of 0 and 1 and at least −1 there. The same continuation supplies absolute convergence and agreement on a right half-plane and boundedness in vertical strips away from the two poles. This integral result supplies test data for the pole direction of Cogdell (2004), §4.2, p. 222 (PDF p. 225).
