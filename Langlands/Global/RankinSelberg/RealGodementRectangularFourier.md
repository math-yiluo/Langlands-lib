# Complementary real rectangular affine Fourier slices

Partition the entries of a real rectangle into two sets, each with its prescribed fixed values. Integrate a Schwartz function over the slice in which the first set varies, using the positive phase paired with the first fixed values. The result equals the integral of the full positive Fourier transform over the slice in which the second set varies, using the negative phase paired with the second fixed values. The measures are the products of the self-dual real measure for the same nonzero additive parameter, and both integrands are absolutely integrable.

The proof identifies rectangular entries with Euclidean coordinates and splits those coordinates isometrically into the two parts of the partition. Uncurrying and the Euclidean coordinate equivalences preserve the specified product measures. The full Schwartz transform is the actual rectangular Fourier integral because the Euclidean inner product is the entry pairing. Transport the real product-slice identity through these equivalences to obtain the two affine matrix integrals.

This is the rectangular analytic identity underlying the real case of Hervé Jacquet, *Archimedean Rankin–Selberg Integrals*, Lemma 7.1. The complementary antidiagonal coordinate sets of that lemma are a specialization.
