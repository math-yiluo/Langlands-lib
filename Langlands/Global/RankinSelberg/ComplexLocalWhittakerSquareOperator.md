# The coordinate square operator on complex Whittaker coefficients

The Lie generator attached to a real matrix direction \(X\) acts by the
right derivative \(R(X)\) on the complex standard module. An intertwiner
preserves that action by its defining differential clause. Applying
the clause twice sends its square to \(R(X)R(X)\) on the output.

The operator in this module sums both successive real-matrix-unit
derivatives and successive imaginary-matrix-unit derivatives. Linearity
therefore sends the coordinate square sum on the inducing vector to that
differential operator on the actual Whittaker coefficient.
