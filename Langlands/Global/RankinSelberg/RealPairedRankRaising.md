# One-step real rank raising for a smooth Whittaker pair

`RealPairedRankRaisingStepStatement` concerns two jointly continuous
complex Banach representations of positive-rank real general linear
groups. Use their completed projective product with the genuine smooth
structure of the separate group actions. The continuous complex-linear
form has the independent upper-unipotent covariance at opposite
nonzero real scales and is invariant under the upper unipotent subgroup
of the larger comparison group padded into both factors. The two
comparison groups have adjacent positive ranks and embed as leading
blocks into both ambient factors.

For arbitrary compatible quotient measures and the supplied additive
and bi-invariant trailing measures, the smaller quotient integral
with determinant exponent `s - 1` times a positive scalar equals a
finite sum of larger quotient integrals at exponent `s`. The larger
terms have compactly supported Schwartz weights of the full bottom
row. The finite family is chosen before `s`. At a spectral point the
comparison assumes absolute integrability of each larger term and
gives absolute integrability on the smaller quotient. The positive
scalar depends on the measures. If one of the two ambient groups is
strictly larger than the larger comparison group, every larger row
weight can be absorbed into its smooth vector, with equality at each
quotient point.

Use mapped rank raising with the simultaneous leading-block embedding.
Its exponential compatibility identifies the compensating subgroup
averages in the product-group smooth topology. Consecutive leading
padding composes to a single padding, giving the specified smaller
coefficient. The linear splitting of a bottom row into its initial
entries and its last entry transports the compact weights. For the
conditional absorption use the upper column in the strictly larger
factor, with identity in the other factor.

This is the one-step integral comparison in Lemma 14.1, pp. 105–107
of the author-posted edition, together with its use of Proposition 6.2
for absorption in a larger factor.
[H. Jacquet, *Archimedean Rankin–Selberg Integrals* (2009)](https://www.math.columbia.edu/~hj/PerfectRankinSelberg.pdf#page=105).
