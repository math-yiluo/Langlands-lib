# Iterated compact differences of complex standard vectors

The difference for a unitary element is its right-translation map minus
the identity. A list of elements gives an ordered product of differences.
The bound of order J controls each product of length at most J by a
uniform constant times the product of the displacements of the elements
from the identity, measured in matrix operator norm. The empty list gives
the uniform compact value bound. Prepending an element gives a first
difference bound for a product of length at most J from a bound of order
J + 1. An averaging operator commuting with right compact translations
commutes with every such product. Its value and first-difference error
bounds therefore give residual bounds at the same order and with loss of
one order, respectively.

For a fixed compact-finite vector, restrict the image of that vector
under all compact-equivariant endomorphisms to the compact subgroup.
Each restriction is a matrix coefficient of the original finite orbit
span, evaluated against a linear functional on that span. These
coefficient functions lie in a fixed finite-dimensional space. Compact
restriction is injective on the standard module, so the preimage of that
space is finite-dimensional. Compact point evaluations separate its
vectors, making any given linear functional a finite linear combination
of those evaluations there. The sum of the norms of the coefficients
gives the asserted bound by the compact value bound, uniformly over the
commuting endomorphisms.
