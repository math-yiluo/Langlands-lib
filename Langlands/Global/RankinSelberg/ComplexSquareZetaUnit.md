# A constant nonzero normalized complex square matrix integral

Fix a positive rank, any Haar measure on the complex general linear group, and inducing data whose angular weights are nondecreasing. There is a compact-finite inducing vector and a standard square polynomial Gaussian for which the square zeta integral converges absolutely on a right half-plane. On that half-plane its product with the reciprocal standard \(L\)-factor is a nonzero constant.

The upper-entry integral of the angular test separates as the conjugate of a compact profile and the exact scalar angular Gaussians. Take the standard section of that profile as the inducing vector. The compact integral is its positive squared norm. The product Tate reduction then evaluates the diagonal integral by the separate scalar Gaussian integrals. Their Gamma factors cancel the reciprocal standard \(L\)-factor on the right half-plane. The remaining constants come from the matrix Haar comparison, the upper-entry Gaussian mass, the compact integral and the scalar Tate measure. All are nonzero.

This gives a test for the complex square zeta integral in Jacquet, *Archimedean Rankin–Selberg Integrals* (2009), §4.7, using the scalar Tate Gaussian normalization.
