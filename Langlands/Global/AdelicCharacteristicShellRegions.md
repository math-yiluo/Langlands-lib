# Disjoint measurable characteristic shells

`characteristicShellRegion` uses the actual minimal-Levi height product with heights in the intersection \(C_{T+S}^G\cap C_T^Q\), and takes its image in the norm-one rational quotient. `characteristicRegion_eq_iUnion_shellRegions` first writes a point of the region at \(T+S\) in common Siegel coordinates by means of the two rational covers. The parabolic chamber partition assigns its height to a shell. Conversely the height of each shell representative satisfies the inequalities of \(C_{T+S}^G\).

`exists_pairwiseDisjoint_characteristicShellRegion` compares the shell images with the images of the parabolic characteristic Siegel sets and applies the rational separation result there. The lower simple-root bound for \(T\) works for every increment \(S\). `characteristicShellRegion_top` recovers the characteristic region at \(T\) as the full-group shell when the increment is nonnegative in every upper weight coordinate.

For measurability, every linear functional of the height in positive torus coordinates is continuous. The proof writes the functional by the perfect pairing with the real character space and checks continuity on pure tensors by the character logarithm. Membership in the coroot space is a closed condition because it is the fixed-point locus of the projection onto that space, tested by the same linear functionals. Together with the weak inequalities this gives a closed set. The finitely many strict root inequalities give an open set in the positive torus. That open set is second countable and locally compact, so it is a countable union of compact subsets. Intersecting each of them with the closed conditions, adjoining the compact Iwasawa factors, restricting to the closed ambient norm-one subgroup, and taking the quotient image all preserve compactness. The resulting countable union is Borel on the quotient.

`exists_integral_characteristicRegion_eq_sum_shellRegions` applies the finite disjoint-union formula to an integrable function on the characteristic region. This supplies the decomposition of the original integral in (7.20) before replacing its integrand with weak constant terms.

The source is P. Delorme, *On the spectral theorem of Langlands*, Advances in Mathematics 426 (2023), 109107, pp. 23–24 and 26–27, (7.6), (7.13), and (7.20). The journal PDF in `delorme-2023-spectral-theorem-langlands` has SHA-256 `9b2e82af72ac45c9745357d475153d43d29ec3e1ea68bf51980acf466110e2df`.
