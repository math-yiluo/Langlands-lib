# Harish-Chandra's reproducing kernel on `G(𝔸)`

Module `Langlands.Global.AdelicReproducingKernelStatement` states
`Langlands.Global.AdelicReproducingKernelStatement`. It does not prove it.

| Declaration | Kind | Source |

## Statement

Let `F : Type` be a number field and `G` an affine group scheme over `F`, locally of finite
type and connected reductive (`IsConnectedReductive G`). Let `K` be a maximal compact subgroup of
`G(𝔸) = AdelicGroupPoints F G` (`IsMaximalCompactSubgroup F G K`: `K` is compact and no strictly
larger compact subgroup contains it), and let `K_∞ = K.comap (ofInfinite F G)` be its archimedean
part. Fix the smooth atlas `hsmooth : SmoothOfRelativeDimension d G.X.hom` and the commutator
premise `hcalc` that define the enveloping algebra of `G(F_∞)` and its centre `𝔷`.

Let `Φ : G(𝔸) → ℂ` be

1. left invariant under the rational points `G(F)` (`IsLeftInvariantUnder F G (rationalSubgroup F G)`);
2. smooth (`IsAdelicSmoothFunction`: continuous, with plot-smooth archimedean slices, and right
   invariant under an open subgroup of `G(𝔸_f)`);
3. `K_∞`-finite (`IsInfiniteSubgroupFiniteFunction`: the right translates of `Φ` by `K_∞` span a
   finite-dimensional space);
4. `𝔷`-finite (`IsCenterFiniteFunction`: an ideal of finite codimension of `𝔷` annihilates every
   archimedean slice of `Φ`).

Then there is `A : G(𝔸) → ℂ`, continuous and with compact support, such that

`Φ g = ∫ a, Φ (g * a) * A a ∂(Measure.haar)` for every `g ∈ G(𝔸)`,

the integral being the Bochner integral for the Haar measure `Measure.haar` of `G(𝔸)`. No growth
condition is imposed on `Φ`.

## Sources

- Harish-Chandra, *Discrete series for semisimple Lie groups II*, Acta Math. 116 (1966) 1–111,
  registered as `harish-chandra-1966-discrete-series-ii`, §8, Theorem 1 and its proof, printed
  pp. 18–19 (PDF pp. 18–19). For a connected real reductive group with maximal compact subgroup
  `K` (the notation of §7), let `𝔅` be the subalgebra of the enveloping algebra generated by its
  centre and the complexified Lie algebra of `K`. If `f` is a smooth function from the group to a
  finite-dimensional complex vector space `V` whose transforms `b f` (`b ∈ 𝔅`) span a
  finite-dimensional space, then for every neighbourhood `U` of `1` there is a smooth compactly
  supported `α`, supported in `U` and invariant under conjugation by `K`, with `f * α = f`.
  Footnote (1) on p. 18 records that no condition on `f` at infinity is needed.
- C. Mœglin and J.-L. Waldspurger, *Spectral decomposition and Eisenstein series*, Cambridge
  University Press 1995, registered as `moeglin-waldspurger-1995-spectral-decomposition`, chapter
  file `05.2_pp_18_40_Automorphic_forms_growth_constant_terms.pdf`: I.2.5 (smooth functions on
  `G(𝔸)` and right convolution `δ(f)φ(g) = ∫ f(h) φ(gh) dh`, printed pp. 26–27), and I.2.17,
  Lemma (printed p. 38): for finitely many finite-dimensional spaces of automorphic forms there is
  a smooth compactly supported, bi-`K`-finite `f` on `G(𝔸)` with `δ(f)φ = φ` for all of them,
  citing Harish-Chandra's Theorem 1 above.

## Provenance (derived)

The statement is the adelic form of Harish-Chandra's theorem, as in the Lemma of
Mœglin–Waldspurger I.2.17, for one function and without the moderate growth that is part of their
notion of automorphic form; Harish-Chandra's theorem itself assumes no growth. The derivation from
Harish-Chandra's archimedean theorem runs as follows.

- A maximal compact subgroup of `G(𝔸) = G(F_∞) × G(𝔸_f)` is the product of its two projections,
  since that product is a compact subgroup containing it; so `K = K_∞ × K_f` and `K_∞` is a maximal
  compact subgroup of `G(F_∞)`.
- Let `L` be an open compact subgroup of `G(𝔸_f)` fixing `Φ` on the right. For a finite adelic
  point `b`, the archimedean slice `h ↦ Φ(h, b)` is smooth, `K_∞`-finite and annihilated by the
  ideal of condition 4. Left `G(F)`-invariance makes the slice at `γ b l` (`γ ∈ G(F)`, `l ∈ L`)
  a left translate of the slice at `b` by `γ_∞`, and right convolution commutes with left
  translation. Since `G(F) \ G(𝔸_f) / L` is finite, finitely many slices determine `Φ`.
- `G(F_∞)` has finitely many connected components, and `K_∞` meets each of them. Restricting the
  finitely many slices and their left translates by representatives of the components in `K_∞`
  to the identity component gives finitely many functions on a connected reductive group,
  `𝔅`-finite for the maximal compact subgroup `K_∞ ∩ G(F_∞)°` (left translation commutes with
  the right action of the enveloping algebra). Harish-Chandra's theorem, applied to the
  vector-valued function formed by them, gives one smooth compactly supported `α` on `G(F_∞)°`
  reproducing all of them, hence all slices of `Φ`.
- The function `A(a) = c · α(a_∞⁻¹) 1_L(a_f)` on `G(𝔸)`, with `α` extended by zero, is continuous
  (`L` is open and compact) and compactly supported. The adelic Haar measure is a multiple of the
  product of Haar measures on `G(F_∞)` and `G(𝔸_f)`, and reductive groups are unimodular, so
  right `L`-invariance of `Φ` turns `∫ Φ(g a) A(a) da` into `(φ * α)(g_∞)` for the slice
  `φ` of `Φ` at `g_f`, for a suitable constant `c > 0`.

The conclusion records only continuity and compact support of the kernel, which is what its
applications to growth estimates use; Harish-Chandra's kernel is moreover smooth and supported
near the identity.
