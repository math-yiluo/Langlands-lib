# Compactness of the adelic quotients of the units of a central division algebra

`Langlands.Global.DivisionAlgebraUnitsCompactQuotientStatement` is a
proposition definition (a `def` returning `Prop`). This module states it and
does not prove it.

Informally: if `A` is a finite-dimensional central division algebra over a
number field `E` and `G = GL₁(A)` is its group of units, then the norm-one
quotient `G(E) \ G(𝔸_E)¹` and the canonical quotient
`[G] = A_G G(E) \ G(𝔸_E)` are compact. Moreover, for each central
homomorphism `i : T ⟶ G` from a split torus `T` whose character restriction
is injective with finite-index range, the quotient of `G(𝔸_E)` by the
positive real points of `T` and the rational points is compact. Without the
conditions on the character restriction this last claim fails: for
`E = A = ℚ` and the trivial torus the quotient is the idele class group
`ℚˣ \ 𝔸_ℚˣ`, whose idelic norm maps it onto the noncompact `ℝ_{>0}`
(Getz–Hahn (2.18), p. 68). The first assertion is Fujisaki's lemma. It is also the case `G = GL₁(A)` of the
compactness criterion for groups whose semisimple part is anisotropic, often
called Godement's compactness criterion (Platonov–Rapinchuk, Theorem 5.6).

## Hypotheses

The statement quantifies over the following data.

- `E : Type` with `[Field E] [NumberField E]`: a number field.
- `A : Type` with `[Ring A] [Nontrivial A] [Algebra E A]
  [FiniteDimensional E A] [Algebra.IsCentral E A]`, and the hypothesis
  `∀ a : A, a ≠ 0 → IsUnit a`. Together these say that `A` is a
  finite-dimensional central division algebra over `E`. The division property
  is a hypothesis on the given ring structure, not a `DivisionRing` instance.
  The hypothesis has the same form as
  `Langlands.Shimura.CentralSimpleUnitaryData.IsDivision`, and every division
  ring satisfies it.
- `G := FiniteAlgebraUnits.groupScheme (Module.finBasis E A)`. This is the
  Library's unit group scheme of `A`, built from the basis `Module.finBasis E A`.
  It is a smooth affine group scheme over `Spec E`. Its points over a
  commutative `E`-algebra `R` are `(R ⊗[E] A)ˣ`
  (`FiniteAlgebraUnits.affinePointsEquiv`), and its rational points are `Aˣ`
  (`FiniteAlgebraUnits.rationalPointsEquiv`). The instances
  `FiniteAlgebraUnits.groupScheme_isAffine` and
  `FiniteAlgebraUnits.groupScheme_smooth` supply the affineness and the local
  finite type that the three quotients need.
- `IsConnectedReductive G`. This holds mathematically, because `GL₁(A)`
  becomes `GL_d` over an algebraic closure, where `d² = dim_E A`. This module
  does not prove it, so it is a hypothesis.

## Conclusion

The conclusion is a conjunction of three clauses. Every quotient below is a
`LeftCosetSpace`, that is, a space of cosets `Γ x` with the quotient topology.
The adelic point groups carry the Library's global-function evaluation
topology (module `Langlands.Global.AdelicGroupPointTopology`).

1. `CompactSpace (AdelicGroupPoints.NormOneRationalQuotient E G)`. This is
   `G(E) \ G(𝔸_E)¹`. Here `AdelicGroupPoints.normOneSubgroup E G` is the
   intersection, over all algebraic characters `χ : G ⟶ 𝔾_m` over `E`, of the
   kernels of the adelic absolute values `|χ|`. The quotient is by the
   principal rational points `rationalNormOneSubgroup E G`. Mathematically,
   the characters of `GL₁(A)` defined over `E` are the powers of the reduced
   norm. So `G(𝔸_E)¹` is the group of units `x` of `𝔸_E ⊗ A` with
   `|Nrd(x)|_𝔸 = 1`.
2. `CompactSpace (RestrictionSplitCentre.CanonicalQuotient E G)`. This is
   `[G] = A_G G(E) \ G(𝔸_E)`, the space that carries `canonicalMeasure`.
   Here `A_G` is `RestrictionSplitCentre.canonicalSubgroup E G`, the positive
   real points of the chosen split component of `Res_{E/ℚ} G`.
   Mathematically, for `GL₁(A)` this is `ℝ_{>0}`, embedded as scalars
   diagonally at the archimedean places.
3. For every commutative group scheme `T` over `Spec E` and every
   `i : T.toGrp ⟶ G` such that
   - `characterRestrict i` is injective and has finite-index range,
   - there is a splitting `T.toGrp ≅ splitTorus (Spec E) I` for some finite
     `I : Type`, and
   - `IsCentral i` holds,

   `CompactSpace (PositiveRationalQuotient E G T i)` holds: the quotient of
   `G(𝔸_E)` by the subgroup generated by the positive real points of `T` and
   the rational points is compact. These are the
   quotient hypotheses of
   `Langlands.Automorphic.GroupSchemeFunctions.GroupSchemeCompactQuotientL2DecompositionStatement`,
   whose separate Haar right-invariance hypothesis plays no role in
   compactness. For this `G`, clause 3 discharges that statement's hypothesis
   `CompactSpace (PositiveRationalQuotient F G T i)`.

Nothing is asserted about measures, finite volume, or the case where `A` is not
a division algebra. For `A = M_n(E)` with `n ≥ 2` the quotients are not compact.

## Correspondence and derived content

The statement is derived content. The sources state clause 1, and clauses 2
and 3 are reformulations of clause 1 on Library objects.

- **Clause 1.**
  - Platonov–Rapinchuk's `G_A^{(1)}` and Getz–Hahn's `G(𝔸_F)¹` are the
    Library's `normOneSubgroup`: the intersection of the kernels of the
    adelic absolute values of all characters defined over the base field.
  - For `G = GL₁(D)`, the semisimple part is `SL₁(D)`, which is anisotropic
    by Proposition 2.12. Theorem 5.6 then gives compactness of
    `G_A^{(1)}/G_K`.
  - In Weil's form, write `D = A`, `ν = Nrd` and `dim_E A = d²`.
    - Since `A` is central over `E`, Weil IX §2, Corollary 1 to
      Proposition 6 (printed p. 169) applies, and both
      `x ↦ ax` and `x ↦ xa` have determinant `N_{A/E}(a) = ν(a)^d`. This is
      an identity of polynomial functions with coefficients in `E`. By the
      Corollary of Proposition 3 in IV §3 (p. 74), both modules of a unit
      `a` of `D_𝔸` therefore equal `|ν(a)|_𝔸^d`.
    - With `μ = 1`, the two inequalities of Theorem 4 read
      `|ν(a)|_𝔸^d ≤ 1` and `|ν(a)|_𝔸^d ≥ 1`. Since `d > 0`, they cut out
      exactly `D_1 = {a : |ν(a)|_𝔸 = 1}`. Mathematically this is
      `G(𝔸_E)¹`, because the characters of `GL₁(A)` defined over `E` are
      the powers of `ν`.
    - Weil's topology on `D_𝔸ˣ` (IV §3, Definition 2, printed p. 71) makes
      `x ↦ (x, x⁻¹)` a homeomorphism onto its image in `D_𝔸 × D_𝔸`. The
      point topology of `G(𝔸_E)` can be read in the same way, from the
      coordinates of a unit and of its inverse in the basis
      `Module.finBasis E A`. The coordinate ring of `G` is generated by the
      coordinates and the inverse of the norm of the generic element
      (`FiniteAlgebraUnits.determinant`). The coordinates of `x⁻¹` lie in it
      by Cramer's rule. The inverse of the norm of `x` is the norm of `x⁻¹`,
      a polynomial in the coordinates of `x⁻¹`.
    - By the product formula, `Dˣ ⊆ D_1`, so `D_1` is a union of cosets
      `xDˣ`. The projection `D_𝔸ˣ → D_𝔸ˣ / Dˣ` is open, so its
      restriction identifies `D_1 / Dˣ` with the image of `D_1`.
      Theorem 4 then says that `D_1 / Dˣ` is compact.
    - Inversion is a homeomorphism of `D_1` that takes the classes `xDˣ`
      to the classes `Dˣx⁻¹`. It identifies `D_1 / Dˣ` with the
      left-coset quotient `NormOneRationalQuotient E G` of clause 1.
- **Clause 2.** This is clause 1 transported along Getz–Hahn's
  homeomorphism (2.22).
- **Clause 3.** This is clause 1 transported along the splitting of
  `G(𝔸_E)` as the positive real points of a central split torus times
  `G(𝔸_E)¹`.

Weil's Chapter IV Theorem 4 applies to every finite-dimensional division
algebra over an A-field. The comparison of its two modules with the
`E`-valued reduced norm uses the central case of Chapter IX, as reflected by
the hypothesis `Algebra.IsCentral E A`.

## Proof outline

Clauses 2 and 3 follow from clause 1 by the homeomorphisms below.

1. **Clause 1** is the analytic content, Fujisaki's lemma above. It can be
   proved by either of two routes.
   - Weil's argument for Theorem 4: a Minkowski-type lemma for a compact set
     of large Haar measure in `A_𝔸`, together with the discreteness of `A`
     in `A_𝔸` and the compactness of `A_𝔸 / A`.
   - Reduction theory. The Library's
     `Langlands.Global.AdelicSiegelFactors.centralSplitCompactQuotient`
     proves `AdelicSiegelFactors.CentralSplitCompactQuotientStatement`
     (Springer's compactness theorem). For a connected reductive `G` over a
     number field with a maximal split torus `s : S ⟶ G` such that
     `IsCentral s`, it gives a compact subset of `normOneSubgroup` that
     meets every left coset of the principal rational points.
     `AdelicGroupPoints.HasCompactNormOneRationalCover.compactSpace`
     (module `Langlands.Global.AdelicNormOneCompactCover`) turns such a
     cover into `CompactSpace (NormOneRationalQuotient E G)`. For
     `GL₁(A)` the required maximal split torus is the central scalar
     torus. Mathematically, every split torus of `GL₁(A)` is central: a
     nonzero weight space of its conjugation action on `A` consists of
     nilpotent elements, so it is zero because `A` is a division algebra.
2. **Clause 3 from clause 1.** Under exactly the hypotheses of clause 3, the
   Library's `PositiveRealTorus.positiveQuotientHomeomorph` is a homeomorphism
   `NormOneRationalQuotient E G ≃ₜ PositiveRationalQuotient E G T i`.
3. **Clause 2 from clause 1.** The chosen split component
   `RestrictionSplitCentre.canonicalComponent E G` satisfies the hypotheses
   of the Library's `RestrictionSplitCentre.normOneQuotientHomeomorph`:
   - its positive image is the canonical subgroup, by
     `canonicalSubgroup_eq_imageSubgroup`;
   - its character restriction is injective, by
     `canonical_characterRestrict_injective`, using
     `Langlands.GroupScheme.centralSplitTorusCharacter` and the hypothesis
     `IsConnectedReductive G`;
   - its character restriction has finite-index range, by
     `canonical_finiteIndex`;
   - it is a central split torus, by `isSplitComponent_canonicalComponent`.

   So `normOneQuotientHomeomorph` identifies
   `NormOneRationalQuotient E G` with `CanonicalQuotient E G`.
