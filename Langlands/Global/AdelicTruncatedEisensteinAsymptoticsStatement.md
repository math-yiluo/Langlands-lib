# Lapid's Proposition 1: truncated inner products of Eisenstein series, under local regularity

Module `Langlands.Global.AdelicTruncatedEisensteinAsymptoticsStatement` states one
proposition, `Langlands.Global.LocalTruncatedEisensteinInnerProductStatement`:
E. Lapid's Proposition 1 (formula (2)) for the Eisenstein series, intertwining
operators, inner products `(·,·)_Q` and norm-one truncation of the Library, for
parameters in a compact set on which the meromorphic continuations are holomorphic,
and for a chosen maximal compact subgroup `K` (see "The compact subgroup `K`").
The module does not prove it.

In namespace `Langlands.Global.AdelicGroupPoints` it defines
`parameterConj F P : X^*(P) ⊗ ℂ →ₗ[ℝ] X^*(P) ⊗ ℂ`, complex conjugation `λ ↦ λ̄`
of the scalar factor, and proves `parameterConj_tmul` and
`parameterConj_imaginaryParameter`: `λ̄ = −λ` for imaginary `λ = i m`,
`m ∈ X^*(P) ⊗ ℝ`.

It also defines, in namespace `Langlands.Global.ArthurTruncation`:

- `corootVector D α : V T`, the real coroot `α^∨ ∈ 𝔞_0` of a relative root `α`
  given by the reflection data `D` (`RelativeRootReflectionData.realCoroot`), and
  `0` for a character that is not a relative root;
- `StandardIndex hG hi ν`, the type of subsets `S ⊆ Δ_0` (the elements of
  `(simpleFinset ν hG hi).powerset`), which index the standard parabolic
  subgroups `Q ⊇ P_0`, and `S.roots : Set (characterLattice T)`, the set
  `Δ_0^Q = S`;
- `IsStandardRegularParameter D J R λ`: `R < ⟨Re λ, α^∨⟩` for every
  `α ∈ Δ_0 ∖ J`, for `λ ∈ X^*(P) ⊗ ℂ`, `P` the standard parabolic with
  `Δ_0^P = J`; here `Re λ` (`parameterRealPart`) is read in `𝔞_0^* = Vstar T`
  through `restrictToTorus` and paired (`pair T`) with `α^∨ = corootVector D α`;
- `corootCovolume hG hi ν D S e c : ℝ`, the volume `vol(𝔞_Q^G / L_Q)` described
  under "Normalizations" below;
- scoped instances giving the standard representatives `Standard.parabolicScheme`
  and `Standard.leviScheme` the finiteness and affineness instances, and the
  composite `Standard.unipotentToParabolic ≫ Standard.parabolicHom` (equal to
  `Standard.unipotentHom`) its closed-immersion instance, which
  `IsSquareIntegrableInducedForm`, `IsIwasawaCompatibleMeasure`,

**Intertwining operators.** The display at the top of Lapid's p.312,
`∫_{(V(𝔸) ∩ wU(𝔸)w⁻¹) \ V(𝔸)} φ_λ(w⁻¹vg) dv`, is the operator in its twisted
realization: as a function of `g` it is `e^{⟨wλ, H_Q(g)⟩}` times an element of
`A_Q` (Bernstein–Lapid §2.2 write `[M(w, λ)φ]_{wλ}` for it). The operator
`M(w, λ) : A_P → A_Q` of the Working hypothesis, with values in `A^2_Q`, and of
(2) is the untwisted one,
`M(w, λ)φ(g) = e^{−⟨wλ, H_Q(g)⟩} ∫ φ_λ(w⁻¹vg) dv`; for `P = Q` and `w = 1` it is
`φ`, whereas the displayed integral is `φ_λ`. This is the Library's
`IsIwasawaCompact.intertwiningOperator`: `heightPower (−wλ)` (the factor
`e^{−⟨wλ, H_Q(g)⟩}` for the height of `K` along `Q`) times the intertwining
integral. That integral is taken over `(V(F) ∩ wU(𝔸)w⁻¹) \ V(𝔸)` for the Haar
measure of `V(𝔸)` with `vol(V(F) \ V(𝔸)) = 1` (`intertwiningMeasure`); since the
integrand is left invariant under `V(𝔸) ∩ wU(𝔸)w⁻¹` and the quotient of this
group by its rational points has volume `1`, it equals the integral over
`(V(𝔸) ∩ wU(𝔸)w⁻¹) \ V(𝔸)` for the quotient measure (Mœglin–Waldspurger
II.1.6, Remark; Bernstein–Lapid §2.2; see the Markdown of
`AdelicIntertwiningOperator`).

`IsMeromorphicInCoordinatesOn f D` includes that `D` is open and dense and
that `f` is holomorphic on `D`. A continuation that agrees with the convergent
series on a nonempty open set is determined on its domain of holomorphy, so
`E λ` is the value at `λ` of the meromorphically continued Eisenstein series for
`λ ∈ DE`, and `∑ a, coeff S t a λ • ψ S t a` that of `M(w, λ)v` for `λ ∈ DM`.

**Smooth families.** Lapid's `φ(λ)` is a smooth map into a finite-dimensional
subspace of `A^2_P`. Writing `φ(λ) = ∑_j a_j(λ) v_j` in a basis `v_j`, and
`φ'(λ') = ∑_l a'_l(λ') v'_l`, both sides of (2) are sesquilinear in
`(φ(λ), φ'(λ'))` for fixed `(λ, λ')`: the Eisenstein series, `Λ^T` and
`M(w, λ)` are linear in the vector, and `(·,·)_{G \ G(𝔸)^1}` and `(·,·)_Q` are
sesquilinear. So (2) for the family is the finite combination
`∑_{j,l} a_j(λ) conj(a'_l(λ'))` of (2) for the pairs `(v_j, v'_l)`, with a
constant uniform on compact sets because the `a_j`, `a'_l` are bounded there.
This uses (2) for the pairs `(v_j, v'_l)` on the compact set, that is, the
holomorphy of each `E(·, v_j, λ)` near it; Lapid's hypothesis, that only
`E_P(·, φ(λ), λ)` is smooth, also covers families whose poles cancel, and that
case is not covered here.

### Conclusion

For `r : ℕ` and vectors `gen j ∈ V T` with `0 < ⟨α, gen j⟩` for every simple root
`α` (`eval T α (gen j)`), and a compact set `C` of coordinate pairs
`(x, x') ∈ ℝ^{n J} × ℝ^{n J'}` such that, with `λ = i e_J(x)` and
`λ' = i e_{J'}(x')` (`imaginaryParameter`),

- `λ ∈ i(𝔞_P^G)^*`: `restrictToTorus F i ν J.roots (e J x) ∈ relativeRootSpanReal i`
  (the real span of the relative roots is `(𝔞_0^G)^*`, and `𝔞_P^*` embeds in
  `𝔞_0^*` by restriction); likewise `λ' ∈ i(𝔞_{P'}^G)^*`;
- `λ ∈ DE ∩ DM` and `λ' ∈ DE' ∩ DM'`,

there are `δ > 0`, `B` and `t₀` such that for every `τ : Fin r → ℝ` with `τ ≥ 0`
and `t₀ ≤ ∑ j, τ j`, with `T = ∑ j, τ j • gen j`, and for every `(x, x') ∈ C`:

- `q ↦ Λ^T E(λ)(q.out)` and `q ↦ Λ^T E'(λ')(q.out)` are in `L²` of
  `G(F) \ G(𝔸)^1` (`MemLp … 2 μQ`), where `Λ^T` is
  `normOneTruncation hG hi D hK₀ hρ T` applied to the restriction of `E λ` to
  `G(𝔸)^1`;
- if `∏_{α ∈ Δ_Q} ⟨wλ + w'λ̄', α^∨⟩ ≠ 0` for every `S` and every `t : Ω S`,
  `t' : Ω' S`, then
  `‖∫ Λ^T E(λ) · conj(Λ^T E'(λ')) dμQ − mainTerm T λ λ'‖ ≤ B · exp(−δ ∑_j τ_j)`.

The main term is, with `Λ = wλ + w'λ̄' ∈ X^*(Q) ⊗ ℂ`
(`weylParameter (leviToParabolic J) (cw S t) (π S) λ + weylParameter
(leviToParabolic J') (cw' S t') (π S) (parameterConj F P' λ')`):

`mainTerm T λ λ' = ∑_S ∑_{t, t'} corootCovolume S · exp ⟨Λ, T_Q⟩ ·
(∑_{a, b} coeff S t a λ · conj(coeff' S t' b λ') · (ψ S t a, ψ' S t' b)_Q) /
∏_{α ∈ Δ_0 ∖ S} ⟨Λ, α^∨_Q⟩`.

Here `⟨Λ, T_Q⟩ = complexParameterPairing F Q Λ (toCharacterDual F i ν S.roots T)`
is the pairing of `Λ` with the image `T_Q` of `T` in `𝔞_Q = Hom(X^*(Q), ℝ)`;
since `Λ ∈ 𝔞_{Q,ℂ}^*` vanishes on `𝔞_0^Q`, this is Lapid's `⟨wλ + w'λ̄', T⟩`.
`α^∨_Q = toCharacterDual F i ν S.roots (corootVector D α)` is the image in `𝔞_Q`
of the coroot of `α ∈ Δ_0 ∖ Δ_0^Q`, which is Arthur's coroot of the restriction
of `α` to `𝔞_Q` (Arthur 2005, p.26), and `Δ_0 ∖ Δ_0^Q` indexes `Δ_Q`.
`(ψ, ψ')_Q = inducedInnerProduct K (hK S).isCompact … (μM S) (e S) (β S) ψ ψ'`,
and `∑_{a,b} coeff · conj(coeff') · (ψ, ψ')_Q` is
`(M(w, λ)v, M(w', λ')v')_Q` computed through the expansions. The conjugate
`λ̄'` comes from the second, conjugated entry of the inner products: the
exponents `w'λ'` of the constant terms of `E'(λ')` enter conjugated. The second
Eisenstein series and the operators `M(w', λ')` are evaluated at `λ'` itself, as
in (2). Since `λ'` is imaginary, `λ̄' = −λ'` (`parameterConj_imaginaryParameter`)
and `Λ = wλ − w'λ'`.

**Cone and size of `T`.** Lapid's cone is generated by `dim 𝔞_0^G` elements of the
positive chamber; here any finite number of generators in the open chamber is
allowed, and the cone may have components in `𝔞_G`. Suppose `Δ_0 ≠ ∅`. For any
norm on `𝔞_0` and with `T^G` the projection of `T` to `𝔞_0^G`, there are
`c, c' > 0` with
`c ∑_j τ_j ≤ ‖T^G‖` and `‖T^G‖, ‖T‖ ≤ c' ∑_j τ_j` on such a cone (a simple root
`α` satisfies `α(T) = α(T^G) ≥ min_j α(gen j) · ∑_j τ_j`), so the threshold and the bound in
`∑_j τ_j` are Lapid's `‖T‖ ≫ 0` and `O(e^{−δ‖T‖})`. Both sides depend only on
`T^G`: the truncation through `τ̂_P`, and the main term because `Λ` vanishes on
`𝔞_G`. A finitely generated cone in the open chamber of `𝔞_0` projects into a
cone of `𝔞_0^G` contained in one generated by `dim 𝔞_0^G` elements of the open
chamber, so the conclusion is Lapid's for each such cone.

If `Δ_0 = ∅` (`G` has no proper standard parabolic subgroup), the condition on
the `gen j` is empty, every `T` has `T^G = 0`, and `∑_j τ_j` is not comparable
with `‖T^G‖` (a generator `0` is allowed). In this case `P = P' = Q = G` and (2)
has no error term: the truncation has the single term for `P_0 = G`, with
`τ̂_G = 1`, so `Λ^T E(λ) = E(λ)`; `i(𝔞_G^G)^* = 0`, `Ω(G, G)` consists of the
identity, `M(1, 0)v = v`, and the main term is `vol(𝔞_G^G / L_G) (v, v')_G`, the
inner product of `v` and `v'` on `G(F) \ G(𝔸)^1` for the normalizations below. The
asserted bound then holds for every `δ`.

**Order of quantifiers.** `K` is chosen first, before the reflection data and all
the other data. `δ`, `B` and `t₀` depend on the cone and on `C`; Lapid's
`δ` depends only on the cone, and his constant is uniform on compact sets. The
square integrability of the truncations, implicit in Lapid's left side, is part
of the conclusion.

**Singular hyperplanes.** The bound is asserted for `(λ, λ')` off the hyperplanes
`⟨wλ + w'λ̄', α^∨⟩ = 0`, where `mainTerm` is the finite sum above. The test is
the product `denom` built from the same `Λ` as the main term. Lapid states
(2) for all `(λ, λ') ∈ i(𝔞_P^G)^* × i(𝔞_{P'}^G)^*`: by §7, p.325 and footnote 2,
`M(λ, λ', T)` extends to a smooth function across these hyperplanes (the
argument of Arthur 1982 uses only the holomorphy of the intertwining operators
on the imaginary axis). The statement does not define this extension. Since the
hyperplanes are a finite union of proper real hyperplanes of
`i(𝔞_P^G)^* × i(𝔞_{P'}^G)^*` (there `wλ + w'λ̄' = wλ − w'λ'`, and each
`⟨wλ − w'λ', α^∨⟩` is a nonzero linear form, because `w` maps `(𝔞_P^G)^*` onto
`(𝔞_Q^G)^*` and `α^∨_Q ≠ 0` lies in `𝔞_Q^G`), they have measure zero, which is
enough where the statement is integrated over `(λ, λ')`, as in Lapid's §8.

## Normalizations

Every measure in (2) is fixed:

- **`G(𝔸)`:** Mathlib's Haar measure `Measure.haar`.
- **`M_Q(𝔸)`:** `μM S` is a Haar measure with
  `IsIwasawaCompatibleMeasure K (hK S).isCompact … (μM S)`:
  `∫_G f = ∫_K ∫_{M_Q} ∫_{N_Q} δ_Q(m)⁻¹ f(umk) du dμM(m) dk`, with
  `vol(N_Q(F) \ N_Q(𝔸)) = 1` and `vol(K) = 1`.
- **`𝔞_Q`:** Lebesgue measure in the coordinates `parameterCoordinates F Q (e S)`
  dual to a basis `e S : ℝ^{n S} ≃ X^*(Q) ⊗ ℝ`. The weights `β S` are continuous,
  compactly supported, of integral `1`; then `inducedInnerProduct … (e S) (β S)`
  is Lapid's `(φ, ψ)_Q = ∫_{A_Q N_Q(𝔸) M_Q(F) \ G(𝔸)} φ ψ̄` for the quotient
  measure `dg / da` with `da` the measure on `A_Q ≅ 𝔞_Q`.
- **`𝔞_G`:** `c : Fin m → V T` is a basis of `𝔞_G = relativeCentralDirections i`
  (the common kernel of the relative roots in `𝔞_0`); the measure on `𝔞_G` is
  Lebesgue measure in the coordinates `y ↦ ∑_k y_k c_k`.
- **`G(𝔸)^1` and `G(F) \ G(𝔸)^1`:** `μ₁` is a Haar measure on
  `normOneSubgroup F G` with `dg = dμ₁ · d𝔞_G`: for a map
  `s : ℝ^m → G(𝔸)` with `H_G(s(y)) = ∑_k y_k c_k`, that is
  `characterLog F G (s y) χ = ⟨χ|_T, ∑_k y_k c_k⟩` for every rational character
  `χ` of `G`,
  `∫_{G(𝔸)} f = ∫_{ℝ^m} ∫_{G(𝔸)^1} f(x · s(y)) dμ₁(x) dy` for every compactly
  supported continuous `f`. Since `G(𝔸)^1` is the kernel of `H_G`, `x · s(y)`
  runs once through `G(𝔸)` and the inner integral does not depend on the choice
  of `s(y)` in its coset. The inner product on `G(F) \ G(𝔸)^1` uses the quotient
  measure `μQ = LeftCosetSpace.discreteQuotientMeasure (rationalNormOneSubgroup F G) … μ₁`.
  For `μ₁ = Measure.haar` this is `normOneRationalQuotientHaarMeasure F G`; the
  normalization of `μ₁` is left free because Mathlib's Haar measures on `G(𝔸)`
  and on `G(𝔸)^1` are normalized independently.
- **`𝔞_Q^G` and `L_Q`:** `L_Q` is the lattice in `𝔞_Q^G` spanned by the coroots
  `α^∨`, `α ∈ Δ_Q` (Arthur 1980, p.115; Arthur 2005, p.94 writes `Z(Δ_P^∨)`).
  The measure on `𝔞_Q^G` is the one with `d𝔞_Q = d𝔞_Q^G · d𝔞_G` for the
  decomposition `𝔞_Q = 𝔞_Q^G ⊕ 𝔞_G`, which is Arthur's normalization
  (Arthur 1980, p.115: `a ↦ H_Q(ax)` measure preserving from
  `A_Q(ℝ)^0 ∩ G(𝔸)^1` onto `𝔞_Q^G`, with `dg = dg^1 da_G`). For this measure,
  `vol(𝔞_Q^G / L_Q)` is the `d𝔞_Q`-volume of the parallelepiped spanned by the
  `α^∨_Q`, `α ∈ Δ_0 ∖ Δ_0^Q`, and the images in `𝔞_Q` of the `c k`, because the
  parallelepiped of the `c k` has `d𝔞_G`-volume `1`. `corootCovolume` is this
  volume, computed with Mathlib's `parallelepiped` and `volume` in the
  coordinates of `e S`. When `Q = G` it is the ratio of the two measures on
  `𝔞_G`, which is `1` when `e S` is compatible with `c`.
- **Pairings:** `⟨Λ, T_Q⟩` and `⟨Λ, α^∨_Q⟩` as above; `⟨Re λ, α^∨⟩` in
  `IsStandardRegularParameter` through `restrictToTorus` and `pair T`.

The choices left free are consistent with (2): replacing `e S` by `e S ∘ (κ •)`
(`κ > 0`) multiplies the coordinates of `𝔞_Q` by `κ`, divides `(·,·)_Q` by
`κ^{n S}` and multiplies `corootCovolume S` by `κ^{n S}`; replacing `c` by `κ • c`
multiplies `μ₁` (through the relation with `Measure.haar`), hence the left side,
and every `corootCovolume S` by `κ^m`.

`Langlands.GMFamily.theta` is not used: it is defined in the abstract Euclidean
model of `Langlands.LinearAlgebra.GMFamilies`, with the volume of an inner
product, whereas the measure on `𝔞_Q^G` here is fixed by the Iwasawa
decomposition and the measure on `𝔞_G`. With `θ_Q` taken for the measure above,
`corootCovolume S / ∏_{α} ⟨Λ, α^∨_Q⟩ = θ_Q(Λ)⁻¹` (Arthur 2005, p.94).

## The compact subgroup `K`

Lapid (§2, p.311) fixes one maximal compact subgroup `K` of `G(𝔸)` in good
position with respect to `M_0`. This is Arthur's notion of `K` admissible relative
to `M_0` (Arthur 1981, §1, pp.9–10): at an archimedean place the Lie algebras of
`K_v` and `A_{M_0}` are orthogonal for the Killing form; at a finite place the
vertex of `K_v` in the Bruhat–Tits building is special and lies in the apartment
of a maximal split torus of `M_0`; and `K_v` is the standard integral subgroup
for almost all `v`. As recorded in the Markdown of `ArthurTruncationStatement`,
this condition cannot be stated with the Library's present objects. The
properties of `K` that the Library can state (maximality, `Q(𝔸)K = G(𝔸)`, the
positive-torus commutation) do not characterize good position: for `G = PGL₂`
over `ℚ`, the full stabilizer at one prime of an edge of the tree (the normalizer
of an Iwahori subgroup), with the standard compact subgroups at the other places,
has these properties but is not the stabilizer of a special vertex (see the
Markdown of `AdelicTemperedVanishingReduction`).

The proposition therefore has the form that `TruncationProjectionStatement`
uses for Arthur's Proposition 13.1(a),(b): it asserts that **some** maximal
compact `K` with these properties satisfies the conclusion for all the remaining
data, which are all built with this `K`. Lapid's Proposition 1 for an admissible
`K` gives such a `K`: an admissible `K` is maximal compact (each `K_v` is maximal
compact, and a compact subgroup of `G(𝔸)` containing `K` projects into each
`K_v`); it satisfies `Q(𝔸)K = G(𝔸)` for every standard `Q` (Arthur 1981,
property (i)); and it satisfies the positive-torus commutation, because the
positive torus lies in the archimedean factor, where
`K_v ∩ P_0(F_v) = (K_v ∩ N_0(F_v))(K_v ∩ M_0(F_v))` (property (iii)),
`K_v ∩ N_0(F_v) = 1`, and `M_0` centralizes `A_0` (see the Markdown of
`ArthurTruncationStatement`). The existential form is weaker than Lapid's result,
which holds for every admissible `K`, and it asserts nothing for the other
compact subgroups with the listed properties.

## Scope

The statement is a proposition; the module does not prove it. It does not claim:

- the holomorphy of the Eisenstein series on all of `i𝔞_P^*` (Lapid's
  Proposition 6), nor that `DE` contains `i(𝔞_P^G)^*`: only compact sets inside
  the domains of holomorphy are considered;
- Theorem 1 (Arthur's formula for all `(λ, λ')`, which follows from
  Proposition 1 once that holomorphy is known);
- the case of smooth families `φ(λ)` whose Eisenstein series are smooth only
  because poles cancel (see "Smooth families");
- the value of (2) on the singular hyperplanes, or the smooth extension of
  `M(T; ·, ·)` across them;
- (2) for every admissible `K`, or for every maximal compact `K` with the listed
  properties: only for one such `K`;
- any statement for non-standard parabolic subgroups `P`, `P'`, or for a
  `ParabolicRootPresentation`; the regularity condition for the continuations
  and expansions is `IsStandardRegularParameter`, a translate of the positive
  chamber of `𝔞_P^*`.
