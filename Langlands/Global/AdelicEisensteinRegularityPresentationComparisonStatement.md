# Comparison of regularity for two root presentations of one parabolic

Module `Langlands.Global.AdelicEisensteinRegularityPresentationComparisonStatement` states
`Langlands.Global.RootPresentationRegularityComparisonStatement` as a proposition. It supplies
no proof.

## Statement

Let `F` be a number field and `G` a connected reductive group over `F`
(`IsConnectedReductive G`, with `IsAffineHom G.X.hom` and `LocallyOfFiniteType G.X.hom`). Let

- `p₁ : P₁ → G` with Levi `l₁ : M₁ → P₁`, split component `a₁ : A₁ → M₁` and root presentation
  `r₁ : ParabolicRootPresentation p₁ l₁ a₁`;
- `p₂ : P₂ → G` with Levi `l₂ : M₂ → P₂`, split component `a₂ : A₂ → M₂` and root presentation
  `r₂ : ParabolicRootPresentation p₂ l₂ a₂`;
- `e : P₂ → P₁` with `e ≫ p₁ = p₂`, such that `p₁` and `p₂` have the same image in `G` and
  `l₁ ≫ p₁` and `l₂ ≫ p₂` have the same image (`SubgroupFunctor.range`).

The two presentations may use different maximal split tori `r₁.data.T`, `r₂.data.T`, different
generic cocharacters and different index sets. The instance hypotheses (monomorphic torus
inclusions, finite relative Weyl groups, monomorphic split-component maps) and the split-torus
witnesses `hA₁`, `hT₁`, `hA₂`, `hT₂` are those required by
`ParabolicRootPresentation.IsRegularParameter` (module
`Langlands.Global.AdelicEisensteinConvergenceStatement`).

The conclusion: for every real `R` there is a real `R'` such that for every complex parameter
`λ ∈ X^*(P₁) ⊗ ℂ`, if `parameterRestrict e λ` is `R'`-regular for `r₂`, then `λ` is
`R`-regular for `r₁`.

Recall that `λ` is `R`-regular for a presentation when `R < B(Re λ|_A, α)` for every root `α` of
the split component `A` in the unipotent radical, where `B` is the form on `X^*(A) ⊗ ℝ` induced
from the relative Weyl invariant form of the presentation's maximal split torus.

## Source and provenance

Provenance: derived. J. Bernstein and E. Lapid, *On the meromorphic continuation of Eisenstein
series*, J. Amer. Math. Soc. 37 (2024), §2.2, p. 191, registered as
`bernstein-lapid-2024-meromorphic-continuation-eisenstein`, measure regularity by
`⟨Re λ, α^∨⟩ ≫ 0` for the simple roots `α` of `P`. That condition depends only on the
parabolic. The Library's predicate `IsRegularParameter` instead uses a positive definite form
constructed from the chosen maximal split torus. The proposition says that the two notions of
"sufficiently regular" agree up to a change of threshold for any two presentations of one
parabolic and one Levi.

## Why it holds

This explains the truth of the statement; the module contains no proof.

- By `ParabolicRootPresentation.isRegularParameter_iff_ambientBounds` (module
  `Langlands.Global.AdelicEisensteinRegularityParabolicTransport`), `R`-regularity for a
  presentation with torus `T` is the set of inequalities `R < B_T(λ_T, β)` for the positive roots
  `β` of `T` not in the monoid generated by the Levi simple roots, that is, for the roots of `T`
  in the unipotent radical. Here `λ_T` is the restriction of `Re λ` to `T` through the Levi and
  `B_T` is the relative Weyl invariant form of `T` (`relativeWeylInvariantForm`).
- `λ_T` comes from a character of the Levi, so it is fixed by the relative Weyl group of the
  Levi and orthogonal to the roots of the Levi. For a root `β` with reflection `s_β` in the
  relative Weyl group, invariance of `B_T` under `s_β` gives
  `B_T(λ_T, β) = ⟨λ_T, β^∨⟩ · B_T(β, β) / 2`, with `B_T(β, β) > 0`.
- The two tori are maximal split tori of the common Levi, hence conjugate by a rational point
  `m` of the Levi. Conjugation by `m` carries the roots of one torus in the unipotent radical to
  those of the other and preserves the coroot pairings `⟨λ_T, β^∨⟩`, since `λ` is a character.
- The roots involved are finitely many and the factors `B_T(β, β) / 2` are positive, so a large
  enough `R'` for the second presentation forces the bound `R` for the first.
