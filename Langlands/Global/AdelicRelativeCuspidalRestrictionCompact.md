# Compact restriction of a relatively cuspidal family to a boundary region

`AdelicRelativeCuspidalRestrictionCompactStatement` uses a compatible standard parabolic family over a number field, a fixed open right finite level, and a positive-rank representation with adelic height \(H\). Let \(E\) be a complex Hilbert space and let evaluation at each ambient adelic point \(x\) be a continuous linear functional \(e_x\) on \(E\). For each \(q\in E\), assume that \(x\mapsto e_x(q)\) is continuous, smooth on archimedean slices, and relatively cuspidal along \(P\). It is left invariant by \(P_0(F)\) and \(U_P(\mathbb A)\) and fixed by the given right level. Each archimedean right-orbit derivative word is bounded by a word-specific constant times \(H(x)^R\|q\|\) with a common \(R\ge0\).

Let \((X,\mu)\) have supplied adelic representatives \(x_z\), with measurable scalar evaluations in the almost-everywhere strong sense. Assume almost every \(x_z\) can be written with compact left minimal-parabolic and right factors, and with a positive torus factor in a weak simple-root chamber. The log norms of restricted roots outside the Levi simple-root set of \(P\), and of auxiliary characters spanning with the restricted simple roots, are bounded uniformly. The finite coordinates lie in a fixed compact set. Suppose \(z\mapsto H(x_z)^{-m}\) belongs to \(L^2(\mu)\) for some integer \(m\ge0\). Finite \(\mu\) permits \(m=0\).

The theorem constructs a continuous linear map \(E\to L^2(\mu)\) that takes \(q\) to the almost-everywhere class of \(z\mapsto e_{x_z}(q)\), and proves that it is Hilbert–Schmidt and compact. The relative rapid-decay bound gives an operator norm bound \(C H(x_z)^{-m}\) on the evaluation functional. This puts each evaluated function in \(L^2\) and bounds the norm of the map. The squared bound is integrable, so the Hilbert–Schmidt evaluation criterion applies. Compactness follows from Hilbert–Schmidt compactness.

This is the boundary restriction argument of Bernstein–Lapid, §7.4, pp. 216–217, for the specified boundary representatives and measure.
