# Kummer theory for one radical of prime degree

Author: Worker 226.

This module collects the facts about one radical extension `L = K(β)`, `β ^ p = a ∈ K`, that
the Kummer case of the prime-degree second inequality needs (Artin–Tate, Chapter VI §2,
Theorems 3 and 4, and the local norm facts used in the proof of Lemma 2 of §3). The results
are algebraic or local: they concern the Kummer group of one radical, the splitting and
ramification of places, and which local elements are norms. Nothing here is global class
field theory. Everything is in namespace `Langlands.Global.Arithmetic.KummerRadical`.

Throughout, `p` is a natural number, usually a prime (`[Fact p.Prime]`), and
`ζ ∈ K` is a primitive `p`-th root of unity (`IsPrimitiveRoot ζ p`).

## The Kummer group of one radical

These statements hold for arbitrary fields `K ⊆ L` (an `Algebra K L`).

- `exists_apply_eq_pow_mul`: if `x ∈ L`, `x ≠ 0`, `x ^ p ∈ K` and `σ ∈ Aut(L/K)`, then
  `σ x = ζ ^ j · x` for some `j < p`. Indeed `(σ x / x) ^ p = 1`, and every `p`-th root of
  unity in `L` is a power of `ζ` (`IsPrimitiveRoot.eq_pow_of_pow_eq_one`). Here `p ≠ 0`.
- `exists_eq_algebraMap_mul_pow`: let `L/K` be finite Galois with `|Aut(L/K)| = p` prime, let
  `α ∈ L ∖ K` with `α ^ p ∈ K`, and let `γ ∈ L` with `γ ^ p ∈ K`. Then `γ = c · α ^ k` for
  some `c ∈ K` and `k : ℕ`. Proof: pick `σ` with `σ α ≠ α`. Then `σ α = ζ ^ j α` with
  `j ≢ 0 mod p`, and `σ γ = ζ ^ i γ`. Choose `k` with `j k ≡ i mod p`. Then
  `δ = γ / α ^ k` is fixed by `σ`. Since `p` is prime, `σ` generates `Aut(L/K)`
  (`zpowers_eq_top_of_prime_card`), so `δ` is fixed by the whole group and lies in `K`
  (`IsGalois.mem_range_algebraMap_iff_fixed`). For `γ = 0` take `c = 0`.
- `exists_eq_pow_mul_pow_of_eq_pow`: in the same situation, with `α ^ p = a ∈ K`, if
  `b ∈ K` is the `p`-th power of some `γ ∈ L`, then `b = a ^ k · c ^ p` for some `k : ℕ` and
  `c ∈ K`. Take the `p`-th power of `γ = c α ^ k`.

These are the case of a cyclic group of prime order of Artin–Tate's Theorem 3 and its
Corollary 3. The Kummer group `A = {γ : γ ^ p ∈ K}` of `K(a^{1/p})` is generated by `K^×` and
`a^{1/p}`. Equivalently, `K^× ∩ L^{×p} = a^ℕ K^{×p}`.

## Local splitting at finite places

From here on, `K` and `L` are number fields. `v` is a finite place of `K` and `w` a finite
place of `L` with `w ∣ v` (`[w.asIdeal.LiesOver v.asIdeal]`). The completions are
`v.adicCompletion K` and `w.adicCompletion L`. The hypothesis `Algebra.adjoin K {β} = ⊤`
says `L = K(β)`, and `β ^ p = a`.

- `finrank_adicCompletion_eq_one`: if `a = c ^ p` for some `c ∈ K_v`, then
  `[L_w : K_v] = 1`. Here `p ≠ 0`. In `L_w`, the images of `β` and `c` have the same
  `p`-th power. So if `c ≠ 0`, then `β = ζ ^ i c` with `i ∈ ℕ`, and the image of `β` lies in
  the `K_v`-subalgebra `⊥`; if `c = 0`, then `β = 0`. By induction on `Algebra.adjoin`, all of
  `L` maps into `⊥`. The Library's `subalgebra_eq_top_of_algebraMap_mem` (module
  `Langlands.Global.AdelicFunctoriality.LocalDegree`: `L` is dense in `L_w`) then gives
  `⊥ = ⊤`.
- `exists_pow_eq_of_finrank_eq_one`: conversely, if `[L_w : K_v] = 1`, then `a` is a `p`-th
  power in `K_v`, namely of the element of `K_v` corresponding to the image of `β`. This
  direction needs neither `ζ` nor `L = K(β)`.

Together: `v` splits completely in `K(a^{1/p})` iff `a ∈ K_v^{×p}` (Artin–Tate, Theorem 4.1,
at finite places).

## Radicals of units away from `p`

`O_v` is `v.adicCompletionIntegers K`, and `residue` is the residue map of a local ring.

- `exists_pow_eq_of_residue_pow_eq` (Hensel's lemma for `p`-th roots): if `p` is a unit of
  `O_v`, `a ∈ O_v` is a unit, and the residue of `a` is the `p`-th power of the residue of
  some `b ∈ O_v`, then `a = c ^ p` for some `c ∈ O_v`. `O_v` is adically complete (Library
  `Langlands.ClassField.Global.isAdicComplete_adicCompletionIntegers`), hence Henselian
  (Mathlib's `IsAdicComplete.henselianRing`). Apply `HenselianRing.is_henselian` to
  `X ^ p - a` and `b`: the derivative `p b ^ (p - 1)` is a unit.
- `ramificationIdx_eq_one_of_valued_eq_one`: let `L/K` be Galois of degree `p` prime, with
  `L = K(β)`, `β ^ p = a`. If `p` is a unit of `O_v` and `a` has valuation one in `K_v`, then
  the ramification index of `w` over `v` is `1`. Proof: `e · f` divides `p`
  (`localDegree_dvd` and `localDegree_eq` of the LocalDegree module). If `a` is a `p`-th
  power in `K_v`, then `e · f = [L_w : K_v] = 1` by `finrank_adicCompletion_eq_one`.
  Otherwise, by the previous lemma the residue `ā` is not a `p`-th power in the residue field
  `κ_v`, so `X ^ p - ā` is irreducible over `κ_v` (Mathlib's
  `X_pow_sub_C_irreducible_of_prime`). The image of `β` is a unit of `O_w`, and its residue is
  a root of `X ^ p - ā`. Hence `f = [κ_w : κ_v] ≥ p` (`minpoly.eq_of_irreducible_of_monic`,
  `minpoly.natDegree_le`; the residue degree of the completions equals the inertia degree by
  the Library's `FiniteExtension.completionIntegers_inertiaDeg`). With `e · f ∣ p` this forces
  `e = 1`.
- `exists_localNorm_eq_of_valued_eq_one`: under the same hypotheses, every unit `u` of `K_v`
  of valuation one is `localNorm v w c` for a unit `c` of `L_w` of valuation one. This is the
  Library's `FiniteExtension.exists_localNorm_eq_of_ramificationIdx_eq_one` (module
  `Langlands.Global.AdelicFunctoriality.UnramifiedUnitNorm`) applied with the previous lemma.

This is the "if" direction of Artin–Tate, Theorem 4.2, for one radical, together with the
fact used in Lemma 2 of §3 that units at unramified places are local norms. The Lean proof of `e = 1` is internal. It uses the
residue-field extension where Artin–Tate use the discriminant of `X ^ p - a`.

## Local norms at finite places

`localNorm v w : L_w →* K_v` is the Library's local norm
(`Langlands.Global.AdelicFunctoriality.FiniteExtension.localNorm`). On `K_v` it is the
`[L_w : K_v]`-th power (`localNorm_algebraMap`).

- `exists_localNorm_eq_of_finrank_eq_one`: if `[L_w : K_v] = 1`, then every unit of `K_v`
  is a local norm of a unit of `L_w`, namely of its own image.
- `exists_localNorm_eq_pow`: if `L/K` is Galois of degree `p`, then the `p`-th power of every
  unit `u` of `K_v` is a local norm. The local degree `d` divides `p` (`localDegree_dvd`),
  say `p = d m`. Then `u ^ p` is the norm of the image of `u ^ m`.

## Infinite places

`v` is an infinite place of `K` and `w` an infinite place of `L` with `w ∣ v`
(`[w.LiesOver v]`). The completions are `v.Completion` and `w.Completion`.

- `dvd_two_of_isReal`: if `v` is real and `p ≠ 0`, then `p ∣ 2`. The image of `ζ` under the
  real embedding is a primitive `p`-th root of unity in `ℝ` of absolute value `1`, so its
  square is `1`.
- `eq_two_of_isReal`: if moreover `p` is prime, then `p = 2`.
- `isReal_of_sq_eq`: let `L = K(β)`, `β ^ 2 = a`. If `v` is real and `a` is a square in
  `K_v`, then `w` is real. On `K` the embedding of `w` agrees with the real embedding of `v`,
  possibly after complex conjugation, so it agrees with it in either case. `a = c ^ 2` with
  `c ∈ K_v ≅ ℝ` gives `β ↦ ±c`, a real number. So the embedding of `w` commutes with complex
  conjugation on `K` and on `β`, hence on `L = K(β)` (induction on `Algebra.adjoin`).
- `isUnramified_of_pow_eq`: let `p` be prime and `L = K(β)`, `β ^ p = a`. If `a` is a `p`-th
  power in `K_v`, then `w` is unramified over `K`. A complex `v` is always unramified. For a
  real `v`, `p = 2`, and `w` is real by the previous lemma.

  This is the "if" direction of Artin–Tate, Theorem 4.1, at infinite places.
- `exists_infinite_localNorm_eq_of_isUnramified`: if `w` is unramified over `K`, then every unit
  of `K_v` is a local norm (Library
  `Langlands.Global.AdelicFunctoriality.InfiniteExtension.localNorm`). The local degree is
  `1` (`IsUnramified.finrank_eq_one`).
- `exists_infinite_localNorm_eq_pow`: if `K` contains a primitive `p`-th root of unity for a
  prime `p`, then the `p`-th power of every unit of `K_v`
  is a local norm. If `w` is unramified, use the previous lemma. If `w` is ramified, `v` is real
  (`IsRamified.liesOver_isReal_under`), so `p = 2`, the local degree is `2`
  (`IsRamified.finrank_eq_two`), and `u ^ 2` is the norm of the image of `u`.

## The radical extension `K(x^{1/p})`

Here `K` is any field, `p : ℕ` and `x ∈ K`.

- `radicalExtension K p x` is `AdjoinRoot (X ^ p - C x)`, the ring `K[X] / (X ^ p - x)`. It
  is a field when `X ^ p - x` is irreducible; Lean records that as the instance argument
  `[Fact (Irreducible (X ^ p - C x))]` of the results below.
- `radicalRoot K p x` is the class of `X` (`AdjoinRoot.root`).
- `radicalRoot_pow`: `radicalRoot K p x ^ p = x`.
- `adjoin_radicalRoot`: `K[radicalRoot K p x]` is the whole ring (`AdjoinRoot.adjoinRoot_eq_top`).
- `irreducible_of_forall_pow_ne`: for `p` prime, `X ^ p - x` is irreducible if `x` is not a
  `p`-th power in `K` (Mathlib's `X_pow_sub_C_irreducible_of_prime`).

Assume now that `X ^ p - C x` is irreducible.

- `finrank_radicalExtension`: `[K(x^{1/p}) : K] = p`, the degree of `X ^ p - x`
  (`AdjoinRoot.powerBasis`). There is also an instance `Module.Finite K (radicalExtension K p x)`.
- `isGalois_radicalExtension`: if `K` contains a primitive `p`-th root of unity `ζ`, then
  `K(x^{1/p})/K` is Galois: it is a splitting field of `X ^ p - x`
  (`isSplittingField_AdjoinRoot_X_pow_sub_C` and `isGalois_of_isSplittingField_X_pow_sub_C`
  in Mathlib's `Mathlib.FieldTheory.KummerExtension`).
- `numberField_radicalExtension`: if `K` is a number field, so is `K(x^{1/p})`
  (`NumberField.of_module_finite`). This is a theorem, not an instance.

## Provenance

The statements follow Artin–Tate, *Class Field Theory*, Chapter VI §2, Theorem 3 with
Corollary 3 (printed p. 22) and Theorem 4 (printed p. 23), specialised to one radical of prime
degree `p`, together with the local norm facts used in the proof of Lemma 2 of §3 (printed
p. 25): `p`-th powers are local norms, every element is a norm at a split place, and units are
norms at an unramified place. The Lean proofs are internal. Two of them differ from the book:
the proof that units have unramified radicals goes through the residue-field extension, and
the real-place analysis is done by hand. The construction `radicalExtension` is Mathlib's
`AdjoinRoot`.
