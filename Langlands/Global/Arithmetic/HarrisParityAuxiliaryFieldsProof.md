# Split quadratic CM auxiliaries for Harris's parity argument

`Langlands.Global.Harris.harrisParityAuxiliaryFields` proves
`Langlands.Global.Harris.HarrisParityAuxiliaryFieldsStatement`. Clozel doubles
the totally real degree in the paragraph preceding Corollary 5.4.2 (printed
p. 132). The simultaneous splitting argument comes from the example in
Sørensen, §1 (author manuscript, pp. 1–2). The three-field cover is a derived
input for the finite parity reduction. Sources are `clozel-1991-selfdual-galois`
and `sorensen-patching-lemma`. [Sørensen's manuscript](https://mathweb.ucsd.edu/~csorense/patch.pdf)

## Choosing a radicand

For a prescribed finite set `V`, let `m` be four times the product of its
rational residue characteristics. Dirichlet's theorem gives infinitely many
positive rational primes `q` congruent to `1` modulo `m`. The proof discards
the finitely many rational primes that ramify in the field `M` to be avoided.
At a place of `M` above a remaining prime `q`, the normalized additive
valuation of `q` is one. A square would have even valuation. For every residue
characteristic `p` of a place in `V`, divisibility of `1 - q` by `4p` gives the
strict Hensel bound for `X² - q` at `1` in the `p`-adic integers. The resulting
square root maps into the actual completion of `E` at the prescribed place.

## Constructing the quadratic CM field

The proof takes a square root `x` of the chosen radicand in `AlgebraicClosure E`
and forms `N = E(x)`. The quadratic degree forces `N ∩ M` to be either `E`
or `N`. If `N ∩ M` were `N`, then `M` would contain `x`, contradicting
the nonsquareness. At each place of `N` above a prescribed place, the local
square root in the base completion gives completion degree one by the Library's
`KummerRadical.finrank_adicCompletion_eq_one`.

For `N₀`, the proof joins the image of `E₀` to the rational root field inside
`N`. Both lie in the maximal totally real subfield of `N`. The nonsquareness
of the radicand in `E` gives nonsquareness in the image of `E₀`, so
`squareRoot_sup_finrank` gives `[N₀ : ℚ] = 2 [E₀ : ℚ]`. The compositum of `N₀`
and the image of `K₀` contains the image of `E = E₀ K₀` and the root `x`, so it
is `N`. The bound `[E : ℚ] ≤ 2 [E₀ : ℚ]` and the tower degree formulas give
`[N : N₀] ≤ 2`. Total complexity of `N`, inherited from `E`, excludes
degree one. Thus `N` is a totally complex quadratic extension of `N₀` and is CM.

## Covering the unramified places

The proof chooses `d₁` nonsquare in `E` and then `d₂` nonsquare in
`N₁ = E(x₁)`, where `x₁² = d₁` and `x₂² = d₂`. The product `d₁d₂` is
nonsquare in `E`, since in `N₁` dividing a square witness by the square
`d₁` would make `d₂` square. The fields generated by `x₁`, `x₂` and `x₁x₂`
are pairwise distinct by division of the generators and equality of quadratic
degrees. The product radicand is positive and locally square at `V`, so the
same construction applies to all three fields.

The exceptional set consists of places ramified in `N₁N₂`. At a place outside
that set, choose an arithmetic Frobenius at an upper place of the compositum.
It sends each root `x₁` and `x₂` to itself or its negative. If both signs are
negative it fixes their product, so it fixes at least one of the three fields
pointwise. In the residue field of that quadratic field, the power map by the
size of the base residue field is the identity. Its order is the residue
degree, which is therefore one. The ramification index divides the upper
ramification index and is also one. Galois invariance gives these indices
at every place of the quadratic field over the base place. Their product is
the actual completion degree.
