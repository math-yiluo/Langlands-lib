# Restriction of norm-trivial characters at the bottom prime step of a cyclic tower

`Langlands.Global.HeckeCharacter.cyclicPrimeNormCharacterRestriction` proves
`CyclicPrimeNormCharacterRestrictionStatement.{uF, uK, uE}` with the three
field universes independent. The statement is in
`Langlands/Global/Arithmetic/CyclicPrimeNormCharacterRestrictionStatement.lean`.

## Setting and notation

Let `F ⊂ K ⊂ E` be number fields with `IsScalarTower F K E`, each of
`K/F`, `E/K` and `E/F` Galois with cyclic Galois group, and
`p = [K : F]` prime. Write `n = [E : F]` and `m = [E : K]`; Mathlib's
`Module.finrank_mul_finrank` gives `n = p * m`. Write `N_{L/M}` for the
actual idele-class norm `ideleClassNorm (K := M) (L := L)` and
`ι : C_F → C_K` for `ideleClassInclusion (K := F) (L := K)`.
Let `χ` be a Hecke character of `K` trivial on `N_{E/K}(C_E)`.

The proof uses these results of the Library:

- `cyclicNormKernelCharacter` (module `CyclicNormKernelCharacterProof`),
  applied to `E/F`, `E/K` and `K/F`. It gives characters `η` of `C_F`,
  `θ` of `C_K` and `ρ` of `C_F` whose kernels are exactly the norm images
  `N_{E/F}(C_E)`, `N_{E/K}(C_E)` and `N_{K/F}(C_K)`. It also gives
  `orderOf η = n` and `orderOf θ = m`, and it says that the characters
  trivial on `N_{E/K}(C_E)` are exactly the powers `θ^i` with `i < m`,
  and those trivial on `N_{K/F}(C_K)` are exactly the `ρ^i` with `i < p`.
- `ideleClassNormTower`: `N_{E/F} = N_{K/F} ∘ N_{E/K}`.
- `ideleClassNormInclusionPower`: `N_{K/F}(ι x) = x^p`.
- `ideleClassNorm_equiv` (module `IdeleClassNormTransport`): the norm
  commutes with a compatible pair of field isomorphisms.
- `IsTrivialOnNorm.pow_finrank_eq_one`: a character trivial on norms from
  an extension of degree `d` has trivial `d`-th power.

## Step 1: the pullback of `η` along the norm has order `m`

Put `ψ = η ∘ N_{K/F}`, a Hecke character of `K`. By the norm tower,
`ψ(N_{E/K} y) = η(N_{E/F} y) = 1`, so `ψ` is trivial on
`N_{E/K}(C_E)` and `ψ^m = 1`.

Conversely suppose `ψ^d = 1`. For `x ∈ C_F` the norm of the inclusion gives
`ψ(ι x) = η(x^p) = η(x)^p`, so `η(x)^{p d} = 1` for every `x`, that is,
`η^{p d} = 1`. Since `orderOf η = n = p * m`, `p * m` divides `p * d`, so
`m` divides `d`. Taking `d = orderOf ψ` shows `orderOf ψ = m`.

## Step 2: `χ` is a power of `ψ`

Both `ψ` and `χ` are trivial on `N_{E/K}(C_E)`, so both are powers of `θ`.
The cyclic subgroup generated by `ψ` lies in the one generated by `θ`.
Both have cardinality `m` (`Nat.card_zpowers`), so they are equal
(`Subgroup.eq_of_le_of_card_ge`). Hence `χ` lies in the subgroup generated
by `ψ`. Since `ψ` has finite order, `χ = ψ^k` for some natural number `k`,
that is, `χ(y) = η(N_{K/F} y)^k` for every `y ∈ C_K`.

This is the step the Markdown of the statement phrases as an identification
of the `E/K` norm quotient with the subgroup `N_{K/F}(C_K) / N_{E/F}(C_E)`
of the `E/F` norm quotient. The Lean proof does not form these quotient
groups: it compares orders of characters instead.

## Step 3: Galois invariance

Let `τ ∈ Gal(K/F)`. `HeckeCharacter.comap τ.toRingEquiv χ` sends `y` to
`χ(τ y)`, where `τ y` is `ideleClassEquiv τ.toRingEquiv y`. Applying
`ideleClassNorm_equiv` to the identity of `F` and `τ`, which are compatible
because `τ` fixes `F`, gives `N_{K/F}(τ y) = N_{K/F}(y)`. With Step 2,
`χ(τ y) = η(N_{K/F}(τ y))^k = η(N_{K/F} y)^k = χ(y)`. This proves the first
conjunct. The Lean proof uses this naturality of the norm in place of the
product of Galois conjugates (`ideleClassNormGaloisProduct`) mentioned in
the statement's Markdown.

## Step 4: the kernel of the restriction

Assume `χ^p = 1` and `χ ≠ 1`, and put `χ_F = χ ∘ ι`. By Step 2 and the norm
of the inclusion, `χ_F(x) = (η(x)^p)^k`.

- `χ_F` is trivial on `N_{K/F}(C_K)`: for `y ∈ C_K`,
  `χ_F(N_{K/F} y) = (η(N_{K/F} y)^k)^p = χ(y)^p = 1`.
- `χ_F ≠ 1`: otherwise `η^{p k} = 1`, so as in Step 1 `m` divides `k`.
  Since `orderOf ψ = m`, this gives `χ = ψ^k = 1`, a contradiction.

By the description of the characters trivial on `N_{K/F}(C_K)`,
`χ_F = ρ^i` for some `i < p`, and `i ≠ 0` because `χ_F ≠ 1`. As `p` is
prime, `gcd(i, p) = 1` (`Nat.coprime_of_lt_prime`). If `χ_F(x) = 1`, then
`ρ(x)^i = 1` and `ρ(x)^p = 1`, so `ρ(x) = ρ(x)^{gcd(i, p)} = 1` by
`pow_gcd_eq_one`. Since the kernel of `ρ` is exactly `N_{K/F}(C_K)`, `x` is
a norm. The converse inclusion is the first bullet. This is
`HasNormKernel K χ_F`, the second conjunct.

## Sources

The characters with kernel exactly a norm image are those of Arthur–Clozel,
*Simple Algebras, Base Change, and the Advanced Theory of the Trace
Formula* (1989), `arthur-clozel-1989-base-change`: a character of
`𝔸_F^×` "vanishing exactly on `F^× N(𝔸_E^×)`" appears in the proof of
Theorem III.3.1, printed p. 201, and before Theorem III.4.2, printed p. 202.

The statement is the character-theoretic input for applying
Clozel–Rajan, *Solvable base change*, arXiv:1806.02513v1,
`clozel-rajan-2018-solvable-base-change`, Theorem 1 (PDF p. 2), at a prime
step of the cyclic descent argument at the end of §2 (PDF p. 7). There the
top step `E/E'` has prime degree `p`. The authors write the character
attached to `E/E'` as `η ∘ N_{E'/F}` for a character `η` of order `d`
defining `E/F`, and its restriction to `C_F` as `η^{d/p}`. Steps 2 and 4
carry out the analogous computation with the prime step at the bottom:
`χ = (η ∘ N_{K/F})^k`, and the restriction `χ ∘ ι` is `η^{p k}`. The
bottom-step orientation is a derived application, as the statement's
Markdown records.
