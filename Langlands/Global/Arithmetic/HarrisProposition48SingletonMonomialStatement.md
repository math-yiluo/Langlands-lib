# The monomial Galois family in the singleton branch

`Proposition48SingletonMonomialRestrictionStatement` uses the rank-one algebraic realization and its finite-index Galois induction. It takes the auxiliary-valid problem `Q`, an actual prime cyclic step `D` in `Q.L`, and one Mackey double coset. Write `b = [E':E]-1` and `M = D · E'` for the actual compositum `Q.mackeyField D 1`. The displayed field homomorphism `j` embeds `E'` as the trivial conjugate and then includes it in `M`. The four chosen algebraic closure embeddings lift the field maps from `E` to `E'` and to `D`, and from `D` and `E'` to `M`. The claims about the induced actions are claims up to linear equivalence. Changing an algebraic closure embedding changes restriction by conjugacy, and the rank-one action is invariant under conjugation in the inducing Galois group.

The statement returns a coefficient field and a compatible rank-one realization of `ξalg = ξ · ‖·‖^(b/2)`. The parity hypothesis in the auxiliary datum makes this character algebraic, since the idelic norm at each complex place is the squared absolute value. The actual family `R` of rank `[E':E]` is induced from these characters. Its restriction `RD` to `D` is the pullback of `R` on the actual absolute groups. Every member of `RD` is induced from the restriction of the rank-one action along `j`, is irreducible, and has scalar intertwining endomorphisms. The families have common raw Euler polynomials over the same coefficient field. Their unitary normalizations give the unramified Euler polynomials of induction from the original `ξ` and its actual Mackey norm character, respectively. The raw eigenvalues over `D` have weight `-b` at the chosen complex embedding. All the stated comparisons are outside finite exceptional sets and the coefficient residue characteristic, as appropriate.

In the proof, the singleton condition gives `Gal(Q.L/E) = Gal(Q.L/D) Gal(Q.L/E')` and linear disjointness. The same single double coset occurs in the common algebraic closure, and Mackey restriction identifies `Res_D Ind_{E'}^E` with `Ind_M^D Res_M`. At good places the norm pullback of the Hecke character has the same value as Galois restriction, using the residue degree in the exponent of Frobenius and in the norm of a uniformizer. The rank-one family gives the induced raw Euler factors over both fields. To prove irreducibility, restrict further to the absolute group of `Q.L`. The result is the sum of the conjugate characters. Distinct embeddings over `E` give distinct conjugate norm pullbacks, by relative regularity at a complex place of `E`. The finite extension at the complex place has degree one, and the common algebraic norm shift does not alter the distinctions. Equal `λ`-adic characters would have equal good geometric Frobenius values, hence equal Hecke characters by weak approximation applied to their ratio, which is trivial on uniformizers and local units off a finite set. The resulting distinct character lines are permuted transitively over `D`. Every nonzero invariant subspace is the entire carrier and a commuting endomorphism acts by the same scalar on all lines. The argument also holds after any extension of the coefficient field.

Conjugate inversion and the compact norm-one idele classes give unitarity of the original `ξ`. At a place above `v` the positive idelic norm shift multiplies the value by `q_v^(-b f/2)`, with residue degree `f`. Thus induction multiplies every eigenvalue over `v` by `q_v^(-b/2)`, and similarly over the step field, with its own residue cardinality. This gives both normalized comparisons and the stated raw weight over `D`.
