# The arithmetic Mackey step in Harris’s Proposition 4.8

`proposition48MackeyStep` proves the published auxiliary Mackey step for
arbitrary `uF`. It formalizes the arithmetic part of the induction in
Harris, *The local Langlands conjecture for GL(n) over a p-adic field,
n < p*, Invent. Math. 134 (1998), 177–210. The registered source is
`harris-1998-local-langlands`. The relevant passages are Proposition 4.8,
printed pp. 202–203, TeX lines 1574–1614 and 1630–1648, and the formula
following Definition 4.5, printed p. 201, TeX lines 1526–1532.

The parent’s auxiliary general-position witness supplies a tower from
the local normal closure to the base. Rank greater than one rules out
a tower of length zero by the equality of global and completed degrees
at the unique auxiliary place. Under the compatible completion
identifications, the decomposition isomorphism at that place takes
the penultimate local field to a single global intermediate field `D`.
The last local step shows that `D/E` is cyclic of prime degree. The
subgroup fixing `D` is normal, and its product with the subgroup fixing
`E'` is their join. The double cosets are identified with the cosets of
the join. Their number divides the prime index of the subgroup fixing
`D`, so it is one or `[D:E]`.

For each `E`-automorphism `a` of `L`, the child uses the actual
compositum `D · a(E')` and its normal closure over `D` inside `L`. Its
field maps are the identities on the base and compositum and inclusion
on the normal closure. The imaginary quadratic subfield is the image
of the parent’s subfield. The raw Hecke character is the norm pullback
of the parent character transported by `a` to `a(E')`.

For condition (i), representatives of two complex places over a fixed
place of `D` can be chosen to agree on `D`. Equal restrictions to
`a(E')` would force equality on the compositum. Complex conjugate
restrictions would disagree on the totally complex field `E`. Thus
distinct child places give distinct parent places over a fixed place
of `E`, and the local archimedean norms have degree one. The parent’s
separating values give the required relative regularity for the child.
Those same norm identities carry algebraicity with twist exponent
`[E':E] - 1` to the raw child. This is the inherited parent parity.

For condition (ii), the CM involution of the compositum restricts to
the transported involution of `a(E')`. The field norm commutes with
these involutions, and the adelic norm satisfies the same identity by
weak approximation. The parent’s conjugate inversion identity then
holds for the raw character on idele classes.

For condition (iii), the child’s auxiliary places are the restrictions
of the actual parent top place. Extending primes to `L` proves
uniqueness, and conjugation gives uniqueness at the conjugate place.
The inverse of `a` extends continuously to the top completion and
transports to an automorphism of the local normal closure through the
compatible place isomorphism. The dense Weil projection surjects onto
this finite Galois quotient, which provides a Weil lift `g` of `a`
there. The completed compositum maps to the join of the penultimate
local field and the conjugate of the parent inducing field by `g`.
The commuting completion maps identify the finite idele norm with
the determinant norm on this join. Inverse conjugation and the
parent’s component identity identify its pullback with the child’s
auxiliary local character. The comparison of normalized reciprocity
families gives the corresponding Mackey character on the child Weil
subgroup.

For the child’s general-position witness, rebase the earlier tower
stages over the penultimate field and intersect them with the child’s
normal closure. Each step is prime cyclic or an equality. Removing
repetitions gives the required tower. At each projected stage the Weil
subgroup is generated by the old stage subgroup and the subgroup
fixing the child’s normal closure. The latter is contained in every
conjugate inducing subgroup. Restriction back to the old stage
therefore has a single Mackey constituent. By the character comparison
it is the corresponding parent constituent, which is irreducible by
the parent’s general-position witness.

The rank is `[D · a(E'):D]`, the degree of the inducing extension in
Harris’s induction formula after Definition 4.5. This corrects the rank
expression printed immediately after that formula. The child normal
closure has degree at most `[L:D]`, and the tower law gives
`[L:E] = [D:E][L:D]` with `[D:E]` prime and `[L:D]` positive. This is
the strict decrease of the induction measure, including when the
inducing rank stays the same.
