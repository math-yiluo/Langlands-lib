# Finite-character correction for a cyclic weak descent

Harris 1998, Lemma 1.6, printed pp. 188–189 (registered PDF pp. 12–13), applies a finite determinant twist to put a weak descent under Theorem 1.2. The statements below give the steps on actual ideles and cuspidal realizations. The cyclic weak descent and its local and archimedean properties are supplied by earlier steps of the singleton route.

## Conjugate dual in the descent fibre

`WeakBaseChangeConjugateDualDescentStatement` takes a CM extension `D/E`, actual rank `r + 1` realizations `S` and `T` over `E` and `D`, and an actual contragredient `Sdual` of `S`. If `T` is conjugate self-dual and its class is a weak base change of `S`, then the same `T` is a weak base change of the CM pullback `Sdual.complexConj`. This is the unramified comparison in the paragraph beginning “Finally” of Lemma 1.6 (companion TeX lines 756–761). The CM involutions commute with inclusion of the fields and hence with restriction of places and residue degrees. Taking reciprocal Satake eigenvalues commutes with the residue-degree power operation, so the spectral comparison follows on the transported and dual actual presentations. The actual dual is available by `cuspidalContragredientExistence`. Arthur–Clozel III.6.3 then identifies the conjugate dual as a twist by a power of the norm-kernel character, when the input class over `D` is unitary and Galois stable.

## Real-subfield factorization and extension

`CMNormKernelRealFactorStatement` takes a cyclic Galois extension of CM fields and a Hecke character `η` of `E` trivial on the idele-class norms from `D`. It returns a finite-order character `η₀` of `maximalRealSubfield E` with trivial archimedean component and with `η = η₀ ∘ N_{E/E⁺}`. The same statement applies to every power of the B1 character. The restriction isomorphism `Gal(D/E) ≃ Gal(D⁺/E⁺)` follows from `D = E D⁺` and `E ∩ D⁺ = E⁺`. Norm naturality on the finite norm quotients identifies the pullback under `N_{E/E⁺}` with restriction on these cyclic groups, using the finite idele-class norm comparison. The places of the totally real field `E⁺` split at infinity in the totally real field `D⁺`, so the local factors of `η₀` there are trivial. This is the real norm factorization in companion TeX lines 760–766.

`CMFiniteRealCharacterExtensionStatement` takes a finite-order character `η₀` of `E⁺` trivial on its entire archimedean idele factor. It returns a finite-order character `α` of `E` restricting to `η₀` under the actual idele-class inclusion, with `α · αᶜ = η₀ ∘ N_{E/E⁺}` (companion TeX lines 766–789). Use the quotients of the finite ideles by the closures of the principal subgroups. The compact profinite topology comes from the finite ideal class group and the images of the compact finite integral units. An element of the finite ideles of `E⁺` in the closure of `Eˣ` can be approximated by principal ideles with its exact finite valuations, since the group of fractional ideals is discrete. For such approximants `a`, the ratio `aᶜ/a` is an algebraic unit of absolute value one at every complex embedding, hence a root of unity. Choosing the approximation sufficiently close at a finite place forces this ratio to be one, since the roots of unity of `E` form a finite group. This gives approximation by `(E⁺)ˣ`, proving injectivity on the profinite quotients. Extend through an open finite quotient using `Langlands.Topology.exists_character_of_open_finite_quotient`. The identity `a aᶜ = incl(N_{E/E⁺}(a))` on idele classes gives the product formula, via `ideleClassNormGaloisProduct`.

## Actual determinant twist

`FiniteCharacterConjugateDualCorrectionStatement` takes an actual rank `r + 1` cuspidal realization `S` over a CM field, an actual contragredient, a finite-order character `α`, and the class-level relation `Sᶜ∨ ≃ S ⊗ ((α · αᶜ) ∘ det)`. It also takes unitarity of the central character, regular algebraicity, and a supercuspidal component at the specified finite place of `S`. Its conclusion concerns the actual realization `S.determinantTwist α hα`. Multiplication of characters and the dual contragredient-twist formula give the invariant conjugate pairing on the corrected operators. A finite-order CM character is trivial on the connected archimedean idele factor, so the infinitesimal character stays the same. The actual spectral presentation twists place by place. A smooth determinant character preserves supercuspidality and a unitary one preserves the unitary central character. The local component is therefore square-integrable modulo the center, by the supercuspidal square-integrability theorem with unitary central character, on this same presentation. The corrected class has all three properties required here for Theorem 1.2, with the split-prime condition checked separately for the original auxiliary place. These are the determinant and local steps of Lemma 1.6 (companion TeX lines 778–792).

All four statements are derived interfaces for Harris's argument. Their finite transfer input is the one already used by the Library's cyclic norm-kernel theorem. Their automorphic comparisons use the existing spectral presentations, the actual operator contragredient and the actual finite determinant twist. The matching of compatible families is a subsequent step.
