# Henniart's global characters as data for Harris's Proposition 4.8

Worker 84. The module
`Langlands/Global/Arithmetic/HenniartHarrisDatumStatement.lean` defines one
predicate and three propositions:

- `Langlands.Global.Harris.IsPairedPlaceConfiguration`;
- `Langlands.Global.HarrisDatumCharacterRealizationStatement.{uF,uE,uE',uL}`;
- `Langlands.Global.HenniartCommonBaseRealizationStatement.{uF}`;
- `Langlands.Global.HenniartHarrisDatumStatement.{uF}`.

The three propositions are existence assertions. None of them is proved here.
All fields, places, completions, characters and predicates are the existing
Library and Mathlib objects: `Harris.PlaceDatum`, `Harris.conjPlace`,
`Harris.HasUniquePlaceAbove`, `Harris.IsSplitImaginaryQuadratic` and

## Notation

The local data are a p-adic field `F`, finite intermediate extensions `F'`
and `M` in `AlgebraicClosure F.Carrier`, and `F̃ = galoisClosure F F'`. The
global data are CM number fields `E ⊆ E' ⊆ L`, an imaginary quadratic
subfield `K₀` of `E`, and place data `P, Ps`. Henniart (§§3.3–3.4) uses the
same letters for local and global fields. Locally, `F`, `F'`, `F̃` and `M`
are his `F`, the field `L` of a subgroup `H`, its Galois closure `L̃`, and
`E`. Globally, `E`, `E'`, `L`, `K₀`, `P.v` and `Ps.v` are his `F`, `L`, `L̃`,
`K`, `v₀` and `v₀''`. In Harris's notation (§4, pp.200–201) they are `F`,
`F'`, `F̃`, `E`, `E'`, `L = Ẽ`, `K₀`, `v₁` and `v(*)₁`.

## The paired-place configuration

`Harris.IsPairedPlaceConfiguration K₀ P Ps φ φs θ` is the conjunction in the
conclusion of the published `HarrisPairedPlaceRealizationStatement`, with the
same conjuncts in the same order. The only difference is that
`IsScalarTower E E' L` and `IsNormalClosure E E' L` are conjuncts here
rather than existential instance witnesses. In summary:

- `L` is the normal closure of `E'/E`, and `K₀` is imaginary quadratic with
  `p` split. Each of `E`, `E'` and `L` is generated by its maximal real
  subfield and (the image of) `K₀`.
- `[E' : E] = [F' : F]` and `[L : E] = [F̃ : F]`.
- `P.v, c(P.v), Ps.v, c(Ps.v)` are pairwise distinct places above `p`, and
  each has exactly one place of `L` above it.
- For `Q = P` and `Q = Ps`: `Q.IsCompatible`, continuity of `Q.iso` and
  `Q.isoTilde` in both directions, and compatibility of one group
  isomorphism `θ : Gal(L/E) ≃ Gal(F̃/F)` with `Q.isoTilde`.
- The continuous ring isomorphisms `φ : E'_{P.v'} ≃ F'` and
  `φs : E'_{Ps.v'} ≃ F'` extend the base isomorphisms and agree with the
  maps into `F̃` given by `P.isoTilde` and `Ps.isoTilde`.

The Markdown of `HarrisPairedPlaceRealizationStatement` describes each
conjunct and its sources. With this predicate, that statement says: every
`F'` has a configuration with `E, E', L : Type`.

## Character realization on a configuration

`HarrisDatumCharacterRealizationStatement` fixes arbitrary universes for `F`,
`E`, `E'` and `L`. It says: for every configuration, every reciprocity family
`e` with `NormalizedReciprocity F e`, and every continuous character
`χ₀ : FieldCharacter F F'`, there are `χ, χs : FieldCharacter F F'` and
`ξ : HeckeCharacter E'` with

- `Harris.IsProposition48Datum e χ χs K₀ P Ps ξ`, and
- `χ u = χ₀ u` for every unit `u` of `F'` with
  `(fieldData F F').discreteValuation u = 1`. So `χ / χ₀` is unramified.

The datum contains the field conditions, which the configuration already
supplies. Its character conditions are: `χs` is in general position relative
to `F` through `e`; `ξ` satisfies `IsAlgebraicAfterTwist ([E' : E] − 1)`, is
`E'/E`-regular and satisfies `ξ = ξ⁻¹ ∘ c`; and the components of `ξ` at
`P.v'` and `Ps.v'` are `χ` and `χs`.

### Derived choices

The proposition is a derived formulation, not a verbatim theorem.

1. **Normalization.** Henniart's conditions use the unitary normalization
   `(z/z̄)^{p_v}`. The datum uses Harris's p.195 normalization. When
   `n = [E' : E]` is odd, a character with Henniart's components satisfies
   `IsAlgebraicAfterTwist (n − 1)`, because `|z|^{n−1} = (z z̄)^{(n−1)/2}`.
   When `n` is even, the exponents must be odd, and Harris's p.195 construction
   (components trivial on `ℝˣ`) does not produce them. The expected route
   multiplies Henniart's character by the pullback along the norm of a
   character from `CMHalfWeightCharacterStatement` on `E`. This is the
   twist of Harris–Taylor's Proposition VII.2.7 in the language of the
   datum.
2. **Unramified twist.** Only the units of `F'` are prescribed. The
   proposition asks for agreement with `χ₀` on the units and leaves the
   uniformizer value free, as all three sources do. It does not assert that
   the component at `P` equals `χ₀`.
3. **Auxiliary place.** The proposition asks for some `χs` in general
   position. It does not prescribe `χs`. Henniart's regularity condition 3)
   is a condition on the restriction to units. So is the published
   `HasExactUnitNormStabilizer`, which gives Harris's (4.12) through
   `UnitNormWeilStabilizerTransportStatement`.
4. **Inputs.** `χ₀` is any continuous character of `F'ˣ`. Henniart uses
   finite-order characters; Harris uses arbitrary characters of `W_{F'}`.
   Every configuration is allowed, not only those built in §3.3.

Henniart's §3.4 cites Harris's §4 for the associated cuspidal representation.
This proposition stops at the datum. It does not assert
`Harris.Proposition48Statement`.

## A common base field

`HenniartCommonBaseRealizationStatement` says: for every prime `p`, every
`F : PadicField.{uF} p` and every finite extension `M` of `F` (not
necessarily Galois), there are a CM field `E : Type`, a finite place `v₀` of
`E` and a ring isomorphism `ι₀ : E_{v₀} ≃ F`, continuous in both directions.
For these, every `F'` with `F'.field ≤ M.field` has a configuration over this
`E`, with `E', L : Type`, `P.v = v₀`, and `P.iso` equal to `ι₀` on (the image
of) `E`.

Source: Henniart §3.3 (p.449) fixes one finite Galois extension of `F`
(here, the Galois closure of `M`). He globalizes it once, by a totally real
Galois extension `E⁺/F⁺` with the whole Galois group at one place `v₀` and
quadratic fields `K` and `K'` in which `p` splits, and sets `F = F⁺K'K`. For
every subgroup `H`, §3.4 then uses the fixed field `L` of `H` over this one
`F`. §3.7 needs this common base. In §2.5 (printed p.446), `G(F)` is the
set of associated pairs over one number field `F`, and `R_G(F, v₀)` is
generated by their components at one place `v₀`; §3.7 shows that this
subgroup contains every element factoring through `Gal(E/F)`. The proof of
Harris–Taylor's Lemma VII.2.10 (p.246) makes the same choice. Here an arbitrary finite `M`
replaces Henniart's Galois `E` (pass to its Galois closure). The
configuration of each `F'` keeps all conjuncts of the paired-place
statement, so this proposition implies `HarrisPairedPlaceRealizationStatement`
for `uF` (take `M = F'`). The second place `Ps.v` may depend on `F'`.

## The combination

`HenniartHarrisDatumStatement` has the same common base. For every `F'` in
`M`, every normalized reciprocity family `e` and every continuous `χ₀`, it
gives a configuration over `E` at `v₀` as above, together with `χ, χs, ξ`
satisfying `IsProposition48Datum e χ χs K₀ P Ps ξ` and `χ = χ₀` on the
units. The base `E`, `v₀` and `ι₀` do not depend on `F'`, `e` or `χ₀`. The
proposition combines the common-base realization with the character
realization (at `E, E', L : Type`). It is the input that Henniart's §3.7
applies, through Harris's Proposition 4.8, for every subgroup and every
character.
