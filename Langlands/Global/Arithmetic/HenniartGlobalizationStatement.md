# Henniart's global construction: the relative Weil image contains `R_E`

Author: Worker 84. Statement contribution to task
`34b81620-20d7-4059-9b08-6bedd1c1c029`, revision 1, unit U2 of the H3 assembly
(Henniart 2000, §3). Its design is agreed with Worker 116 (first executor).
Workers 30, 44 and 64 reported no overlapping target.

The module has two auxiliary definitions and two proposition definitions. Nothing
is proved and nothing is assumed.

The target is `Langlands.Global.HenniartGlobalizationStatement`. It says that
Harris (1998), Proposition 4.8 implies `Langlands.Global.RelativeGaloisGlobalization`,
which is Henniart (2000), Théorème 3.1, in the relative form of the Library's
witnessed pairs. Informally: for a p-adic field `K` and a homomorphism `q` from its
absolute Galois group to a finite group, there are a Galois extension `E/F` of number fields, a
finite place `v` of `F` and an isomorphism `K ≅ F_v` such that, after transport
along this isomorphism, every virtual representation of `W_K` that factors through
`q` lies in the Weil image `weilImage F E c v` generated by the local
components of associated pairs.

## Declarations

Notation. `W_K` is the published `Weil.Local.localWeilGroup K` of a p-adic field
`K : PadicField.{uK} p`. For a number field `F`, a finite place `v` and
`h : residuePrime v = p`, `padicFieldAt v h : PadicField p` is the published
canonical completion package of `F_v` with its prime index changed along `h`. Its
carrier is `v.adicCompletion F`, and its `ℚ_p`-algebra structure is the published
`Langlands.Global.CompletionPadic.padicAlgebraAt v h`, the canonical structure with
its prime index changed. `padicField v` is the canonical package itself, indexed by
`residuePrime v`. The published theorem `padicFieldAt_heq` says that the two
packages are heterogeneously equal.

- `padicFieldAtWeilEquiv v h :
  localWeilGroup (padicFieldAt v h) ≃ₜ* localWeilGroup (padicField v)`
  (definition). It is the identity after `subst h`. It only changes the prime
  index and leaves the actual Weil group unchanged.
- `completionWeilTransport v h K f : W_K →ₜ* W_{F_v}` (definition), for a
  `ℚ_p`-algebra isomorphism
  `f : K.Carrier ≃ₐ[ℚ_p] (padicFieldAt v h).Carrier`. The `ℚ_p`-algebra structure
  on the target is the package's own structure. The map is the published local
  Weil map `extensionWeilMap (padicFieldAt v h) K f.symm.toAlgHom : W_K → W_{F_v}`
  of the embedding `f⁻¹ : F_v → K`, followed by `padicFieldAtWeilEquiv v h`.
  The published docstring describes `extensionWeilMap` as `(σ, m) ↦ (ι σ, r m)`,
  with `r` the residue degree. By the published `extensionWeilMap_isOpenEmbedding`
  it is an open embedding. By `extensionWeilMap_range_index` its image has index
  `[K : F_v] = 1`. So the map is an isomorphism of topological groups: the
  transport of Weil groups along `f`. Nothing about it is proved in this module.
- `RelativeGaloisGlobalization` (proposition definition, universe parameters
  `uK uQ uW uP`). For every class family `c : FundamentalClass.ClassFamily.{0}`
  with the published `RelativeWeilGroup.IsArithmeticClassFamily c`, every prime
  `p`, every p-adic field `K : PadicField.{uK} p`, every finite group `Q : Type uQ`
  and every group homomorphism `q : Gal(\bar K/K) →* Q`, there exist
  - number fields `F E : Type` with `[Algebra F E]` and `[IsGalois F E]`,
  - a finite place `v` of `F` and `h : residuePrime v = p`, and
  - a `ℚ_p`-algebra isomorphism `f : K.Carrier ≃ₐ[ℚ_p] (padicFieldAt v h).Carrier`,

  such that for every instance `[CompactSpace (AdeleQuotient F)]` and every
  `x ∈ LocalWeil.unitaryFiniteFactoringSupported.{uK, uW} K q` there is
  `y ∈ weilImage.{0, uW, uP} F E c v` with

  `continuousRestrict (completionWeilTransport v h K f)
    (realizeExact (padicField v) (includeUnitary (padicField v) y)) =
   realizeExact K (includeUnitary K x)`.

  Here `unitaryFiniteFactoringSupported K q` is the published sublattice of the
  unitary lattice of `W_K` spanned by the unitary irreducible classes of the form
  `A ∘ q ∘ (W_K → Gal(\bar K/K))` for a representation `A` of `Q`.
  `weilImage F E c v` is the published integral span of the Weil elements of all
  `WitnessedGenerator F E c v` (Henniart's `RG(F, 0)`, relative form; see below).
  `realizeExact` is the published map from a lattice of irreducible classes to the
  exact Grothendieck group of continuous representations. `continuousRestrict φ`
  is the published restriction of continuous exact virtual representations along
  a continuous homomorphism `φ`. The equation is therefore an equation of genuine
  virtual representations of `W_K`. The Weil-representation carriers of both
  lattices are in the same universe `uW`, and the spectral presentations of the
  witnessed pairs are in universe `uP`.
- `HenniartGlobalizationStatement` (the target, universe parameters
  `uK uQ uW uP`) is
  `Harris.Proposition48Statement.{uK, 0, 0, 0, uP} →
    RelativeGaloisGlobalization.{uK, uQ, uW, uP}`.
  Harris's local field is in universe `uK`. Its global fields `E, E', L` are in
  `Type`, as in the published Henniart–Harris datum statements. Its presentation
  universe `uV` is the presentation universe `uP` of the witnessed pairs.

A `WitnessedGenerator F E c v` is the published record of an associated pair at
`v`. It consists of an irreducible unitary representation `S` of the relative Weil
group `W_{E/F} = RelativeWeilGroup c F E` and a cuspidal realization `T` with a
spectral presentation `P`, weakly associated with `S`. It also contains a place `w`
of `E` above `v`, a reciprocity candidate at `E_w` and a local map
`ι : W_{F_v} → W_{E/F}` with the published `IsLocalRelativeWeilMap`. Finally it
contains the irreducibility and admissibility of `P_v`, the Weil element (the class
of `S ∘ ι` in the unitary lattice, witnessed by an exact expansion) and a unitary
supercuspidal support of `P_v`.

## Relation to the source

Henniart (2000), §2.5, pp.445–446, defines `G(F)` as the set of pairs `(S, P)` with
`S` a continuous irreducible unitary representation of the absolute Weil group
`W_F` of a number field `F`, and `P` an associated unitary cuspidal automorphic
representation of `GL_n(𝔸_F)`, `n = dim S`. He sets `φ_G(S, P) = [S_0]`, the class
of the local component at a finite place `0`, and lets `RG(F, 0)` be the subgroup
of `RG(F_0)` generated by the image of `φ_G`. Théorème 3.1, p.448, reads: for a
finite Galois extension `E` of the p-adic field `F` in `\bar F`, there exist a
number field `F`, a place `0` of `F` and a continuous isomorphism `φ : F_0 → F`
such that the subgroup of `RG(F)` corresponding to `RG(F, 0)` via `φ` contains
every element that factors through `Gal(E/F)`. In this file the local field is `K`
and the number fields are `F` and `E`.

1. *Relative Weil group.* Henniart uses the absolute Weil group `W_F`. The Library's
   witnessed pairs live on `W_{E/F}` for a fixed class family `c`. Under
   `IsArithmeticClassFamily c`, `c` is the arithmetic fundamental class family for
   some family of local invariants (published docstring). Mathematically these
   conditions make `c F E` the fundamental class `u_{E/F}`, so `W_{E/F}` is Tate's
   relative Weil group `W_F / W_E^c`,
   the extension of `Gal(E/F)` by `C_E` with class `u_{E/F}` (Tate 1979, (1.1.1)
   and (1.2), p.4). The relative form loses
   nothing for Henniart's construction. In §3.3, p.449, his global extension is
   Galois, and every `L` of §3.4 lies in it. Each representation
   `S(X) = Ind_{W_L}^{W_F} X_L` has a kernel containing the normal subgroup
   `W_E^c`, so it factors through `W_{E/F}`. Its local component at `0` is
   unchanged. The relative form is what the published `relativePolePairing`
   (Henniart Théorème 2.5 in relative form) needs. The target quantifies over every
   arithmetic `c`; the number fields, the place and `f` may depend on `c`.
2. *The finite quotient.* Henniart fixes a finite Galois extension `M/K`. The
   target allows any homomorphism `q` to a finite group, with no continuity or
   surjectivity assumed. The two forms are equivalent. For `M/K` finite Galois,
   take `q` to be restriction to `Gal(M/K)`. Conversely, let `b` be a continuous
   irreducible class that factors through `q ∘ (W_K → Gal(\bar K/K))`. Its image is
   finite, so its kernel is an open normal subgroup of finite index of `W_K` and
   contains `ker(q ∘ proj)`. Such a subgroup has the form `W_{M_b}` for a finite
   Galois `M_b/K`. Only finitely many classes factor through the finite group
   `q(Gal(\bar K/K))`. The compositum `M` of their fields `M_b` is finite Galois,
   and every `x ∈ unitaryFiniteFactoringSupported K q` factors through `Gal(M/K)`.
   Henniart's theorem for `M` then gives the target for `q`. All classes that
   factor through a finite group are unitary, so working in the unitary lattice
   loses nothing.
3. *The isomorphism.* Henniart's `φ` is a continuous field isomorphism
   `F_0 → K`. The target asks for a `ℚ_p`-algebra isomorphism `f : K ≅ F_v`
   instead, with the canonical `ℚ_p`-structure on `F_v`. Both kinds of map exist
   or fail to exist together. A continuous ring isomorphism is the identity on
   `ℚ`. Both `ℚ_p`-structure maps are continuous, so it is `ℚ_p`-linear by
   density of `ℚ` in `ℚ_p`. Conversely, a `ℚ_p`-linear map of finite-dimensional
   `ℚ_p`-spaces is continuous. "Corresponding via `φ`" is
   expressed by restriction along `completionWeilTransport v h K f`, which is
   transport along `f`. The comparison is an equality in the exact Grothendieck
   group of continuous representations of `W_K`, so no lattice transport along
   field isomorphisms is needed.
4. *Order of quantifiers.* As in Henniart, one choice of `F, E, v, h, f` serves
   every `x`. The `∃` precedes `∀ x`.
5. *Compactness instance.* `weilImage` takes `[CompactSpace (AdeleQuotient F)]` as
   an instance argument. The target quantifies over it. The class is a
   proposition, and the published theorem
   `Langlands.Global.compactSpace_adeleQuotient` proves it, so the quantifier is
   harmless and not vacuous.
6. *Harris as the only hypothesis.* Henniart's §§3.3–3.7, pp.449–451, deduce
   Théorème 3.1 from Harris's theorem (his §3.4). In the Library this is
   `Harris.Proposition48Statement` (Harris 1998, Proposition 4.8, p.201), and
   `HenniartGlobalizationStatement` takes it as its only hypothesis. Henniart's
   remaining inputs are not hypotheses of the target. They are the global choices
   of §3.3 (Henniart 1984, Lemma 3.6), the character constructions of Lemmas 1–3
   (§§3.5–3.6), the twist in §3.7 and Brauer's theorem. A proof must take them, and
   the Library facts that turn Harris's output into witnessed pairs, from proved
   Library material.

The target claims no more than Théorème 3.1. It does not assert any property of
the composite map `R_E(F) → RA(F)` of §§3.7–3.8, the independence of the global
choices, or anything about `automorphicImage`.

## Why the target is true

`RelativeGaloisGlobalization` is Henniart's Théorème 3.1, by items 1–3. Harris's
Proposition 4.8 is a theorem, so the implication is true. The following sketch
follows Henniart §§3.3–3.7 with the Library's objects. It records what a proof
uses; this module proves nothing.

1. Reduce `q` to a finite Galois `M/K` (item 2).
2. Choose a CM field with a place `v₀` and `ι₀ : E_{v₀} ≅ K`. For each
   intermediate field `K'` of `M/K` and each finite-order character of `K'ˣ`,
   choose Harris data over it, prescribed on units. These are the published
   statements `Langlands.Global.HenniartCommonBaseRealizationStatement` and
   `Langlands.Global.HenniartHarrisDatumStatement` (Henniart §3.3; §3.6, Lemma 3).
   The base `F` of the target is this CM field, and `v = v₀`. The continuous ring
   isomorphism `ι₀` gives `h` (the residue characteristic) and `f = ι₀⁻¹`, by
   item 3.
3. Harris's proposition gives a cuspidal class that is a weak automorphic induction
   of the Hecke character `ξ` of `E'`. Weak association of
   `S = Ind_{W_{𝕃/E'}}^{W_{𝕃/F}}(ξ ∘ τ)` with it holds for any finite Galois
   `𝕃/F` containing `E'`. Here `τ` is the transfer and `c` is arithmetic. One
   finite Galois `E/F` containing all the fields `E'` used for a finite Brauer
   generating set serves every generator. This compositum is needed because the
   datum statement gives the fields `E'` separately.
4. Build witnessed pairs from these, for instance with the published constructor
   `WitnessedGenerator.ofUnitaryInduction`. This uses local maps from the published
   `Langlands.ClassField.Global.LocalRelativeWeilMapExistenceStatement`, whose
   hypotheses are those of `IsArithmeticClassFamily c`. It also uses the local
   component of `S` at `v` (the published
   `RelativeWeilGroup.LocalInducedRestrictionStatement` and
   `RelativeWeilGroup.LocalTransferReciprocityStatement`) and a unitary
   supercuspidal support of `P_v` (Henniart Théorème 2.4, the published
   `RelativeWeilGroup.AssociatedSupercuspidalInductionStatement`).
5. By §3.7, `S_v` is an unramified twist of `Ind_{W_{K'}}^{W_K} χ₀` transported
   along `f`. Removing the twist needs a twist relation for unitary unramified,
   in general infinite-order, characters. The published twist statements cover
   finite-order characters only, so this relation is a further statement. Then
   `weilImage` contains the transport
   of every `Ind_{W_{K'}}^{W_K} χ₀` with `χ₀` of finite order. Brauer's theorem for
   `Gal(M/K)` (published `Langlands.FiniteRep.BrauerTheory.finiteBrauerTheory`, proved)
   writes every virtual character of `Gal(M/K)` as an integral combination of such
   induced characters. This gives `x`.

## Intended use

The target is an input to the remaining H3 units. Composed with the published
`relativePolePairing` (under its explicit `RelativeCertifiedPoleComparisons`
hypothesis), it gives Henniart's composite map `R_E(K) → RA(K)` of §3.7 and its
properties of §3.8. Those are separate later statements.
