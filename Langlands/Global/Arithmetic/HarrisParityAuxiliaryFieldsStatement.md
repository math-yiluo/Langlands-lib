# Quadratic CM auxiliary fields for Harris's parity argument

`Langlands.Global.Harris.HarrisParityAuxiliaryFieldsStatement` supplies the actual number fields for the parity-removal argument. Clozel chooses a totally real quadratic extension that splits at the distinguished place to double the totally real degree in the paragraph before Corollary 5.4.2, printed p. 132. Harris–Taylor, Theorem VII.1.9, pp. 230–231, uses quadratic auxiliaries with prescribed splitting and disjointness to patch the Galois representations. Sørensen, §1, Definitions 1 and 2 and the following example, gives the simultaneous prime-splitting argument. [Sørensen's manuscript](https://mathweb.ucsd.edu/~csorense/patch.pdf)

The base is a CM number field `E` with `E = E₀ K₀`, `E₀` totally real and `K₀` imaginary quadratic. All extensions live in one `AlgebraicClosure E`. The predicate `IsParityAuxiliary E₀ K₀ N` says that `N/E` is quadratic, that `N` is CM, and that `N` has a totally real intermediate field `N₀` containing the image of `E₀`, of degree `2 * [E₀ : ℚ]`, for which `N = N₀ K₀` under the canonical map from `E`.

The first conclusion takes any finite set `V` of actual places of `E` and any finite Galois extension `M/E` in the same closure. It supplies a parity auxiliary disjoint from `M` with complete splitting at every place in `V`. This gives the supply required by the quadratic patching statement, including simultaneous splitting at the distinguished automorphic place and a chosen coefficient-labelled place. The second conclusion supplies three distinct parity auxiliaries, each splitting completely at `V`, and a finite set `T` such that every place outside `T` splits completely in at least one of the three. This finite cover lets the parity reduction use finitely many compatible systems to obtain a common exceptional set across the coefficient places.

A proof route for the first conclusion is to choose a positive prime radicand `d` congruent to `1` modulo `8` and the odd rational primes below `V`. The congruences give local square roots. Dirichlet's theorem leaves infinitely many choices, and only finitely many independent rational prime square classes become squares in the finite extension `M/ℚ`. The proof of `padicSquareNonsquare` already implements this argument for one rational prime using Hensel's lemma and `Nat.infinite_setOfPred_prime_and_eq_mod`. Adjoining `√d` to `E` gives the required disjoint quadratic extension. The field `E₀(√d)` is totally real and of doubled degree. At any place above a prescribed place, the two roots of `X² - d` lie in the base completion, so the generator of the auxiliary field lies in its image. The base completion has closed image in the actual upper completion and contains the dense image of the upper number field, which gives local degree one.

For the second conclusion, choose successive radicands `d₁` and `d₂`, taking the second to be a nonsquare in `E(√d₁)`. The three subfields of `E(√d₁, √d₂)` come from `d₁`, `d₂`, and `d₁d₂`. All three radicands are positive and locally square at the prescribed primes. Outside the finite ramification set, the Frobenius element in the biquadratic Galois group belongs to the kernel of at least one of its three nontrivial quadratic characters. The corresponding quadratic field splits completely there. This finite covering condition is a derived input for uniform compatibility.
