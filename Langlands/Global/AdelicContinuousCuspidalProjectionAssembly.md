# Assembly of the continuous cuspidal projection

`continuousCuspidalProjectionExistence_of_analyticInputs` proves
`ContinuousCuspidalProjectionExistenceStatement` from the normalized
radical average test-function proposition and the uniform norm-one
partial convolution decay proposition. The result is universe-general.

For a positive-size closed matrix embedding, choose the central split
torus decomposition for the inducing Levi and the compatible positive
Haar measure. Iwasawa coverage passes from the minimal parabolic to
the inducing parabolic. `parabolicReproducingKernel` gives an adelic
test function whose right convolution reproduces \(\varphi\).
Automorphy makes the right compact translates of \(\varphi\) a finite
sum of translates at fixed compact points with continuous coefficients.
The coefficients have an open finite-adelic level, so their compact
right averages of the reproducing kernel are adelic test functions.
The finite Iwasawa expansion of the reproduced function's pairing
uses exactly these averaged kernels and base points.

The two analytic inputs make the corresponding finite cuspidal Levi
vectors continuous and polynomially bounded in the positive variable.
Smoothing these vectors gives the projection with the required
continuity, parabolic left invariance, moderate growth, and zero
proper constant terms. For every Levi cusp form, maximality of the
Levi compact gives rapid decay on rational representatives in the
norm-one quotient. This controls the integrands for both \(\varphi\)
and the projection. The finite comparison of Levi pairings applies
the transpose formula and the recentered expansion to show equality.

For matrix size zero, the closed embedding makes every group of
test points trivial. All standard parabolics have the same image,
so there are no strictly smaller indices below the inducing one.
The proof takes \(\psi=\varphi\). The norm-one Levi pairing integrands
are constant and integrable by finite quotient volume.

The construction proves the norm-one pairing characterization
of Bernstein–Lapid, *On the meromorphic continuation of Eisenstein
series*, §6.2, equation (6.8). The reproducing kernel and decay inputs
use Mœglin–Waldspurger, *Spectral Decomposition and Eisenstein Series*,
I.2.12, I.2.17–18 and I.3.4.
