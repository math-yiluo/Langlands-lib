# The Eisenstein positive roots and parabolics containing the inducing parabolic

The positive roots in the convergence statement are the nonzero restrictions
of the positive ambient relative roots. The published split-component root
comparison identifies them with the intrinsic positive roots. To prove
that the defining cocharacter is generic on its split component, lift each
root to the ambient torus. Either that lift or its negative is positive for
the ambient generic cocharacter. The nonzero restriction therefore has
strictly positive or strictly negative pairing with the split-component
cocharacter.

For a cocharacter admissible on the full opposite radical, negate a
positive split-component root and apply admissibility to that negative
root. This gives nonnegative pairing of the original root with the negative
admissible cocharacter. Genericity treats every root with nonnegative
pairing. The actual root-sign criterion then places the inducing parabolic
inside the dynamic parabolic of the negative admissible cocharacter.
`oppositeAdmissibleParabolicFactor` realizes that inclusion as an actual
group-scheme morphism commuting with the ambient inclusions.

These internal comparisons specialize the published dynamic and relative
root interfaces to the positive chamber of Mœglin–Waldspurger II.1.5.
