# Weyl separation from the leading chamber

Let `r` be a compatible root presentation of the parabolic `P` and let
`w` be a nonidentity element of its ambient relative Weyl group. Assume
`w` sends positive roots whose restriction to the split component is
zero to positive roots. For every `R`-regular parameter of `P`, the
ambient lift of its restricted real character, after applying `w`,
has pairing less than `-R` with some positive ambient root. The pairings
use the same invariant form and its induced split-component form as
the regularity condition.

The relative Weyl length formula provides a reduced positive root
`α` whose image by `w` is negative. The positivity hypothesis implies
that `α` restricts nontrivially to the split component. The original
regularity inequality then bounds its pairing with the ambient lift
from below by `R`. Weyl invariance of the form transports the inequality,
and the positive root `-wα` reverses its sign.

This is the ambient root inequality in the proof of Bernstein–Lapid
Lemma 6.8. It takes the required positivity on the Levi roots as a
hypothesis and compares directly with the positive ambient roots used
to detect the leading chamber in §6.2.

For a finite set of exponent shifts, bound their pairings with all
positive ambient roots. The root set is finite. A parameter regular
beyond that bound retains a negative pairing after adding any of the
shifts. Since every positive root is a sum of simple roots, some
simple root then has a negative pairing. This gives the exclusion
from the ambient leading chamber at one threshold for the entire
finite set of shifts.

For this last assertion it suffices that the Weyl element preserves
positivity on the selected Levi simple roots. A positive ambient root
restricting to zero pairs trivially with the defining split-component
cocharacter. The standard cocharacter property places it in the
monoid generated by the selected roots. Its Weyl image thus has
nonnegative pairing with the generic cocharacter and is itself a
root. Genericity makes the pairing strictly positive.

The theorem `Langlands.Global.eisensteinAmbientWeylSeparation` applies
this finite-shift result to the data of
`EisensteinAmbientWeylSeparationStatement`.
