# Cuspidal annihilation and the parabolic vanishing criterion

Sources: Mœglin–Waldspurger, *Spectral Decomposition and Eisenstein Series* (1995), I.3.4, pp. 45–46; Bernstein–Lapid, *On the meromorphic continuation of Eisenstein series* (2024), (6.7)–(6.8), p. 202, and (8.1), p. 221. The module **states** the vanishing criterion. It constructs the annihilator subspace and proves its linearity, but does not prove the criterion.

## Fibrewise tests

The cutoff cuspidal tests in the sources pair against cuspidal forms multiplied by a compactly supported function of the split logarithm. Here the zero condition uses the fibrewise version in BL (6.8): for each ambient point \(g\) and each Levi cusp form \(\psi\), pair \(m\mapsto f(mg)\) with \(\psi\) on \(M_P(F)\backslash M_P(\mathbb A_F)^1\). Translating \(g\) varies the split and compact variables of the test; the norm-one slice is the fibre used in the stated comparison. Lean uses exactly `StandardParabolicFamily.IsLeviCuspForm`, `leviPairingIntegrand`, and `leviPairing`. In particular, the Haar measure is the existing norm-one quotient Haar measure; its scaling is immaterial for the zero condition. The input must have absolutely integrable pairing integrands, so a totalized integral at a nonintegrable input cannot witness vanishing.

`InCuspidalAnnihilator` names that test. For a fixed cusp form and a fixed \(g\), `leviPairingIntegrandLinearMap` is pointwise complex-linear in the tested function. The integrable functions form a linear domain there, and Bochner integral additivity and homogeneity on that domain give the complex subspace `cuspidalAnnihilator`. The subspace itself is on ambient functions; the statement below imposes the relevant parabolic invariance and growth separately. No Lean equivalence to a cutoff-test formulation is asserted in this module.

## The proposition

`ParabolicCuspidalVanishingStatement` quantifies over a number field, an actual connected reductive group with a supplied `StandardParabolicFamily`, a maximal compact giving Iwasawa coverage for this family and a positive-size closed matrix embedding for the height. For a member \(P\) and a continuous function \(f\) on the actual \(U_P(\mathbb A_F)M_P(F)\)-quotient, assume a moderate growth bound for the function in this height. For every standard \(Q\subseteq P\), including \(P\) itself, test the existing normalized constant term \(C_Q f\) for membership in the fibrewise cuspidal annihilator for \(Q\). The conclusion is \(f=0\) as a function. The case \(Q=P\) tests \(f\) itself, because the integrand for \(C_P f\) is constant on a mass-one quotient.

This is the continuous number-field formulation of the extension (8.1) to parabolic quotients. The moderate-growth test (on the function itself) comes from MW I.3.4; (8.1) uses its uniform moderate-growth specialization. In the source proof the measurable version concludes almost-everywhere vanishing; continuity and full support of the quotient measure give pointwise vanishing in this version. The common Iwasawa compact and actual matrix height give the adelic models of the slices and the growth condition. The analytic proof needs the parabolic-to-Levi restriction and transitivity, smoothing and cuspidal decay, and density of cuspidal automorphic vectors on the norm-one quotient. These are not consequences of the subspace construction here.
