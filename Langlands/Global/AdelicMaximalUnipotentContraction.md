# Archimedean contraction in the radical of a maximal standard parabolic

`AdelicMaximalUnipotentContractionStatement` starts with a connected reductive group \(G\) over a number field \(F\), a maximal split torus \(T\), and a generic cocharacter specifying simple relative roots \(\Delta\). For \(\alpha\in\Delta\), let \(U\) be an affine closed model of the standard unipotent radical for \(\Delta\setminus\{\alpha\}\), with a specified smooth relative dimension. The statement takes a torus morphism \(T_0\to T\) and uses its image in \(G\) for conjugation.

The theorem constructs global polynomial coordinates \(Q\) on \(U(F_\infty)\), valued in a finite power of the mixed real and complex space of \(F\). The coordinates send the identity to zero and the inverse map is smooth. Fix a compact subset \(\Omega\) of \(U(\mathbb A)\) and a common positive lower bound for the full adelic norms of the simple roots restricted to \(T_0\). For \(x\in\Omega\) and any \(a\) in this weak chamber in the intrinsic positive subgroup of \(T_0(\mathbb A)\), the inverse conjugate \(a^{-1}xa\) lies in the image of \(U(\mathbb A)\) and satisfies

\[
  \big\|Q((a^{-1}xa)_\infty)\big\|
    \le C\,|\alpha(a)|_{\mathbb A}^{-1/[F:\mathbb Q]} .
\]

Here \(|\alpha(a)|_{\mathbb A}\) denotes the full adelic norm of the restricted character and \(C\) depends on the compact subset and the chamber threshold. The exponent converts full idele norm into the common positive real value at the infinite places.

The proof uses the polynomial torus-eigenfunction coordinates of the standard radical. Each coordinate weight is a positive root outside the additive monoid generated by \(\Delta\setminus\{\alpha\}\). Decomposing positive roots into simple roots writes the weight as \(\alpha\) plus a nonnegative integral sum of simple roots. The inverse common scalar of the additional sum is uniformly bounded in the chamber. Evaluation of the eigenfunction identity on actual adelic points and projection to the infinite factor give the estimate. Polynomial evaluation also gives smoothness of the inverse coordinate map.

This is a coordinate form of the chamber contraction used for derivative decay in Mœglin–Waldspurger, §I.2.10, pp. 30–33.
