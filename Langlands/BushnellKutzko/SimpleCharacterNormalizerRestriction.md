# BK93 (3.5.10) from the intertwining and conjugation formulas

The proposition `SimpleCharacterNormalizerRestrictionStatement`, defined in this
module, is BK93 Lemma (3.5.10), together with the normalization of `H^m` used with it
in the proof of (3.5.9). The source states the second assertion as `θ^x = θ` on
`U^m(𝔅_β)`; the first assertion, which its proof needs, is stated in the proof of
(3.5.9) on printed p. 121 for the elements of `𝒮(β)`. Let `[𝔄,n,0,β]` be a
simple stratum and `m = i + 1` with `m < n` and `m < −k₀(β,𝔄)` (`simpleCriticalDepth`,
infinite for scalar `β`). Let `θ ∈ C(𝔄,m−1,β)`, a character of `H^m(β,𝔄)`, and let `x`
normalize the restriction `θ₀` of `θ` to `H^{m+1}(β,𝔄)`. Then `x` normalizes
`H^m(β,𝔄)` and `θ(xux⁻¹) = θ(u)` for `u ∈ H^m(β,𝔄) ∩ U^m(𝔅_β)`.

The module also defines `SimpleCharacterNormalizerRestrictionReductionStatement`: the
implication

`SimpleCharacterRestrictionStatement → SimpleCharacterIntertwiningStatement →
SimpleCharacterCovarianceStatement → SimpleCharacterNormalizerRestrictionStatement`.

`simpleCharacterNormalizerRestrictionReduction` proves it. The three hypotheses are
BK93 (3.3.21) (restriction maps `C(𝔄,m−1,β)` onto `C(𝔄,m,β)`), (3.3.2) with (3.3.17) (the
self-intertwining `Y B_β^× Y` and normalizer `Y 𝔎(𝔅_β)` of a simple character), and
the conjugation formula of (3.3.9). This module states each of them only as a
hypothesis and does not prove it.

Source: Bushnell and Kutzko, *The admissible dual of GL(N) via compact open
subgroups* (1993), literature ID `bushnell-kutzko-1993-admissible-dual-gln`,
SHA-256 `811693b94c03fcbc2cfe33955bc5a00b0fc0b5645ca343f8cc1a3c6511de9c73`:
(3.5.10) and the proof of (3.5.9), printed pp. 121–122 (PDF pp. 133–134); (3.3.2),
printed p. 105 (PDF p. 117); (3.3.9), printed p. 108 (PDF p. 120); (3.3.17), printed
p. 112 (PDF p. 124); (3.3.21), printed p. 114 (PDF p. 126).

## The proof

The proof follows BK's proof of (3.5.10) and supplies its details. BK reduce to the
case `x ∈ 1 + 𝔔^{r−m}𝔑(β)`, with `r = −k₀(β,𝔄)` for nonscalar `β`. The Lean proof makes this
reduction explicit and also proves the normalization of `H^m`. BK settle the case
`m ≤ ⌊r/2⌋` separately by (3.3.2); the Lean argument treats all `m` alike. Here `r` is
`finiteCriticalDepth` (`−k₀(β,𝔄)` for nonscalar `β` and `n` for scalar `β`), so
`m < r`, and `Y_k` denotes the outer factor
`simpleCharacterIntertwiningFactor` at depth `k`: the units `g` with
`g − 1 ∈ 𝔔^{r−k}𝔑_{−r}(β) + 𝔍^t(β)`, `t = ⌊(r+1)/2⌋`, and `Y_k = {1}` for scalar `β`.

1. By the restriction hypothesis, `θ₀ ∈ C(𝔄,m,β)`. By the normalizer formula at
   depth `m`, `x = yk` with `y ∈ Y_m` and `k ∈ 𝔎(𝔅_β)`. By the same formula at depth
   `m − 1`, `𝔎(𝔅_β)` and `Y_{m−1}` normalize `θ`.
2. The element `k` normalizes `H^m` and fixes `θ`. It also maps `U^m(𝔅_β)` to
   itself, because it commutes with `β` and lies in `𝔎(𝔄)`, which normalizes
   `U^m(𝔄)` (`LatticeChain.conj_mem_unitFiltration_of_mem_stabilizer`).
3. `simpleCharacterIntertwiningFactor_restriction` treats `y`. For scalar `β`,
   `y = 1`. Otherwise write `y − 1 = a + w` with `a ∈ 𝔔^{r−m}𝔑_{−r}(β)` and
   `w ∈ 𝔍^t(β)`. By `centralizerLattice_mul_adjointLattice_le_inf`,
   `a ∈ 𝔑_{−m}(β) ∩ 𝔓^{r−m}`, so `g = 1 + a` is a unit in `U^1(𝔄)`. By
   `inv_sub_one_mem_adjointLattice`, also `g⁻¹ − 1 ∈ 𝔑_{−m}(β)`. Then `y = jg` with
   `j = y g⁻¹` and `j − 1 = w g⁻¹`. Since `[β,g⁻¹] = [β,g⁻¹ − 1] ∈ 𝔓^{−m}`, the
   element `g⁻¹` lies in `𝔑_{−r}(β)`, and `mul_mem_simpleJLayer_of_mem_adjointLattice`
   gives `w g⁻¹ ∈ 𝔍^t(β)`. Hence `j ∈ Y_{m−1}`, and `j` normalizes `θ`.
4. `g` normalizes `H^m` (`mem_normalizer_simpleHGroup`). `H^m` is generated by the
   units `h ∈ U^m(𝔄)` with `h ∈ ℌ(β,𝔄)`. For such `h`, put `e = h − 1` and
   `e₁ = e + ae`. Then `ghg⁻¹ = 1 + e₁ + e₁(g⁻¹ − 1)`. Here `e ∈ ℌ ∩ 𝔓^m` with
   `m ≥ ⌊m/2⌋ + 1`, so `mul_mem_simpleHLattice_of_mem_adjointLattice` with `k = m` puts
   `ae` and then `e₁(g⁻¹ − 1)` into `ℌ` (`conj_mem_simpleHCarrier`). The same holds for
   `g⁻¹`, so `g` normalizes the generated group.
5. `g ∈ Y_m`, so the conjugation formula (3.3.9) for `θ` (index `m − 1`, outer factor
   at depth `m`) gives `θ(ghg⁻¹) = θ(h) ψ_{g⁻¹βg−β}(h)`. For `u ∈ U^m(𝔅_β)` the
   additive factor is trivial (`elementCharacter_conj_sub_eq_one`), as in BK's proof:
   `g⁻¹βg − β = g⁻¹[β,a] = [β,a] − g⁻¹a[β,a]`. With `z = u − 1 ∈ 𝔓^m` commuting with
   `β`, `tr([β,a]z) = tr(a(zβ − βz)) = 0`, while
   `g⁻¹a[β,a]z ∈ 𝔓^{(r−m)−m+m} ⊆ 𝔓^1`, where `ψ_A` is trivial.
6. For `u ∈ H^m ∩ U^m(𝔅_β)`, `xux⁻¹ = j(g(kuk⁻¹)g⁻¹)j⁻¹`. Steps 2–5 give
   `θ(xux⁻¹) = θ(g(kuk⁻¹)g⁻¹) = θ(kuk⁻¹) = θ(u)`.

The helper `character_congr` compares the values of a character at equal elements, and
`mem_normalizer_closure_of_forall` shows that an element normalizes a
generated subgroup when it and its inverse conjugate the generators into that
subgroup.
