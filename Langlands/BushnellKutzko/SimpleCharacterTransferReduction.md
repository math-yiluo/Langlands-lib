# Reduction of simple-character transfer to approximation and character restriction

The source is Bushnell and Kutzko, *The admissible dual of GL(N) via compact open subgroups* (1993), `bushnell-kutzko-1993-admissible-dual-gln`, SHA-256 `811693b94c03fcbc2cfe33955bc5a00b0fc0b5645ca343f8cc1a3c6511de9c73`, (3.6.1) and its proof on printed pp. 127–136 (PDF pp. 139–148). The reduction proves the full existence and uniqueness assertion on the literal intersection assuming the named pure approximation, subgroup, critical twist and choice inputs. The restriction proposition is used for its critical twist clause and the choice proposition for its defining-sequence independence clause.

The induction measure is the initial level of the first chain minus its finite critical cutoff. If either chain is terminal, so is the other. Indeed, a strict critical approximation on a nonterminal chain would supply one at a finite critical depth strictly below the level on the other chain. The terminal argument then gives transfer for the empty tails.

For a nonterminal pair, a common splitting basis lets a single simple approximation on the generated-field line define one critical approximation element for both chains. Choose exact defining sequences of the two approximating strata and prepend them to the original strata. Raise each character index to the maximum of its original index and half its critical depth rounded down. The raised normalized depths still agree, and the ramification divisibility gives agreement of the normalized depths for the approximating element. Lower the approximating strata to the raised character indices while preserving their defining-sequence tails. Their critical cutoffs have increased, so the induction hypothesis applies.

The critical-twist comparisons convert the transferred high character into a high restriction on the second chain. The determinant comparison and the intersection factorization extend it uniquely to the full second group with the desired agreement. Finally, independence of defining sequences carries the existence and uniqueness assertion to the given choices.
