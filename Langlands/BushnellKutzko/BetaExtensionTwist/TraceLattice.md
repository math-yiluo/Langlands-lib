# Trace duals of endomorphism lattices

Bushnell–Kutzko, *The admissible dual of GL(N) via compact open subgroups*
(1993), defines the additive dual in (1.1.4), printed p. 22, and uses the
trace-dual lattice identity in (3.1.19), printed p. 96, and Lemma (5.1.10),
printed pp. 161–162. The retained source `bushnell-kutzko-1993-admissible-dual-gln`
has SHA-256 `811693b94c03fcbc2cfe33955bc5a00b0fc0b5645ca343f8cc1a3c6511de9c73`.
Here the pairing takes values in the field and the lattice dual tests whether
trace values lie in the maximal ideal of the ring of integers. This is the
trace-pairing form of the conductor convention for the additive dual.

On the endomorphisms of a finite-dimensional vector space, rank-one maps
show that the trace pairing is nondegenerate. Cyclicity
of the trace makes `ad β` skew for the pairing, and rank-nullity identifies
its image with the annihilator of its kernel. The separation lemma for
finitely generated modules over the valuation ring of a nonarchimedean local
field gives double duality. When two lattices and their duals are finitely
generated, duality exchanges their intersection with the sum of their duals.

With a field-linear projection onto the centralizer that preserves the
radical filtration step, write a trace functional as its restriction along
the projection plus its difference from that restriction. If the original
functional annihilates a radical sublattice containing the centralizer part,
its restriction along the projection belongs to the dual of the radical,
which is the order. The difference kills the centralizer and therefore comes
from the commutator image. This gives the displayed sum decomposition under
the projection and sublattice hypotheses.
