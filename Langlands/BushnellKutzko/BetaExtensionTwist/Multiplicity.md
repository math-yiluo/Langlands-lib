# Heisenberg intertwining multiplicity one

Bushnell–Kutzko, *The admissible dual of GL(N) via compact open subgroups*
(1993), Proposition (5.1.8), printed p. 160, gives the dimension of the
common-domain intertwining space of a Heisenberg representation. Lemma
(5.1.10), printed pp. 161–162, gives the index equality used in that proof.
The retained source `bushnell-kutzko-1993-admissible-dual-gln` has SHA-256
`811693b94c03fcbc2cfe33955bc5a00b0fc0b5645ca343f8cc1a3c6511de9c73`.

The scalar-extension calculation applies to a finite-dimensional
irreducible complex representation of a subgroup `J`, acting by a
specified scalar character on `H ≤ J`, with `H` normal in `J` and the
square of the degree equal to the relative index. At an ambient element
that intertwines the representation, a nonzero intertwining map forces
the two scalar characters to agree on the common domain for `H`. The
representation on linear maps is then trivial on that subgroup of the
common domain for `J`. Its trace outside that subgroup vanishes because
at least one of the two arguments is outside `H`. The finite character
average gives the intertwining dimension times the common-domain index
as the square of the original degree.

For a simple stratum at level zero with a chosen defining sequence and
a simple character for a normalized additive character, the Heisenberg
representation theorem gives scalar action, the degree formula and
support in `J¹ Bˣ J¹`. The simple-stratum group properties give the
normality of `H¹` in `J¹`. The simple-stratum lattice and group
properties also supply the comparison of the common-domain index
with the original index at a centralizing unit. Translation on both
sides by elements of `J¹` transports both domains by the same
conjugation, preserving the index. The dimension formula and the index
bound give dimension at most one, and the intertwining hypothesis makes
the dimension positive.
