# Critical-exponent dilation for a field-line diagonal

Source context: Bushnell–Kutzko, *The admissible dual of GL(N) via compact open subgroups* (1993), `bushnell-kutzko-1993-admissible-dual-gln`, the reduction to a maximal generated subfield in the opening proof of (2.4.1)(i), printed p. 66 (PDF p. 78). The source uses (1.4.13) for critical-exponent scaling and lifts the line element by tensoring with the identity. This module proves the scaling for the Library's repeated diagonal map, by coordinates and adjoint exactness on the line.

`scaledLatticeCoordinates_of_normalizing` gives the actual coordinates for an ambient chain `A` and a chain `C` on `E`, both normalized by `Eˣ`. For any `E`-basis `w`, take `d` to be the shift of an extension uniformizer on `A`, and set `p_j = ν_A(w_j) − d ν_C(1)`. On `C` the uniformizer shift is one. For a nonzero scalar, factor it as a unit of the extension integers times a uniformizer power. The index formula on each chain then gives `ν_A(a w_j) = p_j + d ν_C(a)`. The lattice equivalence follows by rounding the index inequality, and the zero scalar belongs to both lattices. The separate relative-splitting hypothesis is needed in the comparison of endomorphisms, not in this calculation for an individual line.

`DiagonalCriticalExponentStatement` assumes that the base field `F` is a nonarchimedean local field. `[TopologicalSpace F]` and `[IsNonarchimedeanLocalField F]` are explicit binders of the definition, as in the theorem `diagonalCriticalExponent`. The proof applies adjoint exactness on the line, which needs that hypothesis. The statement also uses a nonempty relative splitting basis with `ScaledLatticeCoordinates` and `d > 0`. It assumes `NormalizingUnits C (elementAlgebra b)`: every nonzero element of `F[b]` is represented by a unit of `End_F(E)` stabilizing `C`. In this situation `F[b]` is a field by `isField_of_normalizingUnits`. The statement says that the critical exponent of `Δb` on `A` is `d` times that of `b` on `C` when finite, and is bottom when the latter is bottom.

- The repeated diagonal maps the generated algebra to the generated algebra of its image (`elementAlgebra_diagonal`). Injectivity reflects being the scalar algebra. The formula thus holds in the scalar case using the definition of the critical exponent.
- Otherwise let `k` be the largest critical index on `C`. If `Y` belongs to the ambient adjoint lattice at `d*k+1`, the commutator with `Δb` has entries at depths `k + m_jl`, where `m_jl = ⌈(1 + p_l − p_j)/d⌉`. Apply `exists_commute_sub_mem` in the line algebra to each entry of `Y`. It gives commuting entries with errors at depths `m_jl`. Reassemble them to get a commuting endomorphism of `V` differing from `Y` by ambient filtration depth one. It preserves the chain's lattices since `Y` and the error do. Thus `d*k+1`, and by antitonicity every larger index, is not critical.
- At `d*k`, lift a line adjoint element by the diagonal. If each such lifted element were a centralizer element modulo ambient depth one, taking any diagonal corner would give the analogous decomposition modulo line depth one; the corner commutes with `b` and the two depths `0` and `1` round to themselves. This contradicts criticality of `k`. The ambient greatest critical index is therefore `d*k`.

This is a scaling and coordinate result; it does not construct an approximating stratum.
