# Relative indices and a decreasing measure for transfer

The source is Bushnell and Kutzko, *The admissible dual of GL(N) via compact open subgroups* (1993), `bushnell-kutzko-1993-admissible-dual-gln`, SHA-256 `811693b94c03fcbc2cfe33955bc5a00b0fc0b5645ca343f8cc1a3c6511de9c73`, (1.2.4), (1.4.15), (2.4.1) and the transfer induction on printed pp. 25, 42, 65 and 130–134 (PDF pp. 37, 54, 77 and 142–146). These are arithmetic and depth consequences for the transfer argument.

The critical exponent is finite away from the scalar case and is at least the negative initial level for a pure stratum. Thus a nonterminal simple stratum has critical depth strictly below the initial level. A critical approximation is simple at that critical index. Its critical depth is strictly higher, with the initial level as the finite cutoff if the approximation is scalar. This gives a strictly decreasing natural measure given by the initial level minus the cutoff.

The relative-period theorem computes the base period as the ramification index times the relative period. Replacing the ramification index by a divisor multiplies the two relative periods by the same positive integer. Each new normalized depth is therefore a common further floor division of the original normalized depth, and equality is preserved.
