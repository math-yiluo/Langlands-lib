# Refinement of a lattice chain to a complete chain

The minimal-order choice in Bushnell–Kutzko (1993), (5.1.12) and
(5.2.3), uses a refinement of the relative chain to a complete chain.
To construct a refinement, take a splitting basis whose weights lie
between zero and one less than the period. Order the basis by increasing
weight, using the original indices to order equal weights. The complete
chain in these coordinates has period equal to the dimension.

Writing an original lattice index as \(eq+a\), where \(e\) is the
original period, expresses its membership conditions as a valuation
bound \(q\) and a cutoff at weight \(a\). In the ordered basis, the
cutoff is an initial segment. The corresponding lattice of the complete
chain is at index \(dq+t\), where \(d\) is its period and \(t\) is the
length of that segment. This identifies every original lattice with
one in the complete chain and retains the zeroth lattice.
