# Scalar retractions from finite-index transfer

In the representation extension argument of Bushnell–Kutzko (1993),
(5.2.4), printed pp. 167–169, the obstruction vanishes by a coprimality
argument using finite-index restriction and transfer. A related
calculation at the level of multiplicative characters gives the scalar
correction directly.

For a character on a finite-index subgroup, the transfer formula on a
central element gives the original character raised to the index. If
the character kills its intersection with a normal subgroup of the
ambient group, the transferred character kills that normal subgroup because
each factor is the value on a conjugate of a power of the element.

Given a central scalar homomorphism and two characters whose restrictions
to it are the power maps for coprime exponents, Bézout coefficients give
a product of integral powers of the characters whose restriction is the
identity. The first of the two characters is obtained by transfer from
a character equal to the identity on the scalars. The product preserves
triviality on the supplied normal subgroup.

For an intertwined character, the transfer on an element of its domain
also gives the index power. In the orbit formula each factor is the
value of the character on a conjugate of the corresponding power of the
element, and the orbit lengths sum to the index. An exponent coprime to
the index gives an inverse to the index power on these values. Applying
that inverse to the transferred character extends the original character.
