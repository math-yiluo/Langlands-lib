# Splitting the intersection of two simple-character lattices

The source is Bushnell and Kutzko, *The admissible dual of GL(N) via compact open subgroups* (1993), `bushnell-kutzko-1993-admissible-dual-gln`, SHA-256 `811693b94c03fcbc2cfe33955bc5a00b0fc0b5645ca343f8cc1a3c6511de9c73`, Lemma (3.6.10) and its proof on printed pp. 131 and 140–141 (PDF pp. 143 and 152–153). This module proves the lattice decomposition at identity on the shared endomorphism space.

Choose a simultaneous splitting basis of the two relative chains. An endomorphism over `F` determines an endomorphism over `E` by taking its values on that basis and extending `E`-linearly. On either chain, expressing a vector as a sum of its individual coordinates keeps each coordinate in the starting lattice. A nonzero coefficient shifts the index by the relative period times its uniformizer order, and the same shift applies after taking the image of the basis vector. Thus the projection preserves every filtration step of both chains. Its fixed points are the `E`-linear endomorphisms, which are the centralizer of `β` for `E = F[β]`.

Each simple-character lattice is the sum of its depth-zero centralizer lattice and a submodule contained in the endomorphism filtration at its cutoff. For an element `x` in the intersection at the given depths, apply the common projection to get a centralizer element `p(x)` at both depths. Subtracting `p(x)` preserves membership in each simple-character lattice. The difference has at least the cutoff depth in either chain since the projection fixes its centralizer summand and preserves the cutoff filtration. This yields the required simultaneous decomposition.

For the corresponding group factorization, assume that the two initial groups have their literal lattice carriers. Apply the lattice decomposition to `g−1`, obtaining a centralizer part `b` and a deeper part `y`. The unit `u = 1+b` lies in both relative filtered unit groups. Then `u⁻¹g−1 = u⁻¹y` belongs to both deeper filtration steps because `u⁻¹` preserves both chains. The literal carrier assumptions put `u⁻¹g` in both simple-character lattices, so it lies in the intersection of the two deeper character groups.
