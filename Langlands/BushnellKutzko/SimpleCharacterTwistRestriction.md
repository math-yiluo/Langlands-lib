# Twisting and adjacent restriction

Multiplication by a character followed by transport along an equality of subgroups defines `characterTwistTransport`. The inverse uses the inverse character. Pointwise twisted agreement on the common subgroup determines the transport by extensionality. When the shallower twist restricts to the deeper one, restriction commutes with the two transports and carries the restriction image equality across them.

Equality of the filtered H lattices gives equality of the character domains. Both lattices contain the identity, so a unit in the common absolute filtration lies in a character-domain generating set exactly when its difference from the identity lies in the filtered H lattice.

In the recursion of BK93 (3.2.3), at and above half the critical depth the maximum selecting the witness index is the given index. A character for the original parameter therefore has a unique untwisted witness for the chosen successor. The converse below the critical depth is an explicit premise of `simpleCharacters_twist_cons_iff_of_reverse`. At and above the critical depth the perturbation is trivial by the filtration bound and the defining condition is transport alone. These two ranges give the form of the twist used to pass adjacent restriction down the defining sequence.

For the reverse implication of BK93 (3.3.18), a unit centralizing a point of the approximation coset formally intertwines that stratum, with the point as witness. The formal intertwining neighborhood in (1.5.8) embeds into the outer factor of the character covariance formula through its mixed-lattice summand. Hence the whole formal intertwining double coset lies in the double coset to which covariance applies.

The source is Bushnell and Kutzko, *The admissible dual of GL(N) via compact open subgroups* (1993), (1.5.8), (3.2.3), (3.3.9), (3.3.18) and (3.3.21), printed pp. 44–46, 99, 108 and 112–114.
