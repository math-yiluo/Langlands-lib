# Induction for the simple-stratum lattices

C. J. Bushnell and P. C. Kutzko, *The admissible dual of GL(N) via compact open subgroups*, Annals of Mathematics Studies 129, Princeton University Press, 1993, (2.4.2) and (3.1.7)–(3.1.13), printed pp. 65–66 and 90–94 (PDF pp. 77–78 and 102–106). The registered source is `bushnell-kutzko-1993-admissible-dual-gln`.

`sequence_of_choice` extracts the list and the properties needed for this recursion from a defining-sequence choice. Every term is pure at the common level and order. At each nonterminal step, the next term is simple at the current finite critical depth and is congruent to the current term there. The terminal critical exponent is either minus the level or minus infinity. The two constructions use their published cutoffs and the layers are intersections with the same integer filtration.

For either construction with cutoff function `C`, `Sequence.properties` proves stability of the layer at degree `k ≥ C(t)` under the adjoint lattice of index `−t` when `0 ≤ t` is at most the critical depth. At a critical step of depth `r` with successor `c`, this stability for `c` shows that its layer at `C(r)` contains the mixed adjoint lattice at indices `(C(r),−r)`. In particular it absorbs the centralizer contribution at the cutoff. Above the cutoff the layers therefore equal the successor's layers. Below it the layers are sums of the corresponding centralizer steps and the successor layer at the cutoff. These formulas yield ring closure and fractional commutation. The adjoint splitting formula proves stability below the cutoff using `C(r)−C(t) ≤ r−t`. In the finite terminal case the commutator condition at index `−n` is automatic. In the scalar case each nonnegative layer is the full filtration step.

For the cross-products, the two cutoffs satisfy `C_H(r) ≤ C_J(r)+1` and `C_H(r) ≤ 2C_J(r)` at positive `r`. Fractional transport applies to the successor's J-layer, and the two inequalities put the products in its required H-layer.

For comparison at an arbitrary integer depth `t` below finite critical depth `r`, the induction first identifies the two successor layers at `C(r)` by comparing their simple parameters at depth `r`. At `k ≤ C(r)`, the mixed lattice at indices `(k,−r+C(r)−k)` is equal on the two equivalent parameters whenever `k ≥ t+C(r)−r`. Its splitting identifies the two centralizer summands after adding the common successor layer. Above the cutoff the comparison follows from the common layer. `Sequence.approximation_eq` uses the same comparison on successors to allow any simple critical approximation with any defining-sequence choice.
