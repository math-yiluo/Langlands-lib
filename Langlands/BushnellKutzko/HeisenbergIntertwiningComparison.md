# Intertwining by the trace of a Heisenberg representation

For a representation of an ambient subgroup `J` with scalar action on `H ≤ J` and trace zero on `J \ H`, the space of self-intertwining maps at `x` is nonzero exactly when the two scalar characters agree on `x⁻¹ H x ∩ H`. The representation is finite-dimensional and nonzero, and it is trivial on a normal finite-index subgroup of `J` contained in `H`.

In one direction a nonzero intertwining map equates scalars on its nonzero image. In the other direction the product of traces on `x⁻¹ J x ∩ J` vanishes outside `x⁻¹ H x ∩ H` and equals the square of the representation dimension on that subgroup. The normal subgroup gives a common finite quotient on which the trace average computes a positive dimension. The calculation uses the intertwining direction of `RepresentationIntertwining`.

This gives the part of Bushnell–Kutzko (1993), Proposition (5.1.1), printed p. 158 (PDF p. 170), that compares the intertwining sets. The book uses (4.1.5), printed p. 146 (PDF p. 158), for this step.
