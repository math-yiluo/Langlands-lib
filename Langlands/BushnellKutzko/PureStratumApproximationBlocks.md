# Block filtrations for the reduction to one field line

Source context: Bushnell–Kutzko, *The admissible dual of GL(N) via compact open subgroups* (1993), `bushnell-kutzko-1993-admissible-dual-gln`, (1.2.8), printed p. 26 (PDF p. 38), and the reduction to an `F[β]`-line in the opening proof of (2.4.1)(i), printed p. 66 (PDF p. 78). The block comparison is an internal coordinate lemma for that reduction, not a transcription of a standalone assertion in the source.

Let `A` be a chain on an `E`-vector space `V` and `C` a chain on `E`, both as `F`-spaces. Let the finite `E`-basis `w` split every lattice of `A` (`RelSplits`). The hypothesis `ScaledLatticeCoordinates` says that `a ⋅ w_j` is in `A_i` exactly when `a` is in `C_{⌈(i − p_j)/d⌉}`, for a common positive integer scale `d` and offsets `p_j`. This module does not construct these coordinates.

`BlockFiltrationStatement` states that an endomorphism `X` is in the filtration of `A` at depth `s` exactly when each entry `π_j X ι_l` is in the filtration of `C` at depth `⌈(s + p_l − p_j)/d⌉`. The proof tests a vector from `C_i` in the `l`-th source line of `A_{p_l + d i}` and takes the `j`-th projected component after applying `X`. Conversely, split the input into its basis components and apply the entry bounds. In the latter direction the two rounded bounds add to at least the rounded bound for `i + s − p_j`. These arithmetic steps use `ceilDiv_le_iff`, which characterizes the rounding by multiplication by the positive `d`.

`assembleBlocks` constructs the endomorphism from arbitrary entries; `blockEntry_assembleBlocks` and `eq_of_blockEntry_eq` verify reconstruction and uniqueness by evaluation on the relative basis lines. For the Library's repeated diagonal `Δ`, `blockEntry_commutator` identifies each entry of `[Δb, X]` with `[b, π_j X ι_l]`. The diagonal has equal entries on the diagonal and zero entries elsewhere. For a nonempty basis, `diagonal_mem_endFiltration_iff` therefore reads its membership in the ambient filtration at depth `s` as membership in the line filtration at depth `⌈s/d⌉`.
