# Intertwining layer families along the orbits of two shifting units

The module `Langlands.BushnellKutzko.LatticeChainLayerIntertwining` constructs, from
automorphisms of finitely many successive quotients of a lattice chain, a unit of the order which
conjugates one shifting unit to another modulo the next filtration step. It works for a lattice
chain `𝓛 = (L_i)` over a commutative ring `O` in a vector space over a field `K`, as in
`Langlands.BushnellKutzko.LatticeChain`; the lifting step assumes that `O` is a discrete
valuation ring with fraction field `K`. Layer maps are `LatticeChain.stabilizerLayerEquiv`.

## Arithmetic of the orbits

For integers `e > 0` and `n`, put `g = gcd(e, n)`.

- `exists_orbitRep`: every integer `i` is `r + (a e − c n)` with `0 ≤ r < g`
  (Bézout, through `Int.gcd_eq_gcd_ab`).
- `orbitRep_unique`: in two such representations of one integer the residues `r` agree, and the
  exponent pairs differ by `k (n/g, e/g)` for an integer `k`, because `e/g` and `n/g` are coprime.
- `zpow_mul_zpow_add`: for commuting `P`, `B` in a group,
  `P^{a+a'} B^{c+c'} = (P^a B^c)(P^{a'} B^{c'})`.

## Layer maps

- `stabilizerValuationHom` is the stabilizer valuation as a homomorphism to `Multiplicative ℤ`, and
  `stabilizerValuation_zpow` gives the shift of `g^k`.
- `stabilizerLayerEquiv_congr`: equal stabilizer units have equal layer maps.
- `layerEquiv_zpow_intertwine`: if an automorphism `ψ` of the layer `L_r/L_{r+1}` satisfies
  `ψ ∘ Y₂ = Y₁ ∘ ψ` for two shift-zero units, it satisfies the same relation for all their integer
  powers (induction on the exponent, with `stabilizerLayerEquiv_mul_apply` and
  `stabilizerLayerEquiv_cancel_apply`).
- `layerAction_mul` and `layerAction_one`: the layer action of order elements is multiplicative.

## The orbit construction

Fix stabilizer units `P` of shift `e > 0` and `B₁`, `B₂` of shift `-n`, with `P` commuting with
each `B_j`, and put `Y_j = P^{n/g} B_j^{e/g}`; `stabilizerValuation_orbitUnit` shows that `Y_j` has
shift zero, and `monomial_orbit_zpow` that
`P^{a + k n/g} B^{c + k e/g} = (P^a B^c) Y^k`.

- `orbitConj P B₁ B₂ ψ r a c i` is the automorphism of `L_i/L_{i+1}`, for `i = r + a e − c n`,
  obtained from `ψ_r` by applying the inverse of the layer map of `P^a B₂^c` and then the layer map
  of `P^a B₁^c`.
- `orbitConj_eq`: if each `ψ_r` satisfies `ψ_r ∘ Y₂ = Y₁ ∘ ψ_r`, then `orbitConj` does not depend
  on the representation `(r, a, c)` with `0 ≤ r < g`. By `orbitRep_unique` two representations
  differ by `k (n/g, e/g)`, the two monomials differ by `Y_j^k`, and `layerEquiv_zpow_intertwine`
  moves `ψ_r` past `Y^k`.
- `orbitConj_transport`: composing with the layer maps of a further monomial `P^{a₀} B^{c₀}` adds
  `(a₀, c₀)` to the exponents.
- `exists_layer_intertwiner`: with representatives chosen by `exists_orbitRep`, the family
  `φ_i = orbitConj … i` commutes with the layer maps of `P` and satisfies
  `B₁ ∘ φ_i = φ_{i−n} ∘ B₂` on the layers. Both follow from `orbitConj_transport` with
  `(a₀, c₀) = (1, 0)` and `(0, 1)` together with `orbitConj_eq`.

## Lifting to a unit

`exists_unitFiltration_conj_sub_mem`: let `π` be a uniformizer of `O`, let `B₁`, `B₂` shift the
chain by `-n`, and let `φ_i` be layer automorphisms which commute with
`uniformizerStabilizer π` and satisfy `B₁ ∘ φ_i = φ_{i−n} ∘ B₂`. Then some
`u ∈ unitFiltration 0` satisfies `u⁻¹ B₁ u − B₂ ∈ endFiltration (1 − n)`.

The proof lifts the families `φ_i` and `φ_i⁻¹` to order elements `x` and `x'`
(`exists_order_lift_layers`). The products `x x'` and `x' x` act as the identity on every layer,
so they lie in `1 + 𝔓` (`layerAction_eq_iff`) and are units of `U^1(𝔄)`
(`exists_mem_unitFiltration`). Hence `x` is a unit whose inverse `x' (x x')⁻¹` lies in the order.
On each layer the classes of `B₁ x v` and `x B₂ v` agree, so `B₁ x − x B₂ ∈ 𝔓^{1−n}`, and
`u⁻¹ B₁ u − B₂ = u⁻¹ (B₁ x − x B₂)` lies in `𝔓^{1−n}` by `mul_mem_endFiltration`.

## Source

This is the block-matrix normal form of Bushnell–Kutzko, *The admissible dual of GL(N) via
compact open subgroups* (1993), (2.5.11), printed pp. 82–83 (PDF pp. 94–95), written on the
successive quotients `L_i/L_{i+1}` instead of in a basis: conjugation by a diagonal unit moves the
blocks of `b` along the cosets of `ℤ/eℤ` modulo the subgroup generated by `n`, leaving one block
per coset, which is then put in normal form. It is used in the first case of the proof of
(2.6.1), printed pp. 83–84 (PDF pp. 95–96). Source `bushnell-kutzko-1993-admissible-dual-gln`,
