# Extracting an extension in the induced degree

The degree comparison in Bushnell–Kutzko (1993), (5.1.14)–(5.1.17) and
(5.2.5), gives an extension from the restriction of an induced
representation. The argument here applies to an ambient representation
with a split copy of the original representation of a subgroup \(H\).
Assume its self-intertwining in the ambient group is contained in an
intermediate subgroup \(A\) of finite index. Assume also that the ambient
degree is the degree of the original representation times that index.

Projecting a translated copy back to the original space defines an
intertwiner on the common subgroup. The projection therefore vanishes on
translates outside \(A\). For representatives of the left cosets of \(A\),
the projections onto the translated copies give a left inverse to their
sum. The degree equality makes the sum an isomorphism. The identity
coset component is stable under \(A\), and its action extends the
original one on \(H\). Taking each ambient vector to the function that
projects its translates produces the equivalence with coinduction.
The same projections show that all maps from the original representation
to the restricted ambient representation land in the identity component.
