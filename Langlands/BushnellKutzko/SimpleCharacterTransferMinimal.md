# Simple-character domains for a terminal element

The source is Bushnell and Kutzko, *The admissible dual of GL(N) via compact open subgroups* (1993), `bushnell-kutzko-1993-admissible-dual-gln`, SHA-256 `811693b94c03fcbc2cfe33955bc5a00b0fc0b5645ca343f8cc1a3c6511de9c73`, (3.1.7) and (3.1.15) on printed pp. 90 and 95 (PDF pp. 102 and 107). The domain calculation supplies the terminal group used in the transfer proof on printed pp. 131–133 (PDF pp. 143–145).

At positive depth `k`, with `c = ⌊n/2⌋ + 1`, `simpleHGroup_nil_eq_sup` identifies the terminal domain with the subgroup generated by the relative filtered group at `k` and the ambient filtered group at `max(k,c)`. If `k ≥ c`, the ambient group at `k` already contains the defining carrier. If `k < c`, decompose a carrier element as `g = b + y` with `b` in the centralizer order and `y` of filtration depth at least `c`. Then `b−1` lies in filtration depth `k`, so `b` is a relative filtered unit at `k`. The remaining factor `b⁻¹g` is an ambient filtered unit at `c`. Both generating groups belong to the terminal domain.

The relative group normalizes the deeper ambient group by the hereditary-order normalizer theorem. Thus every element of the generated group is a product `u v` from the two groups in that order. The term `u` is in the centralizer order and `uv−u` is in filtration depth at least `c`, proving `simpleHGroup_nil_mem_lattice`.
