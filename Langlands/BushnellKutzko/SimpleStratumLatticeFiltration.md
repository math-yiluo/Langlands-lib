# Fractional centralizers and mixed adjoint lattices

C. J. Bushnell and P. C. Kutzko, *The admissible dual of GL(N) via compact open subgroups*, Annals of Mathematics Studies 129, Princeton University Press, 1993. The relevant identities are (1.4.8)–(1.4.11) and (3.1.2)–(3.1.4), printed pp. 37–39 and 89 (PDF pp. 49–51 and 101). The registered source is `bushnell-kutzko-1993-admissible-dual-gln`.

Write `P(m)` for the lattice-chain filtration, `Q_b(m)` for its intersection with the centralizer of `b`, and `M_b(m,j)` for the elements of `P(m)` whose commutator with `b` lies in `P(m+j)`. `Parameter` collects the facts used in the lattice induction. Purity gives the level bound, the identity in the product of opposite centralizer steps, and the critical-index bounds in the nonscalar case. These facts transport along equality of orders.

Left and right multiplication of `M_b(m,j)` by `Q_b(s)` give `M_b(m+s,j)`. The product inclusion follows from the commutator identity, and the identity in the opposite-step product gives the reverse inclusion. Above a finite critical exponent `j`, the definition of that exponent gives the first split at depth zero. Transport by `Q_b(m)` and iteration give `M_b(m,j+d) = Q_b(m) + M_b(m+d,j)` for `d ≥ 0`, the mixed-depth version of (1.4.9).

If `c-b` lies in `P(-t)`, the mixed adjoint lattices of `b` and `c` coincide at second indices at most `-t`. At `j = -t`, this equality identifies their left and right fractional products on any lattice stable under the adjoint lattice `M_c(0,-t)`. This is the transport used in the inductive argument. The relative-normalizer lemma applies fractional commutation to the two opposite depths of a unit and its inverse.
