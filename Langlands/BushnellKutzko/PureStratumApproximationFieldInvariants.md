# Field-invariant comparison for embeddings

Source context: Bushnell–Kutzko, *The admissible dual of GL(N) via compact open subgroups* (1993), `bushnell-kutzko-1993-admissible-dual-gln`, opening proof of (2.4.1)(i), printed p. 66 (PDF p. 78), and the field-tower computation on printed p. 71 (PDF p. 83), before (2.4.7). This is a derived comparison for the Library's constructed invariants, not a new approximation assertion.

`SubfieldInvariantsEmbeddingStatement` says that for finite extensions `E/F` and `K/F` of a nonarchimedean local field, an `F`-algebra embedding `E → K` implies `e(E|F) ∣ e(K|F)` and `f(E|F) ∣ f(K|F)`. The types may live in independent universes. `[TopologicalSpace F]`, `[IsNonarchimedeanLocalField F]`, `[FiniteDimensional F E]` and `[FiniteDimensional F K]` are explicit binders of the definition, matching the theorem `subfieldInvariantsEmbedding`. The local-field hypothesis is needed because otherwise the integral closures need not be local. Review `6c44aea0-50b3-4a2a-b3d2-db940da97a31` gives an example: `F = F₅(t)` with the `t`-adic valuation, `E = F₅(u)` with `t = u(u−1)`, and `K = F₅(v)` with `u = v²`. Then two primes lie over `t` in the integral closure for `E` and three in that for `K`, all with residue field `F₅`. The Library's residue lengths are therefore 2 and 3, and the divisibility fails. `subfieldInvariants_of_algEquiv` gets both equalities by applying these divisibilities to an isomorphism and its inverse.

The proof restricts the embedding to the integral closures of `𝒪[F]`, making the larger integer ring an integral algebra over the smaller one. The maximal ideal of the larger ring lies over the maximal ideal of the smaller one by integrality and locality. The resulting module is torsion-free by injectivity and is flat over the DVR. This gives the two divisibilities by Mathlib's `Ideal.ramificationIdx_below_dvd` and `Ideal.inertiaDeg_below_dvd`. The ramification index is already the corresponding ideal invariant after identifying the Jacobson radical with the maximal ideal. For the residue degree, the same length comparison as in `StratumApproximationReduction` identifies the length of the residue field over the base integers with its dimension over the base residue field, hence with inertia degree. This proof requires no separability hypothesis.
