# The level-zero simple character double coset

The remark after Bushnell–Kutzko (1993), (3.3.2), printed p. 105 (PDF p. 117), describes the low-depth intertwining set using the `J` filtration. At depth zero the resulting double coset is `J^1 B_β^× J^1` as used in (5.1.1), printed p. 158 (PDF p. 170).

The proof uses adjoint exactness at the critical depth of each term of the defining sequence. An integral endomorphism with integral commutator is a sum of a centralizer element and a term of critical depth. Replacing the parameter with the next term preserves integrality of the latter term's commutator. This proves by induction that the integral adjoint preimage lies in the `J` lattice. Products from the relative centralizer lattice and the critical adjoint lattice have integral commutator and lie at the cutoff depth. Consequently the positive-depth outer factor in (3.3.2) is `J` at the cutoff. The depth-one group is the product of its relative unit subgroup and the cutoff group by (3.1.15), printed p. 95 (PDF p. 107). Inverting this product on the left and using the product as written on the right absorbs both relative unit factors into the centralizer. For a scalar parameter the centralizer is the full ambient group.
