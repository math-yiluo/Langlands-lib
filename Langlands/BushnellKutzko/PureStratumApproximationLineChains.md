# Chain uniqueness on one field line

Source context: Bushnell–Kutzko, *The admissible dual of GL(N) via compact open subgroups* (1993), `bushnell-kutzko-1993-admissible-dual-gln`, the reduction to a maximal generated subfield in the opening proof of (2.4.1)(i), printed p. 66 (PDF p. 78). It describes the unique `E`-normalized hereditary order in `End_F(E)` by the chain of powers of the maximal ideal. `LineChainTranslationStatement` gives the uniqueness comparison for the Library's indexed-chain model: two chains on a common `E`-line with `Eˣ` acting by chain stabilizers coincide up to an integer translation. Here `F` is a nonarchimedean local field, `E/F` is finite, and being an `E`-line is expressed by a nonzero vector whose `E`-multiples exhaust the space. These hypotheses are explicit binders of the definition: `[TopologicalSpace F]`, `[IsNonarchimedeanLocalField F]` and `[FiniteDimensional F E]` appear in its signature, which is the same as that of the theorem `lineChainTranslation`. The proof needs the local-field hypothesis to make the integer ring of `E` a discrete valuation ring with a uniformizer. Without it the proposition would admit incomplete valued base fields such as `ℚ` with a `p`-adic valuation.

`index_smul_unit` first computes the lattice index of a nonzero vector multiplied by a unit of a normalizing field: it increases by the unit's chain shift. The proof translates the lattice membership equivalence and uses the characterization of the index by all such memberships. This lemma does not require the ambient space to be an `E`-line.

On a line, `uniformizerShift_eq_one_of_cyclic` uses strictness to find a vector at index one more than the index of a fixed generator. The generator is cyclic, so the vector is a nonzero field multiple of it. Factoring that scalar as an integer-ring unit times a uniformizer power shows that the index difference is a multiple of the uniformizer shift. The shift is positive and divides one, so it equals one.

`lineChainTranslation` now writes each nonzero vector as a scalar multiple of a common generator. The two chains give the same index increment, namely the uniformizer exponent of that scalar. Thus all indices differ by the same integer, the difference of the generator's two indices. The lattice membership characterization gives the claimed equalities. This compares two supplied normalized chains; it does not construct one or prove a simple-approximation result.
