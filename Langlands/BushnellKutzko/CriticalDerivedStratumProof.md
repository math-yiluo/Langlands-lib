# The critical derived stratum theorem

`Langlands.BushnellKutzko.criticalDerivedStratum` proves the unchanged Library proposition `Langlands.BushnellKutzko.CriticalDerivedStratumStatement`, with the field and vector space in independent universes. It uses the given equivalent simple stratum and an arbitrary tame corestriction for its actual generated field. The derived chain has the same underlying lattices as the ambient chain, and the simple witness has exactly the same chain and integers as the derived stratum.

Source: Bushnell–Kutzko, *The admissible dual of GL(N) via compact open subgroups* (1993), registered as `bushnell-kutzko-1993-admissible-dual-gln`, (2.4.1)(iii), printed p. 65 (PDF p. 77); source proof printed pp. 73–75 (PDF pp. 85–87). The Lean proof uses a different reduction and a cyclic lift informed by the graded calculations of §2.5 (printed pp. 77–85). It does not assume (2.4.1)(i), (2.4.1)(ii)(c), or the aggregate approximation theorem.

## Implemented proof

Let `β = S.b`, `γ = T.b`, `E = F[γ]`, `K = F[β]`, and `d = −T.r`. After transporting along the ambient chain and depth equalities, equivalence gives `β − γ ∈ P^d`. For a supplied tame corestriction `s`, write `a = s(β − γ)` in `End_E(V)`.

1. **Identify the relevant scalar symbols.** `CriticalDerivedReduction` uses filtered corestriction and adjoint exactness to compare the leading centralizers on the two sides. It proves that `a` has exact degree `d` and that its ambient symbol is represented by a `K`-scalar. It also proves that every homogeneous `E`-scalar symbol can be matched by a `K`-scalar. These are statements about symbols, not inclusion of the actual fields or commutation of `a` with `K`. The argument is implemented in `CriticalDerivedCentralizer`, `CriticalDerivedScalars`, and `CriticalDerivedReduction`.
2. **Normalize the derived operator.** On the extension chain over `E`, the first symbol match shows that `a` is a shifting unit `A`. Let `π` be a uniformizer acting on the chain and let `e` be its period. Write `g = gcd(e,d)`, `e = hg`, `d = kg`, and choose integers `u,v` with `uk+vh=1`. Then `z = A^h π^(-k)` has degree zero and `τ = A^u π^v` has degree `g`. The matches of `A` and `π` give an integral scalar match for `z` with nonzero residue in the residue field of `K`. The degree-zero scalar matches define a map from the residue field of `E` to that of `K`. Lifting the minimal polynomial of the matched residue gives a monic polynomial with unit constant coefficient and irreducible reduction; evaluated at `z`, it lies in filtration degree one. The symbol, residue, polynomial, and normalization modules implement these steps without assuming an embedding `E → K`.
3. **Lift the two normalized symbols.** `CriticalDerivedCyclicBasis` builds cyclic bases on the chain layers, then lifts them to a splitting basis. The cyclic algebra and action modules construct commuting operators `U` and `T'` with `T'^h = π U^u`, matching the symbols of `z` and `τ`. The operator `c = T'^k U^v` therefore matches `A` in degree `d`. The leading-commutation and cyclic-row modules show that each nonzero operator in the model algebra acts by a lattice shift, and that leading commutation with the two generators implies leading commutation with every such operator.
4. **Verify simplicity on the actual derived chain.** `CriticalDerivedSimpleLift` applies the cyclic-row properties to the subalgebra generated by `c`: it is a field with units normalizing the chain. The power and carry identities recover `U` and `T'` from `c` and `π`, so a depth-zero endomorphism commuting with `c` in degree `d` commutes with all the field units on the corresponding layers. The Library's layer-lifting theorem produces a centralizer lift, proving `k₀(c) ≤ d`. The entry module transfers the valuation, stabilizer, and critical exponent back to the extension chain over the integral closure of the base integer ring. Both endomorphisms have valuation `d`, their difference lies in degree `d+1`, and `k₀(c) ≤ d < −(T.r−1)`. This gives the two strata and the required equivalence at parameters `T.r, T.r−1`.
