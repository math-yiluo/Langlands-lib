# The equal-depth calculation for evaluated simple-character commutators

The equal-depth calculation combines the recursive definition of simple characters in Bushnell–Kutzko (1993), (3.2.1)–(3.2.3), the mixed calculation (3.2.8), and the cutoff argument of (3.2.12) and (3.4.1), printed pp. 98–103 and 115–116 (PDF pp. 110–115 and 127–128). `SimpleStratum.simpleCharacter_commutator_eq_adjointPair` takes the lattice and subgroup properties and `SimpleCharacterRelativeCommutatorLaws`. Fix a nonarchimedean local field, a simple stratum `[𝔄,n,0,β]`, a defining sequence starting at `β`, and an additive character of conductor `𝔭_F`. Let `d` be the finite integer critical depth, with value `n` for scalar `β`. For `θ ∈ C(𝔄,i,β)` with `i < d`, the theorem evaluates a commutator of two `J^k` units for `k ≥ 1` and `i+1 ≤ 2k`. Given a witness that the commutator lies in `H^(i+1)`, its value is the additive adjoint pairing on the two differences from one.

`SimpleCharacterRelativeCommutatorLaws` supplies three inputs. Relative filtration units normalize `θ`. Commutators of centralizer units in the character domain have value one. If `k` is below the `J` cutoff `j = ⌊(d+1)/2⌋`, the same holds for a commutator of a relative unit at depth `k` and a `J^j` unit, with the same bounds `k ≥ 1` and `i+1 ≤ 2k`.

For a scalar parameter both units centralize `β`, and the determinant identity and additive annihilator calculation give value one on each side. For a nonscalar parameter at or above the cutoff, the commutator reaches the higher domain used in the character definition. The terminal term is evaluated by the trace-character expansion. At a nonterminal term the high `J` lattices agree with those of the successor, and `simpleJGroup_mem_iff_of_pos` gives equality of the corresponding groups. The defining relation evaluates the commutator by the next character and a depth-controlled trace twist. Their two adjoint products combine by addition of the parameters.

Below the cutoff, write each unit as a relative unit times a unit at the cutoff. The relative commutators have value one and relative conjugation preserves the character, so the value is the commutator evaluation on the two cutoff factors. In additive coordinates the difference between a product and its cutoff factor lies in the `H` layer by the relative multiplication law. The additive annihilator calculation then identifies the resulting value with the adjoint test on the original two units.
