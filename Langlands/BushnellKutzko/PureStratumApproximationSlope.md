# Normalized slope of a pure coset

Source context: Bushnell–Kutzko, *The admissible dual of GL(N) via compact open subgroups* (1993), `bushnell-kutzko-1993-admissible-dual-gln`, the paragraph after (2.4.3), printed p. 67 (PDF p. 79). It observes that congruence to a simple stratum on another chain forces the common element to normalize that chain, with the same normalized initial level. `PureCosetSlopeStatement` is the comparison of initial levels in the chain model, for two pure strata whose cosets share an element. The comparison `n_S/e_S = n_T/e_T` is written as an equality of integer cross-products.

A nonzero pure generator is a chain-stabilizing unit whose shift is its valuation `−n`. An element of its coset differs at depth at least `1−n`. Multiplying by the inverse of the generator therefore gives an endomorphism differing from the identity by depth one. The unit-filtration existence theorem makes it a unit fixing each lattice. Thus the coset element is a unit realizing the same shift. This proves `exists_stabilizer_of_mem_pure_coset`.

For the normalized comparison, `finrank_mul_le_period_mul_ordOf_det` extends the determinant bound of the Library's `CompactLevelBound` from a coordinate space to the actual `V`. Its proof scales the period power of an endomorphism of depth `j` by the uniformizer power `−j`. The result preserves the chain and has integral determinant in a basis extending an integer lattice basis. The determinant bound applied to a shifting unit and its inverse gives equality: `dim_F V * shift = period * ord(det)`. Two shifts of the same coset element consequently have the same normalized ratio. The dimension is positive because the chains are strict.

The module also provides basic order integrality: depth zero is the finitely generated integer subalgebra `A.order`, so `IsIntegral.of_mem_of_fg` makes its elements integral over the base integers. The uniformizer translation formula works for all integer powers. The comparison does not require nonnegative stratum levels or produce an approximating stratum.
