# Transporting a `β`-extension along nested orders

Module: `Langlands.BushnellKutzko.NestedBetaExtensionInductionStatement`.

Source: C. J. Bushnell and P. C. Kutzko, *The admissible dual of GL(N) via compact open
subgroups* (1993), Proposition (5.2.5), printed p. 169 (PDF p. 181): the existence of `κ` with
property (i) (`κ | J¹ = η`) and the displayed equivalence (iii) of the representations induced to
`U(𝔅) U¹(𝔄)`. The notation is that of (5.1.12)–(5.1.13), printed pp. 162–163.

## Statement

`NestedBetaExtensionInductionStatement` quantifies over the following data:
- a nonarchimedean local field `F` and an `F`-vector space `V`, each in its own universe;
- an additive character `ψ_F` with the conductor fixed by `IsBKAdditiveCharacter`;
- simple strata `[𝔄₁, n₁, 0, β]` and `[𝔄₂, n₂, 0, β]` in `A = End_F(V)` with the same element
  `β`, such that every lattice of the chain of `𝔄₁` is a lattice of the chain of `𝔄₂`;
- defining-sequence choices for both strata.

Write `E = F[β]`, `B` for the centralizer of `β`, and `𝔅ᵢ = 𝔄ᵢ ∩ B`. The refinement hypothesis
means that `𝔄₂ ⊆ 𝔄₁`, so `𝔅₂ ⊆ 𝔅₁`. This is the situation `𝔄_M ⊇ 𝔄` of BK93 (5.1.12), with
`𝔄₁` in the role of `𝔄_M`.

Further data:
- simple characters `θᵢ ∈ C(𝔄ᵢ, 0, β)` taking the same values on `H¹(β, 𝔄₁) ∩ H¹(β, 𝔄₂)`. By
  `SimpleCharacterTransferStatement` (BK93 (3.6.1)), this relation determines `θ₂` from `θ₁`.
- representations `ηᵢ` of `J¹(β, 𝔄ᵢ)` lying over `θᵢ` (`IsHeisenbergRepresentation`), on spaces
  in independent universes;
- a `β`-extension `κ₁` of `η₁` (`IsBetaExtension`, BK93 (5.2.1)), on a space in a third
  universe.

The conclusion: there is a representation `κ₂` of `J(β, 𝔄₂)` on the space of `η₂` whose
restriction to `J¹(β, 𝔄₂)` equals `η₂`. Moreover, let `Γ` be the subgroup generated by
`U(𝔅₂) = B^× ∩ U(𝔄₂)` and `U¹(𝔄₂)`, and let `U(𝔅₂) J¹(β, 𝔄₁)` be the subgroup generated by
`U(𝔅₂)` and `J¹(β, 𝔄₁)`. Then the representation of `Γ` coinduced from the restriction of `κ₁`
to `U(𝔅₂) J¹(β, 𝔄₁)` is equivalent to the representation coinduced from `κ₂`.

The inclusions `U(𝔅₂) J¹(β, 𝔄₁) ≤ J(β, 𝔄₁)`, `U(𝔅₂) J¹(β, 𝔄₁) ≤ Γ` and `J(β, 𝔄₂) ≤ Γ` hold
in this situation. The statement quantifies over proofs of them, so that the coinduced
representations can be written. The subgroups are of finite index, so coinduction agrees with
the compact induction of the source.

## Relation to the source

BK93 (5.2.5) has three parts:
- (i) `κ | J¹ = η`;
- (ii) `κ` and `κ_M | U(𝔅) J¹_M` induce equivalent irreducible representations of `U(𝔄)`;
- (iii) the representations of `U(𝔅) U¹(𝔄)` induced from `κ` and from `κ_M | U(𝔅) J¹_M` are
  equivalent.

The statement here is a nested-order version of the existence of `κ` with (i) and (iii). Its
conclusion is a representation `κ₂` extending `η₂` together with the coinduction equivalence over
`Γ = U(𝔅₂) U¹(𝔄₂)`. It contains neither part (ii), which concerns irreducible induction to all of
`U(𝔄₂)`, nor the uniqueness of `κ` nor the irreducibility of the induced representations. That
`κ₂` is again a `β`-extension is BK93 (5.2.7). It is not part of this statement.

In the source, `𝔅₁ = 𝔅_M` is a maximal hereditary `𝔬_E`-order and `κ₁ = κ_M` is the
representation of (5.2.4). That proposition gives `κ_M | U¹(𝔅_m) J¹_M = η̃_M`, where `η̃_M` is
the extension in (5.1.14). Proposition (5.1.18) identifies the further restriction to
`U¹(𝔅) J¹_M` with `η̂_M` of (5.1.16). The induction equivalence in (5.1.16) is the one used to
compare the restrictions in the proof of (5.2.5). Proposition (5.2.7) subsequently proves full
`B^×`-intertwining of `κ_M`.

For the nested pair `𝔄₁ ⊇ 𝔄₂`, the analogue of `η̂_M` is the extension `η̂` on
`U¹(𝔅₂) J¹₁` characterized by its induction to `U¹(𝔄₂)`, which is equivalent to the induction
of `η₂`. This is (5.1.14) with `𝔅₂` as the lower order. In that setting (5.1.19) gives
`B^×`-intertwining of `η̂`. The predicate `IsBetaExtension` supplies full `B^×`-intertwining
for the arbitrary `κ₁` in the statement. After identifying their restrictions to `J¹₁` with
`η₁`, the two extensions `κ₁ | U¹(𝔅₂) J¹₁` and `η̂` differ by a character `χ` trivial on `J¹₁`.
Their intertwiners restrict to the same one-dimensional space for `η₁` by (5.1.8), forcing
`χ(y x y⁻¹) = χ(x)` whenever both sides are defined.

Such a character is trivial. It is trivial on `U¹(𝔅₁) ≤ J¹₁`. On each layer of the filtration of
`U¹(𝔅₂)`, an element is a product of elementary matrices in a basis adapted to both chains:
- diagonal elementary matrices lie in `U¹(𝔅₁)`;
- an off-diagonal one is moved one layer deeper by conjugation with a diagonal element having a
  single uniformizer entry.

Sufficiently deep layers lie in `U¹(𝔅₁)`. Descending through the layers shows that `χ` is
trivial and identifies the restriction of `κ₁` with `η̂`.
