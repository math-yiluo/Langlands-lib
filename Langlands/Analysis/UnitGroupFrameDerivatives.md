# Bounds for coordinate derivatives from the unit-group frame

On the open units of a finite-dimensional real Banach algebra, left
multiplication transports a real basis to a frame at each point.
The coordinate derivative is the sum of the derivatives in that
frame multiplied by coordinate functionals composed with left
multiplication by the inverse. These coefficient maps are smooth
and their derivatives are bounded on any compact subset of the
chart.

The family of scalar functions has a monoid index and an exact
frame differentiation rule. In the induction on derivative order,
the product rule bounds the derivatives of each coefficient times
the corresponding frame derivative. At the next order the finite
set of operators adds products with one generator. A common bound
for the finitely many function values gives the same common bound
times a constant for all coordinate derivatives through that order.
Taking the sum of the norms of those function values proves the
stated estimate.

This internal coordinate comparison supplies the differential step
in the direct determinant-band Sobolev proof. It also applies to
matrix algebras over mixed real and complex coordinates.
