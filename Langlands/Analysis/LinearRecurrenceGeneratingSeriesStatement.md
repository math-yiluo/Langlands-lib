# A uniform polynomial denominator for linear recurrence generating series

Authors: Worker 740.

## Statement

`Langlands.Analysis.LinearRecurrenceGeneratingSeriesStatement` is a closed
`Prop` definition with no parameters and no universe parameters. It uses
Mathlib's `LinearRecurrence ℂ` and `LinearRecurrence.IsSolution` from
`Mathlib/Algebra/LinearRecurrence.lean` without change.

Recall that `E : LinearRecurrence ℂ` consists of an order `E.order : ℕ`
and coefficients `E.coeffs : Fin E.order → ℂ`. A sequence `a : ℕ → ℂ`
satisfies `E.IsSolution a` exactly when

```text
a (n + E.order) = ∑ i : Fin E.order, E.coeffs i * a (n + i)   for every n : ℕ.
```

For every `E`, the statement introduces locally (by `let`, with no new
declaration) the polynomial

```text
Q = 1 - ∑ i : Fin E.order, C (E.coeffs i) * X ^ (E.order - i.val)
```

in `Polynomial ℂ`. It then asserts the conjunction of:

1. `Q.coeff 0 = 1`;
2. there is a real number `ρ` with `0 < ρ` such that for every
   `a : ℕ → ℂ` with `E.IsSolution a` there is a polynomial `A : Polynomial ℂ`
   such that for every `z : ℂ` with `‖z‖ < ρ`:
   - `Summable (fun n : ℕ => a n * z ^ n)`;
   - `Q.eval z ≠ 0`;
   - `∑' n : ℕ, a n * z ^ n = A.eval z / Q.eval z`.

Here `‖z‖` is the usual absolute value on `ℂ`, `Summable` and `∑'` are
Mathlib's unconditional summability and sum in `ℂ`, and `Polynomial.eval`
is ordinary evaluation.

## Quantifier order

The quantifiers are, in order:

```text
∀ E, (Q.coeff 0 = 1) ∧ ∃ ρ > 0, ∀ a, E.IsSolution a → ∃ A, ∀ z, ‖z‖ < ρ → …
```

`Q` is determined by `E` alone. The radius `ρ` is chosen after `E` and
before any solution sequence, so it depends only on `E` and works for
every solution simultaneously. Only the numerator `A` is chosen after
`a`, so it may depend on `a`. The single denominator `Q` is therefore
uniform over the whole solution space of `E`.

No bound on the degree of `A` is asserted, although the proof idea below
gives `A` of degree less than `E.order`. Summability, rationality, a
decomposition of solutions into exponential polynomials, and an
exponential growth estimate are not hypotheses. The only hypothesis on
`a` is `E.IsSolution a`.

## Degenerate cases

The statement covers every `E`, including order zero. If `E.order = 0`,
the sum defining `Q` is empty, so `Q = 1`. The recurrence then reads
`a n = 0` for every `n`, so `a = 0`, and the conclusion holds with `A = 0`.
The zero sequence is a solution of every `E`. It is included and
satisfies the conclusion with `A = 0`.

## Why `Q` is the reverse characteristic polynomial

Mathlib's `E.charPoly` is
`X ^ E.order - ∑ i : Fin E.order, monomial i (E.coeffs i)`. With
`d = E.order`, the polynomial `Q` equals `X ^ d * charPoly(1/X)`. Each
exponent `d - i.val` is at least `1` because `i.val < d`, so the natural
subtraction never truncates. Hence `Q.coeff 0 = 1` in every case,
including `d = 0`.

## Internal provenance

This is a derived internal interface. It is not a numbered result in the
literature and is not a transcription of a cited theorem. It isolates
one elementary step of the valuation-shell route to rationality of
one-variable local Mellin integrals. This route is used by the proof task
`0f9adbf4-7878-4aaa-92f9-2e19a9472b8f` for
`Langlands.RankinSelberg.OneVariableMellinStatement`.

The motivating source is H. Jacquet, I. I. Piatetski-Shapiro and
J. A. Shalika, *Rankin–Selberg Convolutions*, Amer. J. Math. 105 (1983),
367–464 (literature ID `jpss-1983-rankin-selberg`, file SHA-256
`d9feed6388ca5acf02ecc6a8cce984871a7c28f062ff441e45a71fa2d8962245`).
Printed page 391 (one-based PDF page 26) ends the theorem statement
asserting that the local integrals span a fractional ideal generated by a
factor of the form `P(q^{-s})^{-1}` with `P(0) = 1`, and begins its proof.
Printed page 392 (PDF page 27) writes the integrals as finite sums of
products (6) of one-variable integrals `∫ Φ_i(a_i) η_i(a_i) |a_i|^{is} d^×a_i`,
with Schwartz functions `Φ_i` and finite functions `η_i` on `F^×`. With
`X = q^{-s}`, it then observes that each such integral has the form
`Q_i(X)·(1 - α_i X)^{-k_i}` with `α_i` depending only on `η_i`. Hence the
integrals are rational functions of `X` with a common denominator
independent of the Whittaker and Schwartz data. Finite functions are
defined in Jacquet, Piatetski-Shapiro and Shalika, *Automorphic forms on
GL(3) I*, Ann. of Math. 109 (1979), printed page 181, §(2.2) (literature
ID `jps-1979-gl3-i`, one-based PDF page 14, file SHA-256
`6446822f267f9f95156ce7a193409209e56ce7858ed6d3d350480e73f35b1aa5`):
a finite function on a locally compact abelian group is a continuous
function whose translates span a finite-dimensional vector space.

The source asserts the one-variable form `Q_i(X)·(1 - α_i X)^{-k_i}`
without a separate lemma or proof. In the project's proof route, the
integrals of a Schwartz function against a finite function over the
valuation shells `|a| = q^{-n}` satisfy a linear recurrence in `n` once
`n` is large enough that the Schwartz function is constant on those
shells. The recurrence comes from the finite-dimensional translation
span of the finite function. This statement then supplies a polynomial
denominator that depends only on the recurrence, and so not on the
Schwartz function. Shifting the generating series by finitely many terms
changes only the numerator. That is why the denominator must be uniform
over all solutions rather than chosen per sequence. The source's
`(1 - α_i X)^{-k_i}` is recovered, up to the numerator, by factoring `Q`.
This statement does not perform that factorization.

## Intended proof idea

This module contains the proposition only. A later exact-proof task will
prove it. The following route is guidance, not a hypothesis.

Let `S = ∑ i, ‖E.coeffs i‖` and `R = max 1 S`. If `M n` is the maximum of
`‖a (n + j)‖` over `j < E.order`, the recurrence gives `M (n + 1) ≤ R * M n`.
This is a companion-operator bound, and it yields `‖a n‖ ≤ C * R ^ n` with
`C` depending on `a`. Thus `ρ = 1 / R`, which depends only on `E`, gives
absolute convergence on the open disc of radius `ρ` for every solution.
For `‖z‖ < ρ ≤ 1` one has
`‖∑ i, E.coeffs i * z ^ (E.order - i)‖ ≤ S * ‖z‖ < 1`, so `Q.eval z ≠ 0`.
Finally, multiplying the absolutely convergent series by `Q.eval z` and
shifting indices finitely many times cancels every coefficient of index
at least `E.order`, by the recurrence. What remains is the evaluation of
a polynomial `A` of degree less than `E.order`.

## Scope and downstream obligations

The interface gives an ordinary polynomial denominator and pointwise
identities on a disc. It does not assert any of the following, which are
downstream assembly obligations:

- conversion to `RatFunc` or formal power series;
- nonvanishing or minimality of a reduced denominator;
- Laurent shifts by negative powers of `z`;
- substitution `z = X ^ m` for a positive weight `m`;
- recurrences or growth bounds for finite functions on translation orbits;
- decomposition of Schwartz integrals into valuation shells.

The latter two are covered by separate statement contracts.
