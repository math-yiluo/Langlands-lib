# Fourier transformation of polynomial Gaussians

For a finite-dimensional real inner product space, choose a family of coordinate vectors. A polynomial in the corresponding real linear forms times a Gaussian of scale `b > 0` has a Fourier transform of the same polynomial form with Gaussian scale `π² / b`.

The proof uses the Gaussian integral for the constant polynomial. Multiplication by a coordinate corresponds to a line derivative of the Fourier transform. Differentiation in a selected coordinate direction keeps the polynomial form because the derivative of each linear coordinate is constant and the derivative of the Gaussian has a linear factor in that direction. Polynomial induction and linearity complete the argument.

This is a derived analytic ingredient for the standard Schwartz tests of Jacquet, *Archimedean Rankin–Selberg Integrals* (2009), p. 10 and Proposition 8.1, pp. 60–61 of the author version, registered as `jacquet-2009-archimedean-rankin-selberg`, SHA-256 `7a11766eadf25fb7452ce7faa193469791796f572fc654c8fe90a8608bb46afe`.
