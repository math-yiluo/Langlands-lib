# Holomorphy on the interior of the absolute convergence domain

Pointwise convexity of the norm of an integrand provides a linear
combination of two integrable norms as a majorant at each intermediate
parameter. Thus the domain of absolute convergence is real convex.

At an interior parameter, choose a simplex contained in the domain
whose interior contains the parameter. Convexity bounds the integrand
throughout the simplex by the sum of its norms at the vertices. On a
smaller ball the Library's several-variable Cauchy estimate bounds the
complex derivatives by a fixed multiple of this same integrable sum.
The dominated differentiation theorem gives holomorphy of the integral.

The norm of a complex exponential is the real exponential of the real
part. Convexity of the real exponential therefore gives the required
convexity for a scalar multiple of an exponential of a complex-linear
form. These are internal consequences of convexity and dominated
differentiation.
