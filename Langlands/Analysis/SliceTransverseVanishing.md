# A transverse derivative equation on a slice

Use test functions supported in a product of a compact set with
a closed interval in the last coordinate. Integrate a test in
that coordinate from zero, then multiply by a cutoff that equals
one near zero and vanishes off the interval. The primitive has
the same rectangular support, and its last-coordinate derivative
agrees with the original test near the zero slice. Its vanishing
jet order is one higher than that of the original test.

A distribution on the slice annihilates tests with sufficiently
many vanishing jets on the rectangle. Suppose that applying the
distribution to the transverse derivative of a test gives a
finite linear combination of its values on operations preserving
vanishing jets. For a test flat to one order below the known
bound, apply this equation to the cutoff primitive. The right
side vanishes by the known bound. The left side is the value on
the original test because their difference is supported away
from the slice. Repeating the descent gives zero on all tests
supported in the rectangle.

This is a local coordinate form of the transverse vanishing
argument of Shalika (1974), Proposition 2.10. The transverse
coordinate and the equation are hypotheses of this lemma.
