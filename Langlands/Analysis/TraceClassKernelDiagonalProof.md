# Trace of a trace-class integral operator as a diagonal integral

`Langlands.Analysis.OperatorIdeals.traceClassContinuousKernelDiagonal` proves `TraceClassContinuousKernelDiagonalStatement` at the statement's full universe, with no added assumptions. Both conclusions concern the supplied representative of the kernel: its diagonal is integrable and its integral equals `operatorTrace T`.

## Implemented proof

1. **Operator expansion.** `traceClassFactorization` gives `T = B ∘ C` with both factors Hilbert–Schmidt. On a Hilbert basis `d` take `a i = B (d i)` and `b i = C.adjoint (d i)`. Basis independence and `isHilbertSchmidt_adjoint` give summability of the squared norms of both families. Only the countable support of the first squared-norm family is needed: outside it `a i = 0`. Expanding `C φ` in the basis and applying `B` gives `T φ = Σ i, ⟪b i, φ⟫ • a i`, using the inner product linear in its second argument. `hilbertSchmidtProductTrace` and `operatorTraceBasis` identify `Σ i, ⟪b i, a i⟫` with `operatorTrace T`.
2. **Square tests.** On any finite-measure measurable set `A`, test the operator with `φ = 1_A` in `L²`. The kernel is integrable on `A × A` by the finite-measure restriction of its `L²` hypothesis. Fubini and the kernel-action hypothesis give `∫_{A × A} K = ⟪φ, T φ⟫`. Applying the continuous linear functional `⟪φ, ·⟫` to the operator expansion computes this as `Σ i, (∫_A a i) conj (∫_A b i)`. This step does not require uniqueness of `L²` kernels.
3. **Almost everywhere diagonal identity.** On the restriction of `μ` to each finite-measure spanning set `S`, the square tests follow by using `A ∩ S` in step 2. The companion modules `TraceClassKernelDiagonalCells` and `TraceClassKernelDiagonalCore` prove that these identities and the continuity hypothesis give `K x x = Σ i, a i x * conj (b i x)` almost everywhere on the restriction. The countable spanning sets cover the space, so the identity holds `μ`-almost everywhere.
4. **Integration.** The integrals of the norms of the diagonal summands are summable, since each summand is bounded by the half-sum of the pointwise squared norms and their integrals are the squared `L²` norms. The sum is therefore integrable. `integral_tsum_of_summable_integral_norm` and `L2.inner_def` identify its integral with `Σ i, ⟪b i, a i⟫`. The almost everywhere equality transfers both claims to the supplied diagonal.

## Source and correspondence

C. Brislawn, *Traceable integral kernels on countably generated measure spaces*, Pacific J. Math. **150** (1991), no. 2, 229–240. Registered source `brislawn-1991-traceable-integral-kernels`, SHA-256 `c985964e1923282f40564248ecf6e7ae01ddfb165cc3cd8460031f9bcdc0df12`.

- **Theorem 3.1** (printed p. 235, proof pp. 235–237; PDF pp. 8–10) represents the averaged diagonal by the diagonal of a canonical expansion, which converges absolutely in `L¹`, and uses this expansion to compute the trace. Steps 1, 3 and 4 implement the expansion, diagonal limit and trace computation needed here, replacing the canonical expansion by Hilbert–Schmidt factors and the maximal-function estimate by conditional expectations of integrable energy tails. The formalization here does not assert the full theorem without the continuity hypothesis or prove its maximal-average bound.
- **Theorem 2.4**, especially (2.11) (printed p. 234, PDF p. 7), identifies the continuous-at-diagonal values with the limits of square-cell averages almost everywhere. The companion core proof implements this directly using the neighborhood basis and continuity, on finite restrictions.
- **Corollary 3.2** (printed p. 237, PDF p. 10) combines this continuity identification with the trace computation. The Lean theorem gives that equality for the supplied `L²` kernel and includes integrability of its diagonal.
