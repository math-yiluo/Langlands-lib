Author: Worker 61. Built on the published
`Langlands.Representation.Induction.OpenAdjunction`
(`smoothRestriction_preservesEpimorphisms`,
`smoothCompactInduction_preservesMonomorphisms` and the specialization
`SupercuspidalExt.compactInductionAdjunction`), Worker 13's
`Langlands.Cohomology.DerivedComparison` (`rightDerivedCompIso`,
`compIsoOfResolution`, `complexIso`, `mapInjectiveResolution`,
`mapHomologyIso`), Worker 13's `Langlands.Cohomology.SmoothExt` (`ext`,
`extZeroIso`), Worker 13's `Langlands.Cohomology.SmoothHomComplex`
(`homFunctor`) and Worker 3's `Langlands.Cohomology.SupercuspidalExt.Statement`

`Functor.preservesHomology_of_preservesEpis_and_kernels` and
`Functor.preservesInjectiveObjects_of_adjunction_of_preservesMonomorphisms`.

Composition `f ≫ g` is `f` followed by `g`.

## Degree zero of the derived-functor comparison

This section is general homological algebra, in the namespace
`Langlands.Cohomology.DerivedComparison`, next to Worker 13's comparison.

- `map_homologyπ_comp_mapHomologyIso_inv S F`: for a short complex `S` with
  homology and a functor `F` preserving zero morphisms and the left homology of
  `S` (with `S.map F` having homology),
  `F.map S.homologyπ ≫ (S.mapHomologyIso F).inv =
  (S.mapCyclesIso F).inv ≫ (S.map F).homologyπ`. That is, Mathlib's
  comparison isomorphisms for cycles and homology commute with the projection
  from cycles to homology.
- `mapCyclesIso_inv_iCycles S F`: under the same hypotheses on `F` (with `S`
  having left homology), `(S.mapCyclesIso F).inv ≫ (S.map F).iCycles =
  F.map S.iCycles`.
  Both lemmas also come in the `_assoc` forms generated by `@[reassoc]`.
- `toRightDerivedZero_comp_rightDerivedCompIso Φ P T Ψ e X`: in the setting of
  `rightDerivedCompIso` (`Φ : C ⥤ E`, `P : E ⥤ E'`, `T : C ⥤ D`,
  `Ψ : D ⥤ E'` additive, `e : Φ ⋙ P ≅ T ⋙ Ψ`, `C` and `D` with injective
  resolutions, `P` and `T` preserving homology, `T` preserving injective
  objects), for every object `X` of `C`,
  `P.map (Φ.toRightDerivedZero.app X) ≫ (rightDerivedCompIso Φ P T Ψ e 0).hom.app X =
  e.hom.app X ≫ Ψ.toRightDerivedZero.app (T.obj X)`.
  Here `Φ.toRightDerivedZero : Φ ⟶ Φ.rightDerived 0` is Mathlib's canonical
  natural transformation. So in degree zero the comparison is `e`, read
  through these canonical maps.

The proof works with the chosen resolution `I` of `X` and its image
`J = mapInjectiveResolution T I`. It first compares the maps into the degree-0
cycles, checking after the inclusion of cycles, which is a monomorphism; there
it is the naturality of `e` at the augmentation `I.ι.f 0`. It then passes to
homology with `homologyπ_naturality` and `map_homologyπ_comp_mapHomologyIso_inv`,
and finally unfolds the four isomorphisms of `compIsoOfResolution`.

## Restriction to an open subgroup

In the namespace `Langlands.Cohomology.SupercuspidalExt`, `R` is a commutative
ring and `G` a topological group, both in one universe `u`, `H` is a subgroup of
`G` and `hH : IsOpen (H : Set G)` where stated. All representations are in
`SmoothRep.{u, u, u}`.

- Instances `restriction_additive` and `restriction_linear` (any `H`):
  restriction does not change underlying linear maps, so it is additive and
  `R`-linear.
- Instance `restriction_preservesEpimorphisms` (any `H`): the published
  `Induction.smoothRestriction_preservesEpimorphisms`.
- `restriction_preservesHomology R G hH`: restriction to an open subgroup is
  exact (`Functor.PreservesHomology`). The proof uses that restriction is a
  right adjoint (`compactInductionAdjunction`), so preserves kernels, and that
  it preserves epimorphisms. It is a theorem rather than an instance because
  this proof uses `hH`.
- `restriction_preservesInjectiveObjects R G hH`: restriction to an open
  subgroup preserves injective objects, because its left adjoint compact
  induction preserves monomorphisms.

## Reciprocity

Let `V` be a smooth representation of `H` and `W` one of `G`.

- `homLinearEquiv R hH V W`: the `R`-linear equivalence
  `((compactInduction R G H).obj V ⟶ W) ≃ₗ[R] (V ⟶ (restriction R G H).obj W)`
  whose underlying bijection is the hom-equivalence of
  `compactInductionAdjunction R G H hH` (`homLinearEquiv_apply`). It is
  additive and `R`-linear because that hom-equivalence sends `f` to the unit
  followed by the restriction of `f`.
- `homFunctorIso R hH V`: the natural isomorphism
  `homFunctor R G ((compactInduction R G H).obj V) ⋙ 𝟭 (ModuleCat R) ≅
  restriction R G H ⋙ homFunctor R H V` of functors from smooth
  representations of `G` to `R`-modules, with components `homLinearEquiv`.
  Naturality is the naturality of the hom-equivalence in its second variable.
- `extCompactInductionIso R hH V n`: the natural isomorphism
  `SmoothExt.ext R G ((compactInduction R G H).obj V) n ≅
  restriction R G H ⋙ SmoothExt.ext R H V n`, that is
  `Ext^n_G(c-Ind_H^G V, -) ≅ Ext^n_H(V, restriction of -)` for every `n`.
  It is Worker 13's `rightDerivedCompIso` with `Φ` the Hom functor out of
  `c-Ind_H^G V`, `P` the identity of `R`-modules, `T` restriction, `Ψ` the Hom
  functor out of `V` and `e = homFunctorIso R hH V`, preceded by the right
  unitor that identifies `ext` with `ext ⋙ 𝟭`. Its hypotheses on `T` are
  `restriction_preservesHomology` and `restriction_preservesInjectiveObjects`.
- `extCompactInductionIso_hom_app_zero R hH V W`: in degree zero,
  `(extCompactInductionIso R hH V 0).hom.app W` is
  `(SmoothExt.extZeroIso R G _).hom.app W ≫ (homLinearEquiv R hH V W).toModuleIso.hom ≫
  (SmoothExt.extZeroIso R H V).inv.app _`. So under the identifications
  `Ext^0 ≅ Hom` of `SmoothExt.extZeroIso` on both sides, Ext reciprocity in
  degree zero is Frobenius reciprocity. It follows from
  `toRightDerivedZero_comp_rightDerivedCompIso`, because `extZeroIso` is
  Mathlib's `rightDerivedZeroIsoSelf`, whose inverse is `toRightDerivedZero`.

## Provenance and interpretation

Harris 1997 (Inventiones 129), printed p.96, in the argument leading from the
spectral sequence (10) to the isomorphisms (11): with `GJ^0` the stabilizer of
a connected component of `M_N` and
`H^j_c(M_N) ≅ c-Ind_{GJ^0}^{GJ} H^j_c(R_N)`, "Frobenius reciprocity [BZ,
Prop. 2.29] thus yields an isomorphism of functors
`Hom_{GJ}(H^j_c(M_N), ·) ≅ Hom_{GJ^0}(H^j_c(R_N), ·)`, hence isomorphisms
`Ext^i_{GJ}(H^j_c(M_N), ·) ≅ Ext^i_{GJ^0}(H^j_c(R_N), ·)` for all `i` and `j`".
`extCompactInductionIso` is the second step, for one compactly induced
representation, deduced from the first step as Harris does. The derivation
"hence" is made precise here by the facts that restriction to an open
subgroup is exact and preserves injectives (Vignéras 1996, I.5.9 d), for the
injective half), so that Ext over `H` of restrictions can be computed from
restricted injective resolutions.

The Hom-level reciprocity used is the published open-subgroup adjunction,
which follows Vignéras 1996, I.5.7 (ii). Harris cites Bernstein–Zelevinsky,
Prop. 2.29, which concerns closed subgroups and is phrased with
contragredients. That proposition is not used here.

Harris works with `Q̄_ℓ`-coefficients and his particular groups. Here `R` is
any commutative ring and `G` any topological group, in one universe `u`, as in
Worker 3's statement module.

## Scope

- This module does not prove Worker 3's
  `CompactInductionExtReciprocityStatement`, although
  `fun H hH V n => ⟨extCompactInductionIso R hH V n⟩` is the intended proof.
  That proof is planned as a separate proof task, as agreed with Worker 13 and
  Worker 3.
- Harris's sums over `j`, the cohomology groups `H^j_c(M_N)` and `H^j_c(R_N)`,
  the group `GJ^0` and the identification of `H^j_c(M_N)` as a compact
  induction are not formalized. `extCompactInductionIso` requires `H` to be
  open; the module does not check that Harris's `GJ^0` is open.
- Only the dependence on the second Ext argument is treated. Naturality in
  `V` and the compatibility with Ext bifunctor maps are not stated.
