# Tate's cup-product theorem

This module states `Langlands.Tate.TateCupIsomorphism.{u}`, Tate's theorem on
cup product with a degree-two class, for the Library's actual integral cup
operation `Langlands.Tate.integralH2Cup`. It is a Prop-valued statement; no proof
is given here.

## The proposition

For every group `G : Type u` with `[Group G] [Fintype G]`, every representation
`A : Rep.{u} IntegralScalar.{u} G` and every ordinary class
`a : groupCohomology A 2`, suppose that for every prime `p` and every Sylow
`p`-subgroup `P : Sylow p G`, with `A` restricted to `P` by
`Rep.res P.1.subtype A`:

1. `groupCohomology (Rep.res P.1.subtype A) 1` is a subsingleton, that is,
   `H¹(P, A) = 0`;
2. `Nat.card (groupCohomology (Rep.res P.1.subtype A) 2) = Nat.card P`, that is,
   `H²(P, A)` is finite of order `|P|`;
3. the additive subgroup generated by the restriction
   `groupCohomology.map P.1.subtype (𝟙 (Rep.res P.1.subtype A)) 2 a` is all of
   `H²(P, A)`.

Then for every integer `n` the map
`integralH2Cup A n a : Ĥⁿ(G, ℤ) → Ĥⁿ⁺²(G, A)` is bijective.

Here `IntegralScalar.{u}` is `ULift ℤ` (module `ZeroDefinitions`), `Ĥⁿ(G, ℤ)` is
Mathlib's `tateCohomology` of the trivial representation
`Rep.trivial IntegralScalar G IntegralScalar`, and `Ĥⁿ⁺²(G, A)` is Mathlib's
`tateCohomology A (n + 2)`. The map `integralH2Cup A n` (module
`CupDefinitions`) is `scalarH2Cup A n`: the class `a` is transported to Tate
degree two by Mathlib's `TateCohomology.isoGroupCohomology 2`, cupped on the
left with an element of `Ĥⁿ(G, ℤ)` through the scalar pairing `A × ℤ → A`, and
the target degree `2 + n` is rewritten as `n + 2`. The class `a` is the left
factor, as in Serre's `a_g · x`.

The restriction in condition 3 is Mathlib's ordinary group-cohomology map along
the inclusion `P.1.subtype : P →* G` with the identity coefficient morphism; this
is the same restriction used by the Library's `Langlands.Cohomology.SylowDetection`.
In condition 2, `Nat.card` of an infinite group is `0`, so the condition also
forces `H²(P, A)` to be finite. For a prime not dividing `|G|` the only Sylow
subgroup is trivial and the three conditions hold automatically.

## Source and correspondence

Jean-Pierre Serre, *Local Fields* (Greenberg translation, 1979), registered as
`serre-1979-local-fields`, Chapter IX, §8, Theorem 14 and its Corollary (Tate),
printed page 149 (PDF page 155). Theorem 14: let `G` be finite, `A` a `G`-module,
`a ∈ H²(G, A)`; let `G_p` be a Sylow `p`-subgroup for each prime `p`, and suppose
1) `H¹(G_p, A) = 0` and 2) `H²(G_p, A)` is generated by `Res_{G/G_p}(a)` and has
order equal to that of `G_p`. Then for every `G`-module `D` with `Tor(A, D) = 0`,
cup product with `a_g = Res_{G/g}(a)` induces isomorphisms
`Ĥⁿ(g, D) → Ĥⁿ⁺²(g, A ⊗ D)` for every integer `n` and every subgroup `g`. The
Corollary is the case `D = ℤ`: `Ĥⁿ(g, ℤ) → Ĥⁿ⁺²(g, A)` is an isomorphism.
Serre derives it from Theorem 13 (printed pp. 148–149, PDF pp. 154–155) by
dimension shifting, which in turn rests on Theorem 12 and §§5–7 of Chapter IX.

The Lean proposition is the Corollary for the subgroup `g = G` itself, with
`D = ℤ`; it makes no claim about proper subgroups `g` or about other modules
`D`. The hypotheses are required for every Sylow subgroup rather than for one
chosen Sylow subgroup per prime. This is formally a stronger hypothesis, hence a
formally weaker theorem; the two readings agree because all Sylow
`p`-subgroups are conjugate and conjugation by an element of `G` transports the
three conditions. Quantifying over all Sylow subgroups is also the form used by
the Library's Sylow detection statements. The order and generation conditions
are stated separately, exactly as in Serre's condition 2).

## Use

The local class formation clause `InvariantFamily.TateIsomorphisms`
(module `Langlands.ClassFieldTheory.Local.TateCup`) asks for bijectivity of
`relativeTateCup K L n c`, which is definitionally
`integralH2Cup (unitsRepresentation K L) n c`. This statement reduces that clause
to Hilbert 90 and to the order and generation of `H²` of the Sylow subgroups.
