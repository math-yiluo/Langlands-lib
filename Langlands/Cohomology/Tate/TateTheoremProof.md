# Tate's cup-product theorem

This module proves `Langlands.Tate.tateCupIsomorphism : TateCupIsomorphism.{u}`,
the proposition of module `TateTheoremStatement`. Let `G : Type u` be a finite
group, `A : Rep.{u} IntegralScalar.{u} G` and `a : groupCohomology A 2`. Suppose
that for every prime `p` and every Sylow `p`-subgroup `P`, the ordinary group
`H¹(P, A)` is a subsingleton, `H²(P, A)` has cardinality `Nat.card P`, and
`H²(P, A)` is generated by the restriction of `a`. Then for every integer `n`
the map `integralH2Cup A n a : Ĥⁿ(G, ℤ) → Ĥⁿ⁺²(G, A)` is bijective.

The proof uses the comparison theorem `tateComparison : TateComparison.{u}`
(module `TateComparisonProof`). That theorem is in turn proved from
`pGroupTateVanishing` and `sylowTateVanishing`, so the proof needs no
hypotheses beyond the statement.

## Source and route

The mathematics is Serre, *Local Fields* (registered as
`serre-1979-local-fields`), Chapter IX, §8: Theorem 13 (printed pages
148–149, PDF pages 154–155), specialised as in Theorem 14 and its Corollary
(printed page 149, PDF page 155) to `B = ℤ`, `C = A`, `q = 2`, `D = ℤ` and the
subgroup `g = G`. Serre treats a degree-zero class `a` represented by
`α ∈ A^G` through the coefficient map `f(b) = φ(α, b)` and Theorem 12. A
class of degree `q` is reduced to degree `q - 1` by dimension shifting,
because cup products commute with coboundaries. For Theorem 14 he takes
`n_p = -1`. Hypothesis 1) gives surjectivity in degree `-1`, hypothesis 2)
gives bijectivity in degree `0`, and `H¹(G_p, ℤ) = 0` gives injectivity in
degree `1`.

The Lean proof follows this route with two differences. It shifts the
coefficient module `A` twice with the Library's `shiftUp` (the coinduced
embedding), not Serre's induced module. It uses the Library's actual Tate
cup product `tensorCup` together with the scalar pairing `A × ℤ → A`.

## Proof

Helper lemmas are in the namespace `Langlands.Tate.TateTheorem`. The file
disables the instance `ULift.module` so that scalar actions on
`IntegralScalar.{u} = ULift ℤ` modules use the representation's own module
structure.

1. **General lemmas.** `scalarPairing_lift` identifies the linear map of the
   scalar pairing with `TensorProduct.rid`. Hence
   `(scalarPairing A).tensorHom : A ⊗ ℤ ⟶ A` is an isomorphism of
   representations (`scalarPairing_tensorHom_isIso`). `map_bijective_of_comp`:
   if `f ≫ g` induces a bijection on Tate cohomology and `g` is an isomorphism,
   then `f` induces a bijection.
2. **Dimension shifting in the first variable.** `tensorCup_shiftUp_bijective`
   uses the boundary law in the first variable. This is the fourth component of
   `cupLaws` (module `CupLawsProof`), applied to the short exact sequence
   `shiftUpSequence A` tensored on the right with `B`, which is short exact by
   `scalarSplit_tensorRight_shortExact`. The law writes cup product with
   `(shiftUpIso A p).hom x` as cup product with `x` followed by the
   isomorphisms `tensorShiftUpLeftIso A B (p + q)` and `tateDegreeIso`. So the
   first map is bijective when the second is.
3. **Degree zero.** `tensorCup_zeroClass_bijective` uses
   `tensorCup_invariantLeft` (module `CupBoundarySecond`). It writes cup product
   with `zeroClass A α` as the map induced by
   `tensorInvariantLeft A B α : B ⟶ A ⊗ B`, `b ↦ α ⊗ b`, up to `tateDegreeIso`.
4. **Reduction of the main theorem.** The class `a` is the image of
   `aT : tateCohomology A 2` under `TateCohomology.isoGroupCohomology 2`. In
   turn `aT = (shiftUpIso A 1).hom ((shiftUpIso (shiftUp A) 0).hom y)` for some
   `y : tateCohomology (shiftUp (shiftUp A)) 0`, and `y = zeroClass _ α` for an
   invariant vector `α` (`zeroClass_surjective`). Let `B` be the trivial
   representation `IntegralScalar.{u}`, `μ := tensorInvariantLeft _ B α` and
   `μ' := μ ≫ (scalarPairing _).tensorHom : B ⟶ shiftUp (shiftUp A)`, the map
   `k ↦ k • α`. Unfolding `integralH2Cup` gives a composite of an isomorphism
   of degrees, the isomorphism induced by `(scalarPairing A).tensorHom` and
   `tensorCup A B 2 n aT`. Steps 2 (twice) and 3 and `map_bijective_of_comp`
   reduce the bijectivity of this map to the bijectivity of the maps induced
   by `μ'` in every degree.
5. **The comparison theorem.** `tateComparison` is applied to `μ'` with
   `m = -1` for every Sylow subgroup `P`:
   - *Degree `-1`.* `restrictedShiftUpIso` (module `SubgroupShifts`) twice and
     `TateCohomology.isoGroupCohomology 1` identify
     `Ĥ⁻¹(P, shiftUp (shiftUp A))` with `H¹(P, A)`. This group is a
     subsingleton by hypothesis 1, so every map into it is surjective.
   - *Degree `0`.* Let `Ψ` be the analogous linear equivalence from
     `Ĥ⁰(P, shiftUp (shiftUp A))` to `H²(P, A)`. Restriction of
     `μ'` sends the class of `1` to `restrictionZero P _ (zeroClass _ α)`
     (`restrictionZero_zeroClass`, `zeroClass_naturality`). Then
     `restriction_two_shiftUp` and `restriction_two_apply` (from
     `restriction_groupCohomology`, module `PositiveRestriction`) show that
     `Ψ` sends it to the ordinary restriction of `a`.
     `zeroClass_trivial_multiple` shows that every class in `Ĥ⁰(P, ℤ)` is an
     integer multiple of the class of `1`. `bijective_zero_of_generator` then
     proves bijectivity: the image generates the target by hypothesis 3, so
     by hypothesis 2 its additive order is `|P|`. So `k • [1]` maps to zero
     only if `|P|` divides `k`. In that case `k • [1] = 0`, because `|P|` kills
     `Ĥ⁰(P, ℤ)` (`PGroupVanishing.card_smul_eq_zero`).
   - *Degree `1`.* `Ĥ¹(P, ℤ)` is isomorphic to `Additive P →+ ℤ`
     (`TateCohomology.isoGroupCohomology 1` and
     `groupCohomology.H1IsoOfIsTrivial`). That group is a subsingleton because
     `P` is finite and `ℤ` is torsion-free (`integral_addMonoidHom_subsingleton`),
     so every map out of `Ĥ¹(P, ℤ)` is injective.

   Hence `μ'` induces bijections in every degree, which proves the theorem by
   step 4.

The declaration check on `Langlands.Tate.tateCupIsomorphism` reports only the
axioms `propext`, `Classical.choice` and `Quot.sound`.
