# Berkovich's cohomological change of site for equivariant sheaves

Berkovich, *Étale equivariant sheaves on p-adic analytic spaces*, Theorem
1.6.1(iii), printed and PDF pp. 13–14, compares étale cohomology with
quasi-étale cohomology of the inverse image along
`μ : X(G)_qét → X(G)_ét`. It treats both invariant cohomology and
cohomology with its smooth group action. The proof first identifies the
higher direct images of the comparison with their stalks on compact
quasi-étale neighbourhoods, then uses the vanishing for the ordinary
étale-to-quasi-étale comparison. Corollary 1.4.3, p. 10, gives the unit
comparison in degree zero.

`EquivariantEtaleQuasiEtaleCohomologyStatement` asserts a natural
isomorphism between right derived global sections on the equivariant
étale site and right derived global sections on the equivariant
compact-probe site after the actual inverse image. The sheaf categories
use the underlying analytic test objects with their `G`-actions and
jointly covering equivariant families. The coefficient categories are
modules over one commutative ring. The universe contains the groups
and the analytic sites, so both sites have sheafification and enough
injectives for the right-derived functors.

`SmoothEtaleQuasiEtaleCohomologyStatement` asserts a natural isomorphism
in smooth representations. Its étale side first takes the actual
inverse image along the equivariant étale site functor. Its quasi-étale
side takes the change-of-site inverse image followed by the actual
compact-probe inverse image. The two smoothness premises are the
factorization clauses of the topos comparisons. Image pullbacks for
the equivariant étale forgetful functor enter its inverse image, and
the distinguished-étale refinement premise enters the smooth derived
functor. The compact-probe refinements come from the geometric lifting
and compatibility results. Both propositions assume that open
subgroup quotients fit in the geometric universe. The action is over
a fixed complete ultrametric field. The propositions express natural
existence of the comparisons.
