# Discrete Godement cochains and coefficient systems for the compact-probe site

Let the space be a localized analytic space over a complete ultrametric normed field, with an action by a topologized group and finite equivariant refinements for its compact lifts. For a chosen family of points of its compact-probe quasi-étale site, `compactProbeGodementFunctor` is the degree-zero discrete Godement functor on the twisted site. The functor uses the actual site fibers and skyscrapers, right Kan induction, and the discrete coreflector for the compact lift system. It is exact by the point-product lifting argument on the discrete category. `compactProbeGodementComplex` constructs the cochain functor by iterated cokernels, with `compactProbeGodementAugmentation` induced by the natural coaugmentation. `compactProbeGodementGeometricPoints` gives site points from complete algebraically closed normed extension fields. The construction uses sheafification, small hom sets, and pointwise right Kan extension at the specified coefficient universe.

The resolution systems use an explicit jointly faithful family of site fibers. Under this hypothesis the natural coaugmentation is monic. An input $\ell$-divisible system of discrete module sheaves then has the termwise Godement resolution system and its truncation at $D+1$. Each truncated augmentation is a quasi-isomorphism. The construction of the cochains and the exactness of the degree-zero functor work for any family of points. Joint faithfulness is needed to identify the augmented complexes as resolutions.

The constructions extend Berkovich's degree-zero and iterated Godement steps in *Étale equivariant sheaves on p-adic analytic spaces*, §1.5 p. 10, and Harris's coefficient systems and truncation in *Galois properties of cohomological automorphic forms on GL(n)* (1999), §2, Facts 3–4 and Lemmas 2.2 and 2.2 bis, pp. 304–305. The Lean constructions here use the compact-probe covering rule. Compact-support acyclicity and the cohomological dimension bound used for cohomology computations are separate inputs.
