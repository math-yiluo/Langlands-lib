# Harris's divisible systems on the quasi-étale site with an arbitrary family of points

Author: Worker 26. Builds on the following modules:

- `Langlands.Cohomology.DivisibleSystem.AnalyticQuasiEtale` (Worker 26). It specializes
  Harris's §2 constructions to quasi-étale `G`-sheaves, with the Godement functor
  `quasiEtaleGodementFunctor L ρ h R` built from the `L`-valued points of `X`. It also
  provides the Kan extension instance `quasiEtale_hasPointwiseRightKanExtension` and the
  divisible-system types `QuasiEtaleDivisibleSystem` and
  `QuasiEtaleComplexDivisibleSystem`.
- `Langlands.Cohomology.DivisibleSystem.AnalyticQuasiEtaleConstant` (Worker 26). It provides
  the constant system `quasiEtaleConstantSystem ρ R hℓ` of the constant quasi-étale
  `G`-sheaves `R / ℓ ^ m R`, the identification `quasiEtaleConstantSystemObjIso`, and
  `isZero_quasiEtaleGSheafCompactCohomology_constantSystem_obj`, which turns the bound
  `hC` for the constant sheaves into the bound `hT` for the constant system.
- Through these modules: `AnalyticQuasiEtaleCompactCohomology` (Worker 26: sheafification,
  enough injectives, `Γ_{c,X{G}}` and `H^q_c(X{G}, -)`), the twisted-site modules
  `Langlands.Sheaf.Equivariant.TwistedGodement`, `DivisibleSystem.TwistedGodement`,
  `TwistedTruncation` and `TwistedInverseLimit` (Worker 26), Worker 21's quasi-étale site
  with its `L`-valued points, and Worker 3's statements `ProPInvariantSubcomplexStatement`
  and `FiniteCohomologyInverseLimitStatement`. Neither of Worker 3's statements is proved
  in the Library; they appear here only as hypotheses `hS` and `hS'`.

All declarations are in the namespace `Langlands.Cohomology.DivisibleSystem.Analytic`.

## Purpose

In `AnalyticQuasiEtale` and `AnalyticQuasiEtaleConstant`, the Godement resolutions use the
`L`-valued points of `X`, and every result from Lemma 2.2 on assumes `hpoints`: the fibre
functors `(quasiEtalePoints L h x).sheafFiber` at the `L`-valued points are jointly faithful
on sheaves of `R`-modules on the quasi-étale site. For `R ≠ 0` this hypothesis is false on
most spaces of positive dimension, including Harris's domains (see the next section). There
those results hold only vacuously.

The twisted-site layer does not need the `L`-valued points. `discreteZeroFunctor` and
everything built on it take an arbitrary family `points : I → J.Point` of points of the
site, with `I` in the universe of the coefficients. The `L`-valued points are needed only
for compact supports: `quasiEtaleGSheafCompactCohomology L ρ h R` uses their topology and
the action of `G` on them. This module therefore repeats the constructions of
`AnalyticQuasiEtale` and `AnalyticQuasiEtaleConstant` for an arbitrary family of points of
the quasi-étale site. Joint faithfulness is assumed for that family. The compactly
supported cohomology is unchanged.

The module proves nothing new about the quasi-étale site. Every declaration instantiates a
published twisted-site declaration, or composes such instances with the constant-system
isomorphisms of `AnalyticQuasiEtaleConstant`, in the same way as the `L`-point versions.

## Why the `L`-valued points are not enough

This section is a mathematical remark. It is not formalized.

Berkovich defines the quasi-étale topology (1994, §3, printed p.544) by a pretopology. A
family `{f_i : U_i → U}` is a covering if every point of `U` has a neighbourhood of the form
`f_{i_1}(V_1) ∪ ⋯ ∪ f_{i_n}(V_n)` with affinoid domains `V_j ⊆ U_{i_j}`. A sieve is then
covering exactly when it contains a covering family. The Library's
`quasiEtalePrecoverage` states the same neighbourhood condition with the images of atlas
charts, which are compact affinoid domains, and `quasiEtaleTopology` is the topology it
generates.

Let `X` be Hausdorff, and let `ξ` be a point of `X` lying in an affinoid domain `A ⊆ X`
that is not a neighbourhood of `ξ`. Let `S` be the sieve on `X` generated by the embeddings
`A ↪ X` and `X ∖ {ξ} ↪ X`. Both embeddings are quasi-étale.

1. `S` is not covering. Suppose `S` contained a covering family. Then `ξ` has a
   neighbourhood `W` contained in `A ∪ C`, where `C` is the union of the images of the
   finitely many affinoid domains taken from members that factor through `X ∖ {ξ}`. The set
   `C` is compact and does not contain `ξ`. Since `X` is Hausdorff, some neighbourhood `W'`
   of `ξ` misses `C`. Then `W ∩ W' ⊆ A`, so `A` is a neighbourhood of `ξ`, a contradiction.
2. The presheaf `R[h_X]` is the constant presheaf `R`, because `X` is terminal in its
   quasi-étale site. Its subpresheaf `R[S]` is `R` on the objects `U → X` in `S` and `0`
   elsewhere. Let `x` be an `L`-valued point of `X`. Every `L`-valued point `p` of an
   object `U` over `x` lifts to `U ×_X A` if the underlying point of `x` lies in `A`, and
   to `U ×_X (X ∖ {ξ})` otherwise. Both objects lie in `S`. So the inclusion
   `R[S] ⟶ R[h_X]` is an isomorphism on the fibre at `x`.
3. Let `Q` be the cokernel of the sheafified inclusion. The fibre functors commute with
   sheafification and cokernels, so every `L`-valued fibre of `Q` is zero. The identity of
   `Q` and the zero map then have the same image under every fibre functor. If these fibre
   functors were jointly faithful, `Q` would be zero. Then the global section `1` of the
   constant sheaf would lie locally in the image of `R[S]`: there would be a covering sieve
   `T` on `X` such that, for every `g : U → X` in `T`, the restriction of `1` to `U` is the
   image of an element of `R[S](U)`. If `U` is nonempty this restriction is not zero, as
   `R ≠ 0`, so `g` lies in `S`. The empty object lies in `S` anyway. So `T ⊆ S` and `S`
   would be covering, contradicting step 1.

Such `ξ` and `A` exist in many spaces.

- In the closed unit disc `{|T| ≤ 1}`, take `A = {|T| ≤ r}` with `0 < r < 1`, and let `ξ`
  be the Gauss point of `A`. The Gauss points of the discs `{|T| ≤ s}`, `r < s < 1`, lie
  outside `A` and tend to `ξ` as `s` tends to `r`.
- Every nonempty open subspace `W` of the projective space `P^d`, `d ≥ 1`, over `K`
  contains them. This includes Drinfeld's `Ω` and Harris's domains `U(f)`. If `W = P^d`,
  take for `A` a closed polydisc in an affine chart: `A` is compact and nonempty, and
  `A ≠ P^d`. Since `P^d` is connected, `A` is not open, so some `ξ ∈ A` is not an interior
  point. If `W ≠ P^d`, pick `y ∈ W` and a connected open neighbourhood `V ⊆ W` of `y`.
  Analytic spaces are locally connected (Berkovich 1990, §3.2). If `V` were compact, it
  would be closed and open in the connected space `P^d` (Berkovich 1990, Theorem 3.4.8),
  so `V = P^d`, which is impossible. Let `A ⊆ V` be an affinoid neighbourhood of `y`. Then
  `A ≠ V`, and `A` is closed in the connected space `V`, so it is not open in `V`. A point
  `ξ ∈ A` that is not interior to `A` in `V` is not interior in `W` either.

Step 2 only uses that an `L`-valued point of an object `U` factors through every analytic
domain of `U` containing its underlying point. The points `QuasiEtaleSite.toPoint L x` have
this property: their fibre functor `pointFibre` sends `U` to the `L`-valued points of `U`
over `x` (`Langlands.Geometry.Analytic.OverSitePoints`). Any family of points with this
property fails in the same way. A family for which joint faithfulness
can hold must also see points that are not points of Berkovich's space, such as the
higher-rank points of the associated adic space. Neither such points nor a comparison of
topoi is formalized in the Library or covered by the registered literature, so this module
does not claim that a family satisfying `hpoints` exists.

The same phenomenon affects compact supports. `quasiEtaleGSheafCompactCohomology` takes
supports among the `L`-valued points (`isCompactlySupported_quasiEtalePoints_iff`). A
section whose germs at all `L`-valued points vanish has empty support there, so it counts
as compactly supported. This module keeps that definition unchanged.

## Data and universes

As in `AnalyticQuasiEtale`:

- `K : Type u` is a complete ultrametric normed field, and `X` an object of Worker 21's
  `LocalizedCategory.{u, v, v} (K := K)`.
- `G` is a group with `ρ : G →* Aut X` and `[TopologicalSpace G]`. It lies in `Type z`,
  except in the inverse-limit sections, where `G : Type u`. The compact-section and
  inverse-limit sections also assume `[IsTopologicalGroup G]`.
- Write `w = max u (v + 1) uL`. The ring `R : Type w` is commutative, sheaves take values
  in `ModuleCat.{w} R`, and `[Small.{w} G]`.
- `[(compactLiftSystem ρ).HasFiniteEquivariantRefinements (twistedQuasiEtaleTopology ρ)]`
  is assumed throughout.
- New: an index type `I : Type w` and a family
  `points : I → (quasiEtaleTopology X).Point.{w}` of points of the quasi-étale site.
- The compact-section and inverse-limit sections also take `L : Type uL`, a normed field
  with `[NormedAlgebra K L]`, and the point axioms
  `h : ∀ x, QuasiEtaleSite.PointAxioms L x`, because `H^q_c(X{G}, -)` is defined with the
  `L`-valued points.

The universe `uL` occurs in the declarations of the Godement and resolution sections only
inside `w`, so Lean cannot infer it from the arguments. Uses therefore name the universes
explicitly, as in `quasiEtaleFamilyGodementFunctor.{u, v, uL} ρ points R`. This follows the
convention of `QuasiEtaleDivisibleSystem.{u, v, uL}`.

## Hypotheses of Harris's constructions

These are the hypotheses of `AnalyticQuasiEtale`, with the family `points` in place of the
`L`-valued points:

- `[PreservesFiniteLimits (quasiEtaleFamilyGodementFunctor ρ points R)]` and
  `[PreservesFiniteColimits (quasiEtaleFamilyGodementFunctor ρ points R)]`: the Godement
  functor is exact (Harris's Fact 3).
- `hpoints : JointlyFaithful fun i => (points i).sheafFiber`: the fibre functors of the
  family are jointly faithful on sheaves of `R`-modules on the quasi-étale site.
- `hF`: every `H^{r+1}_c(X{G}, G⁰(M))` vanishes, where `G⁰` is the Godement functor of the
  family. Godement sheaves are acyclic for `Γ_{c,X{G}}`.
- `hT`: `H^n_c(X{G}, T m) = 0` for all `m` and `n > D` (Harris's Fact 2). For the constant
  system it is `hC`: `H^n_c(X{G}, R / ℓ ^ m R) = 0` for `n > D`.

## Godement functor

- `quasiEtaleFamilyGodementFunctor ρ points R`: the published `discreteZeroFunctor` for the
  family `points`, an endofunctor of `QuasiEtaleGSheaf ρ (ModuleCat.{w} R)`. It is the
  discrete part of the induction to the twisted site of the product of the skyscraper
  sheaves at the points of the family.
- `quasiEtaleFamilyGodementCoaugmentation ρ points R`: its coaugmentation `𝟭 ⟶ G⁰`, the
  published `discreteCoaugmentation`.
- `mono_quasiEtaleFamilyGodementCoaugmentation ρ points R hpoints F`: the coaugmentation is
  a monomorphism (`mono_discreteCoaugmentation_of_jointlyFaithful`).
- `quasiEtaleGodementFunctor_eq_family L h`: the Godement functor of `AnalyticQuasiEtale`
  is the one of the family `quasiEtaleGodementPoints L h`, by `rfl`. So the
  `L`-point constructions are the special case of this module for that family.

## Godement resolutions (Lemma 2.2 and Lemma 2.2 bis)

Let `T : QuasiEtaleDivisibleSystem ρ R ℓ`.

- `quasiEtaleFamilyGodementResolutionSystem ρ points R T hpoints`: Harris's Lemma 2.2, the
  `ℓ`-divisible system of the Godement resolutions of the levels of `T`
  (`discreteGodementResolutionSystem`).
  `quasiEtaleFamilyGodementResolutionSystemObjIso m` identifies level `m` with the Godement
  complex `discreteComplexFunctor` of `T m`, and
  `quasiEtaleFamilyGodementResolutionSystem_obj_exactAt m q` says that level `m` is exact
  in degree `q + 1`.
- `quasiEtaleFamilyGodementTruncatedResolutionSystem ρ points R T hpoints D`: Harris's
  `G^•_m = τ_{≤ D+1}(F^•_m)` (Lemma 2.2 bis). Further:
  - `quasiEtaleFamilyGodementTruncatedResolutionHom`: the inclusion `G^• ⟶ F^•`, and
    `quasiIso_quasiEtaleFamilyGodementTruncatedResolutionHom m`: it is a
    quasi-isomorphism at every level.
  - `isZero_quasiEtaleFamilyGodementTruncatedResolutionSystem_obj_X m hq`: level `m` is
    zero in degrees `q > D + 1`.
  - `quasiEtaleFamilyGodementTruncatedAugmentation m`: the augmentation `T m ⟶ G^•_m`,
    and `quasiIso_quasiEtaleFamilyGodementTruncatedAugmentation m`: it is a
    quasi-isomorphism.

## Compact sections and (2.3)

These declarations also take `L`, `h`, `hF` and `hT`.

- `isZero_quasiEtaleGSheafCompactCohomology_familyTruncatedResolutionSystem_obj_X m i r`:
  `H^{r+1}_c(X{G}, G^i_m) = 0`.
- `quasiEtaleFamilyGodementTruncatedCompactSectionsSystem ρ points R L h T hpoints D hF hT`:
  the `ℓ`-divisible system of complexes `Γ_{c,X{G}}(G^•_m)` of smooth representations of
  `G`. By `quasiEtaleFamilyGodementTruncatedCompactSectionsSystem_obj m` (`rfl`), level `m`
  is `quasiEtaleGSheafCompactSections` applied termwise to `G^•_m`.
- `quasiEtaleFamilyGodementTruncatedCompactHomologyIso m n`: Harris's (2.3),
  `Hⁿ(Γ_{c,X{G}}(G^•_m)) ≅ H^n_c(X{G}, T m)`.

## Inverse limits: (2.6), (2.7) and Lemma 2.8

Here `G : Type u`. The further data are those of `TwistedInverseLimit`: a filtered preorder
`ι : Type u`, an antitone family `H : ι → Subgroup G` of normal subgroups of finite index
with `Fintype` and `DecidableEq` instances on the quotients, and Worker 61's hypotheses
`hK` (projections of the compact section system stay epimorphisms on `H i`-invariants) and
`hk` (the orders of the kernels of `G ⧸ H j ⟶ G ⧸ H i` are invertible in `R`).

- `quasiEtaleFamilyGodementQuotientInvariantsLimit`: Harris's `C^•_m`, an `ℓ`-divisible
  system of `ℤ`-graded complexes in `Rep R G`.
- `quasiEtaleFamilyGodementQuotientInvariantsLimitToRepExtend`: the comparison (2.6),
  `C^•_m ⟶ Γ_{c,X{G}}(G^•_m)`.
- `quasiIso_quasiEtaleFamilyGodementQuotientInvariantsLimitToRepExtend hS hP hR m htriv`:
  (2.6) is a quasi-isomorphism at level `m`, if every `H i` is a pro-`p` subgroup, `p` is
  invertible in `R` and every `H i` acts trivially on every `H^q_c(X{G}, T m)`.
- `quasiEtaleFamilyGodementQuotientInvariantsLimitHomologyIso hS hP hR m htriv n`: (2.7),
  `Hⁿ(C^•_m) ≅ SmoothRep.toRep.obj (H^n_c(X{G}, T m))`.
- `quasiEtaleFamilyGodementQuotientInvariantsInverseLimit`: Harris's `lim_m C^•_m`.
- `quasiEtaleFamilyGodementQuotientInvariantsInverseLimitHomologyIso hS hS' hP hR htriv hfin n`:
  Harris's Lemma 2.8,
  `Hⁿ(lim_m C^•_m) ≅ limit (m ↦ SmoothRep.toRep.obj (H^n_c(X{G}, T m)))` in `Rep R G`. It
  also takes `hS'` and the finiteness of every `H^q_c(X{G}, T m)` (`hfin`).

## The constant system

Let `hℓ : IsSMulRegular R (ℓ : R)`, and let `quasiEtaleConstantSystem ρ R hℓ` be the
constant system `R / ℓ ^ m R` of `AnalyticQuasiEtaleConstant`.

- `quasiEtaleConstantFamilyGodementResolutionSystem ρ points R hℓ hpoints`: Lemma 2.2 for
  the constant system.
- `quasiEtaleConstantFamilyGodementTruncatedResolutionSystem ρ points R hℓ hpoints D`:
  Lemma 2.2 bis for the constant system.
- `quasiEtaleConstantFamilyGodementTruncatedAugmentation m`: the augmentation
  `R / ℓ ^ m R ⟶ G^•_m` from the constant sheaf of the module `R ⧸ Ideal.span {ℓ ^ m}`. It
  is the augmentation of the general section precomposed with
  `quasiEtaleConstantSystemObjIso`. By
  `quasiIso_quasiEtaleConstantFamilyGodementTruncatedAugmentation m` it is a
  quasi-isomorphism.
- `quasiEtaleConstantFamilyCompactSectionsSystem ρ points R hℓ L h hpoints D hF hC`: the
  section system `Γ_{c,X{G}}(G^•_m)` for the constant system. The bound `hC` becomes the
  bound `hT` through `isZero_quasiEtaleGSheafCompactCohomology_constantSystem_obj`.
- `quasiEtaleConstantFamilyCompactHomologyIso m n`: Harris's (2.3),
  `Hⁿ(Γ_{c,X{G}}(G^•_m)) ≅ H^n_c(X{G}, R / ℓ ^ m R)`, the general (2.3) followed by
  `H^n_c` of `quasiEtaleConstantSystemObjIso`.
- `quasiEtaleConstantFamilyQuotientInvariantsInverseLimit`: `lim_m C^•_m` for the constant
  system (`G : Type u`).
- `quasiEtaleConstantFamilyQuotientInvariantsInverseLimitHomologyIso hS hS' hP hR htriv hfin n`:
  Harris's Lemma 2.8 for the constant system,
  `Hⁿ(lim_m C^•_m) ≅ limit (m ↦ SmoothRep.toRep.obj (H^n_c(X{G}, R / ℓ ^ m R)))`, where
  level `m` of the constant system stands for `R / ℓ ^ m R`. Its elaboration needs
  `synthInstance.maxHeartbeats 60000`.

## Provenance and correspondence

The constructions are those of `AnalyticQuasiEtale` and `AnalyticQuasiEtaleConstant`,
with the same correspondence to Harris. The only change is that Berkovich's embedding
`T ↪ F(T)` is modelled by the Godement functor of an arbitrary family of points. Taking an
arbitrary family, and the remark explaining why, are ours (derived infrastructure).

## Not formalized here

A family of points of the quasi-étale site satisfying `hpoints`, the remark on the
`L`-valued points, the other hypotheses listed above, Harris's spaces, groups and pro-`p`
subgroups, and the comparison with étale cohomology are not formalized here.
