# Finite generation of adic étale cohomology

## Source

J. S. Milne, *Étale Cohomology* (1980), Chapter V, §1, definition of
adic cohomology on printed p. 164 and Lemma 1.11 and its proof on pp.
165–166 (PDF pp. 178–180). Registered ID `milne-1980-etale-cohomology`,
SHA-256 `0a2f02682f82db8385e4dc6c77c7113900b11d6c313f34f8b354a3a9a391d232`.
Milne takes flat `ℤ/ℓ^mℤ`-sheaves forming an `ℓ`-adic system, with
finite étale cohomology at every stage and in every degree. The
limit cohomology is finitely generated over `ℤ_ℓ`, with short exact
reduction sequences. On p. 165 Milne also allows sheaves of modules
over the ring of integers in a finite extension of `ℚ_ℓ`.

Michael Harris, *Galois properties of cohomological automorphic forms on
GL(n)*, J. Math. Kyoto Univ. 39 (1999), §2 p. 304 (PDF p. 6), (2.1),
and §4 p. 311 (PDF p. 13), uses divisible torsion stages and their adic
limit. His `ℓ`-adic coefficient field is a product over the places above
`ℓ`, and the integral coefficient ring is the product of the rings of
integers of these local fields. The registered ID is
`harris-1999-cohomological-automorphic-forms-525a222c4489ee9d`, SHA-256
`525a222c4489ee9d3fe86a52c6c17ffb30fcc7c31f24c16a64669e9615085b26`.

## Statement

`Langlands.Cohomology.DivisibleSystem.EtaleAdicFiniteGenerationStatement`
is stated for an actual scheme `X`, a commutative coefficient ring `R`
complete for the ideal generated by the image of `ℓ`, and the two
separate derived-category instances for the module sheaves and the
coefficient modules. For an `ℓ`-divisible system `T` of étale module
sheaves, it assumes finiteness of `H^q(X, T_m)` as a set, for every
`m` and `q`. The `DivisibleSystem` axioms give the short exact torsion
sequences and the stagewise flatness condition in terms of the kernels
and images of powers of `ℓ`.

The cohomology object in the conclusion is the existing
`EtaleAdicCohomology.adicCohomology` applied to `T.tower`, hence
`H^q = lim_m H^q(X, T_m)`. It is `ℓ`-adically complete and finitely
generated as an `R`-module in every degree. The proposition
asserts that the actual `etaleReductionSequence` is short exact for
every `r` and `q`. Its first term is the cokernel of multiplication by
`ℓ^r` on `H^q`, its middle term is `H^q(X, T_r)`, and its last term
is the kernel of multiplication by `ℓ^r` on `H^{q+1}`. These are the
canonical maps of the reduction sequence, induced by projection and
the connecting maps of the torsion-stage sequences.

## Derivation and coefficient scope

The short exact sequence construction and its conditional exactness
are in [DerivedReductionSequence](DerivedReductionSequence.md).
Finiteness at the stages implies the Mittag–Leffler hypotheses for
exactness of the limits of the needed sequences. In particular the
canonical maps `H^q / ℓ^r H^q → H^q(X,T_r)` are injective and compatible
in `r`. Their compatibility identifies the limit of the reductions
of `H^q` with `H^q` itself. Indeed, a compatible family of reductions
gives a family of stage cohomology classes, hence an element of `H^q`.
The injections show that this element has exactly the specified
reductions. This also proves separatedness, since an element reducing
to zero at every stage of the cohomology system is zero. The reduction
modulo `ℓ` is finite. Lifts of a finite generating set of it generate
`H^q` by the complete version of Nakayama's lemma.

This reasoning works over the stated complete ring, including
`ℤ_ℓ` and the finite products of complete valuation rings used by
Harris. No assumption that `ℓ` itself is a uniformizer is needed. For
an extension of `ℚ_ℓ`, the powers of `(ℓ)` are cofinal with the powers
of the maximal ideal. The finite generation and exactness clauses
are conclusions. This module states the proposition and leaves its
proof and the geometric finiteness premises for applications separate.
