# The localization `ℤ_(p)`

For a prime number `p` (given as `[Fact p.Prime]`), the module
`Langlands.RingTheory.PrimeLocalIntegers` defines

```text
PrimeLocalIntegers p = Localization.AtPrime (Ideal.span {(p : ℤ)}),
```

the localization of `ℤ` at the prime ideal `pℤ`. This is the ring written
`ℤ_(p)` in Kottwitz, *Points on some Shimura varieties over finite fields*,
J. Amer. Math. Soc. 5 (1992), §5, printed p.389 (PDF p.17), source
`kottwitz-1992-points-shimura-finite-fields`, SHA-256
`c7cede3d0a5e201068e6100acbb3832adb67767d69248ec65458e7cc74ee46f1`:
"the localization of `ℤ` at the prime ideal generated by `p`". Exactly the
integers not divisible by `p` become units.

## Declarations

* `primeLocalIntegers_isPrime` is the instance that `pℤ` is a prime ideal,
  from Mathlib's `Ideal.span_singleton_prime` and
  `Nat.prime_iff_prime_int`.
* `PrimeLocalIntegers.mem_primeCompl_iff`: an integer is inverted exactly
  when `p` does not divide it. `natCast_mem_primeCompl` applies this to a
  natural number prime to `p`.
* `PrimeLocalIntegers.isUnit_natCast`: every natural number prime to `p` is a
  unit of `ℤ_(p)`. `ne_zero_of_coprime`: such a number is nonzero.
* `PrimeLocalIntegers.lift h` is the ring homomorphism from `ℤ_(p)` to any
  commutative ring in which every natural number prime to `p` is a unit,
  obtained from `IsLocalization.lift`; `isUnit_intCast_of_mem` checks the
  required unit condition for the integers not divisible by `p`, using
  `n = ±|n|`.
* `PrimeLocalIntegers.ringHom_ext`: any two ring homomorphisms out of
  `ℤ_(p)` into a semiring agree (ring maps out of `ℤ` are unique and the
  localization is an epimorphism). `lift_unique` is the corresponding
  uniqueness of the lift.
* `PrimeLocalIntegers.toRat` is the lift to `ℚ`, where every nonzero integer
  is a unit, and `toRat_injective` proves that it is injective, by
  `IsLocalization.lift_injective_iff`, injectivity of `ℤ → ℤ_(p)` (the
  inverted set contains no zero divisor) and injectivity of `ℤ → ℚ`. Thus
  `ℤ_(p)` is identified with a subring of `ℚ`.

## Scope

These are derived algebraic facts about a Mathlib localization; they add no
assertion from the literature beyond the definition of `ℤ_(p)`. The module
does not describe the image of `toRat` by denominators and does not
construct the completion `ℤ_p`, which is Mathlib's `ℤ_[p]`.
