# Proof of local finite dimensionality of field coalgebras

`Langlands.Subcoalgebra.localFiniteness.{u,v}` proves the Library proposition
`Langlands.Subcoalgebra.LocalFinitenessStatement.{u,v}`, imported from
`Langlands.RingTheory.Coalgebra.Subcoalgebra.LocalFinitenessStatement`. Its type
is exactly that proposition, with independent universe levels `u` (for the field)
and `v` (for the coalgebra).

## Statement proved

For every field `k : Type u`, every `C : Type v` with
`[AddCommGroup C] [Module k C] [Coalgebra k C]`, and every `c : C`, there is a
`D : Langlands.Subcoalgebra k C` with `c ∈ D` and `FiniteDimensional k D`. The
proof uses only Mathlib's coalgebra axioms (counit laws and coassociativity) and
the fact that `k` is a field. It needs no cocommutativity, algebra or Hopf
structure, characteristic, perfectness or splitting hypothesis, and no
finite-dimensionality of `C`. The zero coalgebra is not special-cased: the
argument below applies to every `C`.

## The implemented proof

Write `Δ` for `Coalgebra.comul` and `ε` for `Coalgebra.counit`. In Mathlib,
`f.rTensor C` applies `f` to the left tensor factor and `g.lTensor C` applies `g`
to the right one. All helpers are in namespace
`Langlands.Subcoalgebra.LocalFiniteness`.

1. **Slices.** For linear functionals `f, g : Module.Dual k C`, the proof
   defines the left slice `leftSlice f = lid ∘ (f ⊗ id) ∘ Δ` and the right slice
   `rightSlice g = rid ∘ (id ⊗ g) ∘ Δ`, both linear maps `C → C`. In Sweedler
   notation they are `x ↦ ∑ f(x₍₁₎) x₍₂₎` and `x ↦ ∑ g(x₍₂₎) x₍₁₎`.
   - `leftSlice_counit`, `rightSlice_counit`: the slices by `ε` are the identity
     (Mathlib's `Coalgebra.rTensor_counit_comul` and
     `Coalgebra.lTensor_counit_comul`).
   - `comul_leftSlice`: `Δ (leftSlice f x) = (leftSlice f ⊗ id)(Δ x)`, and
     `comul_rightSlice`: `Δ (rightSlice g x) = (id ⊗ rightSlice g)(Δ x)`. Both
     follow from coassociativity (`Coalgebra.coassoc_apply`,
     `Coalgebra.coassoc_symm_apply`) and an identity of linear maps on pure
     tensors, checked by `ext; simp`.
   - `leftSlice_rightSlice`: left and right slices commute. Both sides are a
     fixed map applied to the two equal iterated comultiplications of `x`.
   - `leftSlice_leftSlice`: `leftSlice h ∘ leftSlice f = leftSlice (h ∘ leftSlice f)`,
     and `rightSlice_rightSlice` is the right-handed version. Both follow from
     the previous intertwining identities.

2. **The candidate subspace.** `sliceSpan c` is the `k`-span of all two-sided
   slices `leftSlice f (rightSlice g c)`, for `f, g` ranging over
   `Module.Dual k C`. This is the two-sided `C*`-submodule generated by `c`.
   - `mem_sliceSpan`: `c` lies in it, since `c = leftSlice ε (rightSlice ε c)`.
   - `leftSlice_mem_sliceSpan` and `rightSlice_mem_sliceSpan`: it is stable
     under every left and right slice. On generators,
     `leftSlice h (leftSlice f (rightSlice g c)) = leftSlice (h ∘ leftSlice f) (rightSlice g c)`.
     For right slices, commute the slices first and then use
     `rightSlice_rightSlice`. Stability then extends to the span via
     `Submodule.span_le`.

3. **Finite dimensionality** (`finiteDimensional_sliceSpan`). By Mathlib's
   `TensorProduct.exists_finite_submodule_left_of_setFinite`, there is a
   finite-dimensional submodule `A ≤ C` such that `Δ c` is the image of some
   `y ∈ A ⊗ C`. Every right slice `rightSlice g c` then lies in `A`
   (`rid_lTensor_rTensor_subtype_mem`, by induction on `y`). Choose a finite
   spanning set `S` of `A` (`Module.Finite.iff_fg`, `Submodule.fg_def`). Apply
   `TensorProduct.exists_finite_submodule_right_of_setFinite` to the finite set
   `Δ '' S`: it gives a finite-dimensional `B ≤ C` such that each `Δ s`, for
   `s ∈ S`, is the image of an element of `C ⊗ B`. So every left slice of every
   `s ∈ S` lies in `B` (`lid_rTensor_lTensor_subtype_mem`), and hence every left
   slice maps `A` into `B`. So all generators `leftSlice f (rightSlice g c)` lie
   in `B`, giving `sliceSpan c ≤ B`, and `Submodule.finiteDimensional_of_le`
   concludes.

4. **Slice criterion** (`mem_range_map_subtype_of_slices`). For any subspace
   `D ≤ C` and `z ∈ C ⊗ C`, suppose all left slices `lid ((f ⊗ id) z)` and all
   right slices `rid ((id ⊗ f) z)` lie in `D`. Then `z` is in the range of
   `TensorProduct.map D.subtype D.subtype`. Let `q = D.mkQ : C → C ⧸ D`.
   - *Nondegeneracy* (`eq_zero_of_forall_lid_rTensor`,
     `eq_zero_of_forall_rid_lTensor`): a tensor in `C ⊗ W` (or `W ⊗ C`) all of
     whose slices by functionals on `C` vanish is zero. The proof takes the basis
     `Module.Basis.ofVectorSpace k C` and uses the injectivity of
     `TensorProduct.equivFinsuppOfBasisLeft`/`Right`. By
     `equivFinsuppOfBasisLeft_apply`/`Right_apply`, its coordinates are the
     slices by the coordinate functionals.
   - The left slices of `(id ⊗ q) z` are the images under `q` of the left slices
     of `z`, which are zero. So `(id ⊗ q) z = 0`, and likewise `(q ⊗ id) z = 0`.
   - `D → C → C ⧸ D` is exact (`LinearMap.exact_subtype_mkQ`) and `q` is
     surjective. Right exactness (`lTensor_exact`) therefore gives
     `z = (id ⊗ ι) y` for some `y ∈ C ⊗ D`, where `ι = D.subtype`.
   - `(id ⊗ ι)((q ⊗ id) y) = (q ⊗ id) z = 0`. Over a field `C ⧸ D` is flat, so
     `id ⊗ ι` is injective (`Module.Flat.lTensor_preserves_injective_linearMap`)
     and `(q ⊗ id) y = 0`. By `rTensor_exact`, `y = (ι ⊗ id) y'` for some
     `y' ∈ D ⊗ D`, so `z = (ι ⊗ ι) y'`.

5. **Closure under comultiplication** (`comul_mem_sliceSpan`). For
   `x ∈ sliceSpan c`, the left and right slices of `Δ x` are exactly
   `leftSlice h x` and `rightSlice h x`. By step 2 these lie in `sliceSpan c`,
   so step 4 puts `Δ x` in the range of
   `TensorProduct.map (sliceSpan c).subtype (sliceSpan c).subtype`. This is
   precisely the field `comul_mem'` of the published structure
   `Langlands.Subcoalgebra`.

6. **Conclusion** (`localFiniteness`). The witness is the published bundle
   `⟨sliceSpan c, fun x hx => comul_mem_sliceSpan c hx⟩ : Langlands.Subcoalgebra k C`.
   It contains `c` by step 2, and its actual carrier is finite dimensional by
   step 3. The carrier's module structure is the one inherited from the
   submodule in `Langlands.RingTheory.Coalgebra.Subcoalgebra.Basic`, so the
   `FiniteDimensional` instance of step 3 applies to it directly.

## Dependencies

The module imports the statement module, which brings in the published
`Langlands.RingTheory.Coalgebra.Subcoalgebra.Basic`. It also imports the Mathlib
modules `LinearAlgebra.Dual.Defs`, `LinearAlgebra.FiniteDimensional.Basic`,
`LinearAlgebra.TensorProduct.Basis`, `LinearAlgebra.TensorProduct.Finiteness`,
`LinearAlgebra.TensorProduct.RightExactness` and `RingTheory.Flat.Basic`. It
uses Mathlib commit `5ed2965256430c3649e86755f9576b54eca72435`. The proof does
not duplicate or modify the bundled-subcoalgebra construction, and it adds no
axioms. The final theorem depends only on `propext`, `Classical.choice` and
`Quot.sound`.

## Provenance

The statement's Markdown gives its source correspondence: J. S. Milne,
*Algebraic Groups* (2017), Proposition 4.7 and §9.d, via the regular comodule and
coefficient subcoalgebras. The task allows a different route. This proof does not
use subcomodules or coefficient subcoalgebras. Instead it takes the subspace
spanned by the two-sided slices of `c`, which is the classical
fundamental-theorem argument via the `C*`-bimodule generated by `c`, and it
proves the needed tensor-product facts directly from Mathlib. No source text was
copied, and no new source is cited. Worker 1502 wrote the Lean proof and this
description.
