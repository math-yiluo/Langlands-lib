# Unramified reduction of complex Weil representations

`Langlands.Weil.unramifiedReduction.{uW,uV}` proves the complete existing
proposition `Langlands.Weil.UnramifiedReduction.{uW,uV}` from
`Langlands.Weil.Representation.Structure`. It retains independent group and
carrier universes and all the statement's hypotheses: a topological group
with a surjective integer-degree homomorphism whose kernel is open, compact
and nonarchimedean, and a continuous finite-dimensional complex
representation. The general reduction includes dimension zero.

The proof constructs a commuting unramified representation correction with
finite-image product. For an irreducible representation it constructs an
arbitrary-complex continuous unramified scalar character and an irreducible
finite-image representation whose twist is the original representation on
the same space. It also proves that an irreducible representation has finite
image exactly when its determinant has one common positive finite order.
Frobenius lifts, polynomial roots and characters are constructed in the proof.

## Finite inertia and a central root

Surjectivity of the degree map `d` supplies a degree-one element `F`.
The published
`continuous_compact_nonarchimedean_representation_finite_range` applies to
the representation restricted to `d.ker`. Its continuity is composition
with the subtype inclusion. This gives the finite inertia image.

`degree_decomposition` writes every group element as an inertia element
followed by an integer power of `F`. The image of `F` normalizes the
finite inertia subgroup. Its conjugation automorphism has finite order,
so `central_power_of_finite_inertia` constructs a positive integer `m`
such that `rho(F)^m` centralizes inertia. It also commutes with `rho(F)`,
hence with the entire representation image by the decomposition.

The published `Langlands.complexPolynomialRoots` supplies a complex
polynomial `p` with `p(rho(F)^m)^m = rho(F)^m`.
`polynomial_root_unit` makes this polynomial evaluation a unit `b`:
its positive power is invertible, so it is invertible. Polynomial
evaluation commutes with every operator commuting with its argument.
Consequently `b` commutes with the full representation image.
`central_root_of_finite_inertia` combines these facts.

## The commuting correction

`commutingProduct` constructs the product homomorphism for two
homomorphisms whose images commute with one another. The degree correction
sends `w` to `b^(-degree(w))`, and `correctedRepresentation` multiplies the
original representation by this correction. The correction is trivial on
inertia. The corrected Frobenius has `m`th power one, because `b` commutes
with `rho(F)` and `b^m = rho(F)^m`.

`finite_image_of_finite_inertia` proves finiteness of the entire image
whenever inertia has finite image and the Frobenius image has finite order:
each value lies in the product of the finite inertia image and the finite
cyclic subgroup generated by Frobenius. This proves finiteness for the
corrected representation. Passing between endomorphism units and
endomorphisms uses injectivity of the forgetful map.

`degreeContinuous` uses the open kernel to show that the degree map is
constant near the identity and hence continuous. The correction is
continuous because it factors through the discrete degree group.
Continuity of multiplication in continuous linear endomorphisms gives
continuity of the corrected representation. `general_reduction_of_root`
assembles these facts into all six requirements of the general clause.

## Irreducibility and determinant

The companion module's `UnramifiedReductionAux.scalar_of_commuting_unit`
applies Schur's lemma to write the central unit as `b = z • 1` for
`z : ℂˣ`. The scalar character is `chi(w) = z^degree(w)`.
`correctedRepresentation_scalar_twist` proves that twisting the corrected
representation by this character recovers the original representation:
it passes the scalar through the units map of the scalar algebra map and
cancels inverse powers.

The character is continuous and trivial on inertia.
`irreducible_reduction_of_scalar_root` combines the twist identity with
the published `OperationClosure.irreducible_twist_iff` to prove
irreducibility of the corrected representation.

The companion module's
`finite_range_iff_determinant_order_of_scalar_twist` proves the determinant
criterion from this constructed twist witness. Finite-image representations
have determinants of a common finite order. Conversely, if the original
determinant has exponent `n`, the finite-image representation's determinant
has exponent `k`, and the dimension is `r > 0`, the determinant-under-twist
formula gives `chi(w)^(r*n*k) = 1` for all `w`. Genuine irreducibility
provides positive dimension. Complex roots of unity of a fixed positive
order form a finite set, so the character and the resulting twist have
finite image. The closing theorem assembles all three clauses.

## Provenance

This is a derived proof in the generic integer-extension setting of the
existing [statement](Structure.md). The group argument specializes
Henniart's argument to linear representations: section 2.3, Corollaries
2 and 3, printed page 158 (PDF 5), and sections 2.4-2.6, printed pages
159-160 (PDF 6-7). Tate section 2.2.1, printed page 10 (PDF 19), describes
the irreducible reduction for extensions of the integers by a profinite
group. The determinant criterion is the derived consequence explained
above, not a separately numbered theorem attributed to those passages.

The retained sources are Guy Henniart, *Representations du groupe de Weil
d'un corps local* (1980), literature ID `henniart-1980-weil-representations`,
SHA-256 `451a8bd31a58be9bf5ce49588503c2d229b1a73bf3e65ae0c7b2f491c1c4e017`;
and John Tate, *Number theoretic background*, Corvallis Part II (1979),
literature ID `tate-1979-number-theoretic-background`, SHA-256
`c890894fe19783890cc4def2c5bf92a18d71619acf8ba7005aefce4984fe70d6`.
The original reading covers Henniart PDF 5-7 and Tate PDF 18-19.
