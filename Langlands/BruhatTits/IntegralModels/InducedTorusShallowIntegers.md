# Integral coefficient algebras and normalized depth ideals

The finite coefficient fields of the induced torus inherit the valuation
from the fixed separable closure. The valuation ring of each is the
integral closure of the maximal unramified integers. Finite separability
makes it finite free over that discrete valuation ring. Tensoring the
product of the rings with the fraction field recovers the product of the
coefficient fields.

The valuation inequality defines an ideal in this product algebra. The
base uniformizer has normalized value one, so its image belongs to the
ideal at each depth at most one. This gives a common denominator for the
shallow positive-depth model.

These definitions realize the integral coefficient rings and depth condition
in the proof of Kaletha–Prasad, *Bruhat–Tits Theory: A New Approach* (2023),
Proposition B.3.6(1), printed p. 659 (one-based PDF p. 690). The ideal is
specified by the normalized inequality on every factor, which for a
ramification index \(e\) selects the power indexed by \(\lceil er\rceil\).
