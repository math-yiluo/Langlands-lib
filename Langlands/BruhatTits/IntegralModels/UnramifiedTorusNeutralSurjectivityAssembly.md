# Reduction of neutral lifting for exact sequences of tori

This internal reduction derives Kaletha–Prasad, *Bruhat–Tits Theory: A New
Approach*, Corollary B.8.6 (printed pp. 682–683 / PDF pp. 713–714), over
the maximal unramified field, from four explicit inputs. It does not prove
these inputs or the unconditional target.

`UnramifiedTorusNeutralSurjectivityAssemblyStatement` is a Prop-valued
definition of this conditional reduction at arbitrary universe `u`. Its
four premises are

1. `ConnectedGenerationStatement` (KP Lemma A.4.26, affine form; Library
   module `Langlands.GroupScheme.IntegralModel.ConnectedGeneration`);
2. `TorusQuotientRationalSurjectivityStatement`: a scheme-surjective
   homomorphism of tori with torus kernel is surjective on rational points
   over a field with trivial torus cocycles;
3. `UnramifiedTorusNeutralFiniteGenerationStatement`: finitely many points
   together with the neutral integral points generate `T(K)`;
4. `TorusSurjectionIsogenySectionStatement`: a scheme-surjective
   homomorphism `q` of tori admits `s` with `q ∘ s = [n]` for some `n > 0`.

Its conclusion is exactly the unchanged
`UnramifiedTorusNeutralSurjectivityStatement`. Premises 2–4 are new
statements, each in its own module with its own Markdown. The theorem
`unramifiedTorusNeutralSurjectivityAssembly` proves the implication.

## Route compared with the source

The source works over the completion `Ô`. It shows that `B^lft(Ô)/B^0(Ô)` is
finitely generated and applies Lemma A.4.25 (surjectivity on neutral points
of a complete DVR). It then descends to `O` by Lemma 8.1.2. The Lean proof
instead stays over the Henselian ring `O = fixedFieldIntegers F`. It applies
Lemma A.4.26 (printed pp. 631–632 / PDF pp. 662–663), which requires only a
Henselian base, to the image of the source neutral model in the target
neutral model. Lemma A.4.26 needs this image to be open and of finite index.
Openness comes from published smoothness and point-topology results.
Finite index comes from premises 2–4.

## The Lean proof

Let `K = inertiaFixedField F`, and let `DB`, `DC` be the supplied model data
for `B` and `C`.

* `fixedFieldIntegers_strictHenselian` gives that `O` is a strictly
  Henselian discrete valuation ring. The residue field is algebraically
  closed, being an algebraic closure of the residue field of `F`
  (`fixedFieldResidueIsAlgClosure`). Both neutral models are geometrically
  connected (`neutral_geometricallyConnected`). Their integral points lie in
  the character-valuation kernels, by `neutralPoints_le_full` and the
  hypotheses on the full models.
* *Model homomorphisms.* `map_characterValuationKernel_le` and the proved
  extension criterion `IntegralModel.smoothHomExtension` extend `q` to a
  homomorphism `DB.neutral → DC.full`. `DC.toNeutral` factors it through the
  neutral model, giving `ψ : DB.neutral → DC.neutral` over `q`. Premise 4
  gives `n > 0` and `s` with `s ≫ q = multiplication C n`. The same two
  steps extend `s` to a homomorphism `DC.neutral → DB.neutral` over `s`.
* `range_map_eq_comap` identifies the image of `ψ` on integral sections with
  the preimage, under the integral-point map of `DC.neutral`, of
  `q(B^0(O))`, the image under `q` of the neutral integral points of `B`.
* *Finite index.* The Library proves that `K` has dimension at most one
  (`henselianDiscreteDimensionLEOne`, using the Henselian valuation of `K`
  with algebraically closed residue field). Then
  `dimensionLEOneTorusCocycleVanishing` gives `HasTrivialTorusCocycles K`.
  Premise 2 makes `q : B(K) → C(K)` surjective, and premise 3 supplies a
  finite set `S` with `⟨S⟩ ⊔ B^0(O) = B(K)`. Hence `C(K)/q(B^0(O))` is a
  finitely generated commutative group generated by the image of `q(S)`.
  By `pow_mem_map_of_section`, the extended section shows that the `n`-th
  power of every point of `C^0(O)` lies in `q(B^0(O))`. The image of
  `C^0(O)` in this quotient is therefore a subgroup of exponent dividing `n`.
  Such a subgroup is finite by `finite_subgroup_of_pow_eq_one`: subgroups of
  finitely generated commutative groups are finitely generated, and
  finitely generated torsion commutative groups are finite. Hence the
  preimage of `q(B^0(O))` has finite index
  (`finiteIndex_comap_of_closure_sup` and
  `finiteIndex_comap_map_of_closure_sup`).
* *Openness.* `smooth_of_torus_kernel` shows that the underlying scheme map
  of `q` is smooth. It is flat (`IsTorus.flat_of_surjective_hom`), so it is
  an fppf quotient by its kernel, and that kernel is a smooth torus
  (`RelativeRoot.IsFppfQuotient.smooth_of_smooth_kernel`). In
  `exists_sectionCongruenceKernel_le_comap`, the neutral integral points
  of `B` are open in `B(K)` (`IntegralModel.isOpen_integralPoints_unramified`).
  The smooth map `q` is open on `K`-points (`isOpenMap_rationalPoint_of_smooth`
  with `henselianSmoothRationalPointOpenMap`), so `q(B^0(O))` is an open
  neighbourhood of `1` in `C(K)`. Cofinality of congruence subgroups
  (`IntegralModel.congruenceSubgroupAt_cofinal_unramified`) gives a positive
  `m` whose congruence kernel in `DC.neutral` lies in the preimage.
* *Lemma A.4.26.* The premise `ConnectedGenerationStatement` is applied with
  `X = DC.neutral.group`, the single smooth geometrically connected
  generator `DB.neutral.group` mapped by `ψ`, and the component
  representative `𝟙 X`. The identity is the relative identity component
  because `X` is geometrically connected
  (`isRelativeIdentityComponent_id_of_geometricallyConnected`). The
  conclusion says that the image of `ψ` is all of `X(O)`. Hence every point
  of `C^0(O)` lies in `q(B^0(O))`, which is the target inclusion.

The main theorem is elaborated under `set_option maxHeartbeats 400000`, a
finite raised limit.
