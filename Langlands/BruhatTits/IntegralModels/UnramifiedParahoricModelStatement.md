## Source content

`k` is a discretely valued field with Henselian integer ring and perfect
residue field. `K` is a maximal unramified extension of `k` with integer
ring `O`, and `G` is connected reductive. Section 8.3 works over `K` only,
writes `G` for `G_K`, and writes `B(G)` for the building of `G(K)`. Let
`Ω` be a nonempty bounded subset of an apartment `A` of `B(G)`.

- The opening of Section 8.3 announces that the groups of (7.7.1),
  including `G(K)^0_Ω ⊂ G(K)^1_Ω`, are schematic and that `G(K)^0_Ω` is
  connected. Their smooth affine models are written `𝒢^0_Ω ⊂ 𝒢^1_Ω`.
- Theorem 8.3.2 says `G(K)^1_Ω` is schematic. That is, it is the group of
  `O`-points of a smooth affine `O`-group scheme `𝒢^1_Ω` with generic fibre
  `G_K`.
- Theorem 8.3.13 says the relative identity component `𝒢^0_Ω` of `𝒢^1_Ω`
  satisfies `𝒢^0_Ω(O) = G(K)^0_Ω`.
- Proposition 7.7.5(1) identifies these groups for bounded `Ω`.
  `G(K)^1_Ω` is the pointwise stabilizer of `Ω` in `G(K)^1`, and `G(K)^0_Ω`
  is the pointwise stabilizer of `Ω` in `G(K)^0`.

Here `G(K)^1` is the common kernel of the valuations of the rational
characters of `G_K`. `G(K)^0` is the subgroup of KP Definition 2.6.23(1).

The building `B(G)` is the reduced building. Section 7.6 is titled "The
(Reduced) Building", and Definition 7.6.1 (printed p. 263 / PDF p. 294)
defines `B(G)` there. Section 7.7, where the chain (7.7.1) and
Proposition 7.7.5 live, starts from an apartment `A ⊂ B` of this building.

## Lean declarations

All types are in the arbitrary universe `u`. `F` is a nonarchimedean local
field. The source's `k`, `K` and `O` are `F`, the existing
`inertiaFixedField F` (the maximal unramified extension inside the chosen
separable closure) and `fixedFieldIntegers F`. A nonarchimedean local field
is complete and discretely valued with finite residue field. So `k = F`
satisfies the corrected Section 8.3 hypothesis: the residue field is
perfect. `G` is an affine group scheme over `F`. The generic group of the
models is `baseChanged F (inertiaFixedField F) G`. A private instance
records that this base change is again affine, by the pullback-stability
of affine morphisms.

`UnramifiedParahoricModelStatement.{u}` is an unproved proposition. Its
inputs are:

- `C : MaximalUnramified.BuildingData F (baseChanged F (inertiaFixedField F) G)`.
  These are the published supplied building data of `G_K` for the
  normalized integer valuation `MaximalUnramified.valuation F` of `K`. An
  element supplies the connected reductivity of `G_K`, a maximal split
  torus `S`, a `KPApartmentData` equipollence class with representing
  centralizer datum, and axiom V6 for that class. So the source's standing
  hypotheses on `G` are part of `C`. The reduced building of `C` is
  `C.ReducedBuilding`, the apartment is `C.Apartment`, and
  `C.apartmentMap : C.Apartment → C.ReducedBuilding` is the inclusion of
  the apartment.
- `E : UnramifiedZeroDatum F (baseChanged F (inertiaFixedField F) G)`. This
  is the published datum (maximally split maximal torus `T`, finite Galois
  splitting field `L` of `T`, simply connected cover of the derived group)
  from which `E.zeroPoints` computes `G(K)^0` (KP Definition 2.6.23(1)).
  `E.zeroPoints` is the subgroup generated by `G(K)^♮` and `T(K)^0`. This
  is the product `G(K)^♮ · T(K)^0` of Definition 2.6.23(1), because
  `G(K)^♮` is normal: Fact 2.6.21 (printed p. 104 / PDF p. 135), applied
  to the inner automorphisms of `G_K`, shows that they preserve `G(K)^♮`.
- `g` in the rational points of `G_K`, and `Ωa : Set C.Apartment`. They
  give the subset `Ω = {g • C.apartmentMap x | x ∈ Ωa}` of the translated
  apartment `g • A`. By the transport of apartments in KP
  Proposition 6.2.5 (printed pp. 215–216 / PDF pp. 246–247), `g • A` is
  the apartment of the maximal split torus `gSg⁻¹`. Any two maximal
  `K`-split tori are `G(K)`-conjugate (recalled in the KP Introduction,
  printed p. xvi / PDF p. 17), so every apartment of the reduced building
  has this form.
- Two hypotheses: `Ωa` is nonempty, and `Ωa` is bounded. Boundedness means
  every real affine function on `C.Apartment` is bounded above on `Ωa`.
  Since `-f` is affine whenever `f` is, every affine function is then
  bounded on `Ωa`. On KP's finite-dimensional real apartment this is
  ordinary boundedness. The action of `g` is an affine isomorphism of `A`
  onto `g • A`, so `Ω` is bounded in `g • A` exactly when `Ωa` is bounded
  in `A`.

The conclusion supplies
`D : RelativeComponentModelData (fixedFieldIntegers F) (inertiaFixedField F) _`.
This is the published structure of two smooth integral models `D.full` and
`D.neutral` of `G_K`. Both are affine, flat and of finite type over the
integers of `K`, and `D.neutral` has connected fibres. It also contains a
model morphism `D.inclusion : D.neutral.Hom D.full (𝟙 _)` exhibiting
`D.neutral` as the relative identity component of `D.full`. The two
equations are:

```lean
D.full.integralPoints =
  characterValuationKernel (MaximalUnramified.valuation F) _ ⊓ C.pointwiseFixer Ω
D.neutral.integralPoints = E.zeroPoints ⊓ C.pointwiseFixer Ω
```

`characterValuationKernel` is the published `G(K)^1`. `C.pointwiseFixer Ω`
is the pointwise fixer of `Ω` for the action of `G(K)` on the reduced
building `C.ReducedBuilding`. That is the building `B(G)` in which
Section 7.7 works. By Proposition 7.7.5(1) the right-hand sides are
therefore `G(K)^1_Ω` and `G(K)^0_Ω`. So `D.full` is KP's `𝒢^1_Ω`
(Theorem 8.3.2) and `D.neutral` is its relative identity component `𝒢^0_Ω`
(Theorem 8.3.13).

The neutral model is required to be affine. This adds nothing to the
source. Definition 4.1.18 (printed p. 162 / PDF p. 193) notes, citing
Prasad–Yu, that the relative identity component of a smooth affine group
scheme is affine. The opening of Section 8.3 also lists `𝒢^0_Ω` among the
smooth affine models.

`characterValuationKernel_inf_pointwiseFixer_singleton` is a proved lemma
that only unfolds definitions. It is an internal consequence of published
API, not a new source claim. For a single point `y`, the full-model
subgroup `G(K)^1 ⊓ C.pointwiseFixer {y}` equals the published one-group
stabilizer `C.oneStabilizer y`. The proof rewrites with
`IntegerValuationBuildingData.pointwiseFixer` and
`KPApartmentData.pointwiseFixer_singleton`.

## Choices and scope

- KP define `G(K)^0` from one maximally split maximal torus `T`. The
  statement quantifies over every datum `E`, which also chooses `L` and the
  simply connected cover. This is no strengthening, because none of these
  choices changes `E.zeroPoints`. Lemma 2.6.24 (printed p. 104 / PDF
  p. 135) says that `G(K)^0` does not depend on `T`. Lemma 2.5.12 (printed
  p. 89 / PDF p. 120) says that `T(K)^0`, the norm image of the maximal
  bounded subgroup of `T(L)`, does not depend on `L`. The image `G(K)^♮`
  of the simply connected cover does not depend on the chosen cover,
  since any two simply connected covers of the derived group are
  isomorphic over it. The Library does not prove these independence
  results. The statement needs them only to compare with the source.
- The statement also quantifies over every supplied `C`. This is no
  strengthening either. The class carried by `C` is an equipollence class
  of valuations of the root datum of `(G_K, S)` satisfying V6. That is
  KP's `ω`-compatibility (Section 6.1, printed p. 199 / PDF p. 230). KP
  Proposition 6.2.6 (printed p. 216 / PDF p. 247) says any two
  `ω`-compatible valuations are equipollent. So the class of `C` is KP's
  canonical apartment `A(S)` of Definition 6.1.27 (printed p. 210 / PDF
  p. 241). Remark 7.6.2 uses these apartments to build the canonical
  building of the quasi-split group `G_K`. The Library proves the rigidity
  step as
  `Langlands.Geometry.Building.IntegerValuation.IsOmegaCompatible.eq_of_hasOppositeFactorPairs`
  (module `IntegerOmegaApartmentRigidity`). That theorem keeps the
  existence of opposite factor pairs as an explicit premise. KP
  Proposition 2.11.17 (printed p. 136 / PDF p. 167) supplies such pairs.
  The choice of `S` is immaterial, since the translates `g • A` cover all
  apartments, as explained above.
- The statement does not assert uniqueness of the models, which KP derive
  from Corollary 2.10.11. It also says nothing about `G(K)^†_Ω`,
  `G(K)^b_Ω` (Proposition 8.3.3) or the generally nonaffine full-fixer
  model of Remark 8.3.4.
- Descent to `𝒪[F]` (KP Section 9.2) is not part of this statement. It is
  the separate statement `UnramifiedSmoothModelDescentStatement` (KP
  Fact 2.10.16).

## Intended consumers

The construction of the integral models for KP Axioms 4.1.20 and 4.1.22
(task `cd2ad671-3edf-4905-a0e2-e7aa2b160b91`) needs this statement. It
builds the models over the integers of the maximal unramified extension,
for the image of each bounded subset of the building of `G` under the
unramified descent embedding into the building of `G_K`. It then descends
them to `𝒪[F]`.
