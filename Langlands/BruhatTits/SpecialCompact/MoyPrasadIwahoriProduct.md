# The original depth product and literal Iwahori intersections

This internal derived unit defines `moyPrasadRadicalDepthProduct` on a supplied original `ParahoricBuildingData G`, its supplied centralizer filtration, an original apartment point, a standard root index and a nonnegative depth. The domain consists of the original generated negative radical-depth subgroup, the literal intersection of the original Moy–Prasad subgroup with the actual standard Levi, and the original generated positive radical-depth subgroup. Its value is their actual ordered multiplication in that same Moy–Prasad subgroup. The definition retains all selected roots, including doubled roots, through `standardRadicalDepth`. No surjectivity assertion is included in the definition.

The abstract group lemmas first distinguish a three-factor product using disjointness of the opposite subgroup and a containing parabolic subgroup, together with disjointness of the middle and positive subgroups. They use the published injectivity of multiplication of two disjoint subgroups. For two prescribed radical subgroups contained in a subgroup `K` and their respective ambient radicals, surjectivity of their multiplication with `K`'s literal middle intersection then identifies the two literal radical intersections. To prove each equality, the implementation compares a supplied product decomposition with the ambient decomposition whose other two entries are the identity. Ambient injectivity identifies the remaining entry with the original element. The same ambient uniqueness and supplied covering prove that the ordered multiplication of the three literal intersections is bijective.

For the actual standard subgroups, only one supplied standard cocharacter and negation stability of relative roots are needed for ambient uniqueness. The standard subgroup inclusions put the positive radical and the Levi in the dynamic parabolic. Negating the standard cocharacter puts the opposite standard radical in the opposite dynamic unipotent. Their intersection is trivial by the published affine opposite-intersection theorem. The actual standard Levi and radical have trivial intersection by the published dynamic-limit uniqueness argument. No topology, compactness, Iwahori existence or full dynamic realization is used for these uniqueness assertions.

Applying these results to `moyPrasadRadicalDepthProduct`, its surjectivity yields both exact equalities between the literal radical intersections and the generated original depth subgroups, and `HasIwahoriIntersectionDecomposition`. On connected reductive supplied data the relative-root negation theorem supplies negation stability. For a generic chamber and a face contained in its simple roots, published standard-cocharacter existence supplies the cocharacter. The previous root-depth preservation unit then yields weak-chamber contraction on every supplied actual compatible standard split component.

Surjectivity of the original depth product remains a substantive explicit hypothesis of every result that needs it. This unit does not prove the Moy–Prasad product theorem, construct a centralizer filtration, show openness or normality, or assert an available-data simultaneous basis. In particular it asserts no depth-zero Iwahori decomposition. The definitions make sense at depth zero, but none of the covering hypotheses is proved there or at positive depth.

Provenance is entirely internal: the abstract disjoint-subgroup multiplication theorem, the published standard subgroup inclusions, the opposite dynamic intersection theorem, uniqueness of the dynamic Levi/radical factors, and the published original root-depth preservation unit. There is no additional external literature assertion.
