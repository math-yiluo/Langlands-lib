# Descent from free operator modules to actual irreducible automorphic subquotients

A complex algebra action on a vector space supplies an action of the
underlying algebra as scalars. It commutes with the original complex
scalars in the required tower. Restricting scalars identifies the algebra
submodules with the complex subrepresentations of the multiplicative
action, since membership is preserved by exactly the same operators.
The order equivalence identifies simplicity and irreducibility.

For the stable matrix automorphic space, a submodule over the free
operator algebra therefore gives a subrepresentation of the pullback of
the original concrete action. Suppose a surjective operator-linear map
has simple target for the full free operator action. Restriction to
complex scalars gives a surjective intertwiner with irreducible target.
The denominator is the kernel of this intertwiner. The surjective
projection from the free algebra onto the concrete algebra carries the
invariant numerator and denominator to an actual irreducible automorphic
operator subquotient. If the target action on the finite translation
generators is the supplied finite adelic representation, the resulting
finite action is equivariantly equivalent to that representation.

This is an internal form of the descent to an automorphic subquotient
in Langlands, *On the notion of an automorphic representation* (1979),
proof of Proposition 2, printed pp. 205–206 (Corvallis Part I PDF pp. 200–201).
