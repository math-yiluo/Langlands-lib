# Classical representatives in nonzero cuspidal convolution eigenspaces

`Langlands.Automorphic.GroupSchemeFunctions.groupSchemeCuspidalConvolutionEigenfunctions`
proves `GroupSchemeCuspidalConvolutionEigenfunctionsStatement`. Consider a
connected reductive affine locally finite-type group over a number field
with a specified smooth relative dimension, a closed matrix representation
of positive size and a subgroup at infinity. The central morphism from a
specified finite split torus has injective rational-character restriction
with finite-index image. The full cusp space uses the parabolic-dynamic
assertion and right invariance of the norm-one Haar measure. Suppose
archimedean conjugation fixes the center of the complex enveloping algebra. If a smooth compact adelic convolution
acts compactly on the full cuspidal Hilbert space and commutes with that
subgroup, each vector in an eigenspace for a nonzero eigenvalue has a
continuous representative on the positive rational quotient. Its adelic
pullback is a classical automorphic function, with all proper radical
periods zero and a uniform polynomial exponent for the real directional
derivative words.

For an eigenvalue `c ≠ 0`, convolution on the Hilbert vector multiplied by
`c⁻¹` gives its smooth representative. Full support of the quotient Haar
measure makes the continuous representative unique and identifies the
continuous and Hilbert convolution equations. The finite-dimensional image
of the eigenspace in the smooth function space is invariant under the
subgroup at infinity by commutation of the Hilbert operators. For an
operator from the enveloping center, differentiating convolution moves the
operator to a new compact test kernel, which supplies a cuspidal Hilbert
class for the derivative. Center conjugation invariance preserves the
pointwise convolution equation, so that class is in the same eigenspace.
The two preserved actions give the respective finite orbit spans. A global
height estimate on the smoothing gives uniform derivative growth, and
continuous representatives of full cusp classes have pointwise vanishing
proper periods.

The finite-dimensional nonzero eigenspace argument uses the compact
operator facts recalled in Getz–Hahn, *An Introduction to Automorphic
Representations* (2024), §9.3, pp. 246–247, around (9.12). The representatives
and their center finiteness are established here using the actual adelic
smoothing and differentiation actions.
