# The adjoint action of \(G(F_\infty)\) fixes the center of the enveloping algebra

Source: Bernstein–Lapid, *On the meromorphic continuation of Eisenstein series*, J. Amer. Math. Soc. 37 (2024), proof of Lemma 8.1 (§8.1, pp. 221–222). In the proof of the "if" direction of Lemma 8.1, BL write: "Hence \(V\) is finite-dimensional by Theorem 7.2. Clearly, \(V\) is \(\mathfrak z\)-invariant." The space \(V\) is defined by a Harish-Chandra equation \(\delta(h)\phi=\phi\). Its \(\mathfrak z\)-invariance uses that every central element \(Y\) of the enveloping algebra commutes with \(\delta(h)\). Differentiating \(\delta(h)\phi\) gives \(\int h(g)\,(\mathrm{Ad}(g^{-1})Y\,\phi)(xg)\,dg\). So the argument needs \(\mathrm{Ad}(g)Y=Y\) for every \(g\in G(F_\infty)\). This module states that fact. BL use it without comment.

## The statement `Langlands.Automorphic.GroupSchemeFunctions.AdelicEnvelopingCenterConjugationStatement`

For every number field \(F\), every affine group scheme \(G\) over \(F\) with a smooth structure `hsmooth` (smooth of relative dimension \(d\)) that is connected reductive (`IsConnectedReductive G`), every point \(g\) of the infinite adelic group `InfiniteAdelicGroupPoints F G` \(=G(F_\infty)\), and every element \(z\) of `AdelicEnvelopingCenter F G hsmooth`, we have
\[
\mathrm{Ad}(g)\,z = z .
\]
Here `AdelicEnvelopingCenter F G hsmooth` is the center of `AdelicEnvelopingAlgebra F G hsmooth`. That algebra is the universal enveloping algebra of the complexification \(\mathbb C\otimes_{\mathbb R}\mathfrak g_\infty\) of the Lie algebra \(\mathfrak g_\infty\) of the real Lie group \(G(F_\infty)\). The automorphism \(\mathrm{Ad}(g)\) is the Library's `adelicEnvelopingConjugation F G hsmooth g`. It is the enveloping extension of the complexified tangent conjugation of \(g\) (module `GroupSchemeInfiniteTranslation`). The proposition is universe-polymorphic in the universe `u` of \(F\).

The Library already proves that \(\mathrm{Ad}(g)\) maps the center to itself (`adelicEnvelopingConjugation_mem_center`, for every \(G\)). This statement asserts that \(\mathrm{Ad}(g)\) fixes the center pointwise when \(G\) is connected reductive.

## Why it holds

The real Lie algebra \(\mathfrak g_\infty\) is the Lie algebra of the real points of the restriction of scalars \(H=\mathrm{Res}_{F/\mathbb Q}G\). Its complexification is the Lie algebra of the complex group \(H(\mathbb C)\cong\prod_{\sigma:F\to\mathbb C}G^\sigma(\mathbb C)\). Since \(G\) is connected, every factor is connected, so \(H(\mathbb C)\) is a connected complex Lie group. The element \(g\in H(\mathbb R)\subseteq H(\mathbb C)\) acts on \(U(\mathfrak g_\infty\otimes\mathbb C)\) through the adjoint action of \(H(\mathbb C)\). The differential of that action is the derivation \(\mathrm{ad}\), which kills every central element. A connected group is generated by any neighbourhood of the identity, and on a neighbourhood of the identity \(\mathrm{Ad}(\exp X)=\exp(\mathrm{ad}X)\). Hence every element of \(H(\mathbb C)\) fixes the center pointwise.

Connectedness of the algebraic group is necessary. The real group \(G(F_\infty)\) itself may be disconnected, and for disconnected real Lie groups the adjoint action can move central elements. For example, the reflection in \(\mathrm O(2)\) acts by \(-1\) on the center \(\mathbb C[\mathfrak{so}(2)]\) in degree one.
