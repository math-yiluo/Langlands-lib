# Discrete decomposition of the compact positive rational quotient

The theorem
`Langlands.Automorphic.GroupSchemeFunctions.groupSchemeCompactQuotientL2Decomposition`
in this module proves
`Langlands.Automorphic.GroupSchemeFunctions.GroupSchemeCompactQuotientL2DecompositionStatement`.

## Hypotheses and conclusion

The standing hypotheses are those of the statement, described in its page
`GroupSchemeCompactQuotientL2DecompositionStatement.md`:

- `F` is a number field and `G` is an affine group scheme over `Spec F`,
  locally of finite type. Neither connectedness nor reductivity of `G` is
  assumed.
- `T` is a commutative group scheme with an isomorphism `e` onto the split
  torus indexed by the finite type `I`. The morphism `i : T.toGrp ⟶ G` is
  central (`hc`). Its character restriction is injective (`hi`) and has
  finite-index range.
- The Haar measure of `normOneSubgroup F G` is right invariant.
- The Library's positive rational quotient `PositiveRationalQuotient F G T i`
  is compact.

Under these hypotheses take the quotient measure
`positiveRationalQuotientHaarMeasure F G T i` and the right action
`haarQuotientL2BoundedRightAction` of the adelic points on its `L²`, both
unchanged. The theorem proves two things. First, every integrable compactly
supported continuous weight on the adelic points integrates the action to a
Hilbert–Schmidt operator. Second, the `L²` space is a Hilbert sum of
topologically irreducible closed invariant subspaces, and each summand is
unitarily equivalent, as a representation, to only finitely many summands.

## The implemented proof

The proof specializes two theorems about general compact left coset spaces,
both in the namespace `Langlands.QuotientIntegration`.
`compactQuotientIntegratedHilbertSchmidt`, in
`Langlands.Analysis.Local.CompactQuotientHilbertSchmidtProof`, proves
`CompactQuotientIntegratedHilbertSchmidtStatement`.
`compactQuotientDiscreteDecomposition`, in
`Langlands.Analysis.Local.CompactQuotientDiscreteDecompositionProof`, proves
`CompactQuotientDiscreteDecompositionStatement`. Neither the measures nor the
action are changed. The inputs are these.

1. *Subgroup and measures.* The closed subgroup is
   `positiveRationalSubgroup F G T i`, generated by the positive real points
   of `T` and the rational points of `G`. It is closed by
   `isClosed_positiveRationalSubgroup`. Its measure is
   `positiveRationalSubgroupHaarMeasure`. That measure is a Haar measure, is
   σ-finite and is right invariant, by the theorems
   `positiveRationalSubgroupHaarMeasure_isHaarMeasure`,
   `positiveRationalSubgroupHaarMeasure_sigmaFinite` and
   `positiveRationalSubgroupHaarMeasure_isMulRightInvariant`. The ambient
   measure is the Haar measure of `AdelicGroupPoints F G`. The quotient
   carries its Borel structure, `positiveRationalQuotient_borelSpace`. Its
   measure is locally finite by
   `positiveRationalQuotientHaarMeasure_isLocallyFiniteMeasure`.
2. *Weil compatibility.* The theorem
   `positiveRationalQuotientHaarMeasure_isCompatible`, in
   `Langlands.Global.AdelicSplitCenterWeil`, states that the quotient measure,
   the subgroup measure and the ambient Haar measure satisfy the unfolding
   identity `IsCompatible`. This is the identity required by both general
   theorems.
3. *The action as right translation.* The original action is the right
   pullback `quotientL2RightTranslate` along
   `Langlands.Global.LeftCosetSpace.rightTranslate`, with the right
   invariance `haarQuotientMeasureRightInvariant`. By
   `coeFn_quotientL2RightTranslate`, each `π a φ` is almost everywhere equal
   to `q ↦ φ (LeftCosetSpace.rightTranslate H a q)`. On a coset `H x` both
   `LeftCosetSpace.rightTranslate` and
   `Langlands.QuotientIntegration.rightTranslate` give `H (x a)`, so after
   induction on the quotient the two functions agree pointwise by
   definition. Hence `π a φ` is almost everywhere equal to
   `q ↦ φ (rightTranslate H a q)`, as both general theorems require.
4. *Hilbert–Schmidt property.* `compactQuotientIntegratedHilbertSchmidt` takes
   the compactness hypothesis, the norm preservation
   `norm_haarQuotientL2BoundedRightAction` and the strong measurability of
   orbits `haarQuotientL2Orbit_aestronglyMeasurable`. It then makes
   `integratedIsometricAction μG π … f hf` Hilbert–Schmidt for every
   integrable compactly supported continuous `f`. By definition this
   operator is `haarQuotientL2IntegratedAction F G T i hi I e hc μG f hf`.
   This is the first conjunct.
5. *Discrete decomposition.* `compactQuotientDiscreteDecomposition` takes the
   same data. It also uses inner-product preservation, which is
   `LinearIsometry.inner_map_map` for `quotientL2RightTranslate`, and
   continuity of orbits, which is the continuity of
   `haarQuotientL2OrbitMap`. It returns the set `A` of the second conjunct
   with its irreducibility, Hilbert-sum and finite-multiplicity clauses
   unchanged.

The proof uses only the axioms `propext`, `Classical.choice` and
`Quot.sound`.

## Original source

J. R. Getz and H. Hahn, *An Introduction to Automorphic Representations*
(2024), registered as `getz-hahn-2024-automorphic-representations`, SHA-256
`a125cf9c0f445972cbbd3552b4fb9905ca948108a05880f90eaa43558d09a507`.

- (2.19)–(2.22), printed pp. 68–69 (one-based PDF pp. 85–86), define
  `G(A_F)^1`, the subgroup `A_G`, the adelic quotient
  `[G] = A_G G(F) \ G(A_F)` with its right action, and its comparison with
  `G(F) \ G(A_F)^1`. `PositiveRationalQuotient F G T i` is the Library's
  version of `[G]`: the positive real points of the central split torus `T`
  play the role of `A_G`.
- Lemma 9.3.1, printed p. 247 (one-based PDF p. 259), deduces a Hilbert
  direct sum decomposition into irreducible subrepresentations with finite
  multiplicities from compactness of the operators `R(f_n)` of a Dirac
  sequence. Corollary 9.3.2 on the same page gets that compactness from the
  fact that Hilbert–Schmidt operators are compact.
- Lemma 9.3.3, printed pp. 247–248 (one-based PDF pp. 259–260), proves in its
  argument that `R(f)` is Hilbert–Schmidt, from a uniform bound on point
  evaluations and finite volume of `[G]`.

The proof here applies the analytic argument to the whole `L²` of the
actual compact quotient, for the general `G` of the statement. It uses no
cuspidal subspace. Lemma 9.3.3 requires the estimate (9.13),
`‖R(f)φ‖_∞ ≪_f ‖φ‖₂`, on the subspace considered. In the imported
Hilbert–Schmidt proof the point evaluations of `R(f)` on the whole `L²` are
given by a continuous family of vectors on the quotient. Compactness bounds
their norms uniformly and makes the quotient volume finite.

Getz and Hahn's cuspidal subspace (6.20)–(6.21), printed p. 168 (PDF
p. 182), is the space of `φ ∈ L²([G])` whose constant terms along every
proper parabolic subgroup vanish. It is set up for a reductive group `G`
(the opening of Chapter 6, printed p. 157, PDF p. 171, and §9.1, printed
p. 239, PDF p. 251), and parabolic subgroups are defined in §1.9 only for
reductive groups. In the book reductive groups are always connected
(Definition 1.5.7, printed p. 15, PDF p. 32). For connected reductive `G`
over a number field, compactness of `[G]` leaves no proper rational
parabolic subgroup. So the condition (6.21) is vacuous and the cuspidal
subspace is all of `L²([G])`. The argument from the book runs as follows.

- Under the hypotheses of Lemma 2.6.2, the map (2.22) is a homeomorphism
  of `G(F) \ G(A_F)^1` onto `[G]` (printed p. 69, PDF p. 86).
- By Theorem 2.6.3 on the same page, compactness then means that every
  `F`-split torus of `G` lies, after base change to an algebraic closure,
  in the radical of the base-changed group.
- For a reductive group the radical is a central torus, and the centre
  meets the derived group in a finite group scheme (printed p. 15, PDF
  p. 32). So the derived group contains no nontrivial split torus, that
  is, it is anisotropic.
- By §1.9 (printed pp. 32–33, PDF pp. 49–50), the derived group is
  anisotropic exactly when `G` is its only parabolic subgroup.

This chain is neither formalized nor used here. The exact statement allows
affine groups that need not be connected or reductive. For these the book
defines no parabolic subgroups and no cuspidal subspace, and the theorem
concerns the whole `L²`.

The weights are compactly supported continuous functions on `G(A_F)` and
not smooth functions on `A_G \ G(A_F)`. No trace-class assertion is made.
The October 2026 author errata, `getz-hahn-2024-errata-2026-10`, have
entries for §1.10, §6.6, §9.2 and §9.5, among others. They list no
correction to Definition 1.5.7, §1.9, Lemma 2.6.2, (2.19)–(2.22),
Theorem 2.6.3, the opening of Chapter 6, (6.20)–(6.21), §9.1,
Lemmas 9.3.1 and 9.3.3 or Corollary 9.3.2.
