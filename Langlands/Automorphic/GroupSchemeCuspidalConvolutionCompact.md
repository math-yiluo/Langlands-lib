# Compact smooth convolution on the cuspidal Hilbert space

`groupSchemeCuspidalConvolutionCompact` proves
`GroupSchemeCuspidalConvolutionCompactStatement`. On the full almost-everywhere
cuspidal `L²` space of an affine locally finite-type connected reductive
group over a number field, every compact smooth adelic kernel acts by a
Hilbert–Schmidt operator and therefore by a compact operator. The statement
uses a positive-size closed matrix embedding, the canonical radical family
from the parabolic-dynamic assertion, and right invariance of norm-one Haar
measure. The central morphism comes from a specified finite split torus and
has injective rational-character restriction with finite-index image.

The compact finite-slice estimates for smoothing extend to the full group
by rational reduction with polynomial control of the adelic height.
Each right-orbit derivative preserves rational left invariance. The
continuous representative of a smoothed cusp class is pointwise cuspidal.
For a proper represented radical, uniqueness of its ambient image
identifies the representative-based average with the canonical normalized
constant term. The resulting smooth function has one polynomial exponent
for all directional derivatives. It is bounded on the norm-one quotient
by cuspidal rapid decay on the Siegel cover.

The quotient convolution kernel gives continuous linear point evaluations
on the cuspidal Hilbert space. The individual bounds for every input
vector imply a uniform bound on the operator norms of these evaluations
by the uniform boundedness principle. The quotient has finite measure,
so the uniform evaluation criterion gives Hilbert–Schmidt summability.
The isometric inclusion into the ambient `L²` space preserves the basis
sum. The Hilbert–Schmidt compactness theorem finishes the proof.

Getz–Hahn, *An Introduction to Automorphic Representations* (2024),
Lemma 9.3.3 and pp. 249–251, deduce Hilbert–Schmidt summability from the
boundedness of cuspidal convolution. Here that boundedness comes from
pointwise rapid decay and uniform boundedness of the evaluation maps.
