# Taylor coefficients in the actual uniform moderate growth space

Fix an open finite level and one matrix-height exponent in the Library's uniform moderate growth space. The product of the weighted derivative maps induces the growth topology and has closed range. For a weakly holomorphic curve on an open subset of the complex plane, each weighted coordinate is analytic in its Banach norm. The first derivative therefore exists in the product as the limit of the difference quotients. Closedness places that derivative in the original growth space. Convergence in the defining topology shows that any continuous linear scalar test commutes with this derivative.

Repeating the argument for the derivative curve gives `taylorCoefficient`, with factorial normalization. Each coefficient has the original growth exponent and finite level. `apply_taylorCoefficient` identifies its value under every continuous linear scalar test, including evaluation of any genuine enveloping derivative at an adelic point.

The construction is derived in the Library's matrix-height topology. Bernstein–Lapid, §3.1, printed p. 192, use continuous linear tests for weak holomorphy. Their growth-space topology in §7.1, printed pp. 210–211, uses the exponential of a minimal-parabolic height on a Siegel domain. Applying their continuation corollary at the end of §8.6, printed p. 226, to supply the Library's holomorphic curve requires a comparison of the growth scales that allows the exponent to change. The smooth, growth-bounded Taylor vectors are the kind of regularized coefficients used in Langlands' proof of Proposition 2, printed p. 206 (Corvallis Part I PDF p. 201).
