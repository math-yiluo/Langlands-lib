# Harish-Chandra finiteness on the archimedean arithmetic quotients

For a connected reductive group over a number field, let \(L\) be the
prescribed compact open subgroup of the finite adelic group and let \(b\)
be the chosen finite point. The arithmetic subgroup is the image in
\(G(F_\infty)\) of the rational points whose finite components stabilize
the coset \(bL\). The theorem `arithmeticHarishChandraFiniteness` proves
`ArithmeticHarishChandraFinitenessStatement.{u}` at arbitrary universe.
It gives finite dimension for the complex span of plot-smooth functions
invariant under that subgroup,
with polynomial growth in the normalized height of the supplied closed
matrix representation, annihilated by the fixed cofinite center ideal
under the actual differential action, and with every right profile on the
prescribed maximal compact in the fixed finite-dimensional profile space.

The proof applies `arithmeticHarishChandraFiniteness_of_leviCenterRestriction`
to `parabolicLeviCenterRestriction`, which gives finite restriction of
the actual archimedean enveloping centers. The
parabolic tangent grading identifies the Levi Lie algebra with the zero weight
space and the radical Lie algebra with the sum of the positive weight spaces.
The PBW projection is finite on centers. Its error lies in the right ideal
generated by the radical tangent directions, whose operators vanish on Levi
restriction of left radical-invariant functions. At a fixed archimedean
translate, compose the projection with the corresponding adjoint automorphism
of the ambient center.

`GroupSchemeArithmeticHarishChandraReduction` extends each arithmetic
function to the rational adelic quotient, with support in the rational
orbit of \(bL\). On each finite slice the extension is zero or a left
pullback of the input. The actual enveloping action commutes with left
pullback because its infinitesimal generators differentiate right orbits.
This preserves the original annihilator and, by associativity, the
original compact profile condition. The extension is fixed by \(L\).
Matrix height comparison and the slice-to-adelic growth theorem give
moderate growth. Restriction at \(b\) recovers the original function, so
the image of the finite-dimensional adelic span contains the arithmetic
span.

On the rational quotient, use strong induction on Lie dimension. For a
positive-size closed matrix realization, the fixed data cut out a
submodule of the actual finite plot-smooth rational functions of moderate
growth. For matrix size zero, closedness of the realization makes the
adelic point group trivial, so the space of scalar functions already has
finite dimension.

Map the submodule to the product of its proper standard constant-term
images. Each constant term is the integral along the actual unipotent
radical quotient with its probability measure. The integral commutes
with the actual archimedean differentiation and preserves the growth,
level, and profile bounds. Evaluating profiles at compact points gives
the right compact translates. For each constant term they all lie in
the image of a linear map from the dual of the finite-dimensional
profile space. The archimedean component of the adelic Iwasawa subgroup
is the prescribed maximal compact. Finitely many
translated restrictions to the actual Levi then detect the parabolic
function. Finite center restriction gives a common cofinite annihilator
on each such restriction. The Levi is a proper smooth closed subgroup
of smaller Lie dimension, so its fixed-data span is finite-dimensional
by induction. There are finitely many standard parabolics. Rational
conjugacy makes vanishing of their proper constant terms sufficient for
cuspidality.

`GroupSchemeFixedProfileCuspidalFiniteness` proves finiteness of the
kernel. The adapted Casimir and the compact profile equations give one
elliptic equation and uniform local derivative estimates. Use as the
size of a cuspidal function the supremum on the norm-one subgroup of
the derivatives from a lifted basis of the center quotient. Individual
growth and cuspidal decay make this supremum finite. Along the positive
split central factor, multiplication in the center quotient gives a
finite system of linear differential equations. Its solution bound
turns the size into a uniform polynomial growth bound. Family rapid
decay bounds the derivatives by half the size outside a common compact
height sublevel on rational representatives of the norm-one subgroup.
On that compact sublevel the local derivative estimates and finite
level give a finite family of evaluations that detects the derivatives.
If the evaluations vanish, the supremum is zero, and the split-center
bound forces the function to vanish. Finiteness of the kernel and of
the constant-term image gives finiteness on the rational adelic quotient.

Harish-Chandra proves finiteness on the real arithmetic quotient by
induction on rational rank using proper constant terms and their cuspidal
kernel. See *Automorphic Forms on Semisimple Lie Groups* (1968), I §2,
Theorem 1, and I §6, printed pp. 8 and 17–20 (PDF pp. 18 and 27–30).
The adelic induction above adapts that argument using Lie dimension.
