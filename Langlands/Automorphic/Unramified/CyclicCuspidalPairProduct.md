# Partial cuspidal pair products over cyclic character powers

Worker 539. This result formalizes the passage from the local denominator identity to the comparison of partial Rankin–Selberg products in Arthur–Clozel, *Simple Algebras, Base Change, and the Advanced Theory of the Trace Formula* (1989), Chapter III, Theorem 3.1, printed p. 201 (PDF p. 215). The statement uses two genuine cuspidal presentations of the same rank to vary the first argument of the pair L-function and a common genuine cuspidal presentation for its second argument. It assumes a cofinite comparison of powered spectra, with a positive exponent dividing the number of twists and equal to the order of the character at the local uniformizer. It supplies a finite omitted set admissible for all the actual twists and the common second presentation. For any supplied partial pair L-functions with this omitted set, the two sums of their meromorphic orders at `1` agree. Existence and individual orders of the partial pair L-functions enter the fibre argument separately.

The omitted set includes the places where the local premises fail and the exceptional sets of the actual twisted presentations and the common second presentation. At a remaining place, the twisted spectra are the original spectra scaled by the respective powers of the local uniformizer value. The local cyclotomic product identity expresses both denominator products through the powered spectra and the same multiplicity. Thus the products of the reciprocal Euler factors agree. The finite families of right half-planes have a common right half-plane. On it, `hasProd_prod` and uniqueness of the Euler product identify the finite products of the two families of partial functions. Meromorphic continuation identifies their germs at `1`, and the order of each finite product is the sum of its factors' orders.
