# Equal local traces on norm determinants in a fibre of weak base change (statement)

Module `Langlands.Automorphic.Unramified.WeakBaseChangeFiberNormTraceStatement`, namespace
`Langlands.Automorphic.Unramified`. It contains one proposition definition and no proof:

- `Langlands.Automorphic.Unramified.WeakBaseChangeFiberNormTraceStatement.{uF, uE, uV, uW}`.

In words: let `E/F` be a cyclic extension of number fields and `Π` a cuspidal automorphic
representation of `GL_n(𝔸_E)` (`n = r + 1`). If `Π` is a weak base change of two cuspidal
automorphic representations `π` and `π'` of `GL_n(𝔸_F)`, then at every finite place `v` of `F`
their local components have the same trace

`tr π_v(f) = tr π'_v(f)`

on every test function `f` on `GL_n(F_v)` whose support lies in `det⁻¹(N((F_v ⊗_F E)^×))`, for
every Haar measure on `GL_n(F_v)`. This is a formulation, for test functions, of the sentence
"On the image of the norm, their local characters coincide" of Arthur–Clozel 1989, Chapter 1,
§6.3(b) (printed p. 57), applied to the representations of a fibre of base change.

The module declares five `local instance`s of `AddCommGroup`: on the Library's form spaces
`Langlands.Automorphic.RationalInvariantFunctions`, `ContinuousRationalFunctions`,
`FiniteSmoothRationalFunctions` and `AutomorphicForms`, and on the carrier `A.Space` of a
`LocalRealization`. They are `Submodule.addCommGroup _`, as in the Library's
`Langlands.Automorphic.SupercuspidalComponent`. They are not exported.

## Statement

For all `F`, `E`, `σ`, `l` with `IsCyclicAlgebra σ l`, all `r`, `T`, `S`, `S'` with
`IsWeakBaseChangeOf T.isoClass S.isoClass` and `IsWeakBaseChangeOf T.isoClass S'.isoClass`, all
presentations `D` of `S` and `D'` of `S'` and every finite place `v` of `F` such that
`D.localComponent v` and `D'.localComponent v` are irreducible, and all admissibility proofs
`hπ`, `hπ'`: for every Haar measure `μ` on `GL_{r+1}(F_v)` and every
`f : SchwartzBruhat (GL_{r+1}(F_v))` with `GeneralLinear.determinant g ∈ N((F_v ⊗_F E)^×)`
whenever `f g ≠ 0`,

`Character.distribution μ (D.localComponent v).representation hπ f =
  Character.distribution μ (D'.localComponent v).representation hπ' f`.

Remarks on the form.

- **Any finite place.** No hypothesis on `v` is made: `v` may be split, inert, ramified, or have
  several places above it. The norm group `N((F_v ⊗_F E)^×)` is the image of the product of the
  local norms `E_w^× → F_v^×` over the places `w | v`, which equals each of them since `E/F` is
  Galois.
- **Any presentations.** `D` and `D'` are arbitrary presentations in the universe `uV` with
  irreducible components at `v`; the Library's
  `CuspidalSpectralPresentation.nonempty_localComponent_equiv` (module
  `Langlands.Automorphic.AssociatedPresentationComparison`) shows that the irreducible local
  components of two presentations of the same realization are equivalent.
- **What is not claimed.** Nothing is said about functions whose support meets determinants
  outside the norm group, where the traces of `π_v` and `π'_v` differ in general (for
  `π' = π ⊗ (κ ∘ det)` with `κ_v` nontrivial), nor about the number of representations in the
  fibre.

## Why the statement is true

1. **The fibre.** The two weak base change conditions give spectral systems `s` of `S`, `s'` of
   `S'` and `t`, `t'` of `T` such that `t` is an arithmetic weak base change of `s` and `t'` one
   of `s'`. At almost all places `t` and `t'` agree: both are the Satake parameters of the
   unramified local components of `T`, which do not depend on the presentation. So `s` and `s'`
   have the same powers by the residue degrees at almost all places,
   `SpectralSystem.SameResidueDegreePowers E s s'`, as in the Library's
   `SpectralSystem.SameResidueDegreePowers.of_common_weakBaseChange` (module
   `Langlands.Automorphic.Unramified.CyclicCuspidalFiberStatement`), which treats a common
   system `t = t'`. The fibre theorem of
   Arthur–Clozel III, Theorem 3.1 (printed p. 201), in the Library's form
   `CyclicCuspidalFiberStatement` (same module), then gives an idele class character `χ` of `F`
   with `χ.IsTrivialOnNorm E` (trivial on the norms of the idele classes of `E`; module
   `Langlands.Global.HeckeCharacterFiniteOrder`) such that `S'` is the twist of `S` by
   `χ ∘ det` (`IsoClass.IsFiniteOrderDeterminantTwist χ`, module
   `Langlands.Automorphic.FiniteOrderDeterminantTwist`).
2. **Local components of the twist.** At `v`, the irreducible local component of `S'` is
   equivalent to `π_v ⊗ (χ_v ∘ det)`, where `π_v` is the one of `S` and `χ_v` the component of
   `χ` at `v`: the twist acts place by place, and irreducible local components do not depend on
   the presentation (remark above).
3. **Triviality on norms.** `χ_v` is trivial on `N((F_v ⊗_F E)^×)`: an element
   `N(y)`, `y = (y_w)_{w | v} ∈ ∏_{w|v} E_w^×`, is the `v`-component of the idelic norm of the
   idele of `E` equal to `y` at the places above `v` and to `1` elsewhere, whose norm is `1`
   away from `v`; so `χ_v(N(y)) = χ(N(y)) = 1`.
4. **Traces.** `(π_v ⊗ (χ_v ∘ det))(f) = π_v(f · (χ_v ∘ det))`, and `f · (χ_v ∘ det) = f`
   because `χ_v(det g) = 1` wherever `f g ≠ 0`. Equivalent admissible representations have the
   same trace (the Library's `Langlands.Character.distribution_eq_of_equivariant`, module
   `Langlands.Representation.Character.Basic`). Hence `tr π'_v(f) = tr π_v(f)`.

## Provenance

This is derived content: the statement is formulated here from the sources below, none of
which states it in this form. Arthur–Clozel 1989, Chapter 1, §6.3(b), printed p. 57, after the
identity (6.5): "By Theorem III.3.1, there are at most ℓ representations on the right-hand side;
they are all of the form `χ ⊗ π` for a unique cuspidal `π`. On the image of the norm, their
local characters coincide." Written with determinant twists, the members of the fibre of one
representative `π` are among the representations `π ⊗ (κ^i ∘ det)`, where `κ` generates the
group of idele class characters of `F` trivial on the norms of the idele classes of `E`.
Henniart–Lemaire 2011, II.6.12 (printed pp. 128–129), use the same description of the fibre
over function fields.
The source asserts that the local characters coincide on the image of the norm. The present
statement is a derived formulation for test functions: for two members of a fibre, the traces
agree on every test function whose support has determinants in the norm group.

Sources: Arthur–Clozel, *Simple Algebras, Base Change, and the Advanced Theory of the Trace
Formula*, Annals of Mathematics Studies 120, Princeton University Press, 1989 (Library source
`arthur-clozel-1989-base-change`): Chapter 1, §6.3 (printed p. 57); Chapter 3, Theorem 3.1
(printed p. 201). Henniart–Lemaire, *Changement de base et induction automorphe pour GL_n en
caractéristique non nulle*, Mémoires de la SMF 124, 2011: II.6.12 (printed pp. 128–129).
