cuspidal data. They are represented by their constituent data.

## Objects and conventions

- `IsobaricData F` (Worker 8, `Langlands.Automorphic.IsobaricData`) is a finite multiset
  of cuspidal classes of positive rank. `D.IsUnitary` says every constituent is
  unitarizable. Unitary data are exactly the unitary cuspidal data `σ` of Definition 4.1,
  up to the order of the blocks. By (2.4) and the irreducibility of the induced
  representation (noted after Definition 4.1), a representation induced from cuspidal
  determines its data and conversely. So uniqueness "among representations induced from
  cuspidal" is uniqueness of unitary data.
- `IsobaricData.IsWeakBaseChangeOf DE DF` and `IsobaricData.IsWeakInductionOf DF DE`
  (module `IsobaricWeakTransfer`) are Definition 1.1 and Definition 6.1 for the
  representations induced from the data, through attached spectral systems. For data of
  single cuspidal classes they are the published class relations (proved there).
- `IsobaricData.galoisOrbit F c` is the data of `Π₁ × Π₁^σ × ⋯ × Π₁^{σ^{ℓ-1}}`: one
  conjugate `c^τ` for each `τ ∈ Gal(E/F)`. For a cyclic group generated by `σ`, these are
  the `c^{σ^i}`, `0 ≤ i < ℓ`. `IsobaricData.IsGaloisStable σ D` is `D^σ = D`.
- The remaining conventions are those of `CyclicTransferStatement`: number fields `F`, `E`
  in universes `uF`, `uE` with `[IsGalois F E]`; explicit compactness of both adele
  quotients; prime degree as `(Module.finrank F E).Prime`, and cyclicity of arbitrary
  degree as a generator `σ`; `η.HasNormKernel E` for the character vanishing exactly on
  `F^× N(𝔸_E^×)`; ranks `r + 1` for `IsoClass F r`; Galois conjugation `c ∘ σ⁻¹`.
- Unitarity of a cuspidal *input* `S` or `T` is
  `RelativeWeilGroup.HasUnitaryCentralCharacter`, as in `CyclicTransferStatement`.
  Unitarity of a cuspidal *output* class `c` is `(ofCuspidal c).IsUnitary`, the convention
  of isobaric data. For cuspidal representations both notions agree with unitarity.
  Neither implication is used.

## The statements

**`CyclicPrimeIsobaricLiftStatement` (Theorem 4.2(a)).** `E/F` is cyclic of prime degree.
`S` is unitary cuspidal over `F` with `π ≇ π ⊗ η`. Then there is a cuspidal class `d` over
`E` of the same rank with `ofCuspidal d` unitary, such that a unitary datum `D` over `E` is
a weak base change of `π` exactly when `D = ofCuspidal d`. The source says "there is a
unique `σ`-stable representation `Π` … lifting `π`; `Π` is cuspidal". Every lift is
automatically `σ`-stable: its conjugate has the same Hecke matrices at almost all places, so
(2.4) applies. So uniqueness among all lifts is the source's uniqueness.

**`CyclicPrimeIsobaricSelfTwistStatement` (Theorem 4.2(b)).** `E/F` is cyclic of prime
degree `ℓ`. `S` is unitary cuspidal with `π ≅ π ⊗ η`. Then there are `m` and a cuspidal
class `d₁` of rank `m + 1` over `E` with:

- `r + 1 = ℓ (m + 1)`;
- `d₁` not stable under any `σ ≠ 1`;
- `galoisOrbit F d₁` unitary;
- a unitary datum over `E` is a weak base change of `π` exactly when it equals
  `galoisOrbit F d₁`.

This is the source's "`Π = Π₁ × Π₁^σ × ⋯ × Π₁^{σ^{ℓ-1}}` is the only lift of `π`", with
`Π₁ ≇ Π₁^σ`. In prime degree every `σ ≠ 1` generates `Gal(E/F)`, so `Π₁ ≇ Π₁^σ` for one
generator gives it for all nontrivial `σ`. The source's `GL(n/ℓ)` is rank `m + 1`.

**`CyclicPrimeIsobaricBaseChangeStatement` (Theorem 4.2(c)).** `E/F` is cyclic of prime
degree. Every unitary datum `DF` over `F` has a unitary weak base change `DE` over `E`, and
every unitary weak base change of `DF` equals `DE`.

**`CyclicPrimeIsobaricStableDescentStatement` (Theorem 4.2(f)).** `E/F` is cyclic of prime
degree with generator `σ`. Every unitary datum `DE` over `E` with `DE^σ = DE` is a weak base
change of some unitary datum over `F`. The source's further sentence "`π` is then induced
from cuspidal" is built into the conclusion, since `π` is given by unitary data.

**`CyclicIsobaricInductionStatement` (Theorem 6.2).** `E/F` is cyclic of any degree, with
generator `σ`. Every unitary datum `DE` over `E` is weakly induced to a unitary datum `DF`
over `F`, and every unitary datum weakly induced from `DE` equals `DF`. The source: "there
exists one, and only one, representation `π_F` of `GL(nℓ, 𝔸_F)` automorphically induced
from `π_E`. Moreover `π_F` is induced from cuspidal." The printed `GL(n, 𝔸_F)` in the
theorem is a slip for `GL(nℓ, 𝔸_F)`, as Definition 6.1 and the proof show. The rank
equation is part of `IsWeakInductionOf`. The source derives uniqueness from (2.4), among
representations induced from cuspidal, which is the uniqueness stated here.

**`CyclicRegularOrbitLiftStatement` (Lemma 6.4; Theorem 4.2(e) in prime degree).** `E/F`
is cyclic of degree `ℓ` with generator `σ`. `T` is unitary cuspidal of rank `m + 1` over
`E` with `π_E ≇ π_E^{σ^i}` for `0 < i < ℓ`. Then there is a cuspidal class `c` over `F` with:

- `r + 1 = (m + 1) ℓ`;
- `ofCuspidal c` unitary;
- `c ≅ c ⊗ η`;
- `galoisOrbit F T` a weak base change of a unitary datum `DF` exactly when
  `DF = ofCuspidal c`.

The source: "there is a unique representation `π_F` of `GL(nℓ, 𝔸_F)` lifted by
`π_E × π_E^σ × ⋯ × π_E^{σ^{ℓ-1}}`. It is cuspidal and `π_F ≅ π_F ⊗ η_{E/F}`." Theorem
4.2(e) is the prime-degree case: "`Π` … lifts some cuspidal representation `π`; `π` is
unique and `π ≅ π ⊗ η`." The published `CyclicRegularInductionStatement` states the
induction form of the same lemma for cuspidal classes.

**`CyclicStableIsobaricDescentStatement` (Lemma 6.3; Theorem 4.2(d) in prime degree).**
`E/F` is cyclic of degree `ℓ` with generator `σ`. `T` is unitary cuspidal over `E` and
`σ`-stable. Then there is a cuspidal class `c` over `F` with `ofCuspidal c` unitary and `T`
a weak base change of `c`. Moreover, every unitary datum `DF` of which `ofCuspidal T` is a
weak base change is `ofCuspidal c'` for a twist `c'` of `c` by some `η^i`, `i < ℓ`. The
source: "there are exactly `ℓ` representations of `GL(n, 𝔸_F)` lifted by `π_E`. They are of
the form `π_F, π_F ⊗ η, …, π_F ⊗ η^{ℓ-1}`; `π_F` is cuspidal". The published
`CyclicStableDescentStatement` states the existence of the twists, that they are exactly the
cuspidal classes lifted by `π_E`, and that they are pairwise distinct. This statement adds
that no representation induced from cuspidal data with more than one constituent is lifted
by `π_E`. The printed "`π_F = π_F ⊗ η`" in Lemma 6.3 is a misprint, as explained in
`CyclicTransferStatement.md`.

**`IsobaricCyclicTransferStatement`** is the conjunction of the seven statements, with
universe parameters `uF uE uV uW`.

## Relation to the cuspidal statements

Together with `CyclicTransferStatement`, every clause of Theorem 4.2(a)–(f), Theorem 6.2
and Lemmas 6.3 and 6.4 is stated:

- (a), (b), (c), (f) and Theorem 6.2 here;
- (d) and Lemma 6.3 split between the two files: existence, the twist description among
  cuspidal classes and distinctness there, and the exclusion of noncuspidal lifts here;
- (e) and Lemma 6.4 in both forms: weak induction there, and weak base change from the
  orbit here.

The weak-induction reformulation of (b) in `CyclicTransferStatement` is a derived
consequence of (b) here together with Theorem 6.2.
