# Common unitary normalization of two cyclic cuspidal spectra

This module proves the common unitary normalization used in the proof of Arthur–Clozel, *Simple Algebras, Base Change, and the Advanced Theory of the Trace Formula* (1989), Chapter III, Theorem 3.1, printed p. 201 (PDF p. 215). The source assumes unitarity at the point where it applies the pair L-function results. This is the justification of that step for the Library's actual realizations and supplied spectra. The proposition `CyclicCuspidalCommonNormalizationStatement` takes two actual cuspidal realizations of the same positive rank over a number field and their genuine presentations. Under `SameResidueDegreePowers`, the proof produces a single actual Hecke character whose determinant twists have unitary central characters and still satisfy `SameResidueDegreePowers` for the twisted presentations. The proof applies to arbitrary number field extensions in the stated domain. The field and presentation universes are independent.

The modulus of any Hecke character is a real power of the normalized idele-class absolute value. The proof uses `HeckeEulerProduct.norm_eq_one_of_ideleClass_eq_one`, which derives unit modulus on the norm-one kernel from its compactness. At a chosen infinite place, the existing exponential ray and character laws give an exponent for the modulus on that ray. For each idele class, the ray parameter given by its log norm divided by the place multiplicity produces a class of the same norm. Their quotient has norm one, so the modulus formula on the ray gives the formula for the original class.

The top standard Hecke operator acts on a nonzero actual spherical vector by the product of the normalized eigenvalues. Its standard diagonal is the scalar matrix of the local uniformizer, so the central character acts on the same vector by its uniformizer value. Cancelling the vector gives the determinant comparison. At one place of the cofinite residue-degree comparison, take the product of the powered eigenvalues and then absolute values. A place above exists and its residue degree is positive, so equality of these powers gives equality of the two central-character moduli at the uniformizer. The normalized idele norm there is the inverse of the residue cardinality, strictly between zero and one. This identifies the two real exponents and hence the unitarizing characters.

The existing `unitarizingCharacter` and its central-character theorem supply both unitary-central twists. On the common unramified set, spectral transport scales both spectra by the same local uniformizer value. Raising the scaled spectra to a residue degree scales the powered spectra by the corresponding power of that same value. This preserves the given comparison.
