# Harris lattice coefficients on genuine small-etale torsors

Author: Worker 13.

This module associates the published whole Harris lattice reductions to a
genuine diagram of finite geometric torsors on the small-etale site of an
actual scheme. It constructs the sheaf transitions and their local
trivializations, then applies the published global-section and derived-section
functors to obtain inverse systems. Cohomology groups and comparison
isomorphisms are constructed from these functors; they are not input fields.

## Inputs and coefficient conventions

Fix a rational algebra `A` with `[Ring A]`, `[Algebra ℚ A]` and
`[Module.Finite ℚ A]`, an involution `σ : AlgebraInvolution ℚ A`, a number
field `L`, a finite-dimensional `L`-module `W`, and
`ρ : (σ.baseChange L).AlgebraicRepresentation W`.
Let `p : FinitePlace ℚ`, let `U` be a compact open level in
`σ.FiniteAdelicPoints`, and let `m : ℕ`. The carriers `A`, `L`, `W`, the
coefficient rings and modules, the residual groups and the scheme
`X : Scheme.{u}` share universe `u`. The site and sheaf category use Mathlib's
corresponding category universes.

Write `R = HarrisCoefficientIntegers L p`, the product of the actual completed
integer rings over every coefficient place of `L` above `p`. Let `Λ` be the
constructed stable full lattice and `I = harrisCoefficientScalarIdeal L p m`,
the ideal generated by the natural-number scalar `m` in `R`. Here `m` is a
scalar parameter, distinct from the exponent notation in the source. No
identification of it with a residue characteristic is implicit.

Put `D = harrisCongruenceGroups ρ p U m` and
`M = harrisCoefficientDiagram ρ p U m`. At `op n`, the underlying module of
`M` is the actual quotient `Λ / I^n Λ`; the group is the quotient of
`U.subgroup` by the kernel of its original action on this reduction.
The coefficient transitions are the original higher-to-lower reductions.
Working in `ModuleCat R` retains integral scalars while the quotient
coefficient rings vary.

The geometric inputs are

```text
Y  : EquivariantDiagram D X.Etale
hY : ∀ n, IsFiniteGeometricTorsor X (Y.action n).
```

Thus `Y` consists of actual etale objects over `X`, actual residual group
actions, and compatible equivariant transition maps. At each index,
`IsFiniteGeometricTorsor` requires a finite acting group, a finite surjective
structure morphism, and a unique group element carrying one geometric lift to
another over each equal base point, for separably closed fields in universe
`u`. This is the existing explicit predicate in [Etale](Etale.md). No
associated sheaf, descent isomorphism or cohomology object is supplied by it.

The construction accepts every `m`, including zero, subject to these genuine
torsor hypotheses. Imported finiteness results for the coefficient modules
themselves require `m ≠ 0`; this module does not extend those results to zero.
The index is all of `ℕᵒᵖ`, including exponent zero.

## The associated sheaf inverse system

Let `J = Scheme.smallEtaleTopology X`. The definition
`harrisEtaleCoefficientSystem` applies `etaleAssociatedVaryingSystem` to
`Y`, `hY` and the actual Harris coefficient diagram `M`. Its codomain is
`ℕᵒᵖ ⥤ Sheaf J (ModuleCat.{u} R)`.
The `_obj` lemma identifies its term `F_n` with
`(etaleAssociatedFunctor X (Y.action n)).obj (M.action n)`.
This is the invariant sheaf associated to the sheafified constant coefficient
representation on the actual etale torsor.

For `f : i ⟶ j`, the published construction has a cospan

```text
F_i --β_f--> B_f <--α_f-- F_j,
```

where `β_f` is `etaleAssociatedReduction`, using the actual coefficient
transition on `Y_i`, and `α_f` is `etaleAssociatedComparison`, using
`Y.obj.map f` and the identity on the restricted target representation.
The geometric torsor comparison theorem proves `α_f` invertible.
The `_map` lemma gives the actual transition `τ_f = β_f ≫ α_f⁻¹`,
and `_comparison` gives `τ_f ≫ α_f = β_f`.
The functor laws are inherited from the proved varying-cover construction.

The `_inclusion` lemma identifies this transition on actual cover sections:

```text
τ_f ≫ inc_j ≫ coverPullback(Y.obj.map f)
  = inc_i ≫ productSheaf(Y_i).map(constantSheaf.map(M.obj.map f)).
```

Here `inc` is the invariant inclusion. The cover morphism points from
`Y_i` to `Y_j`, while its section pullback points in the reverse direction.
The equation retains the original diagonal action, including the inverse
deck transformation in the action on sections.

## Constructed local trivialization

For each `n`, `harrisEtaleCoefficientTrivialization` is an isomorphism
between the pullback of `F_n` to the slice site over `Y_n` and the pullback
of the sheafified constant module `M_n` to that same slice site.
Both pullbacks are the existing `J.overPullback`.

The construction applies `constantAssociatedTrivializationOfSplitting` to
`etaleTorsorSectionSplitting X _ (Y.action n) (hY n) R`, then evaluates it
at `M.action n`. The splitting theorem obtains all translated graph
restrictions from the genuine geometric torsor. The inverse of the resulting
evaluation map is therefore justified by that theorem.

The `_hom` lemma identifies the forward map with `associatedEvaluation` for
the sheafified Harris coefficient action. Concretely it is the pullback of
the invariant inclusion followed by evaluation at the tautological lift.
It is not an unspecified choice of a local isomorphism. This contribution
constructs one such canonical isomorphism at every exponent; it does not
assert an additional cross-exponent compatibility of slice sites.

## Sections, derived objects and cohomology

`harrisEtaleSectionsSystem` postcomposes the constructed sheaf system with
`EtaleDerivedSections.sections X R`. These are ordinary global sections:
the imported functor is naturally isomorphic to evaluation at the terminal
object of the small-etale site.

`harrisEtaleDerivedSystem` postcomposes it with
`EtaleDerivedSections.homotopyFunctor X R`, producing a diagram in
`HomotopyCategory (ModuleCat.{u} R) (ComplexShape.up ℕ)`.
For every `q : ℕ`, `harrisEtaleCohomologySystem q` similarly applies the
ordinary right-derived functor of sections in degree `q`.
Enough injectives for these sheaves is supplied by Mathlib's Grothendieck
abelian category construction on the actual small-etale site.

For each exponent, `harrisEtaleSectionsComplex` applies sections to
Mathlib's chosen injective resolution of `F_n`.
`harrisEtaleDerivedSystemIso` identifies its homotopy-category image with
the corresponding derived-system object.
`harrisEtaleCohomologyIso q n` identifies degree `q` cohomology with
degree `q` homology of this constructed cochain complex.
These are per-object complex representatives; no strictly functorial diagram
of the chosen complexes is claimed.

`harrisEtaleCohomologySystem_map` proves that each cohomology transition is
the cohomology functor applied to `β_f`, followed by that functor applied
to `α_f⁻¹`. Its proof is the functor's `map_comp` law.
`harrisEtaleCohomologyZeroIso` is a natural isomorphism of whole inverse
systems from degree zero cohomology to ordinary sections, obtained by
whiskering the published natural degree zero isomorphism.

The object, transition and local forward-map formulas are definitional
equalities. The comparison and section-inclusion equations specialize the
published varying-cover equations. The representative isomorphisms and
degree zero isomorphism specialize Mathlib's proved derived-section
construction. All intrinsic obligations are inherited from these actual
constructions or proved by the displayed functor laws.

## Remaining geometric and cohomological scope

The supplied scheme and torsors have not been identified with Harris's
particular Shimura levels. The published topological Harris double quotients
do not by themselves imply the geometric torsor hypotheses. In particular,
coefficient action kernels can retain a permanent representation kernel;
they need not form the independently separating tower chosen on p.308.
Actual scheme realization and any comparison with such a tower remain
separate construction or theorem obligations.

This module does not construct an inverse limit or assert that derived
sections or cohomology commute with one. It supplies ordinary cohomology,
without a compact-support construction, smooth adelic or continuous Galois
action, lisse-adic compatibility, or the Ext spectral sequence and
uniformization of Proposition 4.4. Those remain obligations of the broader
Harris framework.

`5ed2965256430c3649e86755f9576b54eca72435`. Every imported project
