# Simultaneous finite reductions of Harris coefficients

This module constructs finite reductions of the full stable Harris coefficient
lattice, their actual image quotient groups and faithful actions over the
quotient coefficient rings. It proves agreement with all component reductions
and compatibility with coefficient and group transitions.

## Original source and derived content

The source is Michael Harris, *Galois properties of cohomological automorphic
forms on GL(n)* (1999), Section 3, printed pp. 306–308, PDF pages 8–10.
The registered source is
`harris-1999-cohomological-automorphic-forms-525a222c4489ee9d`, with SHA-256
`525a222c4489ee9d3fe86a52c6c17ffb30fcc7c31f24c16a64669e9615085b26`.

On p. 306 Harris fixes a finite-dimensional algebraic representation over a
number field `L` and uses the whole coefficient algebra `L ⊗[ℚ] ℚ_p`. The
bottom of p. 307 chooses a stable lattice over its ring of integers. Equation
(3.7) on p. 308 uses that lattice in the coefficient-valued function space.
The subsequent paragraph considers finite modules over the integral
coefficient ring modulo a power of the rational prime, with continuous action
factoring through a finite quotient; (3.8) then compares function spaces.

The simultaneous product reductions, kernel identity and compatible finite
actions here are internal deductions from the actual whole lattice and its
component lattices, rather than assertions reproduced verbatim from Harris.
The Lean name `m` denotes a natural-number scalar and `n` its power exponent.
Taking `m` to be the rational prime gives the coefficient-ring reduction in
the source; it is not a choice of a uniformizer at one coefficient place.
The general construction works for every `m`; finiteness and openness below
require `m ≠ 0`.

## Inputs and notation

For the scalar-ring constructions, `L` is an actual number field and
`p : FinitePlace ℚ`. Write `I = FinitePlacesAbove L p` for the finite subtype
of actual places above `p`, `O_l = CoefficientIntegers l.1` for their completed
valuation rings and `R = HarrisCoefficientIntegers L p = ∏ l : I, O_l`.

The representation constructions additionally use a ring `A` with a
finite-dimensional rational algebra structure, a rational algebra involution
`σ`, a finite-dimensional `L`-module `W`, an algebraic representation
`ρ : (σ.baseChange L).AlgebraicRepresentation W`, and an actual compact open
level `U` in `σ.FiniteAdelicPoints`. Here `A`, `L` and `W` inhabit the same
universe. Let `Λ` be `harrisLattice ρ p U` in the original tensor coefficient
module and `Λ_l` the carrier of
`algebraicCompactCoefficientLattice ρ p l.1 U`. These lattices and their stable
actions are constructed by the imported modules; they are not additional
arguments. In particular no continuity hypothesis on `ρ` is added here.

Every action in this module is the ordinary coefficient representation,
restricted to `U.subgroup` and then reduced. The inverse occurring in Harris's
function-equivariance condition (3.7) is a later function-space convention.

## Principal-power product quotients

The generic `PrincipalProducts` section allows any index type, commutative
rings `R i`, modules `V i` and a scalar tuple `a`. Its equivalences do not
require a finite index or nonzero scalars.

`mem_principalPower_smul_top` identifies membership in `(b)^k • ⊤` with
divisibility by the scalar `b^k`. `principalPowerReductionEquiv` descends a
linear equivalence to these principal-power quotients; its proof transports
divisibility in both directions. Its `_mk` formula gives the original
equivalence on representatives.

`dependentProductReduction` is coordinatewise module reduction, linear over
the actual product of the varying scalar rings. It is surjective by choosing
a representative in each coordinate. Its kernel is exactly `(a)^n • ⊤`,
because componentwise scalar divisibility is the same as divisibility in the
product module. The quotient-by-kernel equivalence therefore gives
`dependentProductReductionEquiv` from the reduction of the product module to
the product of its reductions.

`dependentProductRingReduction` and its surjectivity and kernel lemmas prove
the corresponding ring assertion. The kernel is `(a)^n` in the product ring;
the proof uses the principal-ideal membership formula coordinatewise.
`dependentProductRingReductionEquiv` is the resulting ring equivalence.
Both product equivalences have the stated coordinatewise `_mk` formulas.

## Actual coefficient ring and torsion splitting

`harrisCoefficientScalarIdeal L p m` is the ideal generated by the rational
scalar `m` in `R`. `HarrisCoefficientReductionRing L p m n` is the actual ring
`R / (m)^n`. Its ring equivalence
`harrisCoefficientReductionRingEquiv` identifies it with
`∏ l : I, CoefficientReductionRing l.1 m n`; on a representative `r` it takes
the class of `r` to the classes of `r l`. For nonzero `m`, each component
quotient ring is finite by the imported completed-valuation-ring result.
The finite product and the equivalence prove
`harrisCoefficientReductionRing_finite`.

`harrisLatticeComponentEquiv` composes the whole-lattice splitting with the
equivalence between a product submodule and the product of its subtypes:
`Λ ≃ₗ[R] ∏ l : I, Λ_l`. Its `_action` theorem follows from the original
adelic equivariance of the tensor splitting, after taking each coordinate
and the underlying subtype value.

`harrisTorsionSystem` is the actual functor
`ℕᵒᵖ ⥤ Action (ModuleCat R) U.subgroup` obtained by successive ideal-power
quotients of that integral action. `harrisTorsionAction m n` is its `n`th
term, with carrier `Λ / (m)^n Λ`.
`harrisTorsionEquiv` composes the principal-power quotient of the integral
equivalence with the product quotient equivalence. It identifies that carrier
with `∏ l : I, Λ_l / (m)^n Λ_l`, linearly over `R` acting coordinatewise.
Its `_mk` formula is the corresponding integral component map on
representatives. Its `_action` theorem proves equivariance for the original
`U.subgroup` action by choosing a lattice representative and applying
`harrisLatticeComponentEquiv_action`.

## Common kernel and finite image

`harrisCongruenceKernel m n` is the kernel of the actual whole torsion
representation and is normal. `mem_harrisCongruenceKernel` proves that an
element belongs to it exactly when it belongs to every component torsion
kernel. In the forward direction, the proof puts a chosen component vector
in a single-coordinate product vector, pulls it back through
`harrisTorsionEquiv`, and applies equivariance. In the reverse direction,
componentwise triviality and injectivity of the same equivalence prove
triviality on the whole reduction. Thus `harrisCongruenceKernel_eq` identifies
the kernel with the finite intersection of the actual component kernels.

For nonzero `m`, each component kernel is open by the constructed algebraic
compact-coefficient result. Finite intersection proves
`harrisCongruenceKernel_isOpen`. `HarrisCongruenceGroup` is the quotient of
`U.subgroup` by this kernel. Compactness of `U` and openness of the kernel
prove `harrisCongruenceGroup_finite`.

`harrisTorsionAction_finite` obtains a finite carrier from finite generation
of the whole lattice over `R` and finiteness of `R / (m)^n`, using the
imported module reduction theorem. This is a finiteness assertion; the module
does not assert that the constructed reduction is free.

## Quotient-ring action and transition maps

`harrisFiniteTorsionAction` applies the actual quotient-ring reduction functor
to the integral lattice action. It has the same quotient carrier with
scalars in `HarrisCoefficientReductionRing`; its `_mk` formula reduces the
original action on a representative. `harrisTorsionEquiv_quotient_smul`
proves that the torsion splitting respects these scalars through
`harrisCoefficientReductionRingEquiv`. It takes representatives for both the
quotient scalar and the quotient vector and applies integral linearity.
The formula explicitly views each component as the corresponding
`rationalTorsionAction` module over its own quotient ring.

`harrisFiniteTorsionAction_ker` proves that keeping quotient-ring scalars
leaves the action kernel unchanged, by equality of the underlying
endomorphisms. `harrisFiniteCoefficientAction` then lifts this representation
to `HarrisCongruenceGroup`. Its `_mk` formula recovers the original reduced
action and its `_injective` theorem proves faithfulness, since the quotient
is by exactly the kernel. For nonzero `m`, its `_finite` theorem gives both
a finite acting group and a finite underlying module.

`harrisTorsionSystem_transition_surjective` supplies the actual surjective
coefficient quotient maps for `i ≤ j`, from exponent `j` to exponent `i`.
`harrisTorsionEquiv_transition` proves that the splitting commutes with all
the component quotient transitions, by reducing to a lattice representative.
These coefficient maps are linear over the original integral ring `R`;
their source and target quotient scalar rings differ.

`harrisCongruenceGroups` is the actual inverse-system functor to `GrpCat`
and `harrisCongruenceTransition` is its quotient-group homomorphism, induced
by the identity on `U.subgroup`. The imported generic construction supplies
its functor laws and surjective transitions.
`harrisCongruenceTransition_equivariant` proves that coefficient transitions
intertwine the faithful quotient actions along those group homomorphisms.
The proof uses a group representative and a lattice representative, on
which both routes are definitionally the same.

## Scope

All constructions and algebraic identities include `n = 0`, when the quotient
ring and coefficient module have one element and the image group is trivial.
Nonzero `m` is required only in the stated finiteness and openness results.
No separating or cofinal sequence of subgroups is asserted: these are kernels
of this coefficient action and can contain its permanent kernel. In
particular they are not the independently chosen separating congruence tower
on Harris p. 308.

The module proves openness in the topology of the original compact level; it
does not construct a topology on the full tensor lattice, a topological
splitting, or a continuous action object on the quotient modules. It supplies
coefficient and finite-group inputs, while coefficient-valued function spaces,
the comparisons (3.7)–(3.11), associated local systems, descent, geometric
levels and canonical models remain separate constructions.
