# Source changes of the full Harris derived coefficient diagram

Author: Worker 13.

This internal specialization constructs actual source-change isomorphisms for
the Harris coefficient objects in the derived categories of whole local sheaf
diagrams. The construction preserves the original lattice reductions at every
exponent and every coefficient place. It proves compatibility with the
constructed invariant-evaluation isomorphisms and recovers the ordinary
source-change maps through canonical single-degree comparisons.

The implementation specializes
[VaryingCoverDerivedSourceChange](VaryingCoverDerivedSourceChange.md) and
[HarrisEtaleDerivedLocalTrivialization](HarrisEtaleDerivedLocalTrivialization.md).
Its coefficient provenance and geometric hypotheses are those recorded in
[HarrisEtaleCoefficients](HarrisEtaleCoefficients.md). No new direct assertion
about the original literature is made here.

## Inputs and categories

Work with a finite rational algebra `A : Type u`, an involution
`σ : AlgebraInvolution ℚ A`, a number field `L : Type u`, a finite-dimensional
`L`-module `W : Type u`, and an algebraic representation
`ρ : (σ.baseChange L).AlgebraicRepresentation W`. Let `p` be a rational
finite place, `U` a compact open level of `σ.FiniteAdelicPoints`, and `m : ℕ`
the scalar parameter. Write

```text
R = HarrisCoefficientIntegers L p
D = harrisCongruenceGroups ρ p U m
M = harrisCoefficientDiagram ρ p U m.
```

Thus `R` is the product of the completed integer rings at all places of `L`
above `p`. The underlying coefficient module at exponent `op n` is the
whole stable lattice modulo the `n`th power of the ideal generated by `m`.
The actual coefficient reduction maps and their equivariance are retained.
All exponents in `ℕᵒᵖ`, including zero, are included. No condition `m ≠ 0`
is needed here; imported coefficient-finiteness results retain that condition.

The geometric inputs are an actual scheme `X : Scheme.{u}`, a compatible
equivariant diagram `Y : EquivariantDiagram D X.Etale`, and
`hY : ∀ n, IsFiniteGeometricTorsor X (Y.action n)`. These are actual
étale objects, cover maps and finite geometric torsor hypotheses. They do
not assume any sheaf comparison or derived compatibility.

Fix `f : k ⟶ l` in `ℕᵒᵖ`. With `J = X.smallEtaleTopology`, put

```text
C = EquivariantDiagram D (ModuleCat.{u} R)
K = Under k ⥤ Sheaf (J.over (Y.obj.obj k)) (ModuleCat.{u} R)
L = Under l ⥤ Sheaf (J.over (Y.obj.obj l)) (ModuleCat.{u} R)
B = Under l ⥤ Sheaf (J.over (Y.obj.obj k)) (ModuleCat.{u} R).
```

The construction takes four independent actual localization choices
`HasDerivedCategory.{wC} C`, `HasDerivedCategory.{wK} K`,
`HasDerivedCategory.{wL} L` and `HasDerivedCategory.{wM} B`. These are
Mathlib's unbounded derived categories of integer cochain complexes. Scheme,
coefficient and group carriers share universe `u`. Abelian structures and
exactness come from the imported actual module sheafification and slice
restriction constructions.

## Ordinary source change and evaluation

Let `T_f : L ⥤ B` be `sourceSliceRestriction J (ModuleCat R) Y.obj f`.
It restricts each sheaf along the genuine map `Y.obj.map f`.
Let `P_f : K ⥤ B` be `sourceSliceReindex J (ModuleCat R) Y.obj f`.
It precomposes the whole diagram with `Under.map f`. In particular an arrow
`i.hom : l ⟶ i.right` becomes `f ≫ i.hom`.

Write `H_k` and `N_k` for the existing ordinary Harris coefficient and
constant local diagrams, and `e_k : H_k ≅ N_k` for their constructed
invariant-evaluation isomorphism. The definitions
`harrisEtaleLocalCoefficientSourceChangeIso` and
`harrisEtaleLocalConstantSourceChangeIso` are the components at `M` of the
existing associated and constant source-change functor isomorphisms:

```text
a_f : T_f(H_l) ≅ P_f(H_k)
b_f : T_f(N_l) ≅ P_f(N_k).
```

Their forward components on every common sheaf section are identities, as
proved by the two `..._hom_app_hom_app` lemmas. This is a statement about
the actual sections under the canonical slice restriction identifications.

The theorem `harrisEtaleLocalCoefficientSourceChange_evaluation` proves,
for every `i : Under l`,

```text
pullback(Y.obj.map f).map(e_l.hom.app i) ≫ b_f.hom.app i
  = a_f.hom.app i ≫ e_k.hom.app ((Under.map f).obj i).
```

The right evaluation is the original `etaleAssociatedEvaluationAlong` at
`i.right` and the actual composite cover map `Y.obj.map (f ≫ i.hom)`.
The lemma `harrisEtaleLocalCoefficientDiagramIso_reindex_hom_app` proves this
component formula by definition.
The left evaluation uses `Y.obj.map i.hom` before restriction along
`Y.obj.map f`. The proof specializes the already proved ordinary
source-change square and evaluates its diagram component.

## Derived source change and single-degree recovery

For `q : ℤ`, write `H_kᴰ` and `N_kᴰ` for the existing actual derived
Harris local coefficient and constant objects obtained from
`single_C(q).obj M`. Here `q` is a cochain degree, independent of the
coefficient exponent. Write `T_fᴰ` and `P_fᴰ` for the actual exact derived
restriction and reindexing functors.

The definitions `harrisEtaleDerivedLocalCoefficientSourceChangeIso` and
`harrisEtaleDerivedLocalConstantSourceChangeIso` evaluate the generic
derived source-change isomorphisms at precisely `single_C(q).obj M`.
They have types

```text
a_fᴰ : T_fᴰ(H_lᴰ) ≅ P_fᴰ(H_kᴰ)
b_fᴰ : T_fᴰ(N_lᴰ) ≅ P_fᴰ(N_kᴰ).
```

The four definitions with names
`harrisEtaleDerivedRestrictedCoefficientSingleIso`,
`harrisEtaleDerivedRestrictedConstantSingleIso`,
`harrisEtaleDerivedReindexedCoefficientSingleIso` and
`harrisEtaleDerivedReindexedConstantSingleIso` compare each endpoint with
the corresponding ordinary diagram placed in degree `q`. Each first maps
the existing Harris single-degree comparison through `T_fᴰ` or `P_fᴰ`,
then composes with the component of that ordinary functor's
`mapDerivedCategorySingleFunctor q`.

For a coefficient source functor `F_j`, denote its comparison at `M` by
`s_j : F_jᴰ(single_C(q)(M)) ≅ single(q)(F_j(M))`. Let `t` and `r`
be the single-degree comparisons for `T_f` at `F_l(M)` and `P_f` at
`F_k(M)`, respectively. The theorem
`harrisEtaleDerivedLocalCoefficientSourceChangeIso_single` proves

```text
a_fᴰ.hom = T_fᴰ.map(s_l.hom) ≫ t.hom ≫
  single_B(q).map(a_f.hom) ≫ r.inv ≫ P_fᴰ.map(s_k.inv).
```

The corresponding constant theorem proves the same equation for `b_f`.
The paired endpoint comparisons constructed above are precisely
`T_fᴰ.mapIso(s_l) ≪≫ t` and `P_fᴰ.mapIso(s_k) ≪≫ r`.
In Lean, the forward derived map and the middle ordinary map are written as
components of the respective source-change functor isomorphisms at
`single_C(q).obj M` and `M`. These are exactly the definitions of the named
Harris isomorphisms. The proofs directly specialize the proved generic
single-degree formulas. A finite `maxHeartbeats 1600000` is scoped to each
of these two recovery theorems for kernel checking the large coefficient
specialization; the construction and compatibility declarations use the
ordinary limit.

Finally `harrisEtaleDerivedLocalCoefficientIso_sourceChange` proves

```text
T_fᴰ.map(e_lᴰ.hom) ≫ b_fᴰ.hom
  = a_fᴰ.hom ≫ P_fᴰ.map(e_kᴰ.hom).
```

Here `e_kᴰ` and `e_lᴰ` are the existing actual derived Harris
invariant-evaluation isomorphisms. The proof applies the generic derived
compatibility at `single_C(q).obj M`. This is an equality of morphisms
in the actual derived category of `B`, retaining every transition in the
reachable coefficient diagrams.

## Scope

These constructions use supplied genuine scheme torsors. They do not
construct Harris's particular Shimura schemes, identify the coefficient
action kernels with his independently separating levels, or trivialize the
whole infinite tower on one fixed cover. The derived source changes concern
local sheaf diagrams. Compactly supported cohomology, adelic and Galois
actions, inverse limits, analytic uniformization and the Ext spectral
sequence remain separate obligations.

The implementation uses Lean 4.34.0 and Mathlib commit
`5ed2965256430c3649e86755f9576b54eca72435`. All compatibility and
single-degree equations are proved from the imported actual constructions;
no derived comparison is assumed as a field, and no axiom or proof hole
is introduced.
