# Invertibility of the associated-sheaf comparison between finite etale torsors

Author: Worker 266.

`Langlands.Shimura.Coefficients.etaleAssociatedCoverComparison` proves exactly
the published proposition
`Langlands.Shimura.Coefficients.EtaleAssociatedCoverComparisonStatement.{u}`
from `Langlands.Shimura.Coefficients.EtaleCoverComparisonStatement`. The
theorem has that proposition as its type and keeps the single universe `u`.
It retains every quantified input: a scheme `X : Scheme.{u}`, groups
`G H : Type u`, actions `Y : Action X.Etale G` and `Z : Action X.Etale H`,
both unchanged hypotheses `IsFiniteGeometricTorsor X Y` and
`IsFiniteGeometricTorsor X Z`, a homomorphism `φ : H →* G`, an equivariant
cover map `a : Z ⟶ (Action.res X.Etale φ).obj Y`, a commutative ring
`R : Type u` and a representation `M : Action (ModuleCat.{u} R) G`. The
conclusion is invertibility of the constructed morphism

```text
etaleCrossCoverMap X φ a (𝟙 ((Action.res (ModuleCat.{u} R) φ).obj M))
```

from the sheaf associated to `Y` and `M` to the sheaf associated to `Z` and
the restricted representation. No surjectivity of `φ` or `a`, condition on
group orders, finiteness of coefficients or exactness of invariants is
assumed. The module imports the statement and does not change it, its
companion Markdown or anything it imports.

## Declarations

The module adds three auxiliary declarations, each in the namespace
`Langlands.Shimura.Coefficients`.

1. `isIso_overPullback_crossCoverMap` is a local invertibility result on an
   arbitrary site. Let `C` be a category with binary products and `J` a
   Grothendieck topology on it. Assume `Y : Action C G` and
   `Z : Action C H` both satisfy the published `TorsorSectionSplitting` for
   `R`-module sheaves. Take `a` as above, equivariant sheaf
   representations `F` of `G` and `E` of `H`, and an equivariant map
   `c` from the restriction of `F` to `E` whose underlying sheaf map `c.hom`
   is invertible. The result says that the image of `crossCoverMap J φ a c`
   under `J.overPullback (ModuleCat.{u} R) Z.V` is an isomorphism. This is
   the cross-cover map restricted to the localized site over `Z.V`.
2. `etaleIdIsTerminal X` is a definition, not a proposition. It constructs a
   proof-relevant `IsTerminal` structure showing that the identity of `X`,
   viewed as `Scheme.Etale.mk (𝟙 X)`, is terminal in `X.Etale`. The unique
   map from `W` has underlying scheme map `W.hom`. Uniqueness follows from
   the commutativity condition of a morphism over the identity of `X`.
3. `etale_coversTop_of_surjective` states that an object `V : X.Etale` whose
   structure morphism `V.hom` is surjective covers the final object. In
   other words, the one-member family `fun _ : Unit => V` satisfies
   `(Scheme.smallEtaleTopology X).CoversTop`.

## Proof of the local lemma

The proof of `isIso_overPullback_crossCoverMap` uses the published
evaluation equation `crossCoverMap_evaluation` from
`CrossCoverEvaluation`:

```text
overPullback(crossCoverMap J φ a c) ≫ associatedEvaluation J Z E
  = associatedEvaluationAlong J Y a.hom F ≫ overPullback(c.hom).
```

Here `overPullback` abbreviates `(J.overPullback (ModuleCat.{u} R) Z.V).map`.
The right side is an isomorphism, and so is the target evaluation. Mathlib's
`IsIso.of_isIso_fac_right` then shows that the localized cross-cover map is
an isomorphism. The three ingredients are as follows.

- Target evaluation. For each object `U` of the slice over `Z.V`,
  `associatedEvaluation_bijective` from `TrivializationFromSplitting` turns
  the splitting of `Z`, applied to the module sheaf `E.V` and `U`, into
  bijectivity of the component of `associatedEvaluation J Z E` at `U`. A
  bijective linear map is an isomorphism in `ModuleCat` by Mathlib's
  `ConcreteCategory.isIso_iff_bijective`. `NatIso.isIso_of_isIso_app` makes
  the underlying natural transformation invertible, and
  `ObjectProperty.isIso_hom_iff` makes the sheaf morphism invertible.
- Source evaluation along `a.hom`. `associatedTrivializationAlongOfSplitting`
  from `CrossCoverEvaluation`, applied to the splitting of `Y` and to the
  base morphism `a.hom : Z.V ⟶ Y.V`, is a natural isomorphism. Its component
  at `F` is `associatedEvaluationAlong J Y a.hom F`, which is therefore an
  isomorphism.
- Coefficient map. The functor `J.overPullback (ModuleCat.{u} R) Z.V` sends
  the isomorphism `asIso c.hom` to an isomorphism.

## Proof of the covering lemma

By Mathlib's `GrothendieckTopology.coversTop_iff_of_isTerminal` with the
terminal object from `etaleIdIsTerminal`, it suffices to show that the sieve
on the final object generated by `V` is covering. That sieve contains the
sieve generated by the single arrow from `V` to the terminal object, so
`GrothendieckTopology.superset_covering` reduces the claim to that arrow.
Mathlib's `Scheme.ofArrows_mem_smallEtaleTopology_iff` turns this into the
claim that the underlying scheme map of the arrow is surjective. That
underlying map equals `V.hom` by the commutativity condition over `𝟙 X`, and
its surjectivity is the hypothesis.

## Proof of the theorem

After introducing the variables, Mathlib's `Sheaf.isIso_of_coversTop` from
`Mathlib.CategoryTheory.Sites.LocalProperties` reduces global invertibility
to invertibility after pulling back to each member of a family covering the
final object. The family used is the single object `Z.V`, which covers by
`etale_coversTop_of_surjective` applied to the surjectivity conjunct
`hZ.2.2.1 : Function.Surjective Z.V.hom` of `IsFiniteGeometricTorsor X Z`.

The theorem then applies `isIso_overPullback_crossCoverMap` with
`J = Scheme.smallEtaleTopology X`. The two splitting hypotheses are
`etaleTorsorSectionSplitting X G Y hY R` and
`etaleTorsorSectionSplitting X H Z hZ R` from
`TorsorSectionSplittingProof`. These are obtained from the full geometric
torsor hypotheses as published. The representation `F` is the constant
sheaf representation of `M`, and `c` is the image of the identity of the
restricted representation under the action functor induced by
`constantSheaf`. With these choices, `crossCoverMap` is by definition the
statement's `etaleCrossCoverMap`, via `constantCrossCoverMap` in
`CrossCover`. Invertibility of `c.hom` follows from `Functor.map_id` and
`Action.id_hom`, since `c` is an identity.

## Use of the hypotheses

From `IsFiniteGeometricTorsor X Z`, the proof uses the surjectivity conjunct
directly for the cover. It also passes the whole hypothesis to
`etaleTorsorSectionSplitting`, whose proof is described in
`TorsorSectionSplittingProof.md`. `IsFiniteGeometricTorsor X Y` is passed
whole to `etaleTorsorSectionSplitting`. This module does not use the
conjuncts `Finite G`, `Finite H`, `IsFinite Y.V.hom`, `IsFinite Z.V.hom` or
the surjectivity of `Y.V.hom` directly. According to its Markdown, the proof
of `etaleTorsorSectionSplitting` uses only the unique-transport conjunct.
All conjuncts remain in the unchanged hypotheses.

This is an internally derived proof of an existing Library target. It
follows the route suggested in the statement's Markdown: localize at `Z.V`,
compare the two evaluations, and descend along the surjective etale cover.

covering condition by the two auxiliary lemmas proved here. No external
literature is followed or cited.
