# Affine representability of the rational similitude functor

Author: Worker 254.

`Langlands.Shimura.similitudeRepresentability` proves the complete published
proposition `Langlands.Shimura.SimilitudeRepresentabilityStatement.{u}`, with
the same carrier universe `u`. For every ring `A : Type u` with a rational
algebra structure, finite dimensionality over ℚ and an `AlgebraInvolution ℚ A`
`σ`, it gives

- a commutative rational algebra `B` in `CommAlgCat.{u} ℚ`,
- a proof that `B` is finitely presented over ℚ,
- a natural isomorphism from `coyoneda.obj (op B)` to the published
  `σ.similitudeFunctor` composed with the forgetful functor from groups to
  types.

The statement and every declaration it imports are unchanged. The theorem
uses no hypotheses beyond the statement's.

## Proof

The proof chooses a rational basis of `A`, `b := Module.Free.chooseBasis ℚ A`.
Its index type lives in universe `u` and is a `Fintype` because `A` is
finite-dimensional. It then applies the construction in
[SimilitudeCoordinates](SimilitudeCoordinates.md) with `R = ℚ`:

- `B` is `σ.similitudeCoordinateAlgebra b`, the quotient of the polynomial
  ring in the coordinates of g, of its inverse h, the multiplier c and its
  inverse d. The ideal is generated by the coordinates of g h - 1,
  h g - 1 and g σ(g) - c, together with c d - 1.
- Finite presentation is the instance from
  `Algebra.FinitePresentation.quotient`, since the ideal has finitely many
  generators.
- The isomorphism is `σ.similitudeCoyonedaIso b`, from Mathlib's
  `Functor.CorepresentableBy.toIso`. Its bijection sends an algebra map to
  the image of the universal similitude. The inverse evaluates the
  polynomial variables at the coordinates of a given similitude.

The test-algebra universe of the construction is `max 0 u`, which Lean
identifies with `u`, so the result matches the statement's `CommAlgCat.{u} ℚ`
and `GrpCat.{u}` exactly.

The theorem depends only on the standard axioms `propext`,
`Classical.choice` and `Quot.sound`.

## Provenance

The route is the elementary finite-equation argument anticipated in the
unchanged [statement documentation](RepresentabilityStatement.md). That
documentation records the target's provenance (Kottwitz 1992 and Harris
1999). No additional claim about the original literature is made here.
The chosen basis and the coordinate ring are proof devices: the statement
only asserts existence, and no canonical choice of `B` is claimed.
