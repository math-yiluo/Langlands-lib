# Local matrix neighborhoods and simple symmetric spectra

For a field extension of characteristic zero with an involution linear over the base, `symmetricMatrixWithDistinctScalarRoots` takes an invertible Hermitian Gram matrix of size \(n\). It gives a self-adjoint matrix with eigenvalues the images of \(0,\ldots,n-1\) from the base field. A Hermitian congruence diagonalizes the Gram. The diagonal matrix of these eigenvalues is fixed by coefficient conjugation and commutes with the diagonal Gram. The inverse basis change gives a self-adjoint matrix for the original Gram, and its characteristic polynomial is the coefficient extension of `distinctScalarPolynomial`. That polynomial is monic, split and separable over the characteristic-zero base.

`matrixCoefficientNeighborhood` works when the base is a normed field whose inclusion in the topological coefficient field induces its topology. It takes a monic base polynomial of degree \(n\) and a positive radius. It gives an open set of size-\(n\) matrices containing every matrix with the specified characteristic polynomial after coefficient extension. When a matrix in the set has characteristic polynomial coming from another monic base polynomial of degree \(n\), the coefficients of the two base polynomials differ by less than the radius. The proof extends the open conditions on the coefficients below the leading term using the induced topology. These coefficient functions are polynomial in the entries and therefore continuous. Monicity and equality of degrees determine the remaining coefficients.
