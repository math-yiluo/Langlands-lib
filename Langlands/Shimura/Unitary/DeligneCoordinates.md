# The Deligne torus coordinate algebra and affine source

Author: Worker 57.

This module constructs the explicit real algebra

`B = ℝ[a,b,c,d] / (ac − bd − 1, ad + bc)`

and proves, naturally in every commutative real algebra `S`, that real
algebra maps `B → S` classify the actual units of `S[i]`. It also constructs
`Spec B` and its structural morphism to `Spec ℝ`. The represented point
functor is the one defined in `Deligne`, where `S[i]` is identified with
`S ⊗[ℝ] ℂ` by an algebra equivalence natural in `S`.

## Provenance

Kottwitz (1992), Section 1, printed p.654 (PDF p.2), uses
`Res_{ℂ/ℝ} Gₘ` as the source of the real Shimura homomorphism. This page
was read in the original. The coordinate presentation here is a derived
construction of that source's affine algebra: a quadratic element
`a + bi` is invertible precisely when it has an inverse `c + di`.
Multiplication in `S[i]` gives the two displayed equations. They work over
arbitrary commutative real algebras, including nonreduced algebras; no
field or reducedness assumption is used.

The four-variable presentation retains the inverse coordinates explicitly.
The inverse of a unit is unique, so it introduces no additional choices
in the represented points. The proof establishes this through two inverse
maps between algebra homomorphisms and actual units. The module does not
assert a comparison with a three-variable norm-localization presentation.

The polynomial quotient and classifying-map strategy is the same standard
universal-property argument used in the published `SimilitudeCoordinates`
module. This module uses its own quadratic coordinates rather than a basis
of a general algebra. The construction and proofs below are internal
algebraic derivations, not statements quoted from Kottwitz.

## The finite presentation and universal point

`Variable` has the four constructors `re`, `im`, `invRe`, and `invIm`.
Its injective Boolean-pair encoding `Variable.code` proves finiteness.
`PolynomialRing` is the real multivariate polynomial algebra on these
variables. `realRelation` is `ac − bd − 1`; `imagRelation` is `ad + bc`.
`coordinateIdeal` is the ideal generated by these two polynomials.

`coordinateIdeal_fg` proves that this ideal is finitely generated.
`CoordinateRing` is the quotient `B`, and the module provides the actual
`Algebra.FinitePresentation ℝ CoordinateRing` instance using Mathlib's
finite-presentation theorem for a quotient by a finitely generated ideal.
`quotient` is the real algebra quotient map, and `coord` gives the four
images of the polynomial variables.

`coord_real_relation` and `coord_imag_relation` follow from membership of
the two defining polynomials in their span. They prove that the quadratic
elements with coordinates `(a,b)` and `(c,d)` multiply to one in `B[i]`.
Commutativity gives the reverse product. These proofs construct the actual
unit `universalPoint : Points CoordinateRing`; neither inverse equation is
an assumed field.

## Classification of points

For any commutative real algebra `S`, `point f` specializes the universal
unit along `f : CoordinateRing →ₐ[ℝ] S`, using the published coefficient
map on quadratic algebras and Mathlib's map on units. The lemmas
`point_re`, `point_im`, `point_inv_re`, and `point_inv_im` compute all four
coordinates.

Conversely, `values z` assigns to the four variables the coordinates of a
unit `z` and its actual inverse. Taking the real and imaginary coordinates
of the unit inverse equation proves `eval_realRelation` and
`eval_imagRelation`. The resulting `coordinateIdeal_le_ker` permits the
evaluation map to descend to the quotient, defining `classify z`.
`classify_quotient` and `classify_coord` give its evaluation formulas.

`homEquiv` is the equivalence

`(CoordinateRing →ₐ[ℝ] S) ≃ Points S`.

Its left inverse proof uses quotient-map extensionality followed by
polynomial-generator extensionality. All four variables recover their
original values, including the inverse coordinates. Its right inverse
proof uses extensionality of units and the two coordinates of their
underlying quadratic elements. `classify_point` and `point_classify`
record the two inverse equations.

`point_comp` proves that composing a classifying algebra map with
`g : S →ₐ[ℝ] T` specializes the point by the same quadratic coefficient
map used in `DeligneTorus.functor`. The equality is checked on the two
underlying coordinates of units. `classify_map` derives the corresponding
naturality of classifying maps from the inverse equivalence.

## Functor and scheme constructions

`coordinateAlgebra` bundles `B` as a commutative real algebra.
`corepresentableBy` and `coyonedaIso` bundle the proved equivalences and
naturality as

`coyoneda.obj (op coordinateAlgebra) ≅ functor ⋙ forget GrpCat`.

This bundled version uses real algebras in `Type 0`; the preceding
`homEquiv`, point formulas, and naturality results allow test algebras in
arbitrary universes. The distinction is only the universe in which the
representing algebra is bundled, not a restriction to fields or to
finite-dimensional test algebras.

`scheme` is Mathlib's actual `Spec (CommRingCat.of CoordinateRing)`.
`structureMap` is the spectrum map induced by `algebraMap ℝ CoordinateRing`.
Thus the affine source and its morphism to the real base are constructed
from the finite presentation, rather than supplied as arbitrary schemes.
The explicitly bundled representing isomorphism is on real algebras;
this module does not additionally package an equivalence of morphisms in
the category of schemes over `Spec ℝ`.

The module does not yet package a group-scheme structure, prove a splitting
as a torus after extension to `ℂ`, or turn the previously constructed
natural Shimura homomorphism into a scheme morphism. Those are subsequent
steps. The PEL moduli functor, canonical models, level schemes, and
coefficient descent remain obligations of the host task.
