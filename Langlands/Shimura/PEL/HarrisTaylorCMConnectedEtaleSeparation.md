# Étale separatedness of the normalized CM classes on connected tests

`CMConnectedEtaleSeparatedStatement` is the uniqueness part of
`CMConnectedEtaleSheafStatement`. At every small level \(U\) and for every
jointly surjective étale family of connected admissible tests over a
connected admissible test, a family of unpointed normalized CM classes has
at most one amalgamation. The proposition uses the level-\(U\) component of
`unpointedClassTowerPresheaf` and `Presieve.IsSeparatedFor` for the
presieve generated by the family.

`cmConnectedEtale_separated` proves the proposition from
`ConnectedGeometricPointHomFaithfulnessStatement` and
`ConnectedEtaleRationalHomDescentStatement`. Given two amalgamations, it
chooses a point on one member and lifts both classes at its image to
actual polarized representatives satisfying the positive object and
numerical conditions. Path transport gives representatives with those
same polarized objects at every other point. Equality of the local
classes provides a rational isomorphism and a multiplier on each member.
Naturality of geometric point transport extends the orbit equation of each
witness to every point of that member.

The proof compares the witnesses on every connected locally noetherian
common test. The relative dual existence and geometry theorems supply
admissibility over \(F\). The square compatibility theorem uses
`cmSmallLevelRigidity` and one-point Hom faithfulness to identify the
two witness maps after the canonical pullback comparisons. Rational Hom
descent then gives a global equivariant rational isomorphism with the
prescribed restrictions. The proof reflects its polarization equation
and orbit equation from the chosen member and applies
`HarrisNumericalWeilLevel.classOf_eq_iff`. Evaluation at the chosen image
point is injective for compatible geometric families, giving equality of
the two unpointed classes.

This is a direct uniqueness argument for the Library's central simple
unitary data. Its moduli-theoretic motivation is the small-level
representability of the Harris–Taylor moduli problem in *The Geometry and
Cohomology of Some Simple Shimura Varieties* (2001), III.1, p. 91, under
the running division-algebra assumptions of I.7, p. 51. The Lean proof
uses the stated Hom hypotheses and the Library's geometric rigidity
theorem.
