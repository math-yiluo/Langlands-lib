# Orbital integration on a direct product

`TwistedOrbitalProductStatement` considers an isomorphism of topological
groups \(D:G_1\times G_2\to G\) intertwining an endomorphism of \(G\) with
the product of endomorphisms of \(G_1,G_2\). For \(\delta\in G\), the two
coordinates of \(D^{-1}(\delta)\) give the factor twisted centralizers.
Both factors are second countable and locally compact, and the second
factor is Hausdorff. The groups and all three left quotients have their
Borel measurable structures. The factor centralizers are closed. Their
measures are right invariant, sigma-finite and finite on compact sets.
The factor ambient measures are finite on compact sets. Given compatible
factor quotient measures, the statement gives a compatible quotient
measure on the twisted centralizer quotient of \(G\). The ambient
measure is the pushforward of the product ambient measure. The
centralizer measure transports the product centralizer measure by the
induced group isomorphism.

The group isomorphism takes the product of the factor centralizers onto
the actual centralizer. It therefore identifies the actual left quotient
with the product of the factor quotients. The product Weil formula and
transport along this isomorphism prove compatibility. Under that chart
the orbit function of a tensor product is the product of the two orbit
functions. For any complex-valued factor functions, the resulting total
Bochner integral equals the product of the two factor orbital integrals.
The total integral is zero on a nonintegrable orbit function. The
ordinary case uses identity endomorphisms. The centralizer construction
preserves right invariance, finiteness on compact sets and positivity on
open sets when the factor measures have those properties.

This is an internal use of the twisted orbital integrals in Getz–Hahn,
§18.5. The factorization applies to the equivariant splitting of the
norm-one group at a finite place. It uses supplied factor measures and
the stated transported ambient measure.
