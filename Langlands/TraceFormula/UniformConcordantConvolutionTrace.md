# Uniform cuspidal trace comparison for locally concordant convolutions

`Langlands.TraceFormula.UniformConcordantConvolutionTraceStatement` assumes
`GeneralLinearBaseChangeGlobalMatchingStatement` and
`GeneralLinearBaseChangeTraceComparisonStatement`. Its conclusion compares the
cuspidal traces of factorizable convolutions on the adelic points of
`W = Res_{L/K} GL_n` and `G = GL_n` for a cyclic extension `L/K` of number fields.

Fix a split torus `T₀` and central morphisms `iG : T₀ → G` and `iW : T₀ → W`.
Each induces an injective pullback on rational characters with finite-index
image. The morphism `iW` is the composite of `iG` with the Weil unit, and the
Galois automorphism fixes `iW`. The cuspidal construction uses
`ParabolicDynamicStatement` and connected reductivity of `G` and `W`.
On each side the quotient is by the subgroup generated by the image of the
positive real points of `T₀` and the rational points of the group. The chosen
Haar measures of the two norm-one groups are right invariant.

Fix a finite set `S` containing the infinite places and local Haar measures on
`S`. The unit tests are concordant at each finite place outside `S` for Haar
measures giving the respective integral subgroups volume one. The quotient
twist `J` is defined from the Galois automorphism, preservation of the positive
rational subgroup, and measure preservation on the quotient.

There is one positive real scalar `c` for this datum. Consider any two
convolutions `φ = φ₁ * φ₂` and `f = f₁ * f₂` formed with the respective
adelic `Measure.haar`. Each factor is continuous and compactly supported,
with Hilbert–Schmidt cuspidal integrated action. The second factor on each
side has zero integrals on all two-sided translates of the unipotent radicals
of proper rational parabolics. The support of the convolution on the `G`
side projects to regular elliptic elements at a finite place of `K`. On the
`W` side the projection of its norm to a finite place of `L` has this
property. The convolutions have local factorizations with continuous compactly
supported concordant factors at `S` and integral subgroup indicators outside
`S`. Writing `S_W` for the cuspidal submodule of the full quotient `L²` on the
`W` side, the conclusion is

\[
  \operatorname{tr}\bigl(P_{S_W} R_W(\phi) J \iota_{S_W}\bigr)
    = c\,\operatorname{tr}\bigl(R_{G,\mathrm{cusp}}(f)\bigr).
\]

Here `P` is orthogonal projection and `ι` is inclusion. The proposition
includes integrability witnesses for `φ` and `f`. The scalar is chosen before
the local factors, the convolutions and the finite places used for ellipticity.

## Implemented proof

`cuspidalCompressionTraceOfConvolution` writes the integrated convolution
operator as the composition of its two integrated factors. The radical
integral condition puts the image of the second factor in the cuspidal space,
which the first factor preserves. Precomposition by a bounded operator
preserves this range inclusion and trace class, so
`compression_trace_of_range` identifies the trace with its cuspidal
compression. `cuspidalIntegratedTraceOfConvolution` uses the identity
precomposition and the value formula for the restricted action to obtain
the ordinary cuspidal trace.

`uniformConcordantConvolutionTrace` first obtains `c` from global matching.
For each pair of convolutions, matching at norms and vanishing off norms give
the geometric hypotheses of the global trace comparison. This comparison
supplies trace class of the ambient integrated actions and identifies their
traces, with the twist on the `W` side. Proof irrelevance identifies the
quotient twist constructed by the trace comparison with `J`. The two
compression identities give the stated equality.

## Source

Arthur–Clozel, *Simple algebras, base change, and the advanced theory of the
trace formula* (1989), Chapter 1, §4, (4.4)–(4.7), printed pp. 43–44 (PDF
pp. 57–58), pass from associated local factors and the ordinary and twisted
simple trace formulas to an identity of cuspidal traces. The Lean result
factors this argument through the two global comparison propositions and
uses the Library's quotient and Haar conventions. The positive scalar comes
from global matching for these conventions.
