# Weak descent of a stable cusp with a trace identity at a place with one place above (statement)

Module `Langlands.TraceFormula.CyclicStableCuspidalDescentTraceIdentityStatement`, namespace
`Langlands.TraceFormula`. It contains one proposition definition and no proof:

- `Langlands.TraceFormula.CyclicStableCuspidalDescentTraceIdentityStatement.{uF, uE, uV, uW}`.

In words: let `E/F` be a cyclic extension of number fields of degree `l` (any `l ≥ 1`, not
necessarily prime) with generator `σ`, in which every infinite place of `F` splits completely.
Let `v` be a finite place of `F` with only one place `w` of `E` above it, and let `v₁ ≠ v₂` be
finite places of `F` that split completely in `E`. Let `Π` be a cuspidal automorphic
representation of `GL_n(𝔸_E)` (`n = r + 1`) with unitary central character, isomorphic to its
`σ`-conjugate, whose local components at all places above `v₁` and above `v₂` are supercuspidal
and whose local component `Π_w` is irreducible and admissible. Then there is a cuspidal
automorphic representation `π` of `GL_n(𝔸_F)` with unitary central character such that `Π` is a
weak base change of `π`, and, for a `σ`-intertwiner `A` of `Π_w` and a constant `c ≠ 0`,

`tr (Π_w(φ) ∘ A) = c · tr π_v(f)`

for all concordant (matching) test functions `φ` on `GL_n(F_v ⊗_F E)` and `f` on `GL_n(F_v)`
such that `φ` is supported in the `σ`-regular set, `f` in the regular semisimple set, and
`det g` is a norm from `(F_v ⊗_F E)^×` wherever `f g ≠ 0`. The constant does not depend on the
Haar measures.

This is a characteristic-zero form of the comparison step in the global proof of descent for
`σ`-discrete series: Arthur–Clozel 1989, Chapter 1, §6.3(b) (printed pp. 56–57), over number
fields, and Henniart–Lemaire 2011, II.6.16–II.6.17 (printed pp. 131–132), over function fields.
The simple trace formula and its twisted form, compared for a given `σ`-stable cuspidal `Π`,
produce the cuspidal `π` and the identity at `v` (see "Place in the descent argument").

The module declares five `local instance`s of `AddCommGroup`: on the Library's form spaces
`Langlands.Automorphic.RationalInvariantFunctions`, `ContinuousRationalFunctions`,
`FiniteSmoothRationalFunctions` and `AutomorphicForms`, and on the carrier `A.Space` of a
`LocalRealization`. They are `Submodule.addCommGroup _`, as in the Library's
`Langlands.Automorphic.SupercuspidalComponent`. They are not exported.

## Statement

For all `F`, `E`, `σ`, `l`, `v`, `w`, `v₁`, `v₂`, `r`, `T`, `P` as above, assume:

1. `IsCyclicAlgebra σ l`;
2. `IsCyclicShiftSplit F E u.Completion σ l` for every infinite place `u` of `F`;
3. `w` is the only place of `E` above `v`;
4. `v₁ ≠ v₂`, `IsCyclicShiftSplit F E (v₁.adicCompletion F) σ l` and
   `IsCyclicShiftSplit F E (v₂.adicCompletion F) σ l`;
5. `T` has unitary central character and `T.isoClass.IsGaloisStable σ`;
6. `P.localComponent w₁.1` is supercuspidal for every `w₁ : FinitePlacesAbove E v₁`, and
   `P.localComponent w₂.1` is supercuspidal for every `w₂ : FinitePlacesAbove E v₂`;
7. `P.localComponent w.1` is irreducible and admissible.

Then there are `S : CuspidalOperatorRealization F r` and
`D : CuspidalSpectralPresentation.{uF, uV} S` such that

- `S` has unitary central character;
- `IsWeakBaseChangeOf.{uF, uE, uV, uW} T.isoClass S.isoClass`;
- `π_v := D.localComponent v` is irreducible and admissible;
- there are `hπ : Langlands.IsAdmissible π_v.representation`, `hP : Langlands.IsAdmissible PiV`,
  `A : SigmaIntertwiner σv (r + 1) PiV` and `c : ℂ` with `c ≠ 0` such that, for every Haar
  measure `μF` on `GL_{r+1}(F_v)` and `μE` on `GL_{r+1}(E_v)` and all
  `φ : SchwartzBruhat (GL_{r+1}(E_v))` and `f : SchwartzBruhat (GL_{r+1}(F_v))` with
  - `AreConcordant σv l μF μE φ f`,
  - `IsSigmaRegularSupported σv l φ`,
  - `IsRegularSupported f`,
  - `GeneralLinear.determinant g ∈ N(E_v^×)` for every `g` with `f g ≠ 0`,

  we have
  `twistedDistribution μE PiV hP A φ = c * Character.distribution μF π_v.representation hπ f`.

The part of the last clause after `∃ hπ hP A c,` is, by definition, the Library's
`Langlands.BaseChange.IsRegularSigmaLift σv l π_v.representation hπ PiV hP A c` (module
`Langlands.BaseChange.SigmaDiscreteRegularDescentStatement`): the proposition obtained by
replacing it with that predicate is equivalent to this statement by `Iff.rfl`, which was checked
in Lean against both modules. It is written out so that this module does not import that one.

Remarks on the form.

- **Any degree.** `l` is not assumed to be prime, as in the Library's
  `SigmaDiscreteRegularDescentStatement` and `SigmaDiscreteDescentStatement`. The Library
  statements used in "Why the statement is true" for the global comparison
  (`GeneralLinearSimpleTwistedTraceFormulaStatement`,
  `GeneralLinearBaseChangeTraceComparisonStatement`, `CyclicCuspidalFiberStatement`,
  `RegularTransferStatement`) are stated for every cyclic degree, and Arthur–Clozel prove the
  fibre theorem III.3.1 without assuming the degree prime (printed p. 201).
- **The two split places.** The simple trace formula and its twisted form need two auxiliary
  places (Arthur–Clozel, Chapter 1, Lemmas 2.4–2.5, printed pp. 13–15): a place `v₁`, split in
  `E`, where the test function is a coefficient of a supercuspidal representation (conditions (3)
  and (3′)), and a place `v₂`, split in `E`, where it is supported in the regular elliptic set
  (conditions (4) and (4′)). Hypotheses 4 and 6 provide them; the test functions at `v` itself
  are then only restricted to the (`σ`-)regular set. The places above `v₂` carry supercuspidal
  components so that a function supported in the elliptic set has nonzero trace on `Π` there
  (step 3 below).
- **Degree one.** The statement does not require `v ≠ v₁` or `v ≠ v₂`. For `l ≥ 2` these
  inequalities hold automatically: `IsCyclicShiftSplit` at `vᵢ` makes `F_{vᵢ} ⊗_F E ≅ F_{vᵢ}^l`,
  so `vᵢ` has `l ≥ 2` places above it, while `v` has exactly one. For `l = 1` every place of
  `F` has exactly one place above it and `v` may equal `v₁` or `v₂`; the argument for this case
  is separate (see "Why the statement is true").
- **`S` and `D` are produced.** In the descent no cuspidal representation of `GL_n(𝔸_F)` is given
  beforehand: the comparison shows that the twisted trace of `Π` is matched by the trace of a
  cuspidal `π` (Henniart–Lemaire II.6.17, printed p. 132: "il existe une représentation
  automorphe cuspidale ..."). The presentation `D` is also existential because its universe `uV`
  is free; at `v`, the irreducible local component of any other presentation of `S` is
  equivalent to that of `D` (Library `CuspidalSpectralPresentation.nonempty_localComponent_equiv`,
  module `Langlands.Automorphic.AssociatedPresentationComparison`).
- **The determinant condition.** The comparison identifies the twisted trace with a sum over the
  fibre `π ⊗ (κ^i ∘ det)` of `π`, where `κ` is the idele class character of `E/F`; these twists
  all have the same central character on the norm subgroup `Z₁ = N(𝔸_E^×)` of the center, the
  only part of the central character that the comparison fixes. On functions `f` with
  `det g ∈ N(E_v^×)` wherever `f g ≠ 0`, all twists `π_v ⊗ (κ_v^i ∘ det)` have the same trace,
  because `κ_v` is trivial on `N(E_v^×)` ("on the image of the norm, their local characters
  coincide", Arthur–Clozel, printed p. 57). Removing the condition needs the Weyl integration
  argument of Henniart–Lemaire II.6.13 (printed pp. 129–130), which is not part of this
  statement.
- **`A` is existential.** A `σv`-intertwiner of the irreducible admissible `PiV` is unique up to
  a nonzero scalar (Library theorem `SigmaIntertwiner.exists_smul_toLinearMap`), so the identity
  for one `A` gives it for every `A` with a rescaled `c`.
- **`c` before the measures.** Replacing `μE` by `a • μE` (`a > 0`) turns
  `AreConcordant σv l μF μE φ f` into the same condition for `a • φ`, and multiplies the twisted
  trace by `a`; similarly for `μF` and `f`. So the identity for one pair of Haar measures gives it
  for all pairs with the same `c`.
- **What is not claimed.** The statement does not say that `PiV` is a `σ`-lift of `π_v` on all
  concordant pairs (Library `IsSigmaLift`), nor that `π_v` is square-integrable or
  supercuspidal, nor anything about `π` at places other than `v` beyond weak base change.

## Why the statement is true

Write `Π`, `π` for the representations of `T`, `S`, `n = r + 1`, `G = GL_n` and `N` for the
norm maps. The argument follows Arthur–Clozel 1989, Chapter 1, §6.3(b) (printed pp. 56–57, with
the simple trace formulas of Lemmas 2.4–2.5, printed pp. 12–15) and Henniart–Lemaire 2011,
II.6.2–II.6.17 (printed pp. 118–132), for the given `σ`-stable `Π`. The Library statements named
below are statements a proof can use; this module neither assumes nor proves them. The
Library's simple trace formulas (`GeneralLinearSimpleTraceFormulaStatement`,
`GeneralLinearSimpleTwistedTraceFormulaStatement`,
`GeneralLinearBaseChangeTraceComparisonStatement`) are stated on the norm-one groups `G(𝔸)¹`,
not with a character of a subgroup of the center as in the sources followed below.

### Degree one

If `l = 1`, `IsCyclicAlgebra σ 1` says that `E` has dimension one over `F`, so the structure map
is a field isomorphism `e : F ≃+* E`, and `σ` is the identity. No trace formula is needed.

- Take for `S` a representative of the pulled-back class
  `CuspidalOperatorRealization.IsoClass.arithmeticComap e T.isoClass` (module
  `Langlands.Automorphic.OperatorArithmeticTransportClasses`). Its central character is that of
  `T` read through `e`, hence unitary. All residue degrees are one, and the Euler polynomials of
  `S` and `T` agree at corresponding places (Library `CuspidalArithmeticSpectralTransportStatement`),
  which is the weak base change condition. `D` is chosen as in step 6 below.
- At `v`, the map `b : GL_n(F_v) → GL_n(F_v ⊗_F E)` induced by `x ↦ x ⊗ 1` is an isomorphism of
  topological groups, and `σv` is the identity. The local component `π_v` of `S` corresponds to
  `Π_w` under the completion of `e` at `v`, so `PiV ∘ b` is equivalent to `π_v`. Take for `A` the
  identity, a `σv`-intertwiner because `σv` acts trivially.
- In degree one every element is a norm and the pairs associated with a regular `γ` are the
  `b(γ')` with `γ'` conjugate to `γ`. So `AreConcordant σv 1 μF μE φ f` says that `f` and
  `φ ∘ b` have the same regular semisimple orbital integrals, for `μF` and for the Haar measure
  `μ` on `GL_n(F_v)` with `b_* μ = μE` (the module
  `Langlands.BaseChange.CuspidalSigmaLiftStructureDegreeOne` shows that `φ ∘ b` and `φ` concord
  for `μ` and `b_* μ`). Both functions are supported in the regular semisimple set. On such
  functions the trace of `π_v` is the integral against its character, which is locally constant
  on the regular set, and the Weyl integration formula (Henniart–Lemaire II.2.10, printed
  pp. 70–72; Library `WeylIntegrationFormulaStatement`) writes this integral through the
  regular orbital integrals of the function, as in the proof of Henniart–Lemaire II.2.9
  (II.2.11, printed p. 72). Hence `tr π_v(f)` for `μF` equals `tr π_v(φ ∘ b)` for `μ`, which is
  `tr PiV(φ)` for `μE`: the identity holds with `A` the identity and `c = 1`.

### Degree at least two

Now `v ∉ {v₁, v₂}` (remark "Degree one" above), so the three places carry separate local
factors.

1. **Central characters.** The central character `ω_Π` of `Π` is a unitary character of
   `C_E = 𝔸_E^×/E^×`, and `σ`-stability gives `ω_Π ∘ σ = ω_Π`. For the cyclic extension `E/F`,
   `Ĥ^{-1}(Gal(E/F), C_E) ≅ H^1(Gal(E/F), C_E) = 0` by class field theory, so the kernel of the
   norm on `C_E` consists of the quotients `x^σ/x` and `ω_Π` factors through the norm. Let
   `Z₁ := N(𝔸_E^×) ⊂ 𝔸_F^×` be the norm subgroup of the center (Arthur–Clozel I §2.2, printed
   p. 12; Henniart–Lemaire II.6.2, printed p. 118). This gives a character `ω` of `Z₁`, trivial
   on `Z₁ ∩ F^×`, with `ω_Π = ω ∘ N`; it is unitary because `N` maps `𝔸_E^×` onto `Z₁`. Write
   `χ := ω ∘ N = ω_Π` and, at each place `u` of `F`, `Z₁u := N_u(E_u^×)`, an open subgroup of
   finite index in `F_u^×`, with components `ω_u` and `χ_u = ω_u ∘ N_u`. Downstairs only `ω` on
   `Z₁` is fixed, not a full central character: the spectral side over `F` is the space
   `L⁰_ω(G, F)` of cusp forms on `G(𝔸_F)` transforming by `ω` under `Z₁` (Henniart–Lemaire II.6.3,
   printed p. 119; Arthur–Clozel, condition (1), printed p. 13), which contains every cuspidal
   `π'` whose central character extends `ω`. Upstairs it is the space `L⁰_χ(G, E)` of cusp forms
   transforming by `χ` under the full center `Z(𝔸_E)` (Henniart–Lemaire II.6.4, printed p. 120),
   which contains `Π`.
2. **Local conventions, and the test functions at `v`.**
   - *Places.* At a place `u` of `F`, `E_u := F_u ⊗_F E` is a product of `m_u` copies of a
     cyclic field extension `E_{u,1}` of `F_u` of degree `l / m_u` (the completion of `E` at a
     place above `u`). In suitable coordinates `G(E_u) = G(E_{u,1})^{m_u}` and
     `σ(x_1, …, x_{m_u}) = (x_2, …, x_{m_u}, τ_u(x_1))`, with `τ_u` a generator of
     `Gal(E_{u,1}/F_u)` (Arthur–Clozel I §5, printed p. 48; Henniart–Lemaire II.2.8, printed
     p. 67). Then `Z(E_u) = (E_{u,1}^×)^{m_u}`, `Z₁u = N(E_{u,1}^×)` and
     `χ_u(z_1, …, z_{m_u}) = ∏_i χ_{u,1}(z_i)` with `χ_{u,1} := ω_u ∘ N_{E_{u,1}/F_u}`
     (Henniart–Lemaire I.2.9, printed p. 22). At `v`, `m_v = 1`, since `w` is the only place
     above `v`. At `v₁`, `v₂` and the infinite places, `m_u = l`, `E_{u,1} = F_u`, `τ_u` is the
     identity and `Z₁u = F_u^×`.
   - *Functions, measures and operators.* The local factors of the sources' trace formulas are
     functions `f_u ∈ C_c^∞(G(F_u), ω_u)` and `φ_u ∈ C_c^∞(G(E_u), χ_u)` (Henniart–Lemaire
     II.2.6, printed p. 66, and II.6.3–II.6.4, printed pp. 119–120). Such an `f_u` is locally
     constant (smooth at the infinite places), compactly supported modulo `Z₁u`, and satisfies
     `f_u(zg) = ω_u(z)^{-1} f_u(g)` for `z ∈ Z₁u`. The space for `φ_u` is defined in the same
     way, with `Z(E_u)` and `χ_u`. Fix Haar measures `dg_u` on `G(F_u)`, `dz_u` on `Z₁u`,
     `dg_{u,1}` on `G(E_{u,1})` and `dz_{u,1}` on `E_{u,1}^×`. Let `d̄g_u := dg_u / dz_u` on
     `Z₁u \ G(F_u)` and `d̄g_{u,1} := dg_{u,1} / dz_{u,1}` on `Z(E_{u,1}) \ G(E_{u,1})` be the
     quotient measures. Where `E_{u,1} = F_u` (the completely split places, among them `v₁`, `v₂`
     and the infinite places) take the common measures `dg_{u,1} := dg_u` and
     `dz_{u,1} := dz_u`, so that `d̄g_{u,1} = d̄g_u`. Then the reduced orbital integrals below and
     the ordinary orbital integrals on `G(F_u)` use the same ambient, centralizer and quotient
     measures, and so do the operators of `G(E_{u,1}) = G(F_u)`, as in Henniart–Lemaire
     II.2.15–II.2.16 (printed pp. 76–77, with `dg_E = (dg)^d`). On `G(E_u)`, on `Z(E_u)` and on
     `Z(E_u) \ G(E_u) = (Z(E_{u,1}) \ G(E_{u,1}))^{m_u}` take the product measures, as
     Henniart–Lemaire do at a split place (II.2.16, printed p. 77: `d̄g_E = (d̄g)^d`). If `Z₁u`
     acts on `π'_u` by `ω_u` and `Z(E_u)` acts on `Π'_u` by `χ_u`, the operators are
     `π'_u(f_u) = ∫_{Z₁u \ G(F_u)} f_u(g) π'_u(g) d̄g_u` and
     `Π'_u(φ_u) = ∫_{Z(E_u) \ G(E_u)} φ_u(g) Π'_u(g) d̄g_{E_u}` (Henniart–Lemaire II.2.7, printed
     p. 67).
   - *Reduction to one field factor.* For `φ_u = φ_1 ⊗ ⋯ ⊗ φ_{m_u}` with
     `φ_i ∈ C_c^∞(G(E_{u,1}), χ_{u,1})`, put `φ_u^* := φ_1 * ⋯ * φ_{m_u}`, where `*` is the
     convolution on `C_c^∞(G(E_{u,1}), χ_{u,1})` defined by `d̄g_{u,1}`, and extend linearly
     (Henniart–Lemaire I.2.9, printed p. 22). For `m_u = 1`, `φ_u^* = φ_u`. Let `m_u ≥ 2`. The
     integrand `x ↦ φ_u(x^{-1} δ σ(x))` of a `σ`-twisted orbital integral is invariant under
     `Z(E_u)`. Only the `σ`-fixed part `Z(F_u)` of `Z(E_u)` lies in the twisted centralizer of
     `δ`, and `Z(E_u)` is not compact modulo `Z(F_u)`. So the integral over
     `G^σ_δ(F_u) \ G(E_u)` is not absolutely convergent unless the integrand vanishes, and the
     twisted orbital integrals of such `φ_u` are defined by reduction to one field factor. The
     change of variables (5.1)–(5.2) of Arthur–Clozel I §5 (printed pp. 48–49) identifies the
     `σ`-twisted centralizer of `δ = (δ_1, …, δ_{m_u})` with the `τ_u`-twisted centralizer of
     `y := δ_1 ⋯ δ_{m_u}` in `G(E_{u,1})`. It turns the twisted orbital integral of `φ_u` at
     `δ` into the `τ_u`-twisted orbital integral `Λ_{τ_u}(φ_u^*, y)` over
     `G^{τ_u}_y(F_u) \ G(E_{u,1})` ("the quotient measures must be normalized in obvious ways",
     printed p. 49). Henniart–Lemaire I.2.9 fix the quotient measure so that
     `Λ_σ(φ_u, δ) = Λ_{τ_u}(φ_u^*, y)`. Since `E_{u,1}` is a field, `Z(E_{u,1})` is compact
     modulo `F_u^×` and `Λ_{τ_u}(φ_u^*, y)` converges. Hence `(φ_u, f_u)` concord if and only if
     `φ_u^*` and `f_u` concord for `E_{u,1}/F_u` and `τ_u`. That is: the orbital integrals of
     `f_u` vanish at regular non-norms, and at associated pairs `(y, γ)` with `γ` regular the
     two orbital integrals agree, for centralizer measures associated in the sense of
     Henniart–Lemaire I.2.5 and quotient measures compatible with `dg_{u,1}` and `dg_u`. This
     is the local condition of the global concordance of Henniart–Lemaire II.6.6, Définition
     (printed p. 122). There the centralizer measures are associated at every place and give
     volume one to the maximal compact subgroups at almost all places. For continuous
     compactly supported functions the orbital identity is the Library's
     `CyclicProductConvolutionStatement` (module `Langlands.BaseChange.CyclicProductConvolution`),
     and at completely split places it is `SplitMatchingStatement`.
   - *Units.* For an open compact subgroup `K'` of `G(E_{u,1})` on whose center `χ_{u,1}` is
     trivial, let `e_{K'} ∈ C_c^∞(G(E_{u,1}), χ_{u,1})` vanish off `Z(E_{u,1}) K'` and satisfy
     `e_{K'}(zk) = χ_{u,1}(z)^{-1} / vol(Z(E_{u,1}) \ Z(E_{u,1}) K')`. Then `ψ * e_{K'} = ψ`
     for every `ψ ∈ C_c^∞(G(E_{u,1}), χ_{u,1})` that is right invariant under `K'`.
   - *Twisted traces.* Intertwiners are taken in the forward convention of the Library's
     `SigmaIntertwiner`, as in the statement: `A ∘ Π'(g) = Π'(σ(g)) ∘ A`. An irreducible
     `σ`-stable `Π'_u` is `Π'_1 ⊗ ⋯ ⊗ Π'_1` (`m_u` factors), with `Π'_1` a `τ_u`-stable
     representation of `G(E_{u,1})` (Henniart–Lemaire II.2.8, printed pp. 67–68). Let `A_1` be a
     `τ_u`-intertwiner of `Π'_1`, so `A_1 ∘ Π'_1(h) = Π'_1(τ_u(h)) ∘ A_1`, and put
     `A_u(x_1 ⊗ ⋯ ⊗ x_{m_u}) := x_2 ⊗ ⋯ ⊗ x_{m_u} ⊗ A_1(x_1)`. For `g = (g_1, …, g_{m_u})`,
     `A_u(Π'_u(g)(x_1 ⊗ ⋯ ⊗ x_{m_u}))` is
     `Π'_1(g_2) x_2 ⊗ ⋯ ⊗ Π'_1(g_{m_u}) x_{m_u} ⊗ Π'_1(τ_u(g_1)) A_1(x_1)`, which is
     `Π'_u(σ(g)) A_u(x_1 ⊗ ⋯ ⊗ x_{m_u})`. So `A_u` is a `σ`-intertwiner of `Π'_u`. For `τ_u`
     trivial and `A_1` the identity it is the Library's `splitTensorShift`, whose intertwining
     relation is `splitTensorShift_comp` (module `Langlands.BaseChange.SplitPlace`). The operator
     `v_1 ⊗ ⋯ ⊗ v_d ↦ v_d ⊗ v_1 ⊗ ⋯ ⊗ v_{d-1}` displayed by Henniart–Lemaire at a split place
     (II.2.15, printed p. 76) rotates the other way and satisfies the inverse relation
     `Π(δ^σ) = A^{-1} ∘ Π(δ) ∘ A`; there `A_u` is its inverse. With
     `B_i := Π'_1(φ_i)`, the operator `Π'_u(φ_u) ∘ A_u` sends `x_1 ⊗ ⋯ ⊗ x_{m_u}` to
     `B_1 x_2 ⊗ ⋯ ⊗ B_{m_u - 1} x_{m_u} ⊗ B_{m_u} A_1(x_1)`, whose trace is
     `tr (B_1 B_2 ⋯ B_{m_u} A_1)`. Since `B_1 ⋯ B_{m_u} = Π'_1(φ_1 * ⋯ * φ_{m_u})` for the
     convolution defined by `d̄g_{u,1}`, this gives
     `tr (Π'_u(φ_u) ∘ A_u) = tr (Π'_1(φ_u^*) ∘ A_1)`, the computation of Henniart–Lemaire II.2.8
     (printed p. 68) and, with quotient measures, II.2.16 (printed pp. 77–78). For compactly
     supported functions at a split place, the resulting `σ`-lift property of
     `Π'_1 ⊗ ⋯ ⊗ Π'_1` is the Library's `SplitSigmaLiftStatement`.
   - *Averaging at `v`.* `E_v` is a field, so `N_v : E_v^× → Z₁v` is surjective with compact
     kernel `E_v^{×,1}`, the elements of norm one. Hence, for a Haar measure `dz_E` on
     `E_v^×`, the image `dz := (N_v)_* dz_E` is a Haar measure on `Z₁v`. This fails when `E_u`
     has several factors: then the kernel of the norm is not compact. Take `dg_v := μF`,
     `dg_{v,1} := μE` (on `G(E_v) ≅ G(E_w)` through `h`), `dz_{v,1} := dz_E` and `dz_v := dz`.
     From the compactly supported `φ` and `f` of the statement form

     `f^ω(g) := ∫_{Z₁v} f(zg) ω_v(z) dz` and `φ^χ(g) := ∫_{E_v^×} φ(zg) χ_v(z) dz_E`

     (Henniart–Lemaire I.2.10, printed pp. 22–23, and II.2.6, printed p. 66; with this choice
     of `dz` their constant `c` is `1`). Then:
     - *Traces.* If `π'_v` is admissible and `Z₁v` acts on it by `ω_v`, then `π'_v(f)` for `μF`
       equals `π'_v(f^ω)` for `d̄g_v`. Likewise `Π'_v(φ)` for `μE` equals `Π'_v(φ^χ)` for
       `d̄g_{v,1}` when `Z(E_v)` acts on `Π'_v` by `χ_v` (Henniart–Lemaire II.2.7, printed
       p. 67). So the averaged factors recover the distributions `tr π'_v(f)` and
       `tr (Π'_v(φ) ∘ A)` of the statement.
     - *Concordance.* If `(δ, γ)` is an associated pair with `γ` regular and `z ∈ E_v^×`, then
       `(zδ, N_v(z) γ)` is associated, since `N(zδ) = N_v(z) N(δ)`, and the (twisted)
       centralizers are unchanged. Hence the `σv`-twisted orbital integral of `φ^χ` at `δ` is
       `∫ χ_v(z) O^σ_{zδ}(φ) dz_E = ∫ ω_v(N_v z) O_{N_v(z) γ}(f) dz_E = ∫_{Z₁v} ω_v(y) O_{yγ}(f) dy`,
       the orbital integral of `f^ω` at `γ`, for the same centralizer and quotient measures. The
       last equality is the change of variables along `N_v`, which uses the compact kernel. If
       `γ` is regular and not a norm, neither is `yγ` for `y ∈ Z₁v`, so the orbital integral of
       `f^ω` at `γ` vanishes. Thus `φ^χ` and `f^ω` concord (the lemma of Henniart–Lemaire
       I.2.10).
     - *Supports.* `φ^χ` is supported in `E_v^× · supp φ`, inside the `σv`-regular set, and
       `f^ω` in `Z₁v · supp f`, inside the regular semisimple set. As `det(zg) = z^n det g` and
       `z^n ∈ N(E_v^×)` for `z ∈ Z₁v`, `f^ω` still vanishes off `det⁻¹(N(E_v^×))`.
3. **Global test functions.** Fix a finite set `Σ` of places of `F` containing the infinite
   places, `v`, `v₁`, `v₂` and the places where `E`, `ω` or `Π` ramifies. Take decomposable
   `φ_𝔸 = ⊗ φ_u` on `G(𝔸_E)` and `f_𝔸 = ⊗ f_u` on `G(𝔸_F)` with factors as in step 2, concordant
   at every place in the sense of step 2. The global measures are the products of the local
   measures of step 2, with the normalizations of Henniart–Lemaire II.6.6 and II.6.8(iii)
   (printed pp. 122 and 124) at almost all places. For each `u ∈ Σ`, `u ≠ v`, the factor is
   chosen with `tr (Π_u(φ_u) ∘ A_u) ≠ 0`, where `Π_u` is the component of `Π` at `u` and
   `Π_{u,1}` is its factor on `G(E_{u,1})` as in step 2. `Π` is unitary, and so are its local
   components.
   - At `v`: the averaged pair `(φ^χ, f^ω)` of step 2.
   - At `v₁`: `Π_{v₁,1}` is supercuspidal, with `Z(F_{v₁})` acting by `ω_{v₁}`. Let
     `ξ(g) := ⟨Π_{v₁,1}(g)^{-1} e, e⟩` for a unit vector `e`, a coefficient in
     `C_c^∞(G(F_{v₁}), ω_{v₁})`. Let `d` be the formal degree of `Π_{v₁,1}` for `d̄g_{v₁,1}`.
     Schur orthogonality gives `ξ * ξ = d^{-1} ξ`, and `Π_{v₁,1}(ξ)` is `d^{-1}` times the
     orthogonal projection onto `ℂ e`. Take `φ_{v₁} := ξ ⊗ ⋯ ⊗ ξ`, as in Arthur–Clozel (3′),
     and `f_{v₁} := φ_{v₁}^* = d^{1-l} ξ`, a supercuspidal coefficient as in (3) (compare the
     level-zero coefficients of Henniart–Lemaire II.2.16, printed p. 77). As `τ_{v₁}` is
     trivial, step 2 makes the twisted orbital integrals of `φ_{v₁}` the orbital integrals of
     `f_{v₁}`, so the pair concords. With `A_1` the identity,
     `tr (Π_{v₁}(φ_{v₁}) ∘ A_{v₁}) = tr Π_{v₁,1}(f_{v₁}) = d^{-l} ≠ 0`.
   - At `v₂`: `Π_{v₂,1}` is supercuspidal; let `Θ` be its character, a locally constant
     function on the regular set with `Θ(zg) = ω_{v₂}(z) Θ(g)`. `Θ` does not vanish on the open
     set of regular elliptic elements whose image in `PGL_n` is regular. Indeed, the orbital
     integrals of a supercuspidal coefficient vanish off the elliptic set. So the Weyl
     integration formula writes the trace of a coefficient of `Π_{v₂,1}`, which is nonzero as
     computed at `v₁`, as an integral of `Θ` over the elliptic set; and that open set is dense
     in the regular elliptic set. Choose `γ₀` in it with `Θ(γ₀) ≠ 0`, and a compact open
     neighbourhood `Ω` of `γ₀` in it on which `Θ` is constant and such that `ω_{v₂}` is trivial
     on `Z(F_{v₂}) ∩ Ω Ω^{-1}`. Let `φ_1 ∈ C_c^∞(G(F_{v₂}), ω_{v₂})` vanish off `Z(F_{v₂}) Ω`
     and equal `ω_{v₂}(z)^{-1}` at `zx` (`x ∈ Ω`). Choose `K'` as in step 2 with `Ω K' = Ω`,
     and take `φ_{v₂} := φ_1 ⊗ e_{K'} ⊗ ⋯ ⊗ e_{K'}`. The product of the supports lies in
     `Z(F_{v₂}) Ω`, as in Arthur–Clozel (4′). Take `f_{v₂} := φ_{v₂}^* = φ_1`, supported in the
     elliptic set as in (4). The pair concords by step 2, and
     `tr (Π_{v₂}(φ_{v₂}) ∘ A_{v₂}) = tr Π_{v₂,1}(φ_1) = Θ(γ₀) vol(Z(F_{v₂}) \ Z(F_{v₂}) Ω) ≠ 0`.
   - At an infinite place `u`, which splits in `E`: choose
     `ψ_0 ∈ C_c^∞(G(F_u), ω_u)` with `Π_{u,1}(ψ_0) ≠ 0`, put `ψ_0^∨(g) := conj(ψ_0(g^{-1}))`,
     again in `C_c^∞(G(F_u), ω_u)` as `ω_u` is unitary, and `ψ := ψ_0^∨ * ψ_0`. Take
     `φ_u := ψ ⊗ ⋯ ⊗ ψ` and `f_u := φ_u^*`, related by convolution as in Arthur–Clozel I §5
     (printed p. 48). Since `Π_{u,1}` is unitary, `Π_{u,1}(ψ) = Π_{u,1}(ψ_0)^* Π_{u,1}(ψ_0)` is a
     nonzero positive operator, so `tr (Π_u(φ_u) ∘ A_u) = tr (Π_{u,1}(ψ)^l) > 0`.
   - At the other finite places `u` of `Σ`: the factor `Π_{u,1}` is irreducible, admissible
     and `τ_u`-stable, since `σ^{m_u}` fixes the place above `u` and `Π ∘ σ ≅ Π`. Let `A_1` be
     a `τ_u`-intertwiner of it. The Library's `TwistedTraceRegularNonvanishingStatement`, for
     `E_{u,1}/F_u` and `τ_u` (untwisted if `E_{u,1} = F_u`), gives a compactly supported `φ'`
     on `G(E_{u,1})`, supported in the `τ_u`-regular set, with
     `tr (Π_{u,1}(φ') ∘ A_1) ≠ 0`. Regular transfer (Library `RegularTransferStatement`,
     Henniart–Lemaire I.2.5, Proposition, printed p. 18) gives a concordant `f'` supported in
     the regular set. Cut `f'` to `det⁻¹(N(E_u^×))`, where `N(E_u^×) = N(E_{u,1}^×)`; this keeps
     concordance, since the orbital integrals of `f'` vanish off the norms and `det` of a norm
     is a norm. Average the pair as at `v`, with `dz_u := (N_{E_{u,1}/F_u})_* dz_{u,1}`, a Haar
     measure on `Z₁u` because `E_{u,1}` is a field (where `E_{u,1} = F_u` this is the common
     measure of step 2). This gives `φ_1 ∈ C_c^∞(G(E_{u,1}), χ_{u,1})` and `f_u ∈ C_c^∞(G(F_u), ω_u)`, concordant, with
     `tr (Π_{u,1}(φ_1) ∘ A_1) = tr (Π_{u,1}(φ') ∘ A_1) ≠ 0`. If `m_u = 1`, put `φ_u := φ_1`. If
     `m_u ≥ 2`, put `φ_u := φ_1 ⊗ e_{K'} ⊗ ⋯ ⊗ e_{K'}` with `K'` as in step 2 and `φ_1` right
     invariant under `K'`. Then `φ_u^* = φ_1`, so `(φ_u, f_u)` concord in the sense of step 2,
     and `tr (Π_u(φ_u) ∘ A_u) = tr (Π_{u,1}(φ_1) ∘ A_1) ≠ 0`.
   - Outside `Σ`: spherical functions related by the base change homomorphism of Hecke
     algebras, equivariant under the center, equal to the unit elements of Henniart–Lemaire
     II.6.3–II.6.4 at almost all places. Where `m_u ≥ 2` they are compared through the reduction
     of step 2 (Arthur–Clozel I §5, printed pp. 49–50; Henniart–Lemaire I.3.2, printed p. 25),
     and for the field factor by the Library's `UnitFundamentalLemmaStatement` and
     `FundamentalLemmaStatement`.
4. **Comparison of the trace formulas.** By the simple trace formula and its twisted form
   (Arthur–Clozel Lemmas 2.4–2.5, printed pp. 13–15; Henniart–Lemaire II.6.3–II.6.4, printed
   pp. 119–121) the cuspidal traces on `L⁰_ω(G, F)` and `L⁰_χ(G, E)` are sums of (twisted)
   orbital integrals over regular elliptic classes. With the measures of step 3 these sums agree
   by concordance (Arthur–Clozel (4.6), printed pp. 43–44; Henniart–Lemaire II.6.6, printed
   pp. 122–123). This gives, with a constant `c₀ ≠ 0`,
   `Σ_{Π'} tr (Π'(φ_𝔸) ∘ I_σ) = c₀ Σ_{π'} tr π'(f_𝔸)`, over the `σ`-stable cuspidal `Π'` in
   `L⁰_χ(G, E)` and the cuspidal `π'` in `L⁰_ω(G, F)`. Here `I_σ` is taken in the forward
   convention of step 2, `I_σ ∘ Π'(g) = Π'(σ(g)) ∘ I_σ`; on automorphic forms it is
   `ϕ ↦ ϕ ∘ σ^{-1}`. For this operator the diagonal of the kernel of `R(φ_𝔸) ∘ I_σ` is
   `Σ_δ φ_𝔸(x^{-1} δ σ(x))` over `δ ∈ G(E)` modulo `Z(E)`, so the geometric side consists of the `σ`-twisted orbital integrals
   at `x^{-1} δ σ(x)` used in the concordance.
5. **Separation.** Varying the spherical functions outside `Σ` and separating by Hecke
   eigenvalues (Library `BoundedCharacterSeparationStatement`; Arthur–Clozel p. 55, "as in §4")
   keeps on the left only `Π' ≅ Π` (strong multiplicity one, Library
   `CuspidalSpectralRigidityStatement`) and on the right only the `π'` whose unramified base
   change agrees with `Π` outside `Σ`. Factoring the traces over `Σ` (Library
   `GeneralLinearCuspidalLocalComponentTraceStatement` and, at `v`,
   `WeilGeneralLinearCuspidalLocalTwistedTraceStatement`) and using step 2 at `v` gives
   `tr (Π_w(φ) ∘ A) · X = c₀' Σ_{π'} tr π'_v(f) · Y(π')` with
   `X := ∏_{u ∈ Σ, u ≠ v} tr (Π_u(φ_u) ∘ A_u)` and `Y(π') := ∏_{u ∈ Σ, u ≠ v} tr π'_u(f_u)`.
   Here `A_u` is the forward intertwiner `x_1 ⊗ ⋯ ⊗ x_{m_u} ↦ x_2 ⊗ ⋯ ⊗ x_{m_u} ⊗ A_1(x_1)` of
   step 2 at `u`, and `A` at `v` is also forward. On the spherical vectors outside `Σ` the
   forward intertwiners are normalized to fix them. Each local forward intertwiner is unique up
   to a scalar, since the local components are irreducible. So the forward `I_σ` on `Π`, read
   through the factorization of `Π`, is the tensor product of these local operators up to a
   nonzero scalar, which is absorbed in `c₀'` (compare Henniart–Lemaire II.6.5, printed
   pp. 121–122, for their normalized operators). By the choices of step 3, `X ≠ 0`.
6. **Existence of `π`.** `Π_w` is irreducible, admissible and `σv`-stable (`σ` fixes the only
   place `w` above `v` and `Π ∘ σ ≅ Π`), so it has a `σv`-intertwiner `A` and a test function
   `φ⁰` supported in the `σv`-regular set with `tr (Π_w(φ⁰) ∘ A) ≠ 0` (Library
   `TwistedTraceRegularNonvanishingStatement`); regular transfer gives a concordant `f⁰`
   supported in the regular set, cut to `det⁻¹(N(E_v^×))` as above. For this pair the left side
   is nonzero, so the right side is not empty: there is a cuspidal `π` in `L⁰_ω(G, F)` whose
   base change agrees with `Π` outside `Σ`, which is the weak base change condition. Its central
   character `ω_π` extends `ω` and is unitary: `|ω_π|` is a positive character of `C_F` that is
   trivial on the image of `Z₁`, which contains `x^l = N(x)` for every `x ∈ 𝔸_F^×`, so
   `|ω_π|^l = 1` and `|ω_π| = 1`. A presentation `D` of `π` in the universe `uV` with irreducible
   admissible local components exists (Library
   `CuspidalAllLocalIrreduciblePresentationExistenceStatement`, with the universe transport
   `CuspidalSpectralPresentation.exists_small` of module
   `Langlands.Automorphic.CuspidalPresentationUniverse`), and the spectral systems in the weak
   base change condition are taken in the universes `uV`, `uW`.
7. **The fibre and the determinant condition.** The `π'` on the right are the twists
   `π ⊗ (κ^i ∘ det)`, where `κ` is the idele class character of `F` with kernel
   `F^× N(𝔸_E^×)` (Library `CyclicCuspidalFiberStatement`; Arthur–Clozel III, Theorem 3.1,
   printed p. 201; Henniart–Lemaire II.6.12, printed pp. 128–129). The twist by `κ^i ∘ det` has
   central character `ω_π κ^{in}`, which agrees with `ω` on `Z₁` because `κ` is trivial on
   `N(𝔸_E^×)`; so all twists lie in `L⁰_ω(G, F)`, with the same prescribed central type. Every
   `f_u` (`u ∈ Σ` finite, including `f^ω` and `f` at `v`) vanishes off `det⁻¹(N(E_u^×))` and
   `κ_u` is trivial on the norms, so each twist has the same local traces as `π` at the finite
   places of `Σ`; at the split infinite places `κ_u` is trivial. So the right side is
   `c₀' m · tr π_v(f) · Y(π)` with `m ≥ 1` the number of distinct twists.
8. **The identity.** Hence `tr (Π_w(φ) ∘ A) = c · tr π_v(f)` with `c := c₀' m Y(π) / X` for all
   pairs `(φ, f)` at `v` as in the statement, first for one fixed pair of Haar measures at `v`
   (the components at `v` of the global measures) and then, by the scaling remark, for all Haar
   measures. Applied to `(φ⁰, f⁰)` it shows `c ≠ 0`. `PiV` is admissible because `Π_w` is and
   `h` is a homeomorphic isomorphism of finite-dimensional `F_v`-vector spaces; `π_v` is
   admissible by the choice of `D`.

Differences from the sources, none of which changes the content of the comparison:

- the global `σ`-stable `Π` is given (its construction is a separate globalization step);
- the place `v` with only one place `w` above it plays the role of `v₀` in both sources
  (Henniart–Lemaire II.6.1, printed p. 118);
- the auxiliary places are `v₁ ≠ v₂`, split in `E`, with supercuspidal components above both,
  so that Arthur–Clozel's supercuspidal coefficient and regular elliptic support conditions of
  Lemmas 2.4–2.5 can be imposed there. Henniart–Lemaire instead use level-zero test functions
  (II.2.13–II.2.16, printed pp. 74–78) at a split place `v₁` chosen in II.6.7 (printed p. 123)
  and reused in II.6.16 (printed p. 131), with a second split place `v₂` at which supports are
  restricted. Arthur–Clozel's proof of Theorem 6.2(b) also uses an inert place `v₃` with a
  Steinberg component to count the twists in the fibre; here that number only enters the
  constant;
- the degree is any `l`, including `l = 1`;
- the test functions at `v` are compactly supported rather than equivariant under the center,
  and are restricted to the regular sets and to determinants in the norm group;
- the constant multiplies the trace of `π_v` (equivalent since `c ≠ 0`), the intertwiner `A` is
  not normalized, and the measures are arbitrary Haar measures;
- the local components are the Library's `CuspidalSpectralPresentation.localComponent`, with

## Place in the descent argument

Henniart–Lemaire II.6.16–II.6.17 prove the descent of a `σ`-discrete series `Π₀` of `GL_n(E)`,
for a cyclic extension `E/F` of local fields of positive characteristic, globally. In II.6.1
(printed p. 118) they choose a cyclic extension of global function fields with a place `v₀` that
has only one place above it and whose completion is `E/F`. II.6.16 builds a `σ`-stable cuspidal
`Π'` with `Π'_{v₀} ≅ Π₀` from a twisted pseudo-coefficient at `v₀` and level-zero functions at
the split place `v₁`, and II.6.17 compares the trace formulas to obtain a cuspidal `τ'` and the
identity at `v₀`. Arthur–Clozel §6.3(b) runs the same argument over number fields. In
characteristic zero the local extension is globalized by a cyclic extension of number fields with
a place `v` above which the completion is `E/F` (the Library's
`CyclicFieldGlobalizationStatement`), and a `σ`-stable cuspidal `Π` with `Π_w ≅ ρ`, for the given
`σ`-discrete series `ρ`, and supercuspidal components above two split places is the input of this
statement, which gives the cuspidal `π` and the identity at `v`. Read through the completion
isomorphisms, that identity is `IsRegularSigmaLift` for `ρ` and `π_v`, the relation in the
Library's `Langlands.BaseChange.SigmaDiscreteRegularDescentStatement`.
