# Globalization of a cyclic extension of local fields

`Langlands.TraceFormula.cyclicFieldGlobalization` proves
`Langlands.TraceFormula.CyclicFieldGlobalizationStatement.{u, v}` with
independent universes for the two local fields.
[Statement](CyclicFieldGlobalizationStatement.md)

## Lean proof

The field hypothesis on `E` and `IsCyclicAlgebra.isGalois` give a finite Galois
extension `E/F`. The proof uses the residue prime, p-adic algebra structure and
module topology of `F` from `CharZeroPadicRealization`. The map from `Padic p`
to `E` is the composite through `F`. A finite basis over `F` embeds `E`
continuously in a finite product of copies of `F`, so `E` is Hausdorff.
Continuity of the composite scalar map and finite dimensionality over `Padic p`
then identify the given topology of `E` with its p-adic module topology.

`Langlands.Global.totallyRealGaloisRealization` gives totally real number fields
`L/K`, a unique prime `q₀` over `p₀`, the topological ring comparisons `φ` and
`η` with `F` and `E`, and a compatible Galois group isomorphism `θ`. The proof
sets `σK = θ.symm σ`. The degree of `L/K` and the order of `σK` both equal `l`,
so the subgroup generated by `σK` is the whole Galois group. The Galois
fixed-field theorem therefore gives the fixed-element clause of
`IsCyclicAlgebra σK l`.

At `p₀`, `completedTensorComparison` identifies the tensor algebra with the
product over primes above `p₀`. Evaluation at the unique prime identifies this
product with `L_q₀`, and composition with `η` gives `ψ`. The base scalar
comparison holds on the dense image of `K` in `K_p₀` and extends by
continuity. The supplied action comparison on `L`, together with the base
scalar comparison, proves equivariance on simple tensors and hence on the
whole tensor product. The comparison with `L_q₀` is a linear equivalence
over `K_p₀` between modules with the module topology. Both directions are
continuous, so composing with `η` gives both continuity clauses for `ψ`.

For a coefficient field `A`, a `K`-algebra embedding `f : L → A` gives the
coordinate maps `a ⊗ x ↦ a * f (σK ^ i x)` for `i : Fin l`. Each map is
surjective, and equality of two kernels would imply equality of the two powers
of `σK` by evaluating `1 ⊗ x` and subtracting a scalar. Distinct maximal
kernels are pairwise coprime, so the Chinese remainder theorem gives
surjectivity onto the product. Both sides have dimension `l` over `A`, giving
an algebra isomorphism. Acting by `id ⊗ σK` increases the coordinate exponent
by one modulo `l`, giving precomposition by `finRotate l`. At an infinite
place of `K`, total reality gives an upstairs completion of degree one.
Evaluating `infiniteTensorMap` there gives the embedding into the base
completion.

For each finite-place choice the proof adjoins all primes of norm at most
`N` to the excluded set and applies
`Langlands.Global.chebotarevPrimeRealizationStatement`. At a prime with
identity arithmetic Frobenius, the residue-field Frobenius is the identity.
Its order gives residue degree one, and unramifiedness gives ramification
index one. The Library's `localDegree_eq` gives an upstairs completion of
degree one, so evaluation of `finiteTensorMap` provides an embedding for the
same cyclic coordinates. At a prime whose Frobenius is `σK`, the upstairs
prime is fixed by `σK` and thus by the whole Galois group. Transitivity on
primes above the base prime shows that every factor of the extended ideal is
that upstairs prime. The ramification-index count and unramifiedness give
multiplicity one, so the extended ideal itself is prime.

## Relation to the sources

Arthur and Clozel choose the local realization, two split finite places and
an inert place in Chapter I §6.3, printed pp. 54–55 (PDF pp. 68–69). They also
assume that every infinite place splits, and use the inert place to
distinguish twists in the discrete case. The completed tensor comparison
realizes their selected local extension, and successive finite exclusions
give distinct auxiliary places. Registered source
`arthur-clozel-1989-base-change`.
[Arthur–Clozel, §6.3](https://www.math.toronto.edu/arthur/pdf/30.pdf)

In the function-field setting, Henniart and Lemaire identify the topological
completion and the corresponding Galois generator in §II.6.1, printed
p. 118 (PDF p. 126). In §II.6.7, printed p. 123 (PDF p. 131), they choose a
split place with residue cardinality at least `3`. The formal theorem gives
the analogous equivariant completion for number fields and supplies the split
norm bound by taking `N = 3`. The argument is a derived composition of the
p-adic, completion and Chebotarev results above. Registered source
`provided-a0a5445aba86b871bcd83f08813de3234fddb463ff9bb3c9dfd60d2d2fcdae61`.
[Henniart–Lemaire, §§II.6.1 and II.6.7](https://www.numdam.org/item/10.24033/msmf.436.pdf)
