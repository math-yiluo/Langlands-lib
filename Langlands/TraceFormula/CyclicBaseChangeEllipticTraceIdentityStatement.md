# The elliptic twisted trace identity at a non-split place for a cuspidal weak base change (statement)

Module `Langlands.TraceFormula.CyclicBaseChangeEllipticTraceIdentityStatement`, namespace
`Langlands.TraceFormula`. It contains one proposition definition and no proof:

- `Langlands.TraceFormula.CyclicBaseChangeEllipticTraceIdentityStatement.{uF, uE, uV, uW}`.

In words: let `E/F` be a cyclic extension of number fields of prime degree `l`, unramified at the
infinite places. Let `π` be a cuspidal automorphic representation of `GL_n(𝔸_F)` (`n = r + 1`)
with unitary central character, and `Π` one of `GL_n(𝔸_E)` that is a weak base change of `π`.
Let `v ≠ v'` be finite places of `F`, each with only one place of `E` above it (`w` and `w'`),
and assume that the local components `Π_w` and `Π_{w'}` are supercuspidal. Then `Π_w` has a
`σ`-intertwiner `A`, and there is a constant `c ≠ 0` with

`tr (Π_w(φ) ∘ A) = c · tr π_v(f)`

for all concordant (matching) test functions `φ` on `GL_n(F_v ⊗_F E)` and `f` on `GL_n(F_v)`
such that `φ` is supported in the `σ`-elliptic set and `f` in the regular elliptic set, with
`det g` a norm wherever `f g ≠ 0`. The constant does not depend on the Haar measures or on the
test functions.

Identities of this form at a non-split place are obtained from the simple trace formula and its
twisted form in Arthur–Clozel 1989, Chapter 1, §6.3 (printed p. 57, at the place `v₃`) and
Henniart–Lemaire 2011, II.6.13 (printed pp. 129–130, at the place `v₀`), for global pairs
constructed there. For a cuspidal `Π` that is a weak base change of a cuspidal `π`,
Arthur–Clozel 1989, Chapter 3, obtain such an identity at every place in the proof of
Theorem 5.1 (printed p. 212), from the comparison of the full trace formulas. Here the pair
`(π, Π)` is given, with the hypotheses of
`Langlands.Automorphic.Unramified.RestrictedCyclicWeakBaseChangeSupercuspidalStatement` (see
"Use"). The statement is derived content, formulated for this Library (see "Provenance").

The module declares five `local instance`s of `AddCommGroup`: on the Library's form spaces
`Langlands.Automorphic.RationalInvariantFunctions`, `ContinuousRationalFunctions`,
`FiniteSmoothRationalFunctions` and `AutomorphicForms`, and on the carrier `A.Space` of a
`LocalRealization`. They are `Submodule.addCommGroup _`, as in the Library's
`Langlands.Automorphic.SupercuspidalComponent`. They are not exported.

## Statement

For all `F`, `E`, `σ`, `l`, `v`, `w`, `v'`, `w'`, `r`, `S`, `T`, `P` as above, assume:

1. `l` is prime and `IsCyclicAlgebra σ l`;
2. `w` is the only place of `E` above `v`; `v' ≠ v`, and `w'` is the only place of `E` above
   `v'`;
3. `S` has unitary central character;
4. `P.localComponent w.1` and `P.localComponent w'.1` are supercuspidal;
5. the class of `T` is a weak base change of the class of `S`.

Then for every `D : CuspidalSpectralPresentation.{uF, uV} S` whose local component
`π_v := D.localComponent v` is irreducible and admissible, there are
`hπ : Langlands.IsAdmissible π_v.representation`, `hP : Langlands.IsAdmissible PiV`,
`A : SigmaIntertwiner σv (r + 1) PiV` and `c : ℂ` with `c ≠ 0` such that, for every Haar measure
`μF` on `GL_{r+1}(F_v)` and `μE` on `GL_{r+1}(E_v)` and all
`φ : SchwartzBruhat (GL_{r+1}(E_v))` and `f : SchwartzBruhat (GL_{r+1}(F_v))` with

- `IsSigmaEllipticSupported σv l φ`,
- `IsEllipticSupported f`,
- `GeneralLinear.determinant g ∈ N(E_v^×)` for every `g` with `f g ≠ 0`,
- `AreConcordant σv l μF μE φ f`,

we have

`twistedDistribution μE PiV hP A φ = c * Character.distribution μF π_v.representation hπ f`.

Remarks on the form.

- `c` is chosen before the measures and the test functions. For the measures this is no
  restriction: replacing `μE` by `a • μE` (`a > 0`) turns `AreConcordant σv l μF μE φ f` into the
  same condition for `a • φ` and multiplies the twisted trace by `a`; similarly for `μF` and `f`.
  So the identity for one pair of Haar measures gives it for all pairs with the same `c`.
- The three conditions on the supports (`φ` in the `σv`-elliptic set, `f` in the regular
  elliptic set, `det g` a norm wherever `f g ≠ 0`) restrict the class of test functions. The
  identity sketched below holds for all concordant pairs at `v`; the statement keeps the class
  on which it is used (see "Use"). With the determinant condition, every twist
  `π_v ⊗ (χ ∘ det)` by a character `χ` of `F_v^×` trivial on `N(E_v^×)` has the same trace as
  `π_v` on `f`, since `χ(det g) = 1` wherever `f g ≠ 0`. Henniart–Lemaire II.6.13 (printed
  p. 129) obtain `tr ((χ ρ)(f)) = tr ρ(f)`, for every smooth admissible `ρ` and every such `χ`,
  from the vanishing of the orbital integrals of `f` at regular non-norms alone, by the Weyl
  integration formula.
- `σv`-ellipticity (`IsSigmaElliptic`) asks that the norm `N(δ) = δ σv(δ) ⋯ σv^{l-1}(δ)` be
  conjugate in `GL_n(E_v)` to a regular elliptic element of `GL_n(F_v)`. This is weaker than
  asking that `N(δ)` be regular elliptic as an element of `GL_n(E_v) ≅ GL_n(E_w)`, that is, that
  its characteristic polynomial be irreducible over `E_w`. For `l = n = 2` and `a ∈ E_v^×` not
  in `F_v`, the element `δ = [[0, a], [1, 0]]` has `N(δ) = diag(a, σv(a))`, which is split over
  `E_v`, while `N(δ)` is conjugate to the matrix `γ ∈ GL_2(F_v)` of multiplication by `a` on the
  `F_v`-vector space `E_v`, whose characteristic polynomial is irreducible and separable over
  `F_v`. The support hypothesis at a finite place `w` of the upper field in the Library's
  `Langlands.TraceFormula.GeneralLinearSimpleTwistedTraceFormulaStatement` is of the second,
  stronger kind; the argument below does not use that formula.
- The quantifier over `D` covers every presentation whose component at `v` is irreducible and
  admissible. The irreducible local components at `v` of two presentations of `S` are equivalent
  (the Library's `Langlands.Automorphic.CuspidalSpectralPresentation.nonempty_localComponent_equiv`),
  and given one presentation there is one with all local components irreducible and admissible
  (the Library's `Langlands.Automorphic.cuspidalAllLocalIrreduciblePresentationExistence`).
- The statement does not say that `Π_w` is a `σ`-lift of `π_v` (an identity for all concordant
  pairs; Arthur–Clozel, Chapter 3, Theorem 5.1, prove it with the normalized intertwining
  operator), nor that `π_v` is supercuspidal (see "Use").

## Why the statement is true

Write `π`, `Π` for the representations of `S`, `T`, `n = r + 1`, `G = GL_n`, and `η` for the
character of `𝔸_F^×` trivial exactly on `F^× N_{E/F}(𝔸_E^×)`. The argument is that of
Arthur–Clozel 1989, Chapter 3, proof of Theorem 5.1 (printed p. 212), which compares the trace
formula of `G(𝔸_F)` with the twisted trace formula of `G(𝔸_E) ⋊ ⟨σ⟩` for all associated test
functions, without the simplifying conditions of the simple trace formulas. It gives the
identity for all concordant pairs at `v` and does not use the place `v'`, the supercuspidality
of `Π_w` and `Π_{w'}` or the conditions on the supports of `φ` and `f`; the statement keeps
them because it is formulated with the hypotheses and the test class of its use (see "Use").

1. **The setting of Arthur–Clozel.** The central character of `π` is unitary and its cusp
   forms are rapidly decreasing, so they are square-integrable modulo the center and `π` is a
   unitary cuspidal representation. The central character of `Π` agrees with `ω_π ∘ N_{E/F}` at
   almost all places, hence everywhere (Arthur–Clozel, Chapter 3, Proposition 4.4(ii), printed
   p. 210), so `Π` is unitary cuspidal as well. The Library's weak
   base change relation is Arthur–Clozel's relation (1.1) between the Satake parameters at
   almost all places, so `Π` is a weak base change lift of `π` (Chapter 3, Definition 1.1,
   printed p. 199). Moreover `π ≇ π ⊗ (η ∘ det)`: otherwise, by Theorem 4.2(b) (printed
   p. 202), the only lift of `π` would be `Π₁ × Π₁^σ × ⋯ × Π₁^{σ^{l-1}}` for a cuspidal `Π₁` of
   `GL_{n/l}(𝔸_E)`, which is induced from a proper parabolic subgroup, and a cuspidal
   representation with the same Satake parameters almost everywhere would contradict
   Jacquet–Shalika's result (2.4) (printed p. 200) on cuspidal data. So `π` falls under part
   (a) of Theorem 4.2 and the first case of the proof of Theorem 5.1.
2. **`σ`-stability, the intertwiner and admissibility.** `Π ∘ σ` is cuspidal with the same
   Satake parameters as `Π` at almost all places, so `Π ∘ σ ≅ Π` (strong multiplicity one; by
   Theorem 4.2(a), `Π` is the unique `σ`-stable lift of `π`). As `σ` fixes the only place `w`
   above `v`, it extends to an automorphism `σ_w` of `E_w`, and `Π_w ∘ σ_w ≅ Π_w`. Such an
   isomorphism is unique up to a scalar by Schur's lemma; Arthur–Clozel normalize it by
   Whittaker functionals (`I_{σ,v}`; Chapter 1, §2.1, printed p. 10). The map `h` is an
   isomorphism of finite-dimensional `F_v`-algebras `E_v ≅ E_w`, hence a homeomorphism, and
   `h ∘ σv = σ_w ∘ h`. So `PiV` is admissible because `Π_w` is, and `I_{σ,v}`, read through
   `h`, is a `σv`-intertwiner of `PiV`. `π_v` is admissible by hypothesis.
3. **The comparison and the local identity.** For functions `f` on `G(𝔸_F)` and `φ` on
   `G(𝔸_E)` that are associated at every place, Arthur–Clozel obtain at the end of Chapter 2 an
   identity between the discrete parts of the trace formula for `G(𝔸_F)` and of the twisted
   trace formula for `G(𝔸_E) ⋊ σ` ((17.8), printed p. 198, restated in Chapter 3 as the
   equality of (4.1) and (4.2), printed p. 203). In the proof of Theorem 5.1 (printed p. 212)
   they separate the representations by their Satake parameters at almost all places. By
   Chapter 3, Theorem 3.1 (printed p. 201), the cuspidal representations whose parameters are
   those of `π` up to base change are the `π ⊗ (η^i ∘ det)`. They obtain

   `Σ_{i=1}^{l} tr (π ⊗ (η^i ∘ det))(f) = l · tr (Π(φ) I_σ)`

   for associated `f` and `φ`. Since the orbital integrals of `f` vanish at the regular elements
   that are not norms, this is equivalent to `tr π(f) = tr (Π(φ) I_σ)`. They deduce from this
   global identity that at every place `v`

   `tr π_v(f_v) = c · tr (Π_v(φ_v) I_{σ,v})`, with `c` a nonzero constant,

   for all associated `f_v` on `G(F_v)` and `φ_v` on `G(E_v)`, where `I_{σ,v}` is the
   normalized intertwining operator between `Π_v` and `Π_v ∘ σ`. At our place `v`, `Π_v = Π_w`.
4. **Translation into the statement.** Arthur–Clozel's association of `φ_v` and `f_v`
   (Chapter 1, Proposition 3.1 and the definition after it, printed pp. 20 and 26) is the
   matching that `AreConcordant` expresses, for the Haar measures used there; through `h` it is
   the condition `AreConcordant σv l μF μE φ f`. Take for `A` the operator `I_{σ,v}` on the
   space of `PiV` (step 2). Every pair `(φ, f)` of the statement is concordant, so
   `twistedDistribution μE PiV hP A φ = c⁻¹ · Character.distribution μF π_v.representation hπ f`
   for Arthur–Clozel's measures; the other three conditions on `φ` and `f` only select a
   subclass of pairs. By the first remark on the form, the identity then holds for all Haar
   measures with the same constant `c⁻¹ ≠ 0`.

The test class of the statement is large enough to detect `Π_w`. `Π_w` is supercuspidal and
`σv`-stable, so its twisted trace is nonzero on some `φ` supported in the `σv`-elliptic set (the
Library's `Langlands.BaseChange.TwistedSupercuspidalEllipticNonvanishingStatement`, read through
`h`). Such a `φ` has a concordant `f` supported in the regular elliptic set (the Library's
`Langlands.BaseChange.EllipticTransferStatement`, part (1)). Multiplying `f` by the indicator of
the open and closed subgroup `det⁻¹(N(E_v^×))` keeps concordance, because a regular elliptic
element is a norm exactly when its determinant is a norm (the Library's
`Langlands.BaseChange.EllipticNormCriterionStatement`, Arthur–Clozel, Chapter 1, Lemma 1.4,
printed p. 8). For this pair the left side of the identity is nonzero.

`Π` that is a weak base change of a cuspidal `π` with unitary central character), with places
`v` and `v'` as in its use; the test functions at `v` are restricted to the (`σ`-)elliptic sets,
with determinants in the norm group, while Arthur–Clozel's identity holds for all associated
pairs; the constant multiplies the ordinary trace (equivalent to the sources' form since
`c ≠ 0`); the intertwiner `A` is not normalized; the measures are arbitrary Haar measures; the
infinite places of `F` are assumed unramified in `E`; and the local components are the
Library's `CuspidalSpectralPresentation.localComponent`, with `Π_w` read on `GL_n(F_v ⊗_F E)`
through `finiteTensorMap v`.

## Use

The statement has the hypotheses of
`Langlands.Automorphic.Unramified.RestrictedCyclicWeakBaseChangeSupercuspidalStatement`, with the
generator `σ` and the prime degree `l` given explicitly (`IsCyclicAlgebra σ l`) instead of
`IsGalois F E` and a prime degree. That statement says that the class of `S` has a
supercuspidal component at `v`, and the identity is its global input. The local step, which this
module does not contain, runs as follows.

- By the Library's `Langlands.BaseChange.CuspidalSigmaLiftExistenceStatement` (the case of a
  `σ`-stable `Π₁`), the `σ`-stable supercuspidal `Π_w`, read through `h`, is a `σ`-lift of an
  irreducible supercuspidal representation `π⁰` of `GL_n(F_v)`: there is `c' ≠ 0` with
  `tr (Π_w(φ) ∘ A) = c' tr π⁰(f)` for all concordant pairs (the intertwiner of the `σ`-lift
  differs from `A` by a nonzero scalar, by Schur's lemma).
- Let `f` be supported in the regular elliptic set with determinants in `N(E_v^×)`. Every `g`
  with `f g ≠ 0` is then a norm (the Library's
  `Langlands.BaseChange.EllipticNormCriterionStatement`), so the orbital integrals of `f` vanish
  at the regular non-norms, and part (2) of the Library's
  `Langlands.BaseChange.EllipticTransferStatement` gives a concordant `φ` supported in the
  `σv`-elliptic set. With the identity of this module, `tr π_v(f) = (c'/c) tr π⁰(f)` for all such `f`, and
  `c'/c ≠ 0`.
- The Library's `Langlands.BaseChange.EllipticTraceRigidityStatement` concerns an irreducible
  admissible `π`, an irreducible supercuspidal `π₀`, an open subgroup `H` of finite index of
  `F_v^×` and a nonzero constant. Applied with `π = π_v`, `π₀ = π⁰` and `H = N(E_v^×)` (open, of
  index `l`, by local class field theory), it gives `π_v ≅ π⁰ ⊗ (χ ∘ det)` for a character `χ`
  of `F_v^×` trivial on `N(E_v^×)`. A twist of a supercuspidal representation by a character of
  the determinant is supercuspidal, so `π_v` is supercuspidal.

A rigidity statement of this kind needs the supercuspidality of `π⁰`: the trivial and the
Steinberg representations of `GL_2(F_v)` are not twists of each other, while their characters
are negatives of each other on the regular elliptic set. This module contains neither the local
step nor the statements it uses.
