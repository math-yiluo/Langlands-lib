# The simple trace formula for `GL_n` over a number field (statement)

Module `Langlands.TraceFormula.GeneralLinearSimpleTraceFormulaStatement` contains one
proposition definition and no proof:

- `Langlands.TraceFormula.GeneralLinearSimpleTraceFormulaStatement.{u}`.

It states the simple trace formula, which Getz–Hahn attribute to Deligne and Kazhdan, for
`G = GL_n` over a number field `F`. The test function is a convolution `f = f₁ * f₂` on `G(𝔸_F)`. The factor `f₂` integrates to
zero along the unipotent radicals of the proper rational parabolics, and the support of `f` is
regular elliptic at one finite place `v`. Then `R(f)` is trace class on `L²([G])`, and its trace
is a finite sum of volumes times elliptic orbital integrals. The automorphic space, the
operators, the measures and the orbital integrals are the Library's group-scheme objects.

The form for finite sums `f = ∑ⱼ aⱼ ⊗ bⱼ` of tensor test functions is
`Langlands.TraceFormula.GeneralLinearTensorSimpleTraceFormulaStatement` (with
`GeneralLinearTensorSimpleTracePiecesStatement`, module
`Langlands.TraceFormula.GeneralLinearTensorSimpleTraceFormulaStatement`). Both are proved from this
statement and `GeneralLinearRadicalIntegralVanishingStatement` in
`Langlands.TraceFormula.GeneralLinearTensorSimpleTraceFormulaReduction`
(`generalLinearTensorSimpleTraceFormula_of_simpleTraceFormula`,
`generalLinearTensorSimpleTracePieces_of_simpleTraceFormula`).

## Statement

The proposition quantifies over all the parameters above, with `F : Type u`. Let
`μG = Measure.haar` on `G(𝔸_F)`. For all `f₁, f₂ : C_c(G(𝔸_F), ℂ)`, assume:

1. `R_cusp(f₁)` and `R_cusp(f₂)` are Hilbert–Schmidt, for every integrability proof (the
   operators do not depend on the proof).
2. For every proper rational parabolic `P` and all `x, y ∈ G(𝔸_F)`,
   `∫_{N_P(𝔸_F)} f₂(x m y) d(properParabolicHaarMeasure P)(m) = 0`.

Let `f(g) = ∫ f₁(a) f₂(a⁻¹ g) dμG(a)` be the Haar convolution `f₁ * f₂`. Let `v` be a finite
place of `F`. Assume:

3. For every `g` in the closed support `tsupport f`, the component of `g` at `v` is regular
   elliptic in `GL_n(F_v)`.

Define `fOne : G(𝔸_F)¹ → ℂ` by `fOne(x) = ∫_{A⁺} f(a x) da`, for the normalized Haar measure
`da` above. The conclusion is that there is `h : Integrable f μG` such that:

- `R(f) = haarQuotientL2IntegratedAction … μG f h` is trace class on `L²([G])`;
- there are `m : ℕ` and `r : Fin m → Γ` such that
  - the `r j` are pairwise not conjugate under `Γ`;
  - every `r j` is regular elliptic at `v`;
  - every `q ∈ Γ` with `fOne(x⁻¹ q x) ≠ 0` for some `x ∈ G(𝔸_F)¹` is `Γ`-conjugate to some
    `r j`;
  - and for all choices of the data below, the identity below holds.

The data are, for each `j`, with `C_j` the centralizer of `r j` in `G(𝔸_F)¹`:

- Borel measurable structures on `C_j \ G(𝔸_F)¹` and on `(Γ ∩ C_j) \ C_j`;
- a right-invariant measure `μC j` on `C_j`, finite on compacts and positive on nonempty open
  sets;
- a measure `νC j` on `C_j \ G(𝔸_F)¹` compatible with Haar measure on `G(𝔸_F)¹` and `μC j`;
- a measure `νΓC j` on `(Γ ∩ C_j) \ C_j` compatible with `μC j` and counting measure on
  `Γ ∩ C_j`.

For these data:

- every volume `νΓC j (univ)` is finite;
- every orbit function `C_j x ↦ fOne(x⁻¹ (r j) x)` is integrable for `νC j`;
- `operatorTrace R(f) = ∑_j vol((Γ ∩ C_j) \ C_j) · ∫_{C_j \ G(𝔸_F)¹} fOne(x⁻¹ (r j) x) dνC j(x)`.

Here `vol((Γ ∩ C_j) \ C_j)` is `(νΓC j univ).toReal`. The covering condition above asks for a
representative only when `fOne` has a nonzero value on the `G(𝔸_F)¹`-orbit of `q`, not when the
orbit meets the closed support of `fOne`. A class can meet the closed support while `fOne`
vanishes on its whole orbit; such a class has orbital integral zero. The family may also contain
members whose orbit function vanishes identically; they contribute zero. Rescaling `μC j` by
`c > 0` rescales `νΓC j` by `c` and `νC j` by `1/c`, so each summand does not depend on the
choice.

If `n = 0`, no element of `GL_0(F_v)` is regular elliptic: the characteristic polynomial is
`1`, a unit. Hypothesis 3 then forces `f = 0`. The conclusion holds with `m = 0`.

## What is derived, and how it differs from the source

This statement is derived content. Its hypotheses are the form of Getz–Hahn's hypotheses
used in this project.

- **Convolution and continuity.** The source takes `f` smooth. Here `f = f₁ * f₂` with `f₁, f₂`
  only continuous and compactly supported. Smooth functions are finite sums of such
  convolutions (Dixmier–Malliavin, used in the proof of Getz–Hahn Theorem 9.1.2). Both sides of
  the identity are linear in `f`, so finite sums can be handled by a consumer; for finite sums of
  tensor test functions this is `GeneralLinearTensorSimpleTraceFormulaStatement`, proved from this
  statement (above).
- **Hilbert–Schmidt hypothesis.** The source proves that `R_cusp` of a smooth function is
  Hilbert–Schmidt (Theorem 9.1.2). This statement assumes it for the two factors `f₁, f₂`
  only. That is weaker than the hypothesis of the Library's
  `GroupSchemeFullAEL2CuspidalTraceClassStatement`, which asks it for every compactly supported
  continuous function. The statement does not claim that the hypothesis holds.
- **Cuspidal image.** The source assumes that `R(f)` has cuspidal image. Here the assumption is
  hypothesis 2 on `f₂`: its integrals along all two-sided translates of the adelic unipotent
  radicals vanish. This implies that `R(f₂)`, and hence `R(f)`, has cuspidal image
  (`GroupSchemeCuspidalImageStatement`). `GeneralLinearRadicalIntegralVanishingStatement`
  derives hypothesis 2 from a two-block condition at one finite place, as in
  Getz–Hahn Lemma 16.4.2.
- **Ellipticity.** The source assumes that every rational element conjugate into the support is
  elliptic and semisimple. Here the support of `f` is regular elliptic at a finite place `v`. A
  rational element that is conjugate into the support has irreducible separable characteristic polynomial over `F_v`, hence over `F`. So it
  is regular elliptic in `GL_n(F)`, and its centralizer is `E^×` for the field `E = F[γ]` of
  degree `n`.
- **Form of the geometric side.** The source sums over all elliptic semisimple classes. Here
  the sum is over a finite family `r j` of pairwise non-conjugate rational points. Every
  `q ∈ Γ` with `fOne(x⁻¹ q x) ≠ 0` for some `x ∈ G(𝔸_F)¹` is `Γ`-conjugate to some `r j`.
  A class not covered has `fOne` identically zero on its orbit, so its orbital integral is
  zero. The finiteness of the volumes and the integrability of the orbit functions are part of
  the conclusion. The identity is stated for every compatible choice of measures, instead of one
  normalized choice.

## Why it is true

1. `f` is continuous with compact support, hence integrable. `R(f) = R(f₁) R(f₂)` by Fubini and
   left invariance of `μG`. By hypothesis 2, `R(f₂)` maps `L²([G])` into the cuspidal subspace
   (`GroupSchemeCuspidalImageStatement`), and so does `R(f₂)*`, since the adjoint weight `g ↦ conj f₂(g⁻¹)` also satisfies
   hypothesis 2. So `R(f₂) = ι R_cusp(f₂) P`, with `ι` the inclusion and `P` the orthogonal
   projection. Hence `R(f) = ι R_cusp(f₁) R_cusp(f₂) P` is a product of two Hilbert–Schmidt
   operators, and so trace class (this is `GroupSchemeFullL2TraceClassStatement`).
2. Using `G(𝔸_F) = A⁺ × G(𝔸_F)¹` with the product measure and the Weil formula for `Γ`, the
   operator `R(f)` on `L²(Γ \ G(𝔸_F)¹)` has kernel `K(x, y) = ∑_{γ ∈ Γ} fOne(x⁻¹ γ y)`. The
   sum is locally finite, so `K` is continuous. For a trace-class integral operator with
   continuous kernel, the trace is `∫ K(x, x) dx` (Brislawn 1991, registered
   `brislawn-1991-traceable-integral-kernels`; Library statement
   `TraceClassContinuousKernelDiagonalStatement`).
3. The finite components of points of `A⁺` are `1`. So if `fOne(x⁻¹ q x) ≠ 0`, then some
   `a x⁻¹ q x` lies in the support of `f`. Its component at `v` is conjugate to `q`, so `q` is
   regular elliptic at `v`. The support of `fOne` is compact, and its points are regular
   elliptic at `v`. The Library statement `GeneralLinearEllipticClassFinitenessStatement`
   says that only finitely many `GL_n(F)`-classes of rational elements have a
   `GL_n(𝔸_F)`-conjugate in such a compact set. This gives the finite family `r`.
4. Unfolding: `∫_{Γ\G(𝔸_F)¹} ∑_γ fOne(x⁻¹ γ x) dx = ∑_j ∫_{(Γ∩C_j)\G(𝔸_F)¹} fOne(x⁻¹ (r j) x) dx`.
   Each term equals `vol((Γ ∩ C_j) \ C_j) · O_{r j}(fOne)` by the two compatibility
   conditions. The volume is finite because `C_j ≅ 𝔸_E^1` and `E^× \ 𝔸_E^1` is compact.
   Exchanging sum and integral is justified by the same computation applied to `|fOne|`.
   The orbit function of `r j` is continuous, since `fOne` is. It has compact support because
   the orbit map `C_j \ G(𝔸_F)¹ → G(𝔸_F)¹`, `C_j x ↦ x⁻¹ (r j) x`, is proper, so the preimage of
   the compact support of `fOne` is compact. Properness: Getz–Hahn Theorem 17.7.1 (printed
   p. 455) says that `H_γ(𝔸_F) \ H(𝔸_F) → X(𝔸_F)` is proper when `γ` is relatively semisimple
   and its stabilizer `H_γ` is smooth. Take `H = G` acting on `X = G` by conjugation. Then
   `H_γ = C_γ`, and relatively semisimple means semisimple, as in the proof of Corollary
   18.4.1. The element `r j` is semisimple, since its characteristic polynomial is separable.
   Its centralizer is the smooth group `Res_{E/F} 𝔾_m`. For `γ = r j`, the isomorphism (18.28)
   `C_γ(𝔸_F) \ G(𝔸_F) ≅ (A_G \ C_γ(𝔸_F)) \ (A_G \ G(𝔸_F))`, together with
   `A_G \ G(𝔸_F) ≅ G(𝔸_F)¹`, identifies the source's domain with `C_j \ G(𝔸_F)¹`, compatibly
   with the orbit maps. The image lies in the closed subgroup
   `G(𝔸_F)¹`, so the corestricted map is still proper. A compactly supported continuous
   function is integrable for the quotient measure `νC j`, which is finite on compact sets.

## What this module does not claim

It does not claim the Hilbert–Schmidt hypotheses. It does not identify `tr R(f)` with a sum
over automorphic representations. It gives no information when the support of `f` is not
regular elliptic at a finite place, or when hypothesis 2 fails. It does not state the twisted
trace formula. It does not compute local orbital integrals and fixes no normalization of the
centralizer measures beyond compatibility.
