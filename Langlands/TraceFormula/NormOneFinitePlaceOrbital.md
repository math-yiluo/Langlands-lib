# A finite-place factor of the norm-one orbital integral

`NormOneFinitePlaceOrbitalStatement` uses the actual norm-one group of an
affine group scheme locally of finite type over a number field. The
supplied central split torus satisfies the character restriction
hypotheses for the norm-one decomposition. The ambient norm-one Haar
measure is right invariant, and the automorphism fixes the torus
morphism. The local group and the local and global orbital quotients
have their Borel measurable structures. Assume the global twisted
centralizer is commutative. The local ambient Haar and a compatible
finite-place quotient measure are supplied, with a right-invariant
local centralizer measure finite on compact sets and positive on
open sets.

The theorem constructs a global centralizer measure and a compatible
quotient measure with the prescribed norm-one ambient Haar. For an
adelic tensor whose exterior function is invariant under finite-place
insertion, the orbital integral of the positive-torus average is the
local orbital integral times an exterior orbital integral. The same
global and exterior quotient measures work for every pair of tensor
factors. The exterior measure is nonzero, regular and right invariant
on its quotient with the Borel measurable structure. The orbital
integrals use the total Bochner integral, which is zero for
nonintegrable orbit functions.

The proof uses the equivariant splitting into the finite-place group
and its projection kernel. Haar uniqueness puts the scalar for the
given ambient normalization in the measure on the kernel. The
exterior twisted centralizer embeds in the given global centralizer,
so its Haar measure is two-sided. The Weil formula gives a compatible
exterior quotient. The compatible product quotient then gives the
global measure with the exact normalization. The positive averaging
identity and the product orbital integral give the factorization.

This internal lemma implements the distinguished-place integration
step in the global comparison of Arthur–Clozel, Chapter 1, §4.
