# The character twist on the split-centre adelic matrix quotient

**Sources.** Clozel–Rajan, *Solvable base change*, arXiv:1806.02513v1, §4, PDF pp. 10–11, (4.1)–(4.2); Mœglin–Waldspurger, *Stabilisation de la formule des traces tordue*, vol. 2 (2016), X.5.1, printed pp. 1203–1204, the displayed definition of `ρ_disc(γ,ω)` and its two-sided covariance. This is the specialization of the latter action to the restriction of scalars of GL(n), with their twisting character set to the inverse of the character in Clozel–Rajan (1.1).

`adelicGLSplitRationalSubgroup E n` is generated by the rational matrices and the single diagonal positive scalar group (identity in the finite factor). The scalar group is central, so this is their product. `AdelicGLSplitQuotient` is the actual left quotient, with neither a choice of section nor a normality assumption. `adelicEquiv_adelicPositiveScalar` uses agreement on rational scalars and continuity at infinity, and the identity of the finite components, to show that arithmetic transport fixes every such scalar. Together with transport of rational points this gives `adelicEquiv_mem_splitRationalSubgroup`.

A finite-order Hecke character `χ` kills the rational determinant and the positive scalars. For the latter, every scalar has an `m`th root by the real logarithm, where `χ^m = 1` and `m > 0`. Thus `splitQuotientDeterminant` is a continuous function on the quotient; its complex values have norm one. `inverseDeterminantMultiplier` is the complex linear isometry given by multiplication by its inverse, defined on almost-everywhere classes.

Let `θ` be the actual adelic matrix transport and let `δ` be the rational twisting point with `δgδ⁻¹ = θ(g)`. Write `A F(x) = F(θ⁻¹(x))`, `M₋ F(x) = χ(det x)⁻¹ F(x)` and `R(g)F(x) = F(xg)`. At `δ`, the source's formula with `ω_MW = χ⁻¹` gives `K = A M₋`, not `M₋ A`. In the right coordinate `δg`, it gives `D(δg) = χ(det g)⁻¹ K R(g)`. These are `characterTwistingPointOperator` and `characterRightCosetOperator`, respectively. Their almost-everywhere definition uses a Borel quotient measure with the inverse quotient automorphism and the indicated right translations measure-preserving. This module does not assert existence or compare normalizations of the quotient measures. The implemented covariance `D(δg) = R(θ(g)) K` is proved by changing representatives under measure-preserving maps and using multiplicativity of the determinant character.

`characterTwistUnshiftedOperator` is instead `J = M₋ A`. The distinction matters when labeling the summands: if the realized cuspidal space `Π` has arithmetic conjugate equal to its character shift, `J` acts on `Π` while `K` acts on its shifted space. At the level of the global operators, conjugation by multiplication by `χ(det x)` takes `J` to `K`; it also transports ordinary right convolution to convolution with the right-coordinate factor `χ(det g)⁻¹`. Thus one should not apply that factor again to the matching test. The module defines these global operators and proves the indicated covariance, not the summand identifications, trace-class assertions, trace equality, or matching theorem.
