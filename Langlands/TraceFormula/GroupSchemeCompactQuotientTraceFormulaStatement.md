# The trace formula on a compact actual adelic quotient (statement)

Module `Langlands.TraceFormula.GroupSchemeCompactQuotientTraceFormulaStatement` contains one
proposition definition and no proof:

- `Langlands.TraceFormula.GroupSchemeCompactQuotientTraceFormulaStatement.{u}`.

It states the Selberg trace formula for compact quotient (Arthur, *An introduction to the trace
formula*, §1, (1.1)–(1.5)) on the Library's actual adelic quotient `[G] = A⁺ G(F) \ G(𝔸_F)`,
under the hypothesis that `[G]` is compact. For a convolution `f = f₁ * f₂` of compactly
supported continuous functions on `G(𝔸_F)`, the operator `R(f)` on `L²([G])` is trace class,
and its trace has two expansions:

- a **geometric expansion**, a finite sum over rational conjugacy classes of volumes times
  orbital integrals, as in Arthur (1.3)–(1.4);
- a **spectral expansion**, an absolutely convergent sum of the traces of `R(f)` on the members
  of any decomposition of `L²([G])` into irreducible closed invariant subspaces. Regrouped by
  isomorphism class, it is Arthur's `∑_π m(π, R) tr π(f)` of (1.3) and (1.5); the regrouping is
  explained below and is not part of the Lean statement.

The Lean statement equates each expansion with `tr R(f)`. Equating the two expansions with each
other gives Arthur's identity (1.3). For the unitary similitude group `G` over `ℚ` of Kottwitz
(1992), §1, attached to a division algebra with an involution of the second kind, and
`T = A_G`, where `[G]` is compact by a standard theorem not used here, it gives Kottwitz's
(4.1); the section on Kottwitz's (4.1) below explains his central character, his Tamagawa
coefficients and his smooth test functions. The identification of the Tamagawa coefficients
uses that the centralizers of rational elements of this `G` are connected; the section below
also shows how this fails for some other groups.

## Hypotheses

The proposition quantifies over `F : Type u` and all the data above, exactly as the parameter
list of `GroupSchemeCompactQuotientL2DecompositionStatement`, and assumes one more hypothesis:

- `CompactSpace (PositiveRationalQuotient F G T i)`: the quotient `[G]` is compact.

Compactness is a hypothesis; it is not derived from anisotropy. Write `μG = Measure.haar` on
`G(𝔸_F)`, `μQ` and `π` as above. For all `f₁ f₂ : C_c(G(𝔸_F), ℂ)` let

- `f(g) = ∫ f₁(a) f₂(a⁻¹ g) dμG(a)`, the Haar convolution `f₁ * f₂`;
- `fOne(x) = ∫_{A⁺} f(a x) da` for `x ∈ G(𝔸_F)¹`, for the normalized Haar measure `da` above.

There are no other hypotheses on `f₁` and `f₂`.

## Conclusion

There is `h : Integrable f μG` such that, with `R(f) = haarQuotientL2IntegratedAction … μG f h`:

1. **Trace class.** `R(f)` is trace class.
2. **Geometric expansion.** There are `m : ℕ` and `r : Fin m → Γ` such that
   - the `r j` are pairwise not conjugate under `Γ`;
   - every `q ∈ Γ` with `fOne(x⁻¹ q x) ≠ 0` for some `x ∈ G(𝔸_F)¹` is `Γ`-conjugate to some
     `r j`;
   - for all choices of the measure data below, the conclusions below hold.

   With `C_j` the centralizer of `r j` in `G(𝔸_F)¹`, the data are, for each `j`: Borel
   measurable structures on `C_j \ G(𝔸_F)¹` and on `(Γ ∩ C_j) \ C_j`; a right-invariant
   measure `μC j` on `C_j`, finite on compact sets and positive on nonempty open sets; a measure
   `νC j` on `C_j \ G(𝔸_F)¹` compatible with Haar measure on `G(𝔸_F)¹` and `μC j`; and a measure
   `νΓC j` on `(Γ ∩ C_j) \ C_j` compatible with `μC j` and counting measure on `Γ ∩ C_j`.
   For these data:
   - every volume `νΓC j (univ)` is finite;
   - every orbit function `C_j x ↦ fOne(x⁻¹ (r j) x)` is integrable for `νC j`;
   - `operatorTrace R(f) = ∑_j vol((Γ ∩ C_j) \ C_j) · ∫_{C_j \ G(𝔸_F)¹} fOne(x⁻¹ (r j) x) dνC j(x)`,
     where `vol((Γ ∩ C_j) \ C_j)` is `(νΓC j univ).toReal`.
3. **Spectral expansion.** Let `A` be a set of closed subspaces of `L²([G])` such that every
   `P ∈ A`
   - is nonzero and closed,
   - is invariant: `π(a) x ∈ P` for all `a ∈ G(𝔸_F)` and `x ∈ P`,
   - is topologically irreducible: every closed invariant subspace `Q ≤ P` is `0` or `P`,

   and such that the inclusions of the members of `A` form a Hilbert sum of `L²([G])`. These are
   the properties of the decomposition in `GroupSchemeCompactQuotientL2DecompositionStatement`.
   The statement also takes, as an instance argument, that every member of `A` is a complete
   space; this is equivalent to closedness in the complete space `L²([G])`, so it adds nothing
   and only makes the orthogonal projections available. For `P ∈ A` let
   `c_P = p_P ∘ R(f) ∘ ι_P : P → P` be the compression, with `ι_P` the inclusion and `p_P` the
   orthogonal projection. Then
   - every `c_P` is trace class;
   - the family `P ↦ ‖tr c_P‖` is summable;
   - the family `P ↦ tr c_P` has sum `operatorTrace R(f)`.

   Here `tr c_P` is `operatorTrace` on the Hilbert space `P`.

The geometric and spectral clauses are stated for every admissible choice of measures and for
every decomposition `A`. The statement does not assert that a decomposition exists: that is the
second conclusion of `GroupSchemeCompactQuotientL2DecompositionStatement`.

## Measure normalizations and their independence of choices

- The proposition uses fixed measures: `μG = Measure.haar` on `G(𝔸_F)`, which forms both the
  convolution `f = f₁ * f₂` and the operator `R(f)`; `Measure.haar` on `G(𝔸_F)¹`, which enters
  `μQ` (as the image of the quotient measure on `Γ \ G(𝔸_F)¹ ≅ [G]`) and the compatibility
  conditions of the orbital integrals; and the Haar measure `da` on `A⁺` normalized so that
  `μG = da × Measure.haar`, which forms `fOne`. Other choices rescale the identities as
  follows. Hold `Measure.haar` on `G(𝔸_F)¹` fixed and replace `μG` by `c μG` with `c > 0`;
  then `da` becomes `c da`. For a fixed function `f`, `R(f)` and `fOne` are multiplied by `c`.
  For fixed `f₁` and `f₂`, as in the proposition, the convolution `f` is multiplied by `c` as
  well, so `R(f)` and `fOne`, and both sides of every identity, are multiplied by `c²`.
- The measure on `C_j` is arbitrary. Rescaling `μC j` by `c > 0` rescales `νΓC j` by `c` and
  `νC j` by `1/c`, so each product `vol((Γ ∩ C_j) \ C_j) · O_{r j}(fOne)` does not depend on
  the choice. The two compatibility conditions are Arthur's convention that the invariant
  measure on `Γ_γ \ H` is the product of the measures on `H_γ \ H` and `Γ_γ \ H_γ`.
- The covering condition asks for a representative only for a class on whose
  `G(𝔸_F)¹`-orbit `fOne` takes a nonzero value. Every other class has orbital integral zero.
  The family may contain classes with vanishing orbit function; they contribute zero. So the
  finite sum is Arthur's sum over all conjugacy classes of `Γ`.
- On the spectral side, `tr c_P` does not depend on the Hilbert basis of `P` used by
  `operatorTrace`, since `c_P` is trace class, and the sum over `A` converges absolutely, so
  its value does not depend on an ordering of `A`.

## Correspondence with Arthur §1

J. Arthur, *An introduction to the trace formula*, in J. Arthur, D. Ellwood and R. Kottwitz
(eds.), *Harmonic Analysis, the Trace Formula, and Shimura Varieties*, Clay Math. Proc. 4
(2005), registered `arthur-ellwood-kottwitz-2005-harmonic-analysis-shimura`, §1, printed
pp. 7–9 (PDF pp. 19–21).

Arthur takes a locally compact unimodular group `H`, a discrete subgroup `Γ`, the right regular
representation `R` on `L²(Γ \ H)` and `f ∈ C_c(H)`. Here:

- `H = G(𝔸_F)¹` and `Γ` is the image of `G(F)`. The Library's `positiveQuotientHomeomorph`
  identifies `Γ \ G(𝔸_F)¹` with `[G]`, compatibly with right translation by `G(𝔸_F)¹`, and `μQ`
  is the image of the quotient measure on `Γ \ G(𝔸_F)¹`, so `L²([G])` is Arthur's
  `L²(Γ \ H)` and `Γ \ H` is compact. Arthur's test function on `H` is `fOne`. The Library
  statement `PositiveQuotientTraceComparisonStatement` (proved as
  `positiveQuotientTraceComparison`) shows that `R(f)` on `L²([G])` is trace class exactly
  when the operator of `fOne` on `L²(Γ \ G(𝔸_F)¹)` is, with the same trace.
- (1.1): `R(f)` is an integral operator with kernel `K(x, y) = ∑_{γ ∈ Γ} fOne(x⁻¹ γ y)`.
  Arthur notes that on a compact quotient `R(f)` is Hilbert–Schmidt and `R` decomposes
  discretely with finite multiplicities `m(π, R)`.
- (1.2): `tr R(f) = ∫_{Γ\H} K(x, x) dx`. Arthur asks for "many functions" (for Lie groups,
  smooth ones); here `f` is a convolution `f₁ * f₂`, for which `R(f) = R(f₁) R(f₂)` is a product
  of two Hilbert–Schmidt operators.
- The computation after (1.2) gives
  `tr R(f) = ∑_{γ ∈ {Γ}} vol(Γ_γ \ H_γ) ∫_{H_γ \ H} f(x⁻¹ γ x) dx`. This is clause 2:
  `H_γ = C_j`, `Γ_γ = Γ ∩ C_j`, the coefficient `a^H_Γ(γ) = vol(Γ_γ \ H_γ)` is
  `(νΓC j univ).toReal`, and the orbital integral (1.4), `f_H(γ) = ∫_{H_γ\H} f(x⁻¹ γ x) dx`, is
  `orbitalIntegral (r j) (νC j) fOne`.
- The spectral expansion, "restricting `R(f)` to the irreducible subspaces of `L²(Γ \ H)`", is
  clause 3. Since `P` is invariant and closed, `R(f)` maps `P` into itself and `c_P` is the
  restriction of `R(f)` to `P`: `c_P v = ∫ f(a) π(a) v dμG(a)` for `v ∈ P`, a Bochner integral
  with values in `P`. This is the operator `π_P(f)` of the subrepresentation `π_P` on `P`. The
  points of `A⁺` act trivially on `L²([G])` (they are central and lie in the subgroup defining
  `[G]`), and `μG = da × Measure.haar`, so `π_P(f) v = ∫ fOne(x) π(x) v dx` over `G(𝔸_F)¹` for
  `dx = Measure.haar`: `π_P(f)` is the operator of `fOne` on the restriction of `π_P` to
  `G(𝔸_F)¹`, and `tr c_P` is Arthur's (1.5), `f_H(π) = tr π(f)`.
- **Grouping by isomorphism class.** Call `P, Q ∈ A` equivalent when there is a linear
  isometric equivalence `P ≃ Q` intertwining `π`, the relation used in
  `GroupSchemeCompactQuotientL2DecompositionStatement`. Equivalent members have equal traces
  `tr c_P = tr c_Q`. By absolute convergence the sum over `A` may be regrouped by equivalence
  classes, giving `∑_{[π]} m_A(π) tr π(f)` with `m_A(π)` the number of members of `A`
  equivalent to `π`. The decomposition statement asserts that some decomposition has every
  `m_A(π)` finite. When the multiplicities are finite, `m_A(π)` is the multiplicity `m(π, R)`
  of `π` in `R`, independent of `A`; the result is Arthur's `∑_π a^H_Γ(π) f_H(π)` with
  `a^H_Γ(π) = m(π, R)`. The Lean statement does not classify the summands, and neither the
  regrouping nor the independence of `m_A(π)` from `A` is part of it.

## Correspondence with Kottwitz (4.1)

R. E. Kottwitz, *On the λ-adic representations associated to some simple Shimura varieties*,
Invent. Math. 108 (1992), 653–665, registered `kottwitz-1992-lambda-adic-simple-shimura`
(SHA-256 `749af5541a59187e6f9d1089dec9a0e591a2059173da739c7384ff3a7d0dd799`), §1, printed
p. 654 (PDF p. 2), and §4, printed p. 660 (PDF p. 8).

**Kottwitz's group.** Let `K₀` be a totally real number field, `K` a totally imaginary
quadratic extension of `K₀` (Kottwitz's `F₀` and `F`), `D` a division algebra with center `K`
of dimension `n²` over `K`, and `x ↦ x*` an involution of `D` inducing the nontrivial
automorphism of `K / K₀`. Kottwitz's `G` (§1, p. 654) is the group over `ℚ` with
`G(R) = {x ∈ D ⊗_ℚ R : x x* ∈ R^×}` for commutative `ℚ`-algebras `R`, with the similitude
character `c(x) = x x*`. It is connected reductive (see "Connected centralizers" below), and
Kottwitz notes on p. 660 that `G / A_G` is anisotropic over `ℚ`, `A_G` being the split
component of the center (here the scalars `𝔾_m`). For this `G` and a smooth function `f` on `G(𝔸)` whose support is compact
modulo `A_G(ℝ)⁰` and which transforms under `A_G(ℝ)⁰` by a character `χ` (the central
character of the coefficient system `ξ`), (4.1) is "the trace formula for `f`":

`∑_γ τ(G_γ) O_γ(f) = ∑_π m(π) tr π(f)`,

with `γ` over the conjugacy classes of `G(ℚ)`, `G_γ` the centralizer of `γ`, `τ(G_γ)` its
Tamagawa number, `O_γ(f)` the orbital integral over the `G(𝔸)`-conjugacy class of `γ`, and `π`
over the automorphic representations whose central character on `A_G(ℝ)⁰` is `χ⁻¹`.

- **The quotient.** Take `F = ℚ`, `T = A_G` and `i` the inclusion. Restriction of rational
  characters from a connected reductive group to `A_G` is injective with finite-index image,
  so the Library's hypotheses on `T` hold, and `A⁺ = A_G(ℝ)⁰`. Compactness of
  `[G] = A_G(ℝ)⁰ G(ℚ) \ G(𝔸)` for anisotropic `G / A_G` is a standard theorem (Borel–Harish-Chandra,
  Mostow–Tamagawa); this statement assumes compactness and does not use that theorem.
- **The central character.** For `χ = 1`, Kottwitz's `f` is a compactly supported function on
  `G(𝔸) / A⁺ ≅ G(𝔸)¹` and plays the role of `fOne`, and his `π` with multiplicities `m(π)`
  correspond to the irreducible subrepresentations of `L²([G])` with their multiplicities
  `m(π, R)`, as in Arthur.
  For general `χ`, the projection `G(𝔸) = A⁺ × G(𝔸)¹ → A⁺` extends `χ` to a character `χ̃` of
  `G(𝔸)` trivial on `G(𝔸)¹`, hence on `G(ℚ)`. Replacing `f` by `χ̃⁻¹ f` and `π` by `π ⊗ χ̃`
  leaves every `tr π(f)` and every `O_γ(f)` unchanged (the orbits of rational `γ` lie in
  `G(𝔸)¹`) and reduces (4.1) to `χ = 1`. So the central character is a normalization of the
  quotient, and the Lean statement treats `χ = 1`.
- **Connected centralizers.** Over an algebraic closure `ℚ̄`,
  `D ⊗_ℚ ℚ̄ = ∏_ι D ⊗_{K,ι} ℚ̄ ≅ ∏_ι M_n(ℚ̄)` over the embeddings `ι : K → ℚ̄`, and the
  involution, which induces the nontrivial automorphism of `K / K₀`, carries the factor of `ι`
  anti-isomorphically onto the factor of the conjugate embedding `ῑ`. Choose one embedding from
  each pair `{ι, ῑ}`, giving a set `Φ` of `[K₀ : ℚ]` embeddings. For `x ∈ G(ℚ̄)` with
  `c(x) = c` one has `x* = c x⁻¹`, so the `ῑ`-component of `x` is determined by `c` and the
  `ι`-component, and every choice of `c` and of the `ι`-components, `ι ∈ Φ`, occurs. Hence
  `x ↦ (c(x), (x_ι)_{ι ∈ Φ})` is an isomorphism `G_ℚ̄ ≅ 𝔾_m × ∏_{ι ∈ Φ} GL_n`. The centralizer in
  `GL_n` of any element `g` is the unit group of the algebra `{y ∈ M_n : y g = g y}`, a nonempty
  Zariski-open subset of a linear subspace of `M_n`, so it is connected. So the centralizer of
  every element of `G_ℚ̄`, a product of `𝔾_m` and such centralizers, is connected; in
  particular `G_γ` is connected for every `γ ∈ G(ℚ)`. Moreover `γ` is semisimple, because
  rational elements of `G` are semisimple when `G / A_G` is anisotropic over `ℚ`, so `G_γ` is
  connected reductive.
- **The centralizers in clause 2.** Let `γ ∈ G(ℚ)` represent a class, `C_γ` its centralizer in
  `G(𝔸)¹` as in clause 2, so that `Γ ∩ C_γ = G_γ(ℚ)`, and let `G_γ(𝔸)¹` be the norm-one
  subgroup of `G_γ(𝔸)`, the common kernel of the idelic norms `|λ|` of the rational characters
  `λ` of `G_γ`. Then `C_γ = G_γ(𝔸)¹`:
  - `C_γ = G_γ(𝔸) ∩ G(𝔸)¹`, and the rational characters of `G` restrict to rational characters
    of `G_γ`, so `G_γ(𝔸)¹ ⊆ C_γ`;
  - for a rational character `λ` of `G_γ`, `|λ|` is a continuous homomorphism `C_γ → ℝ_{>0}`,
    trivial on `G_γ(ℚ) = Γ ∩ C_γ` by the product formula. It factors through
    `(Γ ∩ C_γ) \ C_γ`, which is compact (step 3 of "Why it is true" below), so its image is a
    compact subgroup of `ℝ_{>0}`, that is `{1}`. So `C_γ ⊆ G_γ(𝔸)¹`.

  Since `A⁺` is central and `G(𝔸) = A⁺ × G(𝔸)¹`, also `G_γ(𝔸) = A⁺ × C_γ`, so
  `G_γ(𝔸) \ G(𝔸) ≅ C_γ \ G(𝔸)¹`.
- **The Tamagawa coefficients.** Page 660 does not spell out the measures in (4.1). Kottwitz
  fixes them for the elliptic terms of the trace formula in *Stable trace formula: elliptic
  singular terms*, Math. Ann. 275 (1986), registered `kottwitz-1986-elliptic-singular-terms`,
  §9.1, printed pp. 391–392 (PDF pp. 27–28), for a connected reductive group `G` over a number
  field `F`: the term of an elliptic semisimple `γ ∈ G(F)` is
  `[G_γ(F) : I(F)]⁻¹ τ(I) O_γ(f)`, where `I` is the identity component of `G_γ`, `τ(I)` its
  Tamagawa number, and `O_γ(f) = ∫_{I(𝔸) \ G(𝔸)} f(g⁻¹ γ g) dg/di` is formed with the
  canonical Haar measures `dg` on `G(𝔸)` and `di` on `I(𝔸)`, those used in the definition of the Tamagawa
  numbers. For Kottwitz's group `G_γ` is connected (see above), so `I = G_γ`, the index
  `[G_γ(ℚ) : I(ℚ)]` is `1`, and the coefficient is `τ(G_γ)`, with the orbital integral over
  `G_γ(𝔸) \ G(𝔸)` formed with the same canonical measure on the centralizer. We read (4.1)
  with this convention, on the norm-one level. Let `dt_γ` be the Tamagawa measure on
  `G_γ(𝔸)¹`, normalized as in the definition of the Tamagawa number, so that `τ(G_γ)` is the
  volume of `G_γ(ℚ) \ G_γ(𝔸)¹` for `dt_γ` and counting measure on `G_γ(ℚ)`. For `χ = 1`, `f` is
  a function on `G(𝔸) / A_G(ℝ)⁰ ≅ G(𝔸)¹`. Fix a Haar measure `dg` on `G(𝔸)¹`, used in
  `tr π(f)`, and let `O_γ(f)` be the integral of `f(g⁻¹ γ g)` over
  `G_γ(𝔸) \ G(𝔸) ≅ G_γ(𝔸)¹ \ G(𝔸)¹` for the quotient measure of `dg` by `dt_γ`.

  For `dg = Measure.haar` and `γ = r j`, take `μC j = dt_γ` on `C_j = G_γ(𝔸)¹` in clause 2
  (a Haar measure; `G_γ(𝔸)¹` is unimodular since `G_γ` is reductive). The measure `νΓC j`
  compatible with `dt_γ` and counting measure then has total mass `τ(G_γ)`, and the measure
  `νC j` compatible with `Measure.haar` and `dt_γ` is the quotient measure in `O_γ`, so the
  `j`-th term of clause 2 is `τ(G_γ) O_γ(fOne)`. Any other compatible `μC j` gives the same
  product (see "Measure normalizations" above), but then its volume factor is not `τ(G_γ)`.
  With the Tamagawa measure on `G_γ(𝔸)` itself, the volume of `A_G(ℝ)⁰ G_γ(ℚ) \ G_γ(𝔸)`
  would depend on a choice of Haar measure on `A_G(ℝ)⁰`; the norm-one form avoids that
  choice. For another Haar measure `dg = c · Measure.haar` and a fixed `f` on `G(𝔸)¹`, both
  sides of (4.1) are multiplied by `c`, so (4.1) for `dg` follows from the case
  `dg = Measure.haar`.
- **Other groups.** For any connected reductive `G` over `ℚ` with `G / A_G` anisotropic over
  `ℚ` and `T = A_G`, the Lean statement applies to the compact quotient `[G]`, and its `j`-th
  term is formed with the full centralizer `C_j`. The direct identification with `τ(G_γ)`
  above uses that `G_γ` is connected, which can fail for such `G`. For example, let `B` be the
  rational quaternion algebra `(−1, −1)`, with generators `u`, `v` such that `u² = v² = −1` and
  `v u = −u v`, and let `G = PGL₁(B)`; then `G(ℝ)` is compact and `A_G` is trivial. Since
  `v⁻¹ u v = −u`, the class of `v` centralizes the class `γ` of `u`. Over `ℚ̄`, where `u`
  becomes a diagonal matrix with distinct eigenvalues and `v` interchanges its eigenlines, the
  class of `v` lies outside the identity component of the centralizer, the image of the
  diagonal torus. For such `γ`, the elliptic term in Kottwitz 1986 §9.1 has `I = G_γ°` and the
  coefficient `τ(I) / [G_γ(ℚ) : I(ℚ)]`; the comparison of that form with the terms of clause 2
  is not made here.
- **Test functions.** Kottwitz's `f` is smooth; for `χ = 1` it is a smooth compactly supported
  function on `G(𝔸)¹`. Here the test functions on `G(𝔸)¹` are the averages `fOne` of
  convolutions `f = f₁ * f₂`. Since `A⁺` is central and `μG = da × Measure.haar`, the average of
  `f₁ * f₂` is the convolution on `G(𝔸)¹` of the averages of `f₁` and `f₂`, and every
  compactly supported continuous function on `G(𝔸)¹` is such an average. So these test
  functions are the convolutions of two compactly supported continuous functions on `G(𝔸)¹`.
  Smooth compactly supported functions are finite sums of such convolutions
  (Dixmier–Malliavin at the archimedean places; at the finite places a function invariant under
  a compact open subgroup `K_f` is its own convolution with the normalized characteristic
  function of `K_f`), and both sides of (4.1) are linear in `f`. So for smooth `f` both sides of
  (4.1) are finite linear combinations of the corresponding sides for such convolutions.

Kottwitz's Lemmas 2 and 4.1 (pseudo-stabilization) and the application to Shimura varieties
are not part of this statement.

## What is derived, and how it differs from the sources

This is a derived statement: Arthur's §1 for `H = G(𝔸_F)¹` and `Γ` the image of `G(F)`,
written with the Library's actual objects.

- **Setting.** Arthur's `H` is any unimodular group with a discrete cocompact `Γ`. Here `H` is
  the norm-one adelic group of an affine group scheme `G`, locally of finite type, with no
  reductivity assumption, and `L²` is taken on `[G] = A⁺ G(F) \ G(𝔸_F)`.
- **Test functions.** Arthur asks that (1.2) hold for `f`. Here `f = f₁ * f₂` on `G(𝔸_F)`,
  for which trace class is the content of `GroupSchemeCompactQuotientL2TraceClassStatement`.
- **Geometric side.** The sum over all conjugacy classes is written as a finite sum over
  representatives covering the classes on which `fOne` does not vanish identically. Finiteness
  of the volumes and integrability of the orbit functions are conclusions, and the identity is
  stated for every compatible choice of centralizer measures.
- **Spectral side.** The sum over equivalence classes `π` with multiplicities is written as a
  sum over the members of an arbitrary decomposition `A`, with traces of compressions.

## Why it is true

Write `H = G(𝔸_F)¹`. The subgroup `Γ` is discrete, since `G(F)` is discrete in `G(𝔸_F)`.
Compactness of `[G]` makes `Γ \ H` compact, so `H = Γ · C` for a compact set `C`
(`exists_isCompact_twistedSupport_of_compactSpace` gives this form of support control).

1. `R(f) = R(f₁) R(f₂)`, and on a compact quotient each `R(fᵢ)` is Hilbert–Schmidt, because
   its kernel is continuous on the compact space `(Γ \ H)²` (Arthur, p. 8; Getz–Hahn
   Lemma 9.3.3). So `R(f)` is trace class.
2. If `fOne(x⁻¹ q x) ≠ 0` with `x = δ c`, `δ ∈ Γ`, `c ∈ C`, then `δ⁻¹ q δ` lies in
   `Γ ∩ C · supp(fOne) · C⁻¹`, which is finite because `Γ` is discrete and the second set is
   compact. So finitely many `Γ`-classes contribute.
3. `Γ ∩ C_γ` is cocompact in `C_γ`, for `γ ∈ Γ`. The finite set `Γ ∩ C γ C⁻¹` contains only
   finitely many `Γ`-conjugates of `γ`; write them `δ_k⁻¹ γ δ_k` with `δ_k ∈ Γ`. If
   `h = δ c ∈ C_γ` with `δ ∈ Γ` and `c ∈ C`, then `δ⁻¹ γ δ = c γ c⁻¹` is one of them, so
   `δ δ_k⁻¹ ∈ Γ ∩ C_γ` for some `k`, and `h ∈ (Γ ∩ C_γ) · (C_γ ∩ ⋃_k δ_k C)`, a product with a
   compact set. A compatible quotient measure is regular, so the volume of the compact space
   `(Γ ∩ C_γ) \ C_γ` is finite.
4. The trace is `∫_{Γ\H} K(x, x) dx` (Library statement
   `TraceClassContinuousKernelDiagonalStatement`), and Arthur's unfolding gives the geometric
   side; the same computation for `|fOne|` gives integrability of the orbit functions. The
   Library's `abstractSimpleTwistedTraceFormula` proves this unfolding identity, for `θ` the
   identity, from trace class, support control, finiteness of the volumes and integrability
   of the orbit functions.
5. On the spectral side, the union of Hilbert bases of the members of `A` is a Hilbert basis of
   `L²([G])`. For a trace-class operator the diagonal sum in any Hilbert basis converges
   absolutely to the trace, and its partial sum over a basis of `P` is `tr c_P`. This gives the
   three spectral conclusions; invariance and irreducibility of the members are not needed for
   them and serve only to identify `tr c_P` with `tr π(f)`.

## What this module does not claim

It does not prove the formula. It does not prove that `[G]` is compact for any `G`, in
particular not for connected reductive `G` with anisotropic `G / A_G`. It does not assert that
a decomposition `A` exists, does not classify the summands, and does not identify the
multiplicities `m(π, R)`. It does not state the twisted (`θ ≠ id`) form, stable conjugacy,
Kottwitz's pseudo-stabilization, or any application to Shimura varieties, and it fixes no
normalization of the centralizer measures beyond compatibility.
