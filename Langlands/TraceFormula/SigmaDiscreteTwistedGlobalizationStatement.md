# Globalization of a σ-discrete series as a local component of a σ-stable cusp (statement)

Module `Langlands.TraceFormula.SigmaDiscreteTwistedGlobalizationStatement`, namespace
`Langlands.TraceFormula`. It contains one proposition definition and no proof:

- `Langlands.TraceFormula.SigmaDiscreteTwistedGlobalizationStatement.{uF, uE, uR, uW}`.

In words: let `E/F` be a cyclic extension of number fields of degree `l` (any `l ≥ 1`, not
necessarily prime) with generator `σ`, in which every infinite place of `F` splits completely.
Let `v` be a finite place of `F` with only one place `w` of `E` above it, and let `v₁, v₂, v₃` be
three distinct finite places of `F`, all different from `v`, that split completely in `E`. Let
`ρ` be a `σ`-discrete series representation of `GL_n(F_v ⊗_F E) ≅ GL_n(E_w)` (`n = r + 1`). Then
there is a cuspidal automorphic representation `Π` of `GL_n(𝔸_E)` with unitary central
character, isomorphic to its `σ`-conjugate, whose local components at all places above `v₁` and
above `v₂` are supercuspidal, and whose local component `Π_w` is irreducible, admissible and
isomorphic to `ρ`.

This is the twisted globalization step of the global proof of descent for `σ`-discrete series in
Henniart–Lemaire 2011, II.6.16 (printed pp. 131–132), with split auxiliary places as in
Arthur–Clozel 1989, Chapter 1, Lemmas 2.4–2.5 (printed pp. 13–15) and §6.3 (printed pp. 54–57).
It is derived content, formulated for this Library; see "Provenance".

The module declares five `local instance`s of `AddCommGroup`: on the Library's form spaces
`Langlands.Automorphic.RationalInvariantFunctions`, `ContinuousRationalFunctions`,
`FiniteSmoothRationalFunctions` and `AutomorphicForms`, and on the carrier `A.Space` of a
`LocalRealization`. They are `Submodule.addCommGroup _`, as in the Library's
`Langlands.Automorphic.SupercuspidalComponent`. They are not exported.

## Statement

For all `F`, `E`, `σ`, `l`, `v`, `w`, `v₁`, `v₂`, `v₃`, `r` as above, assume:

1. `IsCyclicAlgebra σ l`;
2. `IsCyclicShiftSplit F E u.Completion σ l` for every infinite place `u` of `F`;
3. `w` is the only place of `E` above `v`;
4. `v₁ ≠ v₂`, `v₁ ≠ v₃`, `v₂ ≠ v₃`, `v₁ ≠ v`, `v₂ ≠ v`, `v₃ ≠ v`;
5. `IsCyclicShiftSplit F E (vᵢ.adicCompletion F) σ l` for `i = 1, 2, 3`.

Then for every complex vector space `W : Type uR` and every
`ρ : Representation ℂ (GeneralLinear (r + 1) E_v) W` with `IsSigmaDiscreteSeries σv (r + 1) ρ`
there are `T : CuspidalOperatorRealization E r` and
`P : CuspidalSpectralPresentation.{uE, uW} T` such that

- `ClassFieldTheory.Global.RelativeWeilGroup.HasUnitaryCentralCharacter T`;
- `T.isoClass.IsGaloisStable σ`;
- `(P.localComponent w₁.1).IsSupercuspidal` for every `w₁ : FinitePlacesAbove E v₁`, and
  `(P.localComponent w₂.1).IsSupercuspidal` for every `w₂ : FinitePlacesAbove E v₂`;
- `(P.localComponent w.1).IsIrreducible` and `(P.localComponent w.1).IsAdmissible`;
- `Nonempty (Representation.Equiv PiV ρ)`.

The binders appear in the Lean in this order:
`F E` with their instances, `σ l`, hypothesis 1, hypothesis 2, `v w`, hypothesis 3,
`v₁ v₂ v₃`, the six inequalities of hypothesis 4, the three conditions of hypothesis 5, `r`,
the local data at `v`, then `W`, its instances, `ρ` and `IsSigmaDiscreteSeries σv (r + 1) ρ`.
The universe `uW` of the local spaces of `P` is free: it occurs only in the conclusion.

Remarks on the form.

- **Any degree.** `l` is not assumed to be prime, as in the Library's
  `Langlands.BaseChange.SigmaDiscreteDescentStatement`, the descent statement whose global proof
  this globalization serves.
- **Three split places.** The simple twisted trace formula of Arthur–Clozel, Chapter 1,
  Lemma 2.5 (printed pp. 13–15), is stated for test functions with a fixed central character
  and two conditions at finite places of `F` that split completely in `E`. At one such place
  (condition (3′)) the components of the test function at the `l` places above it are
  coefficients of one and the same supercuspidal representation of `GL_n`; this makes the
  operator cuspidal. At another (condition (4′)), if `Ω₁, …, Ω_l` are the supports of the
  components at the `l` places above it, the product `Ω₁Ω₂⋯Ω_l` consists of elements of `GL_n`
  whose image in `PGL_n` is regular elliptic; this restricts the geometric side to
  `σ`-conjugacy classes with regular elliptic norms, and the regularity in `PGL_n` removes the
  indices from their terms (end of the proof of Lemma 2.5, printed p. 15). Here `v₁` and `v₂`
  carry supercuspidal coefficients, so that the cusp produced has supercuspidal components
  above both: the comparison with the simple trace formula over `F` that descends `Π` to a
  cuspidal representation of `GL_n(𝔸_F)` (Arthur–Clozel, Chapter 1, §6.3) needs again a split
  place with a supercuspidal coefficient and a split place with support of the kind of
  condition (4), at which the character of a supercuspidal component does not vanish
  identically on the regular elliptic set. The place `v₃` carries condition (4′), with supports
  whose images in `PGL_n` are moreover strongly regular (step 4). It is also the first place at
  which supports are restricted to isolate the term of the global element `δ` on the geometric
  side, the role of Henniart–Lemaire's place `v₂` in II.6.16 (printed p. 132: "Quitte à
  restreindre ... le support de la fonction `f_{v₂}`, ... seule la σ-orbite de δ contribue").
  With the central character fixed on `Z(𝔸_E)`, this term is indexed by the `σ`-conjugacy class
  of `δ` modulo the rational centre `Z(E)`. A split place cannot tell this class apart from a
  class whose norm is that of `δ` multiplied by an element of `F^×` that is not a norm from
  `E^×`; such classes are removed at finitely many further non-split places chosen in the
  argument (step 5). A dedicated place keeps these support conditions independent of the choice
  of the supercuspidal coefficients at `v₁` and `v₂`. By Chebotarev's density theorem infinitely
  many finite places of `F` split completely in `E`, so such places exist.
- **`vᵢ ≠ v`.** For `l ≥ 2` the place `v` is not split, since only one place lies above it, so
  this is automatic. For `l = 1` every place splits, and without it `v₁ = v` would force `ρ` to
  be supercuspidal.
- **Isomorphism, not equality.** The conclusion is an isomorphism `PiV ≅ ρ` of representations
  of `GL_{r+1}(E_v)`. `P` is existential because its universe `uW` is free; by the Library's
  `Langlands.Automorphic.CuspidalSpectralPresentation.nonempty_localComponent_equiv`, the local
  component at `w` of any other presentation of `T` that is irreducible at `w` is isomorphic to
  that of `P`.
- **Unitary central character.** The central character `ω_ρ` of `ρ` is unitary (`ρ` is
  tempered), and the cusp is produced with a unitary central character. It is asserted because
  the comparison with the trace formula over `F` that follows this step is made among cusps with
  unitary central characters. In the argument below the central character of the cusp is fixed
  in advance: it is a `σ`-invariant unitary character of the idele class group of `E` with
  component `ω_ρ` at `w`.
- **What is not claimed.** Nothing is said about `Π` at `v₃` or at the other places, about a
  `σ`-intertwiner of `Π` or its normalization, or about uniqueness of `Π`.

## Why the statement is true

Write `Π` for the representation of `T`, `n = r + 1` and `G = GL_n`, and identify
`GL_n(F_v ⊗_F E)` with `G(E_w)` through `h`. Then `σv` becomes the action of `σ` on `E_w`
(`σ` fixes `w`, the only place above `v`), and `E_w/F_v` is cyclic of degree `l` with Galois
group generated by `σ`. The argument is that of Henniart–Lemaire 2011, II.6.16 (printed
pp. 131–132), with split auxiliary places as in Arthur–Clozel 1989, Chapter 1, Lemma 2.5 and the
proof of Proposition 2.7 (printed pp. 13–17), and §6.3 (printed pp. 54–57). Henniart–Lemaire
work in positive characteristic; the structure of their argument does not depend on it. As in
both sources, the central character is fixed throughout: a twisted pseudo-coefficient separates
`ρ` only from the representations with the same central character (Henniart–Lemaire II.5.13,
Définition; the Library's `Langlands.BaseChange.IsTwistedPseudoCoefficient`).

1. **The central character.** The central character `ω_ρ` of `ρ` is a unitary character of
   `E_w^×`, and it is `σ`-invariant since `ρ ≅ ρ ∘ σ`. By Hilbert's Theorem 90 for `E_w/F_v` it
   is trivial on the kernel of the norm, so `ω_ρ = ω₀ ∘ N_{E_w/F_v}` for a unitary character
   `ω₀` of `F_v^×` (extend from the open subgroup of norms). Choose irreducible supercuspidal
   representations `π₁⁰` of `GL_n(F_{v₁})` and `π₂⁰` of `GL_n(F_{v₂})` (the Library's
   `Langlands.BaseChange.SupercuspidalExistenceStatement`). Choose a unitary character `ω` of
   `𝔸_F^×/F^×` with `ω_v = ω₀` and with `ω` equal to the central character of `πᵢ⁰` on
   `𝒪_{vᵢ}^×` for `i = 1, 2`. Such an `ω` exists because the subgroup
   `F_v^× × 𝒪_{v₁}^× × 𝒪_{v₂}^×` of `𝔸_F^×` maps homeomorphically onto a closed subgroup of
   `𝔸_F^×/F^×` (it meets `F^×` trivially, and the logarithmic embedding at the infinite places
   maps the `v`-units of `F` onto a lattice with finite kernel), and a unitary character of a
   closed subgroup of a locally compact abelian group extends to the whole group; compare
   Henniart–Lemaire II.6.15 (printed p. 131), where `ω` is adjusted in the same way. The
   character `ω_{vᵢ}` differs from the central character of `πᵢ⁰` by an unramified character
   `|·|^{sᵢ}`, so `πᵢ := πᵢ⁰ ⊗ |det|^{sᵢ/n}` is irreducible supercuspidal with central
   character `ω_{vᵢ}`. Put `χ := ω ∘ N_{𝔸_E/𝔸_F}`. It is a `σ`-invariant unitary character of
   `𝔸_E^×/E^×` with `χ_w = ω_ρ`, and its component at every place of `E` above `vᵢ` is
   `ω_{vᵢ}` (Henniart–Lemaire II.6.16: "Quitte à changer ω, on peut supposer que le
   caractère χ₀ ∘ ι de E×_{v₀} = Z(E_{v₀}) coïncide avec χ_{v₀}"). All test functions below
   transform under the centre by `χ^{-1}` (Arthur–Clozel, conditions (1) and (2), printed
   p. 13; Henniart–Lemaire II.6.4), and the spectral side runs over the cuspidal automorphic
   representations with central character `χ`.

   *Measures, traces and orbital integrals with central character `χ`.* For a place `u` of `F`
   put `E_u := E ⊗_F F_u`, on which `σ` acts through `E`, so that `Z(E_u) = E_u^×` and
   `Z(F_u) = F_u^×`, and write `N` for the norm from `E_u` to `F_u`. A test function at `u` is a
   smooth function `φ_u` on `G(E_u)`, compactly supported modulo `Z(E_u)`, with
   `φ_u(zx) = χ_u(z)^{-1} φ_u(x)` for `z ∈ Z(E_u)`. A Haar measure on `Z(E_u)\G(E_u)` defines
   `Π_u(φ_u) = ∫_{Z(E_u)\G(E_u)} φ_u(g) Π_u(g) dḡ` for a representation `Π_u` with central
   character `χ_u` (Henniart–Lemaire II.2.7, printed pp. 66–67), and the product of these
   measures defines the operator `R_χ(φ)` for `φ = ⊗_u φ_u`. If `u` splits in `E`, then
   `G(E_u) = G(F_u)^l`, `Z(E_u)\G(E_u) = (Z(F_u)\G(F_u))^l`, and the components of `χ` at the `l`
   places above `u` all equal `ω_u`. Functions on `G(F_u)` that transform under the centre by
   `ω_u^{-1}` are then convolved over `Z(F_u)\G(F_u)`:
   `(f₁ * f₂)(x) = ∫_{Z(F_u)\G(F_u)} f₁(y) f₂(y⁻¹x) dȳ`. The integrand is invariant under
   `Z(F_u)`, and `f₁ * f₂` again transforms by `ω_u^{-1}` (Henniart–Lemaire I.2.9, printed
   p. 22, where the convolution is defined by a Haar measure on `Z(E₁)\G(E₁)`).

   Let `δ ∈ G(E_u)` have a regular semisimple norm, and let
   `G_{δ,σ}(F_u) = {g ∈ G(E_u) : g⁻¹δσ(g) = δ}` be its twisted centralizer: the points of an
   inner form of the centralizer of a norm of `δ` (Henniart–Lemaire II.2.3, printed p. 64), a
   torus. For `a ∈ Z(E_u)` one has `(ag)⁻¹δσ(ag) = a⁻¹σ(a)·g⁻¹δσ(g)` and `χ_u(a⁻¹σ(a)) = 1`, as
   `χ_u = ω_u ∘ N` is `σ`-invariant. So `g ↦ φ_u(g⁻¹δσ(g))` is invariant on the left under
   `H_{δ,u} := Z(E_u)G_{δ,σ}(F_u)`, and the twisted orbital integral is taken over the quotient
   by this group:

   `Φ_u(φ_u, δ) := ∫_{H_{δ,u}\G(E_u)} φ_u(g⁻¹δσ(g)) dḡ`.

   The group `H_{δ,u}` is closed: it consists of the `g` for which `z := g⁻¹δσ(g)δ⁻¹` is central
   with `N(z) = 1`. Indeed, by Hilbert's Theorem 90 for `E_u/F_u` such a `z` is `a⁻¹σ(a)` with
   `a ∈ Z(E_u)`, and then `ga⁻¹ ∈ G_{δ,σ}(F_u)`. As `Z(E_u) ∩ G_{δ,σ}(F_u) = Z(F_u)`, the group
   `Z(E_u)\H_{δ,u}` is `Z(F_u)\G_{δ,σ}(F_u)`, which is commutative. The measure `dḡ` on
   `H_{δ,u}\G(E_u)` is the quotient of the Haar measures on `Z(E_u)\G(E_u)` and on
   `Z(F_u)\G_{δ,σ}(F_u)`. The integral converges. Let `C` be a compact set with
   `supp φ_u ⊂ Z(E_u)C`, and suppose `g⁻¹δσ(g) = zc` with `z ∈ Z(E_u)` and `c ∈ C`. Comparing
   the determinants of the norms, `N(z)^n`, and hence `N(z)`, lies in a compact subset of
   `F_u^×` that depends only on `δ` and `C`. By Hilbert's Theorem 90 the norm induces a homeomorphism of
   `Z(E_u)/{a⁻¹σ(a) : a ∈ Z(E_u)}` onto the closed subgroup `N(E_u^×)` of `F_u^×`, so
   `z = z′a⁻¹σ(a)` with `z′` in a compact set `K′` and `a ∈ Z(E_u)`. Then `ga⁻¹ ∈ H_{δ,u}g` and
   `(ga⁻¹)⁻¹δσ(ga⁻¹) = z′c ∈ K′C`. As the norm of `δ` is semisimple, the `σ`-orbit of `δ` is
   closed and `G_{δ,σ}(F_u)\G(E_u)` maps homeomorphically onto it (Henniart–Lemaire II.2.3,
   printed p. 63; Arthur–Clozel, printed p. 15). So the integrand, which is continuous, has
   compact support in `H_{δ,u}\G(E_u)`. For example, if `n = 1` and `u` splits, then `H_{δ,u} = G(E_u)` and
   `Φ_u(φ_u, δ)` is a constant multiple of `φ_u(δ)`. For `l ≥ 2`, an integral over
   `G_{δ,σ}(F_u)\G(E_u)` alone would then be infinite as soon as `φ_u(δ) ≠ 0`: its integrand is
   invariant under the non-compact group `Z(F_u)\Z(E_u)`. Where `E_u` is a field, as at `v`,
   `Z(F_u)\Z(E_u)` is compact. The integral over `G_{δ,σ}(F_u)\G(E_u)` with which
   Henniart–Lemaire (II.2.6, printed p. 66) extend twisted orbital integrals to functions with a
   central character is then a positive constant multiple of `Φ_u(φ_u, δ)`.
2. **The function at `v`.** Let `A` be an isomorphism of `ρ` onto `ρ ∘ σ`. Take for `φ_v` a
   twisted pseudo-coefficient of `(ρ, A)` with central character `χ_w = ω_ρ` (Henniart–Lemaire
   II.5.13, Définition and Proposition, printed p. 113): its twisted trace is `1` on `(ρ, A)`
   and `0` on every `σ`-stable irreducible tempered representation with central character
   `ω_ρ` that is not isomorphic to `ρ`, for every isomorphism onto its `σ`-conjugate. Its
   twisted orbital integrals vanish at the `σ`-regular elements that are not `σ`-elliptic, and,
   `ρ` being `σ`-elliptic, they do not vanish at some `σ`-elliptic `δ₀` (II.5.14, Proposition 2
   and Corollaire 2, printed pp. 114–117; II.6.16, printed p. 131). Twisted orbital integrals
   are locally constant on the `σ`-regular set (Arthur–Clozel, Chapter 1, printed p. 17 and
   §3), so they do not vanish on an open neighbourhood `V` of `δ₀` consisting of `σ`-regular
   `σ`-elliptic elements. The Library's
   `Langlands.BaseChange.TwistedCuspidalPseudoCoefficientExistenceStatement` states these
   properties for a compactly supported twisted pseudo-coefficient in the sense of
   `IsTwistedPseudoCoefficient`, whose separation property likewise concerns only the
   representations with central character `ω_ρ`.
3. **The split places `v₁`, `v₂`.** As in Arthur–Clozel, condition (3′) (printed p. 14), take
   `φ_{vᵢ} = ⊗_j φ_{vᵢ,j}` over the `l` places of `E` above `vᵢ`, so on
   `G(E ⊗_F F_{vᵢ}) = GL_n(F_{vᵢ})^l`, with every `φ_{vᵢ,j}` a coefficient of the same
   supercuspidal representation `πᵢ`, in the form `g ↦ ⟨x^∨, πᵢ(g⁻¹) x⟩`. The central
   character of `πᵢ` is the component of `χ` at these places, so these coefficients transform
   under the centre by `χ^{-1}` and are test functions for the formula with central
   character `χ`.
   Choose them so that the convolution `φ*_{vᵢ} = φ_{vᵢ,1} * ⋯ * φ_{vᵢ,l}`, taken over
   `Z(F_{vᵢ})\G(F_{vᵢ})` as in step 1, is a coefficient of `πᵢ` with nonzero trace on `πᵢ`
   (Arthur–Clozel, printed p. 16: "for correct choices of the functions"). At a split place an
   irreducible `σ`-stable representation of `GL_n(F_{vᵢ})^l` is isomorphic to `τ ⊗ ⋯ ⊗ τ` for
   an irreducible `τ` of `GL_n(F_{vᵢ})` (part (b) of the Library's
   `Langlands.BaseChange.SplitSigmaLiftStatement`). With the cyclic-shift intertwiner its
   twisted trace at `φ_{vᵢ}` is the trace at `φ*_{vᵢ}` of `τ`, integrated over
   `Z(F_{vᵢ})\G(F_{vᵢ})`. Likewise, for compatible measures, the twisted orbital integral
   `Φ_{vᵢ}(φ_{vᵢ}, (δ₁, …, δ_l))` of step 1 is the orbital integral of `φ*_{vᵢ}` at
   `γ = δ₁δ₂⋯δ_l` over `G_γ(F_{vᵢ})\G(F_{vᵢ})`, the quotient by a group that contains the
   centre. In the coordinates of Arthur–Clozel (printed p. 14), where
   `g⁻¹δσ(g) = (g₁⁻¹δ₁g₂, g₂⁻¹δ₂g₃, …, g_l⁻¹δ_lg₁)`, integrating first over `g₂, …, g_l`, each
   modulo `Z(F_{vᵢ})`, gives `φ*_{vᵢ}(g₁⁻¹γg₁)`, and `g ↦ g₁` identifies the twisted
   centralizer with `G_γ(F_{vᵢ})`. See Henniart–Lemaire I.2.9 for functions with a central
   character, and II.2.8 with its Remarque (printed pp. 67–69) for compactly supported
   functions. A coefficient of the supercuspidal `πᵢ` has
   trace zero on every irreducible admissible representation of `GL_n(F_{vᵢ})` with central
   character `ω_{vᵢ}` that is not isomorphic to `πᵢ` (Schur orthogonality for `πᵢ`, which is
   square-integrable modulo the centre, and the vanishing of the coefficients of `πᵢ` on the
   representations outside the Bernstein component of `πᵢ`). Hence a `σ`-stable cusp with
   central character `χ` whose twisted trace at `φ_{vᵢ}` is nonzero has all its components
   above `vᵢ` isomorphic to `πᵢ`, so supercuspidal. For suitable choices the orbital integral
   of `φ*_{vᵢ}` at a regular elliptic element is a nonzero multiple of the value there of the
   character of `πᵢ`, which is a nonzero constant near `1` (Arthur–Clozel, printed p. 16), so it
   does not vanish at the regular elliptic elements close to `1`. The Library's
   `Langlands.BaseChange.SupercuspidalCuspFormStatement` provides a different function: a
   compactly supported `fᵢ` with vanishing constant terms that is a pseudo-coefficient of `πᵢ`
   relative to `ω_{vᵢ}`, kills the traces of the other irreducible admissible representations
   with that central character, and has nonzero orbital integrals at the regular elliptic
   elements near `1`. Using it at `vᵢ` needs further arguments, among them split factors whose
   convolution is `fᵢ` (for example `fᵢ` and unit elements of the Hecke algebra of a compact
   open subgroup under which `fᵢ` is bi-invariant) and the identities of Henniart–Lemaire
   II.2.8; the cuspidality of the operator then comes from the vanishing constant terms.
4. **The element `δ` and the functions at the other places.** For a regular semisimple element
   `γ` of `GL_n` over a field of characteristic zero, with characteristic polynomial `p`, write
   `t·p` for the polynomial `t^n p(X/t)`, the characteristic polynomial of `tγ`. Say that the
   image of `γ` in `PGL_n` is *strongly regular* if `ζγ` is not conjugate to `γ` over an
   algebraic closure for any root of unity `ζ ≠ 1`, that is, if its centralizer in the algebraic
   group `PGL_n` is connected. Then `t ↦ t·p` is injective on the multiplicative group of any
   algebraically closed extension (`t·p = p` forces `t^n = 1`). A regular elliptic element with
   nonzero trace has strongly regular image (compare the coefficients of `X^{n-1}` in
   `ζ·p = p`), so the elements of `GL_n(F_{v₃})` that are regular elliptic with strongly regular
   image in `PGL_n(F_{v₃})` form a nonempty open set. By weak approximation choose `δ ∈ G(E)`
   with `δ_w ∈ V`, whose components `δ_{i,1}, …, δ_{i,l}` at the places above `vᵢ`
   (`i = 1, 2`) have a regular elliptic product `δ_{i,1}⋯δ_{i,l}` close enough to `1` for
   step 3, and whose components at the places above `v₃` have a product
   `γ₃ := δ_{3,1}⋯δ_{3,l}` in that open set (Arthur–Clozel, printed p. 16: "using finite
   approximation, that δ* has elliptic norm (with regular image in PGL(n)) at the place v₂").
   An element of `G(F)` in the norm class of `δ` (Arthur–Clozel, Chapter 1, Lemma 1.1 (i),
   printed p. 3) is conjugate to `γ₃` in `G(F_{v₃})`. So the norm of `δ` is regular elliptic,
   also as an element of `G(F)` (Arthur–Clozel, printed p. 15). Its characteristic polynomial
   `p` lies in `F[X]`, and `t·p ≠ p` for every `t ≠ 1` in an algebraic closure of any completion
   of `F`.

   *Functions concentrated near `δ`.* For a finite place `u` of `F`, with `E_u` as in step 1,
   let `N_u ⊂ F_u^×` be the group of norms from `E_u^×`, a closed subgroup. For `x ∈ G(E_u)`
   the characteristic polynomial `c_u(x)` of `Nx := x σ(x) ⋯ σ^{l-1}(x)` has coefficients in
   `F_u`, since `σ(Nx) = x⁻¹(Nx)x`. Moreover
   `c_u(g⁻¹xσ(g)) = c_u(x)` for `g ∈ G(E_u)` and `c_u(zx) = N(z)·c_u(x)` for `z ∈ Z(E_u)`, where
   `N(z) ∈ N_u` is the norm of `z`. For a compact open neighbourhood `C` of `δ_u` put
   `φ_C(x) := ∫_{Z(E_u)} 1_C(zx) χ_u(z) dz`. This function transforms under the centre by
   `χ_u^{-1}`. For every compact open `C` its support is contained in `Z(E_u)C`; it can be
   smaller, as the integral can cancel. Since `t·q` is close to `q` for `q` close to `p` only
   when `t` is close to `1`, for `C` small enough every `z ∈ Z(E_u)` such that `zC` meets `C` or
   meets the `σ`-orbit of `δ_u` has `N(z)` so close to `1` that `χ_u(z) = ω_u(N(z)) = 1`.
   Assume `C` is that small. For `x ∈ C` the scalars `z` with `zx ∈ C` form a compact open
   subset of `Z(E_u)` that contains `1` and on which `χ_u = 1`. So `φ_C(x)` is its Haar measure,
   which is positive, and the support of `φ_C` is exactly `Z(E_u)C`. A point of the `σ`-orbit
   of `δ_u` in `Z(E_u)C` is `zx` with `x ∈ C` and `χ_u(z) = 1`, where `φ_C(zx) = φ_C(x) > 0`. The
   other points of the orbit lie outside the support. So the integrand
   `g ↦ φ_C(g⁻¹δ_uσ(g))` of the twisted orbital integral `Φ_u(φ_C, δ_u)` of step 1 is
   nonnegative, and positive for `g` near `1`. It is invariant under `H_{δ_u,u}`, and the image
   in `H_{δ_u,u}\G(E_u)` of a neighbourhood of `1` is open, so it has positive measure. As the
   integral over `H_{δ_u,u}\G(E_u)` converges (step 1), `Φ_u(φ_C, δ_u)` is a positive real
   number. For example, if `n = 1` and `u` splits, it is a positive multiple of
   `φ_C(δ_u) > 0`. This is the twisted analogue of the functions of Henniart–Lemaire II.6.8
   (printed pp. 124–125), where the neighbourhood is first restricted so that `ω_v(u′u⁻¹) = 1` and the
   equivariant function is formed afterwards.

   At `v₃` take `φ_{v₃} := φ_{C₃}` with `C₃ = C_{3,1} × ⋯ × C_{3,l}` a product of small compact
   open neighbourhoods of the `δ_{3,j}` in `GL_n(F_{v₃})`. This is `⊗_j φ_{v₃,j}`, where
   `φ_{v₃,j}` is built in the same way on `GL_n(F_{v₃})` from `C_{3,j}`. Its support `Ω_j` is
   contained in `Z(F_{v₃})C_{3,j}`, so `Ω₁Ω₂⋯Ω_l ⊂ Z(F_{v₃})C_{3,1}⋯C_{3,l}`. For small
   `C_{3,j}` this set lies in the open set of elements whose image in `PGL_n(F_{v₃})` is
   strongly regular elliptic, so condition (4′) of Arthur–Clozel holds. Fix a finite set `S`
   of places of `F` containing the infinite places, `v`, `v₁`, `v₂` and `v₃`, such that the
   conditions of Henniart–Lemaire II.6.8 (printed p. 124) hold outside `S`, and moreover
   `δ_u ∈ K_{E_u} := GL_n(𝒪_{E_u})` for `u ∉ S`, with `𝒪_{E_u}` the integral closure of the
   integers of `F_u` in `E_u`. The conditions of II.6.8 are that `E/F` and `ω` are unramified
   there, that the Haar measures are normalized there, and that an element of `G(F)` in the
   norm class of `δ` lies in the maximal compact subgroup. At `u ∉ S` take for `φ_u` the unit element of the spherical Hecke algebra,
   supported on `Z(E_u)K_{E_u}` and transforming under the centre by `χ_u^{-1}`; its twisted
   orbital integral at `δ` is nonzero (Henniart–Lemaire II.6.16, printed pp. 131–132). At the
   other finite places of `S` take `φ_u := φ_{C_u}` for a small `C_u`. At the infinite places,
   which split in `E`, take functions supported near `δ`, compactly supported modulo the
   centre, with nonzero twisted orbital integral `Φ_u(φ_u, δ)` (step 1). The same
   construction gives one, with a nonnegative smooth bump function in place of `1_C`: the
   values of `χ_u` that then occur are close to `1`.
5. **Isolation of the class of `δ` modulo the centre.** Put `φ := ⊗_u φ_u`.
   - *Indexing.* The trace of `R_χ(φ) ∘ I_σ` is the integral of its kernel along the diagonal
     over `G(E)Z(𝔸_E)\G(𝔸_E)`, for the measure on `Z(𝔸_E)\G(𝔸_E)` of step 1.
     The value of the kernel at `(g, g)` is `Σ_{γ ∈ Z(E)\G(E)} φ(g⁻¹γσ(g))` (Arthur–Clozel,
     proof of Lemma 2.5, printed p. 14). The sum runs over `G(E)` modulo the rational centre
     `Z(E) = E^×`, and the summand does not depend on the representative because `χ` is trivial
     on `E^×`. The terms of the geometric side are therefore indexed by the classes of
     `Z(E)\G(E)` under `σ`-conjugation, the sets `{z x⁻¹γσ(x) : z ∈ E^×, x ∈ G(E)}`, with
     regular elliptic norm: `δ` and `zδ` give a single term. As `N(zγ) = N_{E/F}(z)·Nγ`, the
     characteristic polynomial of the norm of such a class is defined only up to `p ↦ t·p`
     with `t ∈ N_{E/F}(E^×)`.
   - *Finiteness.* The support of `φ` lies in `Z(𝔸_E)C` for a compact subset `C` of
     `G(𝔸_E)`: the local functions are compactly supported modulo the centre, and for `u ∉ S`
     the support is `Z(E_u)K_{E_u}`. Suppose that the `σ`-orbit of `γ ∈ G(E)` in `G(𝔸_E)`
     meets this support, that is, `g⁻¹γσ(g) = zc` with `z ∈ Z(𝔸_E)` and `c ∈ C`. Then
     `g⁻¹(Nγ)g = N_{E/F}(z)·N(c)`. Now `det Nγ ∈ F^×` has adelic absolute value `1`, and
     `|N_{E/F}(z)|_{𝔸_F} = |z|_{𝔸_E}`, so comparing determinants shows that `|z|_{𝔸_E}` lies in
     a compact subset of `ℝ_{>0}`. As `𝔸_E^1/E^×` is compact, `z ∈ E^×K` for a compact subset
     `K` of `𝔸_E^×` that depends only on `C`. Replacing `γ` by its multiple by an element of
     `E^×`, we may take `z ∈ K`. Then the coefficients of the characteristic polynomial of `Nγ`
     lie in `F` (Lemma 1.1 (i)) and in a compact subset of `𝔸_E` that depends only on `C`, so
     they lie in a finite set. A regular elliptic norm is semisimple, so its characteristic
     polynomial determines its conjugacy class in `G(E)`. By Lemma 1.1 (ii) (printed p. 3)
     this class in turn determines the `σ`-conjugacy class of `γ`. Hence only finitely many
     classes modulo `Z(E)` with regular elliptic norm have a `σ`-orbit meeting the support of
     `φ`. Only these classes can contribute, and their set can only shrink when the local
     supports are made smaller. This is the finiteness behind Arthur–Clozel, Lemma 2.6
     (printed p. 15).
   - *Separation.* Let `δ'` represent one of these classes other than that of `δ`, and let
     `p' ∈ F[X]` be the characteristic polynomial of `Nδ'`. There is a finite place
     `u ∉ {v, v₁, v₂}` of `F` with `p' ∉ N_u·p`, as follows. Suppose first that `p' ≠ t·p` for
     every `t ∈ F^×`, and take `u = v₃`, where `N_{v₃} = F_{v₃}^×`. If `t ∈ F_{v₃}^×` had
     `p' = t·p`, then `t` would be the only such element of an algebraic closure `Ω` of
     `F_{v₃}`. Since `p, p' ∈ F[X]`, it would be fixed by `Gal(F̄/F)`, with `F̄` the algebraic
     closure of `F` in `Ω`, so `t ∈ F^×`, which is excluded. Otherwise `p' = t₀·p` with a
     unique `t₀ ∈ F^×`, and `t₀` is not a norm from `E^×`. Indeed, if `t₀ = N_{E/F}(z)` with
     `z ∈ E^×`, then `N(zδ)` and `Nδ'` are semisimple with the same characteristic polynomial
     `p'`. They are then conjugate in `G(E)`, and by Lemma 1.1 (ii) `δ'` would be
     `σ`-conjugate to `zδ`. By Hasse's norm theorem for the cyclic extension `E/F` (the
     Library's `GlobalClassFieldTheory.ClassFieldAxiom.hasseNormPrinciple_cyclic`), `t₀` is not
     a local norm at some place. By the global reciprocity law (the product over all places of
     the local norm residue symbols of `t₀` is trivial) there is such a place `u ≠ v`. Every
     element is a norm at a split place, so `u` does not split in `E`. Hence `u` is finite and
     differs from `v₁`, `v₂` and `v₃`. Finally `p' ∉ N_u·p`, since `t₀` is the only `t` with
     `t·p = p'`.
   - *Restriction.* At such a place `u` consider
     `X_u := {x ∈ G(E_u) : c_u(x) ∈ N_u·p'}`. The set `N_u·p'` is closed among the polynomials
     with nonzero constant term, because `N_u` is closed in `F_u^×` and the constant term of
     `t·p'` is `t^n p'(0)`. Hence `X_u` is closed, and it is stable under `Z(E_u)` and under
     `σ`-conjugation. It contains `δ'_u` but not `δ_u`. Shrink `C_u` (or `C₃` if `u = v₃`) so
     that `Z(E_u)C_u` does not meet `X_u`. If `u ∉ S`, add `u` to `S` and replace the unit
     element by `φ_{C_u}` with `δ_u ∈ C_u ⊂ K_{E_u}`, so that the support becomes smaller. Then
     the class of `δ'` no longer meets the support of `φ`. The twisted orbital integral of
     `φ_u` at `δ` stays positive (step 4), and condition (4′) is kept. After finitely many such
     restrictions only the class of `δ` modulo `Z(E)` contributes. Henniart–Lemaire restrict the
     support at one place for the same purpose (II.6.9, printed p. 125, and II.6.16, printed
     p. 132), and Arthur–Clozel, printed p. 17, separate global twisted classes by local
     conditions.
6. **The simple twisted trace formula.** For `φ = ⊗_u φ_u`, the simple twisted trace formula
   with central character `χ` (Arthur–Clozel, Chapter 1, Lemma 2.5, formula (II), printed
   p. 14; Henniart–Lemaire II.6.4, printed pp. 120–121) is an identity for the trace of the
   operator `R_χ(φ) ∘ I_σ` on the space of cusp forms with central character `χ`. By step 5 its
   geometric side is the single term of the `σ`-conjugacy class of `δ` modulo `Z(E)`. This term
   unfolds as follows.
   - *No index.* The stabilizer in `G(E)` of the set `Z(E)δ` under `σ`-conjugation is
     `Z(E)G_{δ,σ}(F)`. Indeed, if `y⁻¹δσ(y) = zδ` with `z ∈ E^×`, then the characteristic
     polynomials of the norms give `N_{E/F}(z)·p = p`, so `N_{E/F}(z) = 1` by strong regularity
     (step 4). By Hilbert's Theorem 90, `z = a⁻¹σ(a)` with `a ∈ E^×`, and `ya⁻¹ ∈ G_{δ,σ}(F)`.
     This is why no index occurs (compare Arthur–Clozel, end of the proof of Lemma 2.5, printed
     p. 15).
   - *Unfolding.* With the sum and the integral interchanged as in Arthur–Clozel (Lemma 2.6 and
     the end of the proof of Lemma 2.5, printed p. 15), the term is
     `∫_{Z(𝔸_E)G(E)\G(𝔸_E)} Σ_{y ∈ Z(E)G_{δ,σ}(F)\G(E)} φ(g⁻¹y⁻¹δσ(yg)) dg`, that is
     `∫_{Z(𝔸_E)G_{δ,σ}(F)\G(𝔸_E)} φ(g⁻¹δσ(g)) dg`. As `Z(𝔸_E) ∩ G_{δ,σ}(𝔸_F) = Z(𝔸_F)`,
     the fibres of the projection onto `Z(𝔸_E)G_{δ,σ}(𝔸_F)\G(𝔸_E)` are copies of
     `Z(𝔸_F)G_{δ,σ}(F)\G_{δ,σ}(𝔸_F)`. The term is therefore
     `vol(Z(𝔸_F)G_{δ,σ}(F)\G_{δ,σ}(𝔸_F)) · ∏_u Φ_u(φ_u, δ)`, with the local twisted orbital
     integrals of step 1 over the quotients `H_{δ,u}\G(E_u)` by `Z(E_u)G_{δ,σ}(F_u)`. The
     local measures on `Z(E_u)\G(E_u)` and `Z(F_u)\G_{δ,σ}(F_u)` have the global ones as
     products, and at almost all places they give volume `1` to the images of the maximal
     compact subgroups (Henniart–Lemaire II.6.4 and II.6.6, printed pp. 120–122). Almost all
     factors then equal `1`. At almost all `u`, `φ_u` is the unit element, and by the
     fundamental lemma for it `Φ_u(φ_u, δ)` is the orbital integral of the unit element of
     `G(F_u)` at a norm of `δ`. That orbital integral is `1` when the norm lies in `GL_n(𝒪_u)`
     and its discriminant is a unit.
   - *The volume.* It is finite: `G_{δ,σ}` is an `F`-torus isomorphic to the centralizer
     `Res_{K/F} 𝔾_m` of a norm of `δ` (it is an inner form of this torus, and a torus has no
     other inner forms), where `K` is a field of degree `n` because the norm is regular
     elliptic. So the quotient is `𝔸_F^× K^×\𝔸_K^×`, which is compact. It is the
     volume of `G_δ^σ(F)Z(𝔸_F)\G_δ^σ(𝔸_F)` in Henniart–Lemaire II.6.4 (printed p. 121).
   - *Nonvanishing.* The local factors are finite (step 1) and nonzero (steps 2–4), so the
     geometric side is not zero.
   - *The display of formula (II).* Arthur–Clozel write the local integrals of formula (II)
     over `G_{δ,σ}(F_v)\G(E_v)`. The unfolding above of their kernel, summed over
     `Z(E)\G(E)` and integrated over `G(E)Z(𝔸_E)\G(𝔸_E)`, gives the quotients by
     `Z(E_u)G_{δ,σ}(F_u)` used here. At a split place with `l ≥ 2`, an integral over
     `G_{δ,σ}(F_u)\G(E_u)` alone does not converge (step 1).

   The spectral side becomes a sum over cusps through the cuspidal spectral decomposition and
   the factorization of cusps: `I_σ` carries a cuspidal summand `Π'` to one isomorphic to `Π' ∘ σ`, so by multiplicity one only
   the `σ`-stable `Π'` contribute to the trace, and for these the restriction of `I_σ` is a
   tensor product of local isomorphisms `A_{Π'_u}` of `Π'_u` onto `Π'_u ∘ σ`. This gives
   `tr(R_χ(φ) ∘ I_σ) = Σ_{Π'} ∏_u tr(Π'_u(φ_u) ∘ A_{Π'_u})` (Henniart–Lemaire II.6.4). Hence
   there is a `σ`-stable cuspidal `Π'` with central character `χ` and
   `tr(Π'_u(φ_u) ∘ A_{Π'_u}) ≠ 0` at every place `u` (Henniart–Lemaire II.6.16, printed
   p. 132). By step 3 the components of `Π'` above `v₁` and `v₂` are supercuspidal. The
   Library's `Langlands.TraceFormula.GeneralLinearSimpleTwistedTraceFormulaStatement` states
   the simple twisted trace formula on `L²` of the quotient of `G(𝔸_E)` by `G(E)` and the
   positive real points of a central split torus, without a fixed central character, for the
   convolution of two compactly supported test functions, the second with vanishing constant
   terms along the proper parabolic subgroups, whose support has norms regular elliptic in
   `GL_n` at one finite place of `E`. It gives the identity between the operator trace
   `tr(R(f) ∘ I_θ)` and a finite sum of twisted orbital integrals; the sum over cusps of
   products of local twisted traces comes, as above, from the cuspidal spectral decomposition
   and the factorization of cusps. Without a fixed central character the argument would need,
   at `v`, a test function that also separates `ρ` from the `σ`-discrete series outside the
   family of its unramified twists, followed by a twist of the cusp by a `σ`-invariant
   unramified unitary character; this sketch does not follow that variant.
7. **The component at `w`.** The twisted orbital integrals of `φ_v` vanish at the `σ`-regular
   elements that are not `σ`-elliptic, so by the twisted Weyl integration formula
   (Henniart–Lemaire II.2.10, formula (1)) the twisted character of `Π'_w` does not vanish on
   the `σ`-elliptic set: `Π'_w` is `σ`-elliptic. It is `σ`-stable (`σ` fixes the only place `w`
   above `v`), generic (the Library's `Langlands.Automorphic.CuspidalLocalGenericityStatement`
   and `Langlands.Automorphic.CuspidalUnitaryLocalGenericityStatement`) and unitary, with central
   character `χ_w = ω_ρ`, hence `σ`-discrete by Henniart–Lemaire II.5.15, Proposition 1
   (printed p. 117: a generic irreducible `σ`-stable representation is `σ`-elliptic if and only
   if it is essentially `σ`-discrete; the direction used is II.5.8, Lemme, printed p. 106, which
   is Arthur–Clozel, Chapter 1, Lemma 2.11). This local proposition, and the consequence used
   here (a generic irreducible `σ`-stable representation with unitary central character whose
   twisted character is nonzero at a function whose `σ`-orbital integrals vanish off the
   `σ`-elliptic set is `σ`-discrete), are inputs of the argument that this module does not
   state. So `Π'_w` is an irreducible tempered `σ`-stable representation with central character
   `ω_ρ` whose twisted trace at `φ_v` is nonzero, and the defining property of `φ_v` (step 2)
   gives `Π'_w ≅ ρ` (Henniart–Lemaire II.6.16, printed p. 132: "elle est σ-discrète
   (5.15, proposition 1), donc isomorphe à Π₀").
8. **Conclusion.** `Π := Π'` is `σ`-stable, has the unitary central character `χ`, and has
   supercuspidal components above `v₁` and `v₂`. A presentation `P` with irreducible admissible
   local components in the universe `uW` is obtained from the Library's
   `Langlands.Automorphic.CuspidalAllLocalIrreduciblePresentationExistenceStatement` and the
   universe change of the module `Langlands.Automorphic.CuspidalPresentationUniverse`, and
   `PiV ≅ ρ` follows from `Π_w ≅ ρ` through `h`.

In Arthur–Clozel and Henniart–Lemaire the descent of `σ`-discrete series is proved from this
globalization, so a proof of the present statement cannot use that descent: neither the Library's
`Langlands.BaseChange.SigmaDiscreteDescentStatement` and
`Langlands.BaseChange.SigmaDiscreteRegularDescentStatement` nor the descent statements
`Langlands.Automorphic.Unramified.CyclicStableDescentStatement` and
`Langlands.Automorphic.Unramified.CyclicPrimeStableCuspidalDescentStatement`, nor the local
Langlands correspondence, whose known proofs use cyclic base change.

## Use

In the global proof of descent for `σ`-discrete series (Henniart–Lemaire II.6.16–II.6.17;
Arthur–Clozel, Chapter 1, §6.3(b)) a cyclic extension of local fields is first globalized (the
Library's `Langlands.TraceFormula.CyclicFieldGlobalizationStatement`, proved as
`Langlands.TraceFormula.cyclicFieldGlobalization`), with all infinite places split and a place
`v` with one place `w` above it. The present statement then globalizes a `σ`-discrete series `ρ`
to a `σ`-stable cusp `Π` with `Π_w ≅ ρ` and supercuspidal components above two split places.
Its conclusions on `T` and `P` (unitary central character, `σ`-stability, supercuspidal
components above `v₁` and `v₂`, irreducible admissible component at `w`) are the hypotheses of
the comparison of trace formulas that descends `Π` to a cuspidal representation `π` of
`GL_n(𝔸_F)` with a regular-supported twisted trace identity at `v` (Henniart–Lemaire II.6.17).
