# The order of a finite étale unipotent group

`Langlands.GroupScheme.finiteEtaleUnipotentOrder.{u}` proves
`Langlands.GroupScheme.FiniteEtaleUnipotentOrderStatement.{u}`. For a finite
étale group scheme `G` over a field `k` of characteristic exponent `p`, it
identifies unipotence with the condition that the number of points over the
given separable closure `Ks` is a power of `p`. [Published statement](FiniteEtaleOrderStatement.md)

## Base change and the point count

Take `K = AlgebraicClosure k` and let `H` be the actual base change of `G`
to `Spec K`. Finiteness and étaleness are preserved by pullback. Unipotence
ascends and reflects along the field extension. The pullback equivalence
identifies `rationalPoints H` with the `K`-points of `G`, and finiteness of
`H` over `K` gives finiteness of its rational point group. [Field extension of unipotence](FieldReflectionProof.md)

For each of `L = K` and `L = Ks`, a point in `G.affinePoints (CommAlgCat.of k L)`
is a lift of `Spec L ⟶ Spec k` along the structure morphism of `G`.
The finite étale rank formula counts these lifts over either separably
closed field by the rank of that morphism at the unique point of `Spec k`.
Thus the finite group `rationalPoints H` has the cardinality in the target.
[Geometric rank formula](../../AlgebraicGeometry/FiniteEtale/GeometricRank.md)

## Forward implication

If `H` is unipotent, its closed upper-unitriangular representation gives an
injective matrix homomorphism on rational points. For a point with matrix
`A` of size `n`, the matrix `M = A - 1` is upper triangular with zero
diagonal. Its characteristic polynomial is `X ^ n`, so Cayley–Hamilton
gives `M ^ n = 0`. [Unitriangular representation](../Rational/UnipotentCompactSubgroups.md)

If `p` is prime, the characteristic-power identity for the commuting
matrices `A` and `1` gives `A ^ (p ^ n) - 1 = M ^ (p ^ n) = 0`. For the
empty index type the matrix ring is subsingleton. Injectivity makes every
rational point have order a power of `p`, so the finite point group has
order a power of `p`. If `p = 1`, each matrix has finite order because it
comes from a finite group. The characteristic-zero periodicity lemma applies
to the corresponding endomorphism and forces it to be the identity.
Injectivity then gives a point group of order one. [Characteristic-zero periodicity](CharZeroPeriodic.md)

## Converse

A finite point group of order `p ^ n` is an `IsPGroup p`. If `p` is prime,
the coordinate vector space of any nonzero matrix representation is killed
by `p`. The finite p-group fixed-vector lemma produces a nonzero fixed
vector in the additive subgroup generated by the orbit of a nonzero vector.
If `p = 1`, the point group is subsingleton, so every vector is fixed.
Since `H` is smooth over the algebraically closed field `K`, the rational
point fixedness criterion gives the fixed vector for the group scheme
representation. Field reflection then gives `IsUnipotent G`.
[Finite p-group fixed vectors](../../Cohomology/Tate/PGroupTateVanishingProof.md)
[Rational point fixedness](../Reductive/InvariantLinearForm.md)
