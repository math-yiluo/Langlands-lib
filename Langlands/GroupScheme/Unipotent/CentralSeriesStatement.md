# Central series of unipotent groups with additive quotients

`Langlands.GroupScheme.UnipotentAdditiveCentralSeriesStatement.{u}` is a
proposition definition. This module defines the proposition and proves
nothing.

## Statement

Let `k : Type u` be a field and let
`G : GroupScheme (Spec (CommRingCat.of k))` satisfy
`[LocallyOfFiniteType G.X.hom]` and `[IsAffineHom G.X.hom]`. If
`IsUnipotent G`, then there exist

- a natural number `m`,
- group schemes `H j` over `Spec k` and homomorphisms `i j : H j ⟶ G` for
  `j : Fin (m + 1)` (the subgroups `Gⱼ`), and
- homomorphisms `f j : H j.castSucc ⟶ 𝔾ₐ` for `j : Fin m`, where
  `𝔾ₐ = VectorGroup.groupScheme (CommRingCat.of k) 1`,

such that:

1. every `i j` is a closed subgroup (`IsClosedSubgroup (i j)`);
2. `SubgroupFunctor.range (i 0) = ⊤`, so `G₀ = G`;
3. `SubgroupFunctor.range (i (Fin.last m)) = ⊥`, so `G_m` is trivial;
4. `range (i j.succ) ≤ range (i j.castSucc)` for every `j : Fin m`, so
   `Gⱼ₊₁ ⊆ Gⱼ`;
5. for every `j : Fin m`, every scheme `T` over `Spec k` and every
   `T`-point `y` of `H j.castSucc`, `y ≫ f j = 1` if and only if the image
   of `y` in `G` lies in `Gⱼ₊₁(T)`: the kernel of `f j : Gⱼ ⟶ 𝔾ₐ` is
   `Gⱼ₊₁`;
6. for every `j : Fin m`, every scheme `T` over `Spec k` and all `T`-points
   `g`, `x` of `G` with `x ∈ Gⱼ(T)`, the commutator `g * x * g⁻¹ * x⁻¹` lies
   in `Gⱼ₊₁(T)`.

The ingredients are existing declarations, used without change.

- `IsUnipotent` and `IsClosedSubgroup` (from
  [`Reductive.Basic`](../Reductive/Basic.md)). `IsUnipotent G` says that
  every nonzero finite-dimensional matrix representation of `G` has a
  nonzero fixed vector. `IsClosedSubgroup i` says that the underlying
  morphism of schemes of the homomorphism `i` is a closed immersion.
- `SubgroupFunctor.range i` (from [`SubgroupFunctor`](../SubgroupFunctor.md))
  assigns to each scheme `T` over `Spec k` the subgroup of `G(T)` of points
  that factor through `i`. `⊤` and `⊥` are the whole and the trivial
  subgroup functors, and `≤` is pointwise inclusion.
- `VectorGroup.groupScheme (CommRingCat.of k) 1` (from
  [`VectorGroupQuotient`](../VectorGroupQuotient.md)) is the additive group
  `𝔾ₐ = Spec k[T]`.
- Products, inverses and `1` of `T`-points are those of the groups
  `T ⟶ G.X` and `T ⟶ 𝔾ₐ.X` given by the group objects (Mathlib's
  `CategoryTheory.MonObj` scoped instances).

## Original source

J. S. Milne, *Algebraic Groups: The Theory of Group Schemes of Finite Type
over a Field* (Cambridge University Press, 2017), source ID
`milne-2017-algebraic-groups`, SHA256
`899b0f44f3c3ccfbaf5ed1cf9c180be7a8016b1c0e9709ca0c206304e5bd1cd0`. Page
numbers are printed page numbers. The one-based PDF page is the printed page
plus 16.

- Proposition 14.21, p.284: "Every unipotent algebraic group admits a
  central normal series whose quotients are isomorphic to algebraic subgroups
  of Ga. In particular, every unipotent algebraic group is nilpotent (a
  fortiori solvable)."
- Definition 6.1, p.124: "A subnormal series of G is a finite sequence
  (Gᵢ)ᵢ₌₀,…,ₛ of algebraic subgroups of G such that G₀ = G, Gₛ = e, and Gᵢ
  is a normal subgroup of Gᵢ₋₁ for i = 1, …, s ... A subnormal series (Gᵢ)ᵢ
  is a normal series ... if each Gᵢ is normal ... in G."
- Definition 6.34, p.133: a nilpotent algebraic group admits "a central
  subnormal series, i.e., a normal series G = G₀ ⊃ G₁ ⊃ ⋯ ⊃ Gₜ = e such that
  each quotient Gᵢ/Gᵢ₊₁ is contained in the centre of G/Gᵢ₊₁".
- Remark 6.35, p.133: "A normal series G = G₀ ⊃ G₁ ⊃ ⋯ is central if and
  only if [G, Gᵢ] ⊂ Gᵢ₊₁ for all i."
- Definition 6.24, p.131: for algebraic subgroups `H₁`, `H₂` of an affine
  `G`, the commutator subgroup `[H₁, H₂]` is "the algebraic subgroup
  generated by the commutator map (h₁, h₂) ↦ [h₁, h₂] : H₁ × H₂ → G", where
  `[h₁, h₂] = h₁h₂h₁⁻¹h₂⁻¹`.
- Theorem 5.39 (homomorphism theorem), p.108: every homomorphism `φ: G → H`
  factors as `G → I → H` with the first map faithfully flat and the second a
  closed immersion; in the proof, `I = G/N` with `N = Ker(φ)`.
- Chapter 14 opening, p.279: "Recall that all algebraic groups are affine
  over a base field k."
- 6.45, p.135: "An algebraic group G is said to be unipotent if every nonzero
  representation of G has a nonzero fixed vector."

The 2022 corrected reprint (source ID `milne-2022-algebraic-groups`; the
one-based PDF page is the printed page plus 11) states Proposition 14.21 on
printed p.284 as "Every unipotent algebraic group has a central normal series
whose quotients are isomorphic to algebraic subgroups of Ga. In particular,
every unipotent algebraic group is nilpotent ...", with "has" in place of
"admits".

The official errata snapshots `milne-2017-algebraic-groups-errata-2026-11-02`
(2017 edition) and `milne-2022-algebraic-groups-errata-2026-11-04` (2022
reprint) were checked. Neither has an entry for printed pp.274–316. The 2017
errata have no entry for pp.108, 124, 131, 133 or 135. The 2022 errata have
a p.108 entry on Remark 5.40 (a misprinted `q`), not on Theorem 5.39.

Milne's proof embeds `G` in the unipotent upper triangular group `Uₙ`, which
has a central series whose quotients are canonically isomorphic to `Ga`
(6.49), and intersects that series with `G` (6.2). This is explanation only.
The statement does not depend on that route.

## Derivation from the source

- **Algebraic group.** Milne's algebraic group is a group scheme of finite
  type over `k`, affine by the Chapter 14 convention. The statement assumes
  `LocallyOfFiniteType G.X.hom` and `IsAffineHom G.X.hom`; an affine morphism
  is quasi-compact, so `G` is of finite type over `k`.
- **The series.** The algebraic subgroups `Gⱼ` are presented by
  closed-immersion homomorphisms `i j : H j ⟶ G` (conditions 1–4). The
  closed subgroups `H j` are affine and of finite type over `k`
  automatically.
- **Central.** By Remark 6.35 the series is central exactly when
  `[G, Gⱼ] ⊂ Gⱼ₊₁` for all `j`. By Definition 6.24, `[G, Gⱼ]` is the
  algebraic subgroup generated by the commutator map `G × Gⱼ → G`, so
  `[G, Gⱼ] ⊂ Gⱼ₊₁` holds exactly when this map factors through the closed
  subgroup `Gⱼ₊₁`. By the Yoneda lemma this is condition 6 for all schemes
  `T` over `k`. Condition 6 also gives normality of each `Gⱼ` in `G`: for
  `j ≥ 1` and `x ∈ Gⱼ(T) ⊆ Gⱼ₋₁(T)`, `g x g⁻¹ = (g x g⁻¹ x⁻¹) x ∈ Gⱼ(T)`.
  So the series is a central normal series in Milne's sense.
- **Quotients isomorphic to algebraic subgroups of `Ga`.** Condition 5 says
  that `Gⱼ₊₁` is the kernel of `f j : Gⱼ → 𝔾ₐ` (Milne's kernel is the fibre
  product with the identity section). By the homomorphism theorem 5.39, `f j`
  then induces an isomorphism of `Gⱼ/Gⱼ₊₁` onto its image, an algebraic
  subgroup of `Ga`. Conversely, an isomorphism of `Gⱼ/Gⱼ₊₁` with an
  algebraic subgroup of `Ga`, composed with the quotient map and the
  inclusion into `Ga`, is a homomorphism `Gⱼ → Ga` with kernel `Gⱼ₊₁`. So
  condition 5 is equivalent to Milne's condition on the quotients.
- **The additive group.** Milne's `Ga` is `Spm(k[T])` with the group law
  `(R, +)`, which is `VectorGroup.groupScheme (CommRingCat.of k) 1`.
- **Unipotent.** Milne's definition is 6.45, which is `IsUnipotent`; its
  comparison is recorded in [`Reductive.Basic`](../Reductive/Basic.md).
- **Scope.** The second sentence of 14.21 (unipotent groups are nilpotent,
  hence solvable) is not part of this statement. A series as in the
  conclusion is a nilpotent series in the sense of Definition 6.34.
- **Universes.** One universe `u` is used for `k` and all group schemes,
  including the subgroups `H j`. Closed subschemes of `G` can be represented
  in universe `u`. This is a formalization convention.
