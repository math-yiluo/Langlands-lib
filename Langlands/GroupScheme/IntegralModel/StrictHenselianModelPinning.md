# Split integral pinnings of the generic fibre of a strictly Henselian reductive model

Module `Langlands.GroupScheme.IntegralModel.StrictHenselianModelPinning`.

## Provenance

These are internal helper lemmas for the proof of
`Langlands.GroupScheme.IntegralModel.UnramifiedSpecialStabilizerStatement`. They do not formalize a
numbered result of a source. Mathematically they say that the integral points `𝒢(R)` of a split
reductive model `𝒢` over a strictly Henselian valuation ring `R` form the "Chevalley group"
subgroup of a split integral pinning of the generic fibre. This group is generated by the bounded
torus points and the integral root-group points. This is the input that Bruhat–Tits II,
§4.6.28–4.6.31 use for the special point attached to `𝒢`. The section on generic roots
adapts a draft by Worker 64.

## Transport along an isomorphism of the ambient group

The ambient group is `G` over a field `k` or a ring `R`, and `Φ : G ≅ H` is an isomorphism of
group schemes.

* `IsNormalizingPoint.comp_iso`: a point `n` that normalizes the torus `i` through `σ` gives a
  point `n ≫ Φ` that normalizes `i ≫ Φ` through the same `σ`.
* `IsRootDatumOf.comp_iso`: if `P` is the root datum of `(G, i)`, it is also the root datum of
  `(H, i ≫ Φ)`. The roots agree by `roots_image_of_transport` for the identity of the torus.
  Normalizing points are transported by the previous lemma.
* `LocalSplitCell.IsWeylPoint.comp_iso`: a Weyl point `weylPoint u v` of a coordinate split
  torus `i` acting through `f` gives the Weyl point `weylPoint (u ≫ Φ) (v ≫ Φ)` of `i ≫ Φ`,
  also acting through `f`.

## Generic fibres

Here `R` is a domain with fraction field `K`, and `N : IntegralModel R K H`.

* `genericSplitTorusHom_eq` and `genericRootHom_eq`: the generic split torus map
  `N.genericSplitTorusHom i` and the generic root map `N.genericRootHom u` are the base changes
  to `K` (`splitTorus.baseChangeHom`, `LocalSplitBigCell.baseChangeRoot`) followed by
  `N.genericIso`.
* `fieldRootPoint_genericRootHom`: the `K`-value `c ↦ u_K(c)` of the generic root map is
  `N.genericRootValue u c` (definitional).
* `isMaximalTorus_genericSplitTorusHom` and `isMaximalSplitTorus_genericSplitTorusHom`: if
  `i : 𝔾_m^I → 𝒢` is maximal on every residue-field fibre, then its generic fibre is a maximal
  torus. Since it is split, it is also a maximal split torus. These use
  `splitTorus.isMaximalTorus_baseChangeHom_of_fibrewise` and `IsMaximalTorus.comp_iso`.
* `isRootDatumOf_genericSplitTorusHom`: a fibrewise root datum `P` of `i` is the root datum
  (`fieldRealization K P`) of the generic fibre. This is the field case of
  `IsFibrewiseRootDatumOf` followed by `IsRootDatumOf.comp_iso`.
* `isRootParametrization_genericRootHom` and `isWeylPoint_genericRootHom`: root
  parametrizations and Weyl points of `i` over `R` give root parametrizations and Weyl points
  of the generic fibre. These use `isRootParametrization_baseChangeRoot`,
  `IsRootParametrization.comp_iso`, `IsWeylPoint.baseChange` and `IsWeylPoint.comp_iso`.

## Integral points and a valuation

Assume in addition that `v : Valuation K ℤₘ₀` satisfies `v c ≤ 1 ↔ c ∈ R` for all `c ∈ K`
(hypothesis `hv`: `R` is the valuation ring of `v`).

* `valuation_algebraMap_unit`: units of `R` have valuation `1`.
* `ΓSpecIso_hom_appTop`: restricting a global function of `Spec R` to `Spec K` is the algebra
  map `R → K` (naturality of `Scheme.ΓSpecIso`).
* `fieldRootPoint_genericRootHom_mem_integralPoints_iff`: for a closed root group `u`, the
  value `u_K(c)` lies in `N.integralPoints` exactly when `v c ≤ 1`
  (`genericRootValue_mem_integralPoints_iff` and `hv`).
* `integralPointMap_map_mem_image`: the image in `H(K)` of `i(t)` for `t ∈ 𝔾_m^I(R)` is
  `i_K(t_K)`, where `t_K ∈ 𝔾_m^I(K)` has coordinates of valuation `1`. So `t_K` lies in the
  character-valuation kernel of the coordinate torus (`mem_split_characterValuationKernel`).
  The proof base-changes `t` along `Spec K → Spec R`. `pointsEquiv_baseChangeSectionHom`
  identifies the coordinates of `t_K` with the images of the unit coordinates of `t`.
  `subgroupPointMap_apply` identifies the image.
* `integralPoints_le_closure`: suppose `R` is moreover strictly Henselian and local,
  `N.group` is reductive, and `i` is a closed fibrewise maximal split torus with fibrewise root
  datum `P`, base `b` and root parametrizations `u`. Then `N.integralPoints` is contained in
  the subgroup generated by two sets. One is the `i_K`-images of the bounded points of the
  coordinate torus. The other is the values `u_{α,K}(c)` with `v c ≤ 1`. The proof applies
  `LocalSplitCell.mem_closure_generators` to `𝒢(R)` and maps its generators with
  `N.integralPointMap` (`MonoidHom.map_closure`). Torus generators are handled by the previous
  item. A root generator `u_α(r)` maps to `u_{α,K}(r)` (`integralPointMap_rootPoint`), and
  `v(r) ≤ 1`.

## The pinning

`exists_splitIntegralPinning_genericSplitTorusHom`. Let `R` be a strictly Henselian local
domain with fraction field `K`, and let `R` be the valuation ring of `v`. Let `N` be a
reductive model over `R` of a connected reductive `K`-group `H`. Let `i` be a closed coordinate
split torus of `N.group` that is maximal on every residue-field fibre. Let `P` be a fibrewise
root datum of `i`, `b` a base, `u` root parametrizations, and suppose the simple reflections
have Weyl points `weylPoint (u a) (u (s_a a))`. Assume the torus-integrality condition
`hK_torus` of `SplitIntegralPinning.ofRootParametrizations` for the subgroup
`N.integralPoints`. Then `N.genericSplitTorusHom i` is a monomorphism on underlying schemes.
It also carries a split integral pinning `Pin` (for `v`) with `Pin.K = N.integralPoints`.

Proof: monomorphy follows from `mono_of_isMaximalSplitTorus`. The pinning is
`SplitIntegralPinning.ofRootParametrizations` applied to the generic data above. The root
condition is `fieldRootPoint_genericRootHom_mem_integralPoints_iff`. The closure condition is
`integralPoints_le_closure`. The torus condition is the hypothesis `hK_torus`.
