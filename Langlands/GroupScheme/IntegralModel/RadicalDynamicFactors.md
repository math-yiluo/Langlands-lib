# Integral factors of a represented radical preimage

This internal supporting development specializes the actual quotient-kernel
opposite-cell factorization to a represented unipotent radical. Smoothness and
connectedness of the ambient special fibre remain explicit hypotheses.
It defines and proves `IntegralModel.RadicalDynamicGeneration` at arbitrary
universe `u`; it does not assert a building or root-fixer comparison.

## Statement and actual maps

For a local domain A with fraction field K, an integral model M, a cocharacter
χ over A, and a homomorphism iR representing the unipotent radical of the
special fibre, write

`B = M.specialFibreSubgroupPoints (rationalPoints.map iR).range`.

Thus B consists of the generic images under `M.integralPointMap` of integral
sections whose actual reductions under `M.reduction` lie in the image of iR
on residue-field points. The generic cocharacter is the actual base change
of χ followed by `M.genericIso.hom`.

`RadicalDynamicGeneration` asserts that B is the subgroup generated by its
intersections with the generic opposite-unipotent subgroup, the generic
centralizer, and the generic positive-unipotent subgroup, in that order.
The proposition definition uses no smoothness or connectedness assumptions.
Its proof `radicalDynamicGeneration` assumes M smooth and its special fibre
connected, and assumes `IsUnipotentRadical iR`. These are supporting
hypotheses, not extra premises added to the host's upstairs root-generation
target.

## Integral factors

The proof takes the published pseudo-reductive quotient by the supplied
represented radical. Kernel-representative transport supplies its connected
unipotent kernel, and smoothness descends from the represented smooth kernel.
The actual pointwise kernel of this quotient equals the image of the given
radical representative. Applying the integral quotient-kernel factorization
therefore yields actual integral opposite-unipotent and parabolic factors,
each reducing into the same radical.

`exists_centralizer_unipotent_factors_of_quotient_reduction_eq_one` is a
separate, more general lemma: an integral parabolic section killed after
reduction by any quotient with separated target splits into centralizer and
positive-unipotent factors, both killed by that quotient. It requires neither
ambient smoothness nor connectedness. The integral dynamic decomposition
produces the factors first. Their reductions belong to the corresponding
dynamic subgroups after base change, and their quotient images belong to
the corresponding dynamic subgroups of the quotient. Their product is one.
The centralizer image therefore also lies in the unipotent subgroup as the
inverse of the other image. Uniqueness of limits in the separated quotient
forces it to be one; the other image is consequently one as well.

Applying this lemma to the parabolic factor and to the radical quotient
proves `exists_radical_triple_factors`: the section is a product of three
actual integral factors, each still reducing into the represented radical.
The private auxiliary lemma `SubgroupFunctor.baseChangeSectionHom_mem`
expresses that a section in any subgroup functor remains in the same
subgroup after base change; it follows from precomposition functoriality
and the actual pullback point correspondence. This is a private version of
the published helper in `IntegralRootCentralizerGeneration`, keeping these
general group-scheme imports independent of the building layer.

## Generic generation and provenance

To prove the forward inclusion of `RadicalDynamicGeneration`, choose the
integral section witnessing membership in B and apply the triple
factorization. Base change and functoriality of dynamic subgroups put each
factor's generic image in its prescribed generic dynamic subgroup. The same
integral factor witnesses its membership in B. The generic point map
preserves their product, proving the inclusion in the threefold supremum.
Each of the three intersections lies in B, giving the reverse inclusion.

This is derived internal support using the published integral
quotient-kernel factorization, unipotent-radical quotient,
kernel-representative transport, integral dynamic decomposition, and
base-change and functoriality results. No new translation from original
literature is claimed. The module neither identifies the intersections with
positive root fixers nor constructs a common torus subgroup independent of
the model or facet.
