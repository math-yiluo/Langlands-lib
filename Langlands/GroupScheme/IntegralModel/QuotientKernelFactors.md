# Integral dynamic factors of a special-fibre quotient kernel

This internal result concerns the original integral sections of a smooth affine
model over a local domain. Its special fibre is assumed connected. The specified
map from that special fibre to a smooth connected affine group is smooth and
surjective and has connected unipotent scheme-theoretic kernel.

`dynamicQuotientKernelSubgroup` is generated by the intersections of the kernel of
reduction followed by this map with the original integral negative dynamic
unipotent, dynamic centralizer, and positive dynamic unipotent functors.
`dynamicQuotientKernelSubgroup_eq` identifies it with the whole kernel.
`exists_dynamicFactors_mem_quotientKernel` supplies a stronger ordered
factorization: every section in the kernel is a product `v * z * u` of integral
sections in those three dynamic functors, and each factor is itself killed by the
same reduction and quotient. `quotientKernel_le_of_dynamicFactors` expresses the
result as a bound by any subgroup containing the three intersections.

The proof uses the published integral opposite-cell lifting criterion with the
identity coordinates in the quotient. This gives integral negative-unipotent and
parabolic factors. Their images lie in the corresponding quotient dynamic
functors by base change and functoriality. Because their product is the identity,
the negative image belongs also to the quotient parabolic. The trivial opposite
unipotent/parabolic intersection kills that image and then the parabolic image.
The integral limit of the parabolic factor gives its integral centralizer factor;
the quotient of the parabolic factor by that limit gives the integral positive
factor. In the quotient, uniqueness of limits of the identity kills the
centralizer image and hence the positive image.

This is a derived internal consequence of the existing opposite-cell, dynamic
base-change, functoriality, and limit theorems. It assumes no Henselian property or
closedness of the residue field and imposes no generic reductivity condition.
It retains the actual integral sections and maps throughout.
