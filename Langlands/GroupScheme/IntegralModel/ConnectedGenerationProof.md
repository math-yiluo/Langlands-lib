# Proof of KP Lemma A.4.26: connected generators recover the relative identity component

The theorem `Langlands.GroupScheme.connectedGeneration.{u}` proves exactly
`Langlands.GroupScheme.ConnectedGenerationStatement.{u}`, the affine form of
Kaletha–Prasad, *Bruhat–Tits Theory: A New Approach* (2023), registered as
`kaletha-prasad-2023-bruhat-tits-new-approach`, SHA-256
`05ad9a284a2577b34d1a270228b74dfb28f520561ae7772d8f38dddb78938732`,
Lemma A.4.26, printed pp. 631–632 / PDF pp. 662–663. The statement, its
hypotheses and the comparison with the source are explained in
`Langlands/GroupScheme/IntegralModel/ConnectedGeneration.md`. This module
leaves that statement and its imports unchanged.

The proof is assembled from these published Library results:

- `Langlands.GroupScheme.connectedGenerationField`, a proof of
  `ConnectedGenerationFieldStatement`. This is the field step: over an
  algebraically closed field, finitely many geometrically connected smooth
  affine groups whose images generate a finite-index subgroup of `G(k)`
  generate exactly `G⁰(k)`.
- `Langlands.GroupScheme.IntegralModel.henselianCongruenceLifting`, a proof
  of `HenselianCongruenceLiftingStatement`. Over a Henselian local ring,
  reduction modulo `𝔪ᵐ` is surjective on the sections of a smooth group
  scheme, for every positive `m`. This is KP Lemma 8.1.3.
- `Langlands.GroupScheme.IntegralModel.greenbergPointModel`, a proof of
  `Langlands.GroupScheme.IntegralModel.GreenbergPointModelStatement`. For
  a DVR `R` with algebraically closed residue field `k`, a positive level
  `m` and a smooth affine group `X`, it gives a smooth affine `k`-group `G`
  and an isomorphism `eX : X(R/𝔪ᵐ) ≃* G(k)`. For each smooth affine `Z` and
  homomorphism `h : Z ⟶ X` it gives a smooth affine `k`-group `G_Z`, an
  isomorphism `eZ : Z(R/𝔪ᵐ) ≃* G_Z(k)` and a homomorphism `g : G_Z ⟶ G`
  compatible with reduction of `h`. `G_Z` is geometrically connected when
  `Z` is. This is the point-level content of KP Theorem A.4.19 used in the
  proof of A.4.26. Its published proof combines the equal and mixed
  characteristic cases through `greenbergPointModel_of_characteristicCases`.
- The relative identity component tools
  `RelativeIdentityComponent.smoothExistence`,
  `RelativeIdentityComponent.smoothDvrAffineness` and
  `IsRelativeIdentityComponent.geometricallyConnected`, together with
  `ConnectedGeneration.generatedImages_le_componentRange` and
  `ConnectedGeneration.eq_componentRange_of_reduction` from the target
  module.

## Implemented proof

The module first proves the conditional theorem
`connectedGeneration_of_prerequisites`. From the three statements above it
derives `ConnectedGenerationStatement`.
`connectedGeneration_of_greenbergPointModel` then inserts the published
field-step and Henselian-lifting proofs. `connectedGeneration` also
inserts the Greenberg proof.

Fix the data of the statement: a Henselian DVR `R` with algebraically closed
residue field, a smooth affine group `X`, smooth affine geometrically connected
groups `Y j` with homomorphisms `f j : Y j ⟶ X`, and a representative
`i : N ⟶ X` of the relative identity component. Write `Γ` for the subgroup
of `X(R)` generated by the images of the `Y j(R)`. By hypothesis `Γ` has
finite index and contains the congruence kernel at some positive level
`m`. Write `red` for reduction modulo `𝔪ᵐ`, that is,
`baseChangeSectionHom (congruenceBaseMap R m)`.

1. **Greenberg models at level `m`.** The Greenberg statement at `R`, `m`
   and `X` gives `G` and `eX`. Applying its functorial clause to each
   `f j` gives `G_j`, `e_j` and `g_j : G_j ⟶ G`, with every `G_j`
   geometrically connected.
2. **The reduced generated subgroup.** `eX (red Γ)` equals the subgroup of
   `G(k)` generated by the images of the `g_j`. Taking subgroup maps
   through the supremum, it suffices to compare one generator. The
   naturality `baseChangeSectionHom_naturality` and the Greenberg
   compatibility identify `eX ∘ red ∘ f j` with `g_j ∘ e_j ∘ red`. Here
   `red` for `Y j` is surjective by Henselian lifting and `e_j` is
   bijective, so precomposition does not change the range
   (`ConnectedGeneration.range_comp_of_surjective`).
3. **Finite index on the special fibre.** `eX ∘ red` is surjective on
   `X(R)` by Henselian lifting for `X`. A surjective homomorphism maps a
   finite-index subgroup to a finite-index subgroup, so the field generated
   subgroup has finite index in `G(k)`.
4. **Field step.** `smoothExistence` gives a relative identity component
   representative `i₀ : G⁰ ⟶ G` over `Spec k`. The field statement
   identifies the subgroup generated by the `g_j` with the range of `i₀`
   on `k`-points.
5. **The identity component of `X`.** `smoothDvrAffineness` shows that
   `N` is affine over `Spec R`. A representative from `smoothExistence`
   is geometrically connected, and
   `IsRelativeIdentityComponent.geometricallyConnected` transfers this to
   `N`. `N` is smooth because `i` is an open immersion into the smooth `X`.
   Applying the Greenberg functorial clause to `i` gives a geometrically
   connected `G_N` and `g_N : G_N ⟶ G`. Its image on `k`-points lies in
   the range of `i₀`, by `generatedImages_le_componentRange` for the
   one-element family `g_N`.
6. **Reduction of `X⁰(R)`.** For `y ∈ N(R)`, Greenberg compatibility for
   `i` writes `eX (red (i y))` as `g_N (e_N (red y))`. This lies in the
   range of `i₀`, which by steps 2 and 4 is `eX (red Γ)`. Injectivity of
   `eX` gives `red (i y) ∈ red Γ`. Hence `red (X⁰(R)) ⊆ red Γ`.
7. **Conclusion.** `eq_componentRange_of_reduction` turns this inclusion,
   together with the level-`m` kernel hypothesis, into
   `Γ = range (rationalPoints.map i)`.

## Comparison with the source proof

KP's proof (printed p. 632 / PDF p. 663) first places `U = Γ` inside
`X⁰(o)`. Since `U` is open of finite index, it is closed, so they show
density: `U` surjects onto `X⁰(o/𝔪ⁿ)` for every `n > 0`. They identify the
image `Uₙ` with the subgroup of `Grₙ(X⁰)(f)` generated by the
`Grₙ(Yᵢ)(f)`, using smoothness of the `Yᵢ` to lift points. Then they apply
[Bor91, Corollary 1.4(a)] and [Bor91, Proposition 2.2] to see that `Uₙ` is
closed, connected and of finite index, hence equal to `Grₙ(X⁰)(f)`.

The implementation keeps the ingredients but changes the bookkeeping:

- It works at the single level `m` from the openness hypothesis, not at
  every level `n`. `eq_componentRange_of_reduction` replaces the
  closedness and density argument: once `red (X⁰(R)) ⊆ red Γ` at level
  `m`, the kernel hypothesis gives equality.
- The first inclusion `U ⊆ X⁰(o)` is the published
  `generatedImages_le_componentRange`.
- Point lifting is KP Lemma 8.1.3 (`henselianCongruenceLifting`).
- The Borel results are packaged in `ConnectedGenerationFieldStatement` and
  its published proof `connectedGenerationField`. The proof applies it to
  the level-`m` model `G` of `X` (the role of `Grₘ(X)`), not to a model of
  `X⁰`. It then compares with the image of the model `G_N` of `N` (the role
  of `Grₘ(X⁰)`) under `g_N`. This avoids the source's identification of
  `Grₘ(X)⁰` with `Grₘ(X⁰)`.
- Only the point models from the Greenberg statement are used, not the
  Greenberg functor or its universal property.

The theorem depends only on the axioms `propext`, `Classical.choice` and
`Quot.sound`.
