# Extension of homomorphisms to smooth integral models

The theorem `Langlands.GroupScheme.IntegralModel.smoothHomExtension` proves
exactly the published proposition `SmoothHomExtensionStatement.{u}`. The base
`R : Type u` is a Henselian discrete valuation domain with separably closed
residue field, and `K : Type u` is its specified fraction field. For integral
models `M` of `G` and `N` of `H`, smoothness of `M` and preservation of their
actual integral-point images by `f : G ⟶ H` imply `Nonempty (M.Hom N f)`.
This is a homomorphism of the supplied group-scheme models with exactly the
generic compatibility required by `M.Hom N f`. No smoothness, reducedness or
connectedness of the target is assumed.

The source is Kaletha–Prasad, *Bruhat–Tits Theory: A New Approach* (2023),
Remark 2.10.3, Lemma 2.10.9, Corollary 2.10.10 and the second part of Remark
2.10.19, printed pp. 122–124 and 127 / PDF pp. 153–155 and 158. The registered
source `kaletha-prasad-2023-bruhat-tits-new-approach` has SHA-256

## Detecting divisibility by integral sections

Three private algebra lemmas implement the coordinate-ring argument of
Lemma 2.10.9. First, `nonempty_algHom_of_smooth` converts the published
existence of a rational point on a nontrivial smooth algebra over a separably
closed field into an algebra homomorphism to that field.

For a smooth `R`-algebra `A`, a uniformizer `π`, and `a : A`, the lemma
`uniformizer_dvd_of_section_values` proves that if `π` divides `σ a` for every
`R`-algebra homomorphism `σ : A →ₐ[R] R`, then the image of `π` divides `a`
in `A`. Write `k` for the residue field and consider `b = 1 ⊗ a` in the
smooth special-fibre algebra `B = k ⊗[R] A`. This algebra is reduced by the
published theorem `SmoothReducedProof.isReduced_of_smooth_of_domain`.
If `b` were nonzero, the localization of `B` away from `b` would be
nontrivial: a zero localization would make `b` nilpotent. The localization
is smooth over `k`, so the first lemma gives a `k`-point where `b` is a unit.
Its restriction to `A` lifts to an `R`-section by
`HenselianSections.exists_lift_of_smooth`. The assumed divisibility makes
the residue of the value of `a` zero, contradicting that unit value.
Consequently `b = 0`. The standard quotient/tensor equivalence identifies
this with membership of `a` in the ideal generated by the image of `π`,
which is the claimed divisibility.

## Clearing generic denominators

The lemma `tensor_mem_range_of_integral_evaluations` proves the following
algebraic form of Lemma 2.10.9. If `z : K ⊗[R] A` has values in the image of
`R → K` under every evaluation induced by an integral section, then `z`
belongs to the image of `A → K ⊗[R] A`.

`IsLocalizedModule.surj` clears a denominator, giving a nonzero `s : R` with
`s • z = 1 ⊗ a`. The DVR factorization of `s` into a unit and a power of `π`
reduces this to `π^n • z = 1 ⊗ a`. Induction on `n` proves the result.
At the successor step, integral values of `z` imply that every section
value of `a` is divisible by `π`. The preceding lemma gives
`a = π • a'`. Since `π` is invertible in `K`, multiplication by its image
on the generic tensor algebra can be cancelled, reducing the exponent.
The zero exponent gives the required preimage directly. This implements
the source's denominator argument by induction rather than choosing a
minimal denominator.

## Passing to actual model coordinates

The public supporting theorem
`genericCoordinate_mem_range_of_integral_values` states the sufficient
coordinate-integrality criterion for a smooth supplied model `M`: a generic
regular function whose values at all points of `M.integralPoints` lie in
the image of `R → K` belongs to the range of `M.genericCoordinate`.
The opposite implication is already supplied by
`mem_integralPoints_iff_genericCoordinates`.

The proof gives `A = AffineCoordinateRing R M.group.X` its smooth algebra
structure using the affine coordinate spectrum identification. It identifies
`K ⊗[R] A` with the coordinate ring of `G` by the base-change coordinate
equivalence followed by the specified generic isomorphism. On `1 ⊗ a`,
this equivalence is exactly `M.genericCoordinate a`.

Every algebra section `A →ₐ[R] R` gives an actual integral section of `M`.
`genericModelPointEquiv_evaluation`,
`genericModelPointEquiv_integralPointMap`, and naturality under change of
coefficients identify tensor evaluation with evaluation at the section's
generic point. The tensor lemma therefore applies, and the coordinate
equivalence transfers its preimage back to an original model coordinate.

## Extending the given homomorphism

For `b` in the coordinate ring of `N`, the final proof tests the generic
pullback `f* (N.genericCoordinate b)` at an integral point `g` of `M`.
`GaloisPointComparison.rationalCoeffEquiv_map` and
`GroupScheme.affinePointEvaluation_postcomp` identify this value with
evaluation of `N.genericCoordinate b` at `rationalPoints.map f g`.
That point is integral by hypothesis; the published coordinate test for
integral points makes the value integral. The supporting criterion above
places the generic pullback in the range of `M.genericCoordinate`.

The companion module `AffineHomExtension` then supplies
`nonempty_hom_of_genericCoordinates M N f`. Its paired Markdown describes
the affine spectrum construction and reflection of the unit and
multiplication identities from the generic fibre, using flatness of the
unit and the square of the source and separatedness of the target.
This yields the required actual group-scheme homomorphism and its specified
generic compatibility.

The proof covers the strictly Henselian statement only. It does not prove
`UnramifiedSmoothHomExtensionStatement`; that requires the separate
coefficient descent argument. The original statement module and all its
imports are unchanged.
