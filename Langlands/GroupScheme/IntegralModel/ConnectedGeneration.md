## Source and scope

The source works over a Henselian discrete valuation ring `o`, with residue
field `f`. Lemma A.4.26 assumes `f` is algebraically closed. Let
`X, Y₁, …, Yₙ` be finite-type group schemes over `o`, with `X` smooth and
each `Yᵢ` smooth with connected fibres. If the images of the groups
`Yᵢ(o)` generate an open subgroup of finite index in `X(o)`, the lemma
identifies that subgroup with `X⁰(o)`.

`ConnectedGenerationStatement.{u}` records the affine specialization:
`X` and all the `Y j` are affine over `Spec R`. Smooth affine morphisms
are of finite type, so the source's finite-type assumptions are retained.
The source connectedness condition is expressed by
`GeometricallyConnected (Y j).X.hom`. This explicitly requires geometric
connectedness of every fibre, which implies the source's connectedness
condition. No completeness assumption on `R` is introduced.

The finite family is indexed by `Fin n`, including `n = 0`. All rings
and schemes have arbitrary universe `u`; no restriction to small
coefficient rings occurs. The target and the component representative
are actual group schemes, and each `f j` and `i` is an actual homomorphism.

## Actual subgroups

`ConnectedGeneration.generatedImages f` is the supremum of the subgroup
ranges of `rationalPoints.map (f j)`. Over the base `Spec R`,
`rationalPoints X` is precisely the group of sections `X(R)`.
Consequently this supremum is the subgroup generated by the indicated
images of the groups `Y j(R)`.

`ConnectedGeneration.sectionCongruenceKernel X m` is the kernel of
`baseChangeSectionHom (IntegralModel.congruenceBaseMap R m) X`.
The coefficient map is the actual quotient
`R → R / (maximalIdeal R)^m`, and its spectrum map and group-scheme
pullback are the Library's existing congruence construction. Thus this
kernel consists exactly of integral sections reducing to the identity
modulo that power of the maximal ideal.

The openness hypothesis is expressed by the existence of a positive
`m` whose congruence kernel is contained in the generated subgroup.
The congruence kernels define the usual congruence topology on sections:
their cosets give the neighbourhoods used in the source proof's density
argument. The statement does not install a `TopologicalSpace` or assert
a separate comparison theorem with a point topology.

The hypothesis `FiniteIndex` is for the generated subgroup in all of
`X(R)`, as in the source. The conclusion equates it with the literal
range of the section homomorphism of every supplied
`i : N ⟶ X` satisfying `IsRelativeIdentityComponent X i`.
That predicate means that `i` is an open immersion representing the
all-test fibre-image criterion for the relative identity component.
This is the meaning of `X⁰(R)` and leaves no abstract subgroup witness
in place of the representing group scheme.

## Proved preliminary inclusion and remaining proof

`ConnectedGeneration.generatedImages_le_componentRange` proves the
first inclusion for any indexed family with geometrically connected
source fibres. The published geometric connectedness helper applies
to each homomorphism and its identity section. Restricting the resulting
relative-component criterion along any section of `Y j` gives a
factorization through `i` by `IsRelativeIdentityComponent.factors_iff`.
Taking subgroup suprema gives the inclusion. This preliminary result
requires neither smoothness nor affineness.

`le_of_reduction_le` proves an elementary lifting step. If `U` contains
the kernel of reduction at one positive level `m`, and the reduction
image of `P` is contained in that of `U`, take `x ∈ P` and choose
`y ∈ U` with the same reduction. The element `x * y⁻¹` lies in the
kernel and therefore in `U`, so `x` lies in `U`.
`eq_componentRange_of_reduction` combines this with the proved
connected-source inclusion. These are internal algebraic consequences
of the actual section homomorphisms, independent of Greenberg theory.

`ConnectedGenerationStatement` is an unproved proposition. Its reverse
inclusion requires the substantive argument of Lemma A.4.26: Henselian
lifting to finite congruence levels, Greenberg groups with connected
fibres, and the fact that the subgroup generated by connected algebraic
subgroup images is closed and connected. Finite index then forces the
image to be the full connected congruence-level group, and the openness
hypothesis recovers the equality on integral sections. Finite index
and congruence openness alone do not establish the conclusion without
the connected-generator hypotheses.

This general lemma is intended for the corrected proof of KP Theorem
8.3.13. It does not construct connected torus or root models or
identify their generated subgroup with a building stabilizer.
