# Connected generators of finite index over an algebraically closed field

`Langlands.GroupScheme.ConnectedGenerationFieldStatement.{u}` is the
field-level step in the proof of Kaletha–Prasad Lemma A.4.26. It is a
prerequisite for `Langlands.GroupScheme.ConnectedGenerationStatement`
(module `Langlands.GroupScheme.IntegralModel.ConnectedGeneration`). It is
an unproved proposition; this module proves nothing.

## Statement

Let `k` be an algebraically closed field in universe `u`. Let `G` be a
group scheme over `Spec k` whose structure morphism is affine and smooth.
Let `n : ℕ` and let `Y : Fin n → GroupScheme (Spec k)` be a finite family
whose structure morphisms are affine, smooth and geometrically connected.
Let `f j : Y j ⟶ G` be homomorphisms of group schemes.

Suppose the subgroup `ConnectedGeneration.generatedImages f` of
`rationalPoints G = G(k)` has finite index. That subgroup is the supremum of
the ranges of the point maps `rationalPoints.map (f j) : Y j (k) → G(k)`,
that is, the subgroup generated by the images of the groups `Y j (k)`.
The proposition asserts that the subgroup then equals
`(rationalPoints.map i).range` for every group scheme `N` over `Spec k`
and every homomorphism `i : N ⟶ G` satisfying
`IsRelativeIdentityComponent G i`. Over a field, such an `i` is an open
immersion onto the identity component `G⁰`, so the conclusion says that
the generated subgroup is exactly `G⁰(k)`.

The family may be empty (`n = 0`). In that case the hypothesis says that
`G(k)` is finite.

## Provenance

This is a derived statement: it is not a numbered result of the source. It
isolates the last paragraph of the proof of Lemma A.4.26 in Kaletha–Prasad,
*Bruhat–Tits Theory: A New Approach* (2023), source
`kaletha-prasad-2023-bruhat-tits-new-approach`, SHA-256
`05ad9a284a2577b34d1a270228b74dfb28f520561ae7772d8f38dddb78938732`,
printed p. 632 / PDF p. 663. There the authors take the subgroup `Uₙ` of
`Grₙ(X⁰)(f)` generated by the groups `Grₙ(Yᵢ)(f)`. By Borel, Corollary
1.4(a), each image of `Grₙ(Yᵢ) → Grₙ(X⁰)` is Zariski closed and connected.
By Borel, Proposition 2.2, `Uₙ` is therefore a closed connected subgroup.
Since it has finite index, it equals `Grₙ(X)⁰(f)`.

The cited results are in Borel, *Linear Algebraic Groups*, second enlarged
edition (1991), source `borel-1991-linear-algebraic-groups-second`,
SHA-256 `7c62e6b8fdb26c0490a3cce4afbd8c9d0c02fb90a1f03e21699bd21ecdfca16e`:

- Proposition 1.2(c), printed p. 46 / PDF p. 57, proved on printed p. 47:
  every closed subgroup of finite index contains `G⁰`.
- Corollary 1.4(a), printed p. 47 / PDF p. 58: the image of a morphism of
  algebraic groups is a closed subgroup.
- Proposition 2.2, printed p. 57 / PDF p. 68: the subgroup generated by a
  family of irreducible varieties through `e` is closed and connected.
  It is a product of finitely many of them.

Kaletha–Prasad apply the step to the Greenberg groups `Grₙ(X⁰)` and
`Grₙ(Yᵢ)`. The proposition states it for an arbitrary smooth affine
`k`-group `G` and a finite family of smooth affine geometrically connected
`k`-groups. It asks for finite index in all of `G(k)`, which is the form the
reduction of the integral hypothesis provides. It needs no Greenberg
functor and no discrete valuation ring. Over an algebraically closed field,
geometric connectedness of `Y j` is the same as connectedness. Affine smooth
groups over a field are linear algebraic groups in Borel's sense, and their
`k`-points are dense.

## Representation choices

- Points are the Library's `rationalPoints`: sections of the structure
  morphism over `Spec k`, with the pointwise group law. The images and the
  generated subgroup are subgroups of this abstract group. No Zariski
  topology on `G(k)` occurs in the statement. Closedness and connectedness
  are steps of the expected proof.
- `G⁰` is represented as in the target statement, by every supplied
  `i : N ⟶ G` with `IsRelativeIdentityComponent G i`. No subgroup witness
  stands in for the representing group scheme.
- All objects live in the arbitrary universe `u`.
