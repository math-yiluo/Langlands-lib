# The full-centre quotient of a connected reductive group has trivial centre

Module `Langlands.GroupScheme.Adjoint.CenterQuotientCenterProof`, author
Worker 4289.

## Theorem

`Langlands.GroupScheme.centerQuotientCenterTrivial :
CenterQuotientCenterTrivialStatement.{u}` proves the published proposition
`CenterQuotientCenterTrivialStatement` of module
`Langlands.GroupScheme.Adjoint.CenterQuotientStatement`, for every universe
`u`. Let `k : Type u` be any field, `G` and `Q` group schemes over
`Spec k` and `q : G ⟶ Q` a homomorphism. Assume that `G` is connected
reductive (`IsConnectedReductive G`), that `Q.X.hom` is affine and locally of
finite type, and that `RelativeRoot.IsFppfQuotient (center G) q` holds: `q`
is flat, surjective and locally of finite presentation, and the range of its
actual kernel inclusion is the full scheme-theoretic centre `center G` on
every test scheme. Then `center Q = ⊥`: on every test scheme `T` over
`Spec k`, the only point of `Q` over `T` that is central in the all-test
sense of `Langlands.GroupScheme.center` is the identity point.

No assumption is added. The field may have any characteristic and need not be
perfect. The centres of `G` and `Q` are the full scheme-theoretic centres,
which may be nonsmooth, and `q` need not be finite. The hypotheses that
`Q.X.hom` is affine and locally of finite type are not used by the proof.

## Proof

The proof is a composition of three published results. It has two halves,
joined by the property `SecondCenterTrivial G` (module
`Langlands.GroupScheme.Adjoint.SecondCenter`). That property says: if a point
`g` of `G` over `T` has every commutator `(a ≫ g) * h * (a ≫ g)⁻¹ * h⁻¹`, for
every `a : T' ⟶ T` and every point `h` of `G` over `T'`, in `center G`, then
`g` lies in `center G`.

1. *Descent half.*
   `CenterQuotientCenter.centerQuotientCenterTrivial_of_secondCenterTrivial`
   (module `Langlands.GroupScheme.Adjoint.CenterQuotientDescent`) reduces
   the target to the claim that every connected reductive group over a field
   has trivial second centre. In that module, a central point `z` of `Q` over
   `T` lifts to a point `g` of `G` over the fppf cover `T ×_Q G` of `T`. The
   commutators of `g` are killed by `q`, so they lie in the kernel, which is
   `center G`. Trivial second centre makes `g` central, so `g ≫ q = 1`.
   Since the cover is an epimorphism, `z = 1`.
2. *Group-theoretic half.*
   `SecondCenter.secondCenterTrivial_of_centralDerivedProduct` (module
   `Langlands.GroupScheme.Adjoint.SecondCenterReductive`) proves
   `SecondCenterTrivial G` for every connected reductive `G` over a field
   `k : Type u`. It takes `ReductiveCentralDerivedProductStatement.{u}` as an
   explicit hypothesis. That statement provides a closed central torus
   `i : Z ⟶ G` and a derived subgroup `d : D ⟶ G` whose product map
   `Z × D ⟶ G` is an epimorphism in `Over (Spec k)`. For a point `g` as
   above, `x ↦ x g x⁻¹ g⁻¹` is a homomorphism into the centre. Hence it kills
   every commutator, so the derived subgroup, which is schematically
   generated by commutators. It also kills the central torus, and so all of
   `G`.
3. *The decomposition.* The hypothesis of step 2 is discharged by the
   published proof `reductiveCentralDerivedProduct :
   ReductiveCentralDerivedProductStatement.{u}` (module
   `Langlands.GroupScheme.Reductive.CentralDerivedProductProof`).

So the Lean term is
`centerQuotientCenterTrivial_of_secondCenterTrivial` applied to
`fun k _ G hG => secondCenterTrivial_of_centralDerivedProduct
reductiveCentralDerivedProduct k G hG`. Every intermediate result is a
published, proved Library theorem; none is a premise of the theorem. The
theorem depends only on the axioms `propext`, `Classical.choice` and
`Quot.sound`.

## Source

J. S. Milne, *Algebraic Groups* (2017), source ID
`milne-2017-algebraic-groups`, Corollary 17.62(e), printed p.372 / PDF p.388,
with its proof on printed p.373 / PDF p.389: for a reductive group `G`, the
quotient `G/Z(G)` has trivial centre. The Lean statement is the derived
packaging recorded in `CenterQuotientStatement.md`.

The Lean route differs from Milne's. Milne takes the inverse image `Z'` of
the centre of `G/Z(G)` in `G`. He applies Corollary 12.41 (printed p.244 /
PDF p.260; the errata snapshot `milne-2017-algebraic-groups-errata-2026-11-02`
requires `N` central, which holds for `N = Z(G)` in `Z'`) to show that `G`
acts trivially on `Z'`, so `Z' = Z(G)`. That argument uses the
multiplicative-type structure of `Z(G)` and of the centre of `G/Z(G)`
(17.62(a)). Here the inverse image is not formed: step 1 is fppf descent of a
single central point, and step 2 replaces 12.41 by the decomposition of `G`
into a central torus and its derived subgroup (Milne 2017, Proposition 21.61
with 12.46(b), as recorded in the Markdown of
`ReductiveCentralDerivedProductStatement`).
