# Faithfully flat graph projections and reductivity of the actual graph

This internal support module constructs the actual simple-root Levi comparison graph as a connected reductive group over an algebraically closed field and supplies the faithfully flat geometry of both projections. It does not assert that either projection is an isomorphism. The primary construction retains the split common torus, both realizing root data and the local comparison hypothesis; it does not assume a global comparison homomorphism or isomorphism.

Throughout, group schemes are actual group schemes over `Spec k`, with `k : Type u` a field. The datum index universes `v` and `w` are independent of `u` and of each other. All containment and normality assertions for subgroup functors apply to every scheme test. Literal functorial range is distinguished from surjectivity of the underlying scheme morphism: faithfully flat surjectivity alone is not asserted to lift points on their original test scheme.

## A projection criterion over every field

`projection_geometry_of_contains_simpleRootLevis` concerns a homomorphism `p : D ⟶ G` between smooth connected affine group schemes. It assumes that `i : T.toGrp ⟶ G` is closed, `T` is a split torus, a root datum `P` realizes `i`, and a cocharacter `ν` of `T` is generic for the actual roots. The range of `p` must contain the entire intrinsic root functor for every simple root for `ν`, every opposite simple root functor, and the entire dynamic centralizer of `ν ≫ i`. It concludes that the underlying scheme morphism of `p` is flat, surjective, locally of finite presentation and quasi-compact. This criterion holds over every field and needs no reductivity assumption.

The proof uses the published smooth connected homomorphism image factorization `p = q ≫ j`, with `j` a closed inclusion and `q` faithfully flat onto its smooth connected affine image. Every supplied containment passes from the range of `p` to the range of `j`. The earlier `isIso_of_simpleRootLevis` makes `j` an isomorphism. Composing `q` with this isomorphism gives the four claimed geometric properties. The proof uses simple-root generation only for the closed image inclusion, and does not infer that a nonclosed projection has full literal functorial range.

## Normal unipotent images over every field

`normal_unipotent_comp_eq_one` takes an epimorphism of underlying schemes `p : D ⟶ C`, where `D` is affine and `C` is smooth affine with trivial unipotent radical. It takes a closed inclusion `i : U ⟶ D` with `U` smooth, connected and unipotent, and with normal all-test range in `D`. It proves that the underlying morphism `i ≫ p` is the constant identity morphism. No finite-kernel assumption, smoothness or connectedness of `D`, or algebraic closedness is required for this auxiliary result.

This extracts the normal-image part of the earlier `HasTrivialUnipotentRadical.of_epi_of_finite_kernel_algClosed` argument, without that theorem's later finite-kernel step. The pointed image envelope of `U ⟶ C` is smooth, connected and affine. The factor map onto it is dominant and schematically dominant because the envelope is reduced. Injective pullback on functions descends unipotence to this image. Normality of `U` gives a conjugation action of `D` on it. Equivariance makes the image envelope stable under the corresponding action on `C`. The epimorphic range of `p` lies in its closed-representable normalizer, so that normalizer is all of `C`. Thus the image is normal in `C`. Triviality of the unipotent radical of `C` kills it, and therefore kills the original composite. This argument uses the published image-envelope, action and normalizer results, including their scheme-level hypotheses.

## The two actual graph projections

Let `D : SimpleComparisonGraph i₁ i₂`, with `G₁`, `G₂` smooth affine group schemes and common commutative group scheme `T`. This record includes splitness of `T`, its chosen generic chamber and simple-root directions, the actual local Levi comparison isomorphisms and root maps, and the generated graph envelope in `G₁ × G₂`.

The four results `positive_le_range_first`, `negative_le_range_first`, `positive_le_range_second`, and `negative_le_range_second` put the full corresponding ambient root functors in the actual projection ranges. They use the previously proved representations by the composites of the actual graph root maps with those projections. The two `torus_le_range_*` results use the actual common torus lift, including when there are no simple roots.

`first_geometry` assumes connectedness of `G₁`, a realizing datum `P₁`, closedness of `i₁`, and the explicit all-test identity `Z_G₁(chamber) = range i₁`. It applies the projection criterion to the first actual projection. `second_geometry` assumes connectedness of `G₂`, a realizing datum `P₂`, closedness of `i₂`, its corresponding chamber-centralizer identity, and equality `roots i₁ = roots i₂`. Equality of the actual root sets identifies the intrinsic simple roots for the same chamber and transfers genericity to the second group. This gives the same geometry for the second projection. Both results work over every field.

`first_geometry_of_reductive` replaces the explicit chamber-centralizer identity and closedness by connected reductivity of `G₁` and maximality of `i₁`. `second_geometry_of_reductive` makes the corresponding replacement for `G₂`, retaining equality of the root sets. They use the published `dynamicCentralizerReductive`, `noTorusUnipotent`, `solvableTorusQuotient`, and `genericCentralizer` proofs through the existing maximal-torus centralizer reduction. They retain no unproved structural centralizer premise. These geometry results also work over every field.

## Reductivity and the constructed certified graph

`SimpleComparisonGraph.isConnectedReductive` additionally assumes that `k` is algebraically closed, that both ambient groups are connected reductive, that both inclusions are maximal tori, that both root data realize the inclusions, and that the actual root sets agree. Both actual projections are faithfully flat and surjective by the preceding results, and hence are epimorphisms of schemes. Every smooth connected normal unipotent closed subgroup of the graph maps trivially in each ambient group by `normal_unipotent_comp_eq_one`. Equality of graph-valued maps is detected by the two projections, so the subgroup inclusion itself is trivial. The graph is already smooth, connected and affine. This proves pseudo-reductivity, which is connected reductivity over the assumed algebraically closed field. It does not use the false assertion that an arbitrary subgroup of a reductive product is reductive.

`ReductiveSimpleComparisonGraph` stores an actual `SimpleComparisonGraph`, a proof of connected reductivity of its envelope, and the four geometric properties of each actual projection. The primary entry `reductiveSimpleComparisonGraph hG₁ hG₂ hT hmax₁ hmax₂ P₁ P₂ hP₁ hP₂ hroots hlocal` constructs this record directly. Its assumptions are: an algebraically closed field; two smooth affine connected reductive ambient groups; a common split torus included as a maximal torus in both; realizing root data `P₁`, `P₂`; equality of the actual root sets; and the exact `RankOneLeviHypothesis i₁ i₂` supplying the local comparisons. It first constructs the earlier actual graph from `hT` and `hlocal` and then applies the proved geometry and reductivity results. No graph object or global inter-group homomorphism is an additional premise of this entry.

## Provenance and limits

The construction is an internal derivation from the actual graph root representatives, the published smooth image factorization, the companion simple-root Levi generation theorem, the normal-image portion of the published central-isogeny reductivity argument, and the published structural centralizer proofs. The argument proves reductivity by the two epimorphic projections, as needed for the graph route. No new classification theorem, root parametrization or global uniqueness assertion is used.

Projection invertibility, the remaining root-factor restriction comparisons, exact Levi gluing and the exact arbitrary-field common-torus isomorphism theorem remain separate obligations. The primary graph reductivity construction here is over algebraically closed fields; only the auxiliary projection and normal-image results are claimed over every field.
