# Affine semisimple neighbourhoods from actual root supports

This derived helper supplies the affine local step in the semisimple case of Conrad, *Reductive group schemes* (2014), Proposition 3.1.9(1). It assumes a smooth affine group over an arbitrary affine base, a closed standard split torus, and a point where the geometric identity component is semisimple and the given torus is self-centralizing in that component. No connectedness of the ambient group or its fibres is assumed. The conclusion is an open neighbourhood contained in the actual semisimple identity-component locus, written directly as the set of fibres satisfying `IsSemisimple`. This is definitionally the same set named by `identityComponentSemisimpleLocus`; the helper does not depend on that additional name or on its comparison module.

The definition `geometricExponentRootSpan` is the integral submodule spanned by the actual geometric root support in the common exponent lattice. It is internal derived data with no additional choices.

The proof first combines the published root-datum existence theorem and `splitSemisimpleRootSpan` to eliminate a chosen root datum: semisimplicity of a connected reductive field group with a split maximal torus is equivalent to finiteness of the quotient of its actual character lattice by the integral span of its actual roots. This is the finite-root-index criterion underlying Milne, *Algebraic Groups* (2017), Proposition 21.48.

At each geometric point in the reductive and self-centralizing loci, the given torus in the actual identity component is maximal. The standard torus character-lattice equivalence maps the integral span of `geometricExponentRoots` to the span of those actual roots and induces an equivalence of quotient lattices. Finiteness of the two quotient types is therefore equivalent. This proves the exact membership criterion, retaining the actual scheme-theoretic finite centre in `IsSemisimple` through the published criterion.

Finally, the reductive identity-component locus is open, the self-centralizing locus is open, and the entire actual geometric root support is locally constant. Intersect the first two opens with the fibre of the root-support map through the initial point. On this open the same finite-index quotient criterion holds at every point, giving the stated semisimple neighbourhood. The implementation derives the assertion entirely from the cited published Library results; it introduces no new source assumption.
