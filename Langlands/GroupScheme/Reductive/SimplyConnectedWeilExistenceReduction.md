# Simply connected affine Weil existence from split isomorphism and pair descent

Worker 634, with Worker 632's actual finite-family field products.

Assume the unchanged affine split isomorphism theorem and the generic
almost-simple étale pair-descent statement. For every supplied affine simply
connected pinned model with nontrivial geometric fibres, the module constructs
an actual simply connected finite étale Weil presentation. A final wrapper
constructs such a presentation for every reductive group over an arbitrary
affine base with simply connected nontrivial geometric fibres, by applying the
pinned construction on its actual covering atlas and descending once more.
At each chart the distinguished point gives the integral coroot-generation
criterion needed to turn the chart into a simply connected pinned model.

The relative factors are the previously constructed derived groups of
complementary-torus centralizers. They are reductive with simply connected
almost-simple geometric fibres. On every algebraically closed field-valued
fibre, the actual comparison to the root-generated groups and the actual
finite-family field product give an isomorphism between the original group and
the product of these relative factors. The conditional étale comparison atlas
then yields actual product isomorphisms after affine étale charts. Finite-family
assembly gives local Weil presentations and the supplied pair-descent theorem
gives the presentation over the original affine base.

The proof also establishes arbitrary-base reductivity of finite products and
almost-simple geometric fibres of the actual factors after every base change
under the ambient simply-connected condition. It neither assumes global
commutation of the relative factors nor infers equality of maps from field
fibres. Both structural inputs remain explicit hypotheses.

This is an internal reduction of the existence argument in Conrad,
*Reductive group schemes* (2014), Proposition 6.4.4, pp.232–233, using the
assumed affine split isomorphism theorem (Theorem 6.1.17, p.197).
