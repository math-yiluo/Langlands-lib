# Actual field comparison of component derived centralizers

This internal module develops the comparison between the relative derived
complementary centralizers and the actual field groups generated by Cartan
components. Its general derived criterion says that a closed normal semisimple
subgroup of a connected reductive group over an algebraically closed field is
the full derived subgroup whenever it contains all actual root groups of a
supplied split maximal torus. Root-and-torus generation makes the fppf product
of the subgroup with the torus the whole group. Locally modulo the normal
subgroup, the torus factors commute, so every actual commutator lies in the
subgroup; semisimple perfectness gives the reverse derived containment.

For the supplied relative component centralizers, the normalized full torus
and complementary torus maps are retained explicitly. The centralizer's actual
field roots are exactly the selected integral Cartan component, by the
published split-torus centralizer root comparison and the integral restriction
criterion. All maps refer to the actual scalar extensions and their canonical
split-torus identifications.

Every component root group lies in the actual complementary centralizer, by
its character's vanishing on the complementary torus and the generic
centralizing cocharacter. Root-generated minimality then constructs an actual
lift of the component-generated field group through that centralizer. The lift
is closed, normal and semisimple, and contains all the centralizer's root
groups. The general criterion identifies it with the actual derived subgroup.
Consequently fieldComponentDerivedIso compares it with the field fibre of the
relative derived group, with exact compatibility of ambient inclusions.

If the original field fibre is semisimple over an algebraically closed field,
the relative derived component fibre is therefore almost simple. In particular,
semisimple ambient geometric fibres give almost-simple geometric fibres of
every actual relative candidate component. The module does not assert that
the candidates multiply to an isomorphism over the base ring.

These results are internal consequences of the imported relative centralizer,
fppf product, root-generation and integral component constructions, motivated
by Conrad, *Reductive group schemes* (2014), Proposition 6.4.4, pp.232–233.
