# Normalized global reflection representatives for split reductive groups over rings

Worker 5397.

Module `Langlands.GroupScheme.Reductive.AffineSplitNormalizedRepresentatives`, namespace
`Langlands.GroupScheme.AffineSplitReflection`. It imports
[`Reductive.AffineSplitReflectionRepresentatives`](AffineSplitReflectionRepresentatives.md),
[`Reductive.LocalSplitRootPairLaws`](LocalSplitRootPairLaws.md) and
[`Reductive.LocalSplitRankOneLaws`](LocalSplitRankOneLaws.md).

This is a step toward clause 4 of
[`Reductive.AffineSplitNormalizerWeylStatement`](AffineSplitNormalizerWeylStatement.md)
(Conrad, *Reductive group schemes*, Example 6.4.3): over a domain, the normalizer points contain a
finite subgroup mapping onto the Weyl group. Conrad obtains this subgroup from the Tits group
`W̃`, an extension `1 → T[2] → W̃ → W → 1`. Over `Spec ℤ` he defines `W̃` as the subgroup of
`N_G(T)` generated by the `N_G(T)(ℤ)`-translates of `T[2]`, and notes that its points contain the
elements `n_a = w_a(X_a)` for every root `a` (p.231). This module gives representatives of the
reflections over an arbitrary commutative ring `R`, with two individual relations satisfied by
these `n_a` (below). It does not prove clause 4. It makes no finiteness or generation claim, and
it does not assert that the family is compatible with a pinning.

## The definition `IsNormalizedRepresentatives`

Let `i : 𝔾_m^I ⟶ G` be a split torus of a group scheme `G` over `Spec R` (with `I` finite and
with decidable equality), and let `P : RootDatum J (I → ℤ) (I → ℤ)` be a root datum on the
coordinate lattices. A family `n : J → (Over.mk (𝟙 (Spec R)) ⟶ G.X)` of points of `G(R)`, one
for each root, satisfies `IsNormalizedRepresentatives i P n` when, for every root `a`:

1. `IsWeylPoint i (n a) (P.reflection a)`: for every test scheme `Z` and torus point `t`,
   `n t n⁻¹ = s_a(t)`. Here `s_a` is the torus automorphism with exponent map
   `P.reflection a` (`splitTorus.exponentIso`). By `IsWeylPoint.isNormalizingPoint`, `n a` is
   then a normalizing point of `i`;
2. `n a * n a = cocharPoint (P.coroot a) (𝟙_ _) (-1) ≫ i`. This is the torus point `a^∨(-1)`,
   whose coordinates are `(-1) ^ (a^∨)_j`, mapped into `G` by `i`.

Provenance: derived. The predicate packages two individual relations satisfied by Conrad's
representatives `n_a = w_a(X_a)` (Corollary 5.1.11, p.142): `n_a` normalizes `T` and maps to
the reflection `s_a`, and `n_a² = a^∨(-1)`. It says nothing about the subgroup these points
generate. In particular, the Tits group of Example 6.4.3 also contains `T[2]`, which the `n_a`
need not generate: for `G = T = 𝔾_m` there are no roots, yet `W̃ = T[2] = μ_2`. Over local rings
the Library already proves both relations, as part of `LocalSplitCell.RankOneLaws` (in
[`Reductive.LocalSplitRankOneLaws`](LocalSplitRankOneLaws.md), third component), via
`localSplitRootPairLaws`.

## Theorems

- `units_map_neg_one`: a ring homomorphism sends the unit `-1` to `-1`.
- `weylPoint_mul_self_of_localizations`: let `G` be affine over `Spec R` and let `u, v` be maps
  `𝔾_a ⟶ G`. Suppose that for every prime `p`, the base changes of `u` and `v` to `R_p` satisfy
  `weylPoint u_p v_p * weylPoint u_p v_p = ν(-1)` for the base-changed torus. Then
  `weylPoint u v * weylPoint u v = ν(-1)` over `R`. Proof: both sides are points of the affine
  scheme `G` over the affine scheme `Spec R`. By `over_hom_ext_of_localization` (clause-1 module),
  it suffices to compare them on every test scheme `W` over some `Spec R_p`. There the
  base-change bijection `GroupScheme.baseChangePointsEquiv` carries the local identity to the
  global one. The Library lemmas `baseChangePointsEquiv_constantWeyl`,
  `baseChangePointsEquiv_torus`, `baseChangePointsEquiv_cocharPoint` and `comp_cocharPoint`
  identify the transported Weyl point and the transported point `ν(-1)`.
- `exists_normalized_representatives`: let `G` be reductive over any commutative ring `R`, with
  a closed torus `i` that is maximal on every fibre, a fibrewise root datum `P`, and free root
  spaces. Then some family `n` satisfies `IsNormalizedRepresentatives i P n`. Proof:
  - Let `x_β = LocalRootGroup.rootParametrizationsOfFreeWeights …` be the global root maps.
    Write `-a` for `P.reflectionPerm a a`; its root is `-P.root a`.
  - For each root `a`, the clause-1 theorems `exists_dualFactorization` and
    `exists_isWeylPoint_of_dualFactorization` give a unit `c_a` of `R`. Set
    `n a := weylPoint x_a (c_a · x_{-a}) = x_{-a}(-c_a) x_a(1) x_{-a}(-c_a)`; it is a Weyl point
    for `P.reflection a`.
  - At each prime `p`, the base changes of `x_a` and `c_a · x_{-a}` to the local ring `R_p`
    satisfy the hypotheses of the published `localSplitRootPairLaws`. This uses
    `IsReductive.baseChange`, `IsClosedSubgroup.baseChange`,
    `splitTorus.isFibrewiseMaximalTorus_baseChangeHom`,
    `IsFibrewiseRootDatumOf.baseChange_implicit`, `isRootParametrization_baseChangeRoot`,
    `IsRootParametrization.scale` and `isWeylPoint_baseChange`. Its third rank-one law, on the
    terminal test scheme, gives the square identity over `R_p`.
  - `weylPoint_mul_self_of_localizations` descends the square identity to `R`.
- `cocharPoint_neg_one_mul_self`: the torus point `ν(-1)` squares to `1`.
- `exists_reflection_representatives`: the same family, in the form used for clause 4. Each
  `n a` induces `RootPairing.Equiv.reflection P a`, with the body of the statement's `Induces`
  relation spelled out. The automorphism `σ` is `splitTorus.exponentCommIso _ I (P.reflection a)`.
  The normalizing condition comes from `IsWeylPoint.isNormalizingPoint`, and the character
  identity from `prod_pointsEquiv_exponentCommIso`. Also, `n a * n a = t ≫ i` for a torus
  point `t` with `t * t = 1`, namely `t = a^∨(-1)`.

The axioms used are `propext`, `Classical.choice` and `Quot.sound`.

## Source

Brian Conrad, *Reductive group schemes*, in *Autour des schémas en groupes* I, Panoramas et
Synthèses 42–43, SMF, 2014, Corollary 5.1.11, p.142, and Example 6.4.3, pp.230–231 (registered
as `conrad-2014-reductive-group-schemes`). The Lean route differs from Conrad's:

- Conrad normalizes `X_{-a}` from a basis `X_a` and uses the Tits group scheme.
- Here the unit `c_a` comes from the clause-1 dual-number argument. The square identity is
  checked at the localizations at primes, using the Library's local rank-one laws.
