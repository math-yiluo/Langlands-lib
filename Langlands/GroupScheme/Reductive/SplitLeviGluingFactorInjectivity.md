# The graph projections are isomorphisms over an algebraically closed field

Author: Worker 712 (unit taken over from Worker 1444's Levi-gluing chain).

This is a derived internal support module for the graph route to the
common-torus Levi gluing statement `SplitCommonTorusLeviGluingStatement`. Its
main theorem `algClosedGraphProjectionIsomorphisms` proves the Library's
proposition `LeviGluing.AlgClosedGraphProjectionIsomorphisms.{u}` (module
`Reductive.SplitLeviGluingDescent`), unchanged. Over an algebraically closed
field, take two connected reductive groups `G₁`, `G₂` with a common split
maximal torus `T` (inclusions `i₁`, `i₂`), root data for both with the same
roots, and a simple comparison graph `D`. The theorem says that both projections
`D.envelope.first` and `D.envelope.second` of the graph group are isomorphisms.
`AlgClosedGraphProjectionIsomorphisms` is the hypothesis `hgraph` of the
Library's `splitCommonTorusLeviGluing_of_algClosedGraphs`. This module does not
itself state or prove the Levi gluing statement.

Throughout, `k : Type u` is a field and all group schemes are over
`Spec (CommRingCat.of k)`. The notation is the Library's:

- `lieKernel H k` is the Lie algebra of `H`, as the group of dual-number points
  over the identity. `Additive (lieKernel H (CommRingCat.of k))` is the same
  group written additively, a `k`-vector space. For `p : H ⟶ G`,
  `Lie.targetMap p k` and the linear map `Lie.targetMapLinear p (CommRingCat.of k)`
  are the differential of `p`: composition of dual-number points with `p`.
- For `t : T ⟶ H` and a character `χ`, `weightSpace t χ` is the `χ`-weight
  space of the Lie algebra of `H` under `T`, and `roots t` is the set of
  nonzero characters with nonzero weight space.
- `IsNormalizingPoint t n σ` says that the rational point `n` of `H`
  normalizes `t` and acts on `T` through the automorphism `σ`.
- A simple comparison graph `D : SimpleComparisonGraph i₁ i₂` (Worker 1444's
  `SplitLeviGluingSimpleGraph`) records that `T` is split (`D.split`), a generic
  chamber cocharacter `D.directions.chamber` with, for every simple root `a`, a
  cocharacter `D.directions.direction a` that annihilates `a`, the local
  comparisons `D.comparisons a`, and the graph envelope `D.envelope`. The local
  comparison `D.comparisons a` holds closed subgroups `left` of `G₁` and `right`
  of `G₂` representing the centralizers of the cocharacter `direction a`, their
  torus factors `leftTorus` and `rightTorus`, and an isomorphism `iso` from
  `left` to `right` compatible with the tori. The envelope is the subgroup
  `D.envelope.group` of `G₁ × G₂` generated by the graphs of these isomorphisms
  and by the diagonal torus. It has torus `D.envelope.torusLift`, local lifts
  `D.envelope.localLift a` of `left` into the graph group, and the two
  projections `first` and `second`.
- `D.GraphDynamicFactors` (Worker 1444's `SplitLeviGluingGraphDynamicFactors`)
  records representatives of the positive and negative dynamic unipotent
  subgroups of the graph group, and the fact that its torus represents the
  centralizer of the chamber. `UnipotentProjectionData p a ν i`
  (`SplitLeviGluingGraphFactorProjections`) is a faithfully flat map `hom` from
  the source of `a` to the canonical representative of the dynamic unipotent
  subgroup of `ν ≫ i` in `G`, with `hom` followed by the inclusion equal to
  `a ≫ p`.

## Definitions

- `intersectionCocharacter t ht ν`: for `t : T ⟶ H`, `p : H ⟶ G` and
  `i : T ⟶ G` with `t ≫ p = i` (`ht`), and a cocharacter `ν` of `T`, the
  cocharacter of the fibre product `subgroupIntersection p i = H ×_G T` whose
  components are `ν ≫ t` and `ν`. Its two projections are recorded in
  `intersectionCocharacter_fst` and `intersectionCocharacter_snd`.
- `WeightKernelTrivial p t χ`: for `p : H ⟶ G`, `t : T ⟶ H` and a character
  `χ` of `T`, the proposition that every Lie vector of `H` in `weightSpace t χ`
  killed by the differential of `p` is zero.

## Main result

- `algClosedGraphProjectionIsomorphisms : AlgClosedGraphProjectionIsomorphisms.{u}`.

## Supporting declarations

Unipotent factors (`H` and `G` affine, `H` locally of finite type, `i` a closed
subgroup `hi`, `t ≫ p = i`):

- `eq_one_of_mem_unipotent_of_comp_eq_one t ht ν hi hinj hg hp`: assume the
  differential of `p` has trivial kernel (`hinj`). Then every point `g` of the
  dynamic unipotent subgroup of `ν ≫ t` in `H` with `g ≫ p = 1` is `1`, on every
  test scheme.
- `UnipotentProjectionData.isIso_of_lieKernel t ht ν hi ha X hinj`: let `a`
  represent the dynamic unipotent subgroup of `ν ≫ t` (`ha`) and
  `X : UnipotentProjectionData p a ν i`. If the differential of `p` has trivial
  kernel, then `X.hom` is an isomorphism.

Weight spaces (`H` and `G` affine):

- `lieVector_eq_zero_iff X` and `targetMapLinear_eq_zero_iff p X`: a Lie vector
  is zero exactly when its dual-number point is `1`, and its image under the
  differential of `p` is zero exactly when the composite point is `1`.
- `weightKernelTrivial_of_not_mem_roots p t hz hr`: if `χ ≠ 0` and `χ` is not a
  root of `t`, then `WeightKernelTrivial p t χ` (the weight space is zero).
- `weightKernelTrivial_of_normalizing p t hn h`: if `n` normalizes `t` through
  `σ` and `WeightKernelTrivial p t χ`, then
  `WeightKernelTrivial p t (σ.inv ≫ χ)`.
- `weightKernelTrivial_zero p t ht hself`: if `i = t ≫ p` is a monomorphism and
  the torus `t` is its own centralizer (`hself`), then
  `WeightKernelTrivial p t 0`.
- `weightKernelTrivial_of_finrank_eq_one p t hdim hX₀ hp`: if
  `weightSpace t χ` has dimension one (`hdim`) and contains a vector `X₀` whose
  image under the differential is nonzero, then `WeightKernelTrivial p t χ`.
- `injective_of_isInternal_of_blocks A B hA hB f hmap hker`: a general linear
  algebra lemma. Let `V` and `W` be internal direct sums of families of
  submodules `A i` and `B i`, and let `f : V →ₗ W` map each `A i` into `B i`. If
  `f` kills no nonzero vector of any `A i`, then `f` is injective.
- `targetMapLinear_injective_of_weightKernelTrivial p t hT h` (`H` and `G`
  locally of finite type, `T` split): if `WeightKernelTrivial p t χ` for every
  `χ`, then the differential of `p` is injective.
- `eq_one_of_targetMap_eq_one p hinj x hx`: injectivity of the linear
  differential in the multiplicative form used above.

The graph (`G₁`, `G₂` affine; `D : SimpleComparisonGraph i₁ i₂`; `p` a
homomorphism from the graph group to an affine `G`):

- `D.weightKernelTrivial_of_orthogonal hG₁ hmax₁ hroots hH htmax p a hχ0 hχa`
  (`k` algebraically closed): let `G₁` be connected reductive with maximal torus
  `i₁`, the roots of `i₁` and `i₂` equal, the graph group connected reductive
  (`hH`) with maximal torus `D.envelope.torusLift` (`htmax`), and
  `D.envelope.localLift a ≫ p` a monomorphism. Then every nonzero character `χ`
  pairing to zero with `D.directions.direction a` satisfies
  `WeightKernelTrivial p D.envelope.torusLift χ`.
- `D.weightKernelTrivial_reflection hP₁ p hj h`: let `P₁` be a root datum of
  `i₁` (`hP₁`) and `P₁.root j` a simple root for the chamber (`hj`). If
  `WeightKernelTrivial p D.envelope.torusLift χ`, then the same holds for
  `P₁.reflection j χ`.
- `D.weightKernelTrivial E hG₁ hmax₁ hP₁ hroots hH htmax p ht hmono χ`
  (`k` algebraically closed): with the hypotheses above, let
  `E : D.GraphDynamicFactors`, `D.envelope.torusLift ≫ p = i` with `i` a

## Proofs

`eq_one_of_mem_unipotent_of_comp_eq_one`. Let `M = H ×_G T` with projections
`fst` (a closed subgroup of `H`, since `i` is closed) and `snd`, and let
`χ = intersectionCocharacter t ht ν`. The dynamic unipotent subgroup of `χ` in
`M` has the representative `κ : K ⟶ M` (`Dynamic.Affine.unipotentHom`). Its
underlying space is connected (`DynamicConnected.connectedSpace_of_isRepresentedBy_unipotent`).
Its image under `snd` lies in the dynamic unipotent subgroup of `ν` in the
commutative group `T`, which is trivial. So every Lie vector `x` of `K` has
`x ≫ κ ≫ fst ≫ p = x ≫ κ ≫ snd ≫ i = 1`. By `hinj`, `x ≫ κ ≫ fst = 1`, and
`κ ≫ fst` is a closed immersion, so `x = 1`. The Lie algebra of `K` is therefore
trivial, and `Lie.points_eq_one_of_subsingleton_lieKernel` makes every point of
`K` trivial, on every test scheme. Finally `g` and `1` define a point `m` of
`M` with `m ≫ fst = g`. It lies in the dynamic unipotent subgroup of `χ`,
because that subgroup is the preimage under the closed subgroup `fst` of the
dynamic unipotent subgroup of `ν ≫ t` in `H`
(`Dynamic.unipotent_eq_comap_of_isClosedSubgroup`). So `m` factors through `K`,
hence `m = 1` and `g = 1`.

`UnipotentProjectionData.isIso_of_lieKernel`. `X.hom` is flat, surjective and
quasi-compact (`X.geometry`). By the Library's
`TorusUnipotentExtension.isIso_of_trivial_kernel`, it suffices that the kernel
of `X.hom` has trivial points. A point `y` of the kernel gives the point
`g = y ≫ kernelι ≫ a` of the dynamic unipotent subgroup of `ν ≫ t` in `H`, with
`g ≫ p = y ≫ kernelι ≫ X.hom ≫ unipotentHom = 1` by `X.comp`. The preceding
lemma gives `g = 1`. Since `kernelι ≫ a` is a monomorphism, `y = 1`.

`weightKernelTrivial_of_normalizing`. The adjoint action of `n⁻¹` carries the
`χ`-weight space to the `σ.inv ≫ χ`-weight space and back
(`IsNormalizingPoint.adjoint_mem_weightSpace`), and it commutes with the
differential (`lieKernelHomLinear_adjoint`). So a vector of weight
`σ.inv ≫ χ` killed by the differential is the image under the action of `n` of
a vector of weight `χ` killed by the differential, which is zero.

`weightKernelTrivial_zero`. A self-centralizing torus contains the zero weight
space in the tangent space of its range
(`RelativeRoot.weightSpace_zero_le_tangentSubmodule_range_of_selfCentralizing`).
So a zero-weight vector is `s ≫ t` for a Lie vector `s` of `T`. If the
differential kills it, then `s ≫ i = 1`, so `s = 1` because `i` is a
monomorphism.

`weightKernelTrivial_of_finrank_eq_one`. Every vector of the weight space is
`c • X₀`. If `c ≠ 0` and `c • X₀` lies in the kernel of the differential, so
does `X₀ = c⁻¹ • (c • X₀)`, contradicting the hypothesis on `X₀`. If `c = 0` the
vector is zero.

`injective_of_isInternal_of_blocks`. The map `f` restricts to injective maps
`A i → B i`. Their direct sum is injective (`DirectSum.lmap_injective`), and it
corresponds to `f` under the isomorphisms `⊕ A i ≅ V` and `⊕ B i ≅ W`.
`targetMapLinear_injective_of_weightKernelTrivial` applies it to the weight
decompositions of both Lie algebras for the split torus
(`isInternal_weightSpace_of_isSplitTorus`). The differential maps the
`χ`-weight space of `t` into the `χ`-weight space of `t ≫ p`
(`lieKernelHomLinear_mem_weightSpace`).

`D.weightKernelTrivial_of_orthogonal`. If `χ` is not a root of the graph torus,
use `weightKernelTrivial_of_not_mem_roots`. Otherwise `χ` is a root of `i₁`
(`D.roots_subset_first`). The local Levi `left` of `a` is connected reductive
(`dynamicCentralizerReductive`). Its roots are the roots of `i₁` pairing to zero
with `direction a` (`dynamicCentralizerRoots`), so `χ` is one of them and
`left` has a nonzero vector `Y` of weight `χ`. Its image `X₀` under the
differential of `localLift a` has weight `χ` for the graph torus
(`leftTorus_localLift`). The differential of `localLift a ≫ p` is injective,
since that map is a monomorphism (`Lie.targetMapLinear_injective`), so the
image of `X₀` under the differential of `p` is nonzero. The graph group is
connected reductive with maximal torus `D.envelope.torusLift`, so its root
spaces are one-dimensional (`rootLieLine_of_dynamicCentralizerReductive`), and
`weightKernelTrivial_of_finrank_eq_one` concludes.

`D.weightKernelTrivial_reflection`. `hP₁` supplies a rational point `n` of `G₁`
normalizing `i₁` through the automorphism `σ` inducing the reflection in
`P₁.root j`. Because `σ` fixes `direction a`, which annihilates `P₁.root j`
(`cocharacter_comp_eq_of_reflection`), `n` lies in the centralizer of that
cocharacter (`constantPoint_mem_centralizer`). It is therefore a point `m` of
the local Levi `left`, which normalizes `leftTorus` through `σ`
(`IsNormalizingPoint.of_comp`). Its image in the graph group normalizes the
graph torus through `σ`. `weightKernelTrivial_of_normalizing` then gives the
result, since `σ.inv ≫ χ = P₁.reflection j χ`.

`D.weightKernelTrivial`. The zero weight is `weightKernelTrivial_zero`: the graph
torus is self-centralizing (`E.torus_selfCentralizing`). Nonzero non-roots are
`weightKernelTrivial_of_not_mem_roots`. For roots, Worker 712's
`positive_induction` (`SplitLeviGluingSimpleGeneration`, no reducedness
assumed) is applied to the property "`WeightKernelTrivial` holds at `ψ` and at
`-ψ`". It holds at a simple root `a` and at `2 • a`, because `±a` and `±2 • a`
are nonzero and pair to zero with `direction a`
(`weightKernelTrivial_of_orthogonal`). It is stable under simple reflections by
`weightKernelTrivial_reflection`, applied to `ψ` and to `-ψ`. So it holds at
every positive root. A root `χ` of the graph torus is a root of `i₁` and pairs
nonzero with the generic chamber. Either `χ` or `-χ` is positive, and the
property at that root gives the property at `χ`.

`algClosedGraphProjectionIsomorphisms`. Let `E` and `F` be Worker 1444's
`D.graphDynamicFactorsOfReductive` and `D.graphFactorProjectionsOfReductive`. The
graph group is connected reductive (`D.isConnectedReductive`), with maximal
torus `D.envelope.torusLift` (`E.isMaximalTorus`). For `p = first`,
`localLift a ≫ p` is the closed inclusion `leftInclusion`. For `p = second`, it
is `iso.hom ≫ rightInclusion`, again a monomorphism. The torus composites are
`i₁` and `i₂`, monomorphisms since maximal tori are closed. So
`D.weightKernelTrivial` holds for both projections, and
`targetMapLinear_injective_of_weightKernelTrivial` makes both differentials
injective. `UnipotentProjectionData.isIso_of_lieKernel`, applied to the
chamber and to its negative, makes the four maps `F.positiveFirst.hom`,
`F.negativeFirst.hom`, `F.positiveSecond.hom` and `F.negativeSecond.hom`
isomorphisms. The negative factor `E.negative.inclusion` represents the
dynamic unipotent subgroup of `-chamber` by
`groupHom_neg_comp_eq_oppositeCocharacter`. Worker 1444's
`E.isIso_first_of_factorIsomorphisms` and `E.isIso_second_of_factorIsomorphisms`
(`SplitLeviGluingGraphFactorIsomorphisms`) then make both projections
isomorphisms.

## Provenance

The assertion is Milne's Proposition 23.18 in the proof of the isogeny theorem:
"The projection p: G × G' → G maps G'' isomorphically onto G" (J. S. Milne,
*Algebraic Groups* (2017), source ID `milne-2017-algebraic-groups`, printed
p.491, PDF p.507; printed page = PDF page − 16). Here it is specialized to a
common torus and the identity on `T`, as used for the isomorphism theorem
(Theorem 23.25 and Remark 23.26, printed pp.492–493). Milne completes the proof
of 23.18 after 23.24 (printed p.492, PDF p.508): the projection is bijective on
rational points and an isomorphism on the torus (23.24), and "p also induces an
isomorphism between all corresponding pairs of root subgroups of G'' and G,
since this is true for the root subgroups U''_α and U_α (α ∈ Δ) by construction
and the others are conjugate to these under the Weyl groups. It therefore
induces an isomorphism between the big cells".

The implemented route differs. It proves injectivity of the differential of
each projection, weight by weight, instead of comparing all root subgroups. The
simple roots are handled through the local Levi subgroups, which embed in the
graph, and through the one-dimensionality of root spaces of the reductive graph
group (Milne, Theorem 21.11(b), printed p.428, PDF p.444: "dim g_α = 1"). Weyl
conjugation transports injectivity from the simple roots to all roots, as in
Milne's argument. The zero weight uses the self-centralizing torus (Milne
23.23). Injectivity of the differential then makes the unipotent factor maps
isomorphisms, by connectedness of dynamic unipotent subgroups and triviality of
groups with zero Lie algebra, which replaces Milne's use of 23.24 and
Theorem 21.51. Reductivity of the graph (Milne 23.20), maximality of its torus
(23.23) and the big-cell assembly are Worker 1444's published modules. The
official errata to Milne (`milne-2017-algebraic-groups-errata-2026-11-02`) have
no Chapter 23 entries for printed pp.491–492 (their Chapter 23 entries are for
p.488 and p.494 onwards). They have no entry for Theorem 21.11. Their p.440
entry rewrites the proof of Theorem 21.51, which this route does not use.
