# The word induction for the Levi graph cell

This internal derived module implements the word induction in Milne,
*Algebraic Groups* (2017), Proposition 23.22, printed p.491 (PDF p.507).
The registered source is `milne-2017-algebraic-groups`; the registered official
2017 errata snapshot is `milne-2017-algebraic-groups-errata-2026-11-02`.
The displayed rank-one closure overline on that page was checked visually.
The errata have no correction to Proposition 23.22. Their p.18 correction
warns against assuming joint continuity of multiplication on rational points
with their induced Zariski topology.

The ambient object here is an arbitrary group with a topology and separately
continuous multiplication. No continuity of inversion or joint multiplication
is assumed. The positive and negative root groups are supplied as families
of abstract subgroups `A i` and `B i`. The three factors are supplied subgroups
`V`, `T`, `U`. The hypotheses say that every `A i` lies in `U`, `V` is generated
as an abstract subgroup by the `B i`, `T` normalizes `V` and `U`, distinct-index
`A i` and `B j` commute, and `A i * B i` lies in the topological closure of
`B i * T * A i`.

`generated_mul_mem_closure_tripleProduct` proves that left multiplication by
the abstract subgroup generated by the union of the positive root groups
together with `V` and `T` preserves the closure of `V * T * U`.
First, its closure is stable under left
multiplication by `V` and `T`, and right multiplication by `U` and `T`.
For a negative-root word `v`, induction shows `a * v` lies in this closure
for every `a` in any `A i`. A first letter from `B j` commutes past `a` when
`i` and `j` differ. For equal indices the rank-one closure hypothesis and
continuity of right translation replace the pair by elements of
`B i * T * A i`; the induction hypothesis and the two left stability results
then apply. Inverse letters belong to the same negative root subgroup.
Right stability extends this conclusion to left stability by each positive
root group on the entire cell closure. A second word induction proves the
claimed stability. The construction `tripleProductClosureAction` uses this
stability to define an actual action of the abstractly generated subgroup on
the cell closure: the underlying operation is left multiplication in `G`,
and its action laws follow from the identity and associativity laws in `G`.
No density premise is needed for this construction.

Applying stability to the identity, which belongs to the cell, proves
`generated_subset_closure_tripleProduct`: the closure of the cell contains the
abstractly generated subgroup. `dense_tripleProduct` concludes density when
that subgroup is dense in the ambient group, by taking closures once more.

This proves the abstract combinatorial density step. It does not construct
root groups, the graph subgroup or an isomorphism of group schemes, and it
does not prove the supplied generation, normalization, commutation or
rank-one closure hypotheses from reductive-group assumptions. These remain
obligations in the proof of `SplitCommonTorusLeviGluingStatement`.
