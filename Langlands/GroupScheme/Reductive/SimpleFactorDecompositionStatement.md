# Statement: the decomposition theorem for semisimple groups over a field

Worker 75.

`Langlands.GroupScheme.SimpleFactorDecompositionStatement.{u} : Prop` is a
statement-only, `Prop`-valued definition. This module does not prove it, and it adds no
other definitions, theorems or axioms. It imports:

- [`Reductive.AlmostSimple`](AlmostSimple.md), which provides `IsAlmostSimple` and,
  through [`Reductive.Semisimple`](Semisimple.md), `IsSemisimple`;
- [`Reductive.CommutingMultiplication`](CommutingMultiplication.md), which provides
  `commutingSubgroupMultiplicationHom j i hc : H ⊗ K ⟶ G`, the multiplication
  homomorphism of two subgroup maps `j : H ⟶ G` and `i : K ⟶ G` whose points commute on
  every test scheme (`hc`); on points it sends `(h, x)` to `(h ≫ j) * (x ≫ i)`;
- [`SimplyConnected.AlmostSimpleProductFactors`](../SimplyConnected/AlmostSimpleProductFactors.md),
  which provides `piFactorInclusionHom A i : A i ⟶ pi A`, the homomorphism inserting the
  `i`-th factor into the finite product `pi A` (identity in all other coordinates).

Through these imports it also uses `pi A`, the categorical finite product of a family of
group schemes from [`Products`](../Products.md); `IsClosedSubgroup` from
[`Reductive.Basic`](Basic.md) (`IsClosedSubgroup n` means that the underlying morphism of
schemes of `n` is a closed immersion); `IsCentralIsogeny` from
[`CentralIsogeny.Basic`](../CentralIsogeny/Basic.md) (the underlying morphism is finite,
flat, surjective and locally of finite presentation, and the scheme-theoretic kernel is
central); and `SubgroupFunctor.range` and `SubgroupFunctor.IsNormal` from
[`SubgroupFunctor`](../SubgroupFunctor.md). For a homomorphism `n : K ⟶ H`,
`(SubgroupFunctor.range n).carrier T` is the set of points `y ≫ n.hom.hom` with
`y : T ⟶ K.X`, for every test scheme `T`; `IsNormal` means that these sets are normal
subgroups of `H(T)` for every `T`; `≤`, `⊥` and equality of subgroup functors are taken
on every test scheme. All group schemes are over `Spec k` and in universe `u`.

It states Conrad's decomposition theorem for semisimple groups (Theorem 5.1.19).

## Two local predicates

For field group schemes `H` and `K` and a homomorphism `n : K ⟶ H`:

- `IsNormalSubgroup n` means `IsClosedSubgroup n`, `Smooth K.X.hom`,
  `ConnectedSpace K.X.left` and `(SubgroupFunctor.range n).IsNormal`. So `n` presents
  `K` as a smooth connected closed normal subgroup of `H`. These are Conrad's "normal
  smooth connected `k`-subgroups" of `H`.
- `IsMinimalNormalSubgroup n` means `IsNormalSubgroup n`, `SubgroupFunctor.range n ≠ ⊥`,
  and for every `K'` and `n' : K' ⟶ H` with `IsNormalSubgroup n'`,
  `SubgroupFunctor.range n' ≠ ⊥` and `range n' ≤ range n`, we have `range n' = range n`.
  These are Conrad's "minimal nontrivial normal smooth connected `k`-subgroups" of `H`.

Both are local `let`s with the ambient group `H` implicit, so they are used both for
`H = G` and for `H = B J` below.

## Statement

For every field `k : Type u` and every group scheme `G` over `Spec k` with
`IsSemisimple G`, there exist a type `I : Type u` with `Finite I`, group schemes
`A i` and homomorphisms `a i : A i ⟶ G` for `i : I` (Conrad's `G_i`) such that:

1. **The `G_i` are the minimal subgroups, each listed once.** Every `a i` satisfies
   `IsMinimalNormalSubgroup`; `i ↦ SubgroupFunctor.range (a i)` is injective; and every
   `n : K ⟶ G` with `IsMinimalNormalSubgroup n` has `range n = range (a i)` for some `i`.
2. **Each `G_i` is `k`-simple:** `IsAlmostSimple (A i)`.
3. **The `G_i` pairwise commute:** for `i ≠ j`, every test scheme `T`, and points
   `x : T ⟶ (A i).X`, `y : T ⟶ (A j).X`, the points `x ≫ (a i).hom.hom` and
   `y ≫ (a j).hom.hom` of `G` commute.
4. **Multiplication is a central isogeny:** there is a homomorphism `p : pi A ⟶ G` with
   `piFactorInclusionHom A i ≫ p = a i` for every `i` and `IsCentralIsogeny p`.

Moreover there exist group schemes `B J` and homomorphisms `b J : B J ⟶ G` for every
`J : Set I` (Conrad's `G_J`) such that:

5. **`G_J` is a normal semisimple subgroup:** `IsNormalSubgroup (b J)` and
   `IsSemisimple (B J)`.
6. **`G_J` is generated by the `G_i` with `i ∈ J`:** `range (a i) ≤ range (b J)` for
   every `i ∈ J`; and for every `n : K ⟶ G` with `IsClosedSubgroup n` and
   `range (a i) ≤ range n` for all `i ∈ J`, we have `range (b J) ≤ range n`. So `G_J` is
   the smallest closed subgroup containing these `G_i`.
7. **The minimal subgroups of `G_J` are the `G_i` with `i ∈ J`:** for every `i ∈ J`
   there is `c : A i ⟶ B J` with `c ≫ b J = a i` and `IsMinimalNormalSubgroup c` (in
   `B J`); and every `n : K ⟶ B J` with `IsMinimalNormalSubgroup n` (in `B J`) has
   `range (n ≫ b J) = range (a i)` for some `i ∈ J`.
8. **Every normal subgroup is a unique `G_J`:** for every `n : K ⟶ G` with
   `IsNormalSubgroup n` there is exactly one `J : Set I` with
   `range n = range (b J)`.
9. **Unique complements:** for every `n : K ⟶ G` with `IsNormalSubgroup n` there are
   `K'` and `n' : K' ⟶ G` with `IsNormalSubgroup n'` and a proof `hc` that the points
   of `n` and `n'` commute on every test scheme, such that
   `IsCentralIsogeny (commutingSubgroupMultiplicationHom n n' hc)`; and every
   `n'' : K'' ⟶ G` with `IsNormalSubgroup n''`, commuting with `n` (any such proof `hc`)
   and with `IsCentralIsogeny (commutingSubgroupMultiplicationHom n n'' hc)` has
   `range n'' = range n'`.

## Source

Brian Conrad, *Reductive group schemes*, in *Autour des schémas en groupes* I, Panoramas
et Synthèses 42–43, SMF, 2014. It is registered as `conrad-2014-reductive-group-schemes`,
file `Conrad--Reductive group schemes (SGA3 notes)--2014.pdf` (SHA-256
`1a63a17e4ccb7e23263d65f05d93a6651262861e978e9eb0c1c53a01fd59140a`). Page numbers are
the printed page numbers.

- Theorem 5.1.19 (Decomposition theorem for semisimple groups), p.150: "Let G be a
  nontrivial connected semisimple group over a field k. The set {G_i}_{i∈I} of minimal
  nontrivial normal smooth connected k-subgroups of G is finite, each G_i is k-simple,
  the G_i's pairwise commute, and the multiplication homomorphism ∏ G_i → G is a central
  isogeny. For each J ⊂ I the normal connected semisimple k-subgroup G_J ⊂ G generated
  by {G_i}_{i∈J} has as its minimal nontrivial normal smooth connected k-subgroups
  precisely the G_i for i ∈ J, and every normal smooth connected k-subgroup N ⊂ G equals
  G_J for a unique J. In particular, for each N there exists a unique N′ that commutes
  with N and makes the multiplication homomorphism N × N′ → G a central isogeny."
- The definition of `k`-simple, p.149: "A connected semisimple group H over a field k is
  k-simple if H ≠ 1 and H has no nontrivial normal smooth connected proper k-subgroup".
- The proof, pp.150–151, which uses Proposition 5.1.17 (the split case, pp.148–149) and
  Galois descent.

## Derivation from the source

- **Semisimple groups.** `IsSemisimple G` is the Library's field-level semisimplicity
  (connected reductive with finite centre), Conrad's "connected semisimple group".
- **The trivial group.** Conrad assumes `G` nontrivial; the Lean statement does not. For
  trivial `G` the theorem holds with `I` empty: the empty product `pi A` is the trivial
  group, its map to `G` is an isomorphism and hence a central isogeny
  (`isCentralIsogeny_of_isIso` in `CentralIsogeny.Basic`), and `G` has no nontrivial
  normal subgroups. So the Lean statement is Conrad's theorem together with this
  degenerate case.
- **The set of `G_i`.** Conrad's finite set is encoded as the finite family `a`,
  together with clause 1: each member is minimal, distinct indices give distinct
  subgroups, and every minimal subgroup occurs. Subgroups are compared through their
  ranges on all test schemes. For closed subgroups, equal ranges means equal closed
  subschemes.
- **`k`-simple.** `IsAlmostSimple H` means `IsSemisimple H`, `H` noncommutative on some
  test scheme, and every smooth connected closed normal subgroup has range `⊥` or `⊤`.
  For a connected semisimple group, being noncommutative is equivalent to `H ≠ 1`. So
  this is Conrad's `k`-simple.
- **The multiplication homomorphism.** Since the `G_i` pairwise commute, the
  multiplication `∏ G_i → G` is the homomorphism that restricts to `a i` on the `i`-th
  factor. Clause 4 asserts a homomorphism `p` with these restrictions that is a central
  isogeny.
- **`G_J`.** "The subgroup generated by" a family of smooth connected subgroups is the
  smallest closed subgroup scheme containing all of them; clause 6 states this
  minimality among all closed subgroups. Conrad's description of `G_J` as normal,
  connected and semisimple is clause 5 (smoothness is part of `IsNormalSubgroup`). The
  `G_i` with `i ∈ J` are subgroups of `G_J` through the factorizations `c` of clause 7,
  which also states that they are minimal in `G_J` and that `G_J` has no other minimal
  subgroups. `J` ranges over all subsets of `I`, including `∅` (trivial `G_J`) and `I`.
- **The complement `N′`.** "Commutes with `N`" is the commutation hypothesis of
  `commutingSubgroupMultiplicationHom`, and `N × N′ → G` is that multiplication
  homomorphism. Uniqueness is stated among smooth connected closed normal subgroups,
  the class of subgroups the theorem is about. Uniqueness is up to equality of ranges.

## Scope

The proposition does not state:

- Proposition 5.1.17 (the split case, with the identification of the root systems of
  the `G_i` with the irreducible components of the root system of `G`);
- Corollary 5.1.18 (absolute simplicity is equivalent to irreducibility of the root
  system over `k_s`);
- that the formation of the `G_i` is compatible with separable extension and Galois
  descent, as in the proof, or Example 5.1.20;
- that `∏_{i∈J} G_i → G_J` is a central isogeny, which appears in the proof;
- Exercise 5.5.2, the generalization to connected reductive groups.
