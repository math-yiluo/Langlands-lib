# Statement: normalizer points of a split maximal torus map onto the Weyl group

Worker 75.

`Langlands.GroupScheme.AffineSplitNormalizerWeylStatement.{u} : Prop` is a
statement-only, `Prop`-valued definition. This module does not prove it, and it adds no
other definitions, theorems or axioms. It imports
[`Reductive.FibrewiseRootDatum`](FibrewiseRootDatum.md) (for `IsFibrewiseRootDatumOf`
and, through it, `splitTorus`, `splitTorus.commGroupScheme`, `splitTorus.pointsEquiv`
and `splitTorus.exponentWeightSpace`), [`Reductive.Basic`](Basic.md) (for `IsReductive`
and `IsClosedSubgroup`), [`Torus.FieldPointMaximality`](../Torus/FieldPointMaximality.md)
(for `IsFibrewiseMaximalTorus` and, through its imports,
[`Torus.Maximal`](../Torus/Maximal.md) with `IsNormalizingPoint`, `normalizerPoints` and
`centralizerPoints`) and Mathlib's `LinearAlgebra.RootSystem.WeylGroup` (for
`RootPairing.weylGroup`).

It states Conrad's Example 6.4.3 over a connected affine base `Spec R`: for a split
reductive group with split maximal torus `T`, the sequence
`1 → T(S) → N_G(T)(S) → W(Φ) → 1` is exact, and over a domain the normalizer points
contain a finite subgroup mapping onto `W(Φ)`.

## Statement and quantifiers

The proposition quantifies over:

- every commutative ring `R : Type u` such that `Spec R` is a connected topological
  space. Mathlib's `ConnectedSpace` includes non-emptiness, so `R` is non-zero. The ring
  may be non-Noetherian and non-local;
- a group scheme `G : GroupScheme (Spec R)` with `hG : IsReductive G`
  ([`Reductive.Basic`](Basic.md)): smooth and affine over `Spec R`, with connected
  reductive geometric fibres. The instance `IsAffineHom G.X.hom` is taken from
  `hG.isAffineHom` by `haveI`;
- a finite type `I : Type u` and a homomorphism `i : splitTorus (Spec R) I ⟶ G` from the
  coordinate split torus `𝔾_m^I`;
- a type `J : Type u` and a Mathlib root datum `P : RootDatum J (I → ℤ) (I → ℤ)` on the
  coordinate character and cocharacter lattices.

It has the four hypotheses that encode Conrad's Definition 5.1.1 in the Library, exactly as
in [`Reductive.AffineSplitIsomorphismTheoremStatement`](AffineSplitIsomorphismTheoremStatement.md)
and [`Reductive.EtaleLocalSplitStatement`](EtaleLocalSplitStatement.md):

1. `IsClosedSubgroup i`: the underlying morphism of schemes of `i` is a closed immersion.
2. `IsFibrewiseMaximalTorus i`: on every residue-field fibre, `i` is a maximal torus
   (`IsMaximalTorus`).
3. `IsFibrewiseRootDatumOf I G i P`: `J` is finite, `P` is reduced, and for every field
   `k` with a ring homomorphism `R → k`, the realization of `P` over `k` is the root datum
   of the base change of `i` to `k`. Here `m : I → ℤ` is the character
   `t ↦ ∏ⱼ tⱼ ^ mⱼ` and `n : I → ℤ` is the cocharacter `s ↦ (s ^ nⱼ)ⱼ`.
4. For every `α : J`, the weight space `splitTorus.exponentWeightSpace I G i (P.root α)`
   in the Lie algebra of `G` is free of rank one over `R`.

## The relation "`n` induces `w`"

The statement introduces, by a local `let`, a relation `Induces n w` between a point
`n : Over.mk (𝟙 (Spec R)) ⟶ G.X` of `G(S)` and an automorphism `w : RootPairing.Aut P`
of the root datum. It holds when there is an automorphism
`σ : 𝔾_m^I ≅ 𝔾_m^I` of commutative group schemes such that

- `IsNormalizingPoint i n σ` ([`Torus.Maximal`](../Torus/Maximal.md)), which is the
  equation `i ≫ (G.innerIso n).hom = CommGroupScheme.groupHom σ.hom ≫ i`. Here
  `G.innerIso n` is conjugation `g ↦ n g n⁻¹`. So conjugation by `n` carries the torus
  into itself and restricts on it to `σ`;
- for every test scheme `U` over `Spec R`, every point `x : U ⟶ 𝔾_m^I` and every
  `m : I → ℤ`,
  `∏ j, (pointsEquiv I U (x ≫ σ_*))_j ^ m j = ∏ j, (pointsEquiv I U x)_j ^ (w • m) j`,
  where `x ≫ σ_*` is `x ≫ (CommGroupScheme.groupHom σ.hom).hom.hom`, the point `σ(x)`.
  The left side is the character `χ_m` evaluated at `σ(x)` and the right side is
  `χ_{w • m}` evaluated at `x`. As the identity holds for all `U` and `x`, it says
  `χ_m ∘ σ = χ_{w • m}` (Yoneda): the map that `σ` induces on the character lattice
  `ℤ^I` is the action of `w`.

The action `w • m` is Mathlib's action of `RootPairing.Aut P` on the weight space,
`w • m = weightHom P w m`; a reflection `Equiv.reflection P α` acts by
`P.reflection α`, that is `m ↦ m - ⟨m, α∨⟩ α`.

## Conclusion

The conclusion is the conjunction of four clauses:

1. `∀ w ∈ P.weylGroup, ∃ n, Induces n w`: every element of the Weyl group is induced by
   a point of `G(S)`. `RootPairing.weylGroup` is the subgroup of `Aut P` generated by the
   reflections; its action on `ℤ^I` is the Weyl group `W(Φ)` of the root system as a
   group of linear automorphisms (Mathlib `range_weylGroup_weightHom`).
2. `∀ n ∈ normalizerPoints i, ∃ w ∈ P.weylGroup, Induces n w`: every point of `G(S)` that
   normalizes `i` (some `σ` with `IsNormalizingPoint i n σ`) induces an element of the
   Weyl group.
3. `∀ n ∈ centralizerPoints i, ∃ t, n = t ≫ i.hom.hom`: every point of `G(S)` that
   centralizes `i` (`i ≫ (G.innerIso n).hom = i`) is the image of a point
   `t : Over.mk (𝟙 (Spec R)) ⟶ 𝔾_m^I`, that is of `T(S) = Hom(M, 𝔾_m(S))`.
4. If `R` is a domain, there is a subgroup `H` of `G(S)` with `H ≤ normalizerPoints i`,
   `Finite H`, and `∀ w ∈ P.weylGroup, ∃ n ∈ H, Induces n w`.

- Example 6.4.3, pp.230–231: "Let S be a connected non-empty scheme, and (G, T, M) a
  split reductive group over S. Since W_G(T) = W(Φ)_S, the short exact sequence of
  S-groups (6.4.1) 1 → T → N_G(T) → W_G(T) → 1 induces an exact sequence of groups
  1 → Hom(M, G_m(S)) → N_G(T)(S) → W(Φ) → 1, where surjectivity holds on the right
  because W(Φ) is generated by reflections s_a (a ∈ Φ) that are induced by elements
  w_a(X_a) ∈ N_G(T)(S) for any O_S-basis X_a of 𝔤_a." It continues: "We claim that
  (6.4.1) is the central pushout of an exact sequence (6.4.2) 1 → T[2] → W̃_G(T) →
  W_G(T) → 1 for a finite flat S-subgroup W̃_G(T) ⊂ N_G(T) such that W̃_G(T)(S) is
  carried onto the finite group W(Φ) of constant sections. In particular, if S = Spec R
  for a domain R (or more generally, if µ₂(S) is finite) then N_G(T)(S) contains a
  finite subgroup mapping onto W(Φ)."
- Definition 5.1.1, p.136 (split reductive groups): "Let G be a reductive group over a
  non-empty scheme S. It is split if there exists a maximal torus T equipped with an
  isomorphism T ≃ D_S(M) for a finite free Z-module M such that: 1. the nontrivial
  weights a : T → G_m that occur on 𝔤 arise from elements of M …, 2. each root space
  𝔤_a is free of rank 1 over O_S, 3. each coroot a∨ : G_m → T arises from an element of
  the dual lattice M∨ …"
- Definition 3.2.1, p.91, and Remark A.1.2, p.267: a torus that is maximal on residue
  fibres is a maximal torus in Conrad's (geometric) sense.

## Derivation from the source

With `M = ℤ^I`, Conrad's `T ≃ D_S(M)` is the coordinate split torus `𝔾_m^I`, and
hypotheses 1–4 transcribe Definition 5.1.1 as in
[`Reductive.AffineSplitIsomorphismTheoremStatement`](AffineSplitIsomorphismTheoremStatement.md).
The root datum of the split group is `P`, with roots `Φ = P.root` (injective, so `Φ` is
indexed by `J`).

- **The map `N_G(T)(S) → W(Φ)`.** A point `n ∈ N_G(T)(S)` acts on `T` by conjugation; on
  `S`-points of `W_G(T) = N_G(T)/Z_G(T)` its image is the induced automorphism of `T`,
  equivalently (since `T` is split and `S` is connected and non-empty) the induced
  automorphism of the constant lattice `M`. The identification `W_G(T) = W(Φ)_S` says
  that this automorphism lies in `W(Φ)`. The relation `Induces n w` records exactly
  this: `σ` is the restriction of conjugation by `n` to `T`, and `w` is the induced map
  on characters. Because `S` is non-empty, the characters `χ_m` are pairwise distinct
  morphisms, so `w • m` is determined by `σ`; and `w` is determined by its action on
  `M` (the action of `Aut P` on the weights is faithful for a root datum). So
  `Induces` is the graph of a map from `normalizerPoints i` to `Aut P`.
- **Direction convention.** The relation uses the pullback `χ ↦ χ ∘ σ`, which is the
  inverse of the left action `(n.χ)(t) = χ(n⁻¹tn)` of `N_G(T)` on characters. Each clause quantifies over all of
  `W(Φ)` or asks only for membership in `W(Φ)`, and `W(Φ)` is closed under inverses, so
  the clauses do not depend on this convention.
- **Exactness.** Clause 2 says the map lands in `W(Φ)`, and clause 1 says it is onto
  `W(Φ)` ("surjectivity holds on the right"). The kernel consists of the normalizing
  points with `w = 1`, that is with `σ` the identity (`σ` is determined by `w` through
  the characters), which are the centralizing points. Clause 3 says these are the points
  of `T`, which is exactness at `N_G(T)(S)`; it is the `S`-points form of `Z_G(T) = T`.
  Injectivity of `Hom(M, G_m(S)) = T(S) → N_G(T)(S)` holds because `i` is a closed
  immersion and is not restated.
- **Finite subgroup.** Clause 4 is the final sentence of the first paragraph quoted
  above, for a domain `R`. "N_G(T)(S) contains a finite subgroup mapping onto W(Φ)" is
  `H ≤ normalizerPoints i`, `Finite H`, and every element of `W(Φ)` induced by a point of
  `H`.
- **Affine base.** The free root-space hypothesis uses `splitTorus.exponentWeightSpace`,
  which the Library defines only over an affine base `Spec R`. Conrad allows any
  connected non-empty scheme `S`. So this proposition specializes the source to affine
  bases; the domain case of clause 4 is already affine in the source.

## Scope

All objects are in universe `u`. The proposition does not state:

- the exact sequence (6.4.1) of `S`-group schemes, the identification
  `W_G(T) = W(Φ)_S`, or the construction of the Tits group `W̃_G(T)` and the sequence
  (6.4.2);
- the generalization of clause 4 to bases with `µ₂(S)` finite;
- the explicit elements `w_a(X_a)` inducing the reflections, the canonicity of the Tits
  group over `ℤ`, or the finiteness of `N_G(T)(ℤ)` (over `R = ℤ` the last one follows
  from clauses 1–3 since `ℤˣ` is finite, but it is not stated separately).
