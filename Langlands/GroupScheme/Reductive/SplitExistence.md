# The Existence Theorem for split reductive groups

Authors: Worker 1398, Worker 239.

Module `Langlands.GroupScheme.Reductive.SplitExistence`. It proves the Library proposition
`Langlands.GroupScheme.SplitReductiveExistenceStatement` (module
`Langlands.GroupScheme.Reductive.SplitClassificationStatement`). The statement says: for every
field `k`, every reduced root datum `P : RootDatum J X Y` with `J` finite and `X` finitely
generated is isomorphic, as a root pairing, to the root datum of a split maximal torus of a
connected reductive group over `k`. All objects are in one universe `u`.

## Declarations

- `splitReductiveExistence_of_nonempty H` (Worker 1398). It proves the statement from a
  hypothesis `H`: every reduced root datum with `J` finite and `X` finitely generated has
  integral Chevalley data with Lie relations (`ChevalleyLieData P b`) for every base `b`. The
  proof takes a field `k` and a reduced root datum `P`. It chooses a base `b` of `P`
  (`nonempty_base_int`, module `IntegralBase`) and data `D : ChevalleyLieData P b` from `H`.
  It then applies `ChevalleyData.exists_split_of_chevalleyData k D.toChevalleyData` (modules
  `ToChevalleyData` and `Assembly`). That theorem gives exactly the body of the statement.
- `splitReductiveExistence` (Worker 239) has type exactly
  `SplitReductiveExistenceStatement.{u}`. It discharges `H` with `nonempty_chevalleyLieData`
  (module `ChevalleyLieExistence`). It depends only on the axioms `propext`,
  `Classical.choice` and `Quot.sound`.

The theorem has no hypothesis on `k`, so imperfect fields and positive characteristic are
included. `G` is a group scheme over `Spec k`, and `i : T ⟶ G` is a homomorphism of group
schemes. Maximality, the roots and the reflections are checked with the Library's
definitions, on all test schemes.

## Overview of the proof

The helper modules are in `Langlands/GroupScheme/SplitExistence/`. Each one has its own
Markdown file. The proof is Chevalley's construction (Steinberg, *Lectures on Chevalley
groups*, §§1–5; Milne, *Algebraic Groups* (2017), Thm. 23.55 and §23.h). It has three stages.

1. **Integral data** (Worker 239; modules `ShortRoots`, `ShortRootModule`, `ShortRootRep`,
   `WeylModule`, `StringRep`, `BlockRep`, `WeightGrading`, `Primitive`, `RootLieAlgebra`,
   `RootLieConj`, `WeylWords`, `MinimalWeights`, `SimpleRootData`, `SimpleWeyl`,
   `ChevalleyLieConstruction`, `ChevalleyLieExistence`, and the interface `ChevalleyLieData`).
   A base exists over `ℤ` (`IntegralBase`, Worker 239). A finite generating family of `X`
   determines the short-root module, a free `ℤ`-module with a weight basis. Each simple root
   acts on it by an integral representation of `SL₂` (`SimpleRootData`). Root operators for
   all roots are obtained by Weyl conjugation inside the rational Lie algebra generated by the
   simple ones. They satisfy the Weyl conjugation formula and the integral Lie relation: for
   `α_j' ≠ -α_j`, the bracket `[e_j, e_j']` is zero or an integral multiple of `e_m` for a root
   `m` with `α_m = α_j + α_j'`. The result is `nonempty_chevalleyLieData`.
2. **Commutator relations** (Worker 1398; modules `LieRootData`, `LieConj`, `MatDeriv`,
   `OdePeel`, `CommutatorOde`, `Integrality`, `CommutatorInt`, `ToChevalleyData`). Over every
   commutative ring, the commutator of two root exponentials `x_j(s)`, `x_j'(t)` with
   `j' ≠ -j` is a product of exponentials of roots `a α_j + c α_j'` with `a, c ≥ 1`. The proof
   solves a matrix differential equation over `ℚ[t][s]`, proves that the parameters are
   integral, and transports the result to any ring. This turns `ChevalleyLieData` into
   `ChevalleyData` (module `ChevalleyData`).
3. **The group** (Worker 1398; the remaining modules). For a field `k`, the Chevalley group
   `G ⊆ GL_n` is generated by the split torus `D(X)` and the root groups (`Generation`, via the
   Library's `pointedImageGeneration`). Over algebraically closed extensions, its points lie in
   the abstract group `Γ` generated by the torus and the root elements. `Γ` carries a Tits
   system (`Subgroups`, `SimpleDecomp`, `OrderedProduct`, `TitsAxioms`, `TitsTransfer`,
   `TitsFinal`). From it, and from the Lie algebra computations in `LieMatrix`, `LieWeights`,
   `TorusWeights`, `RootBound` and `UnipotentLie`, one gets:
   - the torus is its own centralizer, hence maximal (`Centralizer`, `Maximal`);
   - the roots of `G` with respect to `D(X)` are the images of the roots of `P` (`Roots`);
   - points of `G` normalizing the torus realize the reflections (`Normalizing`);
   - the transported datum `chevDatum k P` satisfies `IsRootDatumOf` and is isomorphic to `P`
     (`RootDatumOf`);
   - the base change of `G` to `k̄` has trivial unipotent radical (`UnipotentNormal`,
     `Reductive`), so `G` is connected reductive (`Assembly`).

## Provenance

The target is the Existence Theorem (Milne, *Algebraic Groups* (2017), Thm. 23.55). The
construction is Chevalley's (Steinberg, *Lectures on Chevalley groups*; Milne Thm. 23.72). The
Lean proof differs from both sources in three places. It does not use Serre's theorem or a
Chevalley basis: the integral representation is built directly. It derives the commutator
relations by an ODE and integrality argument. It proves reductivity with the Tits system of
the group of `k̄`-points. The Markdown of each helper module describes its route.
