# Split unipotence of represented products of split normal closed subgroups

This module proves the published proposition
`Langlands.GroupScheme.SplitNormalFppfProductStatement.{u}`: over a field `k`,
let `G` be an affine group scheme of locally finite type, and let `i : H ⟶ G`
and `j : N ⟶ G` be closed subgroups that are smooth, connected, normal and
split unipotent. Let `p : P ⟶ G` represent the fppf-local product of
`range i` and `range j`, with `P` smooth and connected. Then `P` is split
unipotent.

The mathematics is Milne, *Algebraic Groups* (2017), §16.g, printed p.343
(PDF p.359): quotients of split solvable groups are split, and extensions of
split solvable groups are split. The induction carrying this out for `P`
generated by `N` and the image of `H` is the published theorem
`RelativeRoot.SplitSemidirect.isSplitUnipotent_of_surjective` in
`Langlands.GroupScheme.Rational.SplitSemidirectSeries`. This module supplies
its two remaining inputs: the image interface `HomImageHypothesis` and the
surjectivity of the multiplication morphism. All declarations are in namespace
`Langlands.GroupScheme.SplitNormalFppfProduct`.

## The image interface

`homImageHypothesis k : RelativeRoot.SplitSemidirect.HomImageHypothesis k`.
`HomImageHypothesis k` asks the following. Let `φ : A ⟶ G` be a homomorphism
over `Spec k`, with `A` smooth, connected and affine and `G` smooth and affine.
Then `φ` factors as `fac ≫ inc`, where `inc : D ⟶ G` is a closed subgroup with
`D` smooth, connected and affine, and `fac` is flat, surjective and
quasi-compact. The proof applies the published `smoothConnectedHomImage` from
`Langlands.GroupScheme.HomImage`. That theorem needs `G` only locally of finite
type, which follows from smoothness, and it gives one extra conjunct (local
finite presentation of `fac`), which is dropped.

## Normality of a factored subgroup

The proof reuses `SubgroupFunctor.isNormal_range_of_mono_comp` from
[`FppfProductUnipotent`](FppfProductUnipotent.md):
let `p : P ⟶ G` have a monomorphic underlying
morphism over the base, and let `j' : N ⟶ P`. If `range (j' ≫ p)` is normal in
`G` on every test, then `range j'` is normal in `P` on every test. Proof:
conjugate a point of `range j'` by a point `g` of `P`. Compose with `p` and use
normality in `G` to obtain a point of `N` whose image equals the conjugate in
`G`. Then cancel the monomorphism `p`, using that composition with `p`
preserves products and inverses.

## Surjectivity of the multiplication morphism

`surjective_mulPoints`: let `p : P ⟶ G` have a monomorphic underlying morphism
and satisfy `IsFppfProduct (range i) (range j) p`. Let `i' : H ⟶ P` and
`j' : N ⟶ P` satisfy `i' ≫ p = i` and `j' ≫ p = j`, with `range j'` normal in
`P`. Then the underlying morphism of `mulPoints j' i' : N × H ⟶ P`, the
morphism `(n, h) ↦ j' n * i' h`, is surjective.

Proof. The identity point of `P`, composed with `p`, lies in `range p`. By
`IsFppfProduct.mem_range_iff` there are a test scheme `V` and a morphism
`v : V ⟶ P` with surjective underlying morphism, together with points
`a' : V ⟶ H` and `b' : V ⟶ N`, such that `v ≫ p = i a' * j b'`. Cancelling the
monomorphism `p` gives `v = i' a' * j' b'` in `P`. Normality of `range j'`
gives a point `c : V ⟶ N` with `j' c = i' a' * j' b' * (i' a')⁻¹`. Hence
`v = j' c * i' a'`, that is, `v = lift c a' ≫ mulPoints j' i'`. The underlying
morphism of this composite is surjective, so the second factor is surjective
(`Surjective.of_comp`).

## The theorem

`splitNormalFppfProduct : SplitNormalFppfProductStatement.{u}`. Let the data be
as in the statement.

1. `p` is a closed subgroup (`IsFppfProduct.isClosedSubgroup`), so its
   underlying morphism over the base is a monomorphism, and `p` represents
   `range p`.
2. `range i ≤ range p` and `range j ≤ range p` (`IsFppfProduct.left_le`,
   `right_le`), so `i` and `j` factor through `p` as `i'` and `j'`
   (`IsRepresentedBy.factorHom`).
3. `j'` is a closed subgroup (`IsClosedSubgroup.of_comp`), and `range j'` is
   normal in `P` by `SubgroupFunctor.isNormal_range_of_mono_comp`.
4. `P` and `H` are affine over `Spec k`, because closed immersions into the
   affine `G` are affine morphisms.
5. `H` is split unipotent, so it has a split series of some length `n`.
6. `isSplitUnipotent_of_surjective (homImageHypothesis k) n j' i'` applies,
   with the surjectivity input `surjective_mulPoints`, and gives that `P` is
   split unipotent.
