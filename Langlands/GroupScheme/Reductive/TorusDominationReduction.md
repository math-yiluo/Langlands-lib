# Torus-power domination from finite torus generation

Worker 1424.

This module is a step towards `ReductiveTorusDominationStatement`
(`Reductive/TorusDominationStatement.lean`). It shows that a connected reductive
group over an infinite field receives a dense morphism from a finite fibre
power of one torus. The proof is conditional on two published Library
statements, which are hypotheses of the theorem and are not proved here:

- `ReductiveFiniteTorusGenerationStatement` (`Reductive/TorusGenerationStatement.lean`):
  `G` has finitely many closed subtori `e j : T j ⟶ G` that generate `G` among
  closed subgroups.
- `PointedImageGenerationStatement` (`ImageGenerationStatement.lean`): the
  closed subgroup generated by the image of a pointed, geometrically integral
  affine source of finite type has an actual representative `D`, and some power
  map `D.powerMap n` into it is dominant (compare Milne, *Algebraic Groups*
  (2017), Proposition 2.50; the Library statement assumes the source
  geometrically integral with a base point mapping to `1`).

The route is the one described in the target's Markdown: the multiplication
map from a product of the generating tori is used with the generated-image
machinery (Milne, Corollary 17.92 and its proof). The final step, from a finite
power of a torus to a single torus, is not in this module.

## Statement

`ReductiveTorusPowerDominationStatement` is the `Prop`: for every infinite
field `k : Type u` and every group scheme `G` over `Spec k` with
`IsConnectedReductive G`, there are a group scheme `P` over `Spec k`, a natural
number `n` and a morphism `g : finitePower P.X n ⟶ G.X` in `Over (Spec k)` such
that `IsTorus P` and the underlying continuous map of `g.left` has dense range.
Here `finitePower V n` is the categorical `n`-fold product of `V` in
`Over (Spec k)` (the published abbreviation in `ImageGenerationProducts.lean`).
The morphism `g` need not respect any group structure. For `n = 0` the source
is the base `Spec k` itself.

## Auxiliary lemmas

- `prod_ofFn_eq_of_eq_one`: in a monoid, the ordered product
  `(List.ofFn g).prod` of a family `g : Fin m → M` with `g i = 1` for all
  `i ≠ j` equals `g j`. The proof is by induction on `m` with `Fin.cases` on
  `j`.
- `comp_list_prod`: for a monoid object `M` of `Over B` and morphisms
  `a : T ⟶ W`, `l : List (W ⟶ M)`, precomposition commutes with ordered
  products: `a ≫ l.prod = (l.map (a ≫ ·)).prod`. The proof is by induction on
  `l`, with Mathlib's `MonObj.comp_one` and `MonObj.comp_mul`.

## Proof of `reductiveTorusPowerDomination_of_generation`

Let `k` be an infinite field and `G` a connected reductive group over `Spec k`.

1. **Tori.** The generation hypothesis gives `m`, tori `T j` for `j : Fin m`,
   closed subgroup inclusions `e j : T j ⟶ G`, and the generation property.
   Set `T' : ULift (Fin m) → GroupScheme (Spec k)`, `T' j = T j.down`; the
   `ULift` puts the index type in universe `u`, as the Library's `pi`
   requires. Let `P = pi T'` be their finite product. The published
   `IsTorus.pi` (`Torus/Products.lean`) shows that `P` is a torus.
2. **The morphism `f`.** Let `π' j : P.X ⟶ (T j).X` be the `j`-th product
   projection. Define `f : P.X ⟶ G.X` as the ordered product, in the group of
   points `P.X ⟶ G.X`, of the morphisms `π' j ≫ (e j).hom.hom`. On points,
   `f (t_1, …, t_m) = e_1(t_1) ⋯ e_m(t_m)`.
3. **Hypotheses of pointed-image generation.** `G` is smooth and affine over
   `Spec k` (fields of `IsConnectedReductive`).
   - `P.X` is affine over `Spec k`. By `prop_pi_hom` (`ProductProperties.lean`)
     with the property `IsAffineHom`, it suffices that each `(T j).X` is affine.
     Since `(e j).hom.hom` is a morphism over `Spec k`, the structure morphism
     of `(T j).X` is the composite of the closed immersion `(e j).hom.hom.left`
     with the affine structure morphism of `G.X`, and closed immersions are
     affine. The instances that `IsAffineHom` is stable under composition,
     contains identities and respects isomorphisms are supplied explicitly
     (from Mathlib's `IsMultiplicative` and `IsStableUnderBaseChange`
     instances), because instance search does not find them directly.
   - `P.X` is locally of finite type, since a torus is smooth (published
     `IsTorus.smooth`).
   - `P.X` is geometrically integral by `IsTorus.geometricallyIntegral`
     (`Torus/GeometricallyIntegral.lean`).
   - The base point is the identity `1 : Over.mk (𝟙 (Spec k)) ⟶ P.X`. Then
     `1 ≫ f = 1`: by `comp_list_prod` the composite is the ordered product of
     the morphisms `1 ≫ π' j ≫ (e j).hom.hom`, each of which is `1` since
     `π' j` and `(e j).hom.hom` underlie monoid morphisms (`MonObj.one_comp`).

   Pointed-image generation then yields a generated image `D` of `f`: a closed
   subgroup `D.inclusion : D.group ⟶ G` representing the closed envelope of
   `f`, with a factor `D.factor` such that `D.factor ≫ D.inclusion = f`. It
   also yields `n` with `(D.powerMap n).left` dominant.
4. **Every torus factors through `D`.** For each `j`, let
   `s j : (T j).X ⟶ P.X` be the point of `P` whose `j`-th coordinate is the
   identity of `(T j).X` and whose other coordinates are `1`. It is built from
   `Pi.mulSingle` via the published points description `piPointsEquiv`. Then
   `s j ≫ f = (e j).hom.hom`. Again by `comp_list_prod`, the composite is an
   ordered product whose `j`-th factor is `(e j).hom.hom` and whose other
   factors are `1`, so `prod_ofFn_eq_of_eq_one` applies. Hence
   `(e j).hom.hom = (s j ≫ D.factor) ≫ D.inclusion.hom.hom`, that is,
   `FactorsThrough (e j).hom.hom D.inclusion`.
5. **`D` is all of `G`.** The generation property applied to the closed
   subgroup `D.inclusion` gives `SubgroupFunctor.range D.inclusion = ⊤`. In
   particular the identity point `𝟙 G.X` lies in the range, so there is
   `r : G.X ⟶ D.group.X` with `r ≫ D.inclusion.hom.hom = 𝟙 G.X`. Hence the
   underlying map of `D.inclusion.hom.hom.left` is surjective.
6. **Density.** Take `g = orderedPowerMap f n`. By the published
   `GeneratedImage.powerMap_comp`, `g = D.powerMap n ≫ D.inclusion.hom.hom`.
   The underlying map of `(D.powerMap n).left` has dense range (it is
   dominant), and that of `D.inclusion.hom.hom.left` is continuous and
   surjective. The composite therefore has dense range by
   `DenseRange.comp`.

The case `m = 0` needs no separate treatment. Then `P` is the empty product
and `f` is the empty product `1`. The generation property applied to `D`
still forces `D` to be all of `G`, so the argument above goes through
unchanged.
