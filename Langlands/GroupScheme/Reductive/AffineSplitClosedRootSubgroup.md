# Subgroups directly spanned by the root groups of a closed set of roots

This module proves clause 3 of
[`AffineSplitRootSubgroupSpanningStatement`](AffineSplitRootSubgroupSpanningStatement.md):
Conrad, *Reductive group schemes* (2014), Proposition 5.1.16(1), p.147, over an affine base
`Spec R` with `R` any commutative ring. Everything is in
`Langlands.GroupScheme.LocalSplitCell`. The module adds one definition, `rootSpan`, and
theorems. It adds no statements or axioms.

## The span functor

- `rootSpan u Ψ : SubgroupFunctor G`: on a test scheme `Z` over `Spec R`, its points are the
  subgroup of `G(Z)` generated by the root points `u_a(x)` for `a ∈ Ψ` and
  `x ∈ Γ(Z, 𝒪_Z)`. That is, it is the supremum over `a ∈ Ψ` of the ranges of
  `rootPointHom (u a) Z`. Pulling back along `T ⟶ T'` sends generators to generators
  (`comp_rootPoint`), so this is a subgroup functor.
- `iSup_rootFactor_eq_rootSpan`: for an enumeration `e : Fin n ≃ Ψ`, the supremum of the
  root factors `rootFactor u e Z k` (from
  [`LocalSplitHeightSubgroup`](LocalSplitHeightSubgroup.md)) is the span.
- `mem_rootSpan_iff`: suppose that, for an enumeration `e`, a height function
  `w : Fin n → ℕ` and a test scheme `Z`, the commutator of any two root factors lies in the
  subgroup generated by the root factors of strictly larger height. Then a point of `G(Z)`
  lies in the span if and only if it is an ordered product `∏ₖ u_{e k}(c_k)`. This is the
  group-theoretic `GroupTheory.HeightCollection.range_orderedProduct_eq_iSup` from the
  Library.
- `mem_rootSpan_iff_comp`: if the previous description holds on every `Z`, then the points
  of the span are exactly the morphisms that factor through `positiveProductMap u e`.

## Commutator heights

`commutator_rootFactor_le_of_closed`: let `Ψ` be closed with `Ψ ∩ -Ψ = ∅`, and let `ν`
have positive pairing with every root of `Ψ`. Then the commutator bound of
`mem_rootSpan_iff` holds with heights `w k = ⟨α_{e k}, ν⟩`. For `k = l` the commutator is
trivial, since a root group is commutative. For `k ≠ l` the roots `a = e k` and `c = e l`
satisfy `α_c ≠ ±α_a`: `±` because `Ψ ∩ -Ψ = ∅`, and `≠` because the root map is
injective. Clause 4 (`exists_commutator_orderedProduct` in
[`AffineSplitCommutatorRelations`](AffineSplitCommutatorRelations.md)) writes each
commutator `[u_a(x), u_c(y)]` as an ordered product over the roots `d` with
`α_d = p α_a + q α_c` and `p, q > 0`. Each such `d` lies in `Ψ`, by
`mem_of_root_eq_nsmul_add_nsmul` in
[`IntegralClosedRootSetBase`](../../LinearAlgebra/IntegralClosedRootSetBase.md). Its height
`p⟨α_a, ν⟩ + q⟨α_c, ν⟩` is larger than both `⟨α_a, ν⟩` and `⟨α_c, ν⟩`.

## Auxiliary results on fibres

- `isVectorGroupOfDimension_baseChange`: the base change of `𝔾ₐⁿ` along
  `Spec K → Spec R`, for a field `K`, is a vector group of dimension `n`
  (`VectorGroup.baseChangeIso`).
- `connectedSpace_baseChange_vectorGroup`: every field-valued fibre of `𝔾ₐⁿ` is connected.
  The base map is written as `Spec.map` of its ring map (`Spec.preimage`).
- `connectedSpace_baseChange_of_iso`: an isomorphism `V.X ≅ W.X` of underlying schemes over
  the base transports connectedness of base changes from `V` to `W`. It need not respect
  the group laws.

## `exists_closedRootSubgroup` (clause 3)

Let `Ψ` be closed with `Ψ ∩ -Ψ = ∅`. There are a group scheme `H` and a closed subgroup
`j : H ⟶ G` such that the following hold. `H` is smooth over `Spec R`. `H` is normalized
by the torus on points. For every `s ∈ Spec R`, the geometric fibre of `H` at `s` is
connected and unipotent. For every `n` and every enumeration `e : Fin n ≃ Ψ` there is an
isomorphism `φ : 𝔾ₐⁿ ≅ H.X` with `φ ≫ j = ∏ₖ u_{e k}`. This is clause 3 of the target
statement verbatim.

Proof:

1. `exists_base_isPos_of_closed` gives a base `b` for which `Ψ` is positive.
   `exists_coPairing_eq_height` gives a cocharacter `ν` with positive pairing on `Ψ`.
2. By the two previous sections, for every enumeration `e` of `Ψ` the points of
   `rootSpan u Ψ` are exactly the morphisms that factor through `positiveProductMap u e`.
   By [`AffineSplitPositiveProductClosed`](AffineSplitPositiveProductClosed.md), that map
   is a closed immersion, hence a monomorphism.
3. Fix one enumeration `e₀`. The Library's
   `SubgroupFunctor.isClosedRepresentable_of_scheme` (`RepresentabilityTransfer`) gives a
   group scheme `H` and a closed subgroup `j` representing `rootSpan u Ψ`.
4. For every enumeration `e`, both `positiveProductMap u e` and `j` represent the same
   points. The resulting mutual factorizations are inverse isomorphisms, since both maps
   are monomorphisms. This gives the isomorphisms `φ`.
5. Smoothness: `H.X ≅ 𝔾ₐⁿ⁰` over the base, and `𝔾ₐⁿ⁰` is smooth.
6. Torus normalization: a point `h` of `H` maps to an ordered product
   `∏ₖ u_{e₀ k}(c_k)`. By `torus_mul_orderedProduct_mul_inv`, its conjugate by `i(t)` is
   the ordered product with coordinates `α_{e₀ k}(t) c_k`. That product lies in the span,
   so it is `j(h')` for some `h'`.
7. Connected geometric fibres: transport along `H.X ≅ 𝔾ₐⁿ⁰` with
   `connectedSpace_baseChange_of_iso` and `connectedSpace_baseChange_vectorGroup`.
8. Unipotent geometric fibres: every point of the span is an ordered product of roots with
   positive `ν`-pairing. So `orderedProduct_mem_unipotent` puts the image of `j` inside the
   contracting subgroup `U_G(χ)` for `χ = AffineRegularBorel.cocharacter i ν`. The
   theorem `Dynamic.isUnipotent_geometricFiber_of_range_le_unipotent` of the helper module
   [`ContractingClosedSubgroupFibres`](../Dynamic/ContractingClosedSubgroupFibres.md)
   then gives unipotence of every geometric
   fibre of the closed subgroup `H`.

`H` is obtained from an existential statement and is not unfolded to the vector group.
Smoothness and connectedness are transported along the isomorphism `φ` instead.

## Provenance

`rootSpan` is an internal auxiliary definition: the subgroup functor generated by the
root groups `U_a`, `a ∈ Ψ`. Conrad's `U_Ψ` is the closed subgroup representing it, as shown
in steps 3–4. The proof route follows Conrad's proof of Proposition 5.1.16 (pp.147–148).
His descending induction on `ν`-height is replaced by the Library's height-collection
lemma, and his "closed subscheme of `U_{Φ⁺}`" step by
[`AffineSplitPositiveProductClosed`](AffineSplitPositiveProductClosed.md).
