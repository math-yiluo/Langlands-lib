# Fundamental weights in exterior powers of the special-linear column representation

`exteriorColumnRepresentation n d k` is the degree-`d` exterior power of the
standard column representation of `SL_(n+1)` over a field `k`. Its basis is
indexed by the `d`-element subsets of `Fin (n + 1)`. On the determinant-one
diagonal torus, `exteriorWeight s` is the sum of the coordinate characters in
the subset `s`.

`exteriorGL_of_diagonal` computes the exterior action of a diagonal matrix
over any commutative ring. A principal minor is the product of the selected
diagonal entries. In a minor for distinct subsets of the same size, a row
from the second subset outside the first is zero. The resulting exterior
matrix is diagonal, with the products of the selected entries on its diagonal.
Applying this over the coordinate algebra of the torus identifies the
diagonal entries with the universal values of the subset characters.

`exteriorWeight_injective` uses the integral character coordinates of the
determinant-one torus. Equality of two subset characters forces the
difference of their indicator functions to be constant. One subset then
contains the other, and the common cardinality gives equality. The
separating-coefficient lemmas show that every occurring weight is a subset
character and that the corresponding space is one-dimensional, generated by
its basis vector.

With the simple roots numbered by adjacent coordinate differences,
`exteriorColumn_fundamentalWeight` treats the exterior degree `a + 1` for a
vertex `a : Fin n`. The initial `a + 1` coordinates give the integral
fundamental weight at that vertex, by the coordinate fundamental weight
formula. In every increasing subset of this size, each selected coordinate
is at least the corresponding initial coordinate. The difference of the
two subset characters is therefore a sum of adjacent simple roots with
nonnegative coefficients. The theorem gives multiplicity one and this
dominance bound in the actual split pinning.

The calculation follows from the compound minor formula in `MatrixExterior`,
the integral character coordinates in `SpecialLinearFundamentalWeights`, and
the character coefficient comparison in `SeparatingWeightCoefficients`.
