# A finite subgroup of normalizer points inducing the Weyl group

Worker 4348 (Task 6589).

This module works in the namespace `Langlands.GroupScheme.AffineSplitNormalizerWeyl`. It proves
the finiteness half of clause 4 of
[`Reductive.AffineSplitNormalizerWeylStatement`](AffineSplitNormalizerWeylStatement.md)
(Conrad, *Reductive group schemes*, Example 6.4.3). Suppose that for every root `a` a
representative `n_a ∈ G(S)` of the reflection `s_a` with `n_a² ∈ T[2]` is given. Then some finite
subgroup of the normalizer points contains a point inducing every element of the Weyl group.
That subgroup is generated by the representatives `n_b` of a chosen set of simple roots `b` (the
simple roots of a regular functional), not by all the `n_a`. The theorem is
`exists_finite_subgroup_of_representatives`. It holds over every base scheme `S`. The
representatives themselves are an input: this module does not construct them. It adds no
axioms. `lean_check` reports the axioms `propext`, `Classical.choice` and `Quot.sound`.

The subgroup generated by all the `n_a` can be infinite. Over `ℚ`, take `SL₂` with its diagonal
torus. `n_+ = [[0,1],[-1,0]]` and `n_- = [[0,2],[-1/2,0]]` both induce the reflection and both
square to `-1`, but `n_+ n_- = diag(-1/2, -2)` has infinite order. This is why only the simple
representatives are used.

It imports [`Reductive.AffineSplitNormalizerWeylClauses`](AffineSplitNormalizerWeylClauses.md)
(the relation `Induces` and clause 3) and
[`Group.RootPairingCoxeterProof`](../../Group/RootPairingCoxeterProof.md) (the Coxeter
presentation of the Weyl group of a finite crystallographic root pairing over an ordered field).

## Provenance

Everything here is internal to the Library and serves the proof of the published statement.
The mathematical route is the standard Tits-group argument behind the last sentence of Conrad's
Example 6.4.3: the Tits group is a finite extension of `W(Φ)` by `T[2]`. The implemented proof
is different. It uses the Coxeter presentation of `W(Φ)` and a sign computation on the torsion-free
quotient `T(S)/T(S)_tors`, and needs no braid relations. The steps are described below.

## Main theorem

```
theorem exists_finite_subgroup_of_representatives [DecidableEq I] [Finite J] [P.IsReduced]
    (hG : IsReductive G) (hi : IsClosedSubgroup i)
    (hmax : IsFibrewiseMaximalTorus i)
    (n : J → (Over.mk (𝟙 S) ⟶ G.X))
    (hn : ∀ a, Induces i (n a) (RootPairing.Equiv.reflection P a))
    (hsq : ∀ a, ∃ t, t * t = 1 ∧ n a * n a = t ≫ i.hom.hom) :
    ∃ H : Subgroup (Over.mk (𝟙 S) ⟶ G.X),
      H ≤ normalizerPoints i ∧ Finite H ∧ ∀ w ∈ P.weylGroup, ∃ m ∈ H, Induces i m w
```

Here `S` is any scheme, `G` a reductive group scheme over `S`, `i : splitTorus S I ⟶ G` a closed
split torus that is maximal on every fibre, and `P : RootDatum J (I → ℤ) (I → ℤ)` a root datum
with finitely many roots that is reduced. For each root `a`, `n a ∈ G(S)` induces the reflection
`s_a` (in the sense of `Induces`), and `n a * n a` is a point of the torus of order dividing `2`.
The conclusion gives a finite subgroup `H` of the normalizer points that contains, for every
element `w` of the Weyl group, a point inducing `w`. `H` is the image of the subgroup generated
by the `n b` for the simple roots `b` of a regular functional.

`S` need not be affine or connected, and no domain hypothesis is used here.

## Proof

1. **A rational root pairing.** `ratPairing P : RootPairing J ℚ (I → ℚ) (Module.Dual ℚ (I → ℚ))`
   has roots `castVec (P.root a)` (the integer vector cast to `ℚ^I`) and coroots
   `dualOf P (P.coroot a)`, the functional `x ↦ ∑ₗ xₗ ⟨eₗ, a^∨⟩`. The pairing is
   `Module.Dual.eval`, and the reflection permutations are those of `P`. `dualOf_castVec` shows
   `dualOf P y (castVec m) = ⟨m, y⟩`. From it come the axioms and
   `ratPairing_pairing : (ratPairing P).pairing a b = P.pairing a b`, hence crystallographic,
   and `ratPairing_reflection_castVec`: the rational reflection extends the integral one.
2. **Transfer.** `Compat P g g'` says that `g ∈ Aut P` and `g' ∈ Aut (ratPairing P)` act compatibly on
   integral weights. It is stable under products, inverses and powers, it holds for
   corresponding reflections, and it determines `g` (`Compat.eq`, from Mathlib's
   `RootPairing.Equiv.weightHom_injective`).
3. **Coxeter structure (`exists_simple_coxeter`).** Choose `f` nonzero on all roots
   (`Module.exists_dual_forall_apply_ne_zero`) and apply the published
   `Langlands.Group.rootPairingWeylCoxeterStatement_proof` to `ratPairing P`. This gives a finite
   Coxeter system `cs : CoxeterSystem Mx (ratPairing P).weylGroup` on the set `B` of simple roots of `f`, with
   `cs.simple b` the reflection of `b`. Via `Compat`:
   - the integral simple reflections satisfy `(s_a s_b)^{Mx a b} = 1`;
   - every reflection `s_a` of `P` lies in the subgroup generated by the simple ones (closure
     induction on a word for `s_a` in `cs`);
   - for distinct `a, b ∈ B` the roots are linearly independent over `ℤ`. Otherwise reducedness gives
     `root a = - root b`, which contradicts the positivity of `f` on `B`
     (`IsAddIndecomposable.baseOf_subset_pos`). So `P.coxeterWeight a b ≠ 4`
     (`RootPairing.coxeterWeight_ne_four_of_linearIndependent`).
4. **Sign lemma (`isOfFinOrder_of_reflection_pair`).** For `t : I → A` in a commutative group, write
   `charValue t m = ∏ⱼ tⱼ^{mⱼ}`. Then
   `charValue t (s_a m) · charValue t m = charValue t m ^ 2 · charValue t (a) ^ (-⟨m, a^∨⟩)`
   (`charValue_reflection_mul`). If these elements are torsion for all `m`, for two roots `a, b`
   with `⟨a,b^∨⟩⟨b,a^∨⟩ ≠ 4`, then every `tⱼ` is torsion. With `x = charValue t a` and
   `y = charValue t b`, `(x²y^{-q})²(y²x^{-p})^q = x^{4-pq}` (`isOfFinOrder_of_pair`). So `x` is torsion,
   and then `charValue t m ^ 2` is torsion for every `m`.
5. **Finiteness criterion (`finite_of_ker_torsion`).** Let `H ≤ Gt` be finitely generated and
   `f : H →* Q` have finite range. If every kernel element is `τ a` for a torsion `a` and an
   injective homomorphism `τ` from a commutative group, then `H` is finite. The proof: the kernel has
   finite index, so it is finitely generated (Schreier, `Subgroup.fg_of_index_ne_zero`). It embeds
   in the torsion of the commutative group, so it is finite (`CommGroup.finite_of_fg_isMulTorsion`).
6. **Torus points.** `torusHom` sends `x ∈ (Γ(S)ˣ)^I` to the point `x ≫ i` of `G(S)`, which lies
   in `normalizerPoints i`. It does so because the centralizer of `i` is its range
   (`splitTorusCentralizer_eq_range_of_fibrewiseMaximal`, `mem_torusCentralizer_base_iff`).
   `torusHom` is an injective homomorphism. `torsionPoints` is the image of the torsion subgroup.
   It is normal in the normalizer points, because conjugation acts on torus points through `σ`
   (`conj_torusPoint`, from `IsNormalizingPoint.conj_comp`), and group homomorphisms preserve
   torsion.
7. **Products and `Induces`.** `induces_one`, `Induces.mul` (`n` induces `w` and `n'` induces `w'`,
   so `n n'` induces `w' w`), `Induces.inv`, `Induces.pow`. `Induces.mem_centralizerPoints`: a
   point inducing `1` centralizes the torus, since the characters `Pi.single j 1` separate torus
   points (`CommGroupScheme.hom_ext_points`).
8. **The key step (`mem_torsionPoints_of_conj`).** Let `z` induce `1`, and let `n_a`, `n_b` induce
   `s_a`, `s_b` with Coxeter weight `≠ 4`. Assume `n_a z n_a⁻¹ z` and `n_b z n_b⁻¹ z` are
   torsion torus points. Then `z` is a torsion torus point. By clause 3
   (`exists_eq_of_mem_centralizerPoints`) `z = t ≫ i`. Conjugation gives
   `n_c z n_c⁻¹ z = ((t ≫ σ_c) · t) ≫ i`, and `Induces` turns the characters of `t ≫ σ_c` into
   `charValue t (s_c m)`. The sign lemma then applies, and `IsOfFinOrder.pi` concludes.
9. **Assembly.** Let `K = torsionPoints` and `Γ = normalizerPoints ⧸ K`. The images `ν_b` of `n b`
   are involutions in `Γ` (`mem_torsionPoints_of_sq`). For `a ≠ b` in `B`, `z = (n_a n_b)^{Mx a b}`
   induces `(s_b s_a)^{Mx a b} = 1`. The identity `conj_pow_mul_eq_one` (if `x² = y² = 1`,
   conjugation by `x` or `y` inverts `(xy)^m`) gives the two hypotheses of step 8. So
   `(ν_a ν_b)^{Mx a b} = 1`, and `cs.lift` gives `L : (ratPairing P).weylGroup →* Γ`. Let `H₀` be the
   subgroup of the normalizer points generated by the `n b`, `b ∈ B`. It is finitely generated. Its
   image in `Γ` lies in the range of `L`, which is finite. Step 5 shows `H₀` is finite.
   `H = H₀.map N.subtype`. Finally, the elements `w` induced by a point of `H` form a subgroup of
   `Aut P` (step 7). It contains the simple reflections, hence (step 3) every reflection, hence
   `P.weylGroup`.
