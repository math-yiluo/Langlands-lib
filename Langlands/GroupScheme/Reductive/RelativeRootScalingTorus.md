# Exponent projections of split tori over arbitrary rings

This is internal derived material for the torus-conjugacy proof. It constructs maps between finite coordinate split tori using the published diagonalizable group schemes and proves their point formula and fppf geometry. It imposes no field, reducedness, nonzero-ring, or noetherian hypothesis on the commutative ring `R`.

For finite types `I` and `J` with specified `Fintype` instances and integer weights `m : J → I → ℤ`, `splitTorus.exponentLatticeMap m` sends a vector of coefficients to its linear combination of the weights. The actual group-scheme homomorphism `splitTorus.exponentTorusProjection R m : 𝔾_m^I → 𝔾_m^J` transports the diagonalizable restriction map for that lattice homomorphism through the coordinate-torus isomorphisms. This definition is supplied for every family of weights.

The theorem `splitTorus.exponentTorusProjection_points` proves that, on every test scheme `V` over `Spec R`, the output coordinate indexed by `j` is `∏ i, x_i ^ m j i`, with `x_i` the global-unit coordinates of the input point. The proof reuses `RelativeRoot.CharacterFpqc.splitIso_hom_points` for forward compatibility of `Diagonalizable.splitIso` with its natural point model and proves the inverse compatibility. It then computes restriction of a character along the lattice map and uses the explicit inverse of `Diagonalizable.charEquiv` to evaluate the resulting monomial. Test schemes need not be affine.

If the weights are linearly independent over `ℤ`, `splitTorus.exponentTorusProjection_fppf` proves that the underlying scheme morphism is flat, surjective, and locally of finite presentation. Linear independence makes the finite linear-combination map injective. Over a nonzero ring, flatness and surjectivity follow from the existing group-algebra faithful-flatness theorem. For the zero ring, the target scheme is empty, so the morphism is an isomorphism. This case is included in `Diagonalizable.flat_and_surjective_restrictMap_all`.

The finite-presentation argument applies to every map between the finite free lattices. `Diagonalizable.finitePresentation_groupAlgebra` obtains finite presentation of `R[ℤ^I]` over `R` from the smoothness of its split torus. `Diagonalizable.locallyOfFinitePresentation_restrictMap` then uses finite presentation of the source and target group algebras over `R`, together with `RingHom.FinitePresentation.of_comp_finiteType`, to prove finite presentation of the group-algebra map itself. Finally the geometry is transported through the coordinate isomorphisms. The conclusion allows nonsmooth kernels; it does not assert that the exponent projection is smooth or that global points lift. Local lifting and the full isogeny torus-rigidity target are separate results.
