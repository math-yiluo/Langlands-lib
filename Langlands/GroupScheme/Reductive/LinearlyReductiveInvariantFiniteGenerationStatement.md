# Finite generation of invariants of linearly reductive groups

`Langlands.GroupScheme.LinearlyReductiveInvariantFiniteGenerationStatement.{u}`
is a Prop-valued definition. This module defines the proposition and proves
nothing.

## Statement

The proposition says: let `k : Type u` be a field and let
`G : GroupScheme (Spec (CommRingCat.of k))` satisfy
`[LocallyOfFiniteType G.X.hom]` and `[IsAffineHom G.X.hom]`. Then

```text
(∀ (m : ℕ) (σ : MatrixRepresentation G (Fin m)),
    sSup {W : Submodule k (Fin m → k) | σ.IsStableSubspace W ∧ W ≠ ⊥ ∧
      ∀ U : Submodule k (Fin m → k), σ.IsStableSubspace U → U ≤ W →
        U = ⊥ ∨ U = W} = ⊤) →
  ∀ (n : ℕ) (ρ : MatrixRepresentation G (Fin n)),
    ∃ s : Finset (MvPolynomial (Fin n) k),
      (∀ F ∈ s, ρ.IsInvariantPolynomial F) ∧
        ∀ F : MvPolynomial (Fin n) k, ρ.IsInvariantPolynomial F →
          F ∈ Algebra.adjoin k (s : Set (MvPolynomial (Fin n) k))
```

In words: if an affine group scheme `G` of finite type over `k` is linearly
reductive (every finite-dimensional representation is semisimple), then for
every representation `ρ` of `G` on `kⁿ` the `k`-algebra of `ρ`-invariant
polynomials in `n` variables is finitely generated. The finite set `s`
consists of invariant polynomials, and every invariant polynomial is a
polynomial expression in the elements of `s` with coefficients in `k`. Since
the invariant polynomials form a `k`-subalgebra of
`MvPolynomial (Fin n) k` (sums, products and constants of invariant
polynomials are invariant, because evaluation is a ring homomorphism), the
existence of `s` says exactly that this subalgebra is finitely generated.

The ingredients are existing declarations, used without change.

- `MatrixRepresentation G (Fin m)` (from
  [`MatrixRepresentation`](../MatrixRepresentation.md)) is a homomorphism of
  group schemes `σ : G ⟶ GL_m` over `k`, that is, a representation of `G` on
  `kᵐ` with its standard basis. Every finite-dimensional representation is
  isomorphic to one of these, so quantifying over all `m` and all `σ` covers
  all finite-dimensional representations.
- `σ.IsStableSubspace W` (from
  [`MatrixRepresentation.StableSubrepresentation`](../MatrixRepresentation/StableSubrepresentation.md))
  says: for every commutative `k`-algebra `A`, every point `x` of `G` with
  values in `A`, every `v ∈ W` and every `k`-linear map `ℓ : A → k`, applying
  `ℓ` to the coordinates of `σ(x)v` gives a vector of `W`. Since `W ⊗ A` is
  the set of vectors of `Aᵐ` all of whose images under such `ℓ` lie in `W`,
  this says that `σ(x)(W ⊗ A) ⊆ W ⊗ A` for all `A` and `x`. Applying it to
  `x⁻¹` gives equality. So the stable subspaces are exactly the
  subrepresentations.
- The set inside `sSup` is the set of simple subrepresentations of `σ`:
  nonzero stable subspaces `W` whose only stable subspaces contained in `W`
  are `0` and `W`. So the hypothesis says that `kᵐ` is the sum of its simple
  subrepresentations, for every representation `σ`: `G` is linearly
  reductive. The same condition is used in
  `LinearlyReductiveEndomorphismAlgebraStatement` (in
  [`LinearlyReductiveEndomorphismAlgebraStatement`](LinearlyReductiveEndomorphismAlgebraStatement.md),
  Milne's Remark 12.53(b)) and in
  `CharZeroSemisimpleRepresentationIdentityComponentStatement` (in
  [`SemisimpleRepresentationIdentityComponentStatement`](SemisimpleRepresentationIdentityComponentStatement.md)).
- `ρ.IsInvariantPolynomial F` (from
  [`InvariantPolynomialDescent`](InvariantPolynomialDescent.md)), for
  `F : MvPolynomial (Fin n) k`, says: for every scheme `T` over `Spec k`,
  every point `g : T ⟶ G.X` and every vector `x : Fin n → Γ(T, 𝒪_T)`,
  `F(ρ(g) x) = F(x)`, where `ρ(g) = ρ.pointMatrix T g` and `F` is evaluated
  through the structure map `k → Γ(T, 𝒪_T)`. So `F` is a polynomial function
  on `kⁿ` invariant under `G`, scheme-theoretically. By
  `isInvariantPolynomial_iff_universal` (in
  [`InvariantPolynomialUniversal`](InvariantPolynomialUniversal.md)) it is
  equivalent to the single polynomial identity
  `F(ρ(g) X) = F(X)` in `O(G)[X_1, …, X_n]` for the universal point
  `g = 𝟙 G.X`; hence also to the identities `F(ρ(g) X) = F(X)` in
  `R[X_1, …, X_n]` for all commutative `k`-algebras `R` and all
  `g ∈ G(R)`. The same invariance condition, written out without the
  predicate's name, is used in the Library's statement of Haboush's theorem, `ReductiveGeometricReductivityStatement` (in
  [`GeometricReductivityStatement`](GeometricReductivityStatement.md)).
- `Algebra.adjoin k S` (Mathlib) is the `k`-subalgebra generated by a set
  `S`.

For `n = 0` the polynomial ring is `k`, every polynomial is invariant, and
`s = ∅` works.

## Provenance (derived)

Source: J. S. Milne, *Algebraic Groups* (2017), registered as
`milne-2017-algebraic-groups` (SHA-256
`899b0f44f3c3ccfbaf5ed1cf9c180be7a8016b1c0e9709ca0c206304e5bd1cd0`). Printed page
numbers are given; the PDF page is the printed page plus 16.

The statement is Theorem 12.57 together with the first sentence of Aside
12.58. Printed p. 249: "The group GL_n(k) acts linearly on k[T_1, …, T_n] as
follows: let g = (a_ij) ∈ GL_n(k) and let f ∈ k[T_1, …, T_n]; then
(gf)(T_1, …, T_n) = f(T′_1, …, T′_n) with T′_j = Σ_i a_ij T_j."

"THEOREM 12.57 (HILBERT). Let G be a linearly reductive subgroup of GL_n,
and let A = k[T_1, …, T_n]. Then A^G is finitely generated as a k-algebra."

Aside 12.58, printed p. 250: "A similar argument shows, more generally, that
for any linear representation (V, r) of a linearly reductive group G, the
ring Sym(V)^G of invariant polynomials is finitely generated as a
k-algebra."

Definitions used by the source:

- Definition 12.52, printed p. 248: "An algebraic group is linearly reductive
  if every finite-dimensional representation is semisimple." Definition 4.15,
  printed p. 90: "A representation of an algebraic group is simple if it is
  ≠ 0 and its only subrepresentations are 0 and itself. It is semisimple if it
  is a sum of simple subrepresentations."
- Invariants, §4i, printed p. 96: for a representation (V, r) of G,
  "V^G = {v ∈ V | g·v_R = v_R (in V_R) for all k-algebras R and all
  g ∈ G(R)}". Proposition 4.33 (same page): "V^G = {v ∈ V | ρ(v) = v ⊗ 1 in
  V ⊗ O(G)}", for the co-action ρ.
- Conventions: algebraic groups are of finite type ("Conventions and
  notation", printed p. 3: "An algebraic scheme over k (or algebraic
  k-scheme) is a scheme of finite type over k"; printed p. 6: "An algebraic
  group over k is a group object in the category of algebraic schemes over
  k"), and in Chapter 12 they are affine (printed p. 230: "Recall that all
  algebraic groups are affine over a base field k").

The provenance is derived for these reasons.

- Linear reductivity. Milne's Definition 12.52 is the hypothesis, as
  explained above: a finite-dimensional representation is isomorphic to a
  representation on some `kᵐ`, and "semisimple" (Definition 4.15) is the
  equality `sSup {simple stable subspaces} = ⊤`.
- The action and the invariants. The printed formula has `T_j` inside the
  sum `Σ_i a_ij T_j`; read literally it multiplies each `T_j` by the column
  sum `Σ_i a_ij`, which is not an action of `GL_n(k)`. It is a misprint for
  `T′_j = Σ_i a_ij T_i`; the 2022 corrected reprint has the same misprint
  (see below). With this reading, `gf` is `f(gᵀ T)`, where `T` is the column
  of variables. Milne states the action for `g ∈ GL_n(k)`; the same formula
  for `g ∈ GL_n(R)` acting on `R[T_1, …, T_n] = A ⊗ R` defines the
  representation of the algebraic group `GL_n` on `A`, and `A^G` is the
  subspace fixed by `G` in the sense of §4i: the polynomials `f` with
  `f(gᵀ T) = f(T)` in `R[T]` for all `R` and all `g ∈ G(R)`. (Fixing only
  `G(k)` would give a different ring for nonreduced `G`: for `G = μ_p ⊂ GL_1`
  over a field of characteristic `p`, `G(k)` is trivial, while `A^G = k[T^p]`.)
- Milne's ring as a Lean ring of invariants. As `g` runs through `G(R)`, so
  does `h = g⁻¹`, and `gᵀ = (h⁻¹)ᵀ`; and a polynomial is fixed by `g` if and
  only if it is fixed by `g⁻¹`. So Milne's `A^G` is the ring of `F` with
  `F((h⁻¹)ᵀ X) = F(X)` for all `R` and `h ∈ G(R)`, that is, by the
  description of `IsInvariantPolynomial` above, the invariant polynomials of
  the representation `ρ = i.dual`, where `i : G ⟶ GL_n` is the inclusion and
  `MatrixRepresentation.dual` (in
  [`MatrixRepresentationDual`](../MatrixRepresentationDual.md)) is
  the representation with point matrices the inverse transposes
  (`dual_pointMatrix`, `GeneralLinear.matrixContragredient`).
- More general representations. The Lean statement quantifies over every
  representation `ρ : G ⟶ GL_n` of an abstract linearly reductive `G`, not
  only over subgroups of `GL_n`; this is Aside 12.58. For a representation
  `V = kⁿ` with matrices `ρ(g)`, `Sym(V)` is the polynomial ring in the basis
  vectors, on which `g` substitutes `ρ(g)ᵀ X` for the column of variables
  `X`; by the same argument, `Sym(V)^G` is the Lean ring of invariants of
  `ρ.dual`. The Lean ring of invariants of `ρ` itself is the ring of
  invariant polynomial functions on `V`, which is `Sym(V^∨)^G`. Both kinds of
  rings are covered, since `ρ.dual` is again a representation. Milne gives
  12.58 without proof ("a similar argument shows"); the argument below
  applies to every `ρ`. The 2017 wording "any linear representation" is
  read as "any finite-dimensional linear representation", as in the 2022
  reprint: for an infinite-dimensional `V` with trivial action, `Sym(V)^G`
  is a polynomial ring in infinitely many variables, which is not finitely
  generated.
- "Finitely generated as a k-algebra" is the existence of the finite set `s`
  of generators, as explained above.
- One universe `u` is used for `k` and the schemes. This is a formalization
  convention.

Milne's proof (printed p. 249) uses the Reynolds operator, introduced on the
same page: "Let G be a linearly reductive group and (V, r) a representation
of G. Then V has a unique decomposition V = V^G ⊕ V′ with V′ equal to the sum
of all simple subrepresentations on which G acts nontrivially. The Reynolds
operator is the unique linear map ρ: V → V^G with ρ|V^G = id and
ρ(V′) = 0." (Here Milne's `ρ` is the Reynolds operator, not a
representation.) The proof applies `ρ` to an expression
`f = r_1 g_1 + ⋯ + r_m g_m` and uses `ρ(r_i g_i) = ρ(r_i) g_i`, which it does
not prove; it also writes
"a_i ∈ A" for "r_i ∈ A". The official errata snapshot (below) records both:
"The proof of 12.57 is missing a step: first show that the Reynolds operator
is A^G-linear. Also a_i should be r_i." The 2022 reprint supplies the missing
step. The section at the end describes the corrected argument.

Relation to the Library. The Library has no finite generation result for
rings of invariants. Related declarations:

- `ReductiveGeometricReductivityStatement` (Haboush's theorem, in
  [`GeometricReductivityStatement`](GeometricReductivityStatement.md)) uses
  the same invariance condition, written out, to produce single invariant
  polynomials separating a fixed vector from `0`; it does not assert finite
  generation.
- `NagataLinearlyReductiveStatement` (in
  [`Torus.NagataLinearlyReductiveStatement`](../Torus/NagataLinearlyReductiveStatement.md))
  characterizes the groups satisfying the hypothesis in characteristic
  `p > 0`, and `CommutativeLinearlyReductiveMultiplicativeTypeStatement` (in
  [`Torus.LinearlyReductiveMultiplicativeTypeStatement`](../Torus/LinearlyReductiveMultiplicativeTypeStatement.md))
  the commutative ones; both use the same linear-reductivity condition.

## For the later proof (not part of this unit)

A proof of the Lean statement can follow Milne's corrected argument. Write
`A = MvPolynomial (Fin n) k`, `A_d` for the homogeneous polynomials of degree
`d`, and `A^G` for the invariant polynomials of `ρ`.

- Graded pieces. Substituting `ρ(g) X` for `X` preserves degrees, so each
  `A_d` (finite-dimensional, with the basis of monomials) is a
  representation of `G`, and the homogeneous components of an invariant
  polynomial are invariant. Hence `A^G` is spanned by its homogeneous
  elements, and `A^G ∩ A_d` is the fixed subspace of `A_d`. A proof must
  realize `A_d` as a matrix representation after choosing a basis.
- Reynolds operator. By linear reductivity, each `A_d` is the direct sum of
  its fixed subspace and the sum `A_d′` of its simple subrepresentations
  with nontrivial action; let `P : A → A^G` be the projection that is the
  identity on fixed vectors and zero on every `A_d′`. Every simple
  subrepresentation of `A_d` with nontrivial action lies in `A_d′` (its
  projection to the fixed subspace is an equivariant map from a simple
  nontrivial representation to a trivial one, hence zero).
- `A^G`-linearity. Let `f ∈ A^G` be homogeneous of degree `e` and `a` lie in
  a simple subrepresentation `V ⊆ A_d′`. If `f ≠ 0`, multiplication by `f` is
  an injective equivariant map `V → A_{d+e}`, so `fV` is a simple
  subrepresentation with nontrivial action and lies in `A_{d+e}′`; so
  `P(fa) = 0 = f P(a)`. For `a` fixed, `P(fa) = fa = f P(a)`. By linearity
  `P(fa) = f P(a)` for all `f ∈ A^G` and `a ∈ A`.
- Finite generation. Let `𝔞 ⊆ A` be the ideal generated by the homogeneous
  invariants of positive degree. By the Hilbert basis theorem it is
  generated by finitely many of them, `g_1, …, g_m`; take `s = {g_1, …, g_m}`.
  For homogeneous `f ∈ A^G` of degree `d`, show `f ∈ k[g_1, …, g_m]` by
  induction on `d`: for `d = 0`, `f` is a constant; for `d > 0`, `f ∈ 𝔞`, so
  `f = r_1 g_1 + ⋯ + r_m g_m` with each `r_i` homogeneous of degree
  `d - deg g_i < d` (or zero), and applying `P` gives
  `f = P(r_1) g_1 + ⋯ + P(r_m) g_m` with `P(r_i) ∈ A^G` of degree `< d`.
