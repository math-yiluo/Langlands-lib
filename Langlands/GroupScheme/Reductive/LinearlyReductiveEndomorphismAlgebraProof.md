# Proof: linear reductivity via semisimple endomorphism algebras

This module proves the Library statement
`Langlands.GroupScheme.LinearlyReductiveEndomorphismAlgebraStatement` (Milne, *Algebraic
Groups*, Remark 12.53(b)) at every universe `u`, as the theorem
`Langlands.GroupScheme.linearlyReductiveEndomorphismAlgebra`. The statement's Lean/Markdown pair
and the pairs it imports are unchanged.

The proof uses no hypotheses beyond those of the statement. It does not use
`[LocallyOfFiniteType G.X.hom]`. The direction (⇒) uses no hypothesis on `G` at all. The
direction (⇐) uses `[IsAffineHom G.X.hom]`, which the Library needs to build quotient
representations and to move a representation to standard coordinates `Fin N`.

Throughout, `k : Type u` is a field and `G` is a group scheme over `Spec k`. `ρ` is a
`MatrixRepresentation G n` for a finite coordinate type `n`. `Intertwines ρ σ C` and
`IsStableSubspace ρ W` are the Library predicates used in the statement. `ρ(x)` is the matrix of
`ρ` at a point `x` of `G` with values in a commutative `k`-algebra.

## Route

The two sides of the statement are compared through one auxiliary algebra. The *coefficient
subalgebra* `A_ρ` is the `k`-subalgebra of `End_k(kⁿ)` generated by the operators
`v ↦ ℓ(ρ(x) v)`. Here `ℓ` is a `k`-linear functional on a test algebra `S` and `x` is an
`S`-point of `G`. Then:

1. The stable subspaces of `ρ` are exactly the `A_ρ`-submodules of `kⁿ`. So the left side of
   the statement, for `ρ`, says that `kⁿ` is a semisimple `A_ρ`-module.
2. The intertwiners of `ρ` with itself are exactly the `A_ρ`-linear endomorphisms of `kⁿ`. So
   the endomorphism algebra of `ρ` is the image of `End_{A_ρ}(kⁿ)` under a surjective ring
   homomorphism.

(⇒) follows from Mathlib's theorem that the endomorphism ring of a finitely generated
semisimple module is a semisimple ring.

(⇐) shows that every stable subspace `W` has a stable complement, so that the lattice of
`A_ρ`-submodules is complemented, which is Mathlib's definition of a semisimple module. The
complement is the image of an equivariant section `β : ρ/W → ρ` of the quotient map. The
section comes from one fact about semisimple rings: for every `x` there is a `y` with
`x y x = x`. This fact is applied to one endomorphism of the direct sum `ρ ⊕ ρ/W`.

## A ring-theoretic lemma

`Langlands.GroupScheme.LinearlyReductiveEndomorphismAlgebraProof.exists_mul_mul_eq_self`: in a
semisimple ring `R`, every `x` has a `y` with `x * y * x = x`.

Proof. The left ideal `R x` has a complementary left ideal `L` (`exists_isCompl`). Write
`1 = y x + b` with `b ∈ L`. Then `x b = x − x y x`. The left side lies in `L`, since `L` is a
left ideal. The right side lies in `R x`. So `x b = 0`, that is, `x y x = x`.

## Helper declarations

All remaining helpers are in the namespace
`Langlands.GroupScheme.MatrixRepresentation.LinearlyReductiveEndomorphismAlgebraProof`. They are
cited below by their short names. They are kept out of `MatrixRepresentation` itself because the
Library already declares a `MatrixRepresentation.intertwines_one`, in a module this file does not
import.

### The endomorphism algebra

- `intertwines_one`, `Intertwines.add`, `Intertwines.smul`: the identity matrix intertwines
  `ρ` with itself, and intertwiners from `ρ` to `σ` are closed under sums and scalar multiples.
  Each follows because extending scalars `Matrix k → Matrix S` is a ring homomorphism.
- `endSubalgebra ρ`: the subalgebra of `Matrix n n k` whose carrier is
  `{C | Intertwines ρ ρ C}`. Closure under products is the Library's `Intertwines.comp`.
  `mem_endSubalgebra` unfolds membership.
- `adjoin_intertwines_eq`: `Algebra.adjoin k {C | Intertwines ρ ρ C} = endSubalgebra ρ`, by
  `Algebra.adjoin_eq`. The statement is phrased with `Algebra.adjoin`; this lemma rewrites it.

### Binary direct sums

The Library's `MatrixRepresentation.directSum` is indexed by a sigma type. Here a binary direct
sum on `n ⊕ m` is built the same way.

- `binarySumMatrixHom`, `binarySumMatrixHom_naturality`, `binarySumPoints`,
  `binarySumPoints_naturality`: the block-diagonal matrices `fromBlocks ρ(x) 0 0 σ(x)` form a
  monoid homomorphism to the points of `GL_{n ⊕ m}`, natural in the test scheme.
- `binarySum ρ σ : MatrixRepresentation G (n ⊕ m)`, built with the Library's `homOfPoints`.
  `binarySum_pointMatrix_val` gives its point matrices.
- `sectionsFun`, `sectionsFun_zero`, `affineMatrix_eq_map_pointMatrix`: the matrix of a
  representation at an `A`-point equals its point matrix read through the Library's
  `Corepresented.affineSectionsAlgEquiv A`. `sectionsFun` is this isomorphism as a function on
  global sections. Typing it this way lets `Matrix.fromBlocks_map` rewrite in the next lemma.
- `binarySum_affineMatrix`: at an `A`-point `x`, the matrix of `binarySum ρ σ` is
  `fromBlocks ρ(x) 0 0 σ(x)`.
- `intertwines_binarySum_inl`, `intertwines_binarySum_inr`, `intertwines_binarySum_fst`,
  `intertwines_binarySum_snd`: the block inclusions `fromRows 1 0`, `fromRows 0 1` and the block
  projections `fromCols 1 0`, `fromCols 0 1` are intertwiners.
- `exists_fin_intertwined` (assumes `[IsAffineHom G.X.hom]`): every representation `τ₀` on a
  finite coordinate type `o` is isomorphic to a representation `τ` on some `Fin N`. Concretely,
  there are intertwiners `B : τ → τ₀` and `C : τ₀ → τ` with `C B = 1` and `B C = 1`. Proof: apply
  the Library's `exists_subspace_split_coordinates` to the subspace `⊤`, and restrict `τ₀` along
  the resulting coordinates (`restrictCoordinates`, `intertwines_restrictCoordinates`). `B` is
  injective (`coordinateInclusion_injective`) and `B C = 1`, so `C` intertwines by the Library's
  `Intertwines.of_comp_injective`.
- `exists_binarySum_fin` (assumes `[IsAffineHom G.X.hom]`): for `ρ` on `Fin a` and `σ` on
  `Fin b`, there is `τ` on some `Fin N` with intertwining inclusions `i₁ : ρ → τ`, `i₂ : σ → τ` and
  projections `p₁ : τ → ρ`, `p₂ : τ → σ` such that `p₁ i₁ = 1` and `p₂ i₂ = 1`. Apply
  `exists_fin_intertwined` to `binarySum ρ σ` and compose with the block maps. The hypothesis of
  the statement only concerns representations on `Fin N`, which is why this reindexing is needed.

### Coefficient operators and the coefficient subalgebra

- `CoefficientIndex G`: triples `(S, x, ℓ)` of a commutative `k`-algebra `S : CommAlgCat.{u}`,
  an `S`-point `x` of `G` and a `k`-linear map `ℓ : S → k`.
- `coefficientOperator ρ (S, x, ℓ) : Module.End k (n → k)` sends `v` to the vector
  `i ↦ ℓ((ρ(x) v)_i)`, where `v` is viewed in `Sⁿ`. `coefficientOperator_apply` unfolds it.
- `isStableSubspace_iff`: `IsStableSubspace ρ W` holds iff `W` is invariant under every
  coefficient operator. This is a restatement of the Library definition.
- `Intertwines.mulVec_coefficientOperator`: if `C` intertwines `ρ` with `σ`, then
  `C (coefficientOperator ρ ξ v) = coefficientOperator σ ξ (C v)`. This is the Library's
  `Intertwines.linearFunctional_mulVec`.
- `intertwines_of_mulVec_coefficientOperator`: the converse. Fix `S`, `x` and an entry `(i, j)`
  of `C ρ(x) − σ(x) C`, an element of `S`. Since `S` is a `k`-vector space, the entry is zero if
  every `k`-linear `ℓ : S → k` kills it (`Module.forall_dual_apply_eq_zero_iff`). Apply the
  commutation hypothesis to the basis vector `e_j` and to `ξ = (S, x, ℓ)`, and move `ℓ` past the
  `k`-matrix `C` with the Library's `linearFunctional_mulVec_rectangular`. This gives
  `ℓ((C ρ(x))_{ij}) = ℓ((σ(x) C)_{ij})`.
- `coefficientSubalgebra ρ := Algebra.adjoin k (Set.range (coefficientOperator ρ))`, a
  subalgebra of `Module.End k (n → k)` (the algebra `A_ρ` above). `kⁿ` is a module over it
  directly. `coefficientOperator_mem` records that the generators are members. An instance
  records that `kⁿ` is a finite `A_ρ`-module (`Module.Finite.of_restrictScalars_finite`), which
  `IsSemisimpleRing.moduleEnd` needs.
- `apply_mem_of_isStableSubspace` and `Intertwines.mulVec_apply_of_mem`: by induction on
  `Algebra.adjoin`, every element of `A_ρ` preserves every stable subspace and commutes with
  every self-intertwiner. Both properties hold for the generators and are preserved by scalars,
  sums and products.
- An explicit `AddCommGroup` instance for `A_ρ`-submodules of `kⁿ`. Instance search does not find
  `Submodule.addCommGroup` by itself here, because the semiring structure of a subalgebra is not
  syntactically the one underlying its ring structure.
- `toSubspace p := p.restrictScalars k` and `ofStable W hW` translate between `A_ρ`-submodules
  and stable subspaces. Supporting lemmas: `mem_toSubspace`, `isStableSubspace_toSubspace`,
  `toSubspace_ofStable`, `toSubspace_injective`, `toSubspace_le_iff`, `toSubspace_eq_bot_iff`,
  `toSubspace_sSup`, `toSubspace_eq_top_iff`. The bottom, top and supremum lemmas come from
  Mathlib's `Submodule.restrictScalars_*` lemmas.
- `simpleStableSubspaces_eq`: the set inside the `sSup` of the statement equals the image under
  `toSubspace` of the simple `A_ρ`-submodules. The proof uses `isSimpleModule_iff_isAtom`.
- `sSup_simpleStableSubspaces_eq_top_iff`: the left-side condition of the statement for `ρ` is
  equivalent to `IsSemisimpleModule (coefficientSubalgebra ρ) (n → k)`. This combines the
  previous lemma with Mathlib's `sSup_simples_eq_top_iff_isSemisimpleModule`.
- `Intertwines.toLinearMap`: a self-intertwiner `C` gives the `A_ρ`-linear map `v ↦ C v`, by
  `Intertwines.mulVec_apply_of_mem`.
- `intertwines_toMatrix'`: the matrix of an `A_ρ`-linear endomorphism `f` intertwines `ρ` with
  itself. `f` commutes with the generators, so `intertwines_of_mulVec_coefficientOperator`
  applies.
- `endRingHom ρ : Module.End A_ρ (n → k) →+* endSubalgebra ρ` takes the matrix
  (`LinearMap.toMatrix'`) of the underlying `k`-linear map. `endRingHom_surjective`: a
  self-intertwiner `C` is the image of `Intertwines.toLinearMap`, by `LinearMap.toMatrix'_toLin'`.
- `isSemisimpleRing_endSubalgebra`: if `kⁿ` is a semisimple `A_ρ`-module, then `endSubalgebra ρ`
  is a semisimple ring. Mathlib's `IsSemisimpleRing.moduleEnd` makes `End_{A_ρ}(kⁿ)` semisimple,
  and `RingHom.isSemisimpleRing_of_surjective` passes semisimplicity along `endRingHom ρ`.
- `isSemisimpleModule_of_stable_complements`: if every stable subspace `W` has a stable `W'` with
  `IsCompl W W'`, then `kⁿ` is a semisimple `A_ρ`-module. For an `A_ρ`-submodule `p`, take the
  complement `W'` of `toSubspace p`. Then `ofStable W'` is a complement of `p`, because
  disjointness and the sum condition depend only on the underlying sets.

### Stable complements

`exists_stable_complement` (assumes `[IsAffineHom G.X.hom]`). Suppose the endomorphism algebra of
every representation on standard coordinates is semisimple. Let `ρ` be on `Fin a` and let `W` be
stable. Then `W` has a stable complement.

Proof.

1. The Library's `nonempty_quotientRepresentation` gives a quotient representation
   `q.representation` on some `Fin d`. It comes with an intertwining projection `P` with kernel
   `W` and a matrix `s` with `P s = 1`.
2. `exists_binarySum_fin` gives `τ ≅ ρ ⊕ q.representation` on `Fin N`, with `i₁`, `i₂`, `p₁`,
   `p₂`.
3. `X = i₂ P p₁` is a self-intertwiner of `τ`. By hypothesis its endomorphism algebra is
   semisimple, so `exists_mul_mul_eq_self` gives a self-intertwiner `Y` with `X Y X = X`. Its
   membership in the algebra is converted with `adjoin_intertwines_eq`.
4. Let `β = p₁ Y i₂`, an intertwiner from `q.representation` to `ρ`. Multiply `X Y X = X` by
   `p₂` on the left and `i₁` on the right, and use `p₂ i₂ = 1` and `p₁ i₁ = 1`. This gives
   `P β P = P`. Multiplying on the right by `s` gives `P β = 1`.
5. The range of `β` is stable (the Library's `Intertwines.isStableSubspace_range`). It meets `W`
   only in `0`: if `β w ∈ W = ker P` then `w = P β w = 0`. Every `v` is
   `(v − β P v) + β P v`, and the first term lies in `ker P = W`. So the range of `β` is a
   complement of `W`.

## The main theorem

`linearlyReductiveEndomorphismAlgebra`. Fix `k`, `G` and the two instance hypotheses.

- (⇒) Given the left side and `ρ` on `Fin n`, rewrite the goal with `adjoin_intertwines_eq`.
  `sSup_simpleStableSubspaces_eq_top_iff` turns the hypothesis for `ρ` into semisimplicity of
  `kⁿ` over `A_ρ`, and `isSemisimpleRing_endSubalgebra` concludes.
- (⇐) Given the right side and `ρ` on `Fin n`, rewrite the goal with
  `sSup_simpleStableSubspaces_eq_top_iff`. Then apply `isSemisimpleModule_of_stable_complements`
  with the complements from `exists_stable_complement`.

## Relation to the literature

Source: J. S. Milne, *Algebraic Groups: The Theory of Group Schemes of Finite Type over a Field*,
2022 corrected reprint, registered as `milne-2022-algebraic-groups` (SHA-256
`f2ddd8fa4d263085f173934664b246007a2c0bd539739b7c82de39bfb5d21f40`). The cited passages are
Remark 12.53(b), printed p. 248 (PDF p. 259), and Remark 4.18, printed p. 90 (PDF p. 101). Both
were read in this PDF. The errata snapshot `milne-2022-algebraic-groups-errata-2026-11-04`
(SHA-256 `78e5f297fb2c80c6dacf413fc5abd7f82ba3dd5e5f83b5a5205dfd93bf160d49`) has no entry for
either page. The statement's own Markdown documents how the Lean statement corresponds to
Milne's.

Milne proves the necessity through Remark 4.18. The isotypic decomposition of a semisimple
representation gives `End(V) ≅ ∏ M_{s_i}(D_i)` with division algebras `D_i`. The Lean (⇒) has
the same mathematical content. It first identifies `End(V)` with the endomorphisms of a
semisimple module, and then uses Mathlib's `IsSemisimpleRing.moduleEnd`, which Mathlib derives
from the same Wedderburn–Artin decomposition.

For the sufficiency, the Lean route differs from Milne's. Milne first shows that a nonzero map
`α : V → W` has some `β` with `α ∘ β ≠ 0`, because otherwise a nonzero nilpotent right ideal would
exist in `End(V ⊕ W)`. He then applies this to the quotient by a maximal subrepresentation, uses
Schur's lemma to normalize `α ∘ β` to the identity, and inducts on the dimension. The Lean proof
instead uses that every element of a semisimple ring has a quasi-inverse (`x y x = x`). This
gives a section of the quotient map `V → V/W` for every subrepresentation `W` at once, so no
maximal subrepresentation, Schur's lemma or induction is needed. It then uses the definition of
semisimple modules as modules with a complemented submodule lattice. The coefficient subalgebra
`A_ρ`, which recasts subrepresentations and endomorphisms as module-theoretic notions, is an
internal device of this proof and does not appear in Milne.
