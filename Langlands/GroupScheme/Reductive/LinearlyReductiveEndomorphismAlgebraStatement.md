# Linear reductivity via semisimple endomorphism algebras

`Langlands.GroupScheme.LinearlyReductiveEndomorphismAlgebraStatement.{u}` is a
Prop-valued definition. This module defines the proposition and proves
nothing.

## Statement

The proposition says: let `k : Type u` be a field and let
`G : GroupScheme (Spec (CommRingCat.of k))` satisfy
`[LocallyOfFiniteType G.X.hom]` and `[IsAffineHom G.X.hom]`. Then

```text
(∀ (n : ℕ) (ρ : MatrixRepresentation G (Fin n)),
    sSup {W : Submodule k (Fin n → k) | ρ.IsStableSubspace W ∧ W ≠ ⊥ ∧
      ∀ U : Submodule k (Fin n → k), ρ.IsStableSubspace U → U ≤ W →
        U = ⊥ ∨ U = W} = ⊤) ↔
  ∀ (n : ℕ) (ρ : MatrixRepresentation G (Fin n)),
    IsSemisimpleRing (Algebra.adjoin k
      {C : Matrix (Fin n) (Fin n) k | MatrixRepresentation.Intertwines ρ ρ C}).
```

In words: an affine group scheme `G` of finite type over a field `k` is
linearly reductive (every finite-dimensional representation is semisimple)
if and only if the endomorphism algebra of every finite-dimensional
representation of `G` is a semisimple ring.

The ingredients are existing declarations, used without change.

- `MatrixRepresentation G (Fin n)` (from
  [`MatrixRepresentation`](../MatrixRepresentation.md)) is a homomorphism of
  group schemes `ρ : G ⟶ GL_n` over `k`, that is, a representation of `G` on
  `kⁿ` with its standard basis. Every finite-dimensional representation is
  isomorphic to one of these, so quantifying over all `n` and all `ρ` covers
  all finite-dimensional representations.
- `ρ.IsStableSubspace W` (from
  [`MatrixRepresentation.StableSubrepresentation`](../MatrixRepresentation/StableSubrepresentation.md))
  says: for every commutative `k`-algebra `A`, every point `x` of `G` with
  values in `A`, every `v ∈ W` and every `k`-linear map `ℓ : A → k`, applying
  `ℓ` to the coordinates of `ρ(x)v` gives a vector of `W`. Since `W ⊗ A` is
  the set of vectors of `Aⁿ` all of whose images under such `ℓ` lie in `W`,
  this says that `ρ(x)(W ⊗ A) ⊆ W ⊗ A` for all `A` and `x`. Applying it to
  `x⁻¹` gives equality. So the stable subspaces are exactly the subspaces
  stable under `G` in Milne's sense, that is, the subrepresentations.
- The set inside `sSup` is the set of simple subrepresentations of `ρ`:
  nonzero stable subspaces `W` whose only stable subspaces contained in `W`
  are `0` and `W`. So the left side of the equivalence says that every `kⁿ`
  is the sum of its simple subrepresentations, for every representation
  `ρ`. For `n = 0` the condition holds trivially (the empty sum). The same
  condition is used in `CharZeroSemisimpleRepresentationIdentityComponentStatement`
  (in
  [`Reductive.SemisimpleRepresentationIdentityComponentStatement`](SemisimpleRepresentationIdentityComponentStatement.md)).
- `MatrixRepresentation.Intertwines ρ ρ C` (from
  [`Lie.RepresentationSubquotient`](../Lie/RepresentationSubquotient.md)),
  for a matrix `C` with entries in `k`, says: for every commutative
  `k`-algebra `S` and every point `x` of `G` with values in `S`, the image of
  `C` in `Matrix (Fin n) (Fin n) S` commutes with the matrix `ρ(x)`. So `C`
  is a `k`-linear map `kⁿ → kⁿ` whose scalar extension to every `S`
  commutes with the action of `G(S)`. These matrices are closed under sums,
  products and multiplication by elements of `k`, and contain the identity
  matrix, because extension of scalars `Matrix (Fin n) (Fin n) k →
  Matrix (Fin n) (Fin n) S` is a ring homomorphism. So
  `Algebra.adjoin k {C | Intertwines ρ ρ C}`, the `k`-subalgebra of
  `Matrix (Fin n) (Fin n) k` generated by the set, is the set itself: the
  endomorphism algebra of `ρ`. The same subalgebra is used, under the name
  `EndAlg`, in `SemisimpleRepresentationFieldExtensionStatement` (in
  [`Reductive.SemisimpleRepresentationFieldExtensionStatement`](SemisimpleRepresentationFieldExtensionStatement.md),
  Milne's Proposition 4.19).
- `IsSemisimpleRing R` (Mathlib) says that `R`, as a left module over
  itself, is semisimple: every left ideal has a complementary left ideal.
  It is applied to the subalgebra viewed as a ring. For `n = 0` the
  endomorphism algebra is the zero ring, which is semisimple.

## Provenance (derived)

Main source: J. S. Milne, *Algebraic Groups: The Theory of Group Schemes of
Finite Type over a Field*, 2022 corrected reprint, in the project literature
folder, registered as `milne-2022-algebraic-groups` (SHA-256
`f2ddd8fa4d263085f173934664b246007a2c0bd539739b7c82de39bfb5d21f40`). Printed
page numbers are given; the PDF page is the printed page plus 11.

Remark 12.53(b), printed p. 248, reads: "An algebraic group G over k is
linearly reductive if and only if the endomorphism algebra of every
finite-dimensional representation of G is semisimple. The necessity is shown
in 4.18, and we now prove the sufficiency." The proof that follows is
summarized in the last section. The Lean statement is the first sentence.

Definition 12.52, printed p. 248: "An algebraic group is linearly reductive
if every finite-dimensional representation is semisimple." Opening of
Chapter 12, printed p. 230: "Recall that all algebraic groups are affine
over a base field k." Definition 4.15, printed p. 90: "A representation of
an algebraic group is simple if it is ≠ 0 and its only subrepresentations
are 0 and itself. It is semisimple if it is a sum of simple
subrepresentations."

Remark 4.18, printed p. 90, defines the endomorphisms: "Let (V, r) be a
finite-dimensional representation of an algebraic group G over k. By an
endomorphism of (V, r) we mean a k-linear map α: V → V such that
α_R: V_R → V_R commutes with the action of G(R) on V_R for all k-algebras
R. Equivalently, it is an endomorphism of the corresponding comodule." It
continues: "If (V, r) is semisimple and (V, r) = ⨁_i (V_i, r_i)^{s_i} is
the decomposition of V into its isotypic components, then
End(V, r) = ∏_i M_{s_i}(D_i) with D_i = End(V_i, r_i), which is
semisimple." Here each `D_i` is, by the preceding sentence of the remark, a
finite-dimensional division algebra over `k`.

The 2017 edition (`milne-2017-algebraic-groups`, SHA-256
`899b0f44f3c3ccfbaf5ed1cf9c180be7a8016b1c0e9709ca0c206304e5bd1cd0`; the PDF page
is the printed page plus 16) has Definition 12.52 and Remark 12.53(b) on
printed p. 248, and Definition 4.15 and Remark 4.18 on printed p. 90. The
first sentence of Remark 12.53(b) and Definitions 4.15 and 12.52 have the
same wording as in the 2022 reprint. The differences are: the second
sentence of 12.53(b) reads "The necessity is obvious (4.18), and we prove
the sufficiency."; in the proof, "let W be a maximal subrepresentation"
(2022: "… of V") and "we may suppose that it equals id_{V/W}" (2022: "we
may modify it to equal id_{V/W}"); and Remark 4.18 says "for all small
k-algebras R" (2022: "for all k-algebras R").

Conventions for representations, from the 2017 edition (the same passages
are cited in the Markdown of `LinearlyReductiveFieldExtensionStatement`,
submitted separately by the same author):

- Notations, printed p. 3: "All k-algebras and R-algebras are required to
  be commutative and finitely generated unless it is specified otherwise."
  and "An algebraic scheme over k (or algebraic k-scheme) is a scheme of
  finite type over k". Printed p. 6: "An algebraic group over k is a group
  object in the category of algebraic schemes over k." Printed p. 5 calls
  the `k`-algebras of the form `k[T_0, …, T_n]/a` small `k`-algebras and
  states that the inclusion of the category of small `k`-algebras into the
  category of `k`-algebras is an equivalence of categories.
- Chapter 4 opens, printed p. 83, with "Throughout this chapter, G is an
  affine algebraic group over a field k." On the same page: "A linear
  representation of G is a homomorphism r: G → GL_V of group-valued functors.
  When V is finite-dimensional, r is a homomorphism of algebraic groups."
  Printed p. 41: "If V has finite dimension n, then the choice of a basis for
  V determines an isomorphism GL_V → GL_n".
- Subrepresentations. The proof of Corollary 4.8, printed p. 86, states: "A
  subspace W of V is stable under G if and only if it is an O(G)-subcomodule
  of V". These subspaces are the subrepresentations.

The provenance is derived for these reasons:

- The group. Milne's `G` is an affine group scheme of finite type over `k`
  (the affineness convention of Chapter 12). The Lean statement assumes
  `[IsAffineHom G.X.hom]` and `[LocallyOfFiniteType G.X.hom]`; since
  `Spec k` is quasi-compact and `G` is affine over it, the two together say
  that `G` is of finite type over `k`. Smoothness is not assumed, as in
  Milne.
- Representations and linear reductivity. Every finite-dimensional
  representation `(V, r)` is isomorphic, after the choice of a basis of `V`,
  to a homomorphism `G → GL_n`, and an isomorphism of representations
  carries simple subrepresentations to simple subrepresentations and
  induces an isomorphism of endomorphism algebras. So the two sides of the
  equivalence are Definition 12.52 and the condition on endomorphism
  algebras for all finite-dimensional representations.
- Endomorphisms. Milne's endomorphisms commute with `G(R)` on `V_R` for all
  `k`-algebras `R` (small `k`-algebras in 2017), which by the convention of
  p. 3 are commutative and finitely generated. `Intertwines ρ ρ C` asks
  this for every commutative `k`-algebra in universe `u`. The two conditions
  agree: both imply the condition for the coordinate algebra `O(G)`, which is
  a finitely generated `k`-algebra, at its universal point (the identity of
  `G`); conversely, every point of `G` with values in `S` is the image of the
  universal point under a `k`-algebra map `O(G) → S`, and the matrix
  `ρ(x)` is natural in `S` (`MatrixRepresentation.affineMatrix_naturality`),
  so the identity for the universal point gives it for every `S`.
- Semisimple algebra. Milne does not define "semisimple" for algebras in the
  cited passages; Remark 4.18 calls a finite product of matrix algebras over
  finite-dimensional division algebras semisimple, and the proof of 12.53(b)
  uses that a semisimple algebra has no nonzero nilpotent right ideal. The
  Lean statement uses Mathlib's `IsSemisimpleRing`. For a finite-dimensional
  `k`-algebra this is the usual notion: by the Artin–Wedderburn theorem a
  ring is semisimple exactly when it is a finite product of matrix rings over
  division rings, and a finite-dimensional algebra is semisimple exactly when
  its Jacobson radical is zero, equivalently when it has no nonzero nilpotent
  left (or right) ideal.
- `k`, `G` and the test algebras in `IsStableSubspace` and `Intertwines` lie
  in one universe `u`. This is a formalization convention.

reductivity and no form of Remark 12.53(b). The Library Markdown of
`CharacteristicZeroReductiveInvariantIntegralStatement` (in
`Reductive.CharacteristicZeroInvariantIntegralStatement`) cites
Definition 12.52 and Remark 12.53(a), not 12.53(b).
`SemisimpleRepresentationFieldExtensionStatement` (Milne's Proposition 4.19)
uses the same endomorphism subalgebra `EndAlg` for one representation. The
same author has separately submitted statements about linear reductivity
that are not in the Library base commit
`2016a0d994180c702214a75514a08546aa930f07`:

## For the later proof (not part of this unit)

Milne's proof in Remark 12.53(b), with the steps he leaves implicit.

- (⇒) If `V` is semisimple, Remark 4.18 gives
  `End(V) ≅ ∏_i M_{s_i}(D_i)` with each `D_i` a finite-dimensional division
  algebra (Schur's lemma for the simple constituents), and such a product is
  a semisimple ring.
- (⇐), first step. Let `α : V → W` be a nonzero homomorphism of
  finite-dimensional representations. Then some homomorphism `β : W → V`
  has `α ∘ β ≠ 0`. Otherwise, let `x` be the endomorphism `(v, w) ↦ (0, αv)`
  of `V ⊕ W` and `E = End(V ⊕ W)`. For `e ∈ E` with component
  `b : W → V`, one computes `x e x = (v, w) ↦ (0, α b α v) = 0`, so the
  right ideal `xE` satisfies `(xE)² = 0` and contains `x ≠ 0`. A nonzero
  nilpotent right ideal lies in the Jacobson radical, which is zero for the
  semisimple ring `E`. This uses the hypothesis for the representation
  `V ⊕ W`.
- (⇐), second step, by induction on `dim V`. For `V ≠ 0`, let `W` be a
  maximal proper subrepresentation, so `V/W` is simple, and let
  `α : V → V/W` be the quotient map. The first step gives
  `β : V/W → V` with `α ∘ β ≠ 0`; by Schur's lemma `α ∘ β` is an
  automorphism of `V/W`, and replacing `β` by `β ∘ (α ∘ β)⁻¹` gives
  `α ∘ β = id`. Then `V = Im(β) ⊕ W` with `Im(β) ≅ V/W` simple, and `W` is
  a sum of simple subrepresentations by induction (the hypothesis applies to
  `W`). So `V` is a sum of simple subrepresentations.
- In Lean, the direct sum `V ⊕ W` and the subrepresentation `W` and quotient
  `V/W` must be realized as matrix representations on `k^{n+m}`, `k^{dim W}`
  and `k^{dim V/W}` after choosing bases.
