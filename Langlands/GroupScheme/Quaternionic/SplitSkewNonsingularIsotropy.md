# Isotropic rows in a split quaternion algebra

`SplitSkewNonsingularIsotropyStatement` concerns a nonsingular skew-hermitian Gram matrix of rank at least four in a split quaternion chart. If every quadratic form of dimension greater than four over the field is isotropic, the skew form has an isotropic row with an invertible entry at any prescribed coordinate.

The scalar Gram matrix is symmetric, nonsingular and of dimension twice the quaternionic rank. Projection to a prescribed two-dimensional coordinate is surjective. A hyperbolic pair shows that the isotropic vectors span the scalar space. The proof uses this fact to choose an isotropic vector with nonzero projection and nonzero pairing against a vector in the kernel of the projection. Adjusting a second vector by that kernel vector produces an orthogonal vector whose projection is independent of the first.

Complete the first vector to a hyperbolic pair. Its orthogonal complement has dimension at least six and is nonsingular, so it contains a hyperbolic pair. Isotropic vectors span this complement as well. One has projection independent of that of the first vector. The two vectors give the scalar rows of a skew-isotropic quaternionic row, and the prescribed block has nonzero determinant.

The nonsingular row condition at split places is the local representability condition described by Platonov and Rapinchuk, *Algebraic Groups and Number Theory*, §6.6, immediately before Claim 6.3, printed p. 343. The projection argument gives the invertible-coordinate version directly in scalar Gram coordinates. [Chapter 6](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0079816908620711)
