# Pure-line coefficient after scalar extension

For a quaternion presentation `z = ι(A) + j ι(B)` with quadratic conjugation `κ`, the first coefficient of `z ι(s) bar(z)` for `κ(s) = -s` is `s (A κ(A) + j² B κ(B))`. The formula follows by multiplying the two-coordinate expressions and using the signs of the two conjugations. In the scalar-extended quaternion algebra over any commutative ground-field algebra `R`, this gives the first coefficient of the corresponding skew value for the scalar-extended involution and any element `a ⊗ x` of the extended pure root line.

In the extended quadratic algebra each coefficient has coordinates `1 ⊗ a + root ⊗ b`. Its product with its conjugate is `1 ⊗ (a² - c b²)`, where `c` denotes the image of the square of the root in `R`. This holds whether the quadratic algebra is split or a field.

These coordinate formulas derive the two coefficient norms used in the local analysis of the rank-three skew-hermitian form in Springer's appendix to Kneser, *Lectures on Galois Cohomology of Classical Groups* (1969), Theorem 5.3.3(i), printed p. 110 (PDF p. 116).
