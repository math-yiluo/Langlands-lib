# Kneser–Tits for quaternionic symplectic groups of rank one over local fields

Authors: Worker 5197.

`Langlands.GroupScheme.Quaternionic.LocalSymplecticKneserTitsStatement.{u}` is a proposition
definition with arbitrary universe `u`. It states the Kneser–Tits property `G(K)^+ = G(K)`
for the symplectic groups in family (iii) of Prasad–Raghunathan (1985), §2. This module does
not prove the proposition.

## Exact statement

For every `K : Type u` with `[Field K] [ValuativeRel K] [TopologicalSpace K]
[IsNonarchimedeanLocalField K]`, every `D : Type u` with `[DivisionRing D] [Algebra K D]
[Algebra.IsCentral K D] [FiniteDimensional K D]` and `Module.finrank K D = 4`, and every
`σ : FirstKindInvolution K D` with `Module.finrank K σ.symmetricSubspace = 3`: for every
`m : ℕ` with `[NeZero m]` and `m = 2 ∨ m = 3`, and every `H : Matrix (Fin m) (Fin m) D` with

- `σ.IsAntihermitian H`,
- `IsUnit H`, and
- `σ.wittIndex H = 1`,

the group scheme `G = σ.symplecticGroup H` over `Spec K` satisfies

`parabolicElementarySubgroup G = ⊤`.

These are all the data and premises. They are the same as in
`Langlands.GroupScheme.Quaternionic.LocalSymplecticRankOneStatement`. The characteristic of
`K`, including residue characteristic 2, is unrestricted. The instance `[NeZero m]` only
supplies `Nonempty (Fin m)`, which the reduced norm of `M_m(D)` in `symplecticGroup` needs; it
follows from `m = 2 ∨ m = 3`.

## Meaning

- `D`, `σ`, `H` and `σ.symplecticGroup H` are as in the paired Markdown of
  `Langlands.GroupScheme.Quaternionic.LocalSymplecticRankOneStatement`: `D` is a quaternion
  central division algebra, `σ` an involution of the first kind with `dim_K D^σ = 3`, `H` the
  Gram matrix of a nondegenerate trace-valued `σ`-antihermitian form on `D^m` of Witt index 1,
  and `G` its special unitary group (modules `Langlands.GroupScheme.Quaternionic.Involution`,
  `Langlands.GroupScheme.Quaternionic.Forms` and
  `Langlands.GroupScheme.Quaternionic.GroupSchemes`).
- `parabolicElementarySubgroup G` (module `Langlands.GroupScheme.Rational.ElementarySubgroup`)
  is the subgroup of the rational points `G(K)` generated by the `K`-points of the geometric
  unipotent radicals of all parabolic `K`-subgroups of `G`. The equation `= ⊤` says that these
  points generate `G(K)`. This is the equality `G(K)^+ = G(K)` of Prasad–Raghunathan.

## Sources

- G. Prasad and M. S. Raghunathan, *On the Kneser–Tits problem*, Comment. Math. Helv. 60
  (1985), 107–121 (`prasad-raghunathan-1985-kneser-tits`).
  - Introduction, printed p. 107 / PDF p. 2: "G(k)^+ [is] the normal subgroup of G(k) generated
    by the k-rational points of the unipotent radicals of parabolic k-subgroups of G". The
    set of these points is stable under conjugation by `G(k)`, since a conjugate of a parabolic
    subgroup is parabolic. So the subgroup it generates is already normal, and it is
    `parabolicElementarySubgroup G`.
  - §2 (iii), printed p. 111 / PDF p. 6: family (iii) contains "the symplectic group of a
    σ-antihermitian form of rank 2 or 3 and Witt index 1; where σ is an involution of the
    quaternion central division algebra D over K such that the dimension of D^σ, the space of
    symmetric elements, is 3". The same page continues: "For each of the above groups G, it is
    known that G(K)^+ = G(K); see, for example, [8]". The reference [8] is Tits 1977.
- J. Tits, *Groupes de Whitehead de groupes algébriques simples sur un corps*, Séminaire
  Bourbaki 505 (1977) (`tits-1977-whitehead-bourbaki-505`).
  - Introduction, printed p. 218 / PDF p. 2. For an isotropic quasi-simple group `G`, Tits's
    `G^+` is generated by the groups `A(K)`, where `A` runs over the `K`-subgroups isomorphic to
    the additive group. The Whitehead group is `W(G, K) = G(K)/G^+`. Prasad–Raghunathan cite
    `W(G, K) = {1}` from this source as their equality `G(K)^+ = G(K)`. The Lean conclusion
    uses Prasad–Raghunathan's definition through the parabolic radicals, as the task requires.
  - 1.1.5, printed p. 220 / PDF p. 4: "On a W(G, K) = {1} pour toute forme isotrope G de B_n ou
    C_n. Pour C_n, voir [11]." The symplectic groups of this statement are isotropic forms of
    `C_m` (Tits 1966 below). This holds over every field.
  - 1.2, printed p. 221 / PDF p. 5: for `K` a non-discrete locally compact field and `G`
    quasi-simple, simply connected and isotropic over `K`, `W(G, K) = {1}`. A nonarchimedean
    local field is a non-discrete locally compact field. Printed p. 222 / PDF p. 6 adds that
    for p-adic fields the references and sketches of 1.1 give a complete proof.
- J. Tits, *Classification of algebraic semisimple groups*, Proc. Sympos. Pure Math. 9 (1966),
  33–62 (`tits-1966-classification-algebraic-semisimple`), Table II, type `C_n`, printed
  p. 56 / PDF p. 61: these groups are the special unitary groups `SU_{2n/d}(D, h)` of
  nondegenerate antihermitian forms of index `r` relative to an involution of the first kind
  with `dim D^σ = ½d(d + 1)`. Here `d = 2`, `n = m` and `r = 1`.

## Scope

The task allows the Kneser–Tits statement for all isotropic forms of these kinds when the
sources support it. Tits 1977, 1.1.5 does support it: it covers every isotropic form of `C_n`,
over every field. The statement nevertheless keeps Prasad–Raghunathan's exact family (rank 2
or 3, Witt index 1, nonarchimedean local `K`). This is the case needed by
`Langlands.GroupScheme.LocalRankOneKneserTitsStatement` through the classification of
rank-one groups over local fields, and it has the same hypotheses as the rank-one statement.
The larger generality is not stated here.
