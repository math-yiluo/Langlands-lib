# Tits's Clifford algebra of a quaternionic quadratic form

Authors: Worker 118.

Module `Langlands.GroupScheme.Quaternionic.TitsClifford`. The general maps `sandwich` and
`tensorι₂` are in namespace `Langlands.GroupScheme.Quaternionic`, everything else in
`Langlands.GroupScheme.Quaternionic.FirstKindInvolution`. The module imports the reduced trace
of `Langlands.Algebra.CentralSimpleReducedTrace` (submitted with it) and the quadratic forms of
`Langlands.GroupScheme.Quaternionic.Forms`.

## Hypotheses and scope

The construction assumes `K D : Type u` in one arbitrary universe, `[Field K]`,
`[DivisionRing D]`, `[Algebra K D]`, `[FiniteDimensional K D]`, `[Algebra.IsCentral K D]`,
`σ : FirstKindInvolution K D`, `m : ℕ` with `[NeZero m]`, and `q : σ.QuadraticForms (Fin m)`.
The characteristic is unrestricted, including characteristic 2. The sandwich-annihilator
needs none of the finiteness, centrality or `NeZero` hypotheses.

These hypotheses are weaker than the quaternionic setting. They do not require
`Module.finrank K D = 4`, `dim D^σ = 3`, nondegeneracy of `q`, or a local field. So the
definition applies to every quaternion division algebra `D` with such a `σ`, every positive
`m` and every `q`. For a positive integer given as `hm : 0 < m`, the instance is
`NeZero.of_pos hm`. `[NeZero m]` makes `E = M_m(D)` a simple ring, so that its reduced trace
exists. The other hypotheses make `E` finite-dimensional and central over `K`.

Tits's construction concerns involutions of *type 1*, those whose symmetric space in an algebra
of degree `n` has dimension `½ n(n + 1)` (§2.1, printed p. 20). For quaternion `D`
and `E = M_m(D)` (degree `n = 2m`), the conjugate transpose `τ = σ.matrix (Fin m)` fixes
`3m + 4 · ½ m(m − 1) = ½ (2m)(2m + 1)` dimensions exactly when `dim D^σ = 3`. So the
identification with Tits below needs `dim D^σ = 3` (more generally
`dim D^σ = ½ d(d + 1)` for `D` of degree `d`). If `σ` has the other type, the Lean algebra is
still defined, but it is not the algebra Tits defines.

## Definitions

Write `E = Matrix (Fin m) (Fin m) D`, `T = σ.quadraticKernel (Fin m) = {x − ᵗx^σ}`,
`Trd = CentralSimple.reducedTraceLinear K E : E →ₗ[K] K` (the reduced trace, normalized to
agree with matrix trace under every splitting chart), and `ι = TensorAlgebra.ι K : E →ₗ[K] T(E)`.

- `sandwich K A : A ⊗[K] A →ₗ[K] A →ₗ[K] A` is defined for any `K`-algebra `A` as the tensor lift
  of the bilinear map `(b, b₁) ↦ (a ↦ b * a * b₁)`. `sandwich_tmul` proves
  `sandwich K A (b ⊗ₜ b₁) a = b * a * b₁`. This is the task's `Sand`.
- `tensorι₂ K M : M ⊗[K] M →ₗ[K] TensorAlgebra K M` is the tensor lift of
  `(x, y) ↦ ι x * ι y`. `tensorι₂_tmul` proves `tensorι₂ K M (x ⊗ₜ y) = ι x * ι y`.
- `σ.traceAnnihilator m : Submodule K E` is the set of `b` with `Trd (b * a) = 0` for every
  `a ∈ T`, and `σ.sandwichAnnihilator m : Submodule K (E ⊗[K] E)` is the set of `c` with
  `sandwich K E c a = 0` for every `a ∈ T`. The lemmas `mem_traceAnnihilator` and
  `mem_sandwichAnnihilator` unfold membership.
- `σ.relationsOf m Q : Set (TensorAlgebra K E)`, for a representative `Q : E`, is the union of
  the two families

  ```text
  ι b − algebraMap K _ (Trd (b * Q))    for b ∈ traceAnnihilator,
  tensorι₂ c − ι (sandwich c Q)         for c ∈ sandwichAnnihilator.
  ```

- `relationsOf_eq_of_sub_mem` proves `σ.relationsOf m Q = σ.relationsOf m Q'` whenever
  `Q − Q' ∈ T`. The proof uses `Trd (b * (Q − Q')) = 0` and `sandwich c (Q − Q') = 0` with
  linearity.
- `σ.relations m q` is defined by `Quotient.liftOn'` from `relationsOf` and this comparison. No
  representative is chosen, and `relations_mk` gives `relations (mk Q) = relationsOf Q` by
  `rfl`.
- `σ.cliffordIdeal m q := TwoSidedIdeal.span (σ.relations m q)` is the two-sided ideal of
  `T(E)` generated by the relations (`cliffordIdeal_mk`, `relations_subset_cliffordIdeal`).
- **`σ.TitsCliffordAlgebra m q : Type u`** is `TensorAlgebra K E ⧸ (σ.cliffordIdeal m q).asIdeal`,
  Mathlib's quotient of a ring by a two-sided ideal. It carries the instances
  `Ring := Ideal.Quotient.ring _`, `Algebra K := Ideal.Quotient.algebra K` and `Inhabited`.
- `σ.cliffordMk m q : TensorAlgebra K E →ₐ[K] σ.TitsCliffordAlgebra m q` is the quotient map
  `Ideal.Quotient.mkₐ`. The lemmas: `cliffordMk_surjective`; `cliffordMk_eq_zero_iff` (its
  kernel is exactly `cliffordIdeal`); `cliffordMk_eq_zero_of_mem_relations`.
- `σ.cliffordι m q : E →ₗ[K] σ.TitsCliffordAlgebra m q` is the map on degree-one generators,
  `cliffordMk ∘ ι` (`cliffordMk_ι`).
- `σ.cliffordι₂ m q : E ⊗[K] E →ₗ[K] σ.TitsCliffordAlgebra m q` is `cliffordMk ∘ tensorι₂`
  (`cliffordMk_tensorι₂`). `cliffordι₂_tmul` gives `cliffordι₂ (b ⊗ₜ b₁) = cliffordι b * cliffordι b₁`.

## Proved properties

- `cliffordι_eq_algebraMap`: if `Submodule.Quotient.mk Q = q` and `b ∈ traceAnnihilator`, then
  `cliffordι b = algebraMap K _ (Trd (b * Q))`.
- `cliffordι₂_eq`: if `Submodule.Quotient.mk Q = q` and `c ∈ sandwichAnnihilator`, then
  `cliffordι₂ c = cliffordι (sandwich c Q)`.
- `adjoin_range_cliffordι`: the range of `cliffordι` generates the algebra. It is the image of
  `TensorAlgebra.adjoin_range_ι` under the surjection `cliffordMk`.
- `cliffordLift f hf`, for a `K`-algebra map `f : T(E) →ₐ[K] B` that sends every relation to
  zero, is the induced map `σ.TitsCliffordAlgebra m q →ₐ[K] B` (`Ideal.Quotient.liftₐ`). The
  ideal lies in `TwoSidedIdeal.ker f` by `TwoSidedIdeal.span_le`.
  `cliffordLift_cliffordMk` gives `cliffordLift f hf (cliffordMk x) = f x`.

## Identification with Tits's `B`, `B′`, `A`, `Q`

Tits (§2.1–2.2, printed pp. 20–21) takes a central simple algebra `E` and a space of
bilinear forms `(B, π)`. Here `B` is a right `E`-module isomorphic to `E` and `π(s e) = s e^σ`
for a generator `s` and an involution `σ` of type 1. He sets `B′` = the dual of `B`, with the
canonical pairing `⟨ , ⟩ : B′ × B → k`, and

```text
S = {b ∈ B | b = π(b)},   A = {b − π(b) | b ∈ B},   Q = B/A.
```

The elements of `Q` are the quadratic forms. In §2.4 (printed p. 24), a nondegenerate symmetric
`s ∈ S` with involution `σ` identifies `E*` and `B` with `E` by `e* ↦ e^σ` and `b = s e ↦ e`.
Then `π` becomes `σ`. The nondegenerate pairing `(x, y) ↦ Trd(x y)` identifies `B′` with `E`,
"ce qui se traduit par l'«identité» (2) ⟨b′, b⟩ = Trd(b′ b)". In §2.5 (printed p. 25), all of
Tits's "produits" become the ordinary product of `E` under these identifications.

For the Library's data, take `s` to correspond to `1 ∈ E` and the involution to be
`τ = σ.matrix (Fin m)`, so that `π = τ`. Then:

- `B = E` and `B′ = E`. Tits's `⟨b′, b⟩` is `Trd (b′ * b)`, the
  `CentralSimple.reducedTracePairing` of the helper module.
- `A = {b − τ b} = T = σ.quadraticKernel (Fin m)`.
- `Q = E/T = σ.QuadraticForms (Fin m)`, and a representative `Q : E` is Tits's `q̄`.
- `T(B′) = TensorAlgebra K E`. The degree-two piece `B′ ⊗ B′` is `E ⊗[K] E`, embedded by
  `tensorι₂`.

Tits's ideal (§4.1, printed p. 31) is generated by `I₁ ∪ I₂`, where

```text
I₁ = {b′ − ⟨b′, q̄⟩ · 1 | b′ ∈ B′ et ⟨b′, A⟩ = {0}},
I₂ = {c − Sand(c, q̄) | c ∈ B′ ⊗ B′ et Sand(c, A) = {0}},
```

with `Sand(b′ ⊗ b′₁, b) = b′₁ b b′` (§4.1 (1)). The family `I₁` is literally the first family
above. The family `I₂` uses the opposite order to the Lean `sandwich`, which takes the task's
order `Sand (b ⊗ₜ b₁) a = b * a * b₁`.

### The order of `Sand` and the row-vector convention

The two orders give isomorphic algebras. Let `Φ` be the `K`-algebra automorphism of `T(E)`
induced by the `K`-linear bijection `τ : E → E`, so `Φ (ι b) = ι (τ b)`. Since `τ` is an
anti-automorphism,

```text
Φ (ι b ι b₁ − ι (b Q b₁)) = ι (τb) ι (τb₁) − ι (τb₁ · τQ · τb),
```

which is Tits's `c′ − Sand(c′, τQ)` for `c′ = τb ⊗ τb₁`. Also
`Σ τb₁ a τb = τ (Σ b (τa) b₁)` and `τ(T) = T`, so `c ∈ sandwichAnnihilator` exactly when
Tits's `Sand(c′, A) = 0`. Similarly `Φ (ι b − Trd (b Q)) = ι (τb) − Trd (τb · τQ)`, and
`b ∈ traceAnnihilator` exactly when `τb` is, because `Trd (τ x) = Trd x` and
`Trd (x y) = Trd (y x)`. So `Φ` maps the Lean relations for `Q` onto Tits's relations for `τQ`.
Since `Q − τQ ∈ T`, Tits's relations for `τQ` and for `Q` generate the same ideal. Hence `Φ`
induces a `K`-algebra isomorphism from `σ.TitsCliffordAlgebra m q` onto Tits's `Cl(q)`, sending
`cliffordι b` to the class of `ι (τ b)`.

The two trace identities are standard. After a splitting extension, `τ` becomes
`x ↦ h · ᵗx · h⁻¹` (Tits, §2.1, printed p. 20), and the matrix trace is invariant under
transposition, conjugation and cyclic permutation. **This comparison is explanatory and is
not formalized here.**

`Φ` is the passage between column and row conventions. Tits's split identification (§2.4 (6))
uses column vectors and identifies `x ⊗ y ∈ B′` with the matrix `y · ᵗx`, whose transpose is
`x · ᵗy`. The Library writes vectors as rows (module `Forms`: `D^m` is a left `D`-space of
row vectors, with the same Gram matrix as Tits's column convention). For rows `x, y`, the
matrix `ᵗx y` (entries `x i * y j`) is this transposed generator. With it, the relations take
the task's order, as the split computation below shows.

## Split case: the even Clifford algebra, not the full one

Take `D = K` and `σ = id`. The hypotheses above allow this, since `K` is a central division
algebra over itself. Then `E = M_m(K)`, `T` is the space of alternating matrices
(skew-symmetric with zero diagonal, in every characteristic), and `q` is the ordinary quadratic
form `φ(q)(x) = x Q ᵗx` on row vectors `x ∈ K^m`.

- A matrix `b` satisfies `Trd (b a) = 0` for all alternating `a` if and only if `b` is symmetric
  (Tits, proof of Théorème 2, printed p. 33). The symmetric matrices are spanned by the `ᵗx x`.
  The first family gives `ι(ᵗx x) = Trd(ᵗx x Q) = φ(q)(x)`.
- For rows `x, y, z`, the tensor `(ᵗx y) ⊗ (ᵗy z)` lies in the sandwich-annihilator, because
  `ᵗx y a ᵗy z = (y a ᵗy) · ᵗx z = 0` for alternating `a`. The second family gives
  `ι(ᵗx y) ι(ᵗy z) = φ(q)(y) · ι(ᵗx z)`.

Tits's assertions (3) and (4) in the proof of Théorème 2 (printed p. 32) show, in his column
convention, that these special elements span the two annihilators. Both families are linear in
`b` and in `c`. So the linear map `ᵗx y ↦ x · y` (a product of two vectors in the classical
Clifford algebra `C(φ(q))`, where `x · x = φ(q)(x)`) respects the relations. Its image is the
**even** subalgebra `C⁺(φ(q))`. Tits's Théorème 2
(printed pp. 31–32) states that `Cl(q)` is canonically isomorphic to "l'algèbre des éléments
pairs de l'algèbre de Clifford usuelle de φ(q)". Corollaire 2 gives dimension `2^{m−1}`. The
full classical Clifford algebra has dimension `2^m` and also has odd elements.
`σ.TitsCliffordAlgebra m q` is therefore the analogue of the even Clifford algebra and must not
be confused with Mathlib's `CliffordAlgebra` of a quadratic form on `K^m`.

For quaternion `D` the same holds after a splitting field `L`, where `L ⊗ D ≅ M_2(L)`. Tits's
Théorème 2 also asserts `Cl(_{k′}q) = Cl(q) ⊗_k k′`, which yields an even Clifford algebra of a
quadratic form in `2m` variables, of dimension `2^{2m−1}`. **None of these structural
statements (split comparison, scalar extension, dimension, simplicity, spin group) is stated or
proved in this module.** They are later work.

## Provenance

The definition `TitsCliffordAlgebra` and its relation families formalize Tits's §4.1
construction under the §2.4 identifications. The algebra comes from Mathlib's `TensorAlgebra` and
its two-sided-ideal quotient. The Lean code makes two convention choices. It uses the task's order of
`Sand`, which the comparison above relates to Tits's literal order. It also packages the
relations as a function of `q` through the representative-independence lemma. The maps
`sandwich`, `tensorι₂`, `cliffordMk`, `cliffordι`, `cliffordι₂` and `cliffordLift`, and the
annihilator submodules, are internal packaging of the ingredients in the source.
