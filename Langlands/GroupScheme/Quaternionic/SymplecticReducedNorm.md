# Reduced norm of an isometry of a symplectic Gram matrix

Let the first-kind involution on a finite-dimensional central division algebra have symmetric subspace of dimension `d(d+1)/2`, where `d` is the reduced degree. For an invertible antihermitian Gram matrix, every unit preserving the matrix has reduced norm one over any commutative coefficient algebra. Consequently the actual symplectic and unitary inclusions have the same image on every test scheme.

The proof tensors the coefficient algebra with a finite Galois splitting field. This scalar map is injective by flatness over the ground field. In the matrix chart the form becomes an invertible alternating form. Darboux coordinates identify its isometries with symplectic matrices. Their determinant is one, and the chart compares this determinant with the reduced norm. Naturality reflects norm one to the original coefficient algebra. Evaluating the unit group and norm character on the actual relative global sections of a test scheme gives the range assertion.

The step is derived from the matrix-chart and norm comparisons used in the Library's construction of the quaternionic symplectic group. It supplies the norm condition for the cocycle primitive in the type C argument of Kneser, *Lectures on Galois Cohomology of Classical Groups* (1969), §5.8, pp. 92–93 (PDF pp. 98–99).
