# Scalar quadratic forms of matrices with involution

`matrixSesqBilin` is the row pairing of a matrix over an algebra with involution, viewed as bilinear over the central scalars. `scalarQuadratic` applies a scalar linear functional to the value of the pairing on a repeated argument. In the type C model, the quaternion division algebra has an orthogonal involution with one-dimensional alternating subspace. The antihermitian self-pairings belong to that subspace, so a functional nonzero there determines the entire self-pairing.

An invertible change of row coordinates gives an isometry under Gram congruence. When `2` is invertible in the ground field, the scalar form commutes with base change to any commutative ground-field algebra, including every number-field completion. The tensor comparison for rows and scalar extension of the functional give equality on pure tensors, which suffices for equality of the quadratic forms. Over a division algebra an invertible antihermitian Gram matrix gives a nondegenerate scalar form when the functional is nonzero on the alternating subspace. For a nonzero first argument the row multiplied by the Gram matrix has a nonzero coordinate. Varying the corresponding coordinate of the second argument realizes every value of the sesquilinear pairing, and an alternating difference with nonzero scalar coordinate detects that first argument in the polar form.

Kneser's *Lectures on Galois Cohomology of Classical Groups*, §5.8, pp. 92–93 (PDF pp. 98–99), uses the scalar quadratic form of a quaternionic hermitian form. Choose a generator `d` of the alternating line with `ℓ(d) = 1`. Right multiplication of the antihermitian self-pairing by `d⁻¹` gives the corresponding hermitian self-pairing for the canonical quaternion conjugation. Its scalar value is `ℓ(B(x,x))`.
