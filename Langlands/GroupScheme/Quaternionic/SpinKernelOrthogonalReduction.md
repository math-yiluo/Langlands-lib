# Kernel generation from supplied split orthogonal coordinates

This internal supporting unit completes the reduction from split coordinates to the kernel-generation conclusion for arbitrary Levi lifts. The coordinate isomorphism, a lifted cocharacter with its projection formula, and its comparison with the original binary diagonal remain explicit inputs. The unit does not construct those quaternionic coordinates or prove the unconditional `SpinKernelGeneratorStatement`.

Let `K → L` be a field extension, and let `H` be a smooth affine group scheme over `K`. Suppose the base change of the full group `H` is supplied with an isomorphism `Φ` to the split even orthogonal matrix group indexed by a finite type `l`. The definition `orthogonalIdentityComponentIso` composes the Library's identity-component base-change isomorphism with the restriction of `Φ` to identity components. Its companion theorem identifies the composite with the inclusion into the full orthogonal group with the base-changed original inclusion followed by `Φ`.

For `existsUnique_kernel_of_orthogonalCoordinates`, let `G` be simply connected and let `p : G → H⁰` be a central isogeny whose composite with the identity-component inclusion is `f : G → H`. Assume `l` has at least three elements. A supplied morphism `a : Gm_L → G_L` projects under `f_L` and `Φ` to a diagonal orthogonal cocharacter `ν`, whose integer coordinates are exactly `2e_i₀` for a supplied `i₀ : l`. The theorem concludes that every point killed by `f_L`, on every relative `L`-test scheme, is uniquely `a(z)` with `z * z = 1`. The parameter is a subtype containing the square equation; arbitrary nonreduced tests are included.

The proof changes base on `p` and postcomposes with `orthogonalIdentityComponentIso`. Central-isogeny base-change stability and isomorphism transport show that this is a central isogeny to the standard orthogonal identity component. Simple connectedness of `G_L` follows from the published arbitrary-field-extension theorem. The inclusion compatibility, combined with cancellation along the identity-component inclusion, changes the supplied full orthogonal projection formula into the identity-torus formula required by `OrthogonalMatrix.existsUnique_kernel_lift_comp_eq`. The same cancellation shows that a point killed by `f_L` lies in this transported central-cover kernel. The latter theorem supplies existence and uniqueness of the parameter, then subtype extensionality supplies uniqueness with its square equation.

The spin specialization `kernelGenerator_of_orthogonalCoordinates` retains the hypotheses of the original binary spin construction: a central quaternion division algebra of dimension four, a first-kind involution whose symmetric subspace has dimension three, an infinite ground field, a hyperbolic pair, a supplied spin cover, and the degree-two norm-one simple-connectedness statement. Over one field extension it additionally takes the full orthogonal isomorphism `Φ`, the supplied lift `a`, the cocharacter `ν` with coordinate `2e_i₀`, and the full projection formula above. Finally it assumes, for every square-root parameter on every test scheme, that `a(z)` transported by `baseChangePointsEquiv` is the corresponding original binary `μ₂` point followed by `BinarySpinLift.diagonalSpin`. The original binary point is expressed using `SpinKernel.binaryRootPointsEquiv`.

The spin proof obtains the central isogeny factoring the supplied spin cover from `IsSpinCover.exists_centralIsogeny`, applies the preceding unique-parameter theorem, and invokes `isIso_baseChange_diagonalKernel_of_squareRootParametrization`. The published field-extension reflection theorem for the original diagonal kernel then proves `KernelGenerator` for every pair of Levi lifts. There is no characteristic restriction or restriction on coefficient algebras. The supplied coordinate data and binary comparison are substantive remaining hypotheses.
