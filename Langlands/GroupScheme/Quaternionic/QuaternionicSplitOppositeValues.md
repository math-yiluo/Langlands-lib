# Opposite invertible skew values over split quaternion coefficients

Let two blocks have nonempty finite index sets and invertible skew Gram matrices. The coefficient algebra is presented in a two-by-two chart in which the involution satisfies the standard adjugate identity. `QuaternionicSplitOppositeValuesStatement` assumes that the orthogonal sum has a row of self-pairing zero and that some partner pairs with it to give one. It concludes that the two blocks represent opposite invertible skew values.

The two scalar rows of the first vector span a totally isotropic plane for the orthogonal sum of the symmetric scalar Gram forms. Pairing with the two rows of the partner gives the invertible alternating matrix \(J\), so the first two scalar rows are independent. The scalar opposite-binary-subforms theorem yields two orthogonal scalar rows in each block, with opposite invertible diagonal Gram matrices. Recombining the rows into quaternionic coordinates and cancelling \(J\) gives the asserted opposite values. This is the split coefficient calculation used in the opposite-value step of Springer's proof. [Kneser, *Lectures on Galois cohomology of classical groups*, Springer appendix, §1 equation (2) and §3 Lemma 5.3.4(a), pp. 106 and 111](https://www.math.tifr.res.in/~publ/ln/tifr47.pdf)
