# Pure quaternion factors and rank-three Gram models

For the standard conjugation of a central quaternion division algebra, the alternating subspace is three-dimensional when `2` is invertible. The scalar trace shows that the fixed subspace is the scalar line, and rank-nullity gives the alternating dimension. If an involution has a three-dimensional alternating subspace in a four-dimensional division algebra, every nonzero element `a` is the product of two nonzero alternating elements. The alternating subspace meets its image under left multiplication by `a`, since both have dimension three. A nonzero vector in their intersection has the form `a y`, with `y` alternating, and its product with `y⁻¹` is `a`. The inverse of a nonzero alternating element is alternating.

For quaternion conjugation, given a nonzero pure first entry `c` and a nonzero norm representative `a`, factor `a` into two pure elements and put them in the other diagonal positions. This produces an invertible antihermitian rank-three matrix. Its reduced norm is the product of the quaternion norms of `c` and `a`.

This is the pure factorization and prescribed diagonal construction in Kneser's *Lectures on Galois Cohomology of Classical Groups* (1969), §4.3, Lemma 4 and its sublemma, printed pp. 56–57 (PDF pp. 62–63).
