# Antihermitian pairings and their nondegenerate lines

`AntihermitianForm` packages a pairing bilinear over the ground field `F`, acting centrally on the division algebra `D` through its algebra map. The pairing is `D`-linear in its first argument and skew-symmetric under the involution. In the central quaternion application, `F` identifies with the full center of `D`. These identities recover conjugate-linearity in the second argument. An antihermitian Gram matrix gives such a form on its rows. Invertibility makes it left-separating.

A vector of nonzero self-pairing splits the space into its division line and its orthogonal complement. The projection coefficient of a vector `y` onto the line generated by `x` is `B(y,x) B(x,x)⁻¹`. The complement is left-separating when the original pairing is. If the original pairing is left-separating and the scalar linear functional is nonzero on the alternating subspace, the scalar polar form is nondegenerate.

For any pairing, a functional nonzero on the alternating subspace identifies the scalar polar orthogonal complement of the whole division line with the antihermitian orthogonal complement. Varying the division scalar lets the functional detect a nonzero alternating difference whenever the pairing with the fixed vector is nonzero.

These constructions implement the orthogonal-complement step of Kneser's type C argument in *Lectures on Galois Cohomology of Classical Groups*, §5.8, pp. 92–93 (PDF pp. 98–99), with the antihermitian presentation of the Library.
