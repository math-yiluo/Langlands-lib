# Recovering antihermitian isometry from scalar quadratic isometry

Let two left-separating antihermitian pairings have the same finite division rank, with the coefficient division algebra finite-dimensional over the ground field. Assume `2` is invertible. The scalar functional is injective on the alternating subspace and is nonzero on some alternating difference. Under these hypotheses a scalar quadratic isometry gives a division-linear isometry of the pairings.

In positive rank the scalar polarization is nondegenerate, so there is a vector of nonzero scalar value. Its image under the given scalar isometry has the same scalar value. Their antihermitian self-pairings lie in the alternating subspace and therefore agree. The formula for the pairing on division multiples of these vectors gives two isometric embeddings of the same nondegenerate scalar form. Quadratic Witt extension adjusts the scalar isometry so that it matches the entire division lines. The adjusted isometry restricts to the scalar forms of the antihermitian orthogonal complements. Those complements are left-separating and have smaller equal division rank. Induction gives an isometry of the complements, and adjoining the matched division lines gives an isometry of the original pairings. In rank zero both spaces are trivial.

`MatrixScalarIsometryReflectionStatement` specializes this argument to two invertible antihermitian matrices on the same finite row space. The division-linear isometry from the form of `H'` to the form of `H` is right multiplication by the matrix `P` whose rows are its values on the standard basis. The inverse isometry gives an inverse matrix. Equality of the pairings gives `P H σ(P)ᵗ = H'`.

This is a form-level consequence of the scalarization and orthogonal-complement construction used for quaternionic hermitian forms in Kneser, *Lectures on Galois Cohomology of Classical Groups* (1969), §5.8, pp. 92–93 (PDF pp. 98–99). The proof uses quadratic Witt extension to match the entire underlying scalar space of a quaternionic line.
