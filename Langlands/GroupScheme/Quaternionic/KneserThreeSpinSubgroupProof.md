# Proof of Kneser's `A₃ = D₃` subgroup statement, and of the elementary kernel lift

Module `Langlands.GroupScheme.Quaternionic.KneserThreeSpinSubgroupProof`. It proves the Library
statement `SpinKernel.KneserThreeSpinSubgroupStatement` (theorem
`KneserThree.Quaternion.kneserThreeSpinSubgroup`). With the reduction module
`SpinKernelElementaryReduction` it then proves `SpinKernel.SpinKernelElementaryLiftStatement`
(theorem `SpinKernel.spinKernelElementaryLift`). Finally it reduces
`LocalSpinKneserTitsStatement` to `SpinKernelGeneratorStatement`
(`SpinKernel.localSpinKneserTits_of_spinKernelGenerator`).

The route is the explicit form of Kneser's `A₃ = D₃` argument (Tits, Bourbaki 505, 1977,
no. 1.1.6). A hyperbolic pair `(e, f)` and an anisotropic vector `v` orthogonal to it span a
three-dimensional quaternionic form. Its spin group is the special unitary group `SU(H)` of a
four-dimensional Hermitian form over a quadratic subfield `L` of `D`. `SU(H)` is simply connected,
the binary group `SL₂ × SL₁(D)` of the plane `(e, f)` factors through it, and its rational points
are generated by unipotent radicals.

## The vector `v` (section `Vector`)

* `exists_ortho_polar_ne_zero`: let `h` be a hyperbolic pair of `q`, with Witt index `≤ 1`,
  `2 ≠ 0` and at least three coordinates. Then there is `v` with `h(v, e) = h(v, f) = 0` and
  `h(v, v) ≠ 0`. The Library's `Eichler.exists_ne_zero_ortho` gives a nonzero `v ⊥ e, f`, and
  `Eichler.eq_zero_of_ortho_of_quadraticWittIndex_le_one` shows `q(v) ≠ 0`. If `h(v, v) = 0`,
  then for a representative `Q` the value `z = vQσ(v)` satisfies `σ z = -z`. Since `2` is
  invertible, `z` is then alternating (`mem_alternatingSubspace_of_neg` of `KneserThreeUnits`),
  so `q(v) = 0`.
* `entry_eq_sesqForm`: a matrix entry is the sesquilinear form at standard basis vectors.
* `polar_pullback`: the pull-back of `q` along the rows `E = (e, f, v)` has polar matrix
  `polarMatrix (h(v, v)) = [[0, 1, 0], [1, 0, 0], [0, 0, h(v, v)]]`. This uses
  `polar_quadraticPullback`, `sesqForm_gramPullback`, the hyperbolic-pair equations and
  `sesqForm_polar_swap`.
* `isUnit_polarMatrix`: this matrix is invertible when `h(v, v) ≠ 0`, with inverse
  `polarMatrix (h(v, v)⁻¹)`.

## The binary group through the model (sections `BinaryCompat`, `BinaryMain`)

* `presentation_ι_includeRight`, `product_includeRight`: for real coefficients, the matrix
  `Presentation.product` of `KneserThreeCore` has entries `algebraMap R (R ⊗ D) (g i k) * b`.
* `retraction_eq`, `inclusion_eq`, `extendUnits_single`: for a form `q₃` on `D³` with polar
  matrix `polarMatrix s`, the Library's `HyperbolicScheme.extendUnits` from the plane of the
  first two standard rows is the block matrix `[[G, 0], [0, 1]]`.
* `twistInv_mul_product_mul_twist`: `diag(1, ℓ⁻¹, 1) · product g b · diag(1, ℓ, 1)` is
  `[[action ℓ (g, b), 0], [0, 1]]`, where `action` is the Library's `BinaryAction.action` and
  `ℓ` is the alternating unit of `σ`.
* `modelMatrix_binaryHom`: the model matrix (`KneserThreeModelHom`) of a point of
  `Q.binaryHom` (`KneserThreeBinary`) is `Q.binaryMatrix`. This follows from `binaryHom_points`
  and `modelReindex_apply`.
* `binaryHom_comp_modelHom`: `Q.binaryHom ≫ QuatData.modelHom = AmbientBinaryScheme.isometryHom σ
  hdim hsym h₃` for the hyperbolic pair `h₃` of the first two standard rows of `q₃`. Both sides
  are compared on test points after the isometry inclusion. The left side is
  `cmap (binaryMatrix g b)`, which `Presentation.cmap_binary` writes as
  `diag(1, ℓ⁻¹, 1) · product g b · diag(1, ℓ, 1)`. Its hypothesis `norm b = 1` is
  `presentation_norm_eq_one` of `KneserThreeBinary`. The right side is
  `extendUnits h₃ (BinaryScheme.pointMatrix x)` by the Library's `isometryHom_points`.
  `elem_mul_inv` and `tensorAlternatingUnit_inv_val` handle `ℓ⁻¹`.

## The main theorem (section `Main`)

`exists_spin_subgroup`: for `2 ≠ 0`, a hyperbolic pair `h` and `v` as above, set
`μ = h(v, v)` (`σ μ = μ` by `sesqForm_polar_swap`) and `Q = quatData σ hdim hsym h2 hμ hμ0`
(module `KneserThreeQuaternion`). Take:

* `C = HermitianRoot.SchemeModel.groupScheme K L Q.diagForm (Q.diagUnits hδ) …`;
* `c = QuatData.modelHom … ≫ SubspaceScheme.isometryHom hE`, with
  `θ = root`, `θ⁻¹`, `l₁, l₂` the coordinates of `ℓ`, `w = (θ (l₁ l̄₁ - δ l₂ l̄₂))⁻¹`,
  `hq` from `polar_pullback` and `neg_ι_root_mul_elem`, `hσ = sigma_eq_bar`, and `hE` from
  `isUnit_polarMatrix`;
* `j = Q.binaryHom Q.diagForm rfl (Q.diagUnits hδ) … hθ hθinv`.

Then `C` is simply connected (`HermitianRoot.SchemeModel.isSimplyConnected`), and
`parabolicElementarySubgroup C = ⊤`
(`HermitianRoot.SchemeModel.parabolicElementarySubgroup_eq_top`, using
`diagForm_isAnisotropic`). Moreover
`j ≫ c = AmbientBinaryScheme.isometryHom σ hdim hsym h`, by `binaryHom_comp_modelHom`,
associativity, and the Library's `SubspaceScheme.hyperbolic_isometryHom_comp`.

`kneserThreeSpinSubgroup`: `m ∈ {4, 5}` gives at least three coordinates. Witt index `1` gives `v`
by `exists_ortho_polar_ne_zero`. `exists_spin_subgroup` gives `C, c, j`, and the rational point
`j(-1, -1)` lies in `parabolicElementarySubgroup C = ⊤`.
