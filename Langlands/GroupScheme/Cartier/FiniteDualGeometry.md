# Geometric input for finite locally free Cartier duality

This module collects the scheme-theoretic facts used to reduce finite
locally free Cartier duality over an affine base `Spec R` to the
algebraic duality of finite projective Hopf algebras. It imports only
published Library modules and Mathlib.

## Relative spectra of finite projective algebras

For an `R`-algebra `B`, the structure map of its relative spectrum
`(algSpec R).obj (op (CommAlgCat.of R B))` is

- finite exactly when `B` is a finite `R`-module (`isFinite_algSpec_iff`),
- flat exactly when `B` is a flat `R`-module (`flat_algSpec_iff`),
- locally of finite presentation exactly when `B` is a finitely
  presented `R`-algebra (`locallyOfFinitePresentation_algSpec_iff`).

Each is Mathlib's `Spec.map` criterion for the corresponding ring-map
property, applied to the structure map `Spec.map (algebraMap R B)`.
Hence, when `B` is finite and projective as an `R`-module, its spectrum
is finite, flat and locally of finite presentation over `Spec R`
(`algSpec_finiteLocallyFree`): a finite projective module is a finitely
presented module, and an algebra that is finitely presented as a module
is finitely presented as an algebra.

## Coordinates of a finite locally free group scheme

Let `G` be a group scheme over `Spec R`. If its structure map is finite,
its total space is affine (`isAffine_of_isFinite`), since finite
morphisms are affine and `Spec R` is affine. For affine `G`, the Library
supplies the coordinate Hopf algebra `AffineCoordinates.hopfAlgebra G`
and the isomorphism `AffineCoordinates.hopfSpecIso G` between its
spectrum and `G`; the structure map of the spectrum is the isomorphism
followed by the structure map of `G` (`hopfSpec_hom_eq`). The three
morphism properties are stable under composition with an isomorphism,
so the criteria above show that the coordinate Hopf algebra is a finite
module when `G` is finite (`hopfAlgebra_finite`), flat when `G` is flat
(`hopfAlgebra_flat`), and finitely presented when `G` is locally of
finite presentation (`hopfAlgebra_finitePresentation`). In the
finite, flat, locally finitely presented case it is a finite module
and a finitely presented algebra, hence a finitely presented module;
being also flat, it is projective (`hopfAlgebra_projective`).

## Transport along isomorphisms

Precomposition with an isomorphism `e : G ≅ G'` of commutative group
schemes gives an isomorphism `charactersIso e : characters G' ≅
characters G` of character presheaves. Together with the Library's
`additivePointsIso`, it transports the whole conclusion of
`FiniteLocallyFreeCartierDualityStatement` from `G'` to `G`
(`finiteCartierDual_of_iso`): the same dual group scheme `D` works,
with `dual` composed with `charactersIso e` and `bidual` precomposed
with `additivePointsIso e`. The evaluation equation follows from the
Library lemma `characterEvaluation_precompose`, which says that
evaluating a precomposed character at `x` is evaluating the original
character at `x ≫ e.hom`.

## Isomorphisms detected on affine tests

The Zariski topology on schemes over `S` is
`Scheme.zariskiTopology.over S`.

- `affineTests_mem_zariski`: a sieve on `T` that contains every arrow
  from a test whose source scheme is affine is a covering sieve. It
  contains the sieve generated by the affine open cover
  `T.left.affineCover`.
- `isIso_of_bijective_affine`: a morphism `α : F ⟶ G` of
  abelian-group-valued presheaves on `Over S` that are Zariski sheaves
  is an isomorphism if `α.app (op T)` is bijective whenever `T.left` is
  affine. By the previous lemma, `α` is locally injective and locally
  surjective. Mathlib's `Sheaf.isLocallyBijective_iff_isIso` then shows
  that `α` is an isomorphism of sheaves.
- `bijective_app_of_iso`: bijectivity of a component of a natural
  transformation of abelian-group-valued presheaves transfers along an
  isomorphism of test objects (by naturality).
- `isSheaf_additivePoints`: the additive point functor of a commutative
  group scheme is a sheaf for every subcanonical topology. After
  forgetting the group structure it is isomorphic to the representable
  functor `yoneda.obj G.X`.
- `exists_iso_algSpec`: over `Spec R`, a test `T` with affine source is
  isomorphic to the relative spectrum of an `R`-algebra, namely
  `Γ(T.left, ⊤)` with the induced algebra structure, via Mathlib's
  `T.left.isoSpec`.
- `isIso_of_bijective_algSpec`: over `Spec R`, a morphism of Zariski
  sheaves of abelian groups is an isomorphism once it is bijective on
  the relative spectrum of every `R`-algebra.
- `isIso_additivePoints_characters_of_bijective`: in particular, a
  morphism `additivePoints H.toGrp ⟶ characters G` between commutative
  group schemes over `Spec R` is an isomorphism once it is bijective on
  the relative spectrum of every `R`-algebra. Both functors are
  Zariski sheaves: the first by `isSheaf_additivePoints`, the second by
  the Library's `isSheaf_characters`. Here Mathlib supplies that the
  Zariski topology over `S` is subcanonical.

## Provenance

These are standard reductions used in Demazure–Gabriel, *Groupes
algébriques*, Tome I (1970), Chapter II, §1, no. 2, 2.10. There, a
finite locally free group scheme over an affine base is the spectrum of
a finite projective Hopf algebra. The reductions also use the fact that
morphisms of fppf (here Zariski) sheaves can be compared on affine
tests. The Lean proofs use Mathlib's `Spec.map` criteria for morphism
properties and its theory of locally bijective morphisms of sheaves.
