# Density helpers for central extensions of SL₂

This internal supporting module develops the density step used in the proof of
Conrad, *Reductive group schemes* (2014), Proposition 4.3.1, pp.126–131.
It does not prove that proposition.

`rootsOfUnityIdeal A n` is the ideal generated by `Xⁿ⁺¹−1` in `A[X, X⁻¹]`.
It defines the closed subscheme of `(n+1)`st roots of unity over `A`. Indexing
by `n+1` includes all positive orders.

`eq_of_eq_on_quotients` concerns maps from an affine scheme `Spec B` to a scheme
`Z` separated over a scheme `S`. If two maps have the same composite to `S` and
agree on every `Spec(B/Iᵢ)`, where the intersection of the ideals `Iᵢ` is zero,
then they agree. The proof forms their equalizer in schemes over `S`. Its
inclusion is a closed immersion by separatedness. Every quotient scheme factors
through it, so the inclusion induces an injective map on global sections. A
closed immersion into an affine scheme with that property is an isomorphism,
and the equalizer condition gives equality.

This is an elementary derived lemma, rather than a separately numbered statement
in Conrad. The implementation uses Mathlib's closed equalizer and affine closed
immersion APIs.

`polynomial_iInf_span_X_pow_sub_one` states that the ideals `(Xⁿ⁺¹−1)` in `A[X]`
have zero intersection for every commutative ring `A`. A polynomial in the
intersection is divisible by a monic polynomial of strictly greater degree,
which forces it to be zero.

`iInf_span_X_pow_sub_one` states the same zero-intersection result after
localizing at `X`, giving the ring of Laurent polynomials `A[X, X⁻¹]`. Write an
element as a fraction. Membership in each extended ideal implies that a power
of `X` times its numerator belongs to the corresponding polynomial ideal.
Since `X` is relatively prime to `Xⁿ⁺¹−1`, that power can be removed from the
divisibility statement. The polynomial intersection result makes the numerator
zero. No domain, reducedness or noetherian hypothesis is used.
