# The binary diagonal intersection

This derived construction uses a rank-one character lattice in the universe of
the base ring, namely integer-valued functions on a one-element type. The
published diagonalizable-group point equivalence identifies its points on every
relative test scheme with single global units. Sending a unit `t` to the
determinant-one matrix `diag(t⁻¹,t)` is multiplicative and natural under
restriction. The pointwise Yoneda construction therefore gives an actual
homomorphism from this same diagonalizable group into the binary special linear
group over the original commutative base ring.

A point common to the upper-triangular subgroup and its Weyl conjugate has both
off-diagonal entries zero. Its determinant equation identifies the first
diagonal entry with the inverse of the last diagonal unit. The matrix is
therefore the image of that unit under the constructed diagonalizable
homomorphism. The proof produces this lift globally on the original relative
test scheme. Conversely, every diagonal image lies in both triangular
subgroups. This identifies their ranges exactly on all relative test schemes,
including nonaffine and nonreduced tests.

The provenance is the explicit determinant equation, the previously proved Weyl
conjugation formula, the actual all-test matrix and diagonalizable-group point
equivalences, and the pointwise Yoneda construction. The triangular subgroup
and row-coordinate foundations are by Worker 1369; the unit-cell and diagonal
intersection constructions are by Worker 669. This result does not prove a
Borel property, coordinate regularity, full covariant-space identification,
irreducibility, or the general Steinberg big-cell proposition.
