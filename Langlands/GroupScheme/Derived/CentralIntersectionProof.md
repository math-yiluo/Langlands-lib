# Proof of finiteness of the full central intersection with an actual derived subgroup

Authors: Worker 1408 and Worker 846.

`Langlands.GroupScheme.derivedCentralIntersectionFiniteness` proves the Library
proposition `Langlands.GroupScheme.DerivedCentralIntersectionFinitenessStatement.{u}`
(module `Langlands.GroupScheme.Derived.CentralIntersectionStatement`). Fix a
field `k : Type u`, group schemes `G` and `D` over `Spec k` and `d : D ⟶ G`.
Assume `IsConnectedReductive G` and `IsDerivedSubgroup d`. Then there are a group
scheme `C` over `Spec k` and `c : C ⟶ D` with `IsClosedSubgroup c`,
`SubgroupFunctor.range c = (center G).comap d` on every test scheme, and
`IsFinite C.X.hom`. This is Milne, *Algebraic Groups* (2017),
Proposition 19.21(b), printed p.402, for the full scheme-theoretic centre. The
implemented proof follows a different route from the source.

The theorem is one application:
`derivedCentralIntersectionFiniteness_of_algClosed CentralIntersection.finite_of_isAlgClosed`.
The two halves live in helper modules.

## Reduction to an algebraically closed field (Worker 846)

Modules `Derived.CentralIntersectionPullback` and
`Derived.CentralIntersectionReduction`; see their Markdown for details.

- `C` is the pullback `subgroupIntersection d z` of a centre representative
  `z : Z ⟶ G`. Such a `z` is given by the published centre existence for
  connected reductive `G`. `c` is the first projection. It is a closed subgroup,
  as a base change of the closed immersion `z`, and its range is
  `(center G).comap d` on every test scheme.
- Let `K` be the algebraic closure of `k`. After base change to `K`, the
  published results `derivedSubgroupFieldBaseChange`, `center_baseChange` and
  `SubgroupFunctor.range_baseChangeHom` transport every hypothesis. So
  `G_K.X.left` is affine and locally of finite type, `G_K` has trivial unipotent
  radical (a field of `IsConnectedReductive G`), `d_K` is a derived-subgroup
  inclusion, `c_K` is a closed subgroup and the range of `c_K` is
  `(center G_K).comap d_K`.
- `derivedCentralIntersectionFiniteness_of_algClosed` takes as hypothesis
  `hfin`: finiteness of the underlying space of any such `S` over any
  algebraically closed field. It applies `hfin` to `c_K`. The projection from
  the base change to `C` is surjective, so `C.X.left` is finite. A morphism to
  `Spec k` that is locally of finite type and quasi-compact, with finitely many
  points, is finite (`LocallyQuasiFinite.of_finite_preimage_singleton` and
  `IsFinite.of_locallyQuasiFinite`). Hence `IsFinite C.X.hom`.

## The algebraically closed case (Worker 1408)

`CentralIntersection.finite_of_isAlgClosed` (module
`Derived.CentralIntersection.AlgClosed`) supplies `hfin`. Over an algebraically
closed `K`, let `s : S ⟶ D` be a closed subgroup whose points are central in an
affine `G` locally of finite type with trivial unipotent radical.

1. Let `H` be the identity component of the reduced subgroup of `S`, given by
   `ReducedSubgroup.exists_reducedIdentityComponent`. It is a smooth connected
   closed subgroup of `G`, central, and open in `S`.
2. Embed `G` in `GL_N` by `affineLinearEmbeddingStatement`. For a rational point
   `x` of `D` central in `G` and an eigenvalue `c` of `x`, the polynomial
   projector `π` onto the generalized `c`-eigenspace commutes with `G`. So
   `g ↦ det (ρ(g) π + 1 - π)` is a character of `G`. By
   `IsDerivedSubgroup.characterRestrict_eq_zero` it is trivial on `D`, which
   gives `c^a = 1` with `1 ≤ a ≤ N` (module `BlockCharacter`).
3. Every eigenvalue of every rational point of `H` is therefore an `N!`-th root
   of unity. Reducedness, connectedness and an idempotent-determinant argument
   make the universal matrix of `H` unipotent. Right translations then act
   locally unipotently on the coordinate ring, which is generated by the
   matrix entries. So every representation of `H` is unipotent at rational
   points. Commuting unipotent matrices have a common fixed vector, fixed
   scheme-theoretically by reducedness. This gives `IsUnipotent H` (module
   `Unipotence`, using `LinearAlgebra` and `PointVanishing`).
4. `H` is normal, being central. Trivial unipotent radical then gives
   `H = 1`, so the identity of `S` is an open closed point. Translations make
   every closed point open, and the Jacobson property makes `S.X.left` discrete.
   It is affine, so it is finite.

## Scope

The universe `u` and the field `k` are arbitrary. Imperfect fields and positive
characteristic are allowed. `C` keeps its nonreduced structure: the range
equality holds on all test schemes, and finiteness is of the full morphism
`C.X.hom`. The proof uses neither `DerivedSubgroupSemisimpleStatement` nor
`IsSemisimple D`, nor any generation of `G` by a central torus and `D`.
Axioms: `propext`, `Classical.choice`, `Quot.sound`.
