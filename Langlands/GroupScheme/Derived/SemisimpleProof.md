# Proof: actual derived subgroups of connected reductive groups are semisimple

Author: Worker 4065.

`Langlands.GroupScheme.derivedSubgroupSemisimple` proves exactly the published
proposition `Langlands.GroupScheme.DerivedSubgroupSemisimpleStatement.{u}`
(module [`SemisimpleStatement`](SemisimpleStatement.md)). It keeps the
universe parameter `u` and takes no hypotheses.

Fix a field `k : Type u`, group schemes `G` and `D` over `Spec k`, and a
homomorphism `d : D ⟶ G`. Assume `IsConnectedReductive G` and
`IsDerivedSubgroup d`. The theorem proves `IsSemisimple D`, which has two
fields:

- `IsConnectedReductive D`: `D` is smooth, affine and connected over `k`, and
  its base change to `AlgebraicClosure k` has trivial unipotent radical;
- `HasFiniteCenter D`: some actual closed subgroup represents the full
  scheme-theoretic centre of `D` on every test scheme, and its structure
  morphism is finite.

The field may be imperfect and of any characteristic. Nothing is assumed
about `D` or `d` beyond `IsDerivedSubgroup d`. In particular, smoothness,
connectedness and reductivity of `D`, and existence and finiteness of its
centre, are all proved rather than supplied.

## Implemented proof

The Lean proof combines four published Library results. It does not use any
unproved Library statement as a premise; each result below is an
unconditional theorem or a lemma whose hypotheses are discharged here.

1. **Affineness.** `hG.isAffineHom` gives `IsAffineHom G.X.hom`. Since
   `Spec k` is affine, `isAffine_of_isAffineHom` makes `G.X.left` an affine
   scheme. The centre lemma in step 4 needs this.

2. **A central product.** The published proof `reductiveCentralDerivedProduct`
   (module [`Reductive/CentralDerivedProductProof`](../Reductive/CentralDerivedProductProof.md))
   applies to `G`. It gives a commutative group scheme `Z`, a group scheme `E`,
   a central homomorphism `i : Z.toGrp ⟶ G` (`IsCentral i`), and an actual
   derived-subgroup inclusion `e : E ⟶ G` (`IsDerivedSubgroup e`). The
   product map `subgroupProductMap i e` is an epimorphism. The torus, affineness
   and finite-type conjuncts for `Z` are not used.

3. **A finite central intersection.** The published proof
   `derivedCentralIntersectionFiniteness` (module
   [`CentralIntersectionProof`](CentralIntersectionProof.md)) applies to `G`
   and `e`. It gives a group scheme `C` and a closed subgroup `c : C ⟶ E` with
   `SubgroupFunctor.range c = (center G).comap e` on every test scheme, and
   `IsFinite C.X.hom`.

4. **Finite centre of `D`.** The published reduction
   `IsDerivedSubgroup.hasFiniteCenter_of_centralProduct` (module
   [`SemisimpleReduction`](SemisimpleReduction.md)) takes the given `d`, the
   central product `(i, e)` from step 2, and the finite closed subgroup `c` from
   step 3. It returns `HasFiniteCenter D`. Internally it uses the published
   centre comparison from [`CenterReduction`](CenterReduction.md). When the
   central product is epimorphic, that comparison identifies the inverse image
   of the ambient centre with the full centre of `E`. Finiteness is then moved
   from `E` to `D` along the isomorphism `hd.iso he` between the two
   derived-subgroup representatives.

5. **Connected reductivity of `D`.** The published theorem
   `IsDerivedSubgroup.isConnectedReductive` (module
   [`MaximalTorusIntersectionReduction`](MaximalTorusIntersectionReduction.md))
   gives `IsConnectedReductive D` directly from `hd` and `hG`. Its proof
   combines three results. `derivedSubgroupSmoothness` makes `D` smooth, and
   `derivedSubgroupConnectedness` makes `D` connected. Then
   `IsConnectedReductive.of_normalSubgroup` applies, because `d` is a closed
   subgroup whose range is normal (`IsDerivedSubgroup.isNormal`). That theorem
   shows that a smooth connected normal closed subgroup of a connected reductive
   field group is connected reductive. In particular, its geometric unipotent
   radical is trivial.

The anonymous constructor `⟨_, _⟩` of `IsSemisimple D` packages steps 5 and 4.

## Relation to the source

The statement is the semisimplicity part of Milne, *Algebraic Groups* (2017),
Proposition 19.21(c), printed p. 402 (PDF p. 418), with the finite-centre form
of semisimplicity from Proposition 19.10. The implemented route bounds the
centre directly and does not follow Milne's radical argument. `G` is
generated by a central subgroup and its derived subgroup. So the centre of the
derived subgroup centralizes all of `G`, and it lies in the finite
intersection `Z(G) ∩ G^der`. That intersection is Milne's 19.21(b), proved in
the Library as `derivedCentralIntersectionFiniteness`. Reductivity comes from
the normal-subgroup argument instead of the radical of `G^der`.

## Scope

All derived subgroups and centres are the full scheme-theoretic ones on every
test scheme, including nonreduced test schemes. The centre representative
keeps its nonreduced structure, and finiteness is of its whole structure
morphism. The proof works for every exhibiting inclusion `d`, not only for
the representative `e` chosen in step 2. Axioms: `propext`,
`Classical.choice`, `Quot.sound`.
