# Statement: semisimple field groups are perfect

Authors: Worker 1410 (draft), Worker 1383 (source revision).

This module contains one `Prop`-valued definition and no proof.

## The Lean proposition

`Langlands.GroupScheme.SemisimplePerfectStatement.{u}` is

```lean
∀ (k : Type u) [Field k] (G : GroupScheme (Spec (CommRingCat.of k))),
  IsSemisimple G → derivedSubgroup G = ⊤
```

The universe `u` is arbitrary and `k` is an arbitrary field in it, including
imperfect fields and fields of positive characteristic. `G` is an actual group
scheme `Langlands.GroupScheme (Spec k) = Grp (Over (Spec k))`. No further
hypothesis is imposed: there is no perfectness, characteristic, splitting,
algebraic-closedness or extra smoothness assumption beyond what `IsSemisimple`
contains.

- `IsSemisimple G` (from `Reductive/Semisimple`) has two fields.
  `IsConnectedReductive G` says that the structure morphism of `G` is smooth
  and affine, the underlying space of `G` is connected, and the base change of
  `G` to `AlgebraicClosure k` has trivial unipotent radical.
  `HasFiniteCenter G` says that the full scheme-theoretic centre of `G` is
  represented by an actual closed subgroup whose structure morphism is finite.
- `derivedSubgroup G` (from `Derived/Basic`) is the scheme-theoretic derived
  subgroup functor. On a test scheme `T` over `Spec k`, a point `x : T ⟶ G.X`
  belongs to it when `x` factors through every actual closed subgroup
  inclusion `i : H ⟶ G` whose range contains every commutator on every test
  scheme. It is not the abstract commutator subgroup of `G(k)`.
- `⊤` is the total subgroup functor of `G`: on every test scheme it is the
  whole group of points.

So the proposition asserts that, for every semisimple `G`, every point of `G`
on every test scheme, including nonreduced tests, lies in the scheme-theoretic
derived subgroup. Equivalently, every actual closed subgroup inclusion whose
range contains all commutators has total range: one direction is
`derivedSubgroup_le_range`, the other is the defining intersection.

The proposition assumes no representability of `derivedSubgroup G` and no
chosen derived-subgroup inclusion. Its conclusion supplies one for every
semisimple `G`: the identity `𝟙 G` is a closed subgroup
(`derivedSubgroup_total_admissible`) whose range is `⊤`
(`SubgroupFunctor.range_id`). Hence `derivedSubgroup G = ⊤` gives
`IsDerivedSubgroup (𝟙 G)`, and `IsDerivedSubgroup.isRepresentedBy` then
shows that `derivedSubgroup G` is represented by `𝟙 G`. These two
consequences are not stated or proved in this module.

## Source

J. S. Milne, *Algebraic Groups* (2017), source `milne-2017-algebraic-groups`,
SHA-256 `899b0f44f3c3ccfbaf5ed1cf9c180be7a8016b1c0e9709ca0c206304e5bd1cd0`,
Corollary 21.50, printed p. 440 (PDF p. 456): "Every semisimple algebraic
group is perfect." Definition 12.45, printed p. 245 (PDF p. 261), defines an
algebraic group to be perfect "if it equals its derived group". The Lean
proposition is this corollary, with "perfect" unfolded by Definition 12.45
and both notions expressed through the Library predicates below.

The source conventions correspond to the Library interfaces as follows.

- Milne 6.44, printed p. 135 (PDF p. 151), calls a connected group variety
  semisimple when its geometric radical `R(G_{k^a})` is trivial. Section 6h,
  which contains this definition, takes all algebraic groups to be affine.
  Milne 6.46 (same page) defines reductivity through the geometric unipotent
  radical. Unipotent groups are solvable, so the geometric unipotent
  radical lies in the geometric radical and every semisimple group is
  reductive. For reductive groups, Proposition 19.10, printed p. 400
  (PDF p. 416), shows that semisimplicity holds exactly when the centre
  `Z(G)` is finite. The paragraph before it warns that this centre need be
  neither smooth nor connected. The Library's `IsSemisimple` takes the
  reductive finite-centre form as its definition, with the full
  scheme-theoretic centre, as explained in `Reductive/Semisimple.md`.
  `IsConnectedReductive` is Milne's 6.46 notion with `AlgebraicClosure k` in
  place of `k^a`. A smooth affine scheme over a field is of finite type, so
  `G` is an algebraic group in Milne's sense.
- Milne's derived group `DG` (Definition 6.16, printed p. 129 / PDF p. 145) is
  the intersection of the normal algebraic subgroups with commutative
  quotient. For affine or smooth `G`, Proposition 6.18 (same page) identifies
  it with the algebraic subgroup generated by the commutator map. Its proof
  describes this as the smallest algebraic subgroup containing the commutator
  subgroup of `G(R)` for every `k`-algebra `R`. A closed subgroup scheme
  contains the commutators of affine test points exactly when it contains
  those of all test schemes, because factorization through a closed immersion
  is local on the test scheme. That is the condition tested by
  `derivedSubgroup`. So `DG = G` corresponds to `derivedSubgroup G = ⊤`.
  `Derived/StructureStatement.md` explains this correspondence for the
  representability statement. Writing perfectness as an equality of all-test
  subgroup functors is the Library's derived reformulation of `DG = G`. It
  does not require a chosen representative of the derived subgroup.

## Source proof, not formalized here

Milne's proof first reduces to an algebraically closed field. The formation
of `DG` commutes with field extension by Corollary 6.19(a), printed p. 129
(PDF p. 145), and over an algebraically closed field the semisimple group is
split. For a root `α`, 21.23, printed p. 432 (PDF p. 448), takes `G^α` to be
the derived group of `G_α = C_G(T_α)`. The proof of Theorem 21.11 gives a
central isogeny `v_α : SL₂ → G^α`. Proposition 20.24, printed p. 416
(PDF p. 432), shows that `SL₂` and `PGL₂` are perfect. Quotients of perfect
groups are perfect (remark after Definition 12.45), so each `G^α` is perfect
and therefore lies in `DG`. Proposition 21.49(b), printed p. 439
(PDF p. 455), says that a split semisimple group is generated by the `G^α`.
Hence `DG = G`.

A formal proof must also supply the split structure theory over the
algebraic closure, the compatibility of `derivedSubgroup` with base change to
`AlgebraicClosure k`, and the passage from a representing closed subgroup
back to the full test-scheme functor. None of this is proved here.

## What is not stated

The proposition makes no claim about rational points, about the abstract
group `G(k)`, or about groups that are not semisimple. In particular it says
nothing about representability of `derivedSubgroup` for groups outside its
semisimple hypothesis; for semisimple `G` it yields the identity
representative described above. It does not assert that every point of `G`
is a product of a bounded number of commutators.
