# Normality transfer along a central product

This internal reduction for the derived-subgroup semisimplicity proof is by
Worker 1367 and uses Worker 1451's `Derived.EpiProductDetection` helpers. It
asserts no new derived-subgroup geometry.

The first two normality lemmas identify normality of a subgroup functor with its
all-test normalizer being the full group. Normality supplies both conjugation
directions after every refinement of a test scheme.
`normalizerPointsEquivOfIsNormal` constructs the resulting multiplicative
equivalence from normalizer points to all ambient points. Its forward map
forgets the membership witness, and its inverse equips any point with that
witness. The normality-transfer proof uses this inverse to place every point
of `D` in the normalizer before transporting it to `G`.

Suppose an affine group scheme `G` over a field is generated by two actual
homomorphisms `i : Z ⟶ G` and `d : D ⟶ G`, in the sense that their scheme
multiplication map is epimorphic. Assume `i` is central and `d` is a closed
subgroup inclusion. For a closed subgroup `j : H ⟶ D` normal in `D`, its
composite into `G` is normal in `G`. Indeed, its normalizer in `G` has a
closed representative by `hasClosedRepresentative_normalizer_centralizer_range`.
Centrality places the range of `i` in this normalizer, and the normality in
`D`, transported along `d`, places the range of `d` in it. The closed-subgroup
detection lemma then makes the normalizer the whole of `G`.

Consequently, if `G` has trivial unipotent radical, so does `D`. Any smooth,
connected, unipotent normal closed subgroup of `D` becomes such a subgroup
of `G`; its range is trivial there. The monomorphism `d` reflects triviality
of its range in `D`, by `SubgroupFunctor.range_comp_eq_bot_iff`.

The field and universe are arbitrary, and all ranges, centrality, normality
and normalizers retain their full scheme-theoretic meaning on every test
scheme, including nonreduced tests. No smoothness, connectedness or
unipotence is asserted for `D` by these lemmas.
