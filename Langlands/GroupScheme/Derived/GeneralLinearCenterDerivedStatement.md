# Centre and derived group of `GL_n`, and perfectness of `PGL_n`

`Langlands.GroupScheme.GeneralLinearCenterDerivedStatement.{u}` is a
Prop-valued definition. This module defines the proposition and proves
nothing.

## Statement

The proposition says: let `k : Type u` be a field and `n` a natural number,
and write `G = GeneralLinear.groupScheme (Fin n) (CommRingCat.of k)`. Then
the following three assertions hold.

```text
(1) ∀ (T : Over (Spec k)) (g : T ⟶ G.X),
      g ∈ (center G).carrier T ↔
        ∃ u : Γ(T.left, ⊤)ˣ,
          (GeneralLinear.pointsEquiv (Fin n) (CommRingCat.of k) T g : Matrix _ _ _) =
            Matrix.scalar (Fin n) (u : Γ(T.left, ⊤))
(2) IsDerivedSubgroup (SpecialLinear.inclusion (Fin n) (CommRingCat.of k))
(3) ∀ (Q : GroupScheme (Spec k)) (q : G ⟶ Q),
      RelativeRoot.IsFppfQuotient (center G) q → derivedSubgroup Q = ⊤
```

In words: (1) a point of `GL_n` over a test scheme `T` is central exactly when
its matrix is a scalar matrix `u • 1` for a unit `u` of `Γ(T, 𝒪_T)`; (2) the
inclusion `SL_n → GL_n` is a closed subgroup whose points are exactly the
points of the derived subgroup of `GL_n`, on every test scheme; (3) every
quotient of `GL_n` by its centre is perfect.

For `n ≥ 1` the map `u ↦ u • 1` from units to invertible matrices is
injective, so (1) says that the centre of `GL_n` is `𝔾_m`, embedded as the
scalar matrices, and the quotients in (3) are `PGL_n = GL_n / 𝔾_m`. For
`n = 0` this identification fails: `GL_0` is the trivial group, so its centre
is trivial, while every unit `u` gives the same empty matrix `u • 1 = 1`, so
the scalar homomorphism `𝔾_m → GL_0` has kernel all of `𝔾_m`. Assertion (1)
still holds for `n = 0`, since both sides are true for the unique point
(take `u = 1`).

The ingredients are existing declarations, used without change.

- `GeneralLinear.groupScheme (Fin n) (CommRingCat.of k)` (from
  [`GeneralLinear`](../GeneralLinear.md)) is the general linear group scheme
  over `Spec k`, the spectrum of an explicit coordinate ring of `n × n`
  invertible matrices. `GeneralLinear.pointsEquiv (Fin n) R T` is the group
  isomorphism from the points `T ⟶ G.X` over `Spec k` to `GL (Fin n) Γ(T, 𝒪_T)`,
  for every test scheme `T` over `Spec k` (not necessarily affine).
  `Matrix.scalar (Fin n) a` (Mathlib) is the diagonal matrix with all diagonal
  entries `a`.
- `SpecialLinear.inclusion (Fin n) R` (from
  [`SpecialLinear`](../SpecialLinear.md)) is the homomorphism of group schemes
  from `SpecialLinear.groupScheme (Fin n) R`, the spectrum of
  `R[X_(i,j)] / (det(X) - 1)`, to `GL_n`. By `pointsEquiv_inclusion`, on the
  points over every `T` it is the inclusion of determinant-one matrices into
  invertible matrices.
- `center G` (from [`Rational.Characters`](../Rational/Characters.md)) is the
  centre of `G` as a subgroup functor: a point `g : T ⟶ G.X` lies in it when,
  for every `a : T' ⟶ T` over `Spec k`, the restriction `a ≫ g` commutes with
  every point `T' ⟶ G.X`.
- `derivedSubgroup G` (from [`Basic`](Basic.md)) is the scheme-theoretic
  derived subgroup functor: a point over `T` lies in it when it factors
  through every closed subgroup `i : H ⟶ G` (`IsClosedSubgroup i`, the
  underlying morphism of schemes is a closed immersion) whose range contains
  the commutators `x * y * x⁻¹ * y⁻¹` of all points over all test schemes.
  `IsDerivedSubgroup d` says that `d` is a closed subgroup and that its range
  functor `SubgroupFunctor.range d` equals `derivedSubgroup G`. `⊤` is the
  subgroup functor of all points.
- `RelativeRoot.IsFppfQuotient N q` (from
  [`Rational.RootSubgroupStructureStatement`](../Rational/RootSubgroupStructureStatement.md))
  says that the morphism of schemes underlying the homomorphism `q` is flat,
  surjective and locally of finite presentation, and that the range of the
  kernel inclusion `kernelι q` (from
  [`AbelianScheme.Kernel`](../../AbelianScheme/Kernel.md)) is the subgroup
  functor `N`.

The test schemes `T` and `T'` are all schemes over `Spec k`, including
non-affine and nonreduced ones.

## Provenance (derived)

Source: J. S. Milne, *Algebraic Groups* (2017), registered as
`milne-2017-algebraic-groups` (SHA-256
`899b0f44f3c3ccfbaf5ed1cf9c180be7a8016b1c0e9709ca0c206304e5bd1cd0`). Printed page
numbers are given; the PDF page is the printed page plus 16.

The statement combines two examples.

- Example 12.47, printed p. 246: "The centre of GL_n is G_m (nonzero scalar
  matrices), its derived group is SL_n, and the quotient GL_n/G_m = PGL_n is
  perfect (because simple and noncommutative). The sequence (78) is
  1 → μ_n → G_m × SL_n → GL_n → 1."
- Example 6.22, printed p. 130: "Let G = GL_n. Then DG = SL_n. Certainly,
  DG ⊂ SL_n. Conversely, every element of SL_n(k) is a commutator, because
  SL_n(k) is generated by elementary matrices, and every elementary matrix is
  a commutator if k has at least three elements (see 20.24 below for this
  argument in the case n = 2). It follows that D(PGL_n) = PGL_n."

Conjunct (1) is the first clause of Example 12.47, conjunct (2) is the second
clause (equivalently `DG = SL_n` in Example 6.22), and conjunct (3) is the
third clause (equivalently `D(PGL_n) = PGL_n` in Example 6.22). The exact
sequence (78) is not part of the Lean statement.

Definitions used by the source:

- Centre. Proposition 1.92, printed p. 33: "Let H be an algebraic subgroup of
  G. There is a unique algebraic subgroup C = C_G(H) of G such that, for all
  k-algebras R, C(R) = {g ∈ N(R) | g centralizes H(R′) in G(R′) for all
  R-algebras R′}." Printed p. 34: "The centre Z(G) of G is defined to be
  C_G(G)."
- Derived group. Definition 6.16, printed p. 129: "The derived group of G is
  the intersection of the normal algebraic subgroups N of G such that G/N is
  commutative." Proposition 6.18 (same page): "If G is affine or smooth, DG
  is the algebraic subgroup of G generated by the commutator map G × G → G,
  (g1, g2) ↦ [g1, g2] = g1 g2 g1⁻¹ g2⁻¹." Its proof: "By definition, H is the
  smallest algebraic subgroup containing the commutator subgroup of G(R) for
  all R."
- Perfect. Definition 12.45, printed p. 245: "An algebraic group G is perfect
  if it equals its derived group."
- `PGL_n`. Example 5.49, printed p. 111: "The algebraic group PGL_n is defined
  as the quotient GL_n/G_m of GL_n (with G_m embedded diagonally)." Quotient
  maps, Definition 5.5, printed p. 99: "A homomorphism of algebraic groups is
  a quotient map if it is faithfully flat."

The provenance is derived for these reasons.

- The centre. The Library's `center G` tests commutation after restriction
  along every morphism of test schemes `T' ⟶ T`; for affine `T = Spec R` and
  affine `T' = Spec R′` this is Milne's condition "g centralizes G(R′) in
  G(R′) for all R-algebras R′" (the condition `g ∈ N(R)` is automatic when
  `H = G`). Non-affine test schemes add nothing, since commutation of two
  points over `T'` can be checked on an affine open cover of `T'`. Milne's
  `k`-algebras are finitely generated ("Conventions and notation", printed p. 3); since `GL_n`
  is of finite type, any two points over an arbitrary `R`-algebra come from a
  finitely generated `R`-subalgebra, so this restriction gives the same
  condition. Milne's
  "G_m (nonzero scalar matrices)" is made explicit as the scalar matrices
  `u • 1` with `u` a unit of `Γ(T, 𝒪_T)`; this describes the centre on the
  points over every `T` and does not choose an embedding `𝔾_m → GL_n`. For
  `n ≥ 1` it is Milne's statement: `u ↦ u • 1` is injective, and the
  Library's scalar homomorphism `GeneralLinear.scalarTorus` (in
  [`Rational.Characters`](../Rational/Characters.md)) is a closed subgroup
  for a nonempty index type (`GeneralLinear.isClosedSubgroup_scalarTorus`).
  For `n = 0` the scalar matrices form the trivial group, which is the centre
  of the trivial group `GL_0`, and not `𝔾_m`; Milne's identification of the
  centre with `𝔾_m` needs `n ≥ 1`, while the Lean formula holds for every
  `n`.
- The derived group. The Library's `derivedSubgroup` is the intersection of
  all closed subgroups whose range contains every commutator; this is the
  description of `DG` in Proposition 6.18 and its proof, which applies since
  `GL_n` and `PGL_n` are affine. A closed subgroup contains the commutators of
  the points over all affine test schemes exactly when it contains those over
  all test schemes, because factoring through a closed immersion is local on
  the test scheme. So Milne's `DG = SL_n` becomes conjunct (2), and "perfect"
  (Definition 12.45) becomes `derivedSubgroup Q = ⊤`. The Library uses the
  same reading of perfectness in
  [`SemisimplePerfectStatement`](SemisimplePerfectStatement.md), and explains
  the correspondence for the derived group in [`Basic`](Basic.md) and
  [`StructureStatement`](StructureStatement.md).
- `PGL_n` as a quotient. For `n ≥ 1`, Milne's quotient map `GL_n → PGL_n`
  is faithfully flat with kernel the diagonal `G_m`, that is, the centre. It
  is of finite type between noetherian schemes, hence locally of finite
  presentation, so it satisfies `IsFppfQuotient (center G) q` (the kernel
  condition uses conjunct (1)). The Lean statement does not construct
  `PGL_n`. It asserts perfectness for every homomorphism `q : G ⟶ Q`
  satisfying `IsFppfQuotient (center G) q`, with no finiteness assumption on
  `Q`. Such a `q` exists for every `n`: the Library's
  `affineNormalSubgroupQuotient` (in
  [`AffineNormalSubgroupQuotientProof`](../AffineNormalSubgroupQuotientProof.md))
  proves `AffineNormalSubgroupQuotientStatement`, which gives `q` for any
  closed normal subgroup with range `center G`. For `n ≥ 1` one can take the
  scalar subgroup `GeneralLinear.scalarTorus (Fin n) (CommRingCat.of k)`,
  which is a closed subgroup by `GeneralLinear.isClosedSubgroup_scalarTorus`,
  is normal because it is central, and has range `center G` by conjunct (1).
  For `n = 0` the scalar map is not an embedding; instead `G` is trivial, so
  `center G` is the subgroup functor of all points and equals the range of
  the kernel of `𝟙 G`, and `q = 𝟙 G` is an fppf quotient by the centre.
  (An fppf cover with kernel `N` identifies `Q` with the fppf sheaf quotient
  of `G` by `N`, so all such `Q` are isomorphic under `G`; the statement does
  not use this.)
- All `n`. Milne does not restrict `n`. For `n = 0`, `GL_0`, `SL_0`, the
  centre and every quotient by it are trivial, and the centre is not `𝔾_m`
  (see above); for `n = 1`, `GL_1 = 𝔾_m` is commutative, `SL_1` and every
  quotient of `GL_1` by its centre are trivial, and its derived group is
  trivial. All three conjuncts hold in both cases. Milne's reason for (3),
  "simple and noncommutative", applies only for `n ≥ 2`, but the trivial group
  is perfect.
- One universe `u` is used for `k`, the test schemes and `Q`. This is a
  formalization convention.

Remarks on the source argument (not used by the Lean statement).

- Example 6.22 argues with points over `k`. It rests on Proposition 6.21,
  printed p. 130 ("The derived group DG of a connected group variety G is the
  unique connected subgroup variety of G such that (DG)(k^a) = D(G(k^a))"),
  so the argument is to be applied over `k^a`. The argument shows that
  `SL_n(k)` is generated by commutators, which is what Proposition 6.21
  needs; it does not show that every element is a single commutator.
- For `n = 2`, "at least three elements" is not enough. The proof of
  Proposition 20.24, printed p. 416, chooses `b ∈ k^×` with `b ≠ ±1`, which
  needs at least four elements, and `SL_2(F_3)` is not perfect (its
  commutator subgroup has order 8, index 3). Over an algebraically closed
  field this does not matter.
- Remark 6.23, printed p. 131: "The (abstract) group G(k) may have
  commutative quotients without G having commutative quotients, i.e., we may
  have G = DG but G(k) ≠ D(G(k)). This is the case for G = PGL_n if
  k^× ≠ k^×n because there is a surjection det: PGL_n(k) → k^×/k^×n (see 5.49)
  and commutators have determinant 1." Conjunct (3) concerns the
  scheme-theoretic `derivedSubgroup Q`, not the commutator subgroup of the
  abstract group `Q(k)`.

Relation to the Library. The Library has no statement of Example 6.22 or
Example 12.47 and no group scheme `PGL_n`. Related declarations:

- [`Rational.GeneralLinearScalarCenter`](../Rational/GeneralLinearScalarCenter.md)
  factors a morphism into `GL_n` whose point matrices are central on every
  test scheme through the scalar matrices, using Mathlib's
  `Matrix.GeneralLinearGroup.center_eq_range_scalar`. It does not describe
  `center G` itself.
- `SemisimplePerfectStatement` (semisimple groups over a field are perfect,
  with a Library proof) would give conjunct (3) once `Q` is known to be
  semisimple; the Library does not provide this for quotients of `GL_n`.
- `rationalPerfectnessCriterion` (in
  [`RationalPerfectness`](RationalPerfectness.md)) gives
  `range f ≤ derivedSubgroup G` when `k` is algebraically closed, the source
  of `f` is reduced and locally of finite type, and its group of `k`-points
  is perfect. It is a tool for one inclusion of conjunct (2) over an
  algebraically closed field.

## For a proof (not part of this module)

A proof of the Lean statement can follow these steps; the Library proves it as
`generalLinearCenterDerived` in
[`GeneralLinearCenterDerivedProof`](GeneralLinearCenterDerivedProof.md). Write `R = Γ(T, 𝒪_T)`.

- Conjunct (1). If the matrix of `g` is `u • 1`, then by naturality of
  `pointsEquiv` the matrix of `a ≫ g` is the image of `u` times `1`, which
  commutes with every matrix; `pointsEquiv` is a group isomorphism, so `a ≫ g`
  commutes with every point. Conversely, taking `a = 𝟙 T`, the matrix of `g`
  lies in the centre of `GL_n(R)`, which is the image of `Rˣ` by
  `Matrix.GeneralLinearGroup.center_eq_range_scalar`.
- Conjunct (2), closedness and `range ≤ derivedSubgroup`. `SL_n` is the
  closed subscheme `det = 1` of `GL_n`, so the inclusion is a closed
  immersion. Commutators have determinant `1`, so the range of the inclusion
  contains all commutators; hence `derivedSubgroup G ≤ range`
  (`derivedSubgroup_le_range`).
- Conjunct (2), `range ≤ derivedSubgroup`. Let `i : H ⟶ G` be a closed
  subgroup whose range contains all commutators. For `n ≤ 1`, `SL_n` is
  trivial. For `n ≥ 3` and distinct
  `p, q, r`, the elementary matrix `E_pq(t)` is the commutator of `E_pr(t)`
  and `E_rq(1)` over every ring, so it lies in `H`. For `n = 2`, over the
  base change `T'` of `T` along `Spec k[b, b⁻¹, (b² - 1)⁻¹] → Spec k`
  (faithfully flat and of finite presentation),
  `E_12(t) = [diag(b, b⁻¹), E_12(t / (b² - 1))]`, and similarly for `E_21`.
  A point over `T` whose pullback along a flat surjective `T' ⟶ T` factors
  through the closed immersion `i` factors through `i` (the ideal of the
  preimage of `H` vanishes after a faithfully flat extension). Every matrix
  of determinant `1` over a local ring is a product of elementary matrices,
  so every point of `SL_n` over `T` is, on an open cover of `T`, a product of
  points of `H`; factoring through a closed immersion is local on `T`.
- Conjunct (3). Let `i : H ⟶ Q` be a closed subgroup whose range contains all
  commutators. Its preimage under `q` is a closed subgroup of `G` whose range
  contains all commutators (as `q` is a homomorphism) and contains the kernel
  `center G`; by conjunct (2) it contains `SL_n`. Let `x : T ⟶ Q.X`. Since `q`
  is flat, surjective and locally of finite presentation, `x` lifts to `G`
  after the base change `T' = T ×_Q G ⟶ T`, which is again flat and
  surjective; let `g` be the lift. For `n ≥ 1`, let `T'' ⟶ T'` be the base
  change of the `n`-th power map `𝔾_m ⟶ 𝔾_m` along `det g : T' ⟶ 𝔾_m`; it
  is finite locally free of rank `n`, hence flat and surjective, and over
  `T''` there is a unit `s` with `s^n = det g`. Then `g = (s • 1) · h` with
  `h` of determinant `1`, so `q(g) = q(h)` lies in `H` over `T''` (for
  `n = 0`, `g = 1`). By the descent step above, `x` factors through `i`. Hence every point of `Q` lies in `derivedSubgroup Q`.
