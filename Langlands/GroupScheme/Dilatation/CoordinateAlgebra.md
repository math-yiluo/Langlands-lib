# The coordinate algebra for affine dilatation

For a commutative ring \(A\), an element \(\pi\in A\), and an ideal \(I\subseteq A\),
`Dilatation.coordinateAlgebra π I` is the actual \(A\)-subalgebra
\[
  A[I/\pi]\subseteq A[1/\pi]
\]
generated by the elements \(f/\pi\), for \(f\in I\). The ambient ring is Mathlib's
`Localization.Away π`; division uses the inverse of the image of \(\pi\) in that
localization. No inverse in \(A\) is assumed.

The implementation supplies the canonical inclusion into \(A[1/\pi]\), proves
that it is injective, and constructs the affine scheme
\(\operatorname{Spec}(A[I/\pi])\) with its actual morphism to
\(\operatorname{Spec}(A)\). The inclusion is injective because the coordinate
algebra is a subalgebra. This does not assert that the map from \(A\) into either
ring is injective when \(\pi\) is a zero divisor.

For each \(f\in I\), `dividedElement` is an element of this coordinate algebra and
`mul_dividedElement` proves
\[
  \pi\,(f/\pi)=f.
\]
Consequently `ideal_map_le_span` proves
\[
  I\,A[I/\pi]\ \subseteq\ \pi A[I/\pi].
\]
The construction also has the defining least-subalgebra property
`coordinateAlgebra_le_iff` and is monotone in \(I\).

## Source correspondence

Kaletha–Prasad, *Bruhat–Tits theory: a new approach* (2023), Appendix A.5,
Construction A.5.2 and Proposition A.5.3, printed pp. 632–633 (PDF pp. 663–664),
construct the affine blow-up algebra inside the generic-fibre coordinate ring.
For a flat affine finite-type model over a discrete valuation ring, \(A\) is its
coordinate ring, \(\pi\) is the image of a uniformizer, and \(I\) is the ideal of a
closed subscheme of its special fibre. Thus \(\pi\in I\), and the model's generic
coordinate ring is \(A[1/\pi]\). Taking an \(A\)-subalgebra generated by \(I/\pi\)
is the same construction as taking the valuation-ring subalgebra generated by
both \(A\) and \(I/\pi\).

This unit implements that algebra and its spectrum before imposing the model
hypotheses. Its definitions and the elementary identities proved here make sense
for any commutative ring, any \(\pi\), and any ideal. This extension of the
definition does not extend the source's geometric representability conclusions.
The source's official *Corrections and additions* (2024), PDF pp. 49–50, has no
correction to A.5; its Appendix A correction concerns the perfect-residue-field
hypothesis in A.4.

## Implemented proofs and remaining obligations

Generator membership, the least-subalgebra property and monotonicity follow from
`Algebra.adjoin`. The multiplication identity follows from the localization
identity \(\pi\pi^{-1}=1\), then subtype extensionality transfers it to the
coordinate algebra. The ideal containment follows by exhibiting `dividedElement`
as the required multiple for every element of \(I\). The affine scheme morphism
is `AlgebraicGeometry.Spec.map` of the canonical algebra map.

This is a coordinate construction. The flat-test factorization property of
Proposition A.5.3, independence of the uniformizer, finite type and flatness over
the valuation ring, identification of the generic fibre, compatibility with
products, the induced group structure of Lemma A.5.7, and the smoothness result of
Lemma A.5.10 remain separate obligations. No relative identity-component
existence, smoothening, model gluing or descent result is proved or assumed here.
