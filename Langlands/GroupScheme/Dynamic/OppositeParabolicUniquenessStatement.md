# Uniqueness of the opposite dynamic parabolic with a given Levi

Authors: Worker 937 and Worker 1757. Module `Langlands.GroupScheme.Dynamic.OppositeParabolicUniquenessStatement`, namespace `Langlands.GroupScheme`. One closed statement, `OppositeDynamicParabolicUniquenessStatement : Prop`, with universe parameter `u`. Nothing is proved here.

## Source

Armand Borel and Jacques Tits, *Groupes réductifs*, Publ. Math. IHÉS 27 (1965). The relevant source assertions, translated into English and with notation normalized, are:

- **4.8 (opposite parabolic subgroups)**, printed p.88 / PDF p.35. Two parabolic subgroups of `G` are *opposite* if their intersection is a Levi subgroup of each of them. "It is clear that `P_θ⁻` is the only parabolic subgroup opposite to `P_θ` that contains `Z_θ`." Consequently, for a parabolic `P`, the map `P' ↦ P ∩ P'` is a bijection from the parabolics opposite to `P` onto the Levi subgroups of `P`.
- **4.10 (Proposition)**, printed pp.88–89 / PDF pp.35–36. For parabolic subgroups `P`, `P'` of `G` the following are equivalent, among others: a) `P` and `P'` are opposite; e) `P ∩ R_u(P') = P' ∩ R_u(P) = {e}`. In the proof of e) ⇒ a), take `P = P_θ` and `P'` containing the maximal torus `T`. The two trivial intersections give `R_u(P') ⊂ V_θ⁻` and `L ⊂ Z_θ`, where `L` is the Levi subgroup of `P'` containing `T`. If the symmetric root set `Φ(T,L)` were strictly smaller than `[θ]`, or `R_u(P') ≠ V_θ⁻`, there would be a root `a` for which neither `U_a` nor `U_{-a}` is contained in `P'`. This is impossible for a parabolic containing `T`, so `P' = P_θ⁻`. The common-positive-relative-root argument below is a derived adaptation, rather than a translation of this last step.
- **3.18 (Corollary)**, printed p.82 / PDF p.29, and **5.2**, printed p.96 / PDF p.43. For a relative root `a ∈ Φ(S, G)` of a maximal `k`-split torus `S`, the root group `U_(a)` (the group `U_Ψ` of 3.8 for the set `Ψ = (a)` of positive integral multiples of `a`) is a unipotent group defined over `k` (5.2), split over `k` (3.18). By the proof of 3.20 (printed p.83 / PDF p.30), a `k`-split unipotent group is `k`-isomorphic, as a variety, to an affine space.

In §4 Borel and Tits work with the groups over an algebraic closure (with statements about `k`-structures). The Lean statement is a rational version for dynamic parabolics; see Faithfulness.

## The statement

`OppositeDynamicParabolicUniquenessStatement` quantifies over a field `k : Type u`, a group scheme `G` over `Spec k` with `IsConnectedReductive G`, and two cocharacters `χ χ' : (Gm (Spec (CommRingCat.of k))).toGrp ⟶ G`. Write `pt = Over.mk (𝟙 (Spec (CommRingCat.of k)))` for the point over `k`, so that `F.carrier pt` is the subgroup of `k`-points of a subgroup functor `F`. It assumes

1. `Dynamic.centralizer χ' = Dynamic.centralizer χ`, equality of the dynamic centralizers `Z(χ') = Z(χ)` as subgroup functors of `G`;
2. `(Dynamic.parabolic χ).carrier pt ⊓ (Dynamic.parabolic χ').carrier pt = (Dynamic.centralizer χ).carrier pt`, that is, `P(χ)(k) ∩ P(χ')(k) = Z(χ)(k)` in `G(k)`;

and concludes

- `Dynamic.parabolic χ' = Dynamic.parabolic (Dynamic.oppositeCocharacter χ)`, and
- `Dynamic.unipotent χ' = Dynamic.unipotent (Dynamic.oppositeCocharacter χ)`,

equalities of subgroup functors of `G`. Here `Dynamic.parabolic`, `Dynamic.centralizer` and `Dynamic.unipotent` are the published dynamic subgroup functors `P(λ)`, `Z(λ)`, `U(λ)` (limits of `λ(t) g λ(t)⁻¹` as `t → 0` exist, are constant, or equal `1`), and `Dynamic.oppositeCocharacter χ` is the published cocharacter `t ↦ χ(t⁻¹)`. The published `Dynamic.inverseCocharacter` (Aubert/General/RationalOppositeParabolic) has the same body, so it is definitionally equal to `Dynamic.oppositeCocharacter`.

## Why the statement is true

This is a sketch of the expected proof; it is not a Lean proof. The images of `χ` and `χ'` are central in their common centralizer and therefore commute. They generate a `k`-split torus, which is contained in a maximal `k`-split torus `S` of `G`. Let `Φ = Φ(S, G)` be its relative roots. For a cocharacter `λ` of `S`, `Z(λ)` is generated by `Z_G(S)` and the root groups `U_(a)` with `⟨a, λ⟩ = 0`, and `U(λ)` is generated by the `U_(a)` with `⟨a, λ⟩ > 0`. This description allows nonreduced relative root systems, with the positive multiples already grouped into `U_(a)`. Hypothesis 1 says that `⟨a, χ⟩ = 0` exactly when `⟨a, χ'⟩ = 0`. If some root `a` had `⟨a, χ⟩ > 0` and `⟨a, χ'⟩ > 0`, then `U_(a)(k)` would lie in `P(χ)(k) ∩ P(χ')(k)` but meet `Z(χ)(k)` trivially. By 5.2 and 3.18 this is a nontrivial `k`-split unipotent group. As recalled in the proof of 3.20, its underlying variety is an affine space of positive dimension over `k`; it therefore has a nonidentity `k`-point even when `k` is finite. This contradicts hypothesis 2. So `⟨a, χ'⟩ < 0` whenever `⟨a, χ⟩ > 0`, and by the symmetry `Φ = −Φ` also `⟨a, χ'⟩ > 0` whenever `⟨a, χ⟩ < 0`. Hence `χ'` and `χ⁻¹` have the same sign on every relative root, and the root-group descriptions of dynamic parabolics and radicals give `P(χ') = P(χ⁻¹)`, `U(χ') = U(χ⁻¹)` as subgroup functors on every test scheme. This adapts the root-theoretic criterion behind 4.10 e) ⇒ a) to rational points; it does not infer equality of subgroup functors from equality of their `k`-points in general.

## Faithfulness and scope

- **Opposite parabolics.** For dynamic parabolics of a connected reductive group, `P(χ) ∩ P(χ⁻¹) = Z(χ)` and `Z(χ)` is a Levi subgroup of both, so `P(χ⁻¹)` is opposite to `P(χ)` and contains the Levi `Z(χ)`. The statement says that it is the only dynamic parabolic with Levi `Z(χ)` that meets `P(χ)` only in `Z(χ)`, which is the uniqueness claim of 4.8 in the dynamic setting.
- **Rational points.** The source's opposition conditions (4.8, 4.10 a) and e)) are conditions on the groups themselves. Hypothesis 2 is a condition on `k`-points only, which is weaker in general and is what the represented-parabolic statements of Renard VI.9.6 provide. It still suffices over every field, including finite fields, because relative root groups have nontrivial `k`-points. This is a **derived** formulation: it combines 4.8, 4.10 e) ⇒ a), 3.18 and 5.2.
- **Same Levi.** Hypothesis 1 fixes the Levi subgroup. The source's bijection `P' ↦ P ∩ P'` gives existence and uniqueness of an opposite parabolic for each Levi subgroup; only uniqueness is stated here, since existence is provided by `P(χ⁻¹)`.
- **Dynamic setting (derived choice).** Both parabolics are supplied by actual cocharacters, rather than quantified as arbitrary parabolic subgroups as in the source. This formulation gives the full subgroup-functor comparison needed by the existing dynamic Iwahori and contraction interfaces. Realizing arbitrary represented parabolics dynamically is the separate `ParabolicDynamicStatement`; it is not a premise or conclusion of this statement.
- **Field scope.** The field is arbitrary. No maximal torus, root datum, splitness, characteristic, perfectness, infinitude or scheme-level intersection condition is supplied as a hypothesis. The maximal split torus in the sketch is chosen inside the argument. The conclusion still retains arbitrary test schemes, including nonreduced ones.

## Use

This statement is the missing step identifying the opposite parabolic of the represented-parabolic statements `LeviSecondAdjointnessStatement` and `LeviJacquetContragredientStatement` (Renard VI.9.6) with the dynamic `P(χ⁻¹)`. Those statements give the opposite parabolic `P̄` only through its Levi, `mbar ≫ pbar = m ≫ p`, and through `k`-points, `pointImage p ⊓ pointImage pbar = pointImage (m ≫ p)`. Once `P = P(χ)` and `P̄ = P(χ')` with Levi `Z(χ) = Z(χ')`, this statement gives `U(χ') = U(χ⁻¹)`, so the `k`-points of the unipotent radical of `P̄` are those of `U(χ⁻¹)`, the opposite radical used by the published Iwahori decompositions.
