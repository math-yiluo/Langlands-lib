# The actual adjoint determinant restricted to a split torus

The theorem `Langlands.GroupScheme.Dynamic.adjointDeterminantTorusRestriction.{u}` proves the published proposition `AdjointDeterminantTorusRestrictionStatement.{u}` as its entire type. It imports the statement without changing its hypotheses or definitions. The conclusion gives both finiteness of the actual nonzero roots and the equality of algebraic characters with the sum of all those roots, each repeated by the actual dimension of its weight space.

The proof first establishes the identity for an arbitrary automorphic action `a` of an affine group scheme `H` on `U`, with `U` affine over the field and with the supplied natural finite coordinates `c`, and for any homomorphism `j` from a split torus `T` to `H`. The published `Lie.TorusDeterminant` module constructs a finite basis of the actual Lie kernel by collecting bases of its nonzero weight spaces. Its index is the sigma type of an occurring character and `Fin` of that weight space's dimension. The zero character is included in this basis index whenever its weight space is nonzero. The same module proves that over every test algebra the actual differential is diagonal in the extended basis and its determinant is the product of the indexed character values. Its finite-support proof uses the internal weight decomposition and finite coordinates; it needs no finite-type assumption.

The private lemma `character_ext_affine` turns equality of values on all affine tests into equality of algebraic characters. An affine group scheme has a universal affine coordinate point, constructed by `AffineCoordinates.coordinateCogroupIso`; equality at this point, injectivity of the affine coordinate equivalence for the multiplicative group, and cancellation of the coordinate isomorphism identify the underlying morphisms. The split torus is affine by `IsSplitTorus.isAffineHom` and the affineness of the field spectrum. Thus `determinantCharacter_restrict_eq_basisSum` applies this extensionality lemma to the actual determinant character. The evaluation theorem `determinantCharacter_affinePoints` identifies its value with the intrinsic determinant of the actual differential; `differential_det_lieWeightBasis` identifies this determinant with the basis-character product. Evaluation carries the additive sum of algebraic characters to that product.

The lemma `basisSum_eq_rootSum` collects this finite basis sum by its weight-space index. The sum over each `Fin` fiber is its cardinality times the indexed character, hence gives precisely `Module.finrank k (a.lieWeightSpace j α)` as multiplicity. Finite weight support permits rewriting the root `finsum` as the sum over all occurring characters. The two summation sets have the same intersection with the support of the summand: their only possible difference is the zero character, whose multiple is zero. This removes the zero-weight term. The intermediate `Fintype` arguments are supplied from the proved finite support and are not extra hypotheses of `determinantCharacter_restrict_eq_rootSum`.

Finally the target theorem specializes the generic character identity and root-finiteness theorem to the constructed `parabolicAdjointAction hP hU`. Its determinant character is definitionally the published `adjointDeterminantCharacter hP hU c`. No weight decomposition, determinant formula or structural theorem is assumed as an additional input. All roots in the defined root set retain their own dimensions; no grouping by reduced roots is used. The proof includes arbitrary characteristic, rank-zero tori, empty weight bases and arbitrary `j`.

This is an internal proof of the published algebraic statement. Its route uses the published finite-coordinate decomposition, scalar-extension and determinant constructions, rather than an analytic modulus calculation. It makes no assertion about Haar measures, absolute values or the inverse-modulus convention discussed in the statement's Markdown.
