# Connectedness of the unipotent dynamic subgroup of an affine group over a field

Let `k` be a field, `G` a group scheme over `Spec k` whose structure morphism is affine,
`χ : 𝔾_m ⟶ G` a cocharacter (the trivial one included) and `j : H ⟶ G` a homomorphism of group
schemes over `Spec k` that represents `Dynamic.unipotent χ`. The main theorem
`Langlands.GroupScheme.DynamicConnected.connectedSpace_of_isRepresentedBy_unipotent` states that
the underlying space of `H` is connected. Neither smoothness nor connectedness of `G` is assumed.
This is the unipotent assertion of `Langlands.GroupScheme.DynamicConnectedStatement` (module
[`SmoothConnectedStatement`](SmoothConnectedStatement.md)), proved without its smoothness
hypothesis.

All declarations are in the namespace `Langlands.GroupScheme.DynamicConnected`. The grading
identities of the coaction come from the Library module [`CoactionGrading`](CoactionGrading.md).

## An algebraic contraction

This is the argument of Conrad–Gabber–Prasad, *Pseudo-reductive groups*, 2nd ed., Proposition
2.1.8(4), end of the proof of Step 3 (printed p. 56): every point of `U_G(λ)` is joined to the
identity by the extension `𝔸¹ → U_G(λ)` of `t ↦ t.u`. It is implemented on coordinate rings, as
a statement about idempotents.

### Idempotent polynomials

* `X_mul_injective`: multiplication by `X` is injective on `D[X]` (compare coefficients).
* `eq_zero_of_eq_X_mul_sq`: if `q = X q²` then `X^n ∣ q` for every `n` (induction:
  `q = X^n r` gives `q = X q · q = X^{n+1} (r q)`), so every coefficient of `q` vanishes.
* `eq_zero_of_isIdempotentElem_of_eval_zero`: an idempotent `p ∈ D[X]` with `p(0) = 0` is zero.
  Write `p = X q`; then `X q = (X q)² ` gives `q = X q²`.
* `connectedSpace_of_contraction`: let `E : D →+* D[X]` satisfy `E(d)(1) = d` for all `d` and
  `E(d)(0) ∈ {0, 1}` for every idempotent `d`. If `Spec D` is nonempty it is connected. By
  Mathlib's `PrimeSpectrum.isClopen_iff`, a clopen set is `D(e)` for an idempotent `e`. If
  `E(e)(0) = 0`, the idempotent `E(e)` vanishes, so `e = E(e)(1) = 0` and `D(e) = ∅`. If
  `E(e)(0) = 1`, the same argument for `1 - e` gives `e = 1` and `D(e) = Spec D`.

### The graded contraction (section `GradedContraction`)

Fix commutative rings `B`, `K` with `[Algebra K B]`, a ring map `c : B →+* B[T, T⁻¹]` and a ring
map `ε : B →+* K`. Write `b_n` for the coefficient `(c b).coeff n`.

* `contractionIdeal K c ε` is the ideal generated by the `b_n` with `n < 0` and the differences
  `b_0 - ε(b)` (with `ε(b)` mapped to `B`). For the coaction and counit of a corepresented group
  it is, by definition, the published `Corepresented.unipotentIdeal χ`
  (`unipotentIdeal_eq_contractionIdeal`, proved by `rfl`).
* `truncHom` turns a ring map into Laurent polynomials without negative terms into a ring map into
  polynomials (Mathlib's `LaurentPolynomial.trunc`, multiplicative because `toLaurent` is
  injective).
* `contractionAux : B →+* (B ⧸ I)[X]` is `b ↦ ∑_{n ≥ 0} [b_n] X^n`.
* Assume (`hH`) `c(b_n) = b_n T^n` for all `b`, `n`, and (`hA`) `c` is the constant inclusion on
  the image of `K`. `contractionIdeal_le_ker`: both kinds of generators `x` satisfy
  `c(x) = x T^n` for some `n` and lie in `I`, so all coefficients of `c(x)` lie in `I` and
  `contractionAux x = 0`. Hence `contraction : B ⧸ I →+* (B ⧸ I)[X]` is defined by
  `Ideal.Quotient.lift`.
* `eval_zero_contraction`: the value at `0` of `contraction [b]` is `[b_0] = [ε(b)]`.
* `eval_one_contraction`: assume (`hE`) the value of `c(b)` at `T = 1` is `b`. The value at `1`
  of `contraction [b]` is the value at `T = 1` of `[c(b)]`, which is `[b]`
  (`eval₂_one_mapRingHom`: evaluation at `1` commutes with coefficient maps).
* `connectedSpace_contractionIdeal`: if moreover `ε` kills `I`, `K` is nontrivial and its only
  idempotents are `0` and `1`, then `Spec (B ⧸ I)` is connected. The map `ε` descends to
  `B ⧸ I`, which is therefore nontrivial; for an idempotent `[b]`, `ε(b)` is an idempotent of `K`,
  so the value at `0` of `contraction [b]` is `0` or `1`.

### Application (sections `Topology`, `Unipotent`, `Representatives`)

* `counit_eq_zero_of_mem_unipotentIdeal`: the counit kills `unipotentIdeal χ`, since the identity
  point lies in `U_G(χ)` (published `isAffineCutOutBy_unipotent`).
* `connectedSpace_unipotentScheme`: the explicit representative
  `Corepresented.unipotentScheme χ = Spec (B ⧸ unipotentIdeal χ)` of a corepresented group over a
  field is connected. The three hypotheses of `connectedSpace_contractionIdeal` for
  `Corepresented.coaction χ` come from module `CoactionGrading`: `hH` is
  `coaction_coeff_coaction` (`c(b_n) = single n b_n`) rewritten by Mathlib's
  `LaurentPolynomial.single_eq_C_mul_T`; `hA` is `coaction_algebraMap`; `hE` is
  `coaction_comp_laurentEvalOne` (the coaction followed by evaluation at `T = 1` is the
  identity), applied to `b`. The only idempotents of a field are `0` and `1`.
* `connectedSpace_of_iso`: connectedness of the underlying space is invariant under isomorphisms
  of group schemes (the forgetful functor to schemes sends the isomorphism to a homeomorphism,
  Mathlib's `Scheme.homeoOfIso`).
* `connectedSpace_of_isRepresentedBy_unipotent`: for affine `G`, the published representative
  `Dynamic.Affine.unipotentScheme χ` is `Corepresented.unipotentScheme` of the cocharacter in the
  chosen coordinates; an arbitrary representative `H` is isomorphic to it
  (`SubgroupFunctor.IsRepresentedBy.isoOfIsRepresentedBy`), hence homeomorphic
  (`connectedSpace_of_iso`).

## Provenance and scope

Source: Conrad–Gabber–Prasad, *Pseudo-reductive groups*, second edition (2015), source ID
`conrad-gabber-prasad-2015-pseudo-reductive`, Proposition 2.1.8(4) and the end of Step 3 of its
proof (printed pp. 53, 56; PDF pp. 79, 82). The algebraic formulation of the contraction argument
through idempotents of `D[X]` is internal to this formalization. CGP assume that `G` is affine
and of finite presentation over a ring; their unipotent connectedness assertion does not require
smoothness. The Lean theorem only needs `G` affine over a field.
