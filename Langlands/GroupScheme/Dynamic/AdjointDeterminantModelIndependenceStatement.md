# Independence of the adjoint determinant character from the represented models

This module states, without proof, that the algebraic character
`det(Ad | Lie U)` on a parabolic `P(χ)` and on its Levi factor `Z(χ)` does not
depend on two choices: the group schemes representing the dynamic subgroup
functors, and the finite coordinates of `Lie(U)` in which the determinant is
computed.

The only declaration is the Prop-valued definition
`Langlands.GroupScheme.Dynamic.AdjointDeterminantModelIndependenceStatement`,
at a single universe level `u`.

## Setting

The statement quantifies over the following data.

- A commutative ring `R : CommRingCat.{u}`, a group scheme `G` over
  `Spec R`, and an actual cocharacter `χ : (Gm (Spec R)).toGrp ⟶ G`.
- Two representations of the published dynamic unipotent subgroup functor
  `Dynamic.unipotent χ`, namely `hU : (unipotent χ).IsRepresentedBy iU` with
  `iU : U ⟶ G`, and `hU' : (unipotent χ).IsRepresentedBy iU'` with
  `iU' : U' ⟶ G`. Both `U.X.hom` and `U'.X.hom` are affine morphisms
  (`IsAffineHom`).
- Finite coordinates `c : Lie.FiniteCoordinates U d` and
  `c' : Lie.FiniteCoordinates U' d'` of the two Lie algebras, for natural
  numbers `d` and `d'` (`Langlands.GroupScheme.Lie.FiniteAction`). Such
  coordinates are natural `A`-linear
  isomorphisms `Lie(U)(A) ≅ A^d` over all commutative `R`-algebras `A`.
  The statement assumes neither `d = d'` nor any relation between `c` and
  `c'`.

`R` is any commutative ring. In particular `R` may be a field of any
characteristic. No reductivity, connectedness, smoothness or separatedness
of `G` is assumed. Neither is it assumed that `P(χ)` is a proper parabolic
subgroup. The rank of the torus generated by `χ` is unrestricted, so `χ` may
be trivial.

## The statement

The statement is a conjunction of two parts.

1. **Parabolic.** Let `hP : (parabolic χ).IsRepresentedBy iP` and
   `hP' : (parabolic χ).IsRepresentedBy iP'` be two representations of the
   published `Dynamic.parabolic χ`, with `P.X.left` and `P'.X.left` affine.
   Then

   `characterRestrict (isoOfIsRepresentedBy hP hP').hom
      (adjointDeterminantCharacter hP' hU' c') =
    adjointDeterminantCharacter hP hU c`.

2. **Centralizer.** Let `hZ : (centralizer χ).IsRepresentedBy iZ` and
   `hZ' : (centralizer χ).IsRepresentedBy iZ'` be two representations of the
   published `Dynamic.centralizer χ`, with `Z.X.left` and `Z'.X.left` affine.
   Then

   `characterRestrict (isoOfIsRepresentedBy hZ hZ').hom
      ((centralizerAdjointAction hZ' hU').determinantCharacter c') =
    (centralizerAdjointAction hZ hU).determinantCharacter c`.

The ingredients are the following.

- `isoOfIsRepresentedBy hP hP' : P ≅ P'` is the published canonical
  isomorphism between two representing group schemes. It satisfies
  `(isoOfIsRepresentedBy hP hP').hom ≫ iP' = iP`
  (`SubgroupFunctor.IsRepresentedBy.isoOfIsRepresentedBy_hom_comp`). The same
  holds for `Z` and `Z'`.
- `characterRestrict f` is the published restriction of rational characters
  along a homomorphism `f`, namely `χ ↦ f ≫ χ`. So each part says that the
  character built from the primed models, pulled back along the canonical
  isomorphism, is the character built from the unprimed models.
- `adjointDeterminantCharacter hP hU c : rationalCharacters P` is the
  determinant character of the conjugation action
  `parabolicAdjointAction hP hU` of `P` on `U` in the coordinates `c`. Both
  are from `Langlands.GroupScheme.Dynamic.AdjointDeterminant`.
- `centralizerAdjointAction hZ hU : AutomorphicAction Z U` is the action of
  `Z` on `U` by conjugation, from
  `Langlands.GroupScheme.Dynamic.CentralizerAdjointAction`, and
  `AutomorphicAction.determinantCharacter`, from
  `Langlands.GroupScheme.Lie.ActionDeterminant`, is the determinant of the
  differential of an action, read in the given coordinates.

Characters are written additively: `rationalCharacters H` is the additive
group of homomorphisms `H ⟶ G_m`.

## Conjugation orientation

Both actions are by normalized conjugation with the orientation `u ↦ p u p⁻¹`
(respectively `u ↦ m u m⁻¹`). The point `p` is the left factor and `p⁻¹`
the right factor. For the parabolic this is
`Dynamic.parabolicAdjointAction_act_comp`, and for the centralizer it is
`Dynamic.centralizerAdjointAction_act_comp`. The determinant is that of the
differential of this action on `Lie(U)`.

## Scope

The statement is about the actual `P(χ)`, `Z(χ)` and `U(χ)` and their
inclusions into `G`. The determinant characters are the constructed ones, not
abstract characters supplied as hypotheses. No existence of the coordinates
is assumed or claimed: the statement holds for all choices of `c` and `c'`.
Existence of finite coordinates is a separate structural target. The
statement does not compare the determinant on `P` with the one on `Z`; that is
`AdjointDeterminantCentralizerRestrictionStatement`.

## Provenance

The packaging is internal and derived. In the literature the modulus
character `|det(Ad | Lie N)|` of a parabolic `P = MN` is attached to the
group `P` itself. It does not depend on a presentation of `P` or on a basis
of `Lie N`. See Renard, *Représentations des groupes réductifs p-adiques*
(version of 9 July 2026), §V.5.4, printed pp.126–127 / PDF pp.132–133. It is
registered as `renard-2026-representations-groupes-reductifs-padiques`,
SHA256 `7b539996793b7c3620e72e1e389588eb42d1a8e91d6f68b2eead5c2e2ffc182c`.
See also Waldspurger, *La formule de Plancherel pour les groupes p-adiques,
d'après Harish-Chandra* (2003), §I.1, printed pp.238–241 / PDF pp.4–7, where
`δ_P` denotes the module of `P`. It is registered as
`waldspurger-2003-plancherel`, SHA256
`4b6a6162cd0b3863703fb2f65d1698f926caf813114917f2084c16e38f2e30f7`.

In this Library a dynamic subgroup is a subgroup functor of `G`. The
determinant is built from a chosen representing group scheme and chosen
coordinates of its Lie algebra. This statement records that the outcome is
intrinsic. The expected proof follows: the canonical isomorphism carries the
action on `U'` to the action on `U`, its differential identifies the two Lie
algebras, and the determinant of a linear endomorphism does not depend on the
basis. Neither source states this result for arbitrary base rings. It is a
consequence of the definitions.
