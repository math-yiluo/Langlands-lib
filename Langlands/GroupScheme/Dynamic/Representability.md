# Representability of dynamic subgroups of affine group schemes

Let `R` be a commutative ring and `G = Spec B` an affine group scheme over `Spec R`, given as
the published `Corepresented.groupScheme F e` for a group-valued functor `F` on commutative
`R`-algebras corepresented by `B`. Let `χ : 𝔾_{m,Spec R} ⟶ G` be a cocharacter. This module
proves that the published dynamic subgroup functors (`Langlands.GroupScheme.Dynamic.Basic`)

- `P_G(χ)` (`Dynamic.parabolic χ`: points whose limit at zero exists),
- `Z_G(χ)` (`Dynamic.centralizer χ`: points with constant orbit map),
- `U_G(χ)` (`Dynamic.unipotent χ`: points with limit `1`)

are represented by closed subgroup schemes `Spec (B ⧸ I)` of `G`. The ideals `I` are explicit.
Representability holds on all test schemes `T` over `Spec R`, not only on spectra of algebras.
No finite presentation, flatness or smoothness of `G` is assumed.

## Sources

- Conrad–Gabber–Prasad, *Pseudo-reductive groups*, 2nd ed. (2015), Lemma 2.1.4, printed p. 49
  (PDF p. 75). For an affine `k`-scheme `V` with a `GL₁`-action, the closed subscheme cut out
  by `J = ∑_{n<0} k[V] · k[V]_n` represents the points `v` for which `lim_{t→0} t.v` exists.
  The proof: the orbit map of `v` corresponds to `f ↦ ∑_n t^n f_n(v)`, a map
  `k[V] → R[t, 1/t]`, and the limit exists iff this map lands in `R[t]`. The paragraph after
  the proof applies this to `V = G` with `t.g = λ(t) g λ(t)⁻¹`, giving `P_G(λ)`.
- Same book, Lemma 2.1.5, printed p. 50 (PDF p. 76), with proof on printed p. 51 (PDF p. 77).
  `P_G(λ)` is a subgroup scheme; `Z_G(λ) = P_G(λ) ∩ P_G(-λ)` is the centralizer; `U_G(λ)` is
  a closed subgroup scheme. Its ideal is "generated by the `n`-eigenvectors `f ∈ k[G]_n` for
  `n ≤ 0` such that `f(1) = 0`".
- Conrad, *Reductive group schemes* (2014), Theorem 4.1.7(1), p. 112, with proof on p. 113.
  `P_G(λ)`, `U_G(λ)` and `Z_G(λ)` are represented by closed subgroups. The existence of
  `P_G(λ)` is "a special case of [CGP, Lemma 2.1.4] (defining the closed subscheme by the ideal
  of `k[G]` generated by the negative weight spaces `k[G]_n` for `n < 0` …)", and "the proof of
  the existence of `P_G(λ)`, `Z_G(λ)`, and `U_G(λ)` as closed subgroup schemes does not require
  `G` to be finitely presented over `k`."

### How the formalization relates to the sources

The sources grade `k[G]` by weights. Here the grading is encoded by the **coaction**
`c = coaction χ : B ⟶ B[T, T⁻¹]`, the ring map of the conjugation orbit map of the universal
point. Informally, if `b = ∑_n b_n` is the weight decomposition of `b ∈ B`, then
`c(b) = ∑_n b_n T^n`, so the coefficient `(c b).coeff n` is the weight-`n` component `b_n`.
The module does not formalize the weight decomposition itself. It works with the coefficients
of `c(b)` directly, and the three ideals are generated by such coefficients:

- `parabolicIdeal χ` is generated by the coefficients `(c b).coeff n` with `n < 0`. Under the
  informal identification above, this is CGP's `J` (every `f ∈ k[G]_n` equals its own weight-`n`
  component).
- `centralizerIdeal χ` is generated by all coefficients of `c(b) - b`, that is, by the `b_n`
  with `n ≠ 0` and by `b_0 - b`. CGP and Conrad define `Z_G(λ)` as `P_G(λ) ∩ P_G(-λ)`; this
  module instead cuts it out directly from the published definition of `Z_G(χ)` as the
  centralizer (constant orbit map).
- `unipotentIdeal χ` is generated by the coefficients of negative degree and by the elements
  `(c b).coeff 0 - ε(b)`, where `ε = counit e : B ⟶ R` is the counit. CGP generate it by the
  weight vectors of weight `≤ 0` that vanish at `1`. For `n < 0` these are among the negative
  coefficients. For `n = 0`, `b_0 - ε(b)` is such a vector, and conversely a weight-`0` vector
  `f` with `ε(f) = 0` equals `f_0 - ε(f)`. The comparison with CGP's generators is not
  formalized.

The membership criteria below (`mem_parabolic_iff`, `mem_centralizer_iff`, `mem_unipotent_iff`)
are the formal content of CGP's proof. They are proved for affine test schemes through the
comparison in [`AffineExtension`](AffineExtension.md), then extended to all test schemes.

## Laurent polynomials without negative terms (namespace `AffineLine`)

- `coeff_toLaurent_natCast`, `coeff_toLaurent_of_neg`, `coeff_trunc`: coefficients of
  `Polynomial.toLaurent p` and of `LaurentPolynomial.trunc q`.
- `toLaurent_trunc`: a Laurent polynomial with no terms of negative degree is the image of its
  truncation.
- `polynomialLift g hg : C ⟶ A[X]`: a ring map `g : C ⟶ A[X, X⁻¹]` whose values have no terms of
  negative degree, viewed as a ring map to `A[X]` (pointwise `trunc`). `polynomialLift_comp`:
  composing with `A[X] ⟶ A[X, X⁻¹]` gives back `g`.
- `specMap_polynomialToLaurent_cancel`: two morphisms `Spec A[X] ⟶ Spec D` that agree on
  `Spec A[X, X⁻¹]` are equal (because `A[X] ⟶ A[X, X⁻¹]` is injective).

## The coaction (namespace `Corepresented`)

Fix `e : (F ⋙ forget GrpCat).CorepresentableBy B` and write `G = groupScheme F e`.

- `structureMap e : Spec B ⟶ Spec R` is the structure morphism of `G`.
- `universalPoint e` is the identity of `Spec B`, viewed as a point of `G` over `Spec B`.
- `pointRing x : B ⟶ A` is the ring map classifying a point `x : Spec A ⟶ G` over `Spec R`
  (`Spec.preimage` of `x.left`). `homMk_comp_universalPoint`: every such point is the pullback
  of the universal point.
- `coaction χ : B ⟶ B[T, T⁻¹]` is the ring map of `laurentPoint ≫ Dynamic.orbit χ (universalPoint e)`,
  the orbit map `(g, t) ↦ χ(t) g χ(t)⁻¹` of the universal point on `Spec B[T, T⁻¹]`.
  `specMap_coaction` restates the definition.
- `laurentPoint_comp_orbit_left`: for a point `x` over `Spec A`, the orbit map of `x` on
  `Spec A[T, T⁻¹]` is `Spec` of `c` followed by `laurentCoeffMap (pointRing x)`. Proof: the
  naturality `laurentPoint_comp_whiskerRight` of [`AffineExtension`](AffineExtension.md) and
  the published `Dynamic.whiskerRight_orbit`.
- `coeff_laurentCoeffMap_coaction`: the coefficients of that composite are the images of the
  coefficients of `c(b)` under `pointRing x`.

## Points over affine test schemes

- `specMap_pointRing` and `hom_ext_of_pointRing`: a point over `Spec A` is determined by its
  classifying ring map.
- `algebraMap_comp_pointRing`: `R ⟶ B ⟶ A` is the ring map of `f : Spec A ⟶ Spec R`.
- `counit e : B ⟶ R` is the classifying map of the identity point over `Spec R`, that is, the
  counit of the Hopf algebra `B`. `pointRing_one`: the identity point over `Spec A` is
  classified by `counit e` followed by `R ⟶ A`.
- `appTop_ΓSpecIso_inv_of_specMap_eq` and `appTop_ΓSpecIso_inv`: on global sections, a point
  over `Spec A` pulls back `b ∈ B` to `pointRing x b`.
- `homEquiv_symm_pointsEquiv_apply`: for every test scheme `T`, the algebra map
  `B ⟶ Γ(T, 𝒪_T)` classifying a point `x` (through the published
  `Corepresented.pointsEquiv`) sends `b` to the pullback of `b` along `x`.

## From affine test schemes to all test schemes

Let `K` be a subgroup functor of `G` and `I` an ideal of `B`.

- `IsAffineCutOutBy e K I` (a `Prop`): for every ring `A`, every `f : Spec A ⟶ Spec R` and every
  point `x` of `G` over `Spec A`, `x ∈ K` iff `pointRing x` kills `I`.
- `affineSubgroups K A ⊆ F A`: the points of `K` over `Spec A`, transported to `F A` by the
  published `affinePointsEquiv`.
- `isCutOutBy_affineSubgroups`: if `K` is affinely cut out by `I`, then the family
  `affineSubgroups K` satisfies the published `IsCutOutBy e _ I`
  ([`CorepresentedClosedSubgroup`](../CorepresentedClosedSubgroup.md)). So
  `cutGroupScheme` and `cutHom` give the closed subgroup scheme `Spec (B ⧸ I)` and its
  inclusion. `pointRing_algSpecMap` is the computation used here.
- `cutHom_mem`: the inclusion `Spec (B ⧸ I) ⟶ Spec B` is itself a point of `K`.
- `cutSubgroupFunctor_affineSubgroups`: the subgroup functor of `Spec (B ⧸ I)` equals `K` on
  every test scheme `T`. If `x` factors through `Spec (B ⧸ I)`, it lies in `K` by `cutHom_mem`
  and stability of `K` under restriction. Conversely, if `x ∈ K(T)`, then for each `b ∈ I` the
  pullback of `b` to `T` vanishes on every member of the affine open cover of `T` (by the
  affine hypothesis), hence vanishes (`Scheme.OpenCover.ext_elem`).
- `isRepresentedBy_cutHom_affineSubgroups`: `K` is represented by the inclusion
  `Spec (B ⧸ I) ⟶ G` (published `SubgroupFunctor.IsRepresentedBy`).

## Extensions along `Spec A[T, T⁻¹] ⊆ Spec A[T]`

Fix a point `x` over `Spec A`.

- `coeff_laurentCoeffMap` and `laurentCoeffMap_C`: coefficients of `laurentCoeffMap ψ q`, and
  its value on constants.
- `polynomialExtension χ x θ hθ : Spec A[T] ⟶ G`, for a ring map `θ : B ⟶ A[T]` whose
  composite with `A[T] ⟶ A[T, T⁻¹]` is `c ≫ laurentCoeffMap (pointRing x)`. This is `Spec θ`
  over `Spec R`. `laurentInclusion_comp_polynomialExtension`: it extends the orbit map of `x`
  along `Spec A[T, T⁻¹] ⊆ Spec A[T]`. `pointRing_polynomialExtension`: its classifying map is
  `θ`.
- `pointRing_comp_polynomialToLaurent`: conversely, the classifying map of any such extension
  lifts `c ≫ laurentCoeffMap (pointRing x)`.
- `pointRing_polynomialZero_comp`: the value at `T = 0` is classified by the composite with
  evaluation at zero.
- `laurentPoint_comp_fst_comp_left`: the constant map `(g, t) ↦ g` on `Spec A[T, T⁻¹]` is
  `Spec` of `pointRing x` followed by `A ⟶ A[T] ⟶ A[T, T⁻¹]`.

## Membership criteria

For a point `x` over `Spec A` with classifying map `φ = pointRing x`:

- `mem_parabolic_iff`: `x ∈ P_G(χ)` iff `φ((c b).coeff n) = 0` for all `b` and all `n < 0`.
  An orbit extension gives, through `laurentInclusion_comp_of_whiskerLeft`, a ring map to
  `A[T]` lifting `c ≫ laurentCoeffMap φ`, so the negative coefficients vanish. Conversely,
  `polynomialLift` gives such a ring map, `polynomialExtension` the extension over `Spec A[T]`,
  and `whiskerLeft_comp_of_laurentInclusion` an orbit extension in the sense of
  `Dynamic.IsOrbitExtension`.
- `pointRing_coeff_coaction_sub_C` rewrites `φ` applied to the coefficients of `c(b) - b`.
- `mem_centralizer_iff`: `x ∈ Z_G(χ)` iff `φ` kills every coefficient of `c(b) - b` for every
  `b`. The orbit map of `x` is constant iff `c ≫ laurentCoeffMap φ` equals `φ` followed by the
  inclusion of constants. One direction compares the two maps on `Spec A[T, T⁻¹]`; the other
  uses the splitting `laurentPointInv_comp_laurentPoint`.
- `mem_unipotent_iff`: `x ∈ U_G(χ)` iff the negative coefficients are killed and
  `φ((c b).coeff 0 - ε(b)) = 0` for every `b`. The limit is the value at `T = 0` of the
  extension (`evalZero_polynomialPointInv`), whose classifying map sends `b` to the constant
  coefficient `φ((c b).coeff 0)`. The identity point is classified by `ε` (`pointRing_one`).

## Ideals and closed subgroup schemes

- `forall_mem_span_iff`: a ring map kills `Ideal.span s` iff it kills `s`.
- `parabolicIdeal χ`, `centralizerIdeal χ`, `unipotentIdeal χ`: the ideals of `B` described
  above.
- `isAffineCutOutBy_parabolic`, `isAffineCutOutBy_centralizer`, `isAffineCutOutBy_unipotent`:
  `P_G(χ)`, `Z_G(χ)` and `U_G(χ)` are cut out on affine test schemes by these ideals (the
  membership criteria plus `forall_mem_span_iff`).
- `parabolicScheme χ`, `centralizerScheme χ`, `unipotentScheme χ`: the affine group schemes
  `Spec (B ⧸ I)` over `Spec R` (`cutGroupScheme`).
- `parabolicHom χ`, `centralizerHom χ`, `unipotentHom χ`: their inclusions into `G` (`cutHom`).
- `isRepresentedBy_parabolicHom`, `isRepresentedBy_centralizerHom`,
  `isRepresentedBy_unipotentHom`: these inclusions represent `P_G(χ)`, `Z_G(χ)` and `U_G(χ)` on
  every test scheme over `Spec R`.
- `isClosedSubgroup_parabolicHom`, `isClosedSubgroup_centralizerHom`,
  `isClosedSubgroup_unipotentHom`: the inclusions are closed subgroups in the sense of the
  published `IsClosedSubgroup` (closed immersions).

## Scope

The base is affine (`Spec R`) and `G` is corepresented. Only the conjugation action of a
cocharacter is treated, not Conrad's abstract `𝔾_m`-actions. The module does not prove finite
presentation of the subgroup schemes, the identification with CGP's weight-space generators,
or `Z_G(χ) = P_G(χ) ∩ P_G(-χ)`. The semidirect product decomposition
`U_G(χ)(T) ⋊ Z_G(χ)(T) ≅ P_G(χ)(T)` on points is published separately (`Dynamic.LimitMap`).
