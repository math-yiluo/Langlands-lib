# Uniform solvability of represented fppf subgroup products

Original draft: Worker 1378. Current-Library verification and submission: Worker 1576.

This internal module works over an arbitrary base scheme `S`. Its main
theorem `SubgroupFunctor.isSolvableScheme_of_isFppfProduct` takes group
schemes `H`, `J`, `P` and `G` over `S` and homomorphisms
`i : H ⟶ G`, `j : J ⟶ G` and `p : P ⟶ G`. It assumes that `i.hom.hom` is a
monomorphism and that `SubgroupFunctor.range i` is normal. It also assumes
that `p` represents the fppf-local product of the two ranges
(`IsFppfProduct (range i) (range j) p`), and that `H` and `J` satisfy the
published `RelativeRoot.IsSolvableScheme`. It concludes
`RelativeRoot.IsSolvableScheme P`. If the derived series of every point group
of `H` vanishes at step `m` and that of `J` at step `n`, then that of `P`
vanishes at step `n + m` on every test scheme. No field, smoothness,
affineness or connectedness hypothesis is used.

## Auxiliary results

- `exists_fppf_refinement`: two flat, surjective, locally finitely presented
  maps to one test scheme have a common refinement of the same kind, namely
  their fibre product.
- The imported `CenterQuotient.comp_commutatorElement`: precomposition with a
  test morphism commutes with commutators of points.
- `FppfLocallyInProduct.exists_common`: two fppf-local product
  factorizations can be realized on one common cover.
- `FppfLocallyInProduct.commutator`: with a normal left factor `F`, if `x`
  and `y` are fppf-locally in `F · N` and `F · N'`, and every commutator of
  a point of `N` and a point of `N'` lies in `C`, then `⁅x, y⁆` is
  fppf-locally in `F · C`. On a common cover write `x = a b` and
  `y = a' b'`. Modulo the normal subgroup `F(V)` the commutator `⁅a b, a' b'⁆`
  agrees with `⁅b, b'⁆`. So `⁅x, y⁆` is the product of
  `⁅a b, a' b'⁆ ⁅b, b'⁆⁻¹ ∈ F(V)` and `⁅b, b'⁆ ∈ C(V)`.
- `localProduct F N hF T`: for normal `F`, the points over `T` that are
  fppf-locally in `F · N` form a subgroup of the point group.
- `precompHom` and `postcompHom`: precomposition with a test morphism and
  postcomposition with a group-scheme homomorphism, as homomorphisms of point
  groups.
- `derivedRange j e`: the subgroup functor whose points over `V` are the
  images under `j` of the `e`-th derived subgroup of the points of `J` over
  `V`. Its stability under restriction uses that precomposition is a group
  homomorphism and so maps derived subgroups into derived subgroups.
  `range_le_derivedRange_zero` and `commutator_mem_derivedRange` record the
  two facts used below.
- `fppfLocallyInProduct_derivedSeries`: if `p` represents the fppf product
  and `range i` is normal, every point of the `e`-th derived subgroup of the
  points of `P` over `T` maps to a point of `G` that is fppf-locally in
  `range i · derivedRange j e`. The proof is induction on `e`. The base case
  is the representation property of `p`. In the step, the derived subgroup is
  generated by commutators. `FppfLocallyInProduct.commutator` handles the
  generators. `localProduct` is a subgroup, so its preimage contains the
  whole subgroup they generate.

## Proof of the main theorem

Let `m` and `n` be the solvability bounds of `H` and `J`. The functor
`derivedRange j n` is trivial, so it lies in `range i`. Thus every point of
the `n`-th derived subgroup of the points of `P` over `T` maps to a point
that is fppf-locally in `range i · range i`. The published
`FppfLocallyInProduct.mem_range_of_le`, in `SubgroupFunctor.FppfProductLaws`,
uses the monomorphism `i` to place it in `range i` on `T` itself. Hence the
image of the `n`-th derived subgroup of `P(T)` lies in the image of `H(T)`.
Taking `d` further derived subgroups on both sides, and using that images of
commutator subgroups are commutator subgroups of images, the image of the
`(n + d)`-th derived subgroup of `P(T)` lies in the image of the `d`-th derived
subgroup of `H(T)`. For `d = m` the latter is trivial. The product inclusion `p`
is a closed immersion, hence a monomorphism, so postcomposition with it is
injective and the `(n + m)`-th derived subgroup of `P(T)` is trivial.

## Provenance

This is an internal result. It is the scheme-theoretic form, on all test
schemes with fppf-local factorizations, of the elementary fact that an
extension of solvable groups is solvable, used in Milne, *Algebraic Groups*
(2017), Lemma 6.41 for the product `H N` of a normal subgroup and a subgroup.
No external theorem is asserted.
