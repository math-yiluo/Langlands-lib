# Represented products of a closed subgroup with a normal closed subgroup

This module proves the published proposition
`Langlands.GroupScheme.NormalFactorFppfProductExistenceStatement.{u}` as the
theorem `Langlands.GroupScheme.normalFactorFppfProductExistence`. Over every
field `k : Type u`, let `G` be an affine group scheme over `Spec k` that is
locally of finite type. Let `i : H ⟶ G` and `j : N ⟶ G` be closed subgroups,
and suppose the range of `j` is normal. Then some group scheme `P` over
`Spec k` and homomorphism `p : P ⟶ G` satisfy
`SubgroupFunctor.IsFppfProduct (SubgroupFunctor.range i) (SubgroupFunctor.range j) p`.
In words, `p` is a closed immersion and its all-test image is the ordered
fppf-local product of the two ranges. The theorem has no smoothness,
connectedness, reducedness, perfectness or characteristic hypothesis. The
range of `i` need not be normal.

## Source

J. S. Milne, *Algebraic Groups* (2017), Theorem 5.82 and its proof (printed
pp. 120–121). Milne defines `HN` as the image of the multiplication
homomorphism from the semidirect product of `H` acting on `N` by conjugation.
He identifies it with the fppf sheaf generated by `R ↦ H(R)N(R)`. The image
theorem used is Milne, *Algebraic Groups* (2022 corrected reprint),
Theorem 5.39. In the Library it is the proposition `AffineHomImageStatement`,
proved as `Langlands.GroupScheme.affineHomImage`.

## Implemented proof

The proof has two steps. The lemma
`NormalFactorProduct.exists_isFppfProduct_of_affineHomImage` derives the
conclusion for given `k`, `G`, `i` and `j` from `AffineHomImageStatement.{u}`.
It runs as follows.

1. `H` and `N` are affine and locally of finite type over `Spec k`, because
   their structure morphisms factor through `G` by closed immersions.
2. Normality of `range j` implies that `range i` normalizes it. Let `a` be the
   conjugation action `AutomorphicAction.ofNormalizedConjugation j i`. Its
   semidirect product `a.semidirectProduct` has underlying scheme `N.X ⊗ H.X`.
   The structure morphism `pullback.fst N.X.hom H.X.hom ≫ N.X.hom` is affine,
   as a base change of an affine morphism followed by one. It is also locally
   of finite type.
3. **Construction of `P` and its closed embedding.** Apply the image theorem
   to the multiplication homomorphism
   `φ = SubgroupFunctor.normalizedProductHom j i`, which sends `(n, h)` to
   `n h`. This gives a group `I`, a closed subgroup `inc : I ⟶ G` and a
   homomorphism `fac` with `fac ≫ inc = φ`. Take `P = I` and `p = inc`. The
   fppf quotient part of the image theorem makes the underlying morphism
   `q : N.X ⊗ H.X ⟶ I.X` of `fac` flat, surjective and locally of finite
   presentation. The kernel clause of the image theorem is not used.
4. **Identification of the image.** `SubgroupFunctor.normalizedProductHom_hom`
   identifies `q ≫ inc` with the pointwise product `(fst ≫ j) * (snd ≫ i)`.
   `SubgroupFunctor.isFppfProduct_of_fppf_factorization` then shows that `inc`
   represents the fppf product of `range j` and `range i`, in that order.
5. `SubgroupFunctor.IsFppfProduct.swap` (from `SmoothConnectedNormalFactorProduct`)
   uses normality of `range j` to reverse the order of the factors. This gives
   the product of `range i` and `range j` that the target asks for.

`normalFactorFppfProductExistence_of_affineHomImage` unfolds the target
proposition and applies this lemma. The final theorem
`normalFactorFppfProductExistence` passes it the published proof
`affineHomImage : AffineHomImageStatement.{u}`. It therefore has no
theorem-valued premise.
