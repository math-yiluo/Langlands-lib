# Quadratic root depths normalized on the original henselian base field

This internal construction uses the published `henselianSeparableAddValuation` of a henselian integer-valued field `k` on a separable extension `L`. An intermediate field `E` makes `L/E` quadratic. The three fields have independent universes, compatible algebra structures and a scalar-tower witness. The implementation carries separability of `L/k` and `L/E` explicitly, matching the existing quadratic-parameter interface; the latter also follows from the former in the scalar tower. No topology or characteristic restriction is used. The value is normalized on the original `k`; no integer-normalized valuation on `E` is assumed.

Restricting the actual quadratic conjugation from an `E`-algebra automorphism to a `k`-algebra automorphism proves that it preserves this valuation. `towerDepth` is half the second-coordinate value on the existing `rationalParameters E L`; `towerCentralDepth` is the whole second-coordinate value on its literal zero-first-coordinate central subgroup. The published generic coordinate laws give the unique infinite-valued identity, inverse invariance, actual threshold subgroups, the literal doubling relation and central intersection formula, and the internal commutator depth estimate. Embedded base units retain exactly `IntegerValuation.order` of the original valuation.

When the base valuation is surjective, a nonzero trace-zero coordinate exists in the separable quadratic extension, including in characteristic two. Multiplying it by embedded `k`-scalars realizes every integer translate of its finite second-coordinate value. The same central parameters have half those values for the multipliable root. These two affine integer families are injective, so both depth images are infinite. Choosing a sufficiently large integer gives a common nonidentity central element at any prescribed root and central thresholds.

When `E/k` is finite-dimensional, the quadratic degree makes `L/k` finite-dimensional. The published total-degree valuation bound places central depths in `(1/[L:k]) ℤ` and multipliable-root depths in `(1/(2[L:k])) ℤ`. These are containment statements only. In particular, the general tower does not justify the fixed half-integer and quarter-integer bounds of the special case `E = k` without a normalization change.

The result derives the tower normalization and its consequences from the Library's field valuation, actual quadratic parameters, trace-zero existence, generic depth laws and filtration subgroups. It is about those actual parameter groups. It does not identify them with root subgroups of an arbitrary quasi-split group, choose a globally compatible system, prove global V2–V5 or full centralizer compatibility, or construct an apartment. Those obligations remain with the apartment task.
