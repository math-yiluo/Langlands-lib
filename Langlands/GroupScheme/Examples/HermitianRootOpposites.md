# Unitary subgroups and opposite Hermitian root matrices

This internal continuation of `HermitianRootMatrixStructure` constructs the
actual unitary and special unitary subgroups and the opposite root embedding.
It derives these constructions by matrix algebra from the existing coordinate
model. It makes no assertion of full generation or group-scheme classification.

Conjugate transpose fixes the identity and reverses multiplication. Consequently,
preservation of `gram H` is closed under identity, multiplication and inverse
among invertible matrices. `unitarySubgroup H` is exactly that subgroup.
`specialUnitarySubgroup H` intersects it with the determinant homomorphism's
kernel. The upper root embedding lies in this subgroup; so does the diagonal
embedding for every involution-fixed unit.

`swapIndex` interchanges top and bottom and fixes every middle index.
`swapMatrix` is the corresponding matrix with identity middle block. Its square
is the identity, and `swapElement` is the resulting general-linear element.
Left multiplication swaps rows and right multiplication swaps columns.
The swap is self-adjoint for the specified conjugate transpose and preserves
`gram H`. It belongs to the unitary subgroup. There is no assertion that the
swap has determinant one.

`oppositeEmbedding H` conjugates the existing upper root embedding by this
swap. It is an injective group homomorphism, and its matrix entries are obtained
by applying `swapIndex` to both indices of the upper matrix. Its image lies in
the special unitary subgroup: form preservation follows from subgroup closure,
and the swap's determinant cancels against that of its inverse.
`elementarySubgroup H` is the subgroup generated by the two root images.
Both images lie in it, and it is contained in `specialUnitarySubgroup H`.
Equality with the full special unitary subgroup remains a separate obligation.

The proof of inverse closure inserts the isometry identity between conjugate
transposes of the inverse and then cancels the matrix with its inverse.
The swap identities are proved by the finite block-index decomposition and
entrywise multiplication. The opposite and generated subgroup conclusions
then follow from general group operations.

The coefficient and middle-index universes are independent. The hypotheses
are a commutative ring, an involution, a finite index type with decidable
equality, and involution-fixed diagonal weights. Weights may vanish and the
middle index may be empty. No field or characteristic assumption is used.
