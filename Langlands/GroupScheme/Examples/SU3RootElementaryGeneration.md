# Root-generated diagonal and Weyl matrices

This module is an internal calculation from the existing `SU3Root.Parameters` coordinate group, its actual upper and opposite embeddings into `GL₃`, and the published reflection factorization. It introduces no external structural theorem. The subgroup construction uses an arbitrary commutative ring in universe `u`. The generation lemmas use an arbitrary field in universe `u`, with no characteristic restriction. The final specialization allows the ground field of a separable quadratic extension to lie in an independent universe `u` and its extension in universe `v`.

`elementarySubgroup c` is the supremum of the actual image ranges of `Parameters.embedding c` and `oppositeEmbedding c`. The two membership lemmas are the canonical inclusions of these ranges. For an actual root parameter `x` with nonzero second coordinate, `reflectionMatrix_mem_elementarySubgroup` applies the existing three-factor opposite factorization, so its reflection matrix belongs to this subgroup. Applying subgroup multiplication and inversion to two such reflections and using `reflectionMatrix_mul_inv` puts the diagonal with their second-coordinate ratio in the subgroup.

For a scalar `a` moved by the involution, put `d=a-bar(a)`. It is nonzero and satisfies `bar(d)=-d`. The constructions `Parameters.traceOneOfNonfixed a` and `Parameters.traceZeroOfNonfixed a` have coordinates `(1,a/d)` and `(0,1/d)`. Their defining trace-norm equations follow by involutivity, clearing the nonzero denominator and ring identities. Their first and second coordinate formulas are proved, and their second coordinates are nonzero: a moved scalar is itself nonzero. This argument uses neither division by two nor a restriction on the characteristic.

`diagonal_mem_elementarySubgroup_of_nonfixed` observes that these second-coordinate units have ratio exactly `a`. The reflection-ratio lemma therefore puts the diagonal of every nonfixed unit in the subgroup. If there is any moved scalar, `diagonal_mem_elementarySubgroup` proves this for every unit `t`. For a fixed `t`, choose a nonfixed unit `s`; the unit `t/s` is also nonfixed, since fixedness would imply `bar(s)=s` after cancelling `t` and taking inverses. The identity `t=s*(t/s)` and the diagonal homomorphism law complete the proof. The already nonfixed case uses the preceding lemma directly.

`weylElement_mem_elementarySubgroup` uses the nonzero trace-zero parameter to obtain an actual reflection matrix in the subgroup. Its diagonal already belongs by the preceding theorem. Multiplying the reflection by the inverse diagonal leaves the actual Weyl matrix, which therefore also belongs.

For the existing `fieldInvolution k L` on a separable quadratic extension, `fieldInvolution_exists_nonfixed` follows from `conjugation_ne_one`: if every scalar were fixed, extensionality would make conjugation the identity. The two quadratic specialization lemmas apply the preceding results with this proved nontriviality. They require no additional input beyond the actual separable quadratic extension.

These results concern the specified coordinate model and actual matrices. They do not prove generation of the full special-unitary matrix group, construct its group scheme, identify relative-root subgroups of an arbitrary reductive group, prove local division-algebra arithmetic or classification, or prove the unrestricted local Kneser–Tits statement. Those remain separate obligations of the host task.
