# The open locus of torus-centralizer residue fibres

Over a locally Noetherian base, let a smooth separated group have affine geometrically connected residue fibres. Let a smooth separated quasi-compact group with geometrically connected fibres map monomorphically into it, with closed homomorphisms on the residue fibres. The points where the given group on the actual residue fibre is a torus centralizer form an open subset. The inclusion is closed over that open. The residue-fibre condition pulls back along arbitrary base change by the field comparison.

At a point of the locus, the fibre torus factors centrally through the subgroup. Use the étale parameter of its central closed tori to lift this fibre point. The resulting torus homomorphism into the ambient group has a smooth, geometrically connected closed centralizer that contains the pulled-back subgroup. Their equality on the field-valued fibre reflects to the residue fibre of the lifted point, so the smooth subgroup equality open gives an open neighbourhood of equality. The image under the étale map is open. At each point of that image, field reflection supplies the torus-centralizer condition over the original residue field. These étale neighbourhoods cover the locus and identify the pulled-back inclusion with a closed immersion. Closedness descends along this fppf cover.

This is the torus-centralizer case of SGA 3, Exposé XV, Lemma 6.6 with the smoothness and affine-fibre hypotheses used here. The proof uses the étale central-torus parameter and the smooth subgroup equality open. The globally closed case with quasi-compact ambient group is a direct specialization.
