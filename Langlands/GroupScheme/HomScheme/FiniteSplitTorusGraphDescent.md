# Descent from a finite schematic graph image

Let \(R\to B\) be an injective map of commutative rings. Over \(S=\operatorname{Spec}R\), let \(H\) be the split torus with character group \(\mathbf Z^L\) for a finite set \(L\), and let \(G\) be a separated group scheme. Start with a homomorphism \(f:H_B\to G_B\). Its relative graph projects to \(H\times_S G\). Suppose the schematic image of this projection is finite over \(H\), and for each \(n>0\) the restriction of \(f\) to \(H_B[n]\) is the base change of a homomorphism \(H[n]\to G\). `FiniteSplitTorusGraphDescentStatement` asserts that \(f\) is the base change of a unique homomorphism \(H\to G\).

The proof pairs each lifted torsion homomorphism with the inclusion \(H[n]\to H\). After pullback to \(B\) the resulting map to \(H\times_S G\) factors through the schematic image of the graph. The projection \(H_B[n]\to H[n]\) is schematically dominant, so the ideal of the closed image pulls back to zero on \(H[n]\) itself. Each lifted torsion graph therefore factors through the same closed image.

The image is affine since it is finite over the affine scheme \(H\). In the diagonalizable coordinates of \(H\) and its torsion, the affine coefficient-descent theorem applies to the map from \(H_B\) to the image. It descends that map to \(H\). Composing with the image immersion and the second projection gives the descended map to \(G\). Schematic dominance verifies that this map lies over \(S\), and separatedness and flatness of the source let the proof detect the group law and uniqueness after pullback to \(B\).

This derived lemma supplies the descent step of the prolongation argument for `SmoothAffineFiberTorusSubgroupRepresentabilityStatement`. Raynaud compares torsion restrictions and uses a valuative criterion for prolonging a rational map from a normal split torus in *SGA 3*, Exposé XV, proof of Lemma 3.10, recomposed pp. 257–258, PDF pp. 25–26, Gille–Polo edition of 1 December 2008. Here finiteness of the schematic graph image is an explicit premise and Laurent coefficient descent constructs the map.
