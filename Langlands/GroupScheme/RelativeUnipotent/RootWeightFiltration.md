# Positive filtration terms for nonnegative root weights

Author: Worker 204.

Fix a supplied `RootHeightChart C` for an actual group scheme over the
spectrum of an arbitrary commutative ring. `RootWeightData C` supplies
a second family of natural weights on its root indices and the
standard commutator sum bound for those weights on every scheme test.
These weights may vanish. They need not equal the positive heights
already present in the regular chart.

Term `d` throughout this module means cutoff `d+1`. Its point subgroup
is generated by root factors of weight at least `d+1`. Thus term zero
is the subgroup of positive weights, which need not be the full chart
group. `mem_subgroup_iff` identifies a term with the points whose
original chart coordinates vanish below this cutoff. The forward
direction uses positive-cutoff ordered collection, chooses root
parameters, and replaces all lower parameters by zero. Uniqueness of
the original chart then identifies the coordinates. The converse
checks membership of every root factor in the supported product.

The support is a finite subset of the original root indices. Its
enumeration labels standard affine-space coordinates; it does not
reorder the original root product. `CoordinateFiltrationTerm`
represents the supported-coordinate subgroup functor on that actual
coordinate affine space, transfers the subgroup law to it, and gives
the closed inclusion `inclusion`. The resulting group scheme is
smooth and affine over the base.

Every term is normal in the full chart group. The original regular
chart expresses each ambient point as a product of the root factors.
The new sum bound shows each factor normalizes the new level, so
conjugation preserves it. A term whose cutoff exceeds all new weights
is the trivial subgroup on every scheme test.

The provenance is internal: the construction combines the existing
regular scheme chart, positive-cutoff group collection, the
coordinate-subspace representability theorem and the existing
normalizer argument. It establishes actual positive-weight terms
for supplied data. Identifying the first term with a supplied
parabolic radical and descending the terms and their quotients remain
separate obligations.
