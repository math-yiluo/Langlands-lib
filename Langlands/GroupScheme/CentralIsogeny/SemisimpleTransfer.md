# Semisimplicity of the source of a central isogeny

Author: Worker 1224. This is supporting material for task
`569a2d94-1d47-448e-8db3-48ecf4755ef4`, which proves the published
`Langlands.GroupScheme.CentralIsogenyCommonSemisimpleRefinement`. That proof builds
a smooth connected group `D` with a central isogeny `p : D ⟶ C` onto a semisimple
group `C`. This module shows that such a `D` is semisimple.

All groups are actual group schemes over `Spec k` for a field `k`, in an arbitrary
universe `u`. `IsSemisimple` is the Library predicate from `Reductive/Semisimple`:
connected reductive (`IsConnectedReductive`: smooth, affine, connected, and trivial
unipotent radical after base change to `AlgebraicClosure k`) together with
`HasFiniteCenter` (the full scheme-theoretic centre has a finite closed
representative). `IsCentralIsogeny` is the Library predicate from
`CentralIsogeny/Basic`.

## Main result

- `IsSemisimple.of_isCentralIsogeny (p : D ⟶ C) (hp : IsCentralIsogeny p)
  (hC : IsSemisimple C) (hDs : Smooth D.X.hom) (hDc : ConnectedSpace D.X.left) :
  IsSemisimple D`.

  *Proof.* `D` is affine over `k`, because `p` is finite and `C` is affine. Its
  centre is finite by `hasFiniteCenter_of_isCentralIsogeny` below. For the geometric
  unipotent radical, base change `p` to `k̄ = AlgebraicClosure k`
  (`IsCentralIsogeny.baseChange`, from `CentralIsogeny/BaseChange`). The base-changed
  map is again a central isogeny. It is flat and surjective, hence an epimorphism of
  schemes (`Flat.epi_of_flat_of_surjective`), and its full kernel is finite. The
  base change of `C` is smooth and affine and has trivial unipotent radical, because
  `C` is connected reductive. Then
  `HasTrivialUnipotentRadical.of_epi_of_finite_kernel_algClosed` gives triviality of
  the unipotent radical of the base change of `D`.

This is the semisimple case of Milne, *Algebraic Groups* (2017), Lemma 19.14
(printed p. 400, PDF p. 416): if `G' ⟶ G` is an isogeny of connected group varieties
and `G` is reductive or semisimple, then so is `G'`. Milne's semisimple means trivial
radical. The Library predicate instead asks for finite centre, so the centre step
below is written separately for this proof and uses that `p` is a central isogeny
only through finiteness, flatness and surjectivity.

## Supporting lemmas

- `SubgroupFunctor.eq_top_of_hasClosedRepresentative_of_range_le`: over any base,
  let `F` be a subgroup functor of an affine group `C` with a closed representative,
  and let `p : D ⟶ C` be a homomorphism whose underlying morphism over the base is
  an epimorphism. If the range of `p` lies in `F`, then `F = ⊤`.

  *Proof.* `p` factors through the closed representative `j` of `F`. So `j` is an
  epimorphism and a closed immersion, hence an isomorphism
  (`isIso_of_isClosedImmersion_of_epi_over`). Every point of `C` then factors
  through `j`.

- `MatrixRepresentation.isFixedVector_restrict_iff_of_injective`: if
  `q : H ⟶ G` has injective pullback on global functions, a vector is fixed by a
  matrix representation of `G` exactly when it is fixed by its restriction to `H`.

  *Proof.* The fixed-vector equation is an identity of matrices over global
  functions (`isFixedVector_iff_universal`). Restriction applies the injective ring
  map `q.hom.hom.left.appTop` to that identity.

- `IsUnipotent.of_appTop_injective`: under the same injectivity, unipotence of `H`
  implies unipotence of `G`. This follows from the previous lemma and the Library
  definition of `IsUnipotent` (every nonzero representation has a nonzero fixed
  vector).

- `HasTrivialUnipotentRadical.of_epi_of_finite_kernel_algClosed`: over an
  algebraically closed field, let `p : D ⟶ C` be a homomorphism of affine groups,
  with `C` smooth, whose underlying morphism is an epimorphism of schemes and whose
  full kernel is finite. If `C` has trivial unipotent radical, so does `D`.

  *Proof.* Let `i : U ⟶ D` be a smooth connected unipotent normal closed subgroup.
  `U` is geometrically integral (`smoothConnectedAffineGeometry`). The published
  `ImageGeneration.pointedImageGeneration` gives a smooth connected closed subgroup
  `E` of `C` generated by the image of `i ≫ p`, together with a dominant map from a
  power of `U` onto `E`.
  - `E` is unipotent. The factor map `U ⟶ E` is dominant and `E` is reduced, so the
    map has injective pullback on global functions (`app_injective` for
    scheme-theoretically dominant maps). Apply `IsUnipotent.of_appTop_injective`.
  - `E` is normal in `C`. `D` acts on `C` by conjugation through `p`, and on `U` by
    conjugation, which preserves `U` because `U` is normal. The map `i ≫ p` is
    equivariant, so the closed image envelope is stable
    (`closedImageEnvelope_isStable`). Hence the range of `p` lies in the normalizer
    of `E`. That normalizer has a closed representative
    (`hasClosedRepresentative_normalizer_centralizer_range`), so it is all of `C` by
    the first supporting lemma.
  - Hence `E` is trivial, so `i ≫ p = 1`. Then `i` factors through the kernel of
    `p`. A unit-preserving morphism from the reduced connected group `U` to the
    finite kernel is the unit morphism (`finiteTargetOverHom_eq_one_algClosed`, from
    `CentralIsogeny/Uniqueness`). So `i = 1`, and the range of `i` is trivial.

  This follows Milne's proof of Lemma 19.14, where the image of the unipotent
  radical is a smooth connected unipotent normal subgroup of the target, and so is
  trivial.

- `hasFiniteCenter_of_isCentralIsogeny (p : D ⟶ C) (hp : IsCentralIsogeny p)
  [IsAffineHom C.X.hom] (hC : HasFiniteCenter C) : HasFiniteCenter D`.

  *Proof.* The centre `Z ⟶ D` is represented by a closed subgroup
  (`fieldFixedSubgroupRepresentability` for the conjugation action, with
  `conjugation_id_fixedSubgroup_eq_center`). Let `Z` act on `C` by conjugation
  through `z ≫ p`. Its fixed subgroup has a closed representative and contains the
  range of `p`, because central points of `D` commute with all points of `D`. Since
  `p` is an epimorphism over the base, the fixed subgroup is everything, so
  `z ≫ p` lands in the centre of `C`. The centre of `C` has a finite closed
  representative, so `z ≫ p` factors through it by a map `w` with
  `w ≫ zc = z ≫ p`. This composite is finite, since `z` is a closed immersion and
  `p` is finite. Hence `w` is finite (`IsFinite.of_comp`), and `Z` is finite over
  `k`.

## Provenance

The main theorem is the semisimple case of Milne's Lemma 19.14, adapted to the
Library definition of semisimplicity. The proof of the unipotent radical step
follows Milne's argument, using the published image-generation and envelope
stability results. The fixed-vector, closed-representative and centre lemmas are
elementary arguments written for this proof.
