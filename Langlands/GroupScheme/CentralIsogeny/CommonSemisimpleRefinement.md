# Common semisimple refinement of two central isogenies

This module proves the unchanged published proposition
`Langlands.GroupScheme.CentralIsogenyCommonSemisimpleRefinement.{u}` from
`CentralIsogeny/CommonSemisimpleRefinementStatement`, for an arbitrary universe `u`.

## Statement

For every field `k : Type u`, all group schemes `C`, `H`, `G` over `Spec (CommRingCat.of k)` and
all homomorphisms `π : C ⟶ G` and `q : H ⟶ G`: if `C`, `H` and `G` are semisimple and `π` and
`q` are central isogenies, then there are a group scheme `D` over `Spec k` and homomorphisms
`p : D ⟶ C` and `r : D ⟶ H` such that `D` is semisimple, `p` and `r` are central isogenies, and
`p ≫ π = r ≫ q`.

`IsSemisimple` is the Library predicate from `Reductive/Semisimple`: connected reductive
(smooth, affine, connected, and trivial unipotent radical after base change to
`AlgebraicClosure k`) with a finite scheme-theoretic centre. `IsCentralIsogeny` is the Library
predicate from `CentralIsogeny/Basic`: the underlying scheme morphism is finite, flat,
surjective and locally of finite presentation, and the full, possibly nonreduced,
scheme-theoretic kernel is central against all test schemes. The field is arbitrary, including
imperfect fields and positive characteristic.

## Lean proof

`centralIsogenyCommonSemisimpleRefinement` is the term

    centralIsogenyCommonSemisimpleRefinement_of_perfect semisimplePerfect.

- `centralIsogenyCommonSemisimpleRefinement_of_perfect` (`CentralIsogeny/CommonRefinementOfPerfect`)
  derives the target from one hypothesis: for every field `k : Type u` and every semisimple
  group `K` over `Spec k`, `derivedSubgroup K = ⊤`. Here `derivedSubgroup` is the full
  test-scheme derived subgroup functor of `Derived/Basic`, the intersection of all closed
  subgroups containing the commutators.
- `semisimplePerfect : SemisimplePerfectStatement.{u}` (`Derived/SemisimplePerfectProof`) is
  exactly this hypothesis. It is proved unconditionally by integral Weyl averaging over an
  algebraically closed field and fpqc descent to `k`. It is Milne, *Algebraic Groups* (2017),
  Corollary 21.50 (printed p. 440).

The module adds no other step. The rest of this note describes the construction that the
imported conditional theorem carries out, so that the witness is explicit.

## The construction

Fix `π : C ⟶ G` and `q : H ⟶ G` as in the statement. The proof of
`CommonRefinement.hasCommonSemisimpleRefinement_of_perfect` runs as follows; every group below
is a group scheme over the original field `k`.

1. **Hypotheses of the construction.** A central isogeny is finite, flat and surjective, and its
   kernel is central. The semisimple groups `C`, `H` and `G` are smooth, affine and connected.
   `semisimplePerfect` at `C` and at `H` gives `derivedSubgroup C = ⊤` and
   `derivedSubgroup H = ⊤`.

2. **Descended commutators** (`CentralIsogeny/CommutatorDescent`). Because the kernel of `π` is
   central, the commutator `(x, y) ↦ x y x⁻¹ y⁻¹` of `C` depends only on `(π x, π y)`. It
   descends along the fpqc cover `π × π` to a morphism of schemes `G × G ⟶ C`. The same holds
   for `q` and `H`. Together they give a morphism `ambientMap π q : G × G ⟶ C × H`, which sends
   the identity to the identity.

3. **The group `D`** (`CentralIsogeny/CommonRefinementConstruction`). `D` is the closed subgroup
   of the smooth affine group `C × H` generated by the image of `ambientMap π q`. It is produced
   by the published `ImageGeneration.pointedImageGeneration`, which applies because `G × G` is
   affine, of finite type and geometrically integral, and is pointed by the identity. Let
   `i : D ⟶ C ⊗ H` be the inclusion. The theorem makes `i` a closed subgroup inclusion and
   `D` smooth, affine and geometrically connected over `k`, with some dominant ordered power
   map `(G × G)ⁿ ⟶ D`.

4. **The two maps.** `p = i ≫ fst C H : D ⟶ C` and `r = i ≫ snd C H : D ⟶ H` are the two
   projections.

## Why the maps satisfy the Library predicates

- **`p ≫ π = r ≫ q`.** Both composites, applied after the ordered power maps into `D`, are
  ordered powers of the commutator morphism of `G`, because the descended commutators compose
  with `π` and `q` to the commutator of `G`. `D` is reduced, since it is smooth over a field,
  and `G` is separated. Two morphisms from a reduced scheme to a separated scheme that agree
  after a dominant morphism are equal. So `(i ≫ fst C H) ≫ π = (i ≫ snd C H) ≫ q`.

- **Dominance of the projections.** Since `C` is perfect, the inclusion of the closed subgroup
  generated by the commutators of `C` is an isomorphism, so some ordered power of the commutator
  morphism of `C` is dominant. That ordered power factors through `p` (via `π × π`, the ordered
  power map into `D`, and `p`), so `p` is dominant. The same argument with `H` shows that `r` is
  dominant. This is the only place where perfectness enters.

- **`p` and `r` are central isogenies** (`CentralIsogeny/ProjectionCentralIsogeny`). `p` is
  finite: the compatibility makes `D ⟶ C × H` factor through the fibre product `C ×_G H`, whose
  projection to `C` is a base change of the finite `q`. A finite morphism is closed, and a
  dominant morphism with closed range is surjective, so `p` is surjective. `p` is flat, because
  a surjective homomorphism from a quasi-compact group locally of finite type onto a smooth
  group is flat (orbit-map flatness over `AlgebraicClosure k`, descended to `k`). It is locally
  of finite type because its composite with the structure morphism of `C` is, and hence locally
  of finite presentation, as `C` is locally noetherian. Its full kernel is central against all
  test schemes. If `x` is a `T`-point of `D` with `x ≫ p = 1`, then `x ≫ r ≫ q = x ≫ p ≫ π = 1`,
  so `x ≫ r` lies in the kernel of `q` and is central in `H`. Points of `D` are determined by
  their images under `p` and `r`, because `i` is a monomorphism. For every point `y` of `D`
  (after any base change of `x`), the products `x * y` and `y * x` have the same image under
  `p`, since `x ≫ p = 1`, and under `r`, since `x ≫ r` is central; so they are equal. The
  argument for `r` is symmetric, with the roles of `π` and `q` exchanged.

- **`D` is semisimple** (`CentralIsogeny/SemisimpleTransfer`). `D` is smooth over `k` and
  connected, being geometrically connected. It is affine, as it is finite over the affine `C`.
  Its scheme-theoretic centre `Z` is represented by a closed subgroup, and `Z ⟶ D ⟶ C` lands in
  the centre of `C`, because `p` is an epimorphism of schemes. So it factors through the finite
  centre of `C`; as the composite is finite, `Z` is finite over `k`. Its unipotent radical over
  `AlgebraicClosure k` is trivial, because the base change of `p` is an epimorphism with finite
  kernel onto a group with trivial unipotent radical. This is the semisimple case of Milne
  (2017), Lemma 19.14.

The witness `(D, p, r)` therefore satisfies the conclusion over the original field `k`.

## Scope and provenance

The proof composes two imported proved theorems: the conditional assembly
`centralIsogenyCommonSemisimpleRefinement_of_perfect` and the unconditional perfectness theorem
`semisimplePerfect`. Neither theorem, nor any module they import, uses the existence of simply
connected covers, `SimplyConnectedCentralCoverUniversalProperty`, or any supplied cover
factorization, lifting or section law. The result assumes no simply connected source and no
additional structural witness for the refinement.

Mathematically, the classical common refinement is the derived group of the fibre product
`C ×_G H`. Here it is realized as the closed subgroup of `C × H` generated by descended
commutators, where the published generation theorem applies. The route is internal to the
Library; Milne (2017) supplies the perfectness input (Corollary 21.50) and the isogeny transfer
of semisimplicity (Lemma 19.14).
