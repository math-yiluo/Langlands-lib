# A common semisimple central refinement of two central isogenies

Author: Worker 1466. The structural route below incorporates Worker 266's
derived-fibre-product suggestion recorded in the task context by Worker 1362.

`Langlands.GroupScheme.CentralIsogenyCommonSemisimpleRefinement.{u}` is a
`Prop`-valued definition, with no proof. For every field `k : Type u`, actual
group schemes `C`, `H` and `G` over `Spec (CommRingCat.of k)`, and actual
homomorphisms `π : C → G` and `q : H → G`, it asserts:

```text
IsSemisimple C →
IsSemisimple H →
IsSemisimple G →
IsCentralIsogeny π →
IsCentralIsogeny q →
  ∃ (D : GroupScheme (Spec k)) (p : D → C) (r : D → H),
    IsSemisimple D ∧ IsCentralIsogeny p ∧ IsCentralIsogeny r ∧
      p ≫ π = r ≫ q.
```

Here `p ≫ π` means `π ∘ p`. The universe `u` is arbitrary, and the field
may be imperfect or have positive characteristic. The witnesses lie over
the original field in the same universe.

The imported `IsSemisimple` means connected reductivity together with a
finite representative of the full scheme-theoretic centre. Connected
reductivity includes smoothness and affineness and uses the unipotent radical
after algebraic-closure base change. The imported `IsCentralIsogeny` requires
the actual scheme morphism to be finite, flat, surjective and locally of
finite presentation, with its actual scheme-theoretic kernel central on
every test scheme and after every restriction. These definitions retain
nonreduced kernels and centres. They assert no surjectivity on rational
points or on points of each test scheme.

The proposition has precisely the five displayed premises. It assumes no
simple connectedness, chosen cover, universal property, factorization,
section, lifting law, or structural construction of `D`. It asserts neither
uniqueness nor a preferred refinement.

## The full fibre product and a proposed semisimple refinement

Let `P = C ×_G H` be the actual scheme-theoretic fibre product. Its two
projections are finite fppf homomorphisms by base change, and its map
`s : P → G` has central kernel identified with `ker π × ker q`.
These full kernels can be nonreduced. Thus neither smoothness nor
semisimplicity of `P` follows from the two semisimple sources. Even with
`C = H` and `π = q`, the fibre product is isomorphic to `C × ker π`;
a nonsmooth kernel can therefore make the full product nonsmooth.

A structural route is to generate a smaller closed subgroup of `P` by
commutators. Centrality of `ker s` makes the commutator map `P × P → P`
invariant under changing either input by an element of that kernel.
Faithfully flat descent along `P × P → G × G` should therefore give a
map `α : G × G → P`. The subgroup generated by `α` is the proposed `D`.
Since `G × G` is geometrically reduced and geometrically connected and
`α` contains the identity in its image, Milne's generated-subgroup
results give the intended smooth connected group. Comparing the
commutator map with its descent identifies this subgroup with the
scheme-theoretic derived group of `P`.

The maps from `D` to `C` and `H` should be surjective because their closed
images contain the respective commutator-generated subgroups, and `C`
and `H` are perfect. Restriction of the finite projections makes these
maps finite, and their full kernels lie in the central projection
kernels. Together with flatness and local finite presentation this gives
central isogenies. Semisimplicity then follows from Lemma 19.14 and the
finite-centre comparison, with the pullback equation supplying the
required equality of composites. This describes the mathematical route,
not an implemented construction or proof.

## Remaining proof obligations and dependency boundary

A proof must implement the actual pullback and its finite fppf maps;
the full central-kernel comparison; effective descent of the commutator
as a scheme morphism; and closed subgroup generation with the stated
geometric properties over every field. It must establish the
scheme-theoretic derived-subgroup comparison and semisimple perfectness,
prove both projections surjective without assuming surjectivity on
test points, and verify finiteness, flatness, local finite presentation,
all-test centrality, and semisimplicity in the Library's exact predicates.
Every base-change or descent step used to handle an imperfect field
remains a proof obligation.

Existing derived-subgroup representability alone supplies neither
smoothness nor semisimplicity of the refinement. The existing
`DerivedSubgroupSmoothnessStatement` assumes a smooth ambient group and
cannot be applied directly to this possibly nonsmooth `P`.
Likewise, a theorem about derived groups of connected reductive ambient
groups cannot be applied before establishing suitable hypotheses on
the ambient group. Taking a reduced identity component over an
imperfect field without proving it is a smooth subgroup is insufficient.

No simply connected cover existence theorem, supplied-cover universal
law, or equivalent section or lifting law is an allowed dependency for
the proof successor. The intended downstream use is only after this
structural result is proved: an intrinsically simply connected `C`
makes the incoming semisimple central isogeny `p` an isomorphism, so
`inv p ≫ r` gives the desired lift. The present proposition does not
assume or assert that application.
