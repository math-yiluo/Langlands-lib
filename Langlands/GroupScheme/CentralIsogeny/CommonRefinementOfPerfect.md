# Common semisimple refinement when the sources are perfect

This module proves the published proposition `CentralIsogenyCommonSemisimpleRefinement`,
with one extra hypothesis: the two semisimple sources are perfect. Perfectness of every
semisimple group (Milne, *Algebraic Groups* (2017), Corollary 21.50) is not proved here or
anywhere in the Library at this point, so the module does not prove the published target
unconditionally.

All groups are group schemes over `Spec k` for a field `k : Type u`, in an arbitrary universe
`u`. There is no assumption on the characteristic or on perfectness of `k`. `IsSemisimple` is
the Library predicate from `Reductive/Semisimple`, and `IsCentralIsogeny` is the Library
predicate from `CentralIsogeny/Basic`. "Perfect" means `derivedSubgroup K = ⊤` for the
scheme-theoretic closed derived subgroup functor of `Derived/Basic`.

## Main results

- `HasCommonSemisimpleRefinement π q`, for homomorphisms `π : C ⟶ G` and `q : H ⟶ G` of group
  schemes over `Spec k`, is the proposition that there are a group scheme `D` over `Spec k` and
  homomorphisms `p : D ⟶ C` and `r : D ⟶ H` such that `D` is semisimple, `p` and `r` are central
  isogenies, and `p ≫ π = r ≫ q`. It is exactly the conclusion of
  `CentralIsogenyCommonSemisimpleRefinement` for the given data, which is therefore the
  statement that `HasCommonSemisimpleRefinement π q` holds for all central isogenies `π` and `q`
  between semisimple groups. The predicate only names this conclusion; it adds no new notion.

- `CommonRefinement.hasCommonSemisimpleRefinement_of_perfect`: let `π : C ⟶ G` and
  `q : H ⟶ G` be central isogenies between semisimple groups `C`, `H` and `G`, and assume
  `derivedSubgroup C = ⊤` and `derivedSubgroup H = ⊤`. Then `HasCommonSemisimpleRefinement π q`.

- `centralIsogenyCommonSemisimpleRefinement_of_perfect`: suppose that for every field
  `k : Type u` and every semisimple group `G` over `Spec k`, `derivedSubgroup G = ⊤`. Then
  `CentralIsogenyCommonSemisimpleRefinement.{u}` holds. The hypothesis is the statement of Milne
  21.50 in the Library's language; the proof applies
  `hasCommonSemisimpleRefinement_of_perfect` with it at `C` and `H`.

## Proof of `hasCommonSemisimpleRefinement_of_perfect`

1. A central isogeny is finite, flat and surjective, and its full kernel is central. So `π` and
   `q` satisfy the instance hypotheses (quasi-compact, flat, surjective) and the centrality
   hypotheses of `CommonRefinement.exists_closedSubgroup` from `CentralIsogeny/CommonRefinementConstruction`.
   The semisimple groups `C`, `H` and `G` are smooth, affine and connected, through
   `IsSemisimple.isConnectedReductive`.

2. `exists_closedSubgroup`, applied with the perfectness hypotheses, gives a closed subgroup
   inclusion `i : D ⟶ C ⊗ H`, with `D` smooth, affine and geometrically connected, such that
   `(i ≫ fst C H) ≫ π = (i ≫ snd C H) ≫ q` and both projections `i ≫ fst C H` and
   `i ≫ snd C H` have dominant underlying scheme morphisms. `D` is the closed subgroup of
   `C × H` generated by the descended commutators.

3. Worker 1224's `isCentralIsogeny_fst_of_closedSubgroup` and
   `isCentralIsogeny_snd_of_closedSubgroup` (`CentralIsogeny/ProjectionCentralIsogeny`) turn the
   dominance of the projections into central isogenies `p = i ≫ fst C H` and `r = i ≫ snd C H`.
   They need the equation of composites in right-associated form, obtained by reassociating.

4. `D` is connected: it is geometrically connected over a field, and
   `GeometricallyConnected.connectedSpace_of_subsingleton` gives `ConnectedSpace D.X.left`.
   Worker 1224's `IsSemisimple.of_isCentralIsogeny` (`CentralIsogeny/SemisimpleTransfer`), applied
   to the central isogeny `p` onto the semisimple group `C`, shows that the smooth connected
   group `D` is semisimple.

5. The witness is `(D, p, r)`, and `p ≫ π = r ≫ q` is the equation from step 2.

## Scope and provenance

The module is internal assembly for the proof of `CentralIsogenyCommonSemisimpleRefinement`; no
original-literature theorem is formalized beyond what the imported modules already contain.
The common refinement of two central isogenies is classical: the derived group of the fibre
product `C ×_G H` refines both. Here it is realized as the closed subgroup of `C × H` generated
by descended commutators. The only missing input for the unconditional target is perfectness
of semisimple groups, Milne (2017), Corollary 21.50, on printed page 440. It is a hypothesis
of both theorems here, and it is not assumed in any other form. Once that statement is proved
in the Library, the published target is `centralIsogenyCommonSemisimpleRefinement_of_perfect`
applied to it.
