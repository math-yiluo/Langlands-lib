# Regularity and polar defect of the descended odd form

The scalar extension of the descended representative pulls back under
the Hilbert-90 basis to the split quotient-valued quadratic form. The
basis is invertible, so a singular vector in the polar kernel of the
extended form would give such a vector for the split form. The split
form has zero quadratic radical. The ground-field vector embeds into
the extension with its quadratic value and polar row, so the descended
form also has zero quadratic radical.

The polar matrices are congruent after extension. Rank is invariant
under invertible left and right multiplication. Rank is also unchanged
by extending the field, as seen by reducing the ground-field matrix to
diagonal rank normal form and comparing its nonzero diagonal entries.
Rank and nullity then transport the split polar-kernel dimension to the
ground field. The dimension is one when two vanishes and zero otherwise.

This is an internal calculation from the split model and the descended
matrix congruence. It applies over every ground field and retains the
regular odd-dimensional characteristic-two case.
