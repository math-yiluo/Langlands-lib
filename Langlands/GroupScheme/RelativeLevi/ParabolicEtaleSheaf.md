# Étale sheafness and effective descent of actual parabolic families

This internal derived module proves that the actual parabolic presheaf of
a reductive group scheme is a sheaf for the big étale topology on the slice
over the base. Sections remain actual subgroup functors on all test schemes,
with values in `Type (u + 1)`.

`parabolic_isSheafFor_affineEtale` expresses the previously constructed
ordinary affine étale matching descent as the singleton sheaf condition.
`parabolic_isSheaf_etale` then combines this condition with the existing
Zariski sheaf theorem. The implemented proof extends the slice presheaf to
schemes by taking pairs consisting of a structure morphism to the base and
a section over that morphism. The imported slice-extension comparison
transfers the Zariski sheaf condition to this extension.

Étale morphisms are open maps, so their covering families are quasi-compact
covers in the site-theoretic sense. Thus the étale topology equals the
étale quasi-compact topology. The imported general site criterion reduces
the sheaf condition to Zariski sheafness and surjective étale maps of affine
schemes. Representable presheaves satisfy the singleton condition along
these maps because they are effective epimorphisms. The affine parabolic
singleton condition supplies the second component of each pair. The
slice-extension comparison returns the result to the entire slice.

`parabolicEtaleSheaf` is the resulting sheaf with the actual presheaf as its
underlying functor. `parabolic_isSheafFor_etale` applies its sheaf condition
to a singleton generated by any surjective étale parameter morphism.
`existsUnique_restrict_etale` gives unique descent of an ordinarily matching
actual local parabolic, and `matchingEtale` constructs this global family
with an exact restriction recovery law. Source and target need not be
affine, and the covering morphism need not be quasi-compact.

The provenance is internal composition of actual affine parabolic descent,
Zariski sheafness, the scheme site criterion and slice-extension comparison.
No additional external assertion is assumed. The module does not construct
a parameter scheme or prove smooth proper representability.
