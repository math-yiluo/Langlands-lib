# A stable lattice for a bounded subgroup in an actual representation

## Source

Kaletha–Prasad, *Bruhat–Tits Theory: A New Approach* (2023), Proposition
2.2.13: the first paragraph of the proof of part (1) and the last paragraph of
the proof of part (3), printed pp. 74–75 / PDF pp. 105–106. The registered
source is `kaletha-prasad-2023-bruhat-tits-new-approach`, SHA-256
`05ad9a284a2577b34d1a270228b74dfb28f520561ae7772d8f38dddb78938732`. The
definitions it uses are KP Definition 2.2.1 (bounded subsets of `X(k)` for an
affine `k`-scheme `X`), Fact 2.2.4 (morphisms preserve bounded subsets) and
Lemma 2.2.7 (bounded subgroups of `GL_n(k)` and matrix entries), printed
pp. 70–71 / PDF pp. 101–102.

The registered corrections `kaletha-prasad-2024-corrections` (version of
1 November 2024), SHA-256
`23293f51e58d5ceecb91f834743f68058b98431797d2531142415d013fdf61ed`, page 24,
contain no correction to Proposition 2.2.13 itself. They do change the
standing hypothesis of Section 2.2: the last sentence of its first paragraph

## What is declared

All declarations are in the namespace `Langlands.GroupScheme`.

- `MatrixRepresentation.hasBoundedRationalEntries_of_isAlgebraicallyBounded`
  is a **proved theorem**. For a field `K` with a valuative relation, an affine
  group scheme `G` over `Spec K`, a matrix representation
  `ρ : MatrixRepresentation G n` and any set `B` of rational points satisfying
  `IsAlgebraicallyBounded K G.X B`, it gives
  `ρ.HasBoundedRationalEntries B`: for each `i j` there is a nonzero `b : K`
  with `(ρ.rationalMatrix g) i j ≤ᵥ b` for all `g ∈ B`. The bound may depend
  on the entry.
- `MatrixRepresentation.IsStableLatticeFor ρ B Λ` is a **definition**. For a
  subgroup `B : Subgroup (rationalPoints G)` and a submodule `Λ` of `n → K`
  over the canonical valuation integer ring
  `(ValuativeRel.valuation K).integer`, it is the conjunction of
  `Submodule.IsLattice K Λ` and
  `∀ g ∈ B, ∀ v ∈ Λ, (ρ.rationalMatrix g : Matrix n n K) *ᵥ v ∈ Λ`.
- `BoundedSubgroupStableLatticeStatement.{u}` is an **unproved existence
  proposition**, a `Prop`-valued `def`. It quantifies over a field
  `K : Type u` with a valuative relation whose canonical valuation integer
  ring is a discrete valuation ring, an affine group scheme
  `G : GroupScheme (Spec (CommRingCat.of K))` with `IsAffine G.X.left`, a
  natural number `n`, an actual representation
  `ρ : MatrixRepresentation G (Fin n)` and a subgroup
  `B : Subgroup (rationalPoints G)`. Its only additional premise is
  `IsAlgebraicallyBounded K G.X (B : Set (rationalPoints G))`. It asserts that
  there exists a submodule `Λ` of `Fin n → K` over the canonical valuation
  integer ring with `ρ.IsStableLatticeFor B Λ`. Its proof is left to a
  separate successor task.

`IsAlgebraicallyBounded` is the Library's form of KP Definition 2.2.1. Every
global regular function `f` on the actual affine scheme `G.X` has a nonzero
`b : K` with `f(g) ≤ᵥ b` for all `g ∈ B`. This is the multiplicative form of
"`ω(f(g))` is bounded below". `rationalMatrix` is the actual group
homomorphism from rational points of `G` to `GL n K` induced by the given
group-scheme morphism `ρ : G ⟶ GL_n`. No stable lattice, abstract action or
other replacement for `G`, `B` or `ρ` is an input.

`Submodule.IsLattice K Λ` is Mathlib's notion: `Λ` is finitely generated over
the valuation integer ring and its `K`-span is all of `K^n`. Over a discrete
valuation ring this agrees with KP's "lattice", a bounded open `o`-submodule
of `V`. A bounded submodule lies in a finitely generated module and is
finitely generated because the ring is Noetherian. A finitely generated
submodule is open if and only if it spans `V`. By the predicate, each
`ρ.rationalMatrix g` with `g ∈ B` maps `Λ` into `Λ`. Since `B` is a subgroup,
the same holds for `g⁻¹`, so these matrices restrict to automorphisms of `Λ`.
This consequence is not stated separately in Lean.

## Comparison with the source

In the proof of (1) KP fix a faithful representation `G → GL(V)` of the
connected reductive group, a lattice `L ⊂ V` and a bounded subgroup `B` of an
open subgroup of `G(k)`. The image of `B` in `GL(V)` is bounded (Fact 2.2.4),
so its matrix entries are bounded (Lemma 2.2.7). The `o`-submodule `Λ`
generated by all `b · L` with `b ∈ B` is therefore bounded, contains `L` and is
`B`-stable. The last paragraph of the proof of (3) repeats this construction
for a bounded subgroup `B` of `GL(V)`. The present statement isolates exactly
this step. Its specialization to the general linear group scheme and its
identity representation is the construction in (3). The general case is the
construction in the first paragraph of (1) for any representation.
It concludes only that a stable full lattice exists. It does not assert the
other parts of these paragraphs: that `GL(Λ) ∩ G` is a bounded open subgroup,
the Zorn's-lemma argument for maximal bounded subgroups, or maximality
`B = GL(Λ)`.

This is a derived subresult with a deliberately chosen scope.

- **Reductivity** is the standing hypothesis of Section 2.2. The lattice
  construction uses only that `ρ` is a group homomorphism and that `B` has
  bounded matrix entries, so `G` is an arbitrary affine group scheme. It is
  not required to be reductive, connected or of finite type.
- **Faithfulness** is used by KP only to transport conclusions back to `G`:
  boundedness and openness of `GL(Λ) ∩ G` and the description of `B` as a
  stabilizer. The stable lattice itself is built in `V` from `ρ(B)` alone. The
  statement therefore allows any supplied representation and makes no claim
  about `ρ⁻¹(GL(Λ))`.
- **Completeness and local compactness** (finite residue field) are
  hypotheses only of part (2), on pro-`p` subgroups, and are not used in the
  paragraphs above. They must also be absent for the intended consumer, KP
  Proposition 8.3.1. That proposition works over the maximal unramified
  extension, which is in general neither complete nor locally compact.
- **Henselianity** (the corrected standing hypothesis of Section 2.2) and
  **topology** are also not used. The analytic topology enters KP only
  through "open", and openness of `Λ` is replaced by the full-span clause of
  `Submodule.IsLattice`.
- **Discreteness** is an added restriction relative to Section 2.2, which does
  not assume that the valuation is discrete. It makes Mathlib's finitely
  generated lattices agree with KP's bounded open submodules. Over a
  non-discrete valuation ring these differ: for example, the maximal ideal is
  bounded and open but not finitely generated. Outside a few places such as
  §2.2, KP assume throughout the book that the valuation is discrete (printed
  p. 70 / PDF p. 101).
- **Boundedness** is KP's Definition 2.2.1 for `B` in `G` itself, applied to
  all global regular functions on the affine scheme `G.X`. For `G` of finite
  type, with `ω` the additive form of the discrete valuation, this is exactly
  KP's notion. The proved helper replaces KP's appeal to Fact 2.2.4 and
  Lemma 2.2.7 for `ρ(B)`.

## The proved helper

`hasBoundedRationalEntries_of_isAlgebraicallyBounded` pulls back regular
functions along `ρ`. For an entry `(i, j)`, it applies the bound `hB` to the
global regular function
`ρ.hom.hom.left.appTop (GeneralLinear.entrySection n (CommRingCat.of K) i j)`
on `G.X`. This is the pullback under `ρ` of the actual general linear entry
coordinate. The bound gives a nonzero `b` such that every evaluation at
`g ∈ B` satisfies `≤ᵥ b`. Three rewrites identify that evaluation with the
original matrix entry `(ρ.rationalMatrix g) i j`:

- `rationalPointAlgebraHom_apply_appTop` writes the evaluation as the
  global-section pullback along `g`;
- `rationalMatrix_apply_apply` writes the matrix entry as the pullback of the
  entry section along `g ≫ ρ`;
- `Scheme.Hom.comp_appTop` identifies the two pullbacks.

The proof does not use that `B` is a subgroup, that `ρ` is faithful or a
closed immersion, or that the valuation is discrete. It yields one bound per
entry. Combining the finitely many bounds into a common one is part of the
existence proof.

## Intended existence proof (not given here)

The existence target follows the source's algebraic argument. Take the
standard coordinate lattice `O^n` and let `Λ` be the span over `O` of all its
translates `ρ.rationalMatrix g · O^n` with `g ∈ B`. Taking `g = 1` shows that
`Λ` contains `O^n`, so its `K`-span is `K^n`. Because `rationalMatrix` is
multiplicative and `B` is closed under multiplication, `Λ` is preserved by
every `g ∈ B`. By the helper, each entry of each such matrix is bounded. The
valuation is totally ordered, so one nonzero `c` bounds all `n²` entries, and
then `Λ ⊆ c · O^n`. The module `c · O^n` is finitely generated over the
Noetherian ring `O`, so `Λ` is finitely generated. These are proof obligations
of the successor task, not input assumptions.

## Consumer

The consumer is the faithful-representation step of KP Proposition 8.3.1,
printed pp. 295–296 / PDF pp. 326–327. For a faithful representation `ρ`,
that step chooses by Proposition 2.2.13 a lattice `L` with `ρ(K) ⊂ GL(L)`. It
then takes the schematic closure of `ρ(G)` in the general linear model
`GL(L)` and obtains a smooth model by Corollary A.6.5. This module constructs
no integral models, closures or smoothenings and proves no building-model
axioms.
