# Finite group schemes with a trivial fibre

This derived internal construction supplies the finite-group Nakayama
step in finite-subgroup spreading.

The entry construction
`Langlands.GroupScheme.FiniteUnitFibreSpreading.trivialNeighbourhood`
returns a `TrivialNeighbourhood K s`: a specified open neighbourhood,
a proof that it contains the point, and an actual isomorphism from the
restricted group scheme to that open scheme whose morphism is its
structure morphism. It chooses this data from the existence theorem below.
`trivialNeighbourhoodOfExists` is the corresponding constructor from any
proof that a trivial neighbourhood exists.

`Langlands.GroupScheme.FiniteUnitFibreSpreading.exists_isIso_neighbourhood`
states: for any scheme `S`, a group scheme `K` finite over `S`, and a
point `s : S`, if the structure morphism of the actual residue-field
fibre of `K` at `s` is an isomorphism, then the structure morphism of
`K` restricted to some open neighbourhood of `s` is an isomorphism.
No flatness, finite presentation, commutativity, or multiplicative-type
hypothesis is used.

The final theorem
`exists_isIso_neighbourhood_of_finite_neighbourhood` needs only a
supplied finite neighbourhood of `s` and the same trivial-fibre
hypothesis. It applies the finite theorem over that neighbourhood
and maps the smaller open back to the original base.

The algebraic ingredient `exists_principal_certificate` concerns a
finite augmented commutative algebra `B` over a commutative ring `R`.
The augmentation is an algebra map `ε : B →ₐ[R] R`. For an
`R`-field `k`, assume that `k → k ⊗[R] B` is surjective.
Then there is an element `r : R` with unit image in `k` such that, for
every commutative `R`-algebra `A` in which `r` is a unit, the map
`A → A ⊗[R] B` is bijective. The rings `R`, `B`, and `k` share
one arbitrary universe, while the test algebra `A` may have an
independent universe.

The proof uses the idempotent linear projection
`b ↦ b - algebraMap R B (ε b)`. Its image is a finite direct summand
of `B`. The projection vanishes after extension to `k` because that
fibre is generated by the unit. Thus the image module has zero scalar
extension to `k`. The published
`CartierLocusDescent.exists_smul_eq_zero_of_subsingleton` gives one
element `r` that annihilates this image and has unit image in `k`.
After any scalar extension making `r` invertible, every pure tensor
is a scalar multiple of the unit. The augmentation supplies a left
inverse to the scalar map, which proves bijectivity.

Over `Spec R`, the finite group has actual finite coordinate Hopf
algebra `AffineCoordinates.hopfAlgebra K`, with augmentation given
by its counit. The published coordinate-spectrum isomorphism and
Mathlib's `pullbackSpecIso` convert the trivial residue fibre to
bijectivity of its fibre scalar map. The principal certificate then
makes the group trivial over `Spec R[1/r]`. The canonical
`basicOpenIsoSpecAway` identifies this with the open `D(r)`
containing the original point. An affine chart around a point of an
arbitrary base and the standard base-change composition and
residue-field maps give the general theorem.

The transport lemmas in the module explicitly prove compatibility
with repeated base change, arbitrary residue-field extensions,
and passing from an open immersion to its open image.

The provenance is the published coordinate Hopf algebra and point
constructions, the finite-module annihilation theorem just named,
and Mathlib's tensor-product and scheme pullback constructions.
The idempotent-image proof adapts the published
`HopfPointWeightWindow.exists_smul_eq_zero_of_idempotent` argument
to an arbitrary field-valued point using that annihilation theorem.
This module does not prove existence of a finite neighbourhood
from a finite fibre. That separate obligation is needed to apply
the final theorem to SGA 3 IX 6.5.
