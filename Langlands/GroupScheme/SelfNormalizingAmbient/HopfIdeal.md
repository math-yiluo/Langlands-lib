# Ideals of flat groups with domain fibres

This module proves a vanishing criterion for ideals of `S ⊗[R] C`, where `C` is the coordinate
ring of a flat group functor whose fibres over fields are integral domains. Let `L` be such an
ideal. Suppose the points killing `L` are closed under multiplication, and `L` is killed by some
`f` whose value at the unit is `1`. Then `L = 0`. All rings live in one universe `u`.

## Setting

`R` is a commutative ring, `F` is a functor from commutative `R`-algebras to groups, and
`e : (F ⋙ forget GrpCat).CorepresentableBy C` says that the `R`-algebra `C` corepresents the
underlying set functor of `F`. So `e.homEquiv : (C ⟶ T) ≃ F T` for every `R`-algebra `T`. The
unit of `F T` corresponds to the counit `C ⟶ R ⟶ T` (`LinearEmbedding.counit e` and `LinearEmbedding.cls_one` from
`Langlands.GroupScheme.CorepresentedLinearEmbedding`).

## Definitions and basic lemmas

- `tensorPairMap e s x : S ⊗[R] C ⟶ T`, for `R`-algebras `S`, `T`, an algebra map `s : S ⟶ T` and a
  point `x : F T`, is the algebra map equal to `s` on `S` and to the classifying map
  `e.homEquiv.symm x` on `C`. So `tensorPairMap_tmul : tensorPairMap e s x (a ⊗ₜ c) = s a * (e.homEquiv.symm x) c`.
  As a point of the functor `T ↦ (S ⟶ T) × F T`, it is the pair `(s, x)`.
- Naturality of `e`, `e.homEquiv (f ≫ g) = F.map g (e.homEquiv f)`,
  is reused from `LinearEmbedding.pt_comp`, with `pt` unfolded in a local proof binding.
- `tensor_hom_ext`: two algebra maps out of `S ⊗[R] C` agree once they agree on all `a ⊗ₜ 1` and
  on all `1 ⊗ₜ c`.
- `tensorPairMap_comp`: `tensorPairMap e s x ≫ h = tensorPairMap e (s ≫ h) (F.map h x)`.
- `mem_map_annihilator_of_mul_eq_zero`: let `D` be an `R`-algebra, `C'` a flat `R`-algebra,
  `l : D` and `g : D ⊗[R] C'`. If `g * (l ⊗ₜ 1) = 0`, then `g` lies in the ideal generated by the
  image of the annihilator of `l` under `D ⟶ D ⊗[R] C'`. Proof: `x ↦ x * (l ⊗ₜ 1)` is the base
  change to `C'` of multiplication by `l` on `D`. Its kernel is the base change of the
  annihilator of `l`, by exactness of `ker μ ⟶ D ⟶ D` and flatness of `C'`
  (`Module.Flat.rTensor_exact`).
- `mul_eq_zero_of_mem_map`: if `a` kills every element of an ideal `L` of `A` and `g : A ⟶ B` is a
  ring map, then `g a` kills every element of the ideal generated by `g(L)`.

## Main result

**`eq_bot_of_mul_closed`.** Assume

- `C` is flat over `R`;
- `K ⊗[R] C` is an integral domain for every field `K` that is an `R`-algebra (`hdom`);
- `L` is an ideal of `S ⊗[R] C`, for an `R`-algebra `S`, such that for every `R`-algebra `T`,
  every `s : S ⟶ T` and all `x y : F T`: if `tensorPairMap e s x` and `tensorPairMap e s y` both kill `L`,
  then so does `tensorPairMap e s (x * y)` (`hL`);
- `f : S ⊗[R] C` satisfies `tensorPairMap e (𝟙 S) 1 f = 1` (its value at the unit is `1`) and `f`
  kills every element of `L`.

Then `L = ⊥`.

Proof as implemented. Write `D = S ⊗[R] C` and `E = D ⊗[R] C`, with `ι₁ : D ⟶ E` the left
inclusion, `jC : C ⟶ D`, `jE : C ⟶ E` the right inclusions and `sE : S ⟶ E` the inclusion of `S`.
Let `P₁ = e.homEquiv (jC ≫ ι₁)` and `P₂ = e.homEquiv jE` be the two tautological points of
`F E`, and put `Δ = tensorPairMap e sE (P₁ * P₂)`, `φ = tensorPairMap e sE (P₁ * P₂⁻¹)` and
`ι₂ = tensorPairMap e sE P₂`. Then `tensorPairMap e sE P₁ = ι₁`.

1. The shear `σ : E ⟶ E`, equal to `φ` on `D` and to `jE` on `C`, fixes `sE` and `jE`, and
   sends `P₁` to `P₁ * P₂⁻¹` and `P₂` to `P₂`. By `tensorPairMap_comp`, `Δ ≫ σ = ι₁` and `ι₂ ≫ σ = ι₂`.
2. Let `N` be the ideal generated by `ι₁(L)` and `ι₂(L)`, and `π : E ⟶ E ⧸ N`. The maps
   `tensorPairMap e (sE ≫ π) (F.map π P₁)` and `tensorPairMap e (sE ≫ π) (F.map π P₂)` (that is, `ι₁ ≫ π`
   and `ι₂ ≫ π`) kill `L`. By `hL` so does `tensorPairMap e (sE ≫ π) (F.map π (P₁ * P₂)) = Δ ≫ π`.
   Hence `Δ l ∈ N` for every `l ∈ L`.
3. Since `f` kills `L`, `ι₁ f * ι₂ f` kills `N` (`mul_eq_zero_of_mem_map`). So
   `ι₁ f * ι₂ f * Δ l = 0` for `l ∈ L`. Applying `σ` gives `φ f * ι₂ f * (l ⊗ₜ 1) = 0`.
4. Suppose `L ≠ ⊥` and pick `l ∈ L` with `l ≠ 0`. Its annihilator `Ann` is a proper ideal of
   `D`. By step 3 and `mem_map_annihilator_of_mul_eq_zero` (`C` flat), `φ f * ι₂ f` lies in the
   ideal of `E` generated by the image of `Ann`. Choose a maximal ideal `Q ⊇ Ann` and put
   `K = D ⧸ Q`, a field, with quotient map `q : D ⟶ K`. The map `Ψ = q ⊗ id : E ⟶ K ⊗[R] C` kills
   that ideal, so `Ψ (φ f) * Ψ (ι₂ f) = 0`. By `hdom`, `K ⊗[R] C` is a domain, so one factor
   vanishes.
5. Both factors are nonzero, since each specializes to `tensorPairMap e (jS ≫ q) 1 f`, which equals
   `q (tensorPairMap e (𝟙 S) 1 f) = 1` by `tensorPairMap_comp` and the hypothesis on `f`. Here `jS : S ⟶ D` is
   the inclusion.
   - Let `ρ : K ⊗[R] C ⟶ K` be the identity on `K` and `q ∘ jC` on `C`. Then
     `φ ≫ Ψ ≫ ρ = tensorPairMap e (jS ≫ q) 1`, because `Ψ ≫ ρ` identifies the two copies of `C`, so
     `F.map (Ψ ≫ ρ)` sends `P₁ * P₂⁻¹` to `1`.
   - Let `ev : K ⊗[R] C ⟶ K` be the identity on `K` and the counit on `C`. Then
     `ι₂ ≫ Ψ ≫ ev = tensorPairMap e (jS ≫ q) 1`, because `jE ≫ Ψ ≫ ev` is the counit, which classifies `1`.

   This contradicts step 4, so `L = ⊥`.

## Provenance

Internal helper results for the proof of
`Langlands.GroupScheme.SelfNormalizingProperAmbientStatement`. The intended application is the
ideal `L` that cuts out the intersection of a subgroup `H` with its conjugate by the universal
point stabilizing all infinitesimal neighbourhoods of `H`. By the Krull step (module
`Langlands.GroupScheme.SelfNormalizingAmbient.Krull`), `L` is killed by an element congruent to
`1` modulo the augmentation ideal. The vanishing result then shows that the conjugate of `H`
contains `H`. The underlying idea is standard: a subgroup scheme of a flat group with integral
fibres that contains an open neighbourhood of the unit is the whole group. This shear-and-
annihilator argument over an arbitrary base `S` is internal; it avoids base-changing the functor `F`.
