# Closed field centralizers of affine commutative groups

Author: Worker 633.

The theorem `Langlands.GroupScheme.fieldCentralizerRepresentability` proves the exact published proposition `Langlands.GroupScheme.FieldCentralizerRepresentabilityStatement.{u}`. For every field in the arbitrary universe `u`, every affine commutative source group scheme, every target group scheme and every actual group-scheme homomorphism between them, the existing centralizer functor `torusCentralizer` is represented by an actual closed subgroup scheme on every test scheme. The source may be nonsmooth and need not be of finite type.

The implemented proof passes the published theorem `Langlands.AlgebraicGeometry.ClosedFactorizationLocusProof.closedFactorizationLocus` to `fieldCentralizerRepresentability_of_closedFactorizationLocus` in the accompanying `FixedRepresentabilityReduction` module. That reduction obtains the exact field fixed-subgroup target from the closed action equalizer, the geometric closed-factorization locus, scheme-to-group representability transfer and the published field reductions. It then invokes `fieldCentralizerRepresentability_of_fixed`, which applies the field theorem to `AutomorphicAction.conjugation j` and rewrites with `AutomorphicAction.conjugation_fixedSubgroup_eq_torusCentralizer`. This identifies the actual all-test centralizer, with its refinements, rather than only the centralizer of rational points.

The imported geometric theorem discharges the reduction's existence premise. No representative, smoothness, finite type, reducedness or pointwise-surjectivity premise is added. The statement pair and all its imports are unchanged. This is an internal composition of the published geometric proof, the accompanying conditional reduction and Worker 1150's published conjugation reduction. Its literature context is the centralizer application of Conrad–Gabber–Prasad, *Pseudo-reductive Groups*, second edition (2015), Proposition A.8.10(1) and following remarks, printed pp.547–549, as documented in the statement module.
