# Chevalley torus and root maps over a commutative ring

This internal prerequisite extends the existing
`SplitExistence.ChevalleyData` matrix maps to an arbitrary commutative base
ring. Its provenance is the Library's integral Chevalley matrices in
`SplitExistence.Points`, their ring-map naturality in
`SplitExistence.SchemeGroup`, and the represented-functor construction in
`Corepresented`. No additional literature theorem is asserted here.

For supplied `ChevalleyData P b`, `GLR` is the actual general linear group
scheme and `TR` is the diagonalizable group of the entire character
lattice. The natural transformation `torusTransformation` sends a
character to its diagonal matrix of values on the representation weights.
`torusToGLOver` is the induced actual homomorphism of group schemes.
`pointsEquiv_torusToGLOver` computes it on every test scheme, without an
affineness or reducedness assumption.

`torusClassify` is the algebra map from the general linear coordinate
ring to the full group algebra. Matrix diagonal coordinates map to the
weight monomials and inverse diagonal coordinates to their negatives.
The proof of `torusClassify_surjective` uses the full-lattice generation
condition `closure_range_wt`: simultaneous membership of the monomials
of a weight and its negative in the algebra-map range is preserved under
addition and negation. Every monomial therefore lies in that range, and
linear induction covers the entire group algebra. This applies also to
the zero ring. `torusToGLOver_hom_left` identifies the underlying scheme
map as the spectrum of this surjection, proving the closed-subgroup
property over every commutative base ring. Pointwise injectivity also
gives a monomorphism independently.

`GaR`, `gaCoordOver`, `rootPointsHomOver` and `rootToGLOver` give the
actual root homomorphisms from the rank-one additive vector group.
Their naturality follows from ring-map naturality of the integral root
exponentials. `pointsEquiv_rootToGLOver` gives the exponential formula
over every test scheme. Over a field, the two constructed homomorphisms
agree with the existing `torusToGL` and `rootToGL` maps.

These constructions supply ambient matrix maps and a closed torus.
They do not construct the integral subgroup generated by the root maps,
prove its flatness or smoothness, or establish the common actual root
datum of its field fibres.
