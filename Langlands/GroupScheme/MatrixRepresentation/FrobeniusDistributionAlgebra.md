# Bialgebra of the actual Frobenius-origin quotient

This internal derived construction works with an arbitrary field `k : Type u`,
an affine group scheme `G` over `k`, its exponential characteristic `p`, and a
natural height `r`. Write `C` for `CharacterUnits.Coord G` and `Q` for
`FrobeniusCoordinateModel.quotientAlgebra G p r`. The defining ideal of `Q` is
the ideal generated by the `p^r` powers of the actual identity augmentation
coordinates. The characteristic power is a ring endomorphism; no claim that it
fixes the ground field is needed.

The coordinate algebra has the genuine group bialgebra constructed in
`TorusUnipotence.MatrixCoefficients`. Its coproduct is
`CharacterUnits.comul G`, and its counit is `Lie.coordinateOrigin G`.
`quotientIdeal_isCoideal` proves that the defining Frobenius ideal is a
coideal for this bialgebra. The instance `quotientBialgebra` equips the actual
quotient `Q` with the inherited bialgebra structure.

The proof first checks that the identity evaluation kills the ideal. The lemma
`tensor_quotient_pow` says that, in the tensor of two copies of `Q`, the
`p^r` power of the image of any original tensor coordinate is the scalar
image of its identity value's `p^r` power. On pure tensors this is the existing
quotient power identity; it extends to sums because the characteristic power is
a ring homomorphism. The lemma `product_origin_comul` identifies evaluation of
the original coproduct at the two identity points with the original identity
evaluation. It follows that the coproduct followed by both quotient maps kills
every generating Frobenius power. The kernel of this algebra map is an ideal,
so it kills the whole defining ideal. These are precisely the counit and
coproduct conditions required by Mathlib's quotient bialgebra construction.

The formulas `counit_quotientMap` and `comul_quotientMap` record that the
quotient counit and coproduct are reductions of the original ones. In
particular, the coproduct of a quotient coordinate is the original coproduct
followed by the tensor of the two actual quotient maps.

This is a derived algebraic unit with no external citation. Its inputs are the
original group coproduct and counit, the already constructed actual Frobenius
quotient, the proved quotient power identity, and Mathlib's quotient bialgebra
construction. It constructs no antipode, dual convolution action, ordered
coordinates, or Steinberg representation. It assumes no smoothness,
reducedness, perfection or finite-dimensionality. The general Steinberg
big-cell proposition remains a separate obligation.
