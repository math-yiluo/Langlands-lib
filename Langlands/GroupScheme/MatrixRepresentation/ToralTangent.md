# Tangent matrices of linearly reductive groups

Over an algebraically closed field of nonzero characteristic, every actual
tangent matrix of a finite coordinate representation of an affine linearly
reductive group acts semisimply. For a given representation, these matrices
commute pairwise. Choose a finite linear relation among the
iterated prime powers of that matrix. Take scalar roots of the coefficients
and shift the exponents by the first supported index. The resulting sum is
again a tangent matrix, because the differential is linear and each prime
power of a tangent matrix is a tangent matrix. Its corresponding prime power
vanishes by the original relation. The nilpotent tangent obstruction therefore
makes the sum zero. The shifted relation has a nonzero constant derivative,
so it gives a separable annihilating polynomial for the original matrix.

The commutator of two tangent matrices is again a tangent matrix. Take the
commutator of their translation derivations in the reverse order and evaluate
it at the identity. The universal representation matrix converts each
translation derivation into left multiplication by its constant tangent
matrix. The reversal produces the stated order of the matrix commutator.

The differential image is therefore a Lie subalgebra of endomorphisms
consisting of semisimple elements. The adjoint of every element is semisimple
once restricted to this image. If the adjoint of an element `a` has an
eigenvector `z` of eigenvalue `μ`, the adjoint of `z` sends `a` to `-μ z` and
kills `z`. The generalized zero eigenspace of that semisimple adjoint is its
zero eigenspace, so `μ` vanishes. Thus every adjoint on the image is zero.

This is a derived step for the tangent argument of Demazure and Gabriel,
*Groupes algébriques I* (1970), IV §3, Lemma 3.7, printed p. 510. It uses the
published nilpotent tangent obstruction and the actual prime-power operation
on tangent points.
