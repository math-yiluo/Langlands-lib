# Polynomial boxes and Frobenius neighborhoods of the origin

This derived algebraic unit takes a field `k` of exponential characteristic
`p`, a number of coordinates `d`, and a height `r`. It identifies the
polynomial box with coordinate bounds `p^r - 1` with the actual quotient
by the Frobenius image of the origin ideal in `k[X_0, ..., X_(d-1)]`.

For every commutative `k`-algebra `A`, including the zero algebra,
`aeval_pow_eq_origin` proves the following identity. If `t_i^(p^r) = 0`
for every coordinate, the `p^r` power of the value of any polynomial at
`t` is the `p^r` power of its constant coefficient mapped into `A`.
The proof compares two ring homomorphisms on constants and variables,
using the previously constructed `characteristicPowerMap`. Consequently
nilpotence of all the coordinates is equivalent to the vanishing of
these powers for every polynomial with zero constant coefficient.

`originFrobeniusIdeal` is the image of the kernel of the polynomial
constant-coefficient map under that actual characteristic-power ring
endomorphism. This is the ideal generated by the powers of all
polynomials vanishing at the origin. `originFrobeniusIdeal_eq_boxIdeal`
identifies it with `PolynomialBoxQuotient.boxIdeal (bound p d r)`.
One inclusion follows by evaluating in the universal polynomial box:
the power of every origin-vanishing polynomial is zero there. The other
follows because the defining variable powers are Frobenius images of
elements of the origin ideal.

`originQuotientEquiv` is the induced algebra equivalence with the
polynomial box. Its finite coefficient model gives the dimension
`(p^r)^d`. Coordinates outside the support of the bound contribute a
factor one, so this also includes `r = 0`, when the quotient has dimension
one. The zero-coordinate case `d = 0` is included. In exponential
characteristic zero the convention is `p = 1`, and the same formulas hold.

There is no external source assertion or structural existence premise.
This unit does not identify a supplied group law with these polynomial
coordinates, construct root coordinates or a Frobenius-kernel group
scheme, or prove Steinberg irreducibility. It supplies the exact
polynomial origin-ideal comparison needed for such later constructions.
