# Smoothness of the split Cayley automorphism group scheme

Notation as in `Points.md`: `Aut` is the automorphism group scheme of Zorn's split Cayley algebra
over a field `K`. `incl : Aut ⟶ GL₈` is its defining inclusion.

## Dual-number computations

- `map_rootDerFun`, `rootDer_map`: the root derivation matrices `X_α` have integer entries, so
  changing coefficients along a ring homomorphism leaves them unchanged.
- `rootMatrix_eps α`: over the dual numbers `A[ε]`, the root element `x_α(ε)` equals `1 + εX_α`.
  The root matrices are `1 + rX_α + r²X_α⁽²⁾`, and the quadratic term vanishes because `ε² = 0`.
- `epsUnit` is the dual-number unit `1 + ε`, with inverse `1 - ε`.
  `torusGL_epsUnit_one` and `torusGL_one_epsUnit` state that the torus elements with parameters
  `(1 + ε, 1)` and `(1, 1 + ε)` are `1 + εH₁` and `1 + εH₂`, where `H₁ = torusDer 1 0` and
  `H₂ = torusDer 0 1` are the two diagonal torus derivations.

## Lifting Lie elements

Fix an affine group scheme `H` over `K` and a monomorphism `f : H ⟶ Aut`.

- `liftRange f` is the `K`-subspace of `8 × 8` matrices formed by the coordinate matrices
  (`lieMatrix`) of Lie elements of `Aut` that come from Lie elements of `H` through `f`.
- `mem_liftRange_of_point`: if a dual-number point `q` of `H` maps to the matrix `1 + εD` in
  `GL₈(K[ε])`, then `D ∈ liftRange f`. The proof uses `isAutomorphism_one_add_epsilonMatrix_iff`
  to see that `D` is a derivation fixing `1`. It then uses `exists_lieMatrix_eq` to obtain the
  matching Lie element of `Aut`, and `mem_lieKernel_of_comp` to see that `q` lies in the Lie
  kernel of `H`.
- `lift_of_mem_liftRange` (this lemma does not need `f` to be a monomorphism): suppose
  `liftRange f` contains the twelve root derivations `X_α` and the two torus derivations `H₁`
  and `H₂`. Then every Lie element of `Aut` over `K` lifts to `H`. Every derivation matrix is a
  linear combination of these fourteen matrices (`eq_derivCombination`), and `lieMatrix` is
  injective.

## The generating family

- `genGroup K` is indexed by `Root ⊕ Unit`. Each root gets the one-dimensional vector group
  `VectorGroup.groupScheme K 1`, and the extra index gets the published rank-two split torus.
  `genMap K` sends these to `Aut` by `rootMap K α` and `torusMap K` (see `Maps.md`).
- `genGroup_hyp`: every member of the family is smooth, affine and connected over `K`.

## Main theorem

`smooth_automorphismGroup : Smooth (CayleyDatum.split K).automorphismGroup.X.hom`. For every
field `K`, the automorphism group scheme of Zorn's split Cayley algebra over `K` is smooth over
`Spec K`.

Proof as implemented:

1. `GL₈` is smooth (`GeneralLinear.smoothStatement`). `FiniteGeneration.exists_smooth_generated_family`,
   applied to the family `genMap K j ≫ incl`, gives a smooth affine group scheme `H` and a closed
   subgroup `i : H ⟶ GL₈` generated by that family (`IsGeneratedByFamily`).
2. `incl` is a closed subgroup (`Corepresented.isClosedSubgroup_cutHom`), and every generator
   factors through it. By minimality, the range of `i` lies in the range of `incl`. Since `incl`
   represents its range, `i` factors as `f ≫ incl` with `f : H ⟶ Aut`. The map `f` is again a
   closed subgroup (`IsClosedSubgroup.of_comp`), hence a monomorphism.
3. Lie algebra. Each generator `j` factors through `i` as `b_j`.
   - For a root `α`, the dual-number point `ε` of the vector group, composed with `b_α`, maps to
     `x_α(ε) = 1 + εX_α` in `GL₈(K[ε])` (`affinePointsEquiv_rootMap_inclusion`,
     `rootMatrix_eps`).
   - The torus points `(1 + ε, 1)` and `(1, 1 + ε)`, composed with the torus factorization, map
     to `1 + εH₁` and `1 + εH₂` (`affinePointsEquiv_torusMap`, `torusGL_epsUnit_one`,
     `torusGL_one_epsUnit`).

   So `mem_liftRange_of_point` puts all fourteen matrices in `liftRange f`, and
   `lift_of_mem_liftRange` shows that every Lie element of `Aut` lifts to `H`.
4. `smooth_of_fullLie_closed_subgroup` (the published criterion: an affine, locally finite type
   group scheme over a field with a smooth closed subgroup carrying its whole Lie algebra is
   smooth) concludes the proof.

This route is internal to this development. It replaces the usual argument that
`dim Lie(Aut) = 14 = dim Aut`, so that no dimension theory of `Aut` is needed.
