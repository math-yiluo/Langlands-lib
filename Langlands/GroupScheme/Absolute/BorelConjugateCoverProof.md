# Proof that the conjugates of a Borel subgroup cover the group

The module proves

```
theorem Langlands.GroupScheme.borelConjugateCover :
    BorelConjugateCoverStatement.{u}
```

for the published proposition of
[`Absolute.BorelConjugateCoverStatement`](BorelConjugateCoverStatement.md) (Milne,
*Algebraic Groups*, 2017, Theorem 17.33(b), p.362), at an arbitrary universe `u` and with no
further hypotheses. Fix an algebraically closed field `k : Type u`, a group scheme `G` over
`Spec k` whose structure morphism is smooth and affine and whose underlying space is connected,
and a Borel subgroup `i : B ⟶ G` (`IsBorelSubgroup i`). The theorem says that the conjugation
morphism `fst G.X B.X * (snd G.X B.X ≫ i.hom.hom) * (fst G.X B.X)⁻¹ : G.X ⊗ B.X ⟶ G.X`,
`(g, b) ↦ g i(b) g⁻¹`, is surjective on underlying points.

The proof does not follow Milne's route through Theorem 17.33(a). It combines the closed-point
form of the statement, which the Library already gives, with Chevalley's theorem and the
Jacobson property, as sketched in the statement Markdown:

1. every closed point of `G` is a conjugate of a `k`-point of `B` (Library results
   `borelPointContainment`, Milne 17.34, and `borelConjugacy`, Milne 17.9(b));
2. the image of the conjugation morphism is constructible (Chevalley's theorem,
   Mathlib's `Scheme.Hom.isConstructible_image`);
3. in a Jacobson space a constructible set without closed points is empty, so the complement
   of the image is empty.

The module adds two lemmas in the namespace `Langlands.GroupScheme.BorelConjugateCover` and the
target theorem. It imports the statement module, the Library modules
`Absolute.BorelPointContainmentProof` and `Absolute.BorelPointContainmentDensity`, and the Mathlib
modules `Mathlib.AlgebraicGeometry.Morphisms.FinitePresentation` and
`Mathlib.Topology.JacobsonSpace`. No new definitions or axioms are introduced; the target
theorem depends only on `propext`, `Classical.choice` and `Quot.sound`.

## Lemma 1: constructible sets in a Jacobson space

`eq_empty_of_isConstructible_of_inter_closedPoints`: let `X` be a topological space with
`JacobsonSpace X`, and `s ⊆ X` with `IsConstructible s` and `s ∩ closedPoints X = ∅`. Then
`s = ∅`.

This is a general topological fact. The Library's
`KostantRosenlicht.eq_empty_of_isConstructible` proves the special case of the prime spectrum
of a Jacobson ring; the general form is proved here so that it applies directly to the scheme
`G.X.left`.

Proof. In Mathlib, `IsConstructible s` means that `s` lies in the Boolean subalgebra of
`Set X` generated by the open retrocompact sets. These form a sublattice containing `∅` and
`univ` (unions and intersections of open retrocompact sets are open and retrocompact,
`IsRetrocompact.union`, `IsRetrocompact.inter_isOpen`). So the induction principle
`BooleanSubalgebra.closure_sdiff_sup_induction` applies: it suffices to prove the claim for
differences `U \ V` of two open retrocompact sets, and to show that it is preserved by binary
unions.

- *Differences.* `U \ V = U ∩ Vᶜ` is locally closed. If it were nonempty, then since `X` is
  Jacobson it would contain a closed point (`nonempty_inter_closedPoints`), contradicting the
  hypothesis.
- *Unions.* If `(s ∪ t) ∩ closedPoints X = ∅`, then both `s ∩ closedPoints X` and
  `t ∩ closedPoints X` are empty, so `s = t = ∅` by the induction hypotheses and `s ∪ t = ∅`.

## Lemma 2: rational points are conjugates of rational points of `B`

`exists_lift_conjugation_eq`: let `k` be algebraically closed, `G` smooth, affine and connected,
`i : B ⟶ G` a Borel subgroup, and `g` a `k`-point of `G`, i.e.
`g : Over.mk (𝟙 (Spec k)) ⟶ G.X`. Then there are `k`-points `x` of `G` and `b` of `B` with
`lift x b ≫ conjugation i = g`, i.e. `g = x i(b) x⁻¹`. Here `conjugation i` is the Library's
`BorelPointContainment.conjugation i`, which is by definition the morphism of the target
statement.

Proof. By `borelPointContainment` there are a Borel subgroup `i' : B' ⟶ G` and a `k`-point `b'`
of `B'` with `b' ≫ i' = g`. By `borelConjugacy` (second part) applied to `i` and `i'`, there are
a `k`-point `x` of `G` and an isomorphism `φ : B ≅ B'` with `i ≫ int(x) = φ.hom ≫ i'`, where
`int(x) = (G.innerIso x).hom`. Put `b = b' ≫ φ⁻¹`. Composing the identity with `b` and using
`comp_innerIso_hom` and `constantPoint_self` gives `x * (b ≫ i) * x⁻¹ = b ≫ φ.hom ≫ i'`, and the
right side is `b' ≫ i' = g` since `φ⁻¹ ≫ φ.hom = 𝟙`. Finally `lift_conjugation` identifies
`lift x b ≫ conjugation i` with `x * (b ≫ i) * x⁻¹`.

## The theorem

Let `c = (conjugation i).left : (G.X ⊗ B.X).left ⟶ G.X.left`; the goal `Surjective c` is the
target by definition (`change`).

*Finiteness facts.* `B.X.hom` is smooth (`hi.smooth`) and affine
(`hi.isClosedSubgroup.isAffineHom`), and `G.X.left` is an affine scheme
(`isAffine_of_isAffineHom`), hence compact and quasi-separated. The structure morphism
`(G.X ⊗ B.X).hom` is locally of finite type and quasi-compact (after `Over.tensorObj_hom` this is
a composite of a base change of `B.X.hom` and `G.X.hom`), so `(G.X ⊗ B.X).left` is compact
(`QuasiCompact.compactSpace_of_compactSpace`). Since `c ≫ G.X.hom = (G.X ⊗ B.X).hom`
(`Over.w`), `c` is locally of finite type (`locallyOfFiniteType_of_comp`). `G.X.left` is locally
Noetherian (`LocallyOfFiniteType.isLocallyNoetherian`, `Spec k` being Noetherian), so `c` is
locally of finite presentation (Mathlib instance), and `c` is quasi-compact because its source
is compact and its target quasi-separated. Finally `G.X.left` is a Jacobson space
(`LocallyOfFiniteType.jacobsonSpace`).

*Constructibility.* By Chevalley's theorem `Scheme.Hom.isConstructible_image`, applied to `c`
and the constructible set `univ`, the range of `c` is constructible, hence so is its complement.

*No closed point outside the range.* Let `z` be a closed point of `G.X.left`. By
`exists_rationalPoint_of_isClosed` (Library, `Absolute.BorelPointContainmentDensity`; it uses
that `k` is algebraically closed and `G` locally of finite type) there is a `k`-point `g` of `G`
whose underlying point is `z`. Lemma 2 gives `x`, `b` with `lift x b ≫ conjugation i = g`, so by
`comp_left_apply` the image under `c` of the underlying point of `lift x b` is `z`. Hence
`z` lies in the range of `c`.

*Conclusion.* Lemma 1 applied to the complement of the range of `c` shows that the complement
is empty, so the range is all of `G.X.left` (`Set.compl_empty_iff`, `Set.range_eq_univ`), which
is `Surjective c`.
