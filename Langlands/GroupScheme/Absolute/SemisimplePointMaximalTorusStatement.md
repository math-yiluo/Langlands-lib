# Semisimple points lie in maximal tori

`Langlands.GroupScheme.SemisimplePointMaximalTorusStatement.{u}` is a
proposition definition. This module defines the proposition and proves
nothing.

## Statement

Let `k : Type u` be an algebraically closed field, put
`S = Spec (CommRingCat.of k)`, and let `G : GroupScheme S` satisfy
`[Smooth G.X.hom]`, `[IsAffineHom G.X.hom]` and `[ConnectedSpace G.X.left]`.
Let `s : 𝟙_ (Over S) ⟶ G.X` be a point (a `k`-rational point of `G`) such
that, for every `n : ℕ` and every `ρ : MatrixRepresentation G (Fin n)`,

```text
Module.End.IsSemisimple (Matrix.toLin' (ρ.rationalMatrix s : Matrix (Fin n) (Fin n) k))
```

Then there are `T : CommGroupScheme S`, `j : T.toGrp ⟶ G` and a point
`t : 𝟙_ (Over S) ⟶ T.toGrp.X` with `IsMaximalTorus j` and
`t ≫ j.hom.hom = s`.

The ingredients are existing declarations, used without change.

- `MatrixRepresentation G (Fin n)` (from
  [`MatrixRepresentation`](../MatrixRepresentation.md)) is a homomorphism of
  group schemes `G ⟶ GeneralLinear.groupScheme (Fin n) k`, that is, a
  representation of `G` on `kⁿ`. `ρ.rationalMatrix s` (from
  [`MatrixRepresentationRationalPoints`](../MatrixRepresentationRationalPoints.md))
  is the invertible `n × n` matrix with entries in `k` of the rational point
  `s ≫ ρ.hom.hom` of `GL_n`. `Matrix.toLin'` and `Module.End.IsSemisimple`
  are from Mathlib: the latter says that every invariant subspace of `kⁿ`
  has an invariant complement.
- `IsMaximalTorus j` (from [`Torus.Maximal`](../Torus/Maximal.md)) says that
  `T` is a torus (`IsTorus T.toGrp`), that the underlying morphism of schemes
  of `j` is a closed immersion (`IsClosedSubgroup j`), and that `j` does not
  factor through a strictly larger closed torus: whenever `j = f ≫ j'` with
  `j' : T'.toGrp ⟶ G` a closed subgroup and `T'` a torus, `f` is an
  isomorphism.
- `t ≫ j.hom.hom = s` says that the point `s` of `G` is the image of the
  point `t` of `T`, that is, `s` lies in the subgroup `T(k)` of `G(k)`.
- `IsAlgClosed k` and `Smooth` are from Mathlib; `ConnectedSpace G.X.left`
  says that the underlying topological space of `G` is connected.

## Original source

J. S. Milne, *Algebraic Groups: The Theory of Group Schemes of Finite Type
over a Field* (Cambridge University Press, 2017), source ID
`milne-2017-algebraic-groups`, SHA256
`899b0f44f3c3ccfbaf5ed1cf9c180be7a8016b1c0e9709ca0c206304e5bd1cd0`. Page
numbers are printed page numbers. The one-based PDF page is the printed page
plus 16.

- Corollary 17.36, p.363: "Every semisimple element s of G(k) lies in a
  maximal torus."
- Section 17c, p.361: "In this section, G is a connected group variety over
  k algebraically closed." Corollary 17.36 is the last result of Section 17c.
- Chapter 17 opening, p.352: "Recall also that all algebraic groups are
  affine over a base field k".
- p.13: "“group variety” = “smooth algebraic group”."
- Section 17d, p.363: "In this section we define a maximal torus in G to be
  a torus T such that T_{k^a} is maximal in G_{k^a}."
- 9.19, p.171: "Let G be an algebraic group over a perfect field k. An
  element g of G(k) is said to be semisimple (resp. unipotent) if g = g_s
  (resp. g = g_u). Thus, g is semisimple (resp. unipotent) if r(g) is
  semisimple (resp. unipotent) for one faithful representation (V, r) of G,
  in which case r(g) is semisimple (resp. unipotent) for all
  representations r of G."
- Section 9b, p.166: an endomorphism α of a vector space V over k "is
  semisimple if it becomes diagonalizable after an extension of k"; and
  "From linear algebra, we know that α is semisimple if and only if no
  irreducible factor of its minimum polynomial m_α(T) has a multiple root;
  in other words, if and only if the subring k[α] of End_k(V) generated by
  α is étale." Read literally, the middle criterion ("no irreducible factor
  ... has a multiple root") is a slip: see "The semisimple point" below.
  The definition and the étaleness criterion are correct, and the statement
  follows them.
- Proposition 1.41, p.18: "Algebraic subgroups of algebraic groups are
  closed (in the Zariski topology)."

Milne's proof, for orientation only: "Let B be a Borel subgroup of G
containing s, and let T be a maximal torus of G in B. The Zariski closure S
of the subgroup of G(k) generated by s is diagonalizable (because every
representation of S is diagonalizable). Therefore, s is contained in a
conjugate of T (by 16.33d)."

The 2022 corrected reprint (source ID `milne-2022-algebraic-groups`; the
one-based PDF page is the printed page plus 11) states Corollary 17.36 on
printed p.363 with the same words. Its Section 17c convention on p.361
reads "In this section, G is a connected group variety over an

## Derivation from the source

- **The group.** A connected group variety over `k` is a smooth algebraic
  group (p.13), affine by the Chapter 17 convention, with connected
  underlying space: `Smooth G.X.hom` (so `G` is locally of finite type),
  `IsAffineHom G.X.hom` (so quasi-compact, hence of finite type) and
  `ConnectedSpace G.X.left`. The field is algebraically closed throughout
  Section 17c.
- **The semisimple point.** An element of `G(k)` is a section
  `s : 𝟙_ (Over S) ⟶ G.X` (the terminal object of `Over S` is `S` itself).
  By 9.19 (`k` is perfect), `s` is semisimple if and only if `r(s)` is
  semisimple for every representation `(V, r)` of `G`. A representation on
  a finite-dimensional space `V` becomes a `MatrixRepresentation G (Fin n)`
  after choosing a basis, and `r(s)` is then the endomorphism
  `Matrix.toLin' (ρ.rationalMatrix s)` of `kⁿ`. By the definition on
  p.166, an endomorphism is semisimple if it becomes diagonalizable after
  an extension of `k`. As `k` is algebraically closed, this holds exactly
  when the endomorphism is diagonalizable over `k` (its eigenvalues lie in
  `k`, and the dimensions of its eigenspaces do not change under extension
  of the field). That holds exactly when its minimal polynomial is a product
  of distinct linear factors. Since every irreducible polynomial over `k` is
  linear, this means the minimal polynomial is squarefree. For an
  endomorphism of a finite-dimensional vector space over a field, Mathlib
  proves that `Module.End.IsSemisimple` is equivalent to squarefreeness of
  the minimal polynomial (`Module.End.IsSemisimple.minpoly_squarefree` and
  `Module.End.isSemisimple_of_squarefree_aeval_eq_zero`). This also agrees
  with the étaleness criterion on p.166: `k[α] ≅ k[T]/(m_α(T))` is étale
  over the perfect field `k` exactly when `m_α` is squarefree. The middle
  criterion on p.166, read literally, is weaker: the matrix
  `[[1, 1], [0, 1]]` has minimal polynomial `(T − 1)²`, whose only
  irreducible factor `T − 1` has no multiple root, but the matrix is not
  diagonalizable over any field. The intended meaning is that `m_α(T)` has
  no multiple root. The statement does not use the middle criterion.
- **The maximal torus.** A torus in `G` is an algebraic subgroup of `G`
  that is a torus; it is closed (Proposition 1.41). Over the algebraically
  closed field `k`, the Section 17d convention on p.363 says that a maximal
  torus is a torus of `G` not properly contained in another torus of `G`.
  This is `IsMaximalTorus j`: `j : T.toGrp ⟶ G` is a closed subgroup with
  `T` a torus, and whenever `j` factors as `j = f ≫ j'` through a closed
  subgroup `j' : T'.toGrp ⟶ G` with `T'` a torus, the comparison map `f` is
  an isomorphism. Such a factorization says that `T` is contained in `T'`,
  so the condition says that `T` is not properly contained in a larger
  torus of `G`. The torus `T` is required to be commutative
  (`T : CommGroupScheme S`), as every torus is.
- **Lying in the torus.** "`s` lies in a maximal torus `T`" says that `s`
  belongs to the subgroup `T(k)` of `G(k)`, that is, that `s` is the image
  `t ≫ j.hom.hom` of a point `t` of `T`.
- **Universes.** One universe `u` is used for the field and the schemes.
  This is a formalization convention.
