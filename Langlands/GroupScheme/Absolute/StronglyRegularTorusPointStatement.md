# Statement: strongly regular points of a maximal torus over an algebraically closed field

Author: Worker 1531.

This module states one proposition and proves nothing:
`Langlands.GroupScheme.AlgClosedStronglyRegularTorusPointStatement.{u}`, a
`Prop`-valued `def` with one universe parameter `u`. It is the algebraically
closed input of the reduction
`Langlands.GroupScheme.infiniteFieldStronglyRegularPointReduction` (module
`Rational/InfiniteFieldStronglyRegularPointReduction`), which derives
`InfiniteFieldStronglyRegularPointStatement` from it.

## Lean statement

```lean
def AlgClosedStronglyRegularTorusPointStatement : Prop :=
  ∀ (k : Type u) [Field k] [IsAlgClosed k] (G : GroupScheme (Spec (CommRingCat.of k))),
    IsConnectedReductive G →
    ∀ (T : CommGroupScheme (Spec (CommRingCat.of k))) (j : T.toGrp ⟶ G),
      IsMaximalTorus j →
      ∃ U : Set T.X.left, IsOpen U ∧ U.Nonempty ∧
        ∀ t : 𝟙_ (Over (Spec (CommRingCat.of k))) ⟶ T.X, Set.range t.left ⊆ U →
          ∀ {Z : Over (Spec (CommRingCat.of k))} (c : Z ⟶ G.X),
            c * (toUnit Z ≫ t ≫ j.hom.hom) = (toUnit Z ≫ t ≫ j.hom.hom) * c →
              ∃ s : Z ⟶ T.X, s ≫ j.hom.hom = c
```

The module imports `Langlands.GroupScheme.Torus.Maximal` (for `IsMaximalTorus`)
and `Langlands.GroupScheme.Reductive.Basic` (for `IsConnectedReductive`).

## Meaning

- `k` is an algebraically closed field. `G` is a connected reductive group
  scheme over `Spec k` (`IsConnectedReductive`, module `Reductive/Basic`):
  smooth, affine and connected, with trivial unipotent radical over the
  algebraic closure.
- `T` is a commutative group scheme over `k` and `j : T ⟶ G` a homomorphism
  with `IsMaximalTorus j` (module `Torus/Maximal`): `T` is a torus, `j` is a
  closed immersion, and no strictly larger closed torus of `G` contains it.
  Over an algebraically closed field this is the usual maximal torus.
- `U` is a nonempty open subset of the underlying topological space of the
  scheme `T`.
- `t : 𝟙_ ⟶ T.X` is a `k`-point of `T`, a morphism from the terminal object
  `Spec k` of `Over (Spec k)`. The condition `Set.range t.left ⊆ U` says that
  the point of `T` it defines lies in `U`. On a test `Z` the point `t ≫ j` of
  `G` is pulled back as `toUnit Z ≫ t ≫ j.hom.hom`.
- The last clause quantifies over every test `Z` over `k`, including nonreduced
  ones. Every `Z`-point `c` of `G` that commutes with the pulled-back point
  `t ≫ j` factors through `j`. So the scheme-theoretic centralizer of `t ≫ j`
  is contained in `T`. Since `T` is commutative, it is exactly `T`.

So the proposition says that the strongly regular points of `T`, those whose
centralizer is `T`, include all `k`-points of some nonempty open subset of `T`.

## Sources and provenance

Provenance: derived.

- R. Steinberg, *Regular elements of semi-simple algebraic groups* (1965),
  source `steinberg-1965-regular-elements-semisimple-groups`, SHA-256
  `bda20c4a4b3cf4a7e9e5b17a4eacd0147d0c9370063ab3a3778114a53c8349b9`.
  - §1 (printed p.49, PDF p.2): the field `K` is algebraically closed, and each
    algebraic group is identified with its group of `K`-points.
  - §2 (printed p.52, PDF p.5): `G` is a semisimple group, `T` a maximal torus,
    `Σ` the roots relative to `T` and `W` the Weyl group.
  - Before 2.15 (printed p.54, PDF p.7): "We will call an element of `G`
    strongly regular if its centralizer is a maximal torus."
  - Proof of 2.15 (printed p.54, PDF p.7): "The strongly regular elements form
    a dense open set in `T`, characterized by `α(t) ≠ 1` for all roots `α`, and
    `wt ≠ t` for all `w ≠ 1` in `W`."
- B. Conrad, O. Gabber and G. Prasad, *Pseudo-reductive groups*, second
  edition (2015), source `conrad-gabber-prasad-2015-pseudo-reductive`, SHA-256
  `1b77af44c90211db9a3efa50896a3722f4fd2df332b7ce2ec52202eb243e7f54`,
  Proposition A.8.10(2) (printed pp.547–548, PDF pp.573–574): if a group with
  linearly reductive geometric fibres, for example one of multiplicative type,
  acts on a smooth scheme `Y`, then the fixed-point scheme `Y^H` is smooth.

Derived content. Steinberg works with semisimple groups and with classical
centralizers, which are groups of `K`-points. Two steps lead to the Lean
statement.

1. Connected reductive groups. The characterization in the proof of 2.15
   rests on 2.8 (printed p.53, PDF p.6): for `t` in `T`, the centralizer
   `G_t` is generated by `T`, the root groups `X_α` with `α(t) = 1` and
   representatives of the `w` in `W` with `wt = t`. This holds unchanged for a
   connected reductive group with its roots and Weyl group relative to `T`. The conditions `α(t) ≠ 1` and `wt ≠ t` cut out
   a nonempty open subset of `T`, and `U` is taken to be it. If `t` lies in
   it, its classical centralizer is `T`. The Lean statement claims only that
   such a `U` exists. It does not claim the characterization, or that `U` is
   the whole strongly regular locus.
2. Scheme-theoretic centralizers. Let `D` be the Zariski closure of the
   subgroup generated by `t` in `T`. It is a closed subgroup of a torus, so of
   multiplicative type. Since `k` is a field, every `k`-algebra `A` is free as
   a `k`-module. A function on `T_A` therefore vanishes on all powers of `t`
   exactly when it lies in the ideal of `D_A`. Working on affine opens of the
   test, a point of `G` on any test commutes with `t` exactly when it commutes
   with `D`. So the
   scheme-theoretic centralizer of `t` is the fixed-point scheme of `D`
   acting on `G` by conjugation. By CGP A.8.10(2) it is smooth. Over the
   algebraically closed `k` a smooth subgroup scheme is determined by its
   `k`-points, which form the classical centralizer `T(k)`. So the
   scheme-theoretic centralizer of `t` is `T`, which is the last clause of the
   Lean statement.

## What is not stated

- The characterization of strongly regular points by roots and the Weyl group
  is not stated, nor is density of strongly regular points in `G`
  (Steinberg 2.15 itself).
- Nothing is proved here; the existence is the obligation of a separate proof.
