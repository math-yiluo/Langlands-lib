# Centralizers of semisimple points in connected solvable groups

`Langlands.GroupScheme.SolvableSemisimpleCentralizerStatement.{u}` is a
proposition definition. This module defines the proposition and proves
nothing.

## Statement

Let `k : Type u` be an algebraically closed field, put
`S = Spec (CommRingCat.of k)`, and let `G : GroupScheme S` satisfy
`[Smooth G.X.hom]`, `[IsAffineHom G.X.hom]`, `[ConnectedSpace G.X.left]`
and `IsSolvableGroupScheme G`. Let `s : 𝟙_ (Over S) ⟶ G.X` be a point
(a `k`-rational point of `G`) such that, for every `n : ℕ` and every
`ρ : MatrixRepresentation G (Fin n)`,

```text
Module.End.IsSemisimple (Matrix.toLin' (ρ.rationalMatrix s : Matrix (Fin n) (Fin n) k))
```

Then for every `C : GroupScheme S` and `c : C ⟶ G` with
`(G.pointCentralizer s).IsRepresentedBy c`:

1. `ConnectedSpace C.X.left`; and
2. there are `U : GroupScheme S` and `u : U ⟶ G` with `IsClosedSubgroup u`,
   `IsUnipotent U` and `Surjective (subgroupMultiplication u c).left`.

The ingredients are existing declarations, used without change.

- `IsSolvableGroupScheme G` (from [`Absolute.Borel`](Borel.md)) says that
  the abstract group of `k̄`-points of the base change of `G` to
  `k̄ = AlgebraicClosure k` is solvable.
- `MatrixRepresentation G (Fin n)` (from
  [`MatrixRepresentation`](../MatrixRepresentation.md)) is a homomorphism of
  group schemes `G ⟶ GeneralLinear.groupScheme (Fin n) k`, that is, a
  representation of `G` on `kⁿ`. `ρ.rationalMatrix s` (from
  [`MatrixRepresentationRationalPoints`](../MatrixRepresentationRationalPoints.md))
  is the invertible `n × n` matrix with entries in `k` of the rational point
  `s ≫ ρ.hom.hom` of `GL_n`. `Matrix.toLin'` and `Module.End.IsSemisimple`
  are from Mathlib: the latter says that every invariant subspace of `kⁿ`
  has an invariant complement.
- `G.pointCentralizer s` (from [`PointCentralizer`](../PointCentralizer.md))
  is the subgroup functor of points `g` of `G` over a test scheme `T` that
  commute with the pullback of `s` to `T`. The same module proves
  `pointCentralizer_isClosedRepresentable`: when `G.X.hom` is separated (for
  example affine), this functor is represented by a closed subgroup, so the
  universally quantified `C`, `c` exist.
- `SubgroupFunctor.IsRepresentedBy` (from
  [`SubgroupFunctor`](../SubgroupFunctor.md)) requires a monomorphism whose
  range equals the given subgroup functor on every test scheme.
- `IsClosedSubgroup u` and `IsUnipotent U` (from
  [`Reductive.Basic`](../Reductive/Basic.md)): the underlying morphism of
  schemes of `u` is a closed immersion, and every nonzero finite-dimensional
  representation of `U` has a nonzero fixed vector.
- `subgroupMultiplication u c` (from
  [`Rational.LeviSubgroup`](../Rational/LeviSubgroup.md)) is the morphism
  `U.X ⊗ C.X ⟶ G.X` over `S` sending `(x, y)` to `u(x) · c(y)`.
  `Surjective` (Mathlib) is surjectivity of the underlying morphism of
  schemes.
- `IsAlgClosed k` and `Smooth` are from Mathlib; `ConnectedSpace G.X.left`
  says that the underlying topological space of `G` is connected.

## Original source

J. S. Milne, *Algebraic Groups: The Theory of Group Schemes of Finite Type
over a Field* (Cambridge University Press, 2017), source ID
`milne-2017-algebraic-groups`, SHA256
`899b0f44f3c3ccfbaf5ed1cf9c180be7a8016b1c0e9709ca0c206304e5bd1cd0`. Page
numbers are printed page numbers. The one-based PDF page is the printed page
plus 16.

- Theorem 16.40, p.338: "Let G be a connected solvable group variety, and
  let s be a semisimple element of G(k). Then C_G(s) is connected and
  G = U·C_G(s) with U a unipotent subgroup of G."
- Section 16e, p.338: "Throughout, G is a connected group variety and k is
  algebraically closed."
- 9.19, p.171: "Let G be an algebraic group over a perfect field k. An
  element g of G(k) is said to be semisimple (resp. unipotent) if g = g_s
  (resp. g = g_u). Thus, g is semisimple (resp. unipotent) if r(g) is
  semisimple (resp. unipotent) for one faithful representation (V, r) of G,
  in which case r(g) is semisimple (resp. unipotent) for all
  representations r of G."
- Section 9b, p.166: an endomorphism α of a vector space V over k "is
  semisimple if it becomes diagonalizable after an extension of k"; and
  "From linear algebra, we know that α is semisimple if and only if no
  irreducible factor of its minimum polynomial m_α(T) has a multiple root;
  in other words, if and only if the subring k[α] of End_k(V) generated by
  α is étale." Read literally, the middle criterion ("no irreducible factor
  ... has a multiple root") is a slip: see "The semisimple point" below.
  The definition and the étaleness criterion are correct, and the statement
  follows them.
- Definition 1.3, p.7: "An algebraic subgroup of an algebraic group
  (G, m_G) over k is an algebraic group (H, m_H) over k such that H is a
  k-subscheme of G and the inclusion map is a homomorphism of algebraic
  groups." Proposition 1.41, p.18: "Algebraic subgroups of algebraic groups
  are closed (in the Zariski topology)."
- p.13: "“group variety” = “smooth algebraic group”."
- 6.45, p.135: "An algebraic group G is said to be unipotent if every
  nonzero representation of G has a nonzero fixed vector."
- Definition 6.26, p.131: "An algebraic group G is solvable if there is a
  subnormal series G = G_0 ⊃ G_1 ⊃ ⋯ ⊃ G_t = e such that each quotient
  G_i/G_{i+1} is commutative." Corollary 6.31, p.132: "Assume that G is
  affine or smooth, and let k′ be a field containing k. Then G is solvable
  if and only if G_{k′} is solvable."
- Proposition 16.34, p.336, over an algebraically closed field: "(c) G is
  smooth and connected, and the abstract group G(k) is solvable; (d) G is
  smooth, connected, and solvable" are equivalent.
- Chapter 16 opening, p.324: "Recall that all algebraic groups are affine
  over a base field k."

Milne's proof, for orientation only, is by induction on `dim G`. If `G` is
commutative, then `C_G(s) = G` and one takes `U = e`. Otherwise let `N` be
the last nontrivial term of the derived series; it is unipotent, connected,
normal and commutative. Lemma 16.39 and the uniqueness of Jordan
decompositions show that `C_G(s)(k) → C_Ḡ(s̄)(k)` is surjective, where
`Ḡ = G/N`; as `C_Ḡ(s̄)` is smooth, `e → C_N(s) → C_G(s) → C_Ḡ(s̄) → e` is
exact; `C_N(s)` is connected by Lemma 16.39 and `C_Ḡ(s̄)` by induction, so
`C_G(s)` is connected. If `Ḡ = U·C_Ḡ(s̄)`, then `G = Ũ·C_G(s)` for the
inverse image `Ũ` of `U`, which is unipotent as an extension of `U` by `N`.

The 2022 corrected reprint (source ID `milne-2022-algebraic-groups`; the
one-based PDF page is the printed page plus 11) states Theorem 16.40 on
printed p.339 and the Section 16e convention on p.338, with the same words.
Its p.166 contains both quoted Section 9b passages with the same words,
including the middle criterion discussed above.

The official errata snapshots `milne-2017-algebraic-groups-errata-2026-11-02`
(2017 edition) and `milne-2022-algebraic-groups-errata-2026-11-04` (2022
reprint) were checked. The 2017 errata have no entry for pp.7, 13, 131,

## Derivation from the source

- **The group.** A connected group variety over `k` is a smooth algebraic
  group (p.13), affine by the Chapter 16 convention, with connected
  underlying space: `Smooth G.X.hom` (so `G` is locally of finite type),
  `IsAffineHom G.X.hom` (so quasi-compact, hence of finite type) and
  `ConnectedSpace G.X.left`. The field is algebraically closed throughout
  Section 16e.
- **Solvability.** By Corollary 6.31, `G` is solvable if and only if
  `G_{k̄}` is. `G_{k̄}` is smooth and connected (a connected algebraic group
  is geometrically connected, since it has a rational point), so by
  Proposition 16.34 (c)⇔(d) it is solvable if and only if the abstract group
  `G(k̄)` is solvable. This is `IsSolvableGroupScheme G`.
- **The semisimple point.** An element of `G(k)` is a section
  `s : 𝟙_ (Over S) ⟶ G.X` (the terminal object of `Over S` is `S` itself).
  By 9.19 (`k` is perfect), `s` is semisimple if and only if `r(s)` is
  semisimple for every representation `(V, r)` of `G`. A representation on
  a finite-dimensional space `V` becomes a `MatrixRepresentation G (Fin n)`
  after choosing a basis, and `r(s)` is then the endomorphism
  `Matrix.toLin' (ρ.rationalMatrix s)` of `kⁿ`. By the definition on
  p.166, an endomorphism is semisimple if it becomes diagonalizable after
  an extension of `k`. As `k` is algebraically closed, this holds exactly
  when the endomorphism is diagonalizable over `k` (its eigenvalues lie in
  `k`, and the dimensions of its eigenspaces do not change under extension
  of the field). That holds exactly when its minimal polynomial is a product
  of distinct linear factors. Since every irreducible polynomial over `k` is
  linear, this means the minimal polynomial is squarefree. For an
  endomorphism of a finite-dimensional vector space over a field, Mathlib
  proves that `Module.End.IsSemisimple` is equivalent to squarefreeness of
  the minimal polynomial (`Module.End.IsSemisimple.minpoly_squarefree` and
  `Module.End.isSemisimple_of_squarefree_aeval_eq_zero`). This also agrees
  with the étaleness criterion on p.166: `k[α] ≅ k[T]/(m_α(T))` is étale
  over the perfect field `k` exactly when `m_α` is squarefree. The middle
  criterion on p.166, read literally, is weaker: the matrix
  `[[1, 1], [0, 1]]` has minimal polynomial `(T − 1)²`, whose only
  irreducible factor `T − 1` has no multiple root, but the matrix is not
  diagonalizable over any field. The intended meaning is that `m_α(T)` has
  no multiple root. The statement does not use the middle criterion.
- **The centralizer.** `C_G(s)` is the scheme-theoretic centralizer, whose
  points over a `k`-algebra `R` are the elements of `G(R)` commuting with
  `s`. On all test schemes this is `G.pointCentralizer s`. Milne uses the
  actual subgroup; the statement allows an arbitrary representative `c`. A
  monomorphism whose range is the given functor on every test scheme is
  determined up to unique isomorphism over `G` (Yoneda), so the conclusions
  do not depend on the choice.
- **Connectedness.** "`C_G(s)` is connected" is `ConnectedSpace C.X.left`.
- **The product.** "`U` a unipotent subgroup of `G`" is an algebraic
  subgroup (Definition 1.3), hence closed (Proposition 1.41), which is
  unipotent (6.45): `IsClosedSubgroup u` and `IsUnipotent U`. No smoothness
  or connectedness of `U` is asserted, as in the source. "`G = U·C_G(s)`"
  says that every element of `G(k)` is a product `xy` with `x ∈ U(k)` and
  `y ∈ C_G(s)(k)`, that is, the multiplication morphism `U × C_G(s) → G`
  is surjective on `k`-points. As `k` is algebraically closed and all three
  schemes are of finite type over `k`, this is equivalent to surjectivity of
  the morphism of schemes (closed points are `k`-points, nonempty fibres of
  finite type over `k` have `k`-points, and a constructible set containing
  all closed points of a scheme of finite type over `k` is the whole space).
  This is `Surjective (subgroupMultiplication u c).left`.
- **Universes.** One universe `u` is used for the field and the schemes.
  This is a formalization convention.
