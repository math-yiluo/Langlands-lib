# Commutative closed subgroups containing rational points

For every field `k : Type u`, every group scheme `G` over `Spec k` whose
underlying scheme is affine, and every actual rational point `x` of `G`,
`RationalPointCommutativeSubgroupStatement.{u}` asserts the existence of a
commutative group scheme `C`, a closed subgroup inclusion `z : C → G`, and an
actual rational point `y` of `C` with `y ≫ z = x`.
No finite type, smoothness, connectedness, perfectness, algebraic closedness or
characteristic assumption is imposed. The theorem
`rationalPointCommutativeSubgroup` proves this proposition.

This is an internal deduction from the quotient-coordinate-algebra construction
in `Absolute.RationalPointClosureProof`. Choose an affine coordinate algebra `B`
for `G` and let `Γ` be the subgroup generated by the integer powers of `x`.
The quotient of `B` by the ideal of functions vanishing on `Γ` defines an actual
closed subgroup. Its rational points in `Γ` separate its coordinate algebra.

More generally, if a rational subgroup `Γ` is abelian, the two universal points
over the tensor square of this quotient algebra commute. Products of pairs of
rational evaluations separate that tensor square, by the existing tensor
separation theorem. At each such pair the two products agree because `Γ` is
abelian. Every pair of points of the closure over any commutative test algebra
is a specialization of the two universal points, so these points commute even
for nonreduced test algebras. The all-test corepresented point equivalence then
gives commutativity of the actual closed subgroup on every test scheme.

Apply this result to `Γ = zpowers x`, which is abelian. Evaluation at `x` kills
the defining ideal and therefore factors through the quotient algebra. Taking
its spectrum gives `y`. The quotient-map composition identity, followed by the
coordinate-model isomorphism, verifies that its image is exactly `x` as a
morphism of schemes over `Spec k`. The quotient map is surjective, so its spectrum
and hence the inclusion into `G` is a closed immersion.

This supporting result does not assert unipotence and does not establish the
general `NoTorusUnipotentStatement`.
