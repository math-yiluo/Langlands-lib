# Rank-one generation of split-torus centralizers in simply connected groups: proof

Lean module: `Langlands.GroupScheme.SimplyConnected.RankOneCentralizerGenerationProof`,
namespace `Langlands.GroupScheme`.

## Statement

```
theorem Langlands.GroupScheme.simplyConnectedRankOneCentralizerGeneration :
    SimplyConnectedRankOneCentralizerGenerationStatement.{u}
```

The type is exactly the published proposition
`Langlands.GroupScheme.SimplyConnectedRankOneCentralizerGenerationStatement.{u}`
of the module
`Langlands.GroupScheme.SimplyConnected.RankOneCentralizerGenerationStatement`,
at an arbitrary universe `u`, with no further hypothesis. Unfolded, it reads:

```
∀ (F : Type u) [Field F] [ValuativeRel F] [TopologicalSpace F]
  [IsNonarchimedeanLocalField F]
  (G : GroupScheme (Spec (CommRingCat.of F))) [IsAffineHom G.X.hom], IsSimplyConnected G →
  ∀ (S : CommGroupScheme (Spec (CommRingCat.of F))) (i : S.toGrp ⟶ G),
    IsMaximalSplitTorus i →
  ∀ (Z : GroupScheme (Spec (CommRingCat.of F))) (j : Z ⟶ G),
    (torusCentralizer i).IsRepresentedBy j →
    (rationalPoints.map j).range ≤
      Subgroup.closure (⋃ a ∈ relativeRoots i,
          ((centralizerPoints i ⊓ Subgroup.closure
            (((RelativeRoot.subgroup i a).carrier (Over.mk (𝟙 _)) :
                Set (rationalPoints G)) ∪
              (RelativeRoot.subgroup i (-a)).carrier (Over.mk (𝟙 _))) :
            Subgroup (rationalPoints G)) : Set (rationalPoints G))) ⊔
        (Plancherel.rationalCommonNormKernel Z).map (rationalPoints.map j)
```

In words: let `G` be a simply connected group over a nonarchimedean local
field `F`, of any characteristic, with a maximal split torus `S` and the
represented centralizer `Z = Z_G(S)`. Every rational point of `Z` lies in the
subgroup generated by the groups `Z(F) ∩ ⟨U_a(F), U_{-a}(F)⟩`, for the
relative roots `a`, and the norm-one subgroup `Z(F)^1` of the points `z` with
`|χ(z)| = 1` for every rational character `χ` of `Z`.

## Proof

The Lean proof is the single application

```
simplyConnectedRankOneCentralizerGeneration_of_kneserTits rootGeneratedCentralizerRankOne
  simplyConnectedKneserTitsCentralizer
```

of the theorem `simplyConnectedRankOneCentralizerGeneration_of_kneserTits`
(module `SimplyConnected.RankOneCentralizerGenerationReduction`), whose type
`SimplyConnectedRankOneCentralizerGenerationReductionStatement.{u}` is the
implication from two statements to the target. Both are discharged by
theorems proved in the Library without hypotheses:

1. `rootGeneratedCentralizerRankOne` (module
   `Rational.RootGeneratedCentralizerRankOneProof`): over any field, for a
   connected reductive group with maximal split torus `S`, every rational point
   of `Z_G(S)` in the subgroup generated by the root groups is a product of
   centralizing elements of the rank-one groups `⟨U_a(k), U_{-a}(k)⟩`.
2. `simplyConnectedKneserTitsCentralizer` (module
   `SimplyConnected.KneserTitsCentralizerProof`): over a nonarchimedean local
   field, `Z(F)` lies in the subgroup generated by `Z(F) ∩ G(F)^+` and
   `Z(F)^1`.

The reduction bounds `Z(F)` by the second statement and then the first
summand by the first statement. The statement is not modified and no
hypothesis is added.

## Literature

V. Platonov and A. Rapinchuk, *Algebraic Groups and Number Theory* (1994),
Chapter 7, §7.2: the definition of `G_K^+`, printed p. 406; Theorem 7.4
(Prasad–Raghunathan), printed p. 411, generation of the rational points of the
centralizer of a maximal split torus by those of its intersections with the
rank-one components, in `K`-rank at least 2; and Theorem 7.6, printed p. 414,
the Kneser–Tits conjecture over non-Archimedean locally compact fields. The
book's local fields have characteristic zero (printed p. 5); the Lean statement
has no restriction on the characteristic.

T. Kaletha and G. Prasad, *Bruhat–Tits Theory: A New Approach* (2023),
Chapter 2, §2.2, after Proposition 2.2.14, printed p. 76: `G(k)^+ = G(k)` for a
`k`-isotropic simply connected `G` over a non-discrete locally compact field,
citing G. Prasad and M. S. Raghunathan, *On the Kneser–Tits problem*, Comment.
Math. Helv. 60 (1985), 107–121 (page reference to be added).
