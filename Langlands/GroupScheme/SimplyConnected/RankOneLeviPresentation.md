# Rank-one Levi presentations and the reduction to split rank one

Authors: Worker 1757.

This module states, as the proposition definition
`Langlands.GroupScheme.RankOneLeviPresentationStatement.{u}` with arbitrary
universe `u`, the structure of the centralizer of a maximal split torus that
Prasad and Raghunathan use to prove their Theorem A. It holds over every
field. The module also proves one theorem,
`Langlands.GroupScheme.localKneserTitsRankOneReduction_of_rankOneLeviPresentation`.
It shows that this statement and
`Langlands.GroupScheme.LocalSimplyConnectedCocycleVanishingStatement.{u}`
together imply `Langlands.GroupScheme.LocalKneserTitsRankOneReductionStatement.{u}`.
The module proves neither the structure statement nor the cocycle vanishing.

## Exact statement

Let `k : Type u` be any field (`[Field k]`). Let
`G : GroupScheme (Spec (CommRingCat.of k))` have `[IsAffineHom G.X.hom]`,
`IsSimplyConnected G`, `IsAlmostSimple G` and `¬ IsAnisotropic G`. Then there
exist the following data.

- A cocharacter `χ : (Gm (Spec (CommRingCat.of k))).toGrp ⟶ G`.
- Group schemes `M`, `D`, `Q` over `Spec (CommRingCat.of k)` and
  homomorphisms `j : M ⟶ G`, `d : D ⟶ M` and `q : M ⟶ Q`.
- An index type `J : Type u` and families
  `Mi Di Qi A : J → GroupScheme (Spec (CommRingCat.of k))`.
- For every `i : J`, homomorphisms `m i : Mi i ⟶ M`, `di i : Di i ⟶ Mi i`,
  `qi i : Mi i ⟶ Qi i`, `φ i : Qi i ⟶ Q`, `a i : A i ⟶ G` and
  `e i : Mi i ⟶ A i`.

These data satisfy the following seventeen clauses.

1. `(Dynamic.centralizer χ).IsRepresentedBy j`.
2. `SubgroupFunctor.range d = SubgroupFunctor.range (kernelι q)`.
3. `(rationalPoints.map d).range ≤ Subgroup.closure (⋃ i, (rationalPoints.map (m i)).range)`.
4. `(rationalPoints.map q).range ≤ Subgroup.closure (⋃ i, (rationalPoints.map (φ i)).range)`.
5. `m i ≫ q = qi i ≫ φ i` for every `i`.
6. `SubgroupFunctor.range (di i) = SubgroupFunctor.range (kernelι (qi i))` for every `i`.
7. `Mono (di i).hom.hom` for every `i`.
8. `Smooth (qi i).hom.hom.left` for every `i`.
9. `Surjective (qi i).hom.hom.left` for every `i`.
10. `IsAffineHom (Di i).X.hom` for every `i`.
11. `IsSimplyConnected (Di i)` for every `i`.
12. `e i ≫ a i = m i ≫ j` for every `i`.
13. `IsAffineHom (A i).X.hom` for every `i`.
14. `IsSimplyConnected (A i)` for every `i`.
15. `IsAlmostSimple (A i)` for every `i`.
16. `splitRank (A i) = 1` for every `i`.
17. For every `i`, all group schemes `P` and `U` over `k`, and all maps
    `p : P ⟶ A i` and `r : U ⟶ P`:

    ```
    IsParabolicSubgroup p →
    LeviSubgroup.IsGeometricUnipotentRadical r →
    (rationalPoints.map (r ≫ p ≫ a i)).range ≤ parabolicElementarySubgroup G
    ```

The closures in clauses 3 and 4 are taken in `rationalPoints M` and in
`rationalPoints Q`.

These are all the quantified data and clauses. The field `k` is arbitrary:
any characteristic, not necessarily perfect, finite or infinite. The
statement does not say that `j`, `m i`, `e i` or `a i` are closed
immersions. It does not say that `Q` or `Qi i` is commutative or a torus, or
that `M` is reductive. It does not say that `J` is finite or nonempty, or that
`Di i` is the derived group of `Mi i`. Clause 7 makes each `di i` a
monomorphism, but no clause makes `d` one. The intended witnesses below have
all of these extra properties, but the statement asks only for the seventeen
clauses.

The names mean the following.

- `Dynamic.centralizer χ` is the subgroup functor of `G` of points that
  commute with the image of `χ`, that is, the centralizer `Z_G(χ)`.
  `F.IsRepresentedBy j` means that `j` is a monomorphism of schemes over the
  base and its range is exactly `F`.
- `kernelι q : kernel q ⟶ M` is the kernel inclusion, the pullback of the
  unit section of `Q` along `q`. `SubgroupFunctor.range` is the subgroup
  functor of scheme-valued points in the image. Clauses 2 and 6 thus say that
  `d` and `di i` have the same points as the kernels, on every test scheme.
- `rationalPoints H` is the group of `k`-points, and `rationalPoints.map f` is
  the induced group homomorphism.
- `IsSimplyConnected` (semisimple, and every central isogeny from a semisimple
  group onto it is an isomorphism), `IsAlmostSimple` (simplicity over `k`, the
  source's "`k`-simple", not absolute simplicity), `IsAnisotropic` (split rank
  zero) and `splitRank` (the `ℕ∞`-valued `k`-rank) are as in
  `Langlands.GroupScheme.LocalKneserTitsRankOneReductionStatement`.
- `IsParabolicSubgroup p` and `LeviSubgroup.IsGeometricUnipotentRadical r` are
  the same predicates as in the radical premise of `LocalKneserTitsStatement`.
  `parabolicElementarySubgroup G` is the subgroup of `G(k)` generated by the
  `k`-points of all such radicals of `G`, so clause 17 says that the image in
  `G(k)` of the `k`-points of each parabolic radical of `A i` lies in this
  subgroup.

In words: an isotropic `k`-simple simply connected group `G` has a
cocharacter whose centralizer `M` has a normal subgroup `D` with quotient
`Q`. It also has a family of subgroups `M_i ⟶ M`, each with a simply connected
kernel `D_i` of a smooth surjection `M_i ⟶ Q_i` that is compatible with
`M ⟶ Q`. The images of the `M_i(k)` generate a subgroup containing the image
of `D(k)`. The images of the `Q_i(k)` generate a subgroup containing the image
of `M(k)`. Each `M_i ⟶ M ⟶ G` factors through a simply connected `k`-simple
group `A_i` of split rank one mapping to `G`, whose rational parabolic
radicals land in the parabolic elementary subgroup of `G`.

## Theorem proved here

`localKneserTitsRankOneReduction_of_rankOneLeviPresentation` assumes
`hloc : LocalSimplyConnectedCocycleVanishingStatement.{u}` and
`hpres : RankOneLeviPresentationStatement.{u}`. It proves
`LocalKneserTitsRankOneReductionStatement.{u}`. Recall what that requires. Let
`F` be a nonarchimedean local field and `G` an affine, simply connected,
`F`-simple, isotropic group over `F`. Let `N` be a normal subgroup of `G(F)`
that contains every rational parabolic radical of `G`. Suppose `N` contains
the image of `A(F)` for every map `A ⟶ G` from a simply connected `F`-simple
group of split rank one whose rational parabolic radicals map into `N`. Then
`N = ⊤`.

The proof runs as follows.

1. Apply `hpres` to `F` and `G` to obtain the data and clauses 1–17.
2. `parabolicElementarySubgroup_le_iff` and the radical premise give
   `parabolicElementarySubgroup G ≤ N`.
3. `QuotientGeneration.generatedPoints_eq_top_of_localCocycleVanishing`
   (module `Langlands.GroupScheme.SimplyConnected.LocalQuotientGeneration`)
   uses clauses 2–11 and `hloc`. It shows that the images of the `M_i(F)`
   generate `M(F)`.
4. For each `i`, clause 17 and step 2 show that the rational parabolic
   radicals of `A i` map into `N`. The rank-one premise of the reduction then
   gives `(rationalPoints.map (a i)).range ≤ N`.
5. By clause 12, `j` maps the image of each `M_i(F)` into the image of
   `A_i(F)`, hence into `N`. By step 3 the image of `M(F)` under `j` lies in
   `N`.
6. `centralizer_rationalPoints_sup_parabolicElementarySubgroup_of_localField`
   (module `Langlands.GroupScheme.Rational.CentralizerElementaryProduct`) is
   applied to the connected reductive group `G` and `χ`. It gives
   `Z_G(χ)(F) ⊔ parabolicElementarySubgroup G = ⊤`. By clause 1 and
   `SubgroupFunctor.rationalPoints_eq_range`, `Z_G(χ)(F)` is the image of
   `M(F)` under `j`. Steps 2 and 5 then give `N = ⊤`.

The proof uses neither the rational Bruhat decomposition nor the
assumption that the split rank is at least 2. Inspection shows that both
declarations use only the standard axioms.

## Source and derivation

G. Prasad and M. S. Raghunathan, *On the Kneser–Tits problem*, Comment.
Math. Helv. 60 (1985), 107–121, registered as
`prasad-raghunathan-1985-kneser-tits`. The retained PDF has one cover page,
so printed page = PDF page + 105. The source works over an arbitrary field
`k`.

The statement is derived. It collects the structural facts that the source
uses in its proof of Theorem A, in a form that the diagram chase of
`LocalQuotientGeneration` can consume. In the source's notation:

- `S` is a maximal `k`-split torus and `T ⊇ S` a maximal `k`-torus. `Δ` is a
  basis of the absolute roots, `Δ₀ ⊆ Δ` the roots trivial on `S`, and
  `Θ_1, …, Θ_r` the Galois orbits (for the `*`-action) in `Δ − Δ₀`
  (Sections 1.2 and 1.6, printed pp. 108–109 / PDF pp. 3–4).
- Since `G` is simply connected, `T` is the direct product of the
  one-dimensional coroot tori `T_α`, `α ∈ Δ`. `T_Θ` denotes the subtorus
  generated by the `T_α` with `α ∈ Θ` (Section 1.3, printed p. 108 /
  PDF p. 3).
- `M` is the centralizer of `S` and `𝒢` its derived group, which is
  anisotropic and simply connected. `G_Θ` is the product of the isotropic
  `k`-simple factors of the derived group of the Levi subgroup attached to
  `Θ`. `ℳ_Θ` is the centralizer in `G_Θ` of the maximal split torus `S_Θ` of
  `G_Θ` contained in `S`, and `ℳ_Θ = M ∩ G_Θ` (Section 1.5, printed
  pp. 108–109 / PDF pp. 3–4).
- Each `G_{Θ_i}` is semisimple, simply connected, `k`-simple and of `k`-rank 1
  (Section 1.6, printed p. 109 / PDF p. 4).

The intended witnesses are as follows.

- `χ`: a cocharacter of `S` with `Z_G(χ) = Z_G(S) = M`, for example one that
  pairs nontrivially with every `k`-root. `j` is the inclusion of `M`.
- `D = 𝒢` and `Q = M/𝒢`, with `d` the inclusion and `q` the quotient map.
- `J` indexes the orbits `Θ_i`. `Mi i = ℳ_{Θ_i}`, with `m i` the inclusion
  into `M`. `Di i` is the derived group of `ℳ_{Θ_i}` and
  `Qi i = ℳ_{Θ_i}/Di i`, with `qi i` the quotient map. `φ i` is induced by
  `m i`.
- `A i = G_{Θ_i}`, with `a i` and `e i` the inclusions.

Clauses 1, 2 and 5–10, 12 and 13 then record standard facts about
centralizers of tori, derived groups and quotients of smooth affine groups.

Clause 3 is the first step of the source's proof of Theorem A (printed
p. 110 / PDF p. 5). From the Tits index, every `k`-simple normal subgroup of
`𝒢` lies in some `G_{Θ_i}`, hence in `ℳ_{Θ_i}`. Since `𝒢` is the direct
product of these subgroups, the subgroup generated by the `ℳ_{Θ_i}(k)`
contains `𝒢(k)`.

Clause 4 is derived from the torus computation in the same proof. There
`M/𝒢` is identified with `T_{Δ−Δ₀}`, the direct product of the tori
`T_{Θ_i}`. In the same way, `ℳ_{Θ_i}/Di i` is identified with `T_{Θ_i}`.
The product of the `φ i` is then an isomorphism `∏ Qi i → Q`, so the images
of the `Qi i(k)` generate all of `Q(k)`.

Clause 11 is derived as well. `Di i` is a connected normal semisimple
subgroup of `𝒢`, normalized by `M`. It is therefore a product of
`k`-simple factors of the simply connected group `𝒢`, so it is simply
connected. The source does not state this clause.

Clauses 14–16 are Section 1.6's description of `G_{Θ_i}`. The
`IsAlmostSimple` predicate asks for simplicity over `k`, not absolute
simplicity. This matters: `G_{Θ_i}` need not be absolutely simple, for
example a Weil restriction of `SL_2` inside a quasi-split special unitary
group.

Clause 17 is derived. Every parabolic `k`-subgroup of `G_{Θ_i}` has the form
`P_{G_{Θ_i}}(λ)` for a `k`-cocharacter `λ`, with unipotent radical
`U_{G_{Θ_i}}(λ)`. This radical lies in `U_G(λ)`, the unipotent radical of the
parabolic `k`-subgroup `P_G(λ)` of `G`.

The source's own proof of Theorem A differs from the chase used here. It
maps `M` into the Levi subgroups attached to the complements `Θ'_i` of the
`Θ_i` (its `ℳ_i` and `𝒢_i`), and uses its Theorem B, an injectivity statement
for first Galois cohomology, to obtain surjectivity of
`M(k)/𝒢(k) → ∏ ℳ_i(k)/𝒢_i(k)`. Here the subgroups `ℳ_{Θ_i}` themselves map
to `M`. Surjectivity of `ℳ_{Θ_i}(F) → Qi i(F)` comes from vanishing of first
cohomology of the simply connected kernels over a local field. That is the
explicit hypothesis `hloc` of the theorem; Section 2 of the source
(printed p. 111 / PDF p. 6) records this vanishing for nonarchimedean local
fields. The final step `G(F) = M(F) · G(F)^+` uses the opposite-cell
argument of `CentralizerElementaryProduct` in place of the source's Bruhat
decomposition (Section 1.6).

## Why the statement is expected to hold

The derivation above sketches the argument. Nothing in it is proved in this
module. The pieces it would need include: the Tits index and the decomposition
`T = ∏ T_α` for simply connected groups; the subgroups `G_Θ` and `ℳ_Θ`;
simple connectedness of derived groups of Levi subgroups; existence of a
cocharacter with centralizer `Z_G(S)`; and the description of parabolic
subgroups and their radicals by cocharacters.

## Relation to other statements

The theorem reduces `LocalKneserTitsRankOneReductionStatement` to two
explicit inputs: the structure statement here and local cocycle vanishing.
Together with `localKneserTits_of_rankOne_of_reduction`, the local
Kneser–Tits statement `LocalKneserTitsStatement` then follows from three
inputs: this structure statement, `LocalSimplyConnectedCocycleVanishingStatement`
and `LocalRankOneKneserTitsStatement`. This module does not prove that
consequence or any of the three inputs.
