# Reduction of local Kneser–Tits to groups of split rank one

Authors: Worker 1757.

This module states, as the proposition definition
`Langlands.GroupScheme.LocalKneserTitsRankOneReductionStatement.{u}` with
arbitrary universe `u`, the reduction of the local Kneser–Tits theorem to
groups of split rank one. It uses the same formulation with normal subgroups
and rational parabolic radicals as
`Langlands.GroupScheme.LocalKneserTitsStatement`. The module also proves
two short theorems. They show that the reduction and
`Langlands.GroupScheme.LocalRankOneKneserTitsStatement` together give
`LocalKneserTitsStatement`. The module does not prove the reduction or the
rank-one statement.

## Exact statement

Let `F : Type u` have instances `[Field F] [ValuativeRel F]
[TopologicalSpace F] [IsNonarchimedeanLocalField F]`. Let
`G : GroupScheme (Spec (CommRingCat.of F))` have `[IsAffineHom G.X.hom]`,
`IsSimplyConnected G`, `IsAlmostSimple G`, and `¬ IsAnisotropic G`. Let `N`
be a subgroup of `rationalPoints G` with `N.Normal`. Assume the two
premises below.

1. Radical premise, exactly as in `LocalKneserTitsStatement`. For all group
   schemes `P` and `U` over `F` and all maps `p : P ⟶ G` and `r : U ⟶ P`,

   ```
   IsParabolicSubgroup p →
   LeviSubgroup.IsGeometricUnipotentRadical r →
   (rationalPoints.map (r ≫ p)).range ≤ N.
   ```

2. Rank-one premise. For every group scheme
   `A : GroupScheme (Spec (CommRingCat.of F))` with `[IsAffineHom A.X.hom]`
   and every group-scheme homomorphism `a : A ⟶ G`, suppose `A` satisfies
   `IsSimplyConnected A`, `IsAlmostSimple A` and `splitRank A = 1`. Suppose
   also that for all `P`, `U`, `p : P ⟶ A` and `r : U ⟶ P` with
   `IsParabolicSubgroup p` and `LeviSubgroup.IsGeometricUnipotentRadical r`
   we have `(rationalPoints.map (r ≫ p ≫ a)).range ≤ N`. Then
   `(rationalPoints.map a).range ≤ N`.

Then `N = ⊤`.

These are all the quantified data and premises. The characteristic of `F`
is unrestricted. No splitting, quasi-splitting or absolute simplicity is
assumed for `G` or `A`, and no maximal split torus is chosen. The map `a`
is an arbitrary homomorphism: it need not be a closed immersion, and its
image need not be normal.

In words: let `N` be a normal subgroup of `G(F)` that contains every
rational parabolic radical of `G`. Suppose also that `N` contains the image
of `A(F)` for every simply connected `F`-simple group `A` of split rank one
mapping to `G` whose own rational parabolic radicals map into `N`. Then `N`
is all of `G(F)`.

The names are used as in `LocalRankOneKneserTitsStatement` and
`LocalKneserTitsStatement`; their paired Markdown files explain them.
`splitRank` is the `ℕ∞`-valued split rank of
`Langlands.GroupScheme.Rational.SplitTorus`, and `IsAnisotropic G` means
`splitRank G = 0`. `IsAlmostSimple` means simplicity over `F`
(the source's "`k`-simple"), not absolute simplicity.

## Theorems proved here

`rationalPoints_range_le_of_localRankOneKneserTits` assumes
`LocalRankOneKneserTitsStatement.{u}`. It takes a nonarchimedean local field
`F : Type u`, an affine group scheme `A` over `F` with
`IsSimplyConnected A`, `IsAlmostSimple A` and `splitRank A = 1`, an arbitrary
homomorphism `a : A ⟶ G`, and a normal subgroup `K` of `rationalPoints G`.
Suppose every `(rationalPoints.map (r ≫ p ≫ a)).range` lies in `K`, where
`p` is a parabolic subgroup of `A` and `r` represents its geometric
unipotent radical. Then `(rationalPoints.map a).range ≤ K`. The proof
applies the rank-one statement to the inverse image
`K.comap (rationalPoints.map a)`. This is a normal subgroup of
`rationalPoints A` that contains every rational parabolic radical of `A`,
so it is all of `rationalPoints A`. The argument is the one used for
`rationalPoints_range_le_of_localKneserTits` in
`Langlands.GroupScheme.SimplyConnected.KneserTitsFactorReduction`, with the
rank-one statement in place of the general one.

`localKneserTits_of_rankOne_of_reduction` proves
`LocalKneserTitsStatement.{u}` from `LocalRankOneKneserTitsStatement.{u}`
and `LocalKneserTitsRankOneReductionStatement.{u}`. Given the data of the
general statement, it applies the reduction to the same `F`, `G` and `N`
and the same radical premise. The previous theorem, with `K = N`,
discharges the rank-one premise for every `A` and `a`.

Both theorems use only the standard axioms.

## Source and derivation

G. Prasad and M. S. Raghunathan, *On the Kneser–Tits problem*, Comment.
Math. Helv. 60 (1985), 107–121, registered as
`prasad-raghunathan-1985-kneser-tits`. The retained PDF has one cover page,
so printed page = PDF page + 105.

The statement is derived from the source's reduction, Section 1.6, printed
p. 109 / PDF p. 4. There `G` is a `k`-simple simply connected `k`-isotropic
group and `G(k)^+` is the normal subgroup generated by the rational points of
the unipotent radicals of the parabolic `k`-subgroups (Section 1.4, printed
p. 108 / PDF p. 3). Section 1.5 (printed p. 108 / PDF p. 3) attaches to each
orbit `Θ_i` of the Galois group on the non-anisotropic simple roots a
semisimple simply connected `k`-subgroup `G_{Θ_i}` of `G`. Section 1.6 notes
that `G_{Θ_i}` is `k`-simple of `k`-rank 1. It deduces from the Bruhat
decomposition that `G(k) = M(k) · G(k)^+`, where `M` is the centralizer of a
maximal split torus. With Theorem A (`M(k)` is generated by the subgroups
`M_{Θ_i}(k) ⊆ G_{Θ_i}(k)` when the `k`-rank is at least 2) it concludes that
the Kneser–Tits problem over `k` has an affirmative answer if and only if
`H(k)^+ = H(k)` for every `k`-simple simply connected group `H` of `k`-rank 1.

The source states the reduction for an arbitrary field and proves Theorem A
from its Theorem B (printed pp. 109–110 / PDF pp. 4–5). This proposition is
the "if" direction over a nonarchimedean local field, in the
universal-property form of the general statement. The rank-one groups
`G_{Θ_i}` become the arbitrary maps `a : A ⟶ G` of the rank-one premise.
Rank-one equality `A(F)^+ = A(F)` is replaced by the inclusion of the image of
`A(F)` in `N` whenever the rational parabolic radicals of `A` map into `N`.
The theorems above show that rank-one Kneser–Tits gives this premise for every
such map.

## Why the statement is expected to hold

The following outlines the source's argument. Nothing in it is proved in
this module.

If `splitRank G = 1`, the rank-one premise with `A = G` and `a = 𝟙 G`
gives `N = ⊤` from the radical premise. If the split rank is at least 2,
write `G(F) = M(F) · G(F)^+` by the rational Bruhat decomposition. Here
`G(F)^+ ⊆ N` by the radical premise and normality. The rational parabolic
radicals of each `G_{Θ_i}` lie in rational parabolic radicals of `G`, so the
inclusion `G_{Θ_i} ⟶ G` satisfies the rank-one premise and `G_{Θ_i}(F) ⊆ N`.
Theorem A then gives `M(F) ⊆ N`, so `N = ⊤`. Over a nonarchimedean local
field the first Galois cohomology of a simply connected semisimple group
vanishes (Kneser in characteristic zero, Bruhat–Tits in general). This makes
the surjectivity step in the proof of Theorem A immediate, without Theorem B.
The separately submitted module
`Langlands.GroupScheme.SimplyConnected.LocalQuotientGeneration` carries out
that diagram chase for given quotient data; this module does not import it. The proposition here still
needs the construction of `M`, the subgroups `G_{Θ_i}`, the radical
comparison and the rational Bruhat decomposition.

## Relation to other statements

`LocalKneserTitsStatement` implies this proposition, because its conclusion
already follows from the radical premise. Conversely,
`localKneserTits_of_rankOne_of_reduction` shows that this proposition and
`LocalRankOneKneserTitsStatement` together imply `LocalKneserTitsStatement`.
The general local Kneser–Tits theorem thus splits into two independent
obligations. One is the rank-one case, which needs the classification of
rank-one groups over local fields. The other is this reduction, which needs
the structure theory of Sections 1.5–1.6 of the source.
