# Local Kneser–Tits in terms of rational parabolic radicals

Authors: Worker 1756.

`Langlands.GroupScheme.LocalKneserTitsStatement.{u}` is a proposition
definition, with arbitrary universe `u`. It states the local Kneser–Tits
theorem for an isotropic almost-simple simply connected group, expressed
using normal subgroups containing rational parabolic radicals. This module
does not prove the proposition.

## Exact statement

Let `F : Type u` have instances `[Field F] [ValuativeRel F]
[TopologicalSpace F] [IsNonarchimedeanLocalField F]`. Let
`G : GroupScheme (Spec (CommRingCat.of F))` have `[IsAffineHom G.X.hom]`,
`IsSimplyConnected G`, `IsAlmostSimple G`, and `¬ IsAnisotropic G`.

For every subgroup `N` of `rationalPoints G`, if `N.Normal` and, for all
actual group schemes `P` and `U` over `F` and maps `p : P ⟶ G` and
`r : U ⟶ P`,

```
IsParabolicSubgroup p →
LeviSubgroup.IsGeometricUnipotentRadical r →
(rationalPoints.map (r ≫ p)).range ≤ N,
```

then `N = ⊤`.

These are all the quantified data and premises. In particular the
characteristic of `F` is unrestricted. No splitting, quasi-splitting,
absolute simplicity, or choice of maximal split torus is assumed.
`IsAlmostSimple G` means simplicity over `F`, in the convention of
`Langlands.GroupScheme.Reductive.AlmostSimple`, and does not mean absolute
simplicity. `IsSimplyConnected G` is algebraic simple connectedness in
`Langlands.GroupScheme.SimplyConnected.Basic`.

`rationalPoints G` is the actual group of sections over `Spec F`.
`rationalPoints.map (r ≫ p)` sends a section of `U` to its image in `G`.
`IsParabolicSubgroup p` is the existing smooth closed subgroup predicate,
defined by containment of a Borel after algebraic closure.
`LeviSubgroup.IsGeometricUnipotentRadical r` requires both the ground-field
unipotent radical property and the same radical property after algebraic
closure. It therefore specifies the geometric unipotent radical of `P`
over the original field; it supplies no chosen representative and asserts
no general existence theorem.

## Source and reformulation

G. Prasad and M. S. Raghunathan, *On the Kneser–Tits problem*, Comment.
Math. Helv. 60 (1985), 107–121, registered as
`prasad-raghunathan-1985-kneser-tits`. The retained PDF has one cover page,
so printed page = PDF page + 105.

The introduction on printed p. 107 / PDF p. 2 defines `G(F)^+` as the
normal subgroup of `G(F)` generated by the rational points of the
unipotent radicals of all parabolic `F`-subgroups. Section 1.4, printed
p. 108 / PDF p. 3, repeats this definition for subgroups. Section 2,
printed p. 111 / PDF p. 6, gives the affirmative local result in arbitrary
characteristic, using reduction to rank one, vanishing of first Galois
cohomology, and classification of the rank-one groups.

The proposition uses the universal property of the source's normal
subgroup generation: if `G(F)^+ = G(F)`, any normal subgroup containing
all the generating images is all of `G(F)`. Conversely, taking `N` to
be the normal subgroup generated by those images recovers
`G(F)^+ = G(F)`. This is a reformulation of the source theorem, not an
additional assumption about a supplied group. The geometric-radical
predicate makes the unipotent-radical convention explicit also over
imperfect fields.

## Relation to the factor target

This proposition is an arithmetic prerequisite for
`Langlands.GroupScheme.SimplyConnectedKneserTitsFactorStatement`; it does
not prove that target. To use it for a closed normal almost-simple
subgroup `a : A ⟶ G`, one must still prove that `A` is simply connected
and that the inverse image in `A(F)` of the root-generated subgroup of
`G(F)` is normal and contains all the rational parabolic-radical images
of `A`. Those structural and root-comparison obligations are retained.
