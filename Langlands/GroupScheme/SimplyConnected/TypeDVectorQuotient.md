# The descended vector quotient of a numbered type-D group

This internal construction combines the actual numbered vector central isogeny
with descent from its Galois-stable integral character image. Its inputs are
actual Tits index data, simple connectedness, type-D Cartan entries with rank at
least four, and fixation of the vertex numbered zero under every star action.
There is no characteristic restriction.

Simple connectedness ascends to the separable closure, where the integral
coroot criterion gives full coroot span. The chosen vector map has character
image generated by the roots and the fundamental weight at numbered vertex
zero. Star fixation preserves this lattice under the ordinary Galois action.
The full-kernel descent construction then returns an actual affine group over
the ground field, a central isogeny to it, and an isomorphism recovering the
chosen vector map over the separable closure.

`TitsIndexData.typeDVectorQuotientRecovery` is the selected actual descended
quotient, including its affine target, arrow, central-isogeny property,
isomorphism over the separable closure and exact recovery equation.
`typeDVectorQuotientRecovery_locallyOfFiniteType` transports local finite type
through that isomorphism and descends it along the fpqc separable-closure map.
Affineness of the target scheme also gives affineness of its structural map
over the field.

`typeDVectorQuotientRecovery_fundamentalWeight` proves the all-test-scheme
alignment predicate. Full integral coroot span makes the simple-coroot
pairings separate characters, so any character satisfying the predicate's
fundamental-weight pairings equals the actual dual-basis weight. A vertex
numbered zero is the specified inverse image of zero under the numbering.
The recovery equation sends every tested kernel point into the kernel of the
split vector map. The split kernel-alignment theorem then sends it to one
under the fundamental weight. This argument applies to arbitrary test schemes,
including nonreduced schemes in characteristic two.

The companion theorem
`Langlands.GroupScheme.typeDVectorQuotient :
TypeDVectorQuotientStatement.{u}` assembles these properties, with the original
field, numbering and hypotheses. The construction and lemmas do not need the
statement's additional absolute-almost-simplicity hypothesis. Rank four retains
the exact vertex numbered zero. This proves the vector-quotient input of the
aligned-spin-cover reduction; it does not supply the separate presentation of
the quotient as a quaternionic special orthogonal group or the parent spin cover.

The construction derives from the imported Library results; it does not use
spin presentations or rank-one classification.
