# Rank-one generation of split-torus centralizers in simply connected groups

Authors: Worker 1663.

`Langlands.GroupScheme.SimplyConnectedRankOneCentralizerGenerationStatement.{u}`
is a proposition definition, not a proof. For a simply connected group `G`
over a nonarchimedean local field `F` with a maximal split torus `S` and the
represented centralizer `Z = Z_G(S)`, it states that every rational point of
`Z` is a product of elements of `Z(F) ∩ ⟨U_a(F), U_{-a}(F)⟩`, for relative
roots `a`, and one element of the norm-one subgroup `Z(F)^1`.

## The proposition

For every `F : Type u` with `[Field F] [ValuativeRel F] [TopologicalSpace F]
[IsNonarchimedeanLocalField F]`, every `G : GroupScheme (Spec (CommRingCat.of F))`
with `[IsAffineHom G.X.hom]` and `IsSimplyConnected G`, every
`S : CommGroupScheme (Spec (CommRingCat.of F))` and `i : S.toGrp ⟶ G` with
`IsMaximalSplitTorus i`, and every `Z : GroupScheme (Spec (CommRingCat.of F))`
and `j : Z ⟶ G` with `(torusCentralizer i).IsRepresentedBy j`, the range of
`rationalPoints.map j : Z(F) →* G(F)` is contained in

```
Subgroup.closure (⋃ a ∈ relativeRoots i,
    (centralizerPoints i ⊓ Subgroup.closure (U_a(F) ∪ U_{-a}(F))))
  ⊔ (Plancherel.rationalCommonNormKernel Z).map (rationalPoints.map j)
```

where `U_a(F)` is `(RelativeRoot.subgroup i a).carrier (Over.mk (𝟙 _))`.
These are all the quantified data and hypotheses. The universe `u` is
arbitrary. The field `F` has no characteristic hypothesis, and `G` is not
assumed quasi-split, split or `F`-simple.

## Exact objects

- `IsSimplyConnected G` (module `Langlands.GroupScheme.SimplyConnected.Basic`)
  says that `G` is semisimple and that every central isogeny onto `G` from a
  semisimple group is an isomorphism.
- `IsMaximalSplitTorus i` (module `Langlands.GroupScheme.Rational.SplitTorus`)
  says that `i` is a split subtorus and that every factorization of `i`
  through another split subtorus is an isomorphism.
- `(torusCentralizer i).IsRepresentedBy j` says that `j` is a monomorphism
  whose range on every test scheme is the centralizer functor of `i`. So `Z`
  is `Z_G(S)` and `rationalPoints.map j` is injective with range
  `centralizerPoints i`.
- `centralizerPoints i` (module `Langlands.GroupScheme.Torus.Maximal`) is the
  subgroup of `G(F)` of points normalizing `i` through the identity of `S`,
  that is, the rational points of `Z_G(S)`.
- `relativeRoots i` (module `Langlands.GroupScheme.Rational.RelativeRoots`)
  is the set of nonzero characters of `S` with a nonzero weight space in the
  Lie algebra of `G`.
- `RelativeRoot.subgroup i a` (module `Langlands.GroupScheme.Rational.RootSubgroup`)
  is the subgroup functor of points `g` whose orbit map `s ↦ i(s) g i(s)⁻¹`
  factors as `s ↦ e(a(s))` for a morphism `e` from the affine line with
  `e(0) = 1`. Its `F`-points contain the standard root group `U_{(a)}(F)`,
  which is generated by `U_a(F)` and `U_{2a}(F)` when `2a` is a root: on
  these elements the orbit map is a polynomial in `a(s)` without constant
  term. A larger `U_a(F)` only enlarges the right-hand side, so the
  proposition is no stronger than its standard form.
- `Plancherel.rationalCommonNormKernel Z` (module
  `Langlands.Representation.Plancherel.RationalCharacterValuation`) is the
  subgroup `Z(F)^1` of points `z` with `|χ(z)| = 1` for every rational
  character `χ` of `Z`.

## Source and derived formulation

V. Platonov and A. Rapinchuk, *Algebraic Groups and Number Theory*,
Academic Press, 1994, registered as
`platonov-rapinchuk-1994-algebraic-groups-number-theory`. Printed page =
PDF page − 10.

- Section 7.2, printed p. 406: for an `F`-simple, simply connected,
  `F`-isotropic group, `G(F)^+` is the subgroup generated by the rational
  points of unipotent radicals of parabolic `F`-subgroups. The
  Kneser–Tits problem asks whether `G(F)^+ = G(F)`.
- Theorem 7.6 (Platonov), printed p. 414: the Kneser–Tits conjecture holds
  over non-archimedean locally compact fields. The book's standing
  convention is characteristic zero (footnote on printed p. 5).
- Printed p. 410, after Proposition 7.7: in proving Proposition 7.6 one sees
  that `G(F)^+ = G(F)` is equivalent to `H(F) ⊆ G(F)^+` for
  `H = Z_G(S)`.
- Theorem 7.4 (Prasad–Raghunathan, Ann. of Math. 119 (1984)), printed
  p. 411: if `G` has `F`-rank at least 2, then `H(F)` is generated by the
  groups `H_i(F)`, where `H_i = H ∩ G_i` and `G_i` is the `F`-rank-one,
  `F`-simple, `F`-isotropic component of the derived group of the Levi
  subgroup `Z_G(T(θ_i ∪ Π_0))` attached to one orbit `θ_i` of distinguished
  vertices of the Tits index. Over a non-archimedean local field the
  cohomological input of its proof is `H¹(F, G_0) = 1` (printed p. 412).

Tasho Kaletha and Gopal Prasad, *Bruhat–Tits Theory: A New Approach*,
registered as `kaletha-prasad-2023-bruhat-tits-new-approach`, printed p. 76
/ PDF p. 107, after Proposition 2.2.14: by the solution of the Kneser–Tits
problem in [PR85] (Prasad–Raghunathan, Comment. Math. Helv. 60 (1985)),
`G(k)^+ = G(k)` for a non-discrete locally compact field `k` and a
`k`-isotropic simply connected `G`, with no characteristic restriction.
The positive-characteristic cases of Theorem 7.4 and of the Kneser–Tits
theorem rest on the cited Prasad–Raghunathan papers, which are not in the
project literature; they were not checked directly.

The proposition is a derived formulation and not a numbered result. The
following standard argument is its provenance; it is not proved here.
A simply connected `G` is the direct product of `F`-simple simply connected
factors `G_k`, and `S` and `Z` split accordingly. Every relative root of `G`
is a relative root of exactly one factor, and its root group lies in that
factor.

- If `G_k` is `F`-anisotropic, its split torus is trivial and its part of
  `Z` is `G_k` itself. Every rational character of `Z` is trivial on the
  semisimple group `G_k`, so `G_k(F) ⊆ Z(F)^1`.
- If `G_k` has `F`-rank one with relative roots `±a` (and possibly `±2a`),
  then `Z_k(F) ⊆ G_k(F) = G_k(F)^+` by Kneser–Tits, and
  `G_k(F)^+ = ⟨U_{(a)}(F), U_{(-a)}(F)⟩`. Indeed `U_{(±a)}` are the
  unipotent radicals of the two minimal parabolic subgroups containing `S`,
  so `E = ⟨U_{(a)}(F), U_{(-a)}(F)⟩ ⊆ G_k(F)^+`. The Bruhat decomposition
  and a Weyl representative inside `E` give `G_k(F) = E · Z_k(F)`, and
  `Z_k(F)` normalizes `U_{(±a)}`, so `E` is normal in `G_k(F)`. Every proper
  parabolic `F`-subgroup is `G_k(F)`-conjugate to `Z_k U_{(a)}`, so its
  unipotent radical has rational points in `E`. Hence
  `Z_k(F) ⊆ Z(F) ∩ ⟨U_a(F), U_{-a}(F)⟩`.
- If `G_k` has `F`-rank at least 2, Theorem 7.4 gives `Z_k(F)` generated by
  the `H_i(F)`. Each `G_i` is simply connected of `F`-rank one, so
  Kneser–Tits gives `H_i(F) ⊆ G_i(F) = G_i(F)^+`. The root groups of `G_i`
  for its relative roots lie in `U_{(±a_i)}` of `G`, where `a_i` is the
  simple relative root attached to `θ_i`. Hence
  `H_i(F) ⊆ Z(F) ∩ ⟨U_{a_i}(F), U_{-a_i}(F)⟩`.

Multiplying the factors gives the asserted containment.

This module supplies no proof. The proof is a separate obligation. The
proposition is an input to the proof of
`Langlands.Geometry.Building.SimplyConnectedReflectionTranslationStatement`,
where `Z(F)^1` lies in the kernel of the reduced centralizer translation and
the rank-one pieces are shown to be generated by reflection
representatives.
