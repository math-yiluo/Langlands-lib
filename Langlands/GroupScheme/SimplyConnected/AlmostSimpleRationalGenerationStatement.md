# Rational generation of simply connected groups by almost-simple factors

Authors: Worker 1526.

`Langlands.GroupScheme.SimplyConnectedAlmostSimpleRationalGenerationStatement.{u}`
is a proposition definition, not a proof. Let `G` be a simply connected group
over a field `k`. The proposition states that `G(k)` is generated by the
images of `A(k)` for the closed normal almost-simple subgroups `A` of `G`.
It is stated in the equivalent form that every subgroup of `G(k)` containing
all these images is `G(k)`.

## The proposition

For every `k : Type u` with `[Field k]`, every
`G : GroupScheme (Spec (CommRingCat.of k))` with `IsSimplyConnected G`, and
every subgroup `H : Subgroup (rationalPoints G)`: if

```
(rationalPoints.map a).range ≤ H
```

for every `A : GroupScheme (Spec (CommRingCat.of k))` and every `a : A ⟶ G`
with `IsAlmostSimple A`, `IsClosedSubgroup a` and
`(SubgroupFunctor.range a).IsNormal`, then `H = ⊤`.

These are all the quantified data and hypotheses. The universe `u` is
arbitrary, and the field `k` is arbitrary: no characteristic, perfectness,
topology or isotropy hypothesis is made. No new definition is introduced.

## Exact objects

- `IsSimplyConnected G` (module `Langlands.GroupScheme.SimplyConnected.Basic`)
  says that `G` is semisimple and that every central isogeny onto `G` from a
  semisimple group is an isomorphism.
- `IsAlmostSimple A` (module `Langlands.GroupScheme.Reductive.AlmostSimple`)
  says that `A` is semisimple, is not commutative, and that every smooth
  connected closed subgroup of `A` with normal range has range trivial or all
  of `A`. This is Milne's definition 19.7 of an almost-simple group over `k`,
  so for semisimple groups it means `k`-simple in the sense of PR85 §1.1, not
  absolutely simple.
- `IsClosedSubgroup a` (module `Langlands.GroupScheme.Reductive.Basic`) says
  that the underlying morphism of schemes of `a` is a closed immersion.
- `(SubgroupFunctor.range a).IsNormal` (module
  `Langlands.GroupScheme.SubgroupFunctor`) says that on every test scheme `T`
  the image of `A(T)` in `G(T)` is a normal subgroup.
- `rationalPoints G` (module `Langlands.GroupScheme.Rational.Characters`) is
  the group `G(k)` of sections of `G` over `Spec k`, and
  `rationalPoints.map a : A(k) →* G(k)` is composition with `a`.

## Source and derived formulation

J. S. Milne, *Algebraic Groups: The Theory of Group Schemes of Finite Type
over a Field*, Cambridge University Press, corrected reprint 2022, registered
as `milne-2022-algebraic-groups`, Theorem 21.51, printed pp. 439–441 / PDF
pp. 450–452: a semisimple algebraic group `G` over a field has only finitely
many almost-simple normal algebraic subgroups `G_1, …, G_r`, and is their
almost-direct product. That is, the multiplication map
`G_1 × ⋯ × G_r → G` is a surjective homomorphism with finite kernel, and the
`G_i` commute in pairs. Every smooth connected normal subgroup of `G` is the
product of the `G_i` it contains.

G. Prasad and M. S. Raghunathan, *On the Kneser–Tits problem*, Comment.
Math. Helv. 60 (1985), 107–121, registered as
`prasad-raghunathan-1985-kneser-tits`, §1.5, printed p. 108 / PDF p. 3: a
semisimple, simply connected `k`-group is a direct product of its connected
`k`-simple normal subgroups.

The proposition is a derived formulation and not a numbered result. The
following standard argument is its provenance; it is not proved here.

1. By Milne 21.51, `G` is the almost-direct product of its almost-simple
   normal subgroups `G_1, …, G_r`. Each `G_i` is a closed subgroup of `G`
   that is normal on every test scheme, and is almost simple. So each
   inclusion `G_i ⟶ G` satisfies the three hypotheses on `a`.
2. The kernel of the multiplication map `μ : G_1 × ⋯ × G_r → G` is finite.
   It is also central: if `g_1 ⋯ g_r = 1`, then each `g_i` lies in `G_i` and
   in the product of the other factors, so it commutes with all factors. So
   `μ` is a central isogeny from a semisimple group. Since `G` is simply
   connected, `μ` is an isomorphism. This is the direct product
   decomposition of PR85 §1.5.
3. Hence `G(k) = G_1(k) × ⋯ × G_r(k)`, and every element of `G(k)` is a
   product of elements of the images of the `G_i(k)`. A subgroup `H`
   containing all these images is `G(k)`.

The hypothesis of the proposition requires `H` to contain the image of
`A(k)` for every closed normal almost-simple subgroup `a : A ⟶ G`, not only
for the inclusions of the factors `G_i`. This only strengthens the
hypothesis, so the proposition is implied by the standard statement. If `G`
is trivial, then `G(k)` is trivial and the conclusion holds.

This module supplies no proof. The proof is a separate obligation.

Related Library statement (a remark): the Library's
`Langlands.GroupScheme.SemisimpleAlmostSimpleGenerationStatement` (module
`Langlands.GroupScheme.Reductive.AlmostSimpleGenerationStatement`) is the
generation consequence of Milne 21.51 for closed subgroups of a semisimple
group over an algebraically closed field. The present proposition is
different: it concerns rational points over an arbitrary field and uses
simple connectedness.

Intended use (a remark, not proved in this module): together with
`Langlands.GroupScheme.SimplyConnectedKneserTitsFactorStatement` and
`Langlands.GroupScheme.SimplyConnectedAnisotropicFactorCentralizerStatement`,
this proposition is an input for deriving
`Langlands.GroupScheme.SimplyConnectedKneserTitsCentralizerStatement`.
