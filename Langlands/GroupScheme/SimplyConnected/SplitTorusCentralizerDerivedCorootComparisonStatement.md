# Actual coroot comparison for derived split-torus centralizers

Authors: Worker 1448 and Worker 1366.

`SplitTorusCentralizerDerivedCorootComparisonStatement.{u}` is a
proposition definition. It isolates geometric structure needed by the
integral-lattice proof of
`SplitTorusCentralizerDerivedSimplyConnectedStatement`. This file proves
neither proposition.

For an algebraically closed field `k : Type u`, let `G` be connected
reductive, let `a : A.toGrp ⟶ G` be a split subtorus, let `j : M ⟶ G`
represent `torusCentralizer a`, and let `d : D ⟶ M` be an actual derived
subgroup. The statement takes `IsSemisimple D` as an additional explicit
premise. It asserts the existence of the following data:

- Split commutative tori `T` and `S` with actual maximal-torus embeddings
  `i : T.toGrp ⟶ G` and `t : S.toGrp ⟶ D`.
- An actual commutative-group-scheme map `φ : S ⟶ T` whose underlying
  group map is a closed immersion and satisfies
  `CommGroupScheme.groupHom φ ≫ i = t ≫ d ≫ j`.
- Finite root index types `J` and `K` in universe `u`, and reduced
  Mathlib root data `P` and `Q` on the actual character and cocharacter
  lattices of `T` and `S`. They satisfy the published predicates
  `IsRootDatumOf i P` and `IsRootDatumOf t Q`.
- A base `b : P.Base` and a subset `s` of its support such that
  `(Q.corootSpan ℤ).map (RealTorusSpace.cocharacterMapInt φ)` equals
  `Submodule.span ℤ (P.coroot '' s)`.

Here `RealTorusSpace.cocharacterMapInt φ` is integral postcomposition by
the actual torus map: it sends a cocharacter `ν : Gm ⟶ S` to `ν ≫ φ`.
The name of its namespace introduces no real scalar extension. The
closedness of `φ` implies injectivity on these integral lattices by the
existing theorem `RealTorusSpace.cocharacterMapInt_injective`. The
statement asks for no arbitrary replacement lattice or freely chosen
linear map. It asserts the equality of integral spans, rather than only
an equality after tensoring with the rationals.

`IsRootDatumOf` uses the actual lattice pairing, the adjoint roots, and
normalizing points realizing root reflections. The two affineness
instances used by this predicate are supplied respectively by `hG` and
the semisimplicity premise `hD`. The representative `j` has exactly the
public all-test centralizer meaning; no closedness assumption on `j` is
added. The predicate `IsDerivedSubgroup d` retains its closed-subgroup
and all-test commutator meaning.

The proposition is a derived formulation of standard geometric steps
in Brian Conrad, *Reductive group schemes* (2014), registered as
`conrad-2014-reductive-group-schemes`:

- Theorem 5.3.1 and its proof, PDF/printed pp. 160 and 163, construct the
  derived group from its root groups and the subtorus generated by its
  coroots. The proof identifies its root system with that of the
  reductive group. Proposition 5.3.4, p. 164, compares the actual maximal
  tori by intersection with the derived group.
- Proposition 5.4.5 and its proof, pp. 177–178, describe the roots and
  root groups of a Levi subgroup using the ambient roots that vanish
  on a defining cocharacter.
- Exercise 6.5.2(iv), p. 236, describes the choice of a cocharacter and
  a Weyl chamber for the centralizer of a torus, and the resulting
  subset of simple roots. Remark 5.4.7, p. 178, records the
  simple-connectedness application.

This proposition is not a verbatim statement of the exercise: that
exercise starts with a simply connected semisimple group and ends with
simple connectedness of the derived centralizer. Here the ambient
group is only connected reductive, and only the geometric coroot-image
comparison is asserted. The maximal-torus and derived-group
comparisons above give its provenance. A proof must construct the data
and establish the displayed integral equality for the actual supplied
objects, including transport to an arbitrary centralizer representative.

No premise or conclusion says that `G` or `D` is simply connected. In
the intended application, a separate necessity criterion makes the
ambient coroot span full; integral saturation for a subset of the
simple coroots and finiteness of the quotient by the semisimple
coroot lattice then make `Q.corootSpan ℤ` full. A separate sufficiency
criterion yields simple connectedness. Those criteria, the proof of
this comparison, semisimplicity of `D`, and the passage from arbitrary
fields to an algebraic closure remain separate proof obligations.
