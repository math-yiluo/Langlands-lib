# Kernel generation of the cocenter of the minimal Levi subgroup

Authors: Worker 183.

The module `Langlands.GroupScheme.SimplyConnected.LeviCocenterKernelGenerationStatement`
defines one proposition, `Langlands.GroupScheme.LeviCocenterKernelGenerationStatement.{u}`,
and proves nothing.

## Statement

Let `k : Type u` be a field (of any characteristic; finite and imperfect fields are
allowed) and `G` a group scheme over `Spec k` with affine structure morphism and
`IsSimplyConnected G`. Let `i : S ⟶ G` be a maximal split torus
(`IsMaximalSplitTorus i`), `ν` a generic cocharacter of `S`
(`IsGenericCocharacter i ν`), and `j : M ⟶ G` a representative of the dynamic
centralizer of `ν`, so `M = Z_G(S)` is the minimal Levi subgroup. Let
`d : D ⟶ M` exhibit the derived subgroup (`IsDerivedSubgroup d`) and
`q : M ⟶ Q` be an fppf quotient whose actual kernel is the range of `d`
(`RelativeRoot.IsFppfQuotient (SubgroupFunctor.range d) q`), so `Q = M/D(M)` is
the cocenter of `M`.

For each simple relative root `α ∈ simpleRelativeRoots i ν` the statement takes:

- a standard cocharacter `ζ α` for `{α}` (`IsStandardCocharacter i ν {α} (ζ α)`:
  orthogonal to `α`, strictly positive on every other simple relative root);
- a representative `l α : L α ⟶ G` of the dynamic centralizer of `ζ α`; this is
  the standard Levi subgroup of `{α}` (the published
  `standardLevi_eq_cocharacterAnnihilatorCentralizer` identifies it with the
  common centralizer of the cocharacters orthogonal to `α`);
- a homomorphism `m α : M ⟶ L α` with `m α ≫ l α = j`;
- `e α : E α ⟶ L α` with `IsDerivedSubgroup (e α)` and an fppf quotient
  `r α : L α ⟶ R α` with kernel the range of `e α`, so `R α` is the cocenter of
  `L α`;
- a homomorphism `π α : Q ⟶ R α` with `q ≫ π α = m α ≫ r α`.

The conclusion is the inclusion of subgroups of `rationalPoints Q`

```
(rationalPoints.map q).range ≤
  Subgroup.closure (⋃ α, ((rationalPoints.map (π α)).ker : Set (rationalPoints Q)))
```

that is, the image of `M(k)` in `Q(k)` lies in the subgroup generated by the
rational points of the kernels of the maps `π α : Q → R α`.

No hypothesis asks for the data to exist. They do exist: standard cocharacters by
the published `standardCocharacter`, derived subgroups by
`derivedSubgroupRepresentability`, cocenters by `cocenterQuotientExistence`, and
the maps `π α` by `SubgroupFunctor.quotientSubgroupHom`.

## Provenance and mathematical content

This proposition is an internal intermediate. It isolates the absolute
root-theoretic half of `RankOneLeviCocenterGenerationStatement` (module
`SimplyConnected.RankOneLeviFactors`): rank-one Levi factors do not appear in
it. The other half is proved in the module
`SimplyConnected.RankOneLeviCocenterGenerationReduction`. Its theorem
`rankOneLeviCocenterGeneration_of_leviCocenterKernelGeneration` derives
`RankOneLeviCocenterGenerationStatement` from this statement, by showing that the
rational kernel of `π α` lies in the image of the cocenter of `M ∩ A_α`, for a
rank-one Levi factor `A_α`.

The content is the following computation (Prasad–Raghunathan, *On the
Kneser–Tits problem* (1985), registered as `prasad-raghunathan-1985-kneser-tits`,
§1.3 and the proof of Theorem A, printed pp.108 and 110). Take a maximal torus
`T ⊇ S` of `M`, a finite Galois splitting field, and a base `Δ` of the absolute
roots compatible with `ν`; let `Δ₀ ⊆ Δ` be the simple roots vanishing on `S`
(the simple roots of `M`). Since `G` is simply connected, the fundamental
weights `ω_β` (`β ∈ Δ`, dual to the simple coroots: `⟨ω_β, γ^∨⟩ = δ_{βγ}`) form a
basis of the characters of `T`. The characters of `T` pulled back from `Q` are
those vanishing on the coroots of `Δ₀`, i.e. the span of the `ω_β` with
`β ∈ Δ − Δ₀`; the Galois group permutes these weights as it permutes their
indices `β`. Borel–Tits, *Groupes réductifs* (1965), Proposition 6.8 (printed
pp.107–108, registered as `borel-tits-1965-groupes-reductifs`) shows that the
restriction to `S` of every root `β ∈ Δ − Δ₀` is a simple relative root, the same
one for Galois-conjugate `β`. For a simple relative root `α` let
`Δ_α = {β ∈ Δ − Δ₀ : β restricted to S equals α}`; so `Δ − Δ₀` is the disjoint
union of the blocks `Δ_α`. (The condition is on the restriction of the root `β`,
not of the weight `ω_β`.) The simple roots of `L α` are `Δ₀ ∪ Δ_α`. Hence the
characters of `T` pulled back from `R α` are spanned by the `ω_β` with `β` in the
other blocks `Δ_{α'}`, `α' ≠ α`. The character group of `ker(π α)` is the quotient
of the characters of `Q` by this sublattice, with basis the restrictions to
`ker(π α)` of the `ω_β` with `β ∈ Δ_α`; it is torsion-free, so `ker(π α)` is a
torus. Since the blocks partition `Δ − Δ₀`, the restriction map from the characters
of `Q` to the product of the character groups of the kernels is an isomorphism, so
multiplication `∏_α ker(π α) → Q` is an isomorphism of tori defined over `k`.
Taking rational points gives `Q(k) = ∏_α ker(π α)(k)`, which is stronger than the
stated inclusion. For example, for split `SL₃` (`S = T`, `Δ₀ = ∅`,
`Δ_{α_i} = {α_i}`), the characters pulled back from `R α_1` are `ℤ ω_2`, and
`ker(π α_1)` is the coroot torus `{diag(t, t⁻¹, 1)}`.

Simple connectedness is needed: for `G = PGL₃` split, `M = Q = T` and the kernel
of `π α` is the image of the coroot `α^∨`, so the kernels generate the image of
`Q^∨ ⊗ k^×` in `T(k) = P^∨ ⊗ k^×`, of index `|k^×/k^{×3}|` (infinite for
`k = ℚ`).
