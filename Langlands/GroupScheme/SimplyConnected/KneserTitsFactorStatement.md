# Kneser–Tits for the isotropic almost-simple factors of a simply connected group

Authors: Worker 1526.

`Langlands.GroupScheme.SimplyConnectedKneserTitsFactorStatement.{u}` is a
proposition definition, not a proof. Let `G` be a simply connected group over
a nonarchimedean local field `F`, with a maximal split torus `S`. The
proposition states that for every isotropic closed normal almost-simple
subgroup `A` of `G`, the image of `A(F)` in `G(F)` lies in the subgroup
`G(F)^+` generated by the root groups `U_α(F)` of all relative roots `α`
of `S` in `G`.

## The proposition

For every `F : Type u` with `[Field F] [ValuativeRel F] [TopologicalSpace F]
[IsNonarchimedeanLocalField F]`, every
`G : GroupScheme (Spec (CommRingCat.of F))` with `[IsAffineHom G.X.hom]` and
`IsSimplyConnected G`, every `S : CommGroupScheme (Spec (CommRingCat.of F))`
and `i : S.toGrp ⟶ G` with `IsMaximalSplitTorus i`, and every
`A : GroupScheme (Spec (CommRingCat.of F))` and `a : A ⟶ G` with
`IsAlmostSimple A`, `IsClosedSubgroup a`, `(SubgroupFunctor.range a).IsNormal`
and `¬ IsAnisotropic A`,

```
(rationalPoints.map a).range ≤
  Subgroup.closure (⋃ α ∈ relativeRoots i, U_α(F))
```

where `U_α(F)` is `(RelativeRoot.subgroup i α).carrier (Over.mk (𝟙 _))`,
viewed as a subset of `rationalPoints G`.

These are all the quantified data and hypotheses. The universe `u` is
arbitrary. The field `F` has no characteristic hypothesis, and `G` is not
assumed quasi-split, split or `F`-simple. No new definition is introduced.
`Subgroup.closure` is the subgroup generated by a set, not a topological
closure.

## Exact objects

- `IsNonarchimedeanLocalField F` (Mathlib, `Mathlib.NumberTheory.LocalField.Basic`)
  says that `F` is a locally compact, nontrivially valued field whose
  topology is the valuation topology.
- `IsSimplyConnected G` (module `Langlands.GroupScheme.SimplyConnected.Basic`)
  says that `G` is semisimple and that every central isogeny onto `G` from a
  semisimple group is an isomorphism.
- `IsMaximalSplitTorus i` (module `Langlands.GroupScheme.Rational.SplitTorus`)
  says that `i` is a closed immersion of a split torus and that every
  factorization of `i` through another closed split torus is an isomorphism.
- `IsAlmostSimple A` (module `Langlands.GroupScheme.Reductive.AlmostSimple`)
  says that `A` is semisimple, is not commutative, and that every smooth
  connected closed subgroup of `A` with normal range has range trivial or all
  of `A`. This is Milne's definition 19.7 of an almost-simple group over `F`,
  so for semisimple groups it means `F`-simple in the sense of PR85 §1.1, not
  absolutely simple.
- `IsClosedSubgroup a` (module `Langlands.GroupScheme.Reductive.Basic`) says
  that the underlying morphism of schemes of `a` is a closed immersion.
- `(SubgroupFunctor.range a).IsNormal` (module
  `Langlands.GroupScheme.SubgroupFunctor`) says that on every test scheme `T`
  the image of `A(T)` in `G(T)` is a normal subgroup.
- `IsAnisotropic A` (module `Langlands.GroupScheme.Rational.SplitTorus`) says
  that the split rank `splitRank A` is `0`, that is, `A` has no closed split
  subtorus of positive dimension. So `¬ IsAnisotropic A` says that `A` is
  `F`-isotropic.
- `rationalPoints G` (module `Langlands.GroupScheme.Rational.Characters`) is
  the group `G(F)` of sections of `G` over `Spec F`, and
  `rationalPoints.map a : A(F) →* G(F)` is composition with `a`.
- `relativeRoots i` (module `Langlands.GroupScheme.Rational.RelativeRoots`)
  is the set of nonzero characters of `S` with a nonzero weight space in the
  Lie algebra of `G`.
- `RelativeRoot.subgroup i α` (module `Langlands.GroupScheme.Rational.RootSubgroup`)
  is the subgroup functor of points `g` whose orbit map `s ↦ i(s) g i(s)⁻¹`
  factors as `s ↦ e(α(s))` for a morphism `e` from the affine line with
  `e(0) = 1`. Its `F`-points contain the standard root group `U_{(α)}(F)`:
  on these elements the orbit map is a polynomial in `α(s)` without
  constant term. A larger `U_α(F)` only enlarges the right-hand side, so the
  proposition is no stronger than its standard form.

## Source and derived formulation

G. Prasad and M. S. Raghunathan, *On the Kneser–Tits problem*, Comment.
Math. Helv. 60 (1985), 107–121, registered as
`prasad-raghunathan-1985-kneser-tits`. The PDF has one cover page, so
printed page = PDF page + 105.

- §1.1 and the introduction, printed p. 107: for a semisimple, simply
  connected, `k`-simple, `k`-isotropic group `G`, `G(k)^+` is the normal
  subgroup of `G(k)` generated by the `k`-rational points of the unipotent
  radicals of the parabolic `k`-subgroups. The Kneser–Tits problem asks
  whether `G(k)^+ = G(k)`.
- §1.5, printed p. 108: a semisimple simply connected `k`-group is the
  direct product of its connected `k`-simple normal subgroups.
- §1.6, printed p. 109: by the Bruhat decomposition, `G(k) = M(k) · G(k)^+`
  for `M = Z_G(S)`.
- §2, printed p. 111, with the reduction to `k`-rank one of the
  introduction and §1.6 (Theorem A): the Kneser–Tits problem has an
  affirmative solution over every nonarchimedean local field `k`, of
  arbitrary characteristic. That is, `G(k)^+ = G(k)` for every `k`-simple,
  simply connected group `G` of positive `k`-rank.

V. Platonov and A. Rapinchuk, *Algebraic Groups and Number Theory*,
Academic Press, 1994, registered as
`platonov-rapinchuk-1994-algebraic-groups-number-theory`, Section 7.2,
Theorem 7.6, printed p. 414 / PDF p. 424: the same Kneser–Tits theorem over
nonarchimedean local fields, under the book's characteristic-zero
convention (footnote, printed p. 5). Tasho Kaletha and Gopal Prasad,
*Bruhat–Tits Theory: A New Approach*, registered as
`kaletha-prasad-2023-bruhat-tits-new-approach`, printed p. 76 / PDF p. 107,
after Proposition 2.2.14: `G(k)^+ = G(k)` for a non-discrete locally compact
field `k` and a `k`-isotropic simply connected `k`-simple `G`, citing [PR85].

J. S. Milne, *Algebraic Groups: The Theory of Group Schemes of Finite Type
over a Field*, Cambridge University Press, corrected reprint 2022, registered
as `milne-2022-algebraic-groups`. Printed page = PDF page − 11.

- Theorem 21.51, printed pp. 439–441 / PDF pp. 450–452: a semisimple group
  `G` over a field is the almost-direct product of its finitely many
  almost-simple normal subgroups `G_1, …, G_r`, and every smooth connected
  normal subgroup of `G` is the product of the `G_i` it contains.
- Printed p. 236 / PDF p. 247, after Definition 12.14: a quotient of a split
  torus is a split torus.

The proposition is a derived formulation and not a numbered result. The
following standard argument is its provenance; it is not proved here.

1. The image of `a` is a smooth connected normal subgroup of `G`, since `A`
   is semisimple. By Milne 21.51 it is the product of the almost-simple
   normal subgroups `G_i` it contains. A product of two or more of them
   would give `A` a proper nontrivial smooth connected normal subgroup, and
   the empty product is excluded because `A` is not commutative. So `a` is
   an isomorphism of `A` onto one factor `G_1`. Since `G` is simply
   connected, the multiplication map `G_1 × ⋯ × G_r → G` is an isomorphism,
   as in step 2 of the Markdown of
   `Langlands.GroupScheme.SimplyConnectedAlmostSimpleRationalGenerationStatement`
   (PR85 §1.5). Write `G = A × G'` with `G' = G_2 × ⋯ × G_r`.
2. `A` is simply connected: if `Ã → A` is a central isogeny from a
   semisimple group, then `Ã × G' → A × G' = G` is one too, hence an
   isomorphism. `A` is `F`-simple by `IsAlmostSimple A`, and `F`-isotropic
   by `¬ IsAnisotropic A`. So Kneser–Tits (PR85 §2) gives
   `A(F) = A(F)^+`.
3. The projections of `S` to `A` and to `G'` are split tori (Milne,
   printed p. 236), and `S` lies in their product, a closed split torus of
   `G`. By maximality, `S = S_A × S'` with `S_A` a maximal split torus of
   `A`. The factor `S'` commutes with `A`, so it acts trivially on the Lie
   algebra of `A`. Each relative root `β` of `S_A` in `A` therefore gives the
   character `α = (β, 0)` of `S`, which lies in `relativeRoots i`. On the
   standard root group `U_{(β)} ⊆ A` the conjugation action of `s ∈ S` is
   that of its component in `S_A`, a polynomial in `α(s)` without constant
   term. So `U_{(β)}(F) ⊆ U_α(F)`.
4. As in step 3 of the Markdown of
   `Langlands.GroupScheme.SimplyConnectedKneserTitsCentralizerStatement`,
   the subgroup `E` of `A(F)` generated by the groups `U_{(β)}(F)` contains
   `A(F)^+`. It contains the unipotent radicals of the two opposite minimal
   parabolic subgroups containing `S_A`. The Bruhat decomposition, with Weyl
   representatives in `E`, gives `A(F) = E · Z_A(S_A)(F)`, and the
   centralizer normalizes every `U_{(β)}`, so `E` is normal in `A(F)`.
   Every parabolic subgroup is `A(F)`-conjugate to a standard one, so
   `A(F)^+ ⊆ E`.
5. By steps 2–4, `A(F) = A(F)^+ ⊆ E`, and by step 3 the image of `E` in
   `G(F)` lies in the subgroup generated by the `U_α(F)`.

This module supplies no proof. The proof is a separate obligation.

Intended use (a remark, not proved in this module): together with
`Langlands.GroupScheme.SimplyConnectedAlmostSimpleRationalGenerationStatement`
and `Langlands.GroupScheme.SimplyConnectedAnisotropicFactorCentralizerStatement`,
this proposition is an input for deriving
`Langlands.GroupScheme.SimplyConnectedKneserTitsCentralizerStatement`.
