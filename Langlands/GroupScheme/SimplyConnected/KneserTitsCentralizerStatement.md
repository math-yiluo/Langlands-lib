# Kneser–Tits for split-torus centralizers in simply connected groups

Authors: Worker 1526.

`Langlands.GroupScheme.SimplyConnectedKneserTitsCentralizerStatement.{u}`
is a proposition definition, not a proof. Let `G` be a simply connected group
over a nonarchimedean local field `F`, with a maximal split torus `S` and the
represented centralizer `Z = Z_G(S)`. The proposition states that every
rational point of `Z` is a product of a point of `Z(F) ∩ G(F)^+` and a point
of the norm-one subgroup `Z(F)^1`. Here `G(F)^+` is the subgroup of `G(F)`
generated by the root groups `U_a(F)` of all relative roots `a`.

## The proposition

For every `F : Type u` with `[Field F] [ValuativeRel F] [TopologicalSpace F]
[IsNonarchimedeanLocalField F]`, every `G : GroupScheme (Spec (CommRingCat.of F))`
with `[IsAffineHom G.X.hom]` and `IsSimplyConnected G`, every
`S : CommGroupScheme (Spec (CommRingCat.of F))` and `i : S.toGrp ⟶ G` with
`IsMaximalSplitTorus i`, and every `Z : GroupScheme (Spec (CommRingCat.of F))`
and `j : Z ⟶ G` with `(torusCentralizer i).IsRepresentedBy j`, the range of
`rationalPoints.map j : Z(F) →* G(F)` is contained in

```
(centralizerPoints i ⊓ Subgroup.closure (⋃ a ∈ relativeRoots i, U_a(F)))
  ⊔ (Plancherel.rationalCommonNormKernel Z).map (rationalPoints.map j)
```

where `U_a(F)` is `(RelativeRoot.subgroup i a).carrier (Over.mk (𝟙 _))`,
viewed as a subset of `rationalPoints G`. These are all the quantified data
and hypotheses, the same as those of
`Langlands.GroupScheme.SimplyConnectedRankOneCentralizerGenerationStatement`.
The universe `u` is arbitrary. The field `F` has no characteristic
hypothesis, and `G` is not assumed quasi-split, split or `F`-simple. No new
definition is introduced. The subgroup `G(F)^+` is written out as
`Subgroup.closure (⋃ a ∈ relativeRoots i, U_a(F))`.

## Exact objects

- `IsSimplyConnected G` (module `Langlands.GroupScheme.SimplyConnected.Basic`)
  says that `G` is semisimple and that every central isogeny onto `G` from a
  semisimple group is an isomorphism.
- `IsMaximalSplitTorus i` (module `Langlands.GroupScheme.Rational.SplitTorus`)
  says that `i` is a split subtorus and that every factorization of `i`
  through another split subtorus is an isomorphism.
- `(torusCentralizer i).IsRepresentedBy j` says that `j` is a monomorphism
  whose range on every test scheme is the centralizer functor of `i`. So `Z`
  is `Z_G(S)` and `rationalPoints.map j` is injective with range
  `centralizerPoints i`.
- `centralizerPoints i` (module `Langlands.GroupScheme.Torus.Maximal`) is the
  subgroup of `G(F)` of points normalizing `i` through the identity of `S`,
  that is, the rational points of `Z_G(S)`.
- `relativeRoots i` (module `Langlands.GroupScheme.Rational.RelativeRoots`)
  is the set of nonzero characters of `S` with a nonzero weight space in the
  Lie algebra of `G`.
- `RelativeRoot.subgroup i a` (module `Langlands.GroupScheme.Rational.RootSubgroup`)
  is the subgroup functor of points `g` whose orbit map `s ↦ i(s) g i(s)⁻¹`
  factors as `s ↦ e(a(s))` for a morphism `e` from the affine line with
  `e(0) = 1`. Its `F`-points contain the standard root group `U_{(a)}(F)`:
  on these elements the orbit map is a polynomial in `a(s)` without
  constant term. So `Subgroup.closure (⋃ a ∈ relativeRoots i, U_a(F))`
  contains the subgroup generated by all `U_{(a)}(F)`. A larger `U_a(F)` only
  enlarges the right-hand side, so the proposition is no stronger than its
  standard form.
- `Plancherel.rationalCommonNormKernel Z` (module
  `Langlands.Representation.Plancherel.RationalCharacterValuation`) is the
  subgroup `Z(F)^1` of points `z` with `|χ(z)| = 1` for every rational
  character `χ` of `Z`.

## Source and derived formulation

G. Prasad and M. S. Raghunathan, *On the Kneser–Tits problem*, Comment.
Math. Helv. 60 (1985), 107–121, registered as
`prasad-raghunathan-1985-kneser-tits`. The PDF has one cover page, so
printed page = PDF page + 105.

- §1.1 and the introduction, printed p. 107: for a semisimple, simply
  connected, `k`-simple, `k`-isotropic group `G`, `G(k)^+` is the normal
  subgroup of `G(k)` generated by the `k`-rational points of the unipotent
  radicals of the parabolic `k`-subgroups. The Kneser–Tits problem asks
  whether `G(k)^+ = G(k)`.
- §1.5, printed p. 108: a semisimple simply connected `k`-group is the
  direct product of its connected `k`-simple normal subgroups.
- §1.6, printed p. 109: by the Bruhat decomposition, `G(k) = M(k) · G(k)^+`
  for `M = Z_G(S)`.
- §2, printed p. 111, with the reduction to `k`-rank one of the
  introduction and §1.6 (Theorem A): the Kneser–Tits problem has an
  affirmative solution over every nonarchimedean local field `k`, of
  arbitrary characteristic. That is, `G(k)^+ = G(k)` for every `k`-simple,
  simply connected group `G` of positive `k`-rank.

V. Platonov and A. Rapinchuk, *Algebraic Groups and Number Theory*,
Academic Press, 1994, registered as
`platonov-rapinchuk-1994-algebraic-groups-number-theory`, Section 7.2,
Theorem 7.6, printed p. 414: the same Kneser–Tits theorem over
nonarchimedean local fields, under the book's characteristic-zero
convention. Tasho Kaletha and Gopal Prasad, *Bruhat–Tits Theory: A New
Approach*, registered as `kaletha-prasad-2023-bruhat-tits-new-approach`,
printed p. 76 / PDF p. 107, after Proposition 2.2.14: `G(k)^+ = G(k)` for a
non-discrete locally compact field `k` and a `k`-isotropic simply connected
`G`, citing [PR85].

The proposition is a derived formulation and not a numbered result. The
following standard argument is its provenance; it is not proved here.

1. `G` is the direct product of `F`-simple simply connected factors `G_k`
   (PR85 §1.5). The torus `S` is the product of the maximal split tori
   `S_k` of the factors, and every relative root of `G` is a relative root
   of exactly one factor, with its root group in that factor.
2. Let `G_iso` be the product of the isotropic factors and `G_an` the
   product of the anisotropic ones. The torus `S` lies in `G_iso`, so `G_an`
   centralizes `S` and `G_an ⊆ Z`. Every rational character of `Z` is
   trivial on the semisimple group `G_an`, so `G_an(F) ⊆ Z(F)^1`.
3. For an isotropic factor `G_k`, the subgroup `E_k` generated by the groups
   `U_{(a)}(F)` for the relative roots `a` of `G_k` equals `G_k(F)^+`. It
   contains the unipotent radicals of the two opposite minimal parabolic
   subgroups containing `S_k`. The Bruhat decomposition, with Weyl
   representatives in `E_k`, gives `G_k(F) = E_k · Z_{G_k}(S_k)(F)`. The
   centralizer normalizes every `U_{(a)}`, so `E_k` is normal in `G_k(F)`.
   Every parabolic subgroup is `G_k(F)`-conjugate to a standard one, so
   `G_k(F)^+ ⊆ E_k`. By Kneser–Tits, `G_k(F) = G_k(F)^+ = E_k`.
4. Write `z ∈ Z(F)` as `z = x · y` with `x ∈ G_iso(F)` and `y ∈ G_an(F)`.
   Then `y ∈ Z(F)^1` by step 2. By step 3, `x` lies in the subgroup
   generated by all `U_{(a)}(F)`, and `x = z y⁻¹ ∈ Z(F)`. So
   `z ∈ (Z(F) ∩ G(F)^+) · Z(F)^1`.

This module supplies no proof. The proof is a separate obligation.

Relation to the rank-one statement (a remark, not proved in this module):
`Langlands.GroupScheme.SimplyConnectedRankOneCentralizerGenerationStatement`
implies the present proposition, because each of its generators
`Z(F) ∩ ⟨U_a(F), U_{-a}(F)⟩` lies in `Z(F) ∩ G(F)^+`. The present
proposition is the Kneser–Tits half of a proof of that statement. The other
half says that `Z(F) ∩ G(F)^+` is generated by the groups
`Z(F) ∩ ⟨U_a(F), U_{-a}(F)⟩`. It is a separate group-theoretic statement
and needs no local-field input. The anisotropic case of the present proposition,
for any connected reductive anisotropic `G`, is covered by the published
`Langlands.GroupScheme.IsAnisotropic.map_normKernel_torusCentralizer_eq_range`.
