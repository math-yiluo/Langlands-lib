# Kneser–Tits for split-torus centralizers from the almost-simple factors

Authors: Worker 1526.

Module `Langlands.GroupScheme.SimplyConnected.KneserTitsCentralizerReduction`.

## The statement

`Langlands.GroupScheme.SimplyConnectedKneserTitsCentralizerReductionStatement.{u}`
is the proposition

```text
SimplyConnectedAlmostSimpleRationalGenerationStatement.{u} →
  SimplyConnectedKneserTitsFactorStatement.{u} →
  SimplyConnectedAnisotropicFactorCentralizerStatement.{u} →
  SimplyConnectedKneserTitsCentralizerStatement.{u}
```

All four propositions are published and unchanged. The theorem
`Langlands.GroupScheme.simplyConnectedKneserTitsCentralizer_of_factors`
proves this implication for every universe `u`; inside the proof the three
hypotheses are named `hA`, `hB` and `hC`. The three hypotheses are explicit
assumptions: this module proves none of them. It is a conditional result, not
an unconditional proof of `SimplyConnectedKneserTitsCentralizerStatement`.
Once the three hypotheses are proved, the conclusion follows by applying this
theorem to their proofs.

The hypotheses say, for a simply connected group `G`:

- `hA`: over every field `k`, every subgroup of `G(k)` containing the image
  of `A(k)` for every closed normal almost-simple subgroup `a : A ⟶ G` is all
  of `G(k)`;
- `hB`: over every nonarchimedean local field `F`, for affine `G` and every
  maximal split torus `i : S ⟶ G`, the image of `A(F)` for every isotropic
  such `a` lies in `G(F)^+`, the subgroup generated by the carriers
  `U_α(F)` of the Library root groups `RelativeRoot.subgroup i α` of all
  relative roots `α ∈ relativeRoots i`;
- `hC`: over every field `k`, for every maximal split torus `i : S ⟶ G`,
  every anisotropic such `a` has range contained in `torusCentralizer i` on
  every test scheme.

The conclusion keeps every binder of the target: every nonarchimedean local
field `F`, every affine simply connected `G`, every maximal split torus `i`
and every represented centralizer `j : Z ⟶ G` of `i`, with arbitrary
universe `u`.

## The Lean proof

Introduce `hA`, `hB`, `hC` and then the data of the conclusion; fix `F`,
`G`, `i` and `j`.

1. Let `K` be the supremum in `G(F)` of the ranges `R p` of
   `rationalPoints.map a` over all isotropic closed normal almost-simple
   subgroups `p = (A, a)`. The index type is a subtype of
   `Σ A, A ⟶ G`. Each `R p` is normal in `G(F)`, because
   `(SubgroupFunctor.range a).IsNormal` gives normality at the base test
   scheme. So `K` is normal by `Subgroup.iSup_normal`.
2. Let `N` be the image of `Plancherel.rationalCommonNormKernel Z` under
   `rationalPoints.map j`. The range of `rationalPoints.map j` is
   `centralizerPoints i`, by `SubgroupFunctor.rationalPoints_eq_range` and
   `torusCentralizer_carrier_base`.
3. `K ⊔ N = ⊤`, by `hA` applied to `K ⊔ N`. An isotropic factor's range lies
   in `K` by `le_iSup`. For an anisotropic factor `a : A ⟶ G`, `hC` gives
   `SubgroupFunctor.range a ≤ torusCentralizer i`. So `a` factors as
   `f ≫ j` with `f : A ⟶ Z`, by `IsRepresentedBy.liftOfLE` and
   `IsRepresentedBy.liftOfLE_comp`. Since `A` is anisotropic and connected
   reductive (it is semisimple), `IsAnisotropic.rationalCommonNormKernel_eq_top`
   gives `A(F)^1 = A(F)`. By `Plancherel.rationalCommonNormKernel_le_comap`,
   `f` maps `A(F)^1` into `Z(F)^1`. So the range of `a` on `F`-points lies
   in `N`.
4. `K` lies in `Subgroup.closure (⋃ α ∈ relativeRoots i, U_α(F))`, by
   `iSup_le` and `hB` applied to each isotropic factor.
5. Let `z ∈ Z(F)`. By step 3, `j(z) ∈ K ⊔ N`. Since `K` is normal,
   `Subgroup.mem_sup_of_normal_left` writes `j(z) = x y` with `x ∈ K` and
   `y ∈ N`. The point `y` lies in the range of `rationalPoints.map j`, so
   `x = j(z) y⁻¹` does too, hence `x ∈ centralizerPoints i` by step 2. By
   step 4, `x` lies in the subgroup generated by the root groups. So
   `j(z) = x y` lies in
   `(centralizerPoints i ⊓ Subgroup.closure (⋃ α ∈ relativeRoots i, U_α(F))) ⊔ N`,
   by `Subgroup.mul_mem_sup`.

The proof uses only the axioms `propext`, `Classical.choice` and
`Quot.sound`.

## Provenance

The provenance is internal. The argument is a group-theoretic assembly of
the three hypotheses with the published Library results named above. It
follows steps 2 and 4 of the derivation in the Markdown of
`Langlands.GroupScheme.SimplyConnectedKneserTitsCentralizerStatement`. That
derivation splits `G` as a direct product of isotropic and anisotropic
factors. The Lean proof avoids a product decomposition: it uses only that the
factors generate `G(F)` (`hA`) and that the subgroup `K` they generate is
normal.
