# Trivial unipotent radical of the actual odd identity component

For at least two hyperbolic indices, the actual identity component of the
split odd norm-one group has trivial unipotent radical over every field.
Together with its proved smoothness and affineness and the connectedness
of the identity component, this supplies an `IsPseudoReductive` object.

Let a smooth connected unipotent normal closed subgroup act in the actual
standard representation. Unipotence gives a nonzero fixed vector.
Normality under the identity component makes the fixed subspace stable
under the connected even elementary images and the short images, with
their exact matrices on all tests. The invariant-subspace lemma therefore
puts the whole hyperbolic hyperplane in the fixed subspace. The smooth
universal-test triviality theorem shows that its actual map into the odd
group is trivial. The identity-component inclusion is monic, so the
subgroup range inside that component is the trivial subgroup functor.

The argument includes imperfect characteristic two: it uses the
hyperbolic hyperplane conclusion rather than irreducibility of the odd
standard representation, and uses reducedness only on the smooth
subgroup's universal test. All subgroup and normality statements retain
their meanings on arbitrary test schemes.

This is an internal proof assembled from the preceding actual generator,
invariant-subspace and reduced-test results. It does not yet transport
to the algebraic closure to assert geometric reductivity, identify
coroots, or prove connectedness of the entire odd group. No new external
literature is translated.
