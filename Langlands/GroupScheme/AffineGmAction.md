# Laurent coordinates for multiplicative group actions

This internal construction extends the published affine coordinate and natural
character action methods to an arbitrary affine scheme over `Spec R`. Its
coordinate algebra is chosen from the essential image of relative spectrum, with
an actual isomorphism to the supplied scheme and a natural equivalence of affine
test points. No group law on the target scheme is assumed.

`GmCoordinateAction.Action` is a natural action of the unit groups of arbitrary
commutative `R`-algebras on the affine point functor of an algebra. Evaluating at
the universal Laurent unit gives its coaction. The action laws imply the counit
and coassociativity identities and hence homogeneity of each Laurent coefficient.
The coaction preserves the base algebra structure.

The maps used in these identities are actual algebra morphisms. `evaluate x t`
evaluates coefficients along `x` and the Laurent variable at `t`;
`coefficientMap f` applies `f` coefficientwise; `diagonalMap B` substitutes the
product of two independent Laurent variables. Naturality at the universal unit
expresses every action as the coaction followed by evaluation. Applying the
unit and multiplication action laws gives the counit and coassociativity
identities. Taking an outer Laurent coefficient of coassociativity proves that
each coefficient of degree `n` is itself homogeneous of degree `n`.

`unitPoint` uses `Gm.affinePointsEquiv` to turn an algebra unit into an actual
point of the chosen multiplicative group scheme. Its unit, multiplication and
refinement lemmas use that multiplicative equivalence and its compatibility
with pullback of global sections. `ofPointwiseSchemeAction` consequently
transfers any actual `PointwiseSchemeAction` of `Gm` on an arbitrary affine
scheme to its coordinate algebra. `pointEquiv_schemeAct` states compatibility
with the supplied action on actual scheme points.

The fixed ideal is generated by all Laurent coefficients of
`coaction(b) - C(b)`. `fixedIdeal_le_ker_iff_isFixed` identifies its vanishing
with fixedness after every algebra refinement. In one direction, coefficient
vanishing makes the universal action constant, and evaluation gives fixedness
under every unit. In the other direction, the universal unit on the Laurent
algebra detects all the coefficient equations.

`isFixed_iff_schemeAction` upgrades this condition to fixedness after every
relative scheme refinement. It applies
`PointwiseSchemeAction.isFixed_iff_affineRefinements`; the actual `Gm` model is
affine over an affine base since its defining scheme is a pullback of affine
schemes. Every acting point on an affine refinement is the image of a unit.
Thus `schemeActionFixedIdeal_le_ker_iff` equates vanishing of the coefficient
ideal with the actual all-scheme fixedness condition. These statements do not
restrict tests to fields or reduced algebras.

`formallySmooth_fixedQuotient` applies the published graded fixed-quotient
lifting argument to the coaction's scalar and homogeneous-coefficient
identities. It concludes formal smoothness over `R` of the coordinate quotient
whenever the original coordinate algebra is formally smooth over `R`. It does
not claim finite presentation or scheme smoothness.

The construction uses Laurent polynomials indexed by the ordinary integers for
an algebra in any universe. It does not identify two differently chosen
representing group schemes. The argument is an internal specialization of the
universal-character argument in `Torus/CharacterActionGrading.lean`, using
Mathlib's Laurent evaluation and the actual multiplicative group point
equivalence. The scheme adapter and all-refinement comparison generalize the
method of `Torus/AffineSchemeAction.lean` by removing its target group-law
assumption. No new external source theorem is asserted.

This module constructs neither an attracting scheme nor a fixed scheme. It does
not prove closedness of parabolic orbits, affine reductive quotients or properness
of the general supplied dynamic quotient. Its arguments use no
regular-cocharacter/Borel or rank-one input.
