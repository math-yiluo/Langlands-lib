# Actual attracting schemes of affine multiplicative-group actions

Let R be any commutative ring and X an affine scheme over Spec R, in any
universe. A supplied actual action s of the chosen multiplicative group on X
has an orbit morphism on every relative test T. `s.IsAttracted x` means that
this orbit extends to a morphism from T times the affine line. Refining T by
any relative scheme preserves this condition.

In the chosen coordinates B of X, the actual Laurent coaction c defines the
ideal generated by every coefficient of c(b) of strictly negative degree.
`attractingAlgebra` is B modulo this ideal. `polynomialOrbit` constructs an
actual algebra map B to C[t] for any B-to-C map killing the ideal; inclusion
in C[t,t⁻¹] recovers the original coaction orbit, and its coefficient of tⁿ
is the image of the degree-n coaction coefficient.

The quotient spectrum `schemeActionAttractingScheme` maps by the closed
immersion `schemeActionAttractingHom` into the same supplied X. It represents
attraction on every relative test scheme: a morphism x factors through this
inclusion if and only if `s.IsAttracted x`. Factorizations are unique because
the inclusion is a monomorphism. No field, reducedness, characteristic or
noetherian assumption is imposed, and tests need not be affine.

The proof first compares the polynomial and Laurent relative spectra with
actual affine-line and multiplicative-group products. The universal Laurent
unit identifies the coordinate coaction orbit with the actual scheme orbit.
A Laurent polynomial is a polynomial exactly when its negative coefficients
vanish, which proves the criterion on algebra spectra. The quotient universal
property supplies the affine factorization. Coordinate isomorphisms transport
it to any affine relative test; an affine open cover and uniqueness of local
factors glue the result on an arbitrary test. Applying the criterion to the
quotient inclusion gives `schemeActionAttractingExtension`, an actual affine-line
extension whose restriction is the orbit of that inclusion.

If X is smooth over Spec R, the constructed attracting scheme is smooth over
Spec R. The coaction counit identifies the sum of its coefficients with b,
and the action laws make each coefficient homogeneous. The published diagonal
averaging theorem gives formal smoothness of the negative quotient. The
published finite-generation theorem for its negative ideal, applied to the
smooth coordinate algebra, gives finite presentation, hence smoothness. The
spectrum criterion transfers this back to the actual scheme.

This is an internal construction from affine coordinates, polynomial and
Laurent universal properties, quotient gluing and the existing graded smoothness
lemmas. It generalizes the affine-group attracting method to arbitrary affine
targets without imposing a target group law. This unit does not construct the
limit map to the fixed scheme, prove orbit closedness or reductive quotient
affineness, or prove `DynamicQuotientProperStatement`.
