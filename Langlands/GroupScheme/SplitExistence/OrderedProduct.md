# `U⁺` is the ordered product of the positive root groups

Author: Worker 1398.

`posList` lists the positive roots once each, sorted by increasing `ℓ` (Lean core
`List.mergeSort`); `orderedProd l c = ∏_{j ∈ l} x_j(c j)`. The main result
`exists_orderedProd_of_mem_posSub` says that every element of `U⁺(K)` is
`orderedProd posList c` for some `c` (Steinberg, *Lectures on Chevalley groups*, §3, Lemma 17).

Proof. `U_{≥m}` (`rootsSub (posGe m)`) is generated by the positive root groups of level
`ℓ ≥ m`. `commutator_mem_rootsSub` turns the `commutator` field of the datum (for any `h`
positive on both roots) into membership of the commutator in the subgroup generated by the root
groups of the roots that can occur. With `h = ℓ`, commutators of positive root elements of
levels `a, b` lie in `U_{≥a+b}` (`commutator_mem_posGe`), so `U⁺` normalizes every
`U_{≥m}` (`posSub_le_normalizer_posGe`). In a sorted list the roots of level `≥ m` are those of
level `m` followed by those of level `≥ m + 1` (`List.filter_ge_eq_append`). `level_step`: on a
list of distinct roots of level `m ≥ 1`, left multiplication by `x_δ(s)` adds `s` to the
coefficient of `δ`, up to a right factor in `U_{≥m+1}`, because the commutators that arise have
level `≥ 2m`. A descending induction on `m` (`opGe_step`), using closure induction on the left,
shows that `U_{≥m}` consists of the ordered products over the roots of level `≥ m`; the bound
`M` on levels starts it, and level `1` is all of `U⁺`. Internal.
