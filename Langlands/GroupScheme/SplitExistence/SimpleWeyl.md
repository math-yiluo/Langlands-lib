# Integral Weyl elements of simple `SL₂`-data (`SimpleWeyl`)

Author: Worker 239.

Fix a root datum `P : RootDatum J X Y` with base `b` and integral `SL₂`-data
`S : SimpleRootData P b`. This module builds integral Weyl elements from `S`. It proves that
conjugation by them preserves the rational Lie algebra generated by the simple root operators.
It deduces that every root weight space of that Lie algebra is a line.

## Preliminaries

- `IsWtHom.conj_linearEquiv`: `IsWtHom.conj` (from `WeightGrading`) for a linear automorphism
  `f` of `X` and its inverse.
- `intCast_ulift_injective`: the cast `ℤ → ULift ℤ` is injective. The homomorphisms `φ i R`
  of `S` are only given for rings `R` in the universe `u`, so integral statements are
  transferred through `ULift.{u} ℤ`.
- `sl2Weyl_eq_upper_lower_upper`, `sl2Weyl_inv_eq_upper_lower_upper`, `sl2Weyl_inv_eq_cube`:
  in `SL₂(R)`, `[[0, 1], [−1, 0]] = u(1) l(−1) u(1)`, its inverse is `u(−1) l(1) u(−1)`, and the
  inverse is also its cube. Here `u(s) = sl2Upper s` and `l(s) = sl2Lower s`.
- `isDividedPowerFamily_E`, `isDividedPowerFamily_F`, `isWtHom_E`, `isWtHom_F`: the fields of
  `S` restated in the vocabulary of `RootExp` and `WeightGrading`.

## The rational Lie algebra

- `corootChar i : X →+ ℚ` is the character `μ ↦ ⟨μ, α_i^∨⟩`.
- `toLieGens : LieGens S.wt b.support` takes `α i = α_i`, `e i = E i 1`, `f i = F i 1` (over
  `ℚ`) and `χ = corootChar`. Uniqueness of coefficients is `coeff_eq_of_sum_eq`, and the
  relations `[e i, f j]` are the fields `E_F_same` and `E_F_comm`. The lemmas `toLieGens_e`,
  `toLieGens_f` and `toLieGens_α` record the fields.
- `divExp_E_conj_mem`, `divExp_F_conj_mem`: conjugation by `divExp (E i) s` or
  `divExp (F i) s` (`s ∈ ℚ`) preserves `lieSpan`. This is `divExp_conj_mem` of `RootLieConj`,
  since `matAd (e i)` and `matAd (f i)` preserve `lieSpan` (`RootLieAlgebra`).

## Weyl elements

- `weylMat i` is the integral matrix `divExp (E i) 1 · divExp (F i) (−1) · divExp (E i) 1`, and
  `weylMatInv i` is `divExp (E i) (−1) · divExp (F i) 1 · divExp (E i) (−1)`. The lemmas
  `weylMat_mul_weylMatInv` and `weylMatInv_mul_weylMat` show that they are mutually inverse,
  by cancelling `divExp D s · divExp D (−s) = 1` (`divExp_mul` from `RootExp`).
- `map_weylMat_eq`, `map_weylMatInv_eq`: their images over any commutative ring are the
  corresponding products of exponentials.
- `map_weylMat`, `map_weylMatInv`: over every commutative ring `R : Type u`, they are the images
  of `sl2Weyl` and `sl2Weyl⁻¹` under `φ i R`, by the factorizations above and the fields
  `φ_upper`, `φ_lower`.
- `mapsWt_weylMat`, `mapsWt_weylMatInv`: they move weights by `s_i` and `s_i⁻¹`, from the
  field `weyl_weight` at `R = ULift ℤ`.
- `isWtHom_weylMat_conj`: conjugation by `weylMat i` sends weight `μ` to `s_i μ`.
- `weylMat_conj_mem`, `weylMatInv_conj_mem`: conjugation by these elements preserves
  `lieSpan`. It is a composite of three conjugations by exponentials.

## Weyl words

- `wordMat w = n_{i₁} ⋯ n_{i_m}` and `wordMatInv w = n_{i_m}⁻¹ ⋯ n_{i₁}⁻¹` for a word
  `w = [i₁, …, i_m]`. The lemmas `wordMatInv_mul_wordMat` and `wordMat_mul_wordMatInv` show
  that they are mutually inverse.
- `mapsWt_wordMat`, `mapsWt_wordMatInv`, `isWtHom_wordMat_conj`, `isWtHom_wordMatInv_conj`:
  they move weights by `weylWord P b w` and its inverse.
- `wordMat_conj_mem`, `wordMatInv_conj_mem`: conjugation by them preserves `lieSpan`.
- `wordMat_conj_wordMatInv_conj`, `wordMatInv_conj_wordMat_conj`: the two conjugations undo
  each other over `ℚ`.

## Root lines

`exists_line_of_root` (for finite `J` and reduced `P`): for every root `j` there is a nonzero
`y ∈ lieSpan` of weight `α_j` such that every element of `lieSpan` of weight `α_j` is a rational
multiple of `y`.

Proof: write `α_j = weylWord P b w α_i` for a simple root `i` (`exists_weylWord_root`), and take
`y = wordMat w · e i · wordMatInv w`. It lies in `lieSpan` and has weight `α_j`. It is nonzero
because `E i 1` is nonzero modulo `2`, hence nonzero over `ℚ`, and conjugation is invertible.
An element `x` of weight `α_j` conjugates back to an element of weight `α_i`, which is a
multiple of `e i` by `exists_eq_smul_e_of_isWtHom`.

## Provenance

The Weyl element `n_α = x_α(1) x_{−α}(−1) x_α(1)` and the conjugation of root spaces by it are
standard (Steinberg, *Lectures on Chevalley groups*, §3, Lemma 19 and its corollaries;
Humphreys, *Introduction to Lie Algebras and Representation Theory*, §14.3). The argument that
the root spaces of the Lie algebra generated by the simple root operators are lines, obtained
by transporting simple root spaces with Weyl words, is internal to this project.
