# Smoothness from a surjective actual identity differential

This is an internal derivation from the published actual Lie-kernel, affine-coordinate and scalar-extension constructions and Mathlib's infinitesimal criterion for formal smoothness. It does not introduce a geometric criterion as an assumption. The final theorem `smooth_of_surjective_differential` says that for an actual homomorphism `f : H ⟶ G` of affine group schemes locally of finite type over a field `k`, smoothness of `H` and surjectivity of `Lie.targetMapLinear f k` imply smoothness of the underlying scheme morphism. It is uniform in the universe. Connectedness and smoothness of `G` are not needed for this smoothness conclusion. Scheme surjectivity is a separate conclusion and is not asserted in this module.

`targetMapLinear_kernelScalarExtension` proves that the actual differential commutes with the published equivalences `kernelScalarExtension`. Tensor induction reduces its proof to naturality of postcomposition on pure tensors. `targetMapLinear_surjective_coefficients` then uses right exactness of tensor products to extend differential surjectivity from `k` to every commutative coefficient `k`-algebra of the same universe. `exists_identityDerivation_lift` translates this conclusion through the coordinate derivation equivalences: every derivation at the target's identity is a derivation at the source's identity precomposed with coordinate restriction.

The algebra-map construction `squareZeroPoint` takes an augmentation `ε : C →ₐ[k] k`, a `k`-linear map `d : C →ₗ[k] B` satisfying Leibniz at `ε`, an ideal `I` with `I² = 0`, and a proof that the values of `d` lie in `I`. It returns the map `a ↦ algebraMap k B (ε a) + d a`. The Leibniz identity forces `d 1 = 0`; multiplicativity follows because products of two values in `I` vanish. The companion lemma `augmentationDifference_leibniz` proves the converse calculation needed here: if an algebra-valued point differs from the constant augmentation by elements of `I`, that difference satisfies Leibniz at the augmentation.

`exists_squareZeroKernel_lift` lifts actual points reducing to the group identity modulo any square-zero ideal `I` of a coefficient algebra `B`. First, the target point minus the constant augmentation is an identity derivation with values in `I`. Differential surjectivity lifts it to a `B`-valued source derivation. Since `k` is a field, choose a complement to the `k`-subspace underlying `I` and project the lifted derivation onto `I`. Projection preserves Leibniz, because the augmentation action is through `k`-scalars. It fixes the target derivation, whose values already lie in `I`. The map `squareZeroPoint` turns the projected derivation into a source point. Coordinate restriction proves that its image is the desired point, and the values in `I` prove that it reduces to the identity.

`restrictionMap_formallySmooth_of_surjective_differential` proves the square-zero lifting property for the relative coordinate algebra. Given a compatible source point modulo `I` and target point over `B`, source smoothness gives a preliminary lift of the source point over `k`. Its image may differ from the required target point. Their group quotient reduces to the identity, so `exists_squareZeroKernel_lift` supplies a lift of this error. Multiplying the correction by the preliminary lift gives the required point with both prescribed image and reduction. Mathlib's `Algebra.FormallySmooth.of_comp_surjective` concludes relative formal smoothness.

Finally, `smooth_of_surjective_differential` supplies finite presentation. The target coordinate algebra is noetherian because it is of finite type over a field. The source coordinate algebra is of finite type over it, hence finitely presented. Formal smoothness and finite presentation give smoothness of the coordinate restriction map. The chosen affine coordinate isomorphisms then transport this to the original scheme morphism. All points and coordinate maps used in these arguments belong to the original group schemes; no alternate formal group or pointwise-surjectivity hypothesis is used.
