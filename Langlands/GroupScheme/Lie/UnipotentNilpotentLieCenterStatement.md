# Unipotence via the centre and the nilpotent Lie algebra

`UnipotentNilpotentLieCenterStatement` formalizes the equivalence (a) ⇔ (b) of Milne, *Algebraic Groups: The Theory of Group Schemes of Finite Type over a Field*, 2022 corrected reprint, Corollary 16.61, printed pp. 346–347. The remaining equivalences (a) ⇔ (c) ⇔ (d) are already the published `Langlands.GroupScheme.UnipotentNilpotentDifferentialStatement`.

The binders are a field `k` and a group scheme `G` over `Spec k` that is smooth, affine and has connected underlying space. This is Milne's "connected group variety"; in the chapters from 9 onwards his algebraic groups are affine. The centre is the all-test centre functor `center G`, which need not be smooth. It enters through any actual closed subgroup `z : Z ⟶ G` representing it. Over a field such a representative exists by the published `center_hasClosedRepresentative`, and unipotence of `Z` does not depend on the chosen representative. The Lie algebra is the actual tangent `Additive (lieKernel G k)`, with the bracket `instAffineKernelLieRing` from `Langlands/GroupScheme/Lie/AffineBracket.lean`. Lie nilpotence is `LieRing.IsNilpotent`, the vanishing of a term of its lower central series.

The proposition says: `G` is unipotent if and only if `Z` is unipotent and the tangent Lie algebra is nilpotent. The forward direction combines unipotence of closed subgroups with nilpotence of each `ad X` and Engel's theorem; nilpotence of `ad X` follows from the nilpotent differential of the adjoint representation and the published comparison of the adjoint differential with this bracket. The converse is Milne's argument, after base change to an algebraically closed field. Assuming the centre is unipotent, the adjoint kernel is unipotent by Proposition 14.23, so a subgroup isomorphic to `G_m` acts faithfully on the tangent. Its weights then have greatest common divisor one, so a nonzero tangent element of that subgroup has a nonzero semisimple adjoint action, contradicting Lie nilpotence.

This file states the proposition only. It does not assert that the centre or the adjoint kernel is smooth.
