# Proof: maximal tori centralize commuting semisimple Lie elements

Worker 1296.

`Langlands.GroupScheme.semisimpleMaximalTorusLieCentralizer` proves the
unchanged `Langlands.GroupScheme.SemisimpleMaximalTorusLieCentralizerStatement.{u}`
at arbitrary universe `u`, with no extra hypothesis.

Let `k : Type u` be an algebraically closed field. Let `G` be a group scheme
over `Spec k` that is smooth and affine with connected underlying space. Let
`j₀ : T₀.toGrp ⟶ G` be a maximal torus. Let `Y` be a semisimple Lie element
of `G`, and suppose every tangent vector `targetMap j₀ k t` of `T₀` lies in
the Lie centralizer `Lie.elementCentralizerLie G Y`. Then
`SubgroupFunctor.range j₀ ≤ Lie.elementCentralizer G Y`: on every test
scheme, every point of `T₀` centralizes `Y`.

## Implemented proof

1. **The torus is split, so its tangent vectors are semisimple.** The torus
   `T₀` is split by `IsTorus.isSplitTorus_of_finiteGaloisSplitting` together
   with the published `torusFiniteGaloisSplitting`. So every tangent vector
   of `T₀` is semisimple, by `Lie.isSemisimpleElement_of_isSplitTorus`. Its
   image in `G` is semisimple by `Lie.IsSemisimpleElement.map`.
2. **A finite list.** Let `V` be the range of the `k`-linear tangent map
   `Lie.targetMapLinear j₀ k`. The Lie kernel of `G` is finite-dimensional,
   so `V` has a finite basis `b`. The list is `Y` followed by the basis
   vectors of `V`, as elements of `lieKernel G k`.
   - Every entry is semisimple, by the hypothesis on `Y` and by step 1.
   - Every basis vector lies in the Lie centralizer of `Y`, by hypothesis.
   - Any two tangent vectors of `T₀` centralize each other. The published
     `Lie.range_le_elementCentralizer_target` says that the commutative
     group `T₀` centralizes each of its own tangent vectors, and this is
     applied to the dual-number point of the other vector.

   So the list satisfies the ordered pairwise hypothesis of the helper
   lemma.
3. **One maximal torus contains the list.** The helper
   `Lie.exists_isMaximalTorus_forall_tangent_of_pairwise`, from
   `Lie/CommutingSemisimpleTorus`, gives a maximal torus
   `i : S.toGrp ⟶ G`. Every entry of the list is in the tangent image of
   `i`. Let `W` be the range of `Lie.targetMapLinear i k`. Then `V ≤ W`: the
   image of the span of `b` under the inclusion `V → Lie G` is generated by
   the basis vectors, which all lie in `W`. That span is all of `V`.
4. **Equal dimension.** The published conjugacy theorem
   `maximalTorusConjugacy` gives an isomorphism `φ : T₀ ≅ S` of commutative
   group schemes. Its underlying isomorphism of group schemes gives
   `Lie.dimension T₀.toGrp = Lie.dimension S.toGrp`, by
   `Lie.dimension_eq_of_iso`. Both `j₀` and `i` are closed immersions, so
   their tangent maps are injective (`Lie.targetMapLinear_injective`), and
   `finrank V = finrank W`. Together with `V ≤ W`, this gives `V = W`
   (`Submodule.eq_of_le_of_finrank_eq`).
5. **Conclusion.** `Y` lies in `W = V`, so `Y = targetMap j₀ k t` for some
   tangent vector `t` of `T₀`. The published
   `Lie.range_le_elementCentralizer_of_tangent` then gives
   `SubgroupFunctor.range j₀ ≤ Lie.elementCentralizer G Y`.

Algebraic closure of `k` is used only in step 1 (splitting of `T₀`) and step 4
(conjugacy). Connectedness of `G` is used only in step 4. The helper lemma in
step 3 works over any field.

## Inputs and provenance

All inputs are published theorems imported by Lean name. They are maximal-torus
existence and conjugacy, finite Galois splitting of tori, semisimplicity of
split-torus tangent vectors, smoothness of semisimple centralizers, the
published proofs of `NilpotentCentralTorusStatement`,
`UnipotentSemisimpleStatement` and `CartanStructureStatement`, and maximal-torus
preservation for semisimple centralizers. No unproved statement is a hypothesis.
The target statement pair and its imports are unchanged.

The route is the one described as the intended route in
`Lie/TorusLieCentralizerStatements.md`, after Borel, *Linear Algebraic Groups*,
Proposition 11.8, combined with conjugacy of maximal tori. The Lean proof uses
a finite list of commuting semisimple elements and iterated semisimple
centralizers, instead of a toral subalgebra. It is an internal derived proof.
