# Proof: a smooth closed subgroup with the full Lie algebra is everything

Authors: Worker 1143 and Worker 1240. Worker 1143 wrote this module (the group-scheme half,
the translation step and the final theorem); Worker 1240 wrote the commutative-algebra module
[SubgroupEqualityAlgebra](SubgroupEqualityAlgebra.md) that it imports.

`Langlands.GroupScheme.lieSubgroupEquality` proves the published proposition
`Langlands.GroupScheme.LieSubgroupEqualityStatement.{u}`
([SubgroupEqualityStatement](SubgroupEqualityStatement.md)). Its type is exactly that
proposition: no hypotheses are added. The axioms used are `propext`, `Classical.choice` and
`Quot.sound`.

## Route

Milne, *Algebraic Groups* (2017), Proposition 10.15, proves the statement by a dimension
count (Proposition 1.37). The formal proof does not follow that route: Mathlib at this pin
lacks the needed dimension theory of smooth algebras. It works instead on coordinate algebras
and on points with values in algebras, and uses no dimension theory. Write `A` for the
coordinate algebra `coordinateAlgebra H` of the ambient group, `B` for `coordinateAlgebra K`,
`φ : A →ₐ[k] B` for the restriction map induced by `j`, and `ε` for the counit
`coordinateOrigin H`. The proof shows that `ker φ = 0`, so `φ` is an isomorphism and hence so
is `j`. It has three steps.

1. **Local step.** Every linear form `δ : A → k` satisfying the Leibniz rule at `ε` is a
   derivation at the identity, hence an element of the Lie algebra of `H`. By hypothesis that
   element comes from `K`, so `δ` factors through `φ` and vanishes on `ker φ`
   (`derivation_eq_zero_of_mem_ker`, this module). Since `A` is Noetherian and `B` is
   formally smooth, Worker 1240's `exists_annihilator_of_derivations` then gives `f : A` with
   `ε f = 1` and `f x = 0` for every `x ∈ ker φ`.
2. **Translation.** Translating `f` by the universal point of `K` gives an element `g` of
   `A ⊗ B`. The kernel `ker φ ⊗ 1` annihilates `g`, and `φ ⊗ id` sends `g` to `1`. Expanding
   `g` in a basis of `B` (Worker 1240's `productMap_mem_map_annihilator`) and using that `φ`
   is surjective gives `e : A` with `φ e = 1` that annihilates `ker φ`.
3. **Connectedness.** Then `1 - e ∈ ker φ` and `e (1 - e) = 0`, so `1 - e` is idempotent.
   Since `Spec A` is connected and `ker φ` is proper, Worker 1240's
   `eq_bot_of_mul_eq_zero_of_one_sub_mem` gives `ker φ = 0`.

## The lemmas of this module

All auxiliary declarations are in the namespace `Langlands.GroupScheme.Lie`. Throughout,
`R : CommRingCat`, `H K : GroupScheme (Spec R)` have affine structure morphisms, and
`j : K ⟶ H`.

**Restriction of coordinates.** `restrictionMap j : coordinateAlgebra H →ₐ[R]
coordinateAlgebra K` is the `pointCoordinates` (in `H`) of the composite
`(coordinateIso K).hom ≫ j.hom.hom`. `restrictionMap_spec` records that `Spec` of this map,
followed by `coordinateIso H`, equals `coordinateIso K` followed by `j`. Further lemmas:

- `pointCoordinates_comp_hom`: for a point `y` of `K` with values in an `R`-algebra, the
  coordinates of `y ≫ j.hom.hom` are `(pointCoordinates K y).comp (restrictionMap j)`.
- `mul_comp_hom` and `one_comp_hom`: composing with `j.hom.hom` preserves products and the
  identity of points. Both come from the monoid homomorphism `affinePointsTarget`.
- `coordinateOrigin_comp_restrictionMap`: `(coordinateOrigin K).comp (restrictionMap j) =
  coordinateOrigin H`.
- `affineMap_eq_iff`: `affineMap G f x = y` holds exactly when
  `f.comp (pointCoordinates G x) = pointCoordinates G y`.

**Translation** (`exists_tensor_of_annihilator`). Assume `restrictionMap j` is surjective and
`f` annihilates its kernel. Then there is `g : A ⊗[R] B` such that `includeLeft x * g = 0`
for every `x ∈ ker φ`, and `productMap φ (AlgHom.id R B) g = algebraMap R B (ε f)`. The proof
works with points with values in `A ⊗ B`. Let `p₁` be the point with coordinates
`includeLeft`, and `κ` the image under `j` of the point of `K` with coordinates
`includeRight`. By `pointCoordinates_comp_hom`, `κ` has coordinates
`includeRight.comp φ`. Take `ψ` and `ψ'` to be the coordinates of `p₁ κ⁻¹` and `p₁ κ`, and
put `g = ψ f`.

- (i) Let `Ψ = productMap ψ includeRight`. Then `Ψ` sends `p₁` to `p₁ κ⁻¹` and fixes `κ`.
  Since `affineMap` is a monoid homomorphism, `Ψ` sends `p₁ κ` to `p₁`, that is,
  `Ψ.comp ψ' = includeLeft`.
- (ii) Let `m = map φ id : A ⊗ B → B ⊗ B`. Then `m.comp ψ'` equals the coordinates of the
  product `y₁ y₂` of the two universal points of `K` with values in `B ⊗ B`, composed
  with `φ`. Hence `m (ψ' x) = 0` for `x ∈ ker φ`, and by right exactness
  (`Algebra.TensorProduct.rTensor_ker`) `ψ' x` lies in the ideal generated by
  `includeLeft (ker φ)`.
- (iii) For `x ∈ ker φ`, `includeLeft x * ψ f = Ψ (ψ' x * includeLeft f)`. By (ii) and
  `f · ker φ = 0` this vanishes, via an induction on the span.
- (iv) `productMap φ id` sends `p₁` and `κ` to the same point, so it sends `p₁ κ⁻¹` to the
  identity. By `pointCoordinates_one`, the composite `(productMap φ id).comp ψ` is then
  `ε` followed by `algebraMap R B`. Applying it to `f` gives the second property.

**Derivations** (`derivation_eq_zero_of_mem_ker`). Let `k` be a field and `H`, `K` group
schemes over `Spec k` with affine structure morphisms. Assume every element of
`lieKernel H k` is `Y.val ≫ j.hom.hom` for some `Y : lieKernel K k`. Then every `k`-linear
`δ : coordinateAlgebra H → k` with
`δ (a * b) = coordinateOrigin H a * δ b + coordinateOrigin H b * δ a` vanishes on
`ker (restrictionMap j)`. The proof builds the derivation `Derivation.mk' δ` and takes its
preimage `X` under `kernelDerivationEquiv H`. It writes `X = Y.val ≫ j.hom.hom` and rewrites
`δ x` through `kernelDerivationEquiv_apply` and `pointCoordinates_comp_hom` as the
coordinates of `Y` applied to `φ x = 0`.

## The final theorem

Fix `k`, `H`, `K` and `j` as in the statement.

- `IsAffineHom K.X.hom` follows from `K.X.hom = j.hom.hom.left ≫ H.X.hom`, since a closed
  immersion is affine.
- **Surjectivity of `φ`.** `Spec φ` equals `coordinateIso K`, then `j`, then the inverse of
  `coordinateIso H`. So `Spec φ` is a closed immersion, and
  `IsClosedImmersion.isAffine_surjective_of_isAffine` together with
  `Scheme.ΓSpecIso_naturality` shows that `φ` is surjective.
- **Smoothness and finiteness.** Through `coordinateIso K`, `Smooth K.X.hom` becomes
  smoothness of `Spec` of `algebraMap k B`. `HasRingHomProperty.Spec_iff` and
  `RingHom.smooth_algebraMap` turn this into `Algebra.Smooth k B`, which includes formal
  smoothness. `coordinateAlgebraFiniteType H` gives `Algebra.FiniteType k A`, so `A` is
  Noetherian.
- **Steps 1–2.** `exists_annihilator_of_derivations` applies to `φ`, `ε` and
  `derivation_eq_zero_of_mem_ker j hlie`. Its hypothesis `ker φ ≤ ker ε` comes from
  `coordinateOrigin_comp_restrictionMap`. It yields `f`. Then `exists_tensor_of_annihilator`
  yields `g` with `(φ ⊗ id) g = 1`. Next, `productMap_mem_map_annihilator` and
  `Ideal.mem_map_iff_of_surjective` give `e` in the annihilator of `ker φ` with `φ e = 1`.
- **Step 3.** `PrimeSpectrum A` is connected: `Scheme.homeoOfIso` of the isomorphism
  `Spec A ≅ H.X.left` underlying `coordinateIso H` carries the connectedness of `H.X.left`.
  The kernel is proper, because `φ 1 = 0` would give `1 = 0` in `k` after applying
  `coordinateOrigin K`. `eq_bot_of_mul_eq_zero_of_one_sub_mem` then gives `ker φ = ⊥`, so
  `φ` is injective.
- **Conclusion.** `φ` is bijective, so `CommAlgCat.ofHom φ` is an isomorphism. Then
  `restrictionMap_spec` writes `j.hom.hom` as a composite of isomorphisms. Every point `x`
  of `H` is therefore `(x ≫ inv j.hom.hom) ≫ j.hom.hom`, which lies in
  `SubgroupFunctor.range j`. Hence `SubgroupFunctor.range j = ⊤`.

## Provenance

The theorem is Milne's Proposition 10.15 in the special case recorded in the statement's
Markdown. The proof route is internal, as described above; it is not taken from Milne or
another source. Its algebraic ingredients are standard: formal smoothness, the Krull
intersection theorem, and idempotents on a connected spectrum. They are documented in
[SubgroupEqualityAlgebra](SubgroupEqualityAlgebra.md).
