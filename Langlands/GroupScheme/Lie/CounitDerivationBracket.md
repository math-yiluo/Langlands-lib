# Convolution commutator of counit derivations

Let `B` be a commutative bialgebra over a commutative ring `R`, and `A` a commutative `R`-algebra. On `WithConv (B →ₗ[R] A)`, the multiplication is convolution: `f * g` is `(f ⊗ g)` evaluated on the comultiplication, where the two resulting coefficients multiply in `A`. Its unit `1` is the counit followed by `R → A`. `IsCounitDerivation f` is the Leibniz rule `f(xy) = f(x)1(y) + 1(x)f(y)` relative to this augmentation; linearity is part of the type of `f`.

`counitDerivationLie R B A` is the Lie subalgebra over `A` defined by this rule, with the commutator of convolution. For closure, pull linear maps back along the coalgebra homomorphism `B ⊗[R] B → B` that multiplies the two factors. On the tensor product, write `f ⊠ g` for the map taking `x ⊗ y` to `f(x)g(y)`. It obeys `(f ⊠ g) * (h ⊠ j) = (f * h) ⊠ (g * j)`. The Leibniz condition is that the pullback be `f ⊠ 1 + 1 ⊠ f`; in the commutator of two such expressions the mixed terms cancel. `IsCounitDerivation.commutator` implements this argument with Mathlib's tensor-product and convolution identities. `counitDerivationLie_coe_lie` exposes the formula on the ambient linear maps. The Lie identities are inherited from the associative convolution algebra, not from a finite-dimensional dual. No antipode, flatness, finite generation, smoothness or characteristic assumption is used.

**Provenance.** This is a derived algebraic tool for the tangent construction in `AffineBracket.lean`. The motivating source is Milne, *Algebraic Groups*, 2022 corrected reprint, §10e, Proposition 10.28 and the paragraph following Proposition 10.29 (pp. 195–196). Milne gives the counit Leibniz rule for the field-valued tangent and transports the composition commutator of left-invariant derivations. The convolution closure argument here works over arbitrary commutative scalar and test rings and requires no choice of an invariant-derivation model.
