# Field-valued fibre orders of supplied Weyl quotients

This internal supporting construction combines the Library's quotient-point comparison, arbitrary base-change transport, and local split point-order theorem. All schemes, rings, fields and coordinate types lie in the same arbitrary universe.

For a group scheme `W` over any scheme `S` and a field-valued base map `b : Spec k ⟶ S`, `groupSchemeFibrePointsEquiv` identifies rational points of the pulled-back group with actual scheme morphisms `x : Spec k ⟶ W` satisfying `x ≫ W.X.hom = b`. The forward map is composition with the first pullback projection. Its inverse uses the universal property of the pullback with the identity of `Spec k`. The two inverse laws follow from the projection formulas and pullback uniqueness.

Suppose that `t : T ⟶ G` has commutative source and is a monomorphism of schemes, that `n : N ⟶ G` represents its actual all-test normalizer, and that a supplied homomorphism `q : N ⟶ W` is surjective on underlying scheme points. Suppose also that `N` and `W` are locally of finite type over `S` and that, on every test scheme, the kernel of `q` is exactly the inverse image under `n` of the actual all-test centralizer of `t`. For an algebraically closed field `k`, `weylQuotientFibrePointEquiv` identifies the actual lifts of `b` into `W` with the original point Weyl quotient evaluated on `Spec k` with structure map `b`.

The proof first identifies these lifts with rational points of the pulled-back quotient. The inverse of `fibreRelativeWeylGroupQuotientPointsEquiv` identifies these points with the rational Weyl group of the pulled-back torus map. Finally, `fibreRelativeWeylGroupEquiv` identifies that group with the original point Weyl quotient on the same test scheme. The monomorphism hypothesis is transported by arbitrary base change. Algebraic closure and local finite type enter through the imported quotient-point comparison, which lifts rational quotient points along the scheme-surjective quotient.

Over a commutative local ring `R`, let `G` be affine and reductive, and let `i` be a closed coordinate torus map that is maximal on every residue-field fibre. For supplied quotient data satisfying the preceding hypotheses, `natCard_quotientFibre_eq_localSplitWeylOrder` states that, for every algebraically closed field with its supplied `R`-algebra structure, the natural cardinality of the actual lifts into `W` equals `localSplitWeylOrder I G i`. The proof transports natural cardinality through `weylQuotientFibrePointEquiv` and applies `natCard_pointWeylGroup_eq_localSplitWeylOrder`. Thus the count is independent of the field and its algebra structure; the common value is the closed-fibre Weyl order.

The quotient and its geometric and kernel hypotheses remain inputs. This module constructs no quotient and proves no finiteness or étaleness of its structure morphism. It supplies the actual field-valued lift count used in a subsequent finiteness argument.
