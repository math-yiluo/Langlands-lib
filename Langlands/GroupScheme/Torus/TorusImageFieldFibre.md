# Maximal torus images and rank comparison on field fibres

This is a focused prerequisite for the valuative step of the self-centralizing conjugacy quotient. It works with supplied group-scheme maps and their actual pullbacks. It asserts neither the existence of a quotient nor the extension of the generic torus as a subgroup.

`IsFibrewiseMaximalTorus.fieldPoint_of_affineResidueFibers` factors a field point through the canonical residue field at its image. It applies `maximalTorusBaseChange` to that residue fibre, which is smooth and affine, and then identifies the iterated pullback with the given field fibre. There is no affineness hypothesis on the original group.

`torusImageMaximal_fieldPoint` takes a flat surjective map `q : G ⟶ Q`, a monomorphism `m : Q ⟶ L` into a locally finite type affine group over the base, and a supplied torus image square `t ≫ q = π ≫ d` with `π` surjective and `d` closed. The source is smooth with connected affine residue fibres. The supplied quotient is smooth, separated and quasi-compact. At an algebraically closed field point the pulled-back quotient is connected by surjectivity from the connected source fibre, and is affine by `finiteTypeMonomorphism` applied to the pulled-back `m`. The maximal-torus clause of `algClosedQuotientSubgroupImage` then gives maximality of the pulled-back `d` from that of `t`. The proof uses smoothness of the image torus and makes no affineness assumption on `Q` over the original base.

`IsMaximalTorus.of_isomorphic_torus` needs an isomorphism of the *sources* of two subgroup maps, not an ambient conjugacy between the maps. Over an algebraically closed field every torus is split. In the smooth connected affine ambient group, every split subtorus extends to a maximal torus, and maximal tori are conjugate. The Library split-rank comparison therefore identifies the rank of the given maximal torus with the ambient split rank. The other closed torus attains that rank by the source isomorphism, so is maximal too.

`IsSplitTorus.fieldIsoLift` chooses the actual isomorphism supplied by `IsSplitTorus.isomorphism_fieldBaseChange_surjective`, with the equation recovering the specified field-fibre isomorphism. The last theorem, `isMaximalTorus_fieldFibre_of_split_of_fieldIso`, uses that choice for two split tori `D, B` over the whole base. An actual group isomorphism `D_K ≅ B_K` at one field point gives isomorphic sources at a second, algebraically closed field point. There, the maximality of `D` and the closedness of `B` give maximality of `B` by the rank comparison. Smoothness, connectedness and affineness of the ambient group are only required on that second fibre. In the valuation-ring application, the two field points are the generic and closed points, and the generic source isomorphism comes from the given conjugacy. The theorem does not lift that generic ambient conjugator.

The pair assembles existing Library theorems on actual residue pullbacks, maximal torus images and ranks, and split-torus isomorphism extension. In particular the image theorem is the published maximal-torus clause of Milne, Proposition 17.20 (`Absolute/QuotientSubgroupImageStatement` and its proof); there is no new transcription of that source.
