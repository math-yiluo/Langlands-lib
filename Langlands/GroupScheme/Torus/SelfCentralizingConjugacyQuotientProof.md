# Affineness of the conjugacy quotient by a self-centralizing maximal torus

`selfCentralizingTorusConjugacyQuotientAffine` proves `SelfCentralizingTorusConjugacyQuotientAffineStatement.{u}` by applying the Library reduction `selfCentralizingTorusConjugacyQuotientAffine_of` to four published results.

1. **Torus-subgroup moduli.** `TorusSubgroupModuli.smoothAffineFiberTorusSubgroupRepresentability` represents the moduli of subtori for a smooth, separated, quasi-compact group with affine residue fibres, by a scheme with a universal torus family. The reduction uses the family `g ↦ g T g⁻¹`; two arrows to the group give the same family exactly when their difference lies in the normalizer.
2. **Smooth normalizer.** `separatedTorusNormalizer` represents the normalizer of the torus by a smooth subgroup. The scheme quotient for the resulting smooth relation and the orbit map gives the required quotient and its fppf projection.
3. **Local representations with central kernel.** This is the composite `localCentralKernelRepresentation_of jetCentralizerStabilization_of_centerDetection (jetConjugationRepresentation_of identityJetLocallyFree)`. Over each affine open of a cover of the base, conjugation on a finite-order identity jet gives a representation into `GL_m`, using locally free identity jets. Stabilization of jet centralizers makes the kernel of each representation central.
4. **Affineness core.** `selfCentralizingQuotientAffineOfRepresentation_of` takes three inputs:
   - `TorusSubgroupModuli.generalLinearAffineSum`: the moduli of subtori of `GL_m` is a disjoint union of affine opens.
   - `relativeTorusImageFactorization`: the image of the torus under the representation is a relative torus, with an fppf projection and a closed subgroup inclusion.
   - The closed-embedding property of the induced conjugacy map. It comes from `selfCentralizingConjugacyClosedEmbedding_of_universallyClosed` applied to `selfCentralizingConjugacyUniversallyClosed_of_valuationLift`, which combines the affine-sum property with the valuative lift `selfCentralizingConjugacyValuationLift`. That theorem is proved in the module `Langlands.GroupScheme.Torus.SelfCentralizingConjugacyValuationLiftProof`, ported with this file; its Markdown describes the proof. It uses the integral closed factor from `Langlands.GroupScheme.Torus.ValuationIntegralFactor`, also ported with this file.

   By self-centralization, the kernel of the ambient representation lies in the torus. The conjugacy map of the image torus therefore descends to the quotient. It is a monomorphism whose underlying morphism is locally of finite type. The valuative lift gives universal closedness, so this map is a closed immersion into the moduli of subtori of `GL_m`. The quotient is quasi-compact over each affine chart, so its closed image meets only finitely many of the affine opens of that moduli, and the quotient over the chart is affine. Affineness of the morphism to the base then follows Zariski locally. Ambient affineness is not assumed.

This assembles the Library reductions towards the result of SGA 3, Exposé XVI, Theorem 4.1(c) and the proof of 4.1(d) (Gille–Polo recomposition, printed pp. 318–319, one-based PDF pp. 18–19). Unlike the source proof, this argument does not first prove the entire moduli of maximal tori affine. It constructs the normalizer quotient for the supplied torus and proves that scheme affine. See `SelfCentralizingConjugacyQuotientStatement.md` for the registered source correspondence and the precise all-test coset relation. The proof does not infer quotient affineness from the self-centralizing Weyl affineness criterion or from reductive fibre affineness.
