# Isogenies from a finite-index cocharacter sublattice

Let \(T\) be a split torus over a field \(F\), with actual integral
cocharacter lattice \(Y=\operatorname{Hom}_F(\mathbb G_m,T)\). Given a
\(\mathbb Z\)-submodule \(L\) of \(Y\) and a positive natural number \(m\)
with \(mY\subseteq L\), `IsSplitTorus.realizeCocharacterSublattice`
constructs a `CocharacterSublatticeRealization L m`. Its fields give a split
torus \(S\), a \(\mathbb Z\)-linear equivalence \(e\colon Y(S)\simeq L\),
and group scheme morphisms \(h\colon S\to T\) and \(g\colon T\to S\).

- `hom_cocharacter` identifies postcomposition with \(h\) with \(e\)
  followed by the inclusion \(L\hookrightarrow Y\).
  `reverse_cocharacter` identifies postcomposition with \(g\), followed
  by \(e\) and the inclusion, with \(y\mapsto my\).
- `hom_isIsogeny` and `reverse_isIsogeny` certify `IsIsogeny` for both
  morphisms. `hom_reverse` gives \(g\circ h=[m]_S\), and `reverse_hom`
  gives \(h\circ g=[m]_T\), using the Library's `multiplication`.
- `range_rationalPoints` identifies \(h(S(F))\), as a subgroup of
  \(T(F)\), with the subgroup generated by \(\ell(a)\) for
  \(\ell\in L\) and \(a\in F^\times\). The field points use
  `rationalPoints` and the values use `CocharacterUniformizer.cocharacterPoints`.

The construction applies over an arbitrary field and allows rank zero.
The scheme isogeny criterion gives finiteness, flatness and surjectivity,
including in inseparable cases.

## Construction

A splitting of \(T\) identifies \(Y\) with the integral coordinate lattice
of a finite product of \(\mathbb G_m\). `Submodule.basisOfPid` supplies an
integral basis of \(L\). The split torus on this basis index set is \(S\), and
its standard cocharacter coordinates composed with the basis equivalence
give \(e\).

Both morphisms use the same construction for an integral linear map out of
the cocharacters of a split torus. In a splitting, sum each coordinate
projection followed by the prescribed image of the corresponding
coordinate cocharacter. The inverse coordinate formula
`splitTorus.cocharacterLatticeEquiv_symm_eq` verifies the induced cocharacter
map. The identity `splitTorus.sum_proj_comp_single` shows that these
cocharacters determine the morphism, so the two composites equal
multiplication by \(m\) on the respective tori.

The two cocharacter maps are injective by the inclusion and by
torsion-freeness over \(\mathbb Z\). The composite formulas imply that a
character equality after pullback gives equality after multiplication by
\(m\). The perfect pairing `IsSplitTorus.isPerfPair_latticePairing` reduces
cancellation to cancellation in \(\mathbb Z\). These two injectivity checks
give each isogeny by `splitTorusIsogenyOfLatticeMaps`.

Evaluation on field points commutes with postcomposition. By
`IsSplitTorus.exists_cocharacterPoints_prod`, each point of \(S(F)\) is a
finite product of cocharacter values, and its image belongs to the
generated subgroup. Conversely, evaluating \(e^{-1}(\ell)\) at \(a\) gives
a preimage of each generator \(\ell(a)\).

## Provenance

Gan and Gao, *The Langlands–Weissman program for Brylinski–Deligne
extensions*, Astérisque 398 (2018), §§3.4–3.5, printed p. 206
(one-based PDF p. 224), use \(L=Y_{Q,n}\) and \(m=n\). They specify \(h\)
from inclusion, \(g\) from multiplication by \(n\), and \(h\circ g\) as the
power map. They also use the values of cocharacters in \(Y_{Q,n}\) to
generate a subgroup of \(T(F)\). The construction uses exactly the
sublattice and multiple-containment properties of that example. The
other composite follows from the same cocharacter calculation, and the
field-point image equality follows from generation on the split source.
