# An index bound for finite Galois cocycles of a torus

`FiniteGaloisTorusPermutationIndexStatement` starts with a commutative group scheme `T` over a field `F` and a specified finite Galois extension `E/F` such that `T` becomes a split torus over `E`. Suppose a subgroup `H` of `Gal(E/F)` permutes a basis of the actual integral character lattice over `E`. For any natural number `n` divisible by the subgroup index, the image of every cocycle under multiplication by `n` is a coboundary on the original cover `Spec E → Spec F`.

`finiteGaloisTorusPermutationIndex` base changes to the fixed field of `H`. The scalar extension comparison for character lattices transports the basis to that of the base-changed torus. Since the relative Galois group is `H`, the transported basis exhibits an induced torus. `inducedTorusWeilDecomposition` gives its Weil restriction presentation from a finite product of intermediate fields. The Weil restriction Picard coboundary theorem trivializes the restricted cocycle because the base is a field. The scalar extension comparison for cocycle values makes the resulting cochain a point over `E` satisfying the coboundary equality on `H`.

`GaloisCocycle.isTrivial_nsmul_of_restricted_coboundary` maps such a cocycle for any commutative group scheme to ordinary first cohomology with coefficients in the additive form of its `E`-points. The given cochain kills restriction of the class. `index_nsmul_eq_zero_of_restriction_eq_zero` then kills the original class by the index and hence by `n`. Multiplication by `n` on the group scheme raises every cocycle value to the `n`th power. The degree-one coboundary identity for the resulting class gives the required cochain, and the Galois component comparison extends the identity to all tests.

This is a derived consequence of induced-torus Hilbert 90 and the restriction–corestriction index law. It applies to the permutation-lattice approach to Platonov and Rapinchuk, *Algebraic Groups and Number Theory* (1994), §6.8, Proposition 6.21, printed pp. 375–377 (PDF pp. 385–387).
