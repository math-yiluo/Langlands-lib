# Smooth transporters in separated groups

For an affine base `Spec R`, an actual diagonalizable group `H = D_R(Λ)`, and two homomorphisms `i₁, i₂ : H ⟶ G`, `PointwiseSchemeAction.separatedTransporterModel` gives an actual closed subscheme of `G`, locally of finite presentation over `G`. Here `G` is separated and locally of finite presentation over `Spec R`; it need not be affine. No finite generation condition on `Λ` is needed for this construction. The model represents `IsTransporter i₁ i₂`: a test point `g` factors through the model exactly when on every refinement `g` conjugates `i₂` to `i₁`. The factorization is unique because the inclusion is a closed immersion. `isTransporter_iff_conjugateRelative` gives the equivalent equality of the actual base-changed homomorphisms over the test scheme.

When `Λ` is finitely generated (torsion is allowed) and `G` is smooth, `smooth_separatedTransporterModel` shows that the model is smooth over `Spec R`. The proof first forms the equalizer of the twisted action `(h,g) ↦ i₁(h) g i₂(h)⁻¹` and the second projection on `H × G`. This equalizer is a pullback of the diagonal of `G`; it is closed by separatedness and locally of finite presentation because the diagonal is a section of a locally finitely presented projection. The free coordinate module of `H` allows the use of `ClosedFactorizationFinitePresentation.model`: it represents points of `G` whose entire product with `H` factors through that equalizer. The universal point of `H` on `H × U`, applied to each test `U`, identifies this condition with all-refinement fixedness for the twisted action, hence with the transporter condition.

For smoothness, `exists_transporter_lift_squareZero` applies the published square-zero cohomology theorem to the two base-changed homomorphisms. The source is affine and flat by relative diagonalizability; the needed low-degree cohomology vanishes for its base change to the thickened point's square-zero quotient. The prescribed residue conjugator therefore lifts exactly. The lifted transporter point factors through the model, and the monomorphism of the model recovers the prescribed point on the square-zero quotient. Local finite presentation and this affine square-zero lifting property give smoothness.

## Henselian consequences

Over a Henselian local ring `R` with a surjective algebra map to a field `k`, `exists_henselian_transporter_of_separated` lifts a prescribed residue transporter of two diagonalizable-source maps, recovering the point exactly. It views the residue point as a point of the smooth transporter model and lifts that point by `HenselianSections.exists_section_of_smooth_of_surjective`. `exists_henselian_transporter_of_diagonalizable_of_separated` transports the argument along the source isomorphism for any relatively diagonalizable source in the Library's finitely generated sense.

`exists_henselian_splitTorus_conjugacy_lift_of_separated` lifts both a prescribed residue conjugacy point and the accompanying isomorphism between the sources of two split-torus maps. It first extends the source isomorphism using `IsSplitTorus.isomorphism_fieldBaseChange_surjective`, and then applies exact transporter lifting to the two maps with that common source. The actual point base-change equivalence proves recovery of the residue conjugator. The corollary `exists_henselian_splitTorus_conjugacy_of_separated` obtains residue conjugacy from `maximalTorusConjugacy` when `k` is algebraically closed, the residue group is connected and affine, and the two residue maps are maximal tori. Affineness here is only of the residue group; the two split integral tori and their residue maximality are supplied inputs.

## Provenance and scope

This is the affine-base diagonalizable-source version of the transporter construction of SGA 3, Exposé XI, [Corollary 5.2](https://wstein.org/sga/SGA3/Expo11.pdf#page=25), with the Henselian lifting step related to the one in [Corollary 5.4](https://wstein.org/sga/SGA3/Expo11.pdf#page=26) (Gille–Polo provisional recomposition, printed pp. 109–110). Unlike the corollary as stated there via the Hom-scheme of a smooth affine group, this construction needs only a separated ambient group: it uses whole-product factorization and square-zero conjugator lifting rather than representing the Hom-scheme. The integral conjugacy argument adapts the Library's `HenselianSplitConjugacy` to that setting. This supplies the Henselian conjugacy step for the valuative argument toward an affine conjugacy quotient. It does not extend a generic torus over a valuation ring or prove the quotient affine.
