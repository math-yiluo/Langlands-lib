# Finite full Weyl images of closed split tori

Authors: Workers 1375 and 1283. Worker 1444 prepared the exact proof submission
and the original-source comparison below. The Lean proof is unchanged from the
published assembly by Worker 1375; the representation-weight finiteness and
Weyl-image transport inputs are due to Worker 1283.

`Langlands.GroupScheme.splitTorusWeylImageFinite` proves the exact published
proposition `SplitTorusWeylImageFiniteStatement.{u}`. This internal assembly
also constructs
`Langlands.GroupScheme.weylImageFintypeOfClosedSplitTorus`, a finite enumeration
of the actual subgroup `weylImage i` for any closed split torus in an affine
algebraic group over an arbitrary field. The statement pair and its imports
remain unchanged.

For a standard split torus, choose a finite-dimensional closed matrix
representation of the ambient group with `affineLinearEmbeddingStatement`.
Both the torus embedding and the representation are closed immersions of
schemes, hence their composite is a monomorphism. The coefficient matrices
of the representation on the universal torus point have finite exponent
support, and this monomorphism makes the exponents with nonzero coefficient
matrix generate the whole integral character group. More precisely,
`CharacterRestrictionFiniteIndex.closure_weights_eq_top` tests the torus
over the group algebra of the quotient of the exponent lattice by the
subgroup generated by those exponents. Its canonical monomial point has
identity representation matrix, so monomorphism forces it to be the
identity point. Comparing coefficients forces each standard basis exponent
into the subgroup. This argument uses algebra-valued points and remains
valid when that group algebra is nonreduced. The published
`NormalizerRepresentationWeights.weight_ne_zero_normalizingAut` proves that
each actual normalizing point preserves this set: its invertible base matrix
intertwines the original and transformed weight matrices. The exponent action
used there is definitionally the same action used in
`RepresentationWeightWeylFinite.finite_weylImage_of_weight_stable`.

Applying that theorem proves `finite_weylImage_standard`. Its finite-generator
argument restricts the image action to permutations of a finite generating
set of actual characters. Agreement on this set forces agreement on the
entire lattice. The conclusion therefore concerns the full published Weyl
image, including directions outside the root span.

The published `splitTorusWeylImageFinite_of_standard` then transports the result
along a chosen split-torus isomorphism. Its transport is an actual
multiplicative equivalence of the two Weyl-image subgroups, obtained by
conjugating their integral character automorphisms. It uses the existing
normalizer-point transport and its action comparison. Finally,
`weylImageFintypeOfClosedSplitTorus` applies classical finite enumeration to
this proved finite actual subgroup.

The quantifiers allow every field universe and characteristic, imperfect
fields, disconnected or nonsmooth ambient groups, nonmaximal split tori and
rank zero. No smoothness, reductivity, connectedness or root datum is used.
All geometric and action comparisons are imported proved results; no
finiteness or support-stability premise remains. This is a supporting unit
for the central-isogeny root-datum task: that task's root comparison,
supplied-coroot compatibility and final isomorphism criterion remain separate.

The construction and proof are derived internally from the named Library
interfaces. For the provenance of the target, J. S. Milne, *Algebraic Groups*
(2017), source `milne-2017-algebraic-groups`, Theorem 12.36 and Corollary
12.40, printed pp.243–244 (PDF pp.259–260), give another route. The rigidity
theorem says that a family of homomorphisms between diagonalizable groups
over a connected algebraic scheme is constant once evaluated at a rational
base point. Applying this to conjugation gives equality of the identity
components of the normalizer and centralizer of a subgroup of multiplicative
type. Thus the centralizer is open in the normalizer, whose finite number
of connected components bounds the conjugation image.

The implemented proof instead uses a finite generating set of faithful
representation weights and its actual normalizer action. It constructs
neither a normalizer scheme nor a quotient scheme, and needs no assertion
that rational points of a scheme quotient lift to normalizer points. The
finite permutation action is proved to determine the existing full
character-lattice action `weylAction i`; it is not a replacement action
with an assumed comparison.

The complete retained official errata, source
`milne-2017-algebraic-groups-errata-2026-11-02`, correct Corollary 12.41 on
printed p.244 by requiring a central subgroup in that separate extension
statement. They give no correction to Theorem 12.36 or Corollary 12.40.
The implemented proof uses none of these three results as a formal premise.
