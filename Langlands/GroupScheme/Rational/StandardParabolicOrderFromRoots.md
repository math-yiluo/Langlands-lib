# Standard-parabolic order from relative-root comparisons

This internal auxiliary proves the standard-subset consequences of explicit
comparisons for arbitrary cocharacters. It is a conditional reduction toward
`StandardParabolicOrderStatement`; it does not establish its structural
premises or prove that unconditional target. Full standard-cocharacter
subgroup realization is not an independent premise of this reduction.

`nonnegativeRelativeRoots i zeta` is the set of actual relative roots whose
pairings with `zeta` are nonnegative; `zeroRelativeRoots i zeta` is the set with
zero pairing. They complement the existing `positiveRelativeRoots` construction
and impose no genericity or structural hypothesis on the cocharacter.

`IsStandardCocharacter.root_pairing_implications_of_subset` assumes a generic
cocharacter `ν`, negation stability of the actual relative roots, `J ⊆ K`, and
standard cocharacters `zeta` and `eta` for these subsets. On every relative
root it proves that nonnegativity for `zeta` implies nonnegativity for `eta`,
zero pairing for `zeta` implies zero pairing for `eta`, and strict positivity
for `eta` implies strict positivity for `zeta`.

For a `ν`-positive root, the imported `PositiveRootInduction` API identifies
zero pairing with membership in the additive monoid generated by the standard
subset and strict positivity with nonmembership. The inclusion of these
monoids follows from `J ⊆ K`. Both pairings are nonnegative. For a `ν`-negative
root, its negative is a positive root by the explicit negation-stability
premise. The original pairings are nonpositive, and their zero-pairing
implication follows from the positive case. Genericity ensures that these two
cases exhaust the actual relative roots. The theorem
`IsStandardCocharacter.root_supports_of_subset` expresses the three implications
as set inclusions of nonnegative, zero, and positive relative roots in their
respective directions.

`standardParabolic_order_of_root_comparison` assumes:

- Genericity and negation stability, as above.
- Existence of a standard cocharacter for every subset of the simple roots.
- For arbitrary cocharacters, dynamic parabolic inclusion is equivalent to
  containment of their nonnegative actual-root supports.
- For arbitrary cocharacters, containment of positive actual-root supports
  implies dynamic unipotent inclusion.

From these premises the theorem proves the entire pointwise conclusion used
by `StandardParabolicOrderStatement`: `J ⊆ K` if and only if `P_J ≤ P_K`, the
corresponding covariant Levi and contravariant unipotent inclusions, and
injectivity on the subtype of subsets of simple roots.

For parabolic inclusion, choose a standard cocharacter for `J`. Membership in
`P_J` implies membership in its dynamic parabolic. The root-support implication
then puts that point in the dynamic parabolic of every standard cocharacter
for `K`, hence in their intersection. For unipotent inclusion, choose a
standard cocharacter for `K` and use the reverse positive-support implication
to obtain membership for every standard cocharacter for `J`. These two steps
use existence and the defining intersections without invoking realization.

The reduction first derives dynamic parabolic realization rather than
assuming it: the nonnegative-root support implication for `J ⊆ J` and the
parabolic comparison put the dynamic parabolic of one standard `J`
cocharacter in that of every other. Thus it lies in the standard intersection;
the reverse inclusion is already supplied by the definition.

For order reflection, choose standard cocharacters for `J` and `K`. This
derived realization and the hypothesized standard parabolic inclusion give an
inclusion between these dynamic parabolics. If a simple root belongs to `J`,
its negative has zero pairing with the first cocharacter. Root detection
forces its pairing with the second to be nonnegative, so the original simple
root cannot have positive pairing there. Standardness therefore forces it to
belong to `K`. Levi inclusion is the previously proved
`standardLevi_mono_of_exists`. Two standard subsets with equal parabolics are
then equal by reflection in both directions.

All comparisons are of the actual `SubgroupFunctor`s on every test scheme,
including nonreduced schemes. The ambient field has arbitrary universe `u`;
no finite or nonempty simple system is assumed. No argument assumes that the
relative roots are reduced. Reductivity and maximal splitness are not premises
of this conditional auxiliary: their role is to establish the structural
premises when applying it to the final target.
