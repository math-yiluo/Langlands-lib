# Isotropy of type D₄ with split vector algebra

`NumberFieldTotallyComplexTypeD4VectorIsotropyStatement` concerns an affine locally finite-type simply connected absolutely almost-simple group over a totally complex number field. Supply actual Tits index data with a numbering by `CartanMatrix.D 4` such that the star action fixes both nonfork vertices. Assume the vector fundamental weight (vertex `0`) is supplied as a star-fixed weight and has zero Tits obstruction. Then the group has positive split rank.

The aligned spin presentation gives a nondegenerate quadratic class over a central division algebra \(D\) of degree \(d\), with matrix size \(m\) and \(md=8\). Over the separable closure, the orthogonal Morita comparison supplies a hyperbolic basis. The vector-kernel alignment identifies the vector fundamental weight with a maximal weight of multiplicity one in the dual vector representation. The composite of the spin cover and the isometry inclusion into the units of \(M_m(D)\) factors through the reduced-norm-one group by semisimplicity. The chart comparison identifies the resulting dual norm-one representation with that same dual vector representation after reindexing. Inverse transpose and the weight-space dimensions commute with this reindexing.

`normOneHomDualTitsObstruction` identifies the obstruction with the opposite Brauer class of \(M_m(D)\). Its vanishing implies that \(D\) is a split matrix algebra. A matrix algebra of size greater than one has nonzero zero divisors, so the division algebra has dimension one and \(m=8\). Transport along its scalar algebra isomorphism preserves the nondegenerate quadratic form and the actual spin cover. `numberFieldTotallyComplexScalarSpinIsotropy` then gives positive split rank.

The vector weight and the underlying orthogonal algebra are described in J. Tits, *Représentations linéaires irréductibles d'un groupe réductif sur un corps quelconque* (1971), §6.2, printed p. 212. The exclusion of a D₄ anisotropic kernel for the endpoint-marked E₆ index is discussed by Platonov and Rapinchuk, *Algebraic Groups and Number Theory* (1994), §6.8, around diagrams (6.53) and (6.57), printed pp. 379 and 382–383. The proof here uses the split vector algebra and scalar quadratic isotropy for this exclusion.
