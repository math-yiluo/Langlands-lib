# Intrinsic root complements and split-central directions

Author: Worker 1230.

`RelativeCentralDirectionsStatement` is the conjunction of two independently named propositions over every field `k`, every actual connected reductive group scheme `G`, and every actual maximal split torus `i : T → G`. The universe parameter is arbitrary. These are statement targets; this file proves neither proposition.

`RelativeCharacterRootComplementStatement` says that `relativeRootSpan i` and the image of `rationalCharacterRestrictRat i` are complementary submodules of the full rationalized character lattice of `T`. It states the corresponding real complement using `relativeRootSpanReal i` and `rationalCharacterRestrictReal i`. In each case, complementarity asserts zero intersection and that the sum is the whole character space. In particular, actual roots together with restricted rational characters of `G` span that space. The roots alone need not span it.

This complement target has no chamber parameter and no root-system, reflection or Coxeter premise. Its intended algebraic proof uses the central and derived parts of `G`, the common-root-kernel comparison, and character restriction. It can therefore be used in proving faithfulness of the action on the root span without assuming that root-system realization. Its separate name permits a proof task independent of the remaining central-direction conclusions.

`RelativeSplitCentralDirectionsStatement` supplies finite-dimensionality of the already published real cocharacter and character spaces `RealTorusSpace.V T` and `Vstar T`. Their real dimensions both equal `splitTorusRank T`, the rational dimension of the full actual character lattice. The dimension of `relativeCentralDirections i`, defined as the simultaneous annihilator of all actual relative roots, equals `rationalCharacterRank G`. Every element of the actual rational-point quotient `relativeWeylGroup i = N(k)/Z(k)` fixes this central subspace pointwise under the published `relativeWeylRealAction`.

For every actual split component `a : A → G`, the second target asserts existence of an actual homomorphism `f : A → T` in the category of commutative group schemes. Its underlying group-scheme map is closed and satisfies `groupHom f ≫ i = a`. The induced real cocharacter map is injective with image exactly `relativeCentralDirections i`. Restriction of characters along `f` has kernel exactly the relative root span, both after rationalization and after real scalar extension. These assertions identify actual scheme morphisms and the published lattice maps, rather than identifying two abstract vector spaces of the same dimension.

The only structural premises are connected reductivity and maximal splitness. The affine and monomorphism instances are extracted from these premises. Existence and uniqueness of a split component and the integral finite-index character-restriction theorem retain their existing owners; this target quantifies over each actual split component. No desired complement, fixedness, embedding, kernel or dimension assertion is supplied as a premise. All actual relative roots are retained, including multipliable roots; no positive-rank assumption excludes the empty root system or a zero-dimensional torus.

The provenance is derived. Borel–Tits, *Groupes réductifs* (1965), Theorem 5.3 and its proof (printed pp.96–97, PDF pp.43–44), identify the connected common root kernel with the central intersection and compare root rank with semisimple split rank. Corollary 5.8 (printed p.98, PDF p.45) gives a root-spanned complement to the characters trivial on the derived intersection. Combining this with the central/derived almost-direct-product description and rational character restriction identifies that complementary subspace with the image of the characters of `G`. This identification uses finite-index lattice comparisons after tensoring with `ℚ` or `ℝ`; it does not identify the integral lattices.

Renard, French version dated 9 July 2026, V.2.5–6 (printed pp.107–108, PDF pp.113–114), gives injective finite-index restriction to the split component. V.3.6–7 (printed p.111, PDF p.117) describes the central and root-spanned summands, and V.3.13 (printed p.112, PDF p.118) describes the central subspace as the root annihilator. Renard's presentation is over a nonarchimedean local field. The arbitrary-field scope here is a derived algebraic formulation using Borel–Tits and finite-dimensional tensor linear algebra. Pointwise fixedness follows geometrically from centrality of the embedded split component; it is stronger than the annihilator invariance proved in the interface module. Finite-dimensionality and comparison of the rational and real dimensions use the actual finite split-torus lattices.

The retained Renard 2010 errata, PDF pp.8–9, correct the earlier-edition character restriction diagram and the semisimple-space notation. The cited 2026 version incorporates these corrections. The present targets use actual restriction maps, place roots in their proper summand, and retain the full central complement.
