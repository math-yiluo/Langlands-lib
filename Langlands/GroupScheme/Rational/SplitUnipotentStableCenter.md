# Split stable central subgroups of split unipotent groups

This helper module serves the proof of
`Langlands.GroupScheme.RelativeRoot.SplitUnipotentStableVectorSubgroupStatement`
(task 6b07dcd2-af39-4c69-8ade-d0faa997a49f). Let k be a field which is
infinite, or over which every smooth connected affine unipotent group is split
(`UnipotentSplitOver k`, defined below). Let U be a nontrivial smooth connected
affine unipotent group over k that is split, and let an affine group H act on
U. The module finds a nontrivial smooth connected closed subgroup W of U with
these properties: W is central, stable under H, split unipotent, and killed by
q = `ringChar k`. The module does not prove that W is a vector group. Its last
theorem takes that step as a hypothesis `hvec` and derives the conclusion of
the target statement for such k.

The two cases on k enter only where a generated subgroup must be shown split.
Over an infinite field this is the new argument of this module, which uses the
density of rational points. Otherwise it is an application of
`UnipotentSplitOver k`. The final proof applies that case to finite fields,
which are perfect, where it follows from
`Langlands.GroupScheme.PerfectFieldUnipotentSplitStatement` (see
`Langlands.GroupScheme.Rational.SplitUnipotentStableVectorReduction`).

The route is the Library's existing one for smooth stable central subgroups,
with splitness carried along. The Library proves the same existence statements
without splitness in
`Langlands.GroupScheme.Rational.UnipotentSmoothCenter`
(`hasNontrivialSmoothStableCentralSubgroup_of_uniformNilpotence`) and in
`Langlands.GroupScheme.Rational.UnipotentTorsionCenter`
(`hasNontrivialSmoothStableTorsionCentralSubgroup_of_central`). The two main
theorems here repeat those proofs, keep the generated subgroups, and add a
proof that they are split. That proof is the new part, and it was worked out
for this formalization. The generated subgroups are commutative and are
generated by images that are homomorphisms on rational fibres, so the
factorization `exists_splitClosed_factor` of
`Langlands.GroupScheme.Rational.SplitUnipotentCommutativeEnvelope` applies
over infinite fields.

Everything is in the namespace `Langlands.GroupScheme.RationalDensity`. Fix a
field k and write S₀ = Spec k. All groups are group objects of `Over S₀`. A
point of X with values in T is a morphism T ⟶ X over S₀. A rational point is a
point with values in S₀ itself.

## Fields over which unipotent groups are split

`UnipotentSplitOver k` is the proposition that every smooth connected affine
unipotent group scheme over S₀ is split unipotent (`IsSplitUnipotent`). It is
the conclusion of `Langlands.GroupScheme.PerfectFieldUnipotentSplitStatement`
for the single field k, without the perfectness hypothesis. This module only
uses it as a hypothesis.

## Two small lemmas

- `commutatorElement_mul_of_commute`: in any group Γ, if ⁅c, y⁆ commutes with
  every element, then ⁅c, x y⁆ = ⁅c, x⁆ ⁅c, y⁆. The proof is a direct
  computation, using ⁅c, y⁆ = c y c⁻¹ y⁻¹.
- `mul_comm_of_isCentral`: let z : Z ⟶ G be a central homomorphism
  (`IsCentral z`) whose underlying morphism is a monomorphism. Then any two
  points of Z with values in the same T commute. Their images in G commute
  because one of them is central, and z is a monomorphism.

## Splitness of generated subgroups

`isSplitUnipotent_of_fibrewise`. Let f : V ⟶ G be a morphism over S₀ into a
group G, and let D be a representative of the closed subgroup generated by f
(`GeneratedImage f`, Library module `Langlands.GroupScheme.ImageGeneration`).
Assume the following:

- the group D.group is smooth and affine over S₀;
- any two points of D.group with values in the same T commute;
- S is a smooth, connected, affine and split unipotent group;
- e : Y ⊗ S ≅ V is an isomorphism, and rational points detect functions on
  Y ⊗ S;
- for every rational point y of Y, the map s ↦ f(e(y, s)), that is
  `pointSection S y ≫ e.hom ≫ f`, is a homomorphism S ⟶ G.

Then D.group is split unipotent.

Proof.

1. The inclusion of D.group into G is a monomorphism, and f factors as
   `D.factor` followed by the inclusion. So each map s ↦ D.factor(e(y, s)) is a
   homomorphism S ⟶ D.group (`SplitSemidirect.isMonHom_of_comp_eq`).
2. `exists_splitClosed_factor`, applied inside the commutative group D.group to
   e.hom ≫ D.factor, gives a split closed subgroup κ : I ⟶ D.group through which
   e.hom ≫ D.factor factors.
3. Then κ followed by the inclusion of D.group is a closed subgroup of G
   through which f factors. By minimality of the generated subgroup
   (`GeneratedImage.minimalHom`) there is m : D.group ⟶ I with m, followed by
   κ and the inclusion, equal to the inclusion. Cancelling the monomorphisms
   gives m ≫ κ = 𝟙 and κ ≫ m = 𝟙.
4. So D.group is isomorphic to the split group I, and `IsSplitUnipotent.of_iso`
   concludes.

## Rational points of iterated commutator sources

The Library's `IteratedCommutator.source G n` is G ⊗ G ⊗ ⋯ ⊗ G with n + 1
factors, and `IteratedCommutator.word G n` is the left-nested commutator map
from it to G.

- `smooth_source`: if G is smooth over S₀, then so is every source. The proof
  is by induction on n: the successor source is a product with G.
- `hasDenseRationalPoints_source`: if k is infinite and G is smooth, affine and
  split unipotent, then every source has dense rational points. The proof is by
  induction on n. Split groups have dense rational points
  (`IsSplitUnipotent.hasDenseRationalPoints`, module
  `Langlands.GroupScheme.Rational.SplitUnipotentRationalDensity`). Products of
  such schemes have dense rational points by `HasDenseRationalPoints.tensor`,
  because the projection of a product onto a flat factor is open
  (`isOpenMap_fst`).

## The central subgroup generated by the last commutator word

`isSplitUnipotent_generatedImage_word`. Let k be infinite and let G be smooth,
affine, connected and split unipotent. Let n be such that the image of
`word G n` lies in the centre of G. Let D be a representative of the closed
subgroup generated by `word G n` that is smooth, affine and central in G. Then
D.group is split unipotent.

Proof. D.group is commutative by `mul_comm_of_isCentral`. Apply
`isSplitUnipotent_of_fibrewise` with S = G. Rational points detect functions on
Y ⊗ G whenever Y ⊗ G is smooth with dense rational points
(`rationalPointsDetectFunctions_of_smooth`, module
`Langlands.GroupScheme.Rational.SplitUnipotentRationalDetection`).

- n = 0: take Y = 𝟙_ and e the left unitor. Each fibre map is the identity of
  G, which is a homomorphism. Detection on 𝟙_ ⊗ G is transported from G along
  the unitor (`RationalPointsDetectFunctions.of_iso`).
- n = m + 1: take Y = `source G m` and e the identity of `source G (m + 1)`.
  For a rational point y of Y let c be its value under `word G m`. The fibre
  map is x ↦ ⁅c, x⁆ (`IteratedCommutator.word_succ_points`). Each ⁅c, x'⁆ is a
  value of `word G (m + 1)`, so it is central by hypothesis.
  `commutatorElement_mul_of_commute` shows that x ↦ ⁅c, x⁆ is multiplicative,
  and the Library's `Semidirect.isMonHom_of_mul_comp` makes it a homomorphism.

`exists_splitStableCentralSubgroup`. Let k be infinite or satisfy
`UnipotentSplitOver k` (hypothesis `hk`), let G be smooth,
affine, connected, unipotent, split and nontrivial, and let b be an action of
an affine group H on G. Then there are a group Z, a homomorphism z : Z ⟶ G and
an action of H on Z with the following properties. z is a closed subgroup, Z is
smooth, affine and connected, z is central, the range of z is stable under b
and nontrivial, Z is split unipotent, and z is equivariant.

The proof is that of the Library's
`hasNontrivialSmoothStableCentralSubgroup_of_uniformNilpotence`, keeping the
generated subgroup.

1. Unipotent groups have uniformly nilpotent points
   (`exists_hasUniformNilpotentPoints_of_isUnipotent`), so some commutator word
   vanishes. `IteratedCommutator.exists_last_nontrivial_word` gives n with
   `word G n` ≠ 1 and `word G (n + 1)` = 1. Then the image of `word G n` is
   central (`IteratedCommutator.center_containsImage_of_succ_eq_one`).
2. The source of `word G n` is geometrically integral
   (`IteratedCommutator.source_good`), and the word sends the unit tuple to 1.
   The Library's pointed image generation theorem
   (`ImageGeneration.pointedImageGeneration`) gives a smooth, affine,
   geometrically connected representative D of the closed subgroup generated by
   the word.
3. The word is equivariant for the action of H on sources
   (`IteratedCommutator.word_equivariant`), so the generated subgroup is stable
   (`closedImageEnvelope_isStable`). The restricted action is `D.action`. The
   generated subgroup lies in the centre (`closedImageEnvelope_le_center`), and
   it is nontrivial because it contains the nontrivial word.
4. D.group is split. If k is infinite this is
   `isSplitUnipotent_generatedImage_word`. Otherwise D.group is smooth, affine
   and connected (it is geometrically connected, and Spec k is a single point),
   and it is unipotent as a closed subgroup of the unipotent group G
   (`unipotentClosedSubgroup`). So `UnipotentSplitOver k` applies.

## The torsion subgroup

`exists_splitStableTorsionCentralSubgroup`. Let k be infinite or satisfy
`UnipotentSplitOver k`, and let z : Z ⟶ G be a closed central subgroup with
nontrivial range. Assume that Z is smooth, affine, connected, commutative
(`IsCommMonObj Z.X`) and split unipotent. Let b be an action of an affine group
H on G and bZ an action on Z, with z equivariant. Let q be a natural number such
that some power q ^ r is a uniform exponent of Z: x ^ (q ^ r) = 1 for every
point x of Z. Then there are a commutative group W, a homomorphism w : W ⟶ G and
an action of H on W with the following properties. w is a closed subgroup, W is
smooth, affine and connected, w is central, the range of w is stable under b and
nontrivial, W is split unipotent, q is a uniform exponent of W, and w is
equivariant.

The proof is that of the Library's
`hasNontrivialSmoothStableTorsionCentralSubgroup_of_central`, keeping the
generated subgroup.

1. Z is nontrivial since z has nontrivial range.
   `PowerImage.exists_last_nontrivial_multiplication` gives n with
   multiplication by q ^ n nontrivial on Z and multiplication by q ^ (n + 1)
   trivial.
2. Z is geometrically integral (`smoothConnectedAffineGeometry`), and
   multiplication by q ^ n sends 1 to 1. Pointed image generation gives a
   smooth, affine, geometrically connected representative D of the closed
   subgroup of Z generated by multiplication by q ^ n. Then w is the inclusion
   of D.group followed by z.
3. The action bZ commutes with multiplication
   (`PowerImage.multiplication_equivariant`), so the generated subgroup is
   stable. Its restricted action makes w equivariant. w is central because z
   is. Its range is nontrivial because multiplication by q ^ n is nontrivial
   and z is a monomorphism.
4. The image of multiplication by q ^ n is killed by q
   (`PowerImage.image_killed_of_succ_eq_one`). So q is a uniform exponent of
   D.group (`PowerImage.GeneratedImage.hasUniformPowerExponent`).
5. D.group is split. If k is infinite, apply `isSplitUnipotent_of_fibrewise`
   with S = Z, Y = 𝟙_ and e the left unitor. Each fibre map is multiplication
   by q ^ n, a homomorphism because Z is commutative. D.group is commutative
   because w is central. Otherwise D.group is smooth, affine and connected, and
   it is unipotent as a closed subgroup of Z, which is unipotent because it is
   split (`IsSplitUnipotent.isUnipotent`, `unipotentClosedSubgroup`). So
   `UnipotentSplitOver k` applies.

W is D.group with the commutative structure `IsCentral.commGroupScheme`.

## The characteristic exponent

`exists_hasUniformPowerExponent_ringChar`. Let G be affine, locally of finite
type and unipotent over S₀, and let z : Z ⟶ G be a homomorphism whose
underlying morphism is a monomorphism. Then some power of `ringChar k` is a
uniform exponent of Z.

- In characteristic 0, `ringChar k` = 0 and 0 ^ 1 = 0, and x ^ 0 = 1 for every
  point x.
- In characteristic p, the Library's
  `exists_hasUniformPowerExponent_of_isUnipotent` gives a power of p that is a
  uniform exponent of G. It passes to Z along the monomorphism
  (`HasUniformPowerExponent.of_mono`).

In characteristic 0 the exponent q = 0 imposes no condition on W.

## Assembly

`splitUnipotentStableVectorSubgroup_of_isVectorGroup` takes two hypotheses.
The first, `hvec`, says that every smooth, affine, connected, commutative group
W over k that is split unipotent and has uniform exponent `ringChar k` is a
vector group (`IsVectorGroup W`). The second, `hk`, says that k is infinite or
satisfies `UnipotentSplitOver k`. From these it proves the conclusion of the
target statement for every U and every action a of an affine group H on U,
where U is smooth, affine, connected, unipotent, split and nontrivial.

The target statement also assumes H smooth. This theorem does not use that
hypothesis.

1. `exists_splitStableCentralSubgroup` (with `hk`) gives a split, smooth, connected, stable
   central subgroup z : Z ⟶ U with nontrivial range. Z is commutative because z
   is central (`IsCentral.isCommMonObj`).
2. `exists_hasUniformPowerExponent_ringChar` gives a power of q =
   `ringChar k` that is a uniform exponent of Z.
   `exists_splitStableTorsionCentralSubgroup` (with `hk`) gives W ⟶ U with all the
   properties above and uniform exponent q.
3. `hvec` gives n and natural coordinates e on W. The remaining fields of the
   target follow from the Library's lemmas for vector groups of dimension n:
   W is unipotent (`IsVectorGroupOfDimension.isUnipotent`), n is positive since
   W ⟶ U has nontrivial range
   (`IsVectorGroupOfDimension.pos_of_range_ne_bot_overRing`), and W is smooth of
   relative dimension n
   (`IsVectorGroupOfDimension.smoothOfRelativeDimension_overRing`).
