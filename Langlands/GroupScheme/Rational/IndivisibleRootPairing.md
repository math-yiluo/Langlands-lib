# Restricting a root pairing to indivisible roots

This internal derived construction works with a root pairing over the rational
numbers. `RootPairing.indivisibleIndices` selects precisely those root indices
whose roots are not natural multiples, with multiplier at least two, of another
root of the pairing. It does not test primitivity in an ambient lattice.

`indivisibleIndices_reflection_iff` applies an involutive root reflection to a
putative divisibility relation. Thus the indivisible indices are invariant under
every root reflection. `indivisiblePairing` restricts the root and coroot
embeddings and the reflection permutations to those indices, keeping the same
perfect pairing and ambient spaces. Its integral pairings are inherited from the
original pairing. No reducedness assertion or nonempty-root assumption is needed.

For crystallographic pairings, `mem_indivisibleIndices_of_mem_baseOf`
uses the published `eq_one_or_two_of_root_eq_nsmul`: if a positive
indecomposable root were a proper natural multiple, the multiplier would be two.
The smaller root is positive, yielding a forbidden sum of two positive roots.

For a finite index type, `image_baseOf_indivisiblePairing` compares positive indecomposable root values
for a rational-valued additive functional that is nonzero on every root. The
original indecomposables are indivisible and remain indecomposable after
restriction. The restricted indecomposables are linearly independent by
`linearIndepOn_root_baseOf'`. Every original root lies in the additive monoid
generated by the original indecomposables or its negative, so every restricted
indecomposable lies in their rational span. An independent set containing a
spanning subset equals that subset, which proves the equality. The proof uses
neither reducedness nor a claim that the roots span the ambient space, and
includes the empty root set. This is a conditional root-pairing result; it does
not construct the structural root pairing of an actual reductive group.

The provenance is internal composition of the published root-pairing reflection
laws, integral-multiplier bound, positive-indecomposable generation, and
linear-independence theorem. No new literature assertion is used.
