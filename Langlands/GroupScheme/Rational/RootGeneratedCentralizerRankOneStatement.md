# Rank-one generation of the root-generated part of a split-torus centralizer

Authors: Worker 1662.

`Langlands.GroupScheme.RootGeneratedCentralizerRankOneStatement.{u}` is a
proposition definition, not a proof. Let `G` be a connected reductive group
over an arbitrary field `k`, with a maximal split torus `S` and centralizer
`Z = Z_G(S)`. Let `G(k)^+` be the subgroup of `G(k)` generated by the
rational root groups `U_a(k)` of all relative roots `a`. The proposition
states that every point of `Z(k) ∩ G(k)^+` is a product of elements of the
groups `Z(k) ∩ ⟨U_a(k), U_{-a}(k)⟩`, where `a` runs over the relative roots.

## The proposition

For every `k : Type u` with `[Field k]`, every
`G : GroupScheme (Spec (CommRingCat.of k))` with
`hG : IsConnectedReductive G`, every
`S : CommGroupScheme (Spec (CommRingCat.of k))` and every
`i : S.toGrp ⟶ G` with `IsMaximalSplitTorus i`, the following holds. Using
the instance `IsAffineHom G.X.hom` supplied by `hG.isAffineHom`,

```
centralizerPoints i ⊓ Subgroup.closure (⋃ a ∈ relativeRoots i, U_a(k))
  ≤ Subgroup.closure (⋃ a ∈ relativeRoots i,
      (centralizerPoints i ⊓ Subgroup.closure (U_a(k) ∪ U_{-a}(k))))
```

as subgroups of `rationalPoints G = G(k)`, where `U_a(k)` is
`(RelativeRoot.subgroup i a).carrier (Over.mk (𝟙 _))`, viewed as a subset
of `rationalPoints G`. These are all the quantified data and hypotheses. The
universe `u` is arbitrary. The field `k` is arbitrary: there is no
characteristic, perfectness or local-field hypothesis. The group `G` is not
assumed semisimple, simply connected, quasi-split or `k`-simple. No new
definition is introduced. `G(k)^+` is written out as
`Subgroup.closure (⋃ a ∈ relativeRoots i, U_a(k))`. The reverse inclusion
holds trivially, because each generator on the right lies in
`centralizerPoints i` and in `G(k)^+`. It is not part of the statement.

If `relativeRoots i` is empty, for example when `G` is `k`-anisotropic, both
closures are trivial and the proposition holds trivially.

## Exact objects

- `IsConnectedReductive G` (module `Langlands.GroupScheme.Reductive.Basic`)
  is the Library's connected reductive field group predicate. Its field
  `isAffineHom` provides the affineness instance that `relativeRoots` and
  `RelativeRoot.subgroup` use.
- `IsMaximalSplitTorus i` (module `Langlands.GroupScheme.Rational.SplitTorus`)
  says that `i` is a split subtorus and that every factorization of `i`
  through another split subtorus is an isomorphism.
- `centralizerPoints i` (module `Langlands.GroupScheme.Torus.Maximal`) is the
  subgroup of `G(k)` of points normalizing `i` through the identity of `S`,
  that is, the rational points `Z(k)` of `Z_G(S)`.
- `relativeRoots i` (module `Langlands.GroupScheme.Rational.RelativeRoots`)
  is the set of nonzero characters of `S` with a nonzero weight space in the
  Lie algebra of `G`, that is, the relative root system `kΦ = Φ(S, G)`.
- `RelativeRoot.subgroup i a` (module `Langlands.GroupScheme.Rational.RootSubgroup`)
  is the subgroup functor of points `g` whose orbit map `s ↦ i(s) g i(s)⁻¹`
  factors as `s ↦ e(a(s))` for a morphism `e` from the affine line with
  `e(0) = 1`.
- `rationalPoints G` (module `Langlands.GroupScheme.Rational.Characters`) is
  the group `G(k)` of `k`-points.

## Comparison with the Borel–Tits root groups

Both sides of the inclusion involve the Library groups `U_a(k)`. This
section relates them to the root groups `U_{(a)}` of Borel–Tits 5.2. There
`U_{(a)}` is the group `G*_{(a)}` of 3.8. Over an algebraic closure it is
generated by the absolute root groups `U_α` whose restriction to `S` is a
positive integer multiple of `a`. By 5.8, `kΦ` is a root system, possibly
non-reduced. So for each relative root `a` there is a unique indivisible
relative root `a₀` with `a = a₀` or `a = 2a₀`. The positive rational
multiples of `a` in `kΦ` are `a₀` and, if it is a root, `2a₀`. Write `S_a`
for the identity component of `ker a`. Since `G` is affine (`hG.isAffineHom`),
the dynamic groups of Conrad–Gabber–Prasad §2.1 apply. For a cocharacter `λ`
of `S`, `U_G(λ)` is the subgroup of points `g` for which
`t ↦ λ(t) g λ(t)⁻¹` extends to the affine line with value `1` at `0`.

The following two inclusions hold for every relative root `a`.

(A) `U_a(k) ⊆ U_{(a₀)}(k)`. Let `g ∈ U_a(k)`. Then there is a morphism `e`
from the affine line to `G` with `e(0) = 1` and `s g s⁻¹ = e(a(s))` for all
points `s` of `S`.

1. For `s` in the scheme-theoretic kernel of `a`, `s g s⁻¹ = e(1) = g`.
   So `g` centralizes `S_a`, and `g ∈ M(k)` for `M = Z_G(S_a)`. This `M` is
   a connected reductive `k`-group (proof of BT 5.3).
2. Choose a cocharacter `λ` of `S` with `n = ⟨a, λ⟩ > 0`. Then
   `λ(t) g λ(t)⁻¹ = e(tⁿ)`, which extends to the affine line with value
   `e(0) = 1` at `0`. So `g ∈ U_G(λ)(k)`. Since `λ` factors through `M`,
   `g ∈ (M ∩ U_G(λ))(k) = U_M(λ)(k)` (CGP Lemma 2.1.5).
3. `U_M(λ)` is a smooth connected unipotent `k`-subgroup, normalized by `S`
   because `S` commutes with `λ` (CGP Lemma 2.1.5 and Proposition
   2.1.8(3), (4)). Its Lie algebra is the sum of the weight spaces of
   `Lie M` on which `λ` has positive weight (CGP Proposition 2.1.8(1)).
   The torus `S_a` acts trivially on `Lie M`. So every weight `c` of `S` on
   `Lie U_M(λ)` is a relative root that is trivial on `S_a`, hence a
   rational multiple of `a`, with `⟨c, λ⟩ > 0`. Then `c` is a positive
   rational multiple of `a₀`.
4. The set of positive rational multiples of `a₀` in `kΦ` is convex. It
   equals `(a₀)`, the set of positive integer multiples of `a₀` in `kΦ`.
   By BT 3.12, `U_M(λ) ⊆ G*_{(a₀)} = U_{(a₀)}`. Hence `g ∈ U_{(a₀)}(k)`.

(B) `U_{(a)}(k) ⊆ U_a(k)`. Let `g ∈ U_{(a)}(k)`.

1. Over an algebraic closure, each generator `U_α` of `U_{(a)}` has
   `α|_S = m·a` with `m ≥ 1`. So the kernel of `a` acts trivially on each
   `U_α` and centralizes `U_{(a)}`. Hence the orbit map
   `s ↦ s g s⁻¹` is invariant under translation by `ker a`. The character
   `a : S → G_m` is a quotient map with kernel `ker a`, because `a ≠ 0`. So
   the orbit map is `s ↦ φ(a(s))` for a morphism `φ` from `G_m` to `G`.
2. Choose `λ` with `n = ⟨a, λ⟩ > 0`. Each `U_α` above has
   `⟨α, λ⟩ = m n > 0`, so it lies in `U_G(λ)`. Since `U_G(λ)` is a closed
   subgroup whose formation commutes with field extension (CGP
   Lemma 2.1.5), `U_{(a)} ⊆ U_G(λ)`. So `t ↦ φ(tⁿ) = λ(t) g λ(t)⁻¹`
   extends to the affine line with value `1` at `0`.
3. Let `f` be a regular function on the affine scheme `G`. Then `f ∘ φ` is
   a Laurent polynomial `p` such that `p(tⁿ)` is a polynomial. Hence `p`
   is a polynomial, and `p(0) = f(1)`. So `φ` extends to a morphism `e`
   from the affine line with `e(0) = 1` and `e(a(s)) = s g s⁻¹`. That is,
   `g ∈ U_a(k)`.

Consequences. If `a` is indivisible, then `a₀ = a` and
`U_a(k) = U_{(a)}(k)`. If `a = 2b` with `b` a relative root, then
`U_{(2b)}(k) ⊆ U_{2b}(k) ⊆ U_{(b)}(k) = U_b(k)`. The comparison does not
identify `U_{2b}(k)` with `U_{(2b)}(k)`, and nothing below needs that.

- The left-hand closure is the Borel–Tits group
  `G(k)^+ = ⟨U_{(a)}(k) : a ∈ kΦ⟩`. It is also generated by the
  `U_{(a)}(k)` with `a` indivisible, because `U_{(2b)} ⊆ U_{(b)}` (BT 3.8,
  as `(2b) ⊆ (b)`).
- On the right, for `a = 2b` the group `⟨U_{2b}(k), U_{-2b}(k)⟩` lies in
  `⟨U_b(k), U_{-b}(k)⟩`. So the term for `2b` is contained in the term for
  `b`. Hence the right-hand side is the subgroup `H_0` generated by the
  groups `Z(k) ∩ ⟨U_{(a)}(k), U_{(-a)}(k)⟩` with `a` indivisible.

## Source and derived formulation

A. Borel and J. Tits, *Groupes réductifs*, Publ. Math. IHÉS 27 (1965),
55–150, registered as `borel-tits-1965-groupes-reductifs`. Printed page =
PDF page + 53. The ingredients below hold for an arbitrary field `k` and a
connected reductive `G` (standing assumption of §5, printed p. 95).

- 3.8, printed pp. 76–77: the groups `G*_ψ` generated by the absolute
  root groups `U_α` with `α|_S ∈ ψ`.
- Proposition 3.12, printed p. 78: a closed connected subgroup normalized
  by `S` whose `S`-weights lie in a convex set `ψ` is contained in `G*_ψ`.
  The proof of Corollary 3.19, printed pp. 82–83, notes that the positive
  rational multiples of a character form a convex set.
- Corollary 5.8, printed p. 98: `kΦ` is a root system.
- 5.1–5.2, printed pp. 95–96: the relative Weyl group
  `kW = N(k)/Z(k)` with `N = N_G(S)`, and the root groups `U_{(a)}`.
- Theorem 5.3, printed pp. 96–97: `kW` is generated by the reflections
  `r_a` in the relative roots. Every connected component of `N_G(S)` meets
  `G(k)`. The proof finds a representative of `r_a` in the `k`-points of
  the derived group of `Z_G(S_a)`, where `S_a` is the identity component of
  `ker a`.
- Theorem 5.15, printed p. 100: `G(k) = U(k) N(k) U(k)` for the unipotent
  radical `U` of a minimal parabolic `k`-subgroup containing `S`. Moreover
  `U n U = U n' U` forces `n = n'`, and `N ∩ U⁻ U = {e}`.
- Proposition 5.16, printed p. 101, and its proof: Theorem 5.15 applied to
  the rank-one group `Z_G(S_a)` for a simple root `a` gives
  `Z_G(S_a)(k) ⊆ P(k) ∪ U_{(a)}(k) r_a U_{(a)}(k) Z(k)`. Also
  `U = V_a ⋊ U_{(a)}`, where `V_a` is the unipotent radical of the standard
  parabolic `P_{\{a\}}` and is normalized by representatives of `r_a`.
- Corollary 5.18, printed p. 101: `(G(k), P(k), N(k), {r_a})` is a Tits
  system.

The proof of Theorem 5.3 also states that `Z_G(S_a)` is a connected
reductive `k`-group.

B. Conrad, O. Gabber and G. Prasad, *Pseudo-reductive groups*, 2nd ed.
(2015), registered as `conrad-gabber-prasad-2015-pseudo-reductive`. Printed
page = PDF page − 26. This source is used only in the comparison of root
groups.

- Lemma 2.1.5, printed p. 50: `U_G(λ)` is a closed subgroup, its formation
  commutes with base change, and `H ∩ U_G(λ) = U_H(λ)` for a closed
  subgroup `H` through which `λ` factors.
- Proposition 2.1.8, printed p. 53: `Lie U_G(λ)` is the sum of the
  positive-weight spaces (1). For smooth `G` over a field, `U_G(λ)` is
  smooth (3) and connected (4).

The proposition is a derived formulation and not a numbered result of the
cited source. For split Chevalley groups it is the classical fact that the
diagonal part of the elementary subgroup is generated by the elements
`h_a(t)`. That case is not in the project literature. The following argument
is its provenance. It is not proved here. Fix a positive system with simple
roots `Δ`. Write `U^+ = U(k)`, `Z = Z(k)`, `N = N(k)`, `W = kW`,
`G_a^+ = ⟨U_{(a)}(k), U_{(-a)}(k)⟩`, and `H_0` for the subgroup generated
by the `Z ∩ G_a^+` with `a` indivisible. By the comparison above, `H_0` is
the right-hand side. The group `W` permutes the indivisible roots.

1. `N` normalizes `H_0`, because `n G_a^+ n⁻¹ = G_{w(a)}^+` for `n`
   representing `w`. The group `G_a^+` lies in `Z_G(S_a)(k)`, whose
   normalizing points have image `{1, r_a}` in `W`. Hence
   `N ∩ G_a^+ ⊆ Z ∪ m_a (Z ∩ G_a^+)` for any `m_a ∈ N ∩ G_a^+` representing
   `r_a`. Such `m_a` exist, by the rank-one Bruhat decomposition of 5.16
   applied to any `v ∈ U_{(a)}(k) \ {1}`.
2. Let `N' = ⟨H_0, m_a : a ∈ Δ⟩`. In `N'/H_0` the classes of the `m_a`
   are involutions. They satisfy the Coxeter relations of `W`, because the
   classes of `n m_a n⁻¹` and `m_b` agree whenever the image of `n` maps `a`
   to `±b`. Since `W` is the Coxeter group on the simple reflections,
   `N'/H_0 ≅ W` and `N' ∩ Z = H_0`.
3. `Y = U^+ N' U^+` is stable under left multiplication by `U^+`, by `H_0`
   and by each `m_a`, `a ∈ Δ`. For `m_a`, write `u = u' v` with `u' ∈ V_a`
   and `v ∈ U_{(a)}(k)`, and let `w` be the image of `n ∈ N'`. If
   `w⁻¹(a) > 0`, then `m_a v n ∈ m_a n U^+`. Otherwise, if `v ≠ 1`, the
   rank-one Bruhat decomposition gives `v = x m y` with
   `x, y ∈ U_{(-a)}(k)` and `m ∈ N ∩ G_a^+` representing `r_a`, so
   `m ∈ m_a H_0` by 1. Then `m_a v n ∈ U^+ m_a m n U^+`. Also
   `m_a⁻¹ ∈ H_0 m_a`. Every `U_{(b)}(k)` is conjugate under `N'` to some
   `U_{(c)}(k) ⊆ U^+`, because `N' → W` is onto and every root is
   `W`-conjugate to a positive one. So `U^+`, `H_0` and the `m_a^{±1}`
   generate a group containing `G(k)^+`, and `G(k)^+ ⊆ Y`.
4. Let `z ∈ Z ∩ G(k)^+` and write `z = u n u'` with `u, u' ∈ U^+` and
   `n ∈ N'`. Then `U z U = U n U` with `z, n ∈ N`, so `z = n` by the
   uniqueness in Theorem 5.15. Hence `z ∈ N' ∩ Z = H_0` by 2.

By the comparison with the Borel–Tits root groups, the left-hand closure is
`G(k)^+` and the right-hand side is `H_0`. So the proposition is the
conclusion `Z ∩ G(k)^+ ⊆ H_0` of this argument.

## Relation to other statements

This module supplies no proof. The proof is a separate obligation. The
Library's unproved `Langlands.GroupScheme.RelativeTitsSystemStatement` and
`Langlands.GroupScheme.RelativeWeylCoxeterStatement` formalize the Tits
system and Coxeter inputs of the argument above. The proposition does not
depend on them.

The proposition is the field-general half of a proof of
`Langlands.GroupScheme.SimplyConnectedRankOneCentralizerGenerationStatement`.
Its left-hand side is the first term of the bound in
`Langlands.GroupScheme.SimplyConnectedKneserTitsCentralizerStatement`, the
Kneser–Tits half. Together the two give the rank-one statement, using that
a simply connected group is connected reductive. This remark is not proved
in this module.
