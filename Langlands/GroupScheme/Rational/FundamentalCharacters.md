# Actual fundamental trace characters and invariant generation

Author: Worker 1284. Provenance: derived from Steinberg's fundamental-character theorem, using the published actual fundamental-representation interface and the actual trace construction.

For a supplied `F : p.FundamentalRepresentation a`, `F.character` is the trace of its actual finite matrix representation as an element of `ClassFunctions.algebra G`. The construction reuses `MatrixRepresentation.traceClassFunction`; its evaluation on every commutative coefficient algebra is the ordinary matrix trace. Thus this character belongs to the actual conjugation equalizer, including its interpretation on nonreduced coefficient algebras.

Given an ordering `e : Fin r ≃ p.base.support` and a family of actual fundamental representations `F`, `p.fundamentalCharacterMap e F` is the algebra homomorphism from `MvPolynomial (Fin r) k` to the actual invariant subalgebra that sends `X j` to `(F (e j)).character`. The variable equation is proved. `fundamentalCharacterEquiv` is expressly conditional: it packages this homomorphism as an algebra equivalence when its bijectivity has been supplied. Its evaluation is definitionally the same homomorphism.

`SplitFundamentalCharacterGenerationStatement.{u}` is a `Prop`-valued definition and remains unproved. It states that over every algebraically closed field `k : Type u`, in every characteristic, for an actual affine group scheme with supplied split pinning and the Library's `IsSimplyConnected` premise, the preceding homomorphism is bijective for every ordering and every supplied family of actual fundamental representations.

This statement does not construct such a family. That separate conclusion is the published `SplitFundamentalRepresentationsExistStatement`, whose exact proof has task `cb3e15ad-e89e-4790-b356-54b978367f7f`. In a quotient proof the family must first be constructed; the universal family quantifier here does not replace that existence obligation. No highest-weight existence, invariant-generation or coroot-span assumption is added to the simply connected group hypotheses. No property of the supplied Steinberg root maps or reflection representatives occurs in this proposition.

These pages were read in full; no project errata note for this source was found.

Steinberg's theorem is formulated for regular functions on an algebraic group over an algebraically closed field. The current proposition is its derived translation to the actual affine coordinate algebra and the all-algebra conjugation equalizer. Establishing equality with the source's rational-point class-function algebra, the corresponding torus restriction and invariant calculation, and the required representation-theoretic comparison are proof obligations. None is assumed in the proposition. The underlying finite matrix representations and torus weight data are the published actual objects; no abstract polynomial replacement of the invariant algebra is introduced.

This module proves neither the generation statement nor a formula for restrictions to a Steinberg product, an isomorphism of the canonical quotient on that product, or descent to arbitrary fields.
