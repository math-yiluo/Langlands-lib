# Characters of torus parts of opposite-pair products

Author: Worker 4348.

Module `Langlands.GroupScheme.Rational.PieceTorusCharacter`, namespace
`Langlands.GroupScheme.SplitPinning`.

Let `k` be a field, `G` an affine group scheme over `Spec k`, and `p : SplitPinning G`. Write
`T = p.torus`, `j = p.inclusion`, `P = p.rootDatum`, and `⟨χ, ν⟩` for
`P.toLinearMap χ ν`, which equals `latticePairing T χ ν` by `p.isRootDatum.1`. The module
computes character values on the torus parts of opposite-pair products of points of a
multipliable relative root group. These are the products described in module
`Rational.PieceCorootFactorization`.

## Definitions

- `charEval χ : rationalPoints T →* kˣ`, for a character `χ` of `T`: the Library evaluation
  `rationalCharacterEval` of the rational character corresponding to `χ`. For a simple index
  `a`, `charEval (fundamentalWeightBasis a)` is the map `fundamentalWeightEval a` of the
  accepted module `Rational.SimpleCorootCoordinates`.
- `torusCocharacterPoint ν : kˣ →* rationalPoints T`: the point `ν(t)` of the torus. It is the
  image under `ν` of the point of `𝔾_m` with coordinate `t` (`Gm.affinePointsEquiv`).

## Results

- `charEval_torusCocharacterPoint χ ν t`: `χ(ν(t)) = t ^ latticePairing T χ ν`. Write `ν ≫ χ`
  as an integer power of the identity of `𝔾_m` (`gmEndEquiv`, `latticePairing_apply`).
- `charEval_map φ χ x`: for an endomorphism `φ` of `T`, `χ(φ(x)) = (φ ≫ χ)(x)`. This holds by
  definition.
- `cocharacterPoint_eq_map ν t`: the point
  `LinkedRootParametrization.cocharacterPoint j ν t` of `G` is the image under `j` of
  `torusCocharacterPoint ν t`. This holds by definition.
- `conj_map_inclusion n x`: for `n` in `normalizerPoints j`,
  `n · j(x) · n⁻¹ = j(σ(x))` with `σ = (normalizingAut j n).hom`. This is
  `isNormalizingPoint_normalizingAut` evaluated at `x`, with `comp_innerIso_hom` and
  `constantPoint_self`.
- `normalizingAut_hom_comp n₀ hn₀ ω`. Suppose `n₀` acts on characters by the reflection in a
  root `m`, that is `weylAction j n₀ χ = χ - ⟨χ, m^∨⟩ m` for all `χ`. Then
  `σ ≫ ω = ω - ⟨ω, m^∨⟩ m` for `σ = (normalizingAut j n₀).hom`. Proof: `ψ = σ ≫ ω` satisfies
  `weylAction j n₀ ψ = ω` (`weylAction_apply`, `Iso.inv_hom_id_assoc`). Hence
  `ω = ψ - ⟨ψ, m^∨⟩ m`. Pairing with `m^∨` and using `⟨m, m^∨⟩ = 2` gives
  `⟨ψ, m^∨⟩ = -⟨ω, m^∨⟩`.
- `coroot_reflectionPerm_self i`: the coroot of `reflectionPerm i i` is `-i^∨`. This is
  `RootPairing.coroot_reflectionPerm` with `coreflection_apply_self`.
- `cocharacterPoint_mem_closure s v`: `s^∨(v)` lies in the subgroup generated by the rational
  points of the root groups of `root s` and `-root s`. This is the word `h_eq_word` of the
  rank-one data of the chosen linked parametrization of `s`.
- `exists_piece_torusPart n₀ hn₀ w hτ`. Let `n₀` act by the reflection in `m`, let `w` be a
  unit, and let `τ` be in the image of `s^∨`. Then `m^∨(w) · n₀ τ n₀⁻¹ = j(x)` for a torus
  point `x`. Every character `ω` with `⟨ω - ⟨ω, m^∨⟩ m, s^∨⟩ = 0` satisfies
  `ω(x) = w^⟨ω, m^∨⟩`. Write `τ = s^∨(v)` and
  `x = m^∨(w) · σ(s^∨(v))`, then apply the previous lemmas.
- `list_ofFn_prod_mul_inv A B hB hAB`, a group lemma. Suppose `B l` commutes with `B m` and
  with `A m` whenever `l ≠ m`. Then `(∏ A)(∏ B)⁻¹ = ∏ₗ A l (B l)⁻¹`, for ordered `List.ofFn`
  products. It follows from the Library lemmas `list_ofFn_prod_inv_of_commute` and
  `list_ofFn_prod_mul_mul`.

## The main theorem

`exists_oppositeProducts_torusPart hline hS F ha0 xm w hX hY hg hg1 hg' hg'' hN hN' hg₀ hg₀1
hg₀' hg₀'' hN₀ hN₀'`.

Setting: as in `PieceCorootFactorization.oppositeProduct_eq_prod`.
- `G` is smooth and locally of finite type over `k`.
- `S` is a split torus with `f : S ⟶ T`, and the composite `groupHom f ≫ j` is a
  monomorphism (instance hypothesis).
- `a ≠ 0` is a character of `S` with `RestrictsToMultipliableLine f P a`.
- `F` is a piece family over `a`, with pieces `(p_l, q_l, m_l)`.
- `xm l` is a root parametrization (`SplitPinning.RootParametrization`) of `m_l`, and
  `w l` is a unit, for each `l`.

Consider the two points

- `g = F.tripleProd X Z Y`, with `Z l = (xm l)(w l)`, `X l ∈ U_{q_l}`, `Y l ∈ U_{p_l}`;
- `g₀ = F.tripleProd 1 Z₀ 1`, with `Z₀ l = (xm l)(1)`.

Assume both are nontrivial points of the restricted root group of `a`. Let `(g', g'')` and
`(g₀', g₀'')` be pairs in the restricted root group of `-a` such that `n = g' g g''` and
`n₀ = g₀' g₀ g₀''` normalize both `j` and `groupHom f ≫ j`. Then there is a torus point `x`
with `n n₀⁻¹ = j(x)`. Moreover, every character `ω` with `⟨ω, q_l^∨⟩ = 0` for all `l`
satisfies

```text
ω(x) = ∏ₗ (w l) ^ ⟨ω, p_l^∨⟩.
```

### Proof

1. Choose opposite pairs `pr l` of `Z l` and `pr₀ l` of `Z₀ l`
   (`splitPinnedOppositeFactorPairs`). By `oppositeProduct_eq_prod`,
   - `n = ∏ₗ A_l` with `A_l = (pr l).1 Z_l (pr l).2 τ_l`;
   - `n₀ = ∏ₗ B_l` with `B_l = (pr₀ l).1 Z₀_l (pr₀ l).2 τ₀_l`.

   Here `τ_l` and `τ₀_l` lie in the image of the coroot of `s_l = reflectionPerm p_l p_l`.
2. `A_l` and `B_l` lie in the piece group of `l`. For the coroot factors this follows from
   `cocharacterPoint_mem_closure`, since `root s_l = -p_l` (`root_reflectionPerm_self`).
   Different piece groups commute (`pieces_commute`, `commute_of_mem_pieceGroup`). So by
   `list_ofFn_prod_mul_inv`, `n n₀⁻¹ = ∏ₗ A_l B_l⁻¹`.
3. Write `μ_l = (pr l).1 Z_l (pr l).2` and `μ⁰_l = (pr₀ l).1 Z₀_l (pr₀ l).2`. Then
   `μ_l (μ⁰_l)⁻¹ = m_l^∨(w l)` by `oppositeProduct_mul_inv` (module
   `Rational.LinkedRootOppositePairs`). Hence
   `A_l B_l⁻¹ = m_l^∨(w l) · μ⁰_l (τ_l τ₀_l⁻¹) (μ⁰_l)⁻¹`.
4. `μ⁰_l` acts on characters by the reflection in `m_l`
   (`weylAction_oppositeFactorPair_eq`). So `exists_piece_torusPart` gives
   `A_l B_l⁻¹ = j(x_l)` with `ω(x_l) = (w l)^⟨ω, m_l^∨⟩` whenever its pairing condition holds.
   Now `m_l^∨ = p_l^∨ + q_l^∨` (`piece_coroot_m`), `⟨m_l, p_l^∨⟩ = 1` (`piece_pairings`), and
   `s_l^∨ = -p_l^∨`. So the condition is `⟨ω, q_l^∨⟩ = 0`, and then
   `⟨ω, m_l^∨⟩ = ⟨ω, p_l^∨⟩`.
5. Take `x = ∏ₗ x_l`. Then `j(x) = ∏ₗ j(x_l)` (`map_list_prod`), and
   `ω(x) = ∏ₗ ω(x_l)` (`List.prod_ofFn`).

## Use

Apply this with `ω` the fundamental weight of the simple root `p_{l₀}` of one piece, when all
`p_l`, `q_l` are simple roots. Then `ω(x) = w_{l₀}`, the `m`-coordinate of `g` at that piece.
So the simple-coroot coordinate at `p_{l₀}` of the torus element `n n₀⁻¹` is the merged-root
coordinate. This is the computation behind the `A₂`-orbit case of `Z(k) ⊆ G(k)^+` for
quasi-split simply connected groups.

## Provenance

This is an internal computation built from published Library results and the task's helper
modules. It makes no new mathematical assertion beyond these computations. Mathematically it
is the standard description of the torus part of the rank-one Weyl element `n(u)` of an
`SU(3)`-type root group: `n(u) = m^∨(w) · n(x_m(1)) · (coroot of p)`.

The `torusCocharacterPoint` interface specializes the existing
`CocharacterUniformizer.cocharacterPoints` from
