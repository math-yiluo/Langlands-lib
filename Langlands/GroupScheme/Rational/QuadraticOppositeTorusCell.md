# The three-factor open cell of a quadratic opposite-Borel torus

Over the quadratic field, a cocharacter of the maximal torus presents
the chosen Borel as a dynamic parabolic. Coefficient conjugation
transports the parabolic, centralizer and unipotent subgroup. The
centralizer is the scalar extension of the original torus, so it stays
fixed. The exact intersection of the two Borel images implies that the
conjugated unipotent subgroup is the opposite dynamic unipotent subgroup.

The product of the centralizer and the negative unipotent subgroup is
isomorphic to the negative parabolic as a scheme. Composing this
isomorphism with the opposite open multiplication cell gives the actual
open immersion `U × T × U⁻ → G`. Its value on an arbitrary scheme test
is the product of the three coordinates.

These are the algebraic cell and the coefficient action used in
Platonov and Rapinchuk, *Algebraic Groups and Number Theory* (1994),
Lemma 6.28, printed pp. 369–370.
