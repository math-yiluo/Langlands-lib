# The Tits normalizer on field points

Let \(k\) be any field and \(p\) a `SplitPinning` of an actual affine group
scheme \(G\) over \(k\). The pinning supplies connected reductivity and the
inclusion \(T \hookrightarrow G\) of the split maximal torus. The definitions
lie in `Langlands.GroupScheme.SplitPinning`.

## Pinned lifts and the quotient map

`normalizedSimpleRootParametrizations` takes the family supplied by
`normalizedRootExistence`. The subsingleton instance from
`SteinbergRootMapUniqueness` gives `normalizedSimpleRootParametrizations_eq`,
which identifies this family with every family normalized by \(p\).
`titsReflectionRepresentatives` then applies
`canonicalSimpleReflectionRepresentatives`, whose representative at each
simple root is `NormalizedRootParametrization.reflectionRepresentative`.
This lift is the product of the first opposite root point, the simple
root point of coordinate one, and the second opposite root point. The
ordered opposite pair is unique.

`titsNormalizerPoint` takes `SimpleReflectionRepresentative.normalizerPoint`
for each such representative. The existing action formula
`SimpleReflectionRepresentative.weylAction_normalizerPoint` shows that the
lift acts on the whole character lattice \(X^*(T)\) by the pinned
simple reflection.

`titsNormalizer` is the subgroup of `normalizerPoints p.inclusion` generated
by these lifts. `titsNormalizerGenerator` is each lift with its membership
proof from `Subgroup.subset_closure`. `titsWeylProjection` composes the inclusion
of this subgroup with the quotient \(N_G(T)(k) \to N_G(T)(k)/C_G(T)(k)\)
given by `relativeWeylGroup p.inclusion`. On each generator it is the quotient
class of the corresponding lift, by reflexivity.

## Simple coroot signs

`titsSignSubgroup` is the subgroup of \(T(k)\) generated by
\(\alpha^\vee(-1)\) for the simple roots \(\alpha\).
`titsSignGenerator` is each such value as a member of that subgroup. The
evaluation is `simpleCorootPoint` on the actual cocharacter. The map
`torusPointsToNormalizer` is induced by \(T \hookrightarrow G\).
The all-test commutativity lemma `range_le_torusCentralizer` puts the image
in the torus centralizer, and `torusCentralizer_carrier_base` specializes
this to rational points of the torus centralizer. Mapping the sign subgroup along
this homomorphism gives `titsSignSubgroupInNormalizer`.

For empty simple-root support the two generating ranges are empty, so
their subgroup closures are trivial. In characteristic two, \(-1=1\) in
the field's unit group, so every simple coroot sign is the identity and the
sign subgroup is trivial.

## Source

Jacques Tits, *Normalisateurs de tores I. Groupes de Coxeter étendus*,
Journal of Algebra 4 (1966), pp. 96–116. Source ID
`tits-1966-normalisateurs-de-tores-i`, SHA-256
`8b05f6f9017c869388181f16c5598f2094d511ea9e46173c2a7b6f68fa6de96f`.
Sections 4.2(1)–(3) and 4.4 (printed pp. 112–114, PDF pp. 17–19)
give the Weyl quotient of the Chevalley torus normalizer, its action, and
the rank-one square elements as coroot values at \(-1\). Section 4.6
(printed pp. 115–116, PDF pp. 20–21) realizes the extended Weyl group
as an image of the braid group in the normalizer when \(-1\ne1\).
The field-point construction here uses the subgroup generated by the pinned
lifts in every characteristic and forms the torus subgroup directly from the
source's coroot values.
