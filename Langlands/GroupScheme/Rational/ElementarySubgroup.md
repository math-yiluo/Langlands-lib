# The elementary subgroup from actual parabolic radicals

Authors: Worker 1331.

This module defines the subgroup `parabolicElementarySubgroup G` of the actual
rational points `G(k)` over an arbitrary field `k : Type u`. It uses all actual
parabolic subgroup embeddings `p : P ⟶ G` and all actual represented geometric
unipotent radicals `r : U ⟶ P`. A generator is a point in the range of
`rationalPoints.map (r ≫ p)`. The elementary subgroup is the ordinary algebraic
subgroup closure of that set, without a topology or a choice of representatives.

Here `IsParabolicSubgroup p` is the existing geometric parabolic predicate.
`LeviSubgroup.IsGeometricUnipotentRadical r` requires that `r` represent the
greatest smooth connected unipotent normal closed subgroup both over `k` and
after base change to its algebraic closure. The definition quantifies over
representatives; it asserts neither their existence nor descent for arbitrary
groups. No split torus, relative root group, or Kneser–Tits equality is assumed.

The module proves that every such radical's point image belongs to the
elementary subgroup and gives its least-subgroup universal property. An
ambient group-scheme isomorphism maps the generator set into the corresponding
generator set: replace `p` by its composite with the isomorphism and keep `r`.
Subgroup closure then gives inclusion of the images of the elementary
subgroups; the inverse isomorphism gives equality. In particular, applying
this to each inner automorphism proves that the elementary subgroup is normal.
Its equality with the normal closure of the generator set follows from
normality and the two universal properties of closure.

## Provenance and scope

G. Prasad and M. S. Raghunathan, *On the Kneser–Tits problem*, Comment.
Math. Helv. 60 (1985), 107–121, registered as
`prasad-raghunathan-1985-kneser-tits`, §1.1, printed p. 107 / PDF p. 2,
defines `G(k)^+` by normal generation from rational points of unipotent
radicals of parabolic `k`-subgroups. The authors work with semisimple groups
in this passage. The present definition makes sense for any group scheme and
uses the Library's actual parabolic and represented geometric radical
predicates. The normality and transport lemmas are internal consequences of
that definition and the published isomorphism transport of parabolic
subgroups. They justify agreement between ordinary and normal generation in
this interface, whenever the represented radicals are supplied.

The module does not prove local Kneser–Tits, existence of parabolic radicals,
or equality with the subgroup generated by relative-root groups of a maximal
split torus. Those remain separate proof obligations.
