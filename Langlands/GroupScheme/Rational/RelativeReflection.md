# Relative reflections

This module defines, for a group scheme homomorphism `i : T ⟶ G` over a field `k`,
which elements of the relative Weyl group `relativeWeylGroup i = N(k)/Z(k)` are
reflections in a relative root. The module needs only the instances `[Mono i.hom.hom.left]`
and `[IsAffineHom G.X.hom]`, which the published relative Weyl action uses. Reductivity,
maximal splitness and genericity are hypotheses of the later structural statements, not of
these definitions.

## Definitions

- `IsRelativeReflection i α w` (for `α : characterLattice T` and `w : relativeWeylGroup i`)
  has three parts.
  - `α` is a relative root (`α ∈ relativeRoots i`).
  - There is a linear form `f` on `V = relativeRootSpan i` with `f (1 ⊗ α) = 2`.
  - The published action `relativeWeylAction i w` on `V` equals Mathlib's
    `Module.reflection` for `1 ⊗ α` and `f`, that is, the map `y ↦ y - f y • (1 ⊗ α)`.

  Such a `w` sends `1 ⊗ α` to `-(1 ⊗ α)`
  (`IsRelativeReflection.relativeWeylAction_apply_self`).
- `relativeReflections i J` is the set of relative reflections in the roots of a set
  `J ⊆ X*(T)`: `{w | ∃ α ∈ J, IsRelativeReflection i α w}`.
- `simpleRelativeReflections i ν` (an abbreviation) is
  `relativeReflections i (simpleRelativeRoots i ν)`. These are the reflections in the
  simple relative roots of the cocharacter `ν`, which are published in
  `Rational.StandardParabolic`.

The linear form `f` is not part of the data. For a finite root set that spans `V` and is
preserved by the reflection, `f` is unique (the coroot), but the definition does not use
this.

## Relation to the sources

Borel–Tits (§5.3, §5.8) let `_kW = 𝒩(S)/𝒵(S)` act on `X*(S) ⊗ ℝ` and write `r_a` for the
orthogonal symmetry, for an admissible inner product, in the hyperplane annihilating the
`k`-root `a`. By §5.8 this is `r_a(c) = c - 2 (a, c)/(a, a) · a`, and §5.3 shows that these
symmetries generate `_kW`. The fundamental reflections of §5.16 are the `r_a` for simple `a`.
Renard V.4.1 writes `S_∅` for the set of reflections `s_α ∈ W_G` attached to the simple roots
`α ∈ Δ_∅`.

The definition here uses only the published action of `N(k)/Z(k)` on the `ℚ`-span of the
relative roots, which is the space spanned by `_kΦ`. On that span, `r_a` is the reflection
`y ↦ y - ⟨y, a^∨⟩ a`, with `a^∨(a) = 2`. So the Borel–Tits reflection `r_a` satisfies
`IsRelativeReflection i a r_a`. Two further facts make a relative reflection in `a` exactly
`r_a`. First, `N(k)/Z(k)` acts faithfully on the root span. Second, a reflection preserving
the finite spanning root set determines its linear form. Both facts are structural theorems
and are **not** assumed here. The uniqueness of the reflection of each simple root, and the
injectivity on simple roots, are stated in `HasRelativeTitsSystem`
(`Rational.RelativeTitsSystemStatement`).

The packaging is **derived**. It is a predicate on the actual quotient
`relativeWeylGroup i` and is stated through Mathlib's `Module.reflection`. It is not a
chosen element. So no choice is made, and the definitions also make sense when the
reflection does not exist or is not unique. In rank zero there are no simple relative
roots, and `simpleRelativeReflections i ν` is empty
(`simpleRelativeReflections_eq_empty`). Nonreduced relative root systems are allowed: when
`α` and `2α` are both roots, a reflection in one is a reflection in the other. Only `α`,
and not `2α`, can be simple, because `2α = α + α`.

## Elementary lemmas

- `mem_relativeReflections`: unfolding.
- `IsRelativeReflection.mem_relativeRoots`: the root of a relative reflection is a
  relative root.
- `IsRelativeReflection.relativeWeylAction_apply_self`: `w` sends `1 ⊗ α` to its negative.
- `relativeReflections_mono`, `relativeReflections_empty` and
  `relativeReflections_subset_simple`: monotonicity in `J`, the empty case, and
  `J ⊆ Δ ⇒ relativeReflections i J ⊆ simpleRelativeReflections i ν`.
- `simpleRelativeReflections_eq_empty`: rank zero.
