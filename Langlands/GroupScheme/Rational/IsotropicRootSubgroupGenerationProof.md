# Root subgroups generate an isotropic almost-simple group: proof

Authors: Worker 180.

The theorem `Langlands.GroupScheme.isotropicRootSubgroupGeneration` has type
exactly `IsotropicRootSubgroupGenerationStatement.{u}` (module
`Rational.IsotropicRootSubgroupGenerationStatement`), at arbitrary universe
`u`: for `G` affine, almost simple and not anisotropic over any field, with
maximal split torus `i`, every closed subgroup `h : H ⟶ G` whose range
contains the range of `i` and every relative root subgroup has full range.
The module also proves three helper theorems. Everything uses only the
standard axioms.

## Helper theorems

`dynamicUnipotent_le_of_rootSubgroups_le`: for connected reductive `G`, a
maximal split torus `i`, any cocharacter `ν` of `S` and any subgroup
functor `F` containing every relative root subgroup,
`Dynamic.unipotent (CommGroupScheme.groupHom ν ≫ i) ≤ F`. The indivisible
positive roots form a finite set (`HasRelativeRootSystem.finite_relativeRoots`
for the published `relativeRootSystem`); enumerating them, the published
`RelativeRoot.dynamicRootOrderedProduct` writes the representative of the
dynamic radical as the image of the ordered product of the root-subgroup
representatives. A point of the radical is therefore a finite product of
points of root subgroups, which lies in `F`.

`IsSemisimple.relativeRoots_nonempty`: a semisimple, not anisotropic group
has a relative root for every maximal split torus. Otherwise the relative
root span is zero, the identity is a derived-subgroup inclusion (by
`semisimplePerfect`), so every rational character vanishes
(`IsDerivedSubgroup.characterRestrict_eq_zero`), and the published
`relativeRankIdentity` gives split rank zero.

`Dynamic.OppositeEnvelope.isNormal_range`: for a smooth connected affine `G`,
a cocharacter `χ` and a smooth affine representative `D` of the closed
subgroup generated by `(u, t, v) ↦ u · χ(t) · v` on `U(χ) × 𝔾_m × U(χ⁻¹)`
(the Library's `Dynamic.OppositeEnvelope`), the range of `D` is normal. The
published `isNormalizedBy_parabolic` and `reversed_unipotent_le` feed
`Dynamic.normalized_subgroup_product_smooth_surjective`, so the
multiplication `D × P(χ) ⟶ G` is smooth and surjective. The normalizer of
the range of `D` has a closed representative
(`hasClosedRepresentative_normalizer_centralizer_range`), and both factors
normalize `D`, so the multiplication factors through it. `G` is reduced
(`isReduced_of_smooth_over_field`), so the surjective multiplication is
scheme-theoretically dominant, and `exists_lift_of_isSchemeTheoreticallyDominant`
lifts the identity of `G` through the normalizer: every point of `G`
normalizes `D`.

## Proof of the theorem

Choose a generic cocharacter `ν` (`IsSplitTorus.exists_genericCocharacter`)
and put `χ = ν ≫ i`; the reversed cocharacter of `χ` is `(-ν) ≫ i`. By the
first helper, `H` contains `U(χ)` and `U(χ⁻¹)`, and it contains the image of
`χ` because it contains `S`. Let `D` be the smooth connected generated
subgroup of `Dynamic.OppositeEnvelope.exists_generatedImage`. The generating
morphism factors through the closed subgroup `h` (`comp_map` writes its value
as a product of three such points), so the range of `D` lies in that of `h`
(`GeneratedImage.range_le`). By the third helper the range of `D` is normal,
and `D` is smooth and connected, so almost simplicity of `G` makes it trivial
or everything. If it were trivial, `χ` would be trivial, hence `ν = 0`
because `i` is a monomorphism; but a relative root exists (second helper)
and pairs nontrivially with the generic `ν`. So the range of `D`, and hence
that of `h`, is everything.

## Relation to the literature

This follows Borel–Tits, *Groupes réductifs* (1965), 4.11, registered as
`borel-tits-1965-groupes-reductifs`: the subgroup generated by the unipotent
radicals of two opposite parabolic subgroups is normal, because its
normalizer contains an open cell. Here the opposite parabolics are
`P(χ)` and `P(χ⁻¹)`, the open-cell argument is replaced by smooth
surjectivity of the product and scheme-theoretic dominance, and the torus
`χ(𝔾_m)` is included in the generated subgroup, as in the Library's
`OppositeEnvelope`.
