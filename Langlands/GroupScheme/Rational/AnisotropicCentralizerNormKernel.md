# Anisotropic centralizers and their full rational norm kernels

Authors: Worker 1676.

This module derives the anisotropic case of the rank-one centralizer-generation
input from published compactness and split-rank results. It does not use
Kneser–Tits, an assumed root-group generation theorem, or simple connectedness.
The field and group schemes have arbitrary universe `u`.

For any group scheme `G` over a field and any split subtorus
`i : S.toGrp ⟶ G`, `IsAnisotropic.torusCentralizer_eq_top` proves equality
of the full subgroup functor `torusCentralizer i` with the whole group.
Anisotropy means that the split rank is zero. The published
`subsingleton_hom_of_isSplitSubtorus` then identifies `i` with the constant
identity homomorphism. Every point commutes with its identity value after
every refinement of every test scheme. This proves the all-test equality,
not just an equality of rational-point subgroups.

For every supplied representative `j : Z ⟶ G` of that centralizer,
`IsAnisotropic.torusCentralizerIso` constructs an isomorphism `Z ≅ G`.
It uses the published uniqueness of representatives of the whole subgroup
functor. Its homomorphism is precisely `j`, as proved by
`torusCentralizerIso_hom`.

Over a nonarchimedean local field, for connected reductive anisotropic `G`,
`IsAnisotropic.rationalCommonNormKernel_eq_top` proves
`Plancherel.rationalCommonNormKernel G = ⊤`. The implemented proof first
uses `anisotropicCompactness_proof` to obtain compactness of the full
rational-point set in the canonical point topology. The published
`compactSubgroup_le_rationalCommonNormKernel` applies to the whole subgroup,
so every rational point has norm one under every rational character.

The represented centralizer is connected reductive by transport across
`torusCentralizerIso` and has split rank zero by the published invariance
of split rank under isomorphism. Applying the preceding norm-kernel result
to `Z` proves `map_normKernel_torusCentralizer_eq_range`: the image of
`Z(k)^1` under the actual map `rationalPoints.map j` is the entire image
of `Z(k)`.

Finally, `centralizer_le_rankOne_sup_normKernel` gives exactly the subgroup
containment used in the rank-one centralizer-generation input: the image
of `Z(k)` lies in the supremum of the subgroup generated by
`centralizerPoints i ∩ ⟨U_a(k), U_{-a}(k)⟩`, over relative roots `a`,
and the image of `Z(k)^1`. The equality of the second summand with the whole
image proves this containment immediately. The root subgroups are the actual
published `RelativeRoot.subgroup` functors.

These are internal consequences of the cited Lean declarations, with no new
literature assertion. The split subtorus need not be maximal, and the group
need not be simply connected. The isotropic case, generation for an
arbitrary simply connected group, and the exact normalizer facet-fixing
theorem remain open.
