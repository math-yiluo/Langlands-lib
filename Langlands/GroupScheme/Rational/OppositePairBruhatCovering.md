# The Bruhat covering for a generic dynamic parabolic from opposite root pairs

Module `Langlands.GroupScheme.Rational.OppositePairBruhatCovering`.

## Setting

Throughout, `k : Type u` is a field and `G` is a group scheme over `Spec k` with
`[IsAffineHom G.X.hom]` and `hG : IsConnectedReductive G`. The torus
`i : T.toGrp ⟶ G` is a maximal split torus (`hi : IsMaximalSplitTorus i`), and
`ν : cocharacterLattice T` is a cocharacter of `T`. Write `χ = groupHom ν ≫ i` for
the corresponding cocharacter of `G`, `U(ν)` for the dynamic unipotent radical
`Dynamic.unipotent χ`, and `P(ν)` for the dynamic parabolic `Dynamic.parabolic χ`.
The closed subgroup scheme `Dynamic.Affine.parabolicHom χ` represents `P(ν)`.

The single rank-one hypothesis is `hE : RelativeRoot.HasOppositeFactorPairs i`. This
Library predicate states the following. For every relative root `a ∈ relativeRoots i`
and every rational point `x ≠ 1` of the root group `RelativeRoot.subgroup i a`, there
are rational points `p₁, p₂` of the opposite root group `RelativeRoot.subgroup i (-a)`
with `p₁ * x * p₂ ∈ normalizerPoints i`. The predicate is
`RelativeRoot.IsOppositeFactorPair i a x (p₁, p₂)`. It asserts nothing about
uniqueness, about the factors being nontrivial, or about reflections.

Genericity is `hν : IsGenericCocharacter i ν`: `⟨α, ν⟩ ≠ 0` for every relative root `α`.

## Statements

The module proves six results. All of them are theorems; none is a new definition
or proposition.

1. `RelativeRoot.hasOrderedRootPointFactorization_of_disjoint_positive_rays hG hi ν hdisj :
   HasOrderedRootPointFactorization i ν`. Here `k` is any field. The hypothesis `hdisj`
   says that distinct roots `a ≠ b` in `RelativeRoot.indivisiblePositiveRoots i ν` have
   disjoint positive integral rays `RelativeRoot.cyclicTail a 1` and
   `RelativeRoot.cyclicTail b 1`. The conclusion is the Library predicate
   `HasOrderedRootPointFactorization i ν`: for every injective enumeration
   `a : Fin n → characterLattice T` of the indivisible positive roots, every rational
   point of `U(ν)` is an ordered product `g 0 * ⋯ * g (n-1)` with `g r` in the root
   group of `a r`.
2. `exists_relativeRootGenerated_mul_centralizer_of_isSepClosed [IsSepClosed k] hG hi hν hdisj g`.
   The hypothesis `hdisj` is the same ray-disjointness, now for both `ν` and `-ν`. The
   conclusion: every `g : rationalPoints G` equals `h * z` with
   `h ∈ relativeRootGeneratedSubgroup i` (the abstract subgroup generated by the rational
   points of all relative root groups) and `z ∈ centralizerPoints i`.
3. `disjoint_positive_rays_of_oppositeFactorPairs hG hi hE ξ`: under `hE`, the ray
   hypothesis of (1) holds for every cocharacter `ξ`.
4. `hasRootGeneratedBruhatCovering_normalizerPoints_of_oppositeFactorPairs hG hi hν hE :
   HasRootGeneratedBruhatCovering i ν (normalizerPoints i)`, for any field `k`. That is,
   `relativeRootGeneratedSubgroup i ⊆ U(ν)(k) · N(k) · U(ν)(k)`, with
   `N(k) = normalizerPoints i`.
5. `exists_unipotent_normalizer_unipotent_of_oppositeFactorPairs [IsSepClosed k] hG hi hν hE g`:
   every rational point is `u * n * v` with `u, v ∈ RationalPoint.unipotent χ` and
   `n ∈ normalizerPoints i`.
6. `borelTorusBruhatCovering_parabolicHom_of_oppositeFactorPairs [IsSepClosed k] hG hi hν hE :
   BorelTorusBruhatCovering (Dynamic.Affine.parabolicHom χ) i`. This is the Library
   predicate `∀ g, ∃ b₁ b₂ n, g = (b₁ ≫ ι) * n * (b₂ ≫ ι)`, where `ι` is the inclusion of
   the representative of `P(ν)`, the `bᵢ` are its rational points and
   `n : normalizerPoints i`.

Despite its name, `BorelTorusBruhatCovering` has no Borel hypothesis: its first argument
is any morphism of group schemes. Result (6) does **not** claim that `P(ν)` is a Borel
subgroup, and no step of the proof uses or proves that. No step uses properness of
`G/P` either.

## Proofs

**(1)** The proof follows the Library proof of `dynamicUnipotent_le_of_disjoint_positive_rays`
(module `Rational.DynamicRadicalRootGeneration`). The ordered product of root groups
replaces the generated subgroup.

- `RelativeRoot.semigroupRealization` realizes the positive semigroup
  `positiveSemigroup ν` as a smooth connected unipotent subgroup `U`, stable under the
  conjugation action of the torus.
- `actionWeightSupport_positiveSemigroup` identifies its weight support with the
  positive relative roots. `iUnion_rootRayBlock` and `rootRayBlock_pairwise`, using
  `hdisj`, decompose this support into the disjoint ray blocks of the enumerated
  indivisible roots.
- `hasOrderedSemigroupProducts_of_unipotent` makes multiplication of the block subgroups,
  in the given order, an isomorphism onto `U`.
- `RelativeRoot.cyclicRootFunctorComparison` identifies each block subgroup with the
  actual root group `RelativeRoot.subgroup i (a r)`.
- `RelativeRoot.dynamicSemigroupComparison` identifies `U` with `U(ν)`.
- `exists_orderedRootPoints` reads off the ordered factorization of rational points.

**(2)** Put `N = relativeRootGeneratedSubgroup i`. By
`dynamicUnipotent_le_relativeRootGeneratedSubgroup` and `hdisj`, both `U(ν)(k)` and
`U(-ν)(k)` lie in `N`. The `-ν` case is rewritten with
`groupHom_neg_comp_eq_oppositeCocharacter`. Over a separably closed field, rational
points of the smooth scheme `G` are dense
(`PointTopology.hasDenseRationalPoints_of_smooth_of_isSepClosed`). The opposite-cell
multiplication `U(-ν) × P(ν) → G` is an open immersion
(`Dynamic.isOpenImmersion_oppositeCellMul`), and its image contains the identity, so it
is nonempty. `Dynamic.exists_mem_open_inv_mul_mem_open` then gives a rational `c` with
`c` and `c⁻¹ * g` in the open cell. `Dynamic.exists_centralizer_of_mem_cell` gives
`c * z₁⁻¹ ∈ N` and `c⁻¹ * g * z₂⁻¹ ∈ N` with `zᵢ` rational points of the dynamic
centralizer of `χ`. The Library `genericCentralizer` identifies that centralizer with
the torus centralizer, since `ν` is generic. So `zᵢ ∈ centralizerPoints i`.
Centralizer points normalize `N`
(`normalizerPoints_le_normalizer_relativeRootGeneratedSubgroup`). Then the `group`
tactic checks
`g = ((c * z₁⁻¹) * (z₁ * (c⁻¹ * g * z₂⁻¹) * z₁⁻¹)) * (z₁ * z₂)`, and the first factor
lies in `N`.

**(3)** This is `HasOppositeFactorPairs.disjoint_cyclicTail_indivisibleRoots` (module
`Rational.RootRayFromFactorPairs`), applied to the indivisible positive roots of `ξ`.

**(4)** All inputs come from `hE` and from the Library's per-group Tits-word theory.

- `RelativeRoot.hasRelativeRootSystem_of_hasOppositeFactorPairs` gives
  `HasRelativeRootSystem i`.
- `RelativeRootReflectionData.ofOppositeFactorPairs` packages the root reflections.
  `hasRelativeWeylCoxeterSystem_of_rootPairingWeylCoxeter`, fed with the published
  abstract `Langlands.Group.rootPairingWeylCoxeterStatement_proof` (the Weyl group of a
  finite crystallographic root pairing is a Coxeter group on its simple reflections),
  gives a Coxeter system on the simple roots of `ν` whose simple generators are relative
  reflections.
- (3) and (1) give `HasOrderedRootPointFactorization i ν`.
  `hasSimpleRootUnipotentFactorization_of_orderedRootPoints` turns it into the
  simple-root factorization of `U(ν)(k)`.
- `hasRootGeneratedSimpleReflectionLifts_of_oppositeFactorPairs` gives lifts `m` of the
  simple reflections inside the rank-one subgroups.
- `hasRootGeneratedBruhatCovering_rankOneLiftPoints` gives the covering by
  `U(ν)(k) · ⟨m⟩ · U(ν)(k)`. The lift subgroup lies in `normalizerPoints i`
  (`RelativeRoot.rankOneLiftPoints_le_normalizerPoints`).

**(5)** By (2) and (3), `g = h * z` with `h` root-generated and `z` centralizing the
torus. By (4), `h = u * n * v`. Then `g = u * (n * z) * (z⁻¹ * v * z)`. Here `n * z` is
a normalizer point, and `z⁻¹ * v * z ∈ U(ν)(k)` by
`centralizerPoints_conj_mem_dynamicUnipotent`.

**(6)** By (5), `g = u * n * v`. `RationalPoint.unipotent_le_parabolic` puts `u, v` in
`P(ν)(k)`. Since `Dynamic.Affine.parabolicHom χ` represents `P(ν)`
(`Dynamic.Affine.isRepresentedBy_parabolicHom`), `u` and `v` are images of rational
points `b₁, b₂` of the representative.

## Independence audit

A private dependency audit followed the full type and value closure of every constant
in this module, restricted to project constants. The six results above, and the Library
inputs `semigroupRealization`, `dynamicSemigroupComparison`,
`cyclicRootFunctorComparison`, `rootPairingWeylCoxeterStatement_proof`,
`hasDenseRationalPoints_of_smooth_of_isSepClosed`, `genericCentralizer`,
`oppositeCellStatement`, `hasRelativeRootSystem_of_hasOppositeFactorPairs`,
`hasRootGeneratedSimpleReflectionLifts_of_oppositeFactorPairs` and
`hasRootGeneratedBruhatCovering_rankOneLiftPoints`, reach none of the following:

- `ParabolicDynamic*`, `RegularCocharacterBorel*`, `RankOneSolvableObstruction*`,
  `RankOneBorelDynamic*`, `RankOnePositiveRootUniqueness*`;
- `rootDatumExistence`, `rootReducedness`, `reductiveGeometricReductivity`,
  `absoluteBruhatCovering`, `steinbergBigCell`;
- `splitRootLieLine`, `splitRankOneRootCell`;
- `isParabolicSubgroup_of_range_eq_dynamicParabolic`.

As a control, the same audit flags `isParabolicSubgroup_of_range_eq_dynamicParabolic`
itself. The module does not use the Library's `rationalBruhat`,
`rootRankOneFactorization`, `relativeRootSystem`, `relativeWeylCoxeter` or
`relativeTitsSystem`. Those depend on properness of `G/P` or on `rootReducedness`. Their
per-group lemmas, which take the rank-one input as a hypothesis, are used instead.

## Provenance

This is an internal assembly of Library theorems; the hypothesis `hE` is the only
rank-one input. Mathematically it is the Tits-system proof of the Bruhat decomposition
`G(k) = B(k) N(k) B(k)` (Borel–Tits, *Groupes réductifs*, Publ. Math. IHÉS 27, 1965,
Théorème 5.15, registered in the Library literature as
`borel-tits-1965-groupes-reductifs`). The generation step (2) is the standard argument
that a dense open cell `U⁻ · P` gives `G(k) = (U⁻ · P)(k) · (U⁻ · P)(k)`. The Lean route
is the one described above; it was written so that the Bruhat covering
hypothesis of the Library's generic big-cell assembly
(`SplitPinning.highestWeightBigCellGeometricData`) can be supplied without the excluded
regular-cocharacter, Borel and parabolic-properness inputs.
