# The simple-root parabolic factorization from the ordered root product

This internal auxiliary module supports the proof task for
`Langlands.GroupScheme.RelativeTitsSystemStatement`. It defines the internal predicate
`HasOrderedRootPointFactorization i ν`: every point of `U(ν)(k)` is an ordered product of
root-subgroup points. It proves the predicate `HasSimpleRootParabolicFactorization i ν` (module
`Langlands.GroupScheme.Rational.RelativeTitsMulCellReduction`) from published structural
statements. It then removes that predicate from the reduction
`relativeTitsSystemStatement_of_rankOneFactorization` of the same module. Finally it supplies the
nondegeneracy field `conj_not_le` from the published nonidentity simple-root points.

At the level of the published statements, `RelativeTitsSystemStatement` is reduced to the
following nine inputs (`relativeTitsSystemStatement_of_structuralStatements`):

- `RelativeWeylCoxeterStatement`;
- `StandardParabolicEndpointsStatement`;
- `RationalBruhatStatement`;
- `RootRankOneFactorizationStatement`;
- `RelativeRootSystemStatement`;
- `GenericCentralizerStatement`;
- `RelativeRoot.DynamicRootOrderedProductStatement`;
- `RelativeRoot.RootSubgroupRealizationStatement`;
- `RelativeRoot.RootSubgroupVectorStatement`.

None of these inputs is proved here, so the module does not prove the target. It adds no
axioms.

## Notation

`B = standardParabolicPoints i ν ∅` is `P_∅(k)`, and `N = normalizerPoints i`. For a character
`a`, `U_a(k)` is the group of `k`-points `(RelativeRoot.subgroup i a).carrier (Over.mk (𝟙 _))`.
`P(ν)`, `Z(ν)` and `U(ν)` are `Dynamic.parabolic`, `Dynamic.centralizer` and
`Dynamic.unipotent` of the cocharacter `CommGroupScheme.groupHom ν ≫ i`. `Z(k)` is
`centralizerPoints i`, the `k`-points of `torusCentralizer i`. The indivisible positive roots
are `RelativeRoot.indivisiblePositiveRoots i ν`: the relative roots `c` with
`0 < ⟨c, ν⟩` that are not `m • b` for a relative root `b` and a natural number `m > 1`.

## Combinatorics of simple roots

Let the simple roots `simpleRelativeRoots i ν` be linearly independent after `toRat`, in
`ℚ ⊗[ℤ] X*(T)`.

- `exists_simpleRelativeRootCoord` gives coordinate functionals `g b : ℚ ⊗[ℤ] X*(T) →ₗ[ℚ] ℚ`,
  one for each simple root `b`, with `g b (toRat c) = 1` if `c = b` and `0` otherwise, for
  simple roots `c`. Each `g b` extends the coordinate `b` of the basis `Module.Basis.span hli`
  of the span of the simple roots, by `LinearMap.exists_extend`.
- `coord_of_mem_closure_simpleRelativeRoots`: let `x` lie in the additive monoid generated by
  the simple roots. Then every coordinate `g b (toRat x)` is nonnegative. If every coordinate of
  `x` away from a simple root `a` vanishes, then `x = m • a` for a natural number `m`. The proof
  is induction on the closure.
- `simpleRelativeRoot_mem_indivisiblePositiveRoots`: a simple root `a` is indivisible. Let
  `m • b = a` with `b` a relative root and `m > 1`. Then `b` is positive, so it lies in the monoid
  generated by the simple roots (`positiveRelativeRoots_subset_closure_simpleRelativeRoots`). The
  coordinates of `b` away from `a` are `0`, so `b = m' • a`. The `a`-coordinate then gives
  `m * m' = 1`, which contradicts `m > 1`.
- `pairing_weylAction_pos_of_isRelativeReflection`: let `ν` be generic and the relative roots be
  stable under negation. Let `n ∈ N` represent a relative reflection in a simple root `a`, and
  let `c ≠ a` be an indivisible positive root. Then `0 < ⟨weylAction i n c, ν⟩`.

  Proof of the last item: `c` lies in the monoid generated by the simple roots. Some coordinate
  `g b (toRat c)` with `b ≠ a` is positive. Otherwise `c = m • a`, and `m = 0`, `m = 1` and
  `m ≥ 2` contradict `c ≠ 0`, `c ≠ a` and the indivisibility of `c`. The reflection formula in
  `IsRelativeReflection` gives `toRat (weylAction i n c) = toRat c - f(c) • toRat a`. Since
  `g b (toRat a) = 0`, the `b`-coordinate of `weylAction i n c` equals that of `c` and is
  positive. `weylAction i n c` is a relative root (`weylAction_mapsTo_relativeRoots`), so its
  pairing with the generic `ν` is nonzero. If the pairing were negative, `-weylAction i n c`
  would be a positive root, and its `b`-coordinate would be nonnegative. That contradicts
  positivity.

## Points of `U(ν)` as ordered products

`exists_orderedRootPoints` takes the following data:

- a representative `jU : U ⟶ G` of `U(ν)`;
- representatives `j r : H r ⟶ G` of `U_{a r}` for `r : Fin n`;
- an isomorphism `e : (∏ᶜ H).X ≅ U.X` with
  `e.hom ≫ jU.hom.hom = RelativeRoot.orderedMultiplication j`.

It proves that every `x ∈ U(ν)(k)` is `(List.ofFn g).prod` with `g r ∈ U_{a r}(k)`. The proof
writes `x` as a `k`-point `y` of `U`. It splits `y ≫ e.inv` into its components with
`RelativeRoot.finiteProductPointsEquiv` and multiplies them with
`RelativeRoot.comp_orderedMultiplication`.

## The ordered factorization predicate

`HasOrderedRootPointFactorization i ν` (with `[IsAffineHom G.X.hom]`) states the following. For
every `n : ℕ` and every injective `a : Fin n → X*(T)` whose range is the set of indivisible
positive roots, every `x ∈ U(ν)(k)` is `(List.ofFn g).prod` for some
`g : Fin n → G(k)` with `g r ∈ U_{a r}(k)` for all `r`. Thus every point of `U(ν)(k)` is an
ordered product of root-subgroup points, in every order of the indivisible positive roots. No
uniqueness of the factors is asserted.

`hasOrderedRootPointFactorization_of_statement` derives the predicate from
`RelativeRoot.DynamicRootOrderedProductStatement`. It covers every field `k`, connected reductive
`G`, maximal split torus `i` and cocharacter `ν`, generic or not. For given `a`, the statement
supplies representatives and the isomorphism `e`, and `exists_orderedRootPoints` gives the
factorization.

## The factorization

`hasSimpleRootParabolicFactorization_of_orderedRootPoints` assumes the following:

- `hν`: `ν` is generic;
- `hneg`: the relative roots are stable under negation;
- `hli`: the simple roots are linearly independent after `toRat`;
- `hfin`: there are finitely many relative roots;
- `hB`: the minimal endpoint identity `B = P(ν)(k)`;
- `hZ`: the generic centralizer identity `Z(ν) = torusCentralizer i`;
- `hprod`: `HasOrderedRootPointFactorization i ν`.

It proves `HasSimpleRootParabolicFactorization i ν`. The proof follows Borel–Tits 5.16. Fix a
simple root `a`, a relative reflection `s` in `a` and `b ∈ B`.

1. `a` is an indivisible positive root (`simpleRelativeRoot_mem_indivisiblePositiveRoots`). By
   `RelativeRoot.exists_fin_enumeration_indivisiblePositiveRoots`, the indivisible positive roots
   have an injective enumeration. Composing it with a transposition puts `a` at the last index
   `Fin.last m`.
2. By `hB`, `b ∈ P(ν)(k)`, so `b` has a limit `z` (`Dynamic.exists_hasLimit`). The limit lies in
   `Z(ν)(k)` (`Dynamic.HasLimit.mem_centralizer`; `G` is separated because it is affine). So
   `z ∈ Z(k)` by `hZ`. The point `z` is its own limit, so `z⁻¹ * b` has limit `z⁻¹ * z = 1`.
   That is, `z⁻¹ * b ∈ U(ν)(k)`.
3. By `hprod`, `z⁻¹ * b = v' * u`, where `v'` is the ordered product of the points `g r` with
   `r < Fin.last m` and `u = g (Fin.last m) ∈ U_a(k)`.
4. Take `b' = z * v'`, so `b = b' * u`. Let `n'` be a representative of `s`. Then
   `n' z n'⁻¹ ∈ Z(k)` (`conj_mem_centralizerPoints`), which lies in `Z(ν)(k) ⊆ P(ν)(k) = B`.
   Each factor `g r` with `r < Fin.last m` lies in `U_c(k)` for an indivisible positive root
   `c ≠ a`. Then `n' g r n'⁻¹ ∈ U_{weylAction n' c}(k)` (`RelativeRoot.normalizer_conj_mem`).
   This root pairs positively with `ν` (`pairing_weylAction_pos_of_isRelativeReflection`), so
   `n' g r n'⁻¹ ∈ U(ν)(k)` (`RelativeRoot.subgroup_le_dynamicUnipotent`). Hence
   `n' v' n'⁻¹ ∈ U(ν)(k) ⊆ P(ν)(k) = B`, and `n' b' n'⁻¹ = (n' z n'⁻¹)(n' v' n'⁻¹) ∈ B`.

The factor `b'` constructed here lies in `B`, but the predicate does not record this.

`hasSimpleRootParabolicFactorization_of_statements` assumes `RelativeRootSystemStatement`,
`StandardParabolicEndpointsStatement`, `GenericCentralizerStatement` and
`RelativeRoot.DynamicRootOrderedProductStatement`, all at universe `u`. It proves
`HasSimpleRootParabolicFactorization i ν` for every field `k`, connected reductive `G`, maximal
split torus `i` and generic `ν`, with the `haveI` instances of the target statement. For fixed
data:

- `HasRelativeRootSystem i` gives `hneg`, `hli` and `hfin` through
  `HasRelativeRootSystem.neg_mem_relativeRoots`, `.linearIndependent_simpleRelativeRoots` and
  `.finite_relativeRoots`. The split torus comes from `hi.isSplitSubtorus.1`.
- `hB` is the first conjunct of the endpoint statement.
- `hZ` is the generic centralizer statement.
- `hprod` is `hasOrderedRootPointFactorization_of_statement`.

`relativeTitsSystemStatement_of_rootProduct` assumes the first seven inputs listed at the top
and the nondegeneracy field `conj_not_le` for all data, as the hypothesis `hconj`. It proves
`RelativeTitsSystemStatement.{u}`. It applies
`relativeTitsSystemStatement_of_rankOneFactorization` and supplies its `hfac` input by
`hasSimpleRootParabolicFactorization_of_statements`.

`relativeTitsSystemStatement_of_structuralStatements` assumes the nine inputs listed at the top.
It proves `RelativeTitsSystemStatement.{u}` by `relativeTitsSystemStatement_of_rootProduct`.
For fixed data, `hconj` is `RelativeSimpleRootPointData.conj_not_le` (module
`RelativeTitsNondegeneracy`), applied to the split torus `hi.isSplitSubtorus.1` and to the
point data `RelativeSimpleRootPointData.of_rootSubgroup_statements` (module
`RootSubgroupNontrivial`). That data is built from the realization and vector statements, the
relative root system `hroot k G hG T i hi`, `hG` and `hi`.

## Provenance

The results are internal and derived. The factorization follows the proof of Borel–Tits,
*Groupes réductifs* (1965), Proposition 5.16 (printed p. 101). There `U` is the semidirect
product of the unipotent radical `V_a` of the standard parabolic subgroup `P_{a}` and of
`U_(a)`, and the proof uses `r · Z(S)(k) V_a(k) = Z(S)(k) V_a(k) · r`. In this module, `V_a(k)`
is replaced by the explicit product `v'` of the root-subgroup points for the indivisible
positive roots other than `a`. The module proves the fact about `r` that it needs: `r` sends
every indivisible positive root other than `a` to a positive root. The proof uses coordinates
with respect to the simple roots. The ordered product of root subgroups is the published
`RelativeRoot.DynamicRootOrderedProductStatement`, which is not proved here.
`HasOrderedRootPointFactorization` records only the consequence of that statement on
`k`-points that the proof uses. With `a` placed last, it replaces the decomposition
`U = V_a · U_(a)` of Borel–Tits 5.16. The definition is internal and has no separate literature
source.

The Lean proof uses the published limit map of the dynamic parabolic, root-subgroup conjugation
by `N(k)`, positivity of root subgroups, the positive-root induction and the ordered-product
point equivalence. The final assembly uses the published nondegeneracy results of
`RelativeTitsNondegeneracy` and `RootSubgroupNontrivial`.
