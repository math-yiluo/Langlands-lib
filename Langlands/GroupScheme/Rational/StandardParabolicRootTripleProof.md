# The standard parabolic triple of relative roots

This module proves the published `Langlands.GroupScheme.StandardParabolicRootTripleStatement`
of Renard V.4.2 (printed p.118 / PDF p.124), in both ambient root sets in the
statement. The theorem has arbitrary universe parameter `u`; it quantifies over
an arbitrary field k, a connected reductive group G, a maximal split torus i, a
generic cocharacter ν, and any subset Θ of the actual simple relative roots.
The ambient set is either all relative roots or the indivisible relative roots.
The original source is David Renard, *Représentations des groupes
réductifs p-adiques*, French edition of 9 July 2026; the proposition cites
Borel–Tits, *Groupes réductifs* (1965), §§5.12–5.18.

The proof uses the published actual relative-root system and chooses a standard
cocharacter ζ vanishing on Θ and positive on the other simple roots. The helper
module `StandardRootClosure` proves positive-root induction in either ambient
set. This identifies the generated parabolic and Levi sets with the weak and
zero loci of ζ. The zero locus on roots is exactly the integral additive subgroup
generated by Θ, and the strict locus is positive for the original generic
cocharacter ν. These facts give the two integral-span formulas, the Levi part of
the parabolic triple, symmetry, and unipotence with the identity Weyl element.
A simple root outside Θ has strictly positive ζ-value, so its negative is absent
from the parabolic set; this also recovers Θ.

Convexity uses explicit closed cones in the real character space with its module
topology. A cocharacter gives a real linear functional by the published real
extension of the lattice pairing; it is continuous in that topology. The weak
locus is a closed halfspace and the zero locus the intersection of opposite
halfspaces. For the strict locus choose a positive integer B bounding the
ν-pairing on the finite set of roots. On roots, strict positivity for ζ is
equivalent to ν ≥ 0 and Bζ − ν ≥ 0: the forward implication uses integral
ζ-values at least one, while the converse uses genericity of ν and B > 0.
The intersection of those two closed halfspaces is therefore a closed cone for
the unipotent part. This construction supplies closedness directly and does not
require a cone-hull closedness theorem.

The route is an internal derivation from the published root-system,
indecomposable-root comparison, standard-cocharacter and sign lemmas. It proves
only the combinatorial triple statement, with the arbitrary-field hypotheses
and both ambient sets of that statement.
