# Compatibility of the fixed closed torus envelope with algebraic closure

Worker 1523. This module states, as two propositions, that the fixed closed
envelope of ground-field tori is compatible with base change to the algebraic
closure. Neither proposition is proved here.

## Statements

For a field `k : Type u` and a group scheme `G` over `Spec k` that is smooth,
affine and has connected underlying space:

- `TorusGeneratedEnvelopeAlgebraicClosureInfiniteStatement.{u}` asserts, for
  every *infinite* such `k`, the equality of subgroup functors
  `(closedTorusEnvelope G).baseChange (toAlgebraicClosure k) =
  closedTorusEnvelope ((GroupScheme.baseChange (toAlgebraicClosure k)).obj G)`.
- `TorusGeneratedEnvelopeAlgebraicClosureFiniteStatement.{u}` asserts the same
  equality for every *finite* such `k`.

Both sides are the existing objects from
`Langlands.GroupScheme.Rational.TorusGeneratedEnvelope`. `closedTorusEnvelope G`
is the intersection, on every test scheme, of the ranges of all closed subgroups
of `G` that contain every closed ground-field torus of `G`.
`SubgroupFunctor.baseChange` along `toAlgebraicClosure k : Spec k̄ ⟶ Spec k`
admits a point of `G_k̄` over a `k̄`-scheme `T'` exactly when the corresponding
`k`-point of `G` over `T'` (regarded as a `k`-scheme) lies in the envelope. The
right side is the same intersection construction in `G_k̄`, so it uses all closed
`k̄`-tori of `G_k̄`. The equality is an equality of subgroup functors: it holds
on every test scheme, reduced or not. Neither side is assumed representable.
No reductivity, solvability, perfection or characteristic restriction is
imposed.

## Source

Conrad–Gabber–Prasad, *Pseudo-reductive groups*, second edition (2015),
Proposition A.2.11, printed pp.477–480 (one-based PDF pp.503–506), registered as
`conrad-gabber-prasad-2015-pseudo-reductive`. For a smooth connected affine group
`G` over a field `k`, let `G_t` be the smooth connected `k`-subgroup generated by
the `k`-tori. The proposition's second paragraph asserts that "the formation of
`G_t` commutes with any extension of the ground field". The two statements here
are the special case of that extension `k̄/k`, in the form `(G_t)_k̄ = (G_k̄)_t`.

The source does not identify its generated subgroup `G_t` with the
intersection-defined `closedTorusEnvelope`. That identification is part of the
formalization: `G_t` is a closed subgroup containing every `k`-torus, and it is
contained in every closed subgroup containing them. It is a proof obligation for
the statements, not a hypothesis of them.

The split into infinite and finite fields follows the source's proof, which
treats the two cases by different arguments:

- for infinite `k` (printed p.478), via the variety of maximal tori `Tor_G`,
  which is unirational (SGA3 XIV Theorem 6.1), so `Tor_G(k)` is Zariski-dense;
  the universal maximal torus over `Tor_G` then maps into `G_t`;
- for finite `k` (printed pp.478–480), via Gabber's argument: the solvable case
  over perfect fields using Theorem B.4.3 and Lemma A.2.12, then the general case
  using that connected semisimple groups over finite fields have a Borel
  subgroup.

The two statements together cover every field. The hypotheses (smooth,
connected, affine over a field) are those of the source; `Infinite k` and
`Finite k` only record which case is stated.

## Use

`TorusGeneratedEnvelope.isNormal_of_algebraicClosure_envelope_eq` (Worker 1436's
reduction module `Rational.TorusGeneratedEnvelopeNormalityReduction`) takes
exactly this equality for `toAlgebraicClosure k` as an explicit hypothesis and
concludes normality of any actual representative of the envelope. With both
statements, a case split on `Finite k` proves
`TorusGeneratedEnvelopeNormalityStatement`, which is the source's deduction
of normality from compatibility with field extension.
