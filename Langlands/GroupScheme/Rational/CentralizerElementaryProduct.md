# Rational points as centralizer points times the elementary subgroup

Author: Worker 1757.

Let `G` be a connected reductive group over a field `k` and `χ` a
cocharacter of `G` defined over `k`. This module proves
`G(k) = E · Z_G(χ)(k)` when the rational points of `G` are Zariski dense.
Here `E` is the subgroup of `G(k)` generated by the rational points of
the unipotent radicals of all parabolic `k`-subgroups, and `Z_G(χ)` is
the centralizer of `χ`. Both local fields and infinite fields satisfy the
density hypothesis. The proof uses the opposite open cell and density.
It does not use Bruhat decompositions or Weyl group representatives.

All rational points are the Library's `rationalPoints G`, the sections of
`G` over `Spec k`.

## Notation

- `Dynamic.parabolic χ`, `Dynamic.unipotent χ` and `Dynamic.centralizer χ`
  are the Library's dynamic subgroup functors `P_G(χ)`, `U_G(χ)` and
  `Z_G(χ)`. A point is in `P_G(χ)` when its conjugation orbit under
  `χ(t)` extends over `t = 0`. It is in `U_G(χ)` when the limit at `0` is
  the identity. It is in `Z_G(χ)` when its orbit is constant.
- For a subgroup functor `H`, `H.rationalPoints` is its subgroup of `G(k)`.
- `opp χ` is a local notation for the cocharacter `t ↦ χ(t⁻¹)`, written as
  the composite with the power map `t ↦ t⁻¹` of `𝔾_m`. This is the form
  used in the Library's `OppositeCellStatement`.
- `parabolicElementarySubgroup G` is the subgroup `E` of `G(k)` described
  above. Its generators are the rational points in the images of the
  geometric unipotent radicals of all actual parabolic `k`-subgroups.
- `HasDenseRationalPoints k G.X` says that the images of the rational
  points are dense in the underlying space of `G`.

## Declarations

In namespace `Langlands.GroupScheme.Dynamic`, for a field `k` and a group
scheme `G` over `Spec k`:

- `oppositeCellMul χ` (an `abbrev`, for `G` with affine underlying
  scheme). This is the multiplication morphism
  `U_G(opp χ) × P_G(χ) → G`, `(u, p) ↦ u * p`. It is defined on the
  Library's closed affine representing schemes
  `Affine.unipotentScheme (opp χ)` and `Affine.parabolicScheme χ`, through
  their inclusions `Affine.unipotentHom (opp χ)` and
  `Affine.parabolicHom χ`. The product is taken in the group structure of
  morphisms into `G`. Its image is the opposite open cell.
- `isOpenImmersion_oppositeCellMul`. If `G` is smooth and affine, then
  `oppositeCellMul χ` is an open immersion.
- `exists_centralizer_of_mem_cell`. Assume `G` is smooth and affine.
  Let `N` be any subgroup of `G(k)` that contains the rational points of
  `U_G(χ)` and of `U_G(opp χ)`. Let `c` be a rational point whose image
  lies in the opposite open cell, the image of `oppositeCellMul χ`. Then
  `c * z⁻¹ ∈ N` for some rational point `z` of `Z_G(χ)`. `N` need not be
  normal here.
- `exists_mem_open_inv_mul_mem_open`. Assume `G` is smooth with connected
  underlying space and dense rational points. Let `O` be a nonempty open
  subset of the underlying space of `G`. Then every rational point `g`
  can be written `g = c * (c⁻¹ * g)` with the images of the rational
  points `c` and `c⁻¹ * g` both in `O`.
- `exists_centralizer_mul_inv_mem`. Assume `G` is smooth, affine and
  connected with dense rational points. Let `N` be a normal subgroup of
  `G(k)` that contains the rational points of `U_G(χ)` and `U_G(opp χ)`.
  Then for every rational point `g` there is a rational point `z` of
  `Z_G(χ)` with `g * z⁻¹ ∈ N`.

In namespace `Langlands.GroupScheme`:

- `exists_parabolicElementarySubgroup_mul_centralizer`. Let `G` be
  connected reductive (`IsConnectedReductive G`) with dense rational
  points. Then every rational point is `e * z` with
  `e ∈ parabolicElementarySubgroup G` and `z` a rational point of
  `Z_G(χ)`.
- `centralizer_rationalPoints_sup_parabolicElementarySubgroup`. Under the

  parabolicElementarySubgroup G = ⊤`.
- `centralizer_rationalPoints_sup_parabolicElementarySubgroup_of_infinite`.
  The same equality for every connected reductive group over an infinite
  field.
- `centralizer_rationalPoints_sup_parabolicElementarySubgroup_of_localField`.
  The same equality for every connected reductive group over a
  nonarchimedean local field `F`.

## Proofs

**The open immersion.** `isOpenImmersion_oppositeCellMul` is the
Library's `oppositeCellStatement`. It is applied to the representing
inclusions of `U_G(opp χ)` and `P_G(χ)` and their representability
theorems `Affine.isRepresentedBy_unipotentHom` and
`Affine.isRepresentedBy_parabolicHom`.

**The cell lemma.** By `isOpenImmersion_oppositeCellMul` the map
`m = oppositeCellMul χ` is an open immersion. A
rational point `c` whose image lies in the image of `m` therefore lifts
through `m` (`FiniteEtaleImage.liftOver`). So `c = u * p` with `u` a
rational point of `U_G(opp χ)` and `p` a rational point of `P_G(χ)`. By
the Library's `Dynamic.exists_hasLimit`, the orbit of `p` has a limit `z`
at `0`. This `z` lies in `Z_G(χ)`, and `p * z⁻¹` has limit `1`, so it lies
in `U_G(χ)`. Hence `c * z⁻¹ = u * (p * z⁻¹)` lies in `N`.

**The density lemma.** A smooth connected group over a field is
irreducible (`DynamicConnected.irreducibleSpace_of_smooth`). The map
`σ : x ↦ x⁻¹ * g` is an endomorphism of the scheme `G`. It has a right
inverse `x ↦ g * x⁻¹`, so it is surjective on points. Hence `σ⁻¹(O)` is a
nonempty open set. In an irreducible space two nonempty open sets meet,
so `O ∩ σ⁻¹(O)` is a nonempty open set. By density it contains the image
of a rational point `c`. The point `c ≫ σ` is `c⁻¹ * g`, so its image lies
in `O`. The base `Spec k` has a single point, so the image of each
rational point is a single point.

**The main theorem.** The opposite open cell is a nonempty open set,
since it contains the image of the identity. Choose `c` by the density
lemma, with `c` and `c⁻¹ * g` both in the cell. The cell lemma gives
rational points `z₁` and `z₂` of `Z_G(χ)` with `c * z₁⁻¹ ∈ N` and
`c⁻¹ * g * z₂⁻¹ ∈ N`. Then
`g * (z₁ * z₂)⁻¹ = (c * z₁⁻¹) * (z₁ * (c⁻¹ * g * z₂⁻¹) * z₁⁻¹)`, which lies
in `N` because `N` is normal. Take `z = z₁ * z₂`.

**The corollaries.** A connected reductive group is smooth, affine and
connected. The subgroup `parabolicElementarySubgroup G` is normal. By the
Library's `dynamicUnipotent_le_parabolicElementarySubgroup`, applied to
`χ` and to `opp χ`, it contains the rational points of `U_G(χ)` and of
`U_G(opp χ)`. The main theorem then gives `g * z⁻¹ ∈ E`, so
`g = (g * z⁻¹) * z`. Density holds over an infinite field by the
Library's `reductiveRationalPointDensity`. It holds over a nonarchimedean
local field by `hasDenseRationalPoints_of_localField`.

## Relation to the literature

Borel–Tits (1965), Proposition 6.25, printed p. 114 / PDF p. 61, states
the following. Let `P` and `P⁻` be opposite minimal parabolic
`k`-subgroups with unipotent radicals `U` and `U⁻`. Then
`G_K = U_K · U⁻_K · P_K` for every extension `K` of `k`, and in particular
`G(k) = U(k) · U⁻(k) · P(k)`. Since `P` is the semidirect product of its
unipotent radical with the centralizer `Z(S)` of a maximal split torus
`S`, this gives `G(k) = E · Z(S)(k)`. Borel–Tits prove 6.25 over every
field through Lemma 6.28, using parabolic subgroups opposite to two given
ones.

The present results are derived variants with an internal proof.

- The cocharacter `χ` is arbitrary, so `P_G(χ)` need not be minimal.
- The conclusion is only modulo the normal subgroup `E` (or any normal
  `N` with the two contraction hypotheses), not the triple product.
- Zariski density of rational points is assumed. This holds over local
  and infinite fields. Proposition 6.25 itself also covers finite fields.
- The proof uses the opposite open cell and density rather than Lemma 6.28.

Choosing `χ` with `Z_G(χ) = Z(S)` for a maximal split torus `S` would give
the classical `G(k) = Z(S)(k) · E`. That choice of cocharacter is not made
or formalized here.
