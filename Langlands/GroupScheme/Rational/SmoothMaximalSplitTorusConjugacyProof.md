# Borel–Tits conjugacy of maximal split tori in smooth connected affine groups

Authors: Worker 45 and Worker 15. Worker 15 designed and compile-checked this
composition of published results. It is recorded in their note
`notes/group-schemes/smooth-split-torus-conjugacy-route.md` (Task 3483, wiki
candidate `3510ba2d-f0ba-46e6-801a-761065e103e6`, under review when this
module was written). Worker 45 put it into this module, checked it again and
wrote this description.

`Langlands.GroupScheme.smoothMaximalSplitTorusConjugacy` proves the exact
published proposition
`Langlands.GroupScheme.SmoothMaximalSplitTorusConjugacyStatement.{u}`
(module `Langlands.GroupScheme.Rational.MaximalSplitTorusStatement`) at an
arbitrary universe `u`. Let `k : Type u` be any field and `G` a group scheme
over `Spec k` with `Smooth G.X.hom`, `IsAffineHom G.X.hom` and
`ConnectedSpace G.X.left`. Let `i : T.toGrp ⟶ G` and `i' : T'.toGrp ⟶ G` be
maximal split tori. Then there are a rational point `g` of `G` and an
isomorphism `e : T.toGrp ≅ T'.toGrp` with
`i ≫ (G.innerIso g).hom = e.hom ≫ i'`.

The field may be finite or infinite, perfect or imperfect, of any
characteristic. No reductivity, solvability or other condition is placed on
`G`. The theorem has no hypotheses beyond those of the statement.

## Source

This is Theorem C.2.3 (Borel–Tits) of Conrad–Gabber–Prasad, *Pseudo-reductive
groups*, second edition (2015), printed p.593 (registered source
`conrad-gabber-prasad-2015-pseudo-reductive`, SHA-256
`1b77af44c90211db9a3efa50896a3722f4fd2df332b7ce2ec52202eb243e7f54`). Lemma
C.2.1 (printed pp.592–593) and Lemma C.2.2 (printed p.593) are the rational
lifting and conjugation steps of its proof. The top-level structure of the
Lean proof follows the printed proof of Theorem C.2.3. Every step is a
published Library theorem, and this module only puts them together. Those
theorems' own modules describe their proofs, which may differ from the
printed ones.

## Declarations in this module

The module defines four theorems and imports the canonical rational-lifting
proof. The first two declarations are internal helpers in the
namespace `Langlands.GroupScheme.SmoothMaximalSplitTorusConjugacyProof`.

1. `torusGeneratedEnvelope_isNormal_of_infinite`. Let `k` be infinite, `G`
   smooth, connected and affine, and `D` a representative of the closed
   subgroup generated by all `k`-tori of `G` (a `TorusGeneratedEnvelope G`).
   Then the image of `D` is normal in `G` on every test scheme. The proof
   applies the published
   `TorusGeneratedEnvelope.isNormal_of_algebraicClosure_envelope_eq`. That
   theorem needs the envelope to commute with base change to the algebraic
   closure. Over an infinite field this is
   `torusGeneratedEnvelopeAlgebraicClosureInfinite_of`, applied to three
   published proofs:
   - `maximalTorusBaseChange` (a closed torus is maximal exactly when its
     base change to an extension field is maximal);
   - `maximalTorusConjugacy` (maximal tori are conjugate over an algebraically
     closed field);
   - `rationalMaximalTorusConjugateDensity`. Let `k` be infinite, `K` its
     algebraic closure and `T₀` a maximal `k`-torus. The `K`-points of `G_K`
     that conjugate `(T₀)_K` onto the base change of some closed `k`-torus
     of `G` have Zariski-dense union of images.
2. `rationalLiftingProperty_of_infinite_of_trivial_splitRadical`. Let `k` be
   infinite, `G` smooth, connected and affine, and `n : N ⟶ G` a split
   unipotent radical (`IsSplitUnipotentRadical n`) with trivial image. Then
   `PseudoParabolicRationalLiftingProperty G` holds. The body repeats that of
   the published
   `PseudoParabolicRationalLiftingAssembly.property_of_trivial_split_radical`,
   but takes normality of the envelope from item 1. The published version takes
   it from the open `TorusGeneratedEnvelopeNormalityStatement`. In detail:
   choose a smooth envelope `D` with `exists_pointed_torusDominated_envelope`.
   For a pseudo-parabolic `i : P ⟶ G`, the published
   `pseudoParabolicSplitRadicalProduct` and the trivial radical give
   `DynamicParabolicPresentation.ofSplitProduct`. This identifies the image of
   `i`, on every test scheme, with the dynamic parabolic of an actual
   cocharacter `E.cocharacter`. The published `D.rational_lifts_dynamic` then
   lifts the given rational point `x` of the given quotient `Q` through the
   given `q`. It uses normality (item 1) and the published rational density
   `torusGeneratedEnvelopeRationalDensity`.
3. `pseudoParabolicQuotientRationalLifting` is imported from
   [PseudoParabolicQuotientRationalLiftingProof](../PseudoParabolicQuotientRationalLiftingProof.md).
   It proves the same `PseudoParabolicQuotientRationalLiftingStatement.{u}`
   (CGP Lemma C.2.1) by split-radical induction, the finite-field lifting
   theorem, and the opposite-envelope lifting proof for infinite fields.
   The independently accepted version of this module supplied the identical
   target through item 2 instead. Integration keeps one global declaration;
   the distinct infinite-field torus-envelope helpers above remain available.
4. `splitTorusIntoPseudoParabolic` proves the published
   `SplitTorusIntoPseudoParabolicStatement.{u}`: every split subtorus can be
   conjugated by a rational point into any given pseudo-parabolic subgroup
   (the split-torus case of CGP Lemma C.2.2). It applies the published
   reduction `splitTorusIntoPseudoParabolic_of_components` to the following
   inputs:
   - `smoothSubgroupQuotientExistence` (quotients by smooth closed subgroups);
   - `pseudoParabolicSubgroupStructure` (pseudo-parabolic subgroups are
     smooth, connected and affine);
   - `pseudoParabolicQuotientPseudoComplete` (pseudo-completeness of the
     quotient);
   - `splitSolvablePseudoCompleteFixedPoint` (CGP Proposition C.1.5);
   - item 3.

The main theorem `smoothMaximalSplitTorusConjugacy` applies the published
assembly
`smoothMaximalSplitTorusConjugacy_of_solvable_of_envelope_of_structure_of_containment`
(module `Rational.MaximalSplitTorusDescentReduction`) to four inputs:

- `solvableMaximalSplitTorusConjugacy` (the solvable case);
- `noProperPseudoParabolicSolvableEnvelope` (with no proper pseudo-parabolic
  subgroup, one smooth connected affine solvable closed subgroup contains every
  split subtorus);
- `pseudoParabolicSubgroupStructure`;
- item 4.

That assembly is a Noetherian induction on closed subgroups. In each smooth
connected affine group, either one solvable closed subgroup contains every
split subtorus, and the solvable case applies there. Or there is a proper
pseudo-parabolic subgroup, every maximal split torus can be conjugated into
it, and the induction hypothesis applies to that subgroup. Conjugacy in the
subgroup then gives conjugacy in `G`.

## Relation to open statements

The published `TorusGeneratedEnvelopeNormalityStatement` (normality of the
torus envelope over every field) has no proof yet. This module does not use it.
In the published rational-lifting assembly it is needed only for infinite
fields, and item 1 proves it there. The finite-field case does not use
envelope normality. No unproved published statement is used as a hypothesis
anywhere in this module.

A compile check of the main theorem reported only the axioms `propext`,
`Classical.choice` and `Quot.sound`.
