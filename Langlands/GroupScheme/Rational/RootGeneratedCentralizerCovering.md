# Rational points as root-generated points times centralizer points

Author: Worker 4348.

Module `Langlands.GroupScheme.Rational.RootGeneratedCentralizerCovering`.

## Statement

Let `k : Type u` be any field, with no condition on characteristic, cardinality or
perfectness. Let `G` be a connected reductive group over `k`
(`IsConnectedReductive G`, with an affineness instance `[IsAffineHom G.X.hom]`), and let
`i : S ⟶ G` be a maximal split torus (`IsMaximalSplitTorus i`). Write

- `G(k)^+ = relativeRootGeneratedSubgroup i`. This is the abstract subgroup of
  `rationalPoints G` generated by the rational points
  `(RelativeRoot.subgroup i a).carrier (Over.mk (𝟙 _))` of the Library root groups of all
  relative roots `a ∈ relativeRoots i` (module `Rational.RootGeneratedSubgroup`);
- `Z(k) = centralizerPoints i`, the rational points of the centralizer of the torus.

The module proves `G(k) = G(k)^+ · Z(k)`, in three forms.

- `exists_relativeRootGenerated_mul_centralizer hG hi g`: every `g : rationalPoints G` is
  `h * z` with `h ∈ G(k)^+` and `z ∈ Z(k)`.
- `relativeRootGeneratedSubgroup_sup_centralizerPoints hG hi`:
  `relativeRootGeneratedSubgroup i ⊔ centralizerPoints i = ⊤`.
- `relativeRootGeneratedSubgroup_eq_top_of_centralizerPoints_le hG hi hZ`: if
  `centralizerPoints i ≤ relativeRootGeneratedSubgroup i`, then
  `relativeRootGeneratedSubgroup i = ⊤`.

The proposition `RootGeneratedCentralizerCoveringStatement.{u}` packages the second form
for all fields, groups and maximal split tori in universe `u`. The theorem
`rootGeneratedCentralizerCovering` proves it. All results use only the axioms `propext`,
`Classical.choice` and `Quot.sound`.

The third form reduces the generation equality `G(k)^+ = G(k)` (for example the
quasi-split simply connected case) to the inclusion `Z(k) ⊆ G(k)^+`.

## Proof

Fix `G`, `i`, and `g ∈ G(k)`. Smoothness of `G` comes from `hG.smooth`. The monomorphism
instance for `i` and splitness of `S` come from `hi.isSplitSubtorus`.

1. `IsSplitTorus.exists_genericCocharacter` gives a generic cocharacter `ν` of `S`. The
   published `genericCentralizer` identifies the dynamic centralizer of `ν` with the
   torus centralizer. The published `rationalBruhat` gives the rational Bruhat
   decomposition for `(i, ν)`.
2. `exists_unipotent_normalizer_unipotent_centralizer_mul` (module
   `Rational.SimplyConnectedCentralizerProductReduction`) writes `g = a * n * b * z`. Here
   `a, b` are rational points of the dynamic unipotent radical `U(ν)`, `n` is a rational
   normalizer point and `z ∈ Z(k)`.
3. The published `rootRankOneFactorization` gives rank-one factorization of every
   nontrivial root-group point, and hence opposite factor pairs
   (`HasOppositeFactorPairs i`), as in `IntegerOmegaApartmentUniqueness`. These pairs give
   disjoint positive root rays (`HasOppositeFactorPairs.disjoint_cyclicTail_indivisibleRoots`).
   `dynamicUnipotent_le_relativeRootGeneratedSubgroup` then puts `U(ν)(k)` inside `G(k)^+`.
4. The published `relativeRootSystem` gives `HasRelativeRootSystem i`.
   `RelativeRoot.subgroupRelativeWeylMap_surjective_of_oppositeFactorPairs`, applied to
   `G(k)^+`, shows that every relative Weyl class has a representative in `G(k)^+`.
   `subgroupRelativeWeylMap_surjective_iff` turns this into a factorization
   `n = h * z'` with `h ∈ G(k)^+` and `z' ∈ Z(k)`.
5. Then `g = (a * h * (z' * b * z'⁻¹)) * (z' * z)`, which the `group` tactic checks. The
   first factor lies in `G(k)^+`: `z'` normalizes `U(ν)(k)`
   (`centralizerPoints_conj_mem_dynamicUnipotent`), so `z' * b * z'⁻¹ ∈ U(ν)(k)`. The second
   factor lies in `Z(k)`.

The other two forms follow: `Subgroup.mul_mem_sup` gives the join equality. If
`Z(k) ≤ G(k)^+`, the join equals `G(k)^+`.

## Provenance

This is an internal assembly of published Library theorems. It introduces no new
mathematical assertion. Mathematically, it is the standard consequence of the rational
Bruhat decomposition (Borel–Tits, *Groupes réductifs*, 1965, Théorème 5.15, registered as
`borel-tits-1965-groupes-reductifs`): `G(k) = U(k) N(k) U(k) Z(k)`, and every relative
Weyl class has a representative in the subgroup generated by the root groups. The Lean
proof uses the Library forms of these facts listed above.
