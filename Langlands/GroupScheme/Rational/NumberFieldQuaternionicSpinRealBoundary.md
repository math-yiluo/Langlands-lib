# The real boundary of the quaternionic spin cover

Module `Langlands.GroupScheme.Rational.NumberFieldQuaternionicSpinRealBoundary`. Its main theorem,
`Langlands.GroupScheme.numberFieldQuaternionicSpinRealBoundary`, proves
`Langlands.GroupScheme.NumberFieldQuaternionicSpinRealBoundaryStatement` in every universe. The
statement is in `Rational.NumberFieldQuaternionicSpinKernelReduction`.

Let `F` be a number field and `D` a central quaternion division algebra with an orthogonal
involution `σ`. Let `q` be a nondegenerate `σ`-quadratic form on `D^n` with `n ≥ 3`, and
`p : G → H = σ.normOneIsometryGroup q` a central isogeny from a simply connected group whose kernel
is identified with `μ₂`. Let `v` be a real place, with completion `K = F_v`, such that
`DivisionOrAnisotropicAt q K` holds. The statement: if the image in `G` of the Kummer cocycle of
`a ∈ Kˣ` is a coboundary over a finite Galois extension `M/K`, then `a` is a square in `K`.

## Auxiliary results

* `isSquare_iff_of_ringEquivReal`, `isSquare_or_isSquare_neg_of_ringEquivReal`,
  `isSquare_add_mul_self_of_ringEquivReal`, `not_isSquare_neg_one_of_ringEquivReal`: for a field
  `K` with a ring isomorphism `ψ : K ≃+* ℝ`, `t` is a square iff `0 ≤ ψ t`. So every element or its
  negative is a square, sums of two squares are squares, and `-1` is not a square.
* `isUnit_of_ne_zero_of_isDomain`: in a finite-dimensional algebra over a field without zero
  divisors, nonzero elements are units (left multiplication is injective, hence surjective).
* `matrixSesqBilin_gramOver_conjugationSkewGram`: over any commutative coefficient algebra `K`,
  the self-value of `gramOver n (conjugationSkewGram q) K` for quaternion conjugation is the
  polar `σ`-self-value times the inverse of the extended alternating unit on the right
  (`quaternionConjugation_baseChange`). This is the base-changed self-value case of
  `sesqForm_conjugationSkewGram`.

## Proof of the main theorem

`K ≅ ℝ` by `InfinitePlace.Completion.ringEquivRealOfIsReal`. `G` is affine since it is semisimple.
`H` is affine since it is a closed subgroup of the unit group of `M_n(D)`.

1. The points `H(K)` correspond bijectively and multiplicatively to `suGroup τ Hs K`, with `τ` the
   quaternion conjugation and `Hs = conjugationSkewGram q` (`normOnePointMatrix`,
   `normOnePointMatrix_mem`, `exists_normOnePointMatrix_eq`).
2. `suGroup τ Hs K` is generated by squares:
   * if `K ⊗ D` is a division algebra, by `mem_closure_squares_of_division`, with
     `N = reducedNorm` (`quaternionConjugation_norm_baseChange`);
   * if the polar form is anisotropic over `K` and `K ⊗ D` has no zero divisors, `K ⊗ D` is again a
     division algebra;
   * if the polar form is anisotropic and `K ⊗ D ≅ M₂(K)` (`quaternionCoefficientDichotomy`), by
     `mem_closure_squares_of_split`. The canonical-conjugation form is anisotropic because it is
     the polar form times a unit.
3. Hence `H(K)` is generated by squares. Squares lift to `G(K)`
   (`IsCentralIsogeny.exists_target_eq_mul_self`), so `G(K) → H(K)` is surjective.
4. `IsCentralIsogeny.isSquare_of_kummerImageTrivial` concludes that `a` is a square.

## Provenance (derived)

Mathematically, the real points `H(K)` are `SO*(2n)` when `D` ramifies at `v`. They are a compact
special orthogonal group when `D` splits at `v` and the form is anisotropic. Both are connected,
so the spinor norm on them is trivial. The Library's statements on connectedness of real points are
stated for `ℝ` in the lowest universe. This proof is universe-polymorphic and replaces
connectedness by generation by squares, which is proved algebraically in
`Quaternionic.SkewUnitaryDivisionSquares` and `Quaternionic.SkewUnitarySplitSquares`.
