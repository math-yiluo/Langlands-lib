# The Weyl character formula from Freudenthal's multiplicity formula

Module `Langlands.GroupScheme.Rational.WeylCharacterFormula`, namespace
`Langlands.GroupScheme`.

This module joins the representation theory of a split reductive group to the
combinatorial result of `Langlands.LinearAlgebra.FreudenthalWeylCharacter`.
Its main theorem, `weylCharacterFormulaStatement_of_freudenthal`, proves
`FreudenthalMultiplicityStatement.{u} → WeylCharacterFormulaStatement.{u}`.
Freudenthal's multiplicity formula, in the form of the proposition
`Langlands.GroupScheme.FreudenthalMultiplicityStatement`, implies the Weyl
character formula `Langlands.GroupScheme.WeylCharacterFormulaStatement`
(Milne, *Algebraic Groups* (2017), Theorem 22.52, in characteristic zero).

## Weight multiplicities

Here `p : SplitPinning G` is a split pinning of an affine group scheme over a
field `k`, and `X = characterLattice p.torus`.

- `SplitPinning.positiveRootMonoid_eq_positiveCone`: `p.positiveRootMonoid`
  is, by definition, the Library's
  `LinearAlgebra.DominantTriangular.positiveCone p.rootDatum p.base`. Both are
  the additive submonoid generated by the simple roots. The proof is `rfl`.
- `SplitPinning.finite_support_finrank_representationWeightSpace` (`k` of
  characteristic zero): for `r : MatrixRepresentation G (Fin n)`, the function
  `μ ↦ dim r_μ` (as an integer) has finite support. The Library's formal
  character `p.formalCharacter r : X →₀ k` has value `(dim r_μ : k)` at `μ`
  (`SplitPinning.formalCharacter_apply`). In characteristic zero this value is
  nonzero exactly when `dim r_μ ≠ 0`.
- `SplitPinning.weightMultiplicity p r : X →₀ ℤ` is the finitely supported
  function `μ ↦ dim r_μ`, and `SplitPinning.weightMultiplicity_apply` gives its
  values.

## The main theorem

`weylCharacterFormulaStatement_of_freudenthal` takes `k`, `G`, `p`, `rho` with
`2 • rho` equal to the sum of the positive roots, a representation `r` and
`lam` with `p.IsHighestWeight r lam`. It chooses `c = p.weightMultiplicity r`,
so the first conjunct of the target holds by `weightMultiplicity_apply`. The
local `J` of the target statement is, by definition, the alternant
`LinearAlgebra.WeylCharacter.alternant p.rootDatum`. The identity
`J(rho) * ofCoeff c = J(lam + rho)` is then
`LinearAlgebra.WeylCharacter.alternant_mul_ofCoeff_eq` applied with the base
`p.base`. Its hypotheses are checked as follows.

- The form `F` and its symmetry, reflection invariance and positive
  definiteness come from `FreudenthalMultiplicityStatement` applied to `r` and
  `lam`. The recursion `hfreud` is the last conjunct of that statement, after
  rewriting `(c μ : ℚ)` as `(dim r_μ : ℚ)`.
- `p.rootIndex` is finite (`SplitPinning.finiteRootIndex`), so it gets a
  `Fintype` structure through `Fintype.ofFinite`. The root datum is reduced
  (`p.isReduced`). The character lattice of the split torus is free of finite
  rank over `ℤ`, by the Library's
  `TorusSurjectionIsogenySection.characterLattice_free_finite`.
- Reflection invariance of `c` is the Library's
  `SplitPinning.finrank_representationWeightSpace_root_reflection`.
- `c(lam) = 1` is the third conjunct of `IsHighestWeight`
  (`dim r_lam = 1`).
- The support of `c` lies below `lam`: if `c(μ) ≠ 0`, the weight space `r_μ`
  is nonzero, and the fourth conjunct of `IsHighestWeight` gives
  `lam - μ ∈ p.positiveRootMonoid`.
- The hypothesis on `rho` in the target statement is literally the
  hypothesis `hρ` of `alternant_mul_ofCoeff_eq`, by
  `positiveRootMonoid_eq_positiveCone`.
