# Pointed domination of the torus envelope by a torus

This internal construction strengthens smooth connected representability of the
fixed envelope of all ground-field tori. For every smooth affine group over an
arbitrary field, it supplies an actual dominant morphism from a ground-field
torus to the envelope, taking the identity to the identity. The morphism need
not be a group homomorphism. No solvability, ambient connectedness, infinite
field, perfection, characteristic, or splitting assumption is imposed.

The proof minimizes coordinate-kernel height among smooth connected closed
subgroups inside the envelope that already admit such a parametrization.
A closed trivial torus supplies the initial member. To adjoin another closed
torus, it applies the published pointed-image generation theorem to the product
of the parametrizing torus with that torus. Its positive dominant ordered power
has the underlying scheme of a finite product of tori as a source. The published
comparison from that group product to the scheme finite power has a section,
so the resulting morphism remains dominant. The identity maps to the identity
by evaluating each factor in the ordered product.

The original subgroup lies in the generated subgroup because its parametrizing
morphism is schematically dominant: the target is reduced, so ordinary
dominance suffices. Factorization through the closed generated inclusion
descends along that parametrization. The other torus lies there by the identity
slice. Every closed subgroup containing all ground-field tori contains the
product morphism and its generated subgroup, keeping the new subgroup inside
the fixed envelope. Minimal height forces the actual inclusion between the
subgroups to be an isomorphism. Thus the minimum contains every ground-field
torus, and the fixed envelope's minimality gives equality of their all-test
ranges.

The resulting domination is transported through the canonical isomorphism
between any two representatives of the fixed envelope. Applying this to the
identity representative shows that any smooth affine torus-generated group
admits pointed torus domination. This is an internal
derivation from published product, pointed-image, coordinate-height, and
closed-immersion factorization results. It proves no radical splitting or
rational conjugacy theorem.
