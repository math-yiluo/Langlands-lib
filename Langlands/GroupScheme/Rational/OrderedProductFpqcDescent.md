# Descent of unique ordered subgroup factors

This internal construction concerns finitely many actual subgroup functors of
a group scheme over any base scheme in universe u. Each functor has a supplied
representing monomorphism. Assume that their original ordered multiplication
is injective on every test scheme, in the supplied order.

`orderedSubgroupProduct F U` is the set of actual points of G over the test U
that are ordered products of one point in each supplied subgroup F. It is a
set, with no assertion of closure under multiplication. This is the explicit
ordered-product interface for subsequent collection arguments.

The first theorem proves that the actual ordered multiplication morphism of
the representing schemes is a monomorphism. It compares points through the
published finite-product coordinates and represented-subgroup equivalences.

`orderedSubgroupFactors_descend` descends any supplied ordered factorization
after an effective epimorphism of test schemes. It returns factors in the
original subgroup functors, their original ordered product, and equality of
each extended original factor with the supplied factor. It requires no
surjectivity of ordered multiplication and no separately supplied descent
datum on the factors. In particular, the theorem applies to an fpqc cover.

The proof reassembles the factors into one point of the product scheme.
Monicity of its multiplication makes this point compatible over every pair
of test morphisms with equal composites to the original test. Effective
epimorphism descent gives an original product-scheme point. The original
structure map also descends, by epimorphism cancellation. Finite-product
coordinates give its factors, and all-test injectivity identifies their
extensions with the supplied factors. The argument includes an empty family.

`mem_orderedSubgroupProduct_comp_iff` expresses preservation and reflection of
membership in this set after an effective epimorphism. Reflection uses the
factor-descent theorem. Preservation pulls back each actual subgroup point;
pullback respects the ordered product.

The provenance is internal composition of the published all-test ordered
product coordinates and the effective-epimorphism construction already used
for represented subgroup membership. No new assertion from original
literature is used. This result descends one ordered factorization; it does
not assert that a subgroup generated by filtered factors has such an ordered
factorization.
