# Ternary SL at an A₂ pair of simple roots: statement

Worker 195, for Task 6836 (8c04a3ed-3fa6-4680-a59f-856738cb578b).

Module `Langlands.GroupScheme.Rational.AdjacentSimpleRootSpecialLinearStatement`.
`Langlands.GroupScheme.AdjacentSimpleRootSpecialLinearStatement.{u}` is a
proposition definition. This module does not prove it.

## The statement

Let `k : Type u` be a field and `G` a group scheme over `Spec k` with affine
structure map. Let `p : SplitPinning G` be a split pinning; by the Library's
definition (module `GroupScheme.Reductive.Pinning`) it contains a proof that `G`
is connected reductive, a split maximal torus, its reduced root datum, a base and
Lie vectors at the simple roots. Let `x : p.NormalizedSimpleRootParametrizations`
be a choice, for every simple root, of an additive parametrization of the whole
root subgroup, equivariant for the torus and with derivative the pinned Lie
vector. Let `a, b` be simple roots with

`p.rootDatum.pairing a b = -1` and `p.rootDatum.pairing b a = -1`,

that is, `⟨a, b^∨⟩ = ⟨b, a^∨⟩ = -1`: `a` and `b` span a root subsystem of type
`A₂`.

The proposition asserts that there is a group homomorphism

`φ : SL₃(k) →* rationalPoints G`

with

- `φ.comp SpecialLinear.TernaryPresentation.left = SplitPinning.rootSL2 x a`, and
- `φ.comp SpecialLinear.TernaryPresentation.right = SplitPinning.rootSL2 x b`.

Here:

- `SpecialLinear.TernaryPresentation.left` and `right` (module
  `GroupScheme.SpecialLinearTernaryPresentation`, submitted with this statement)
  are the block embeddings of `SL₂(k)` into `SL₃(k)` at the indices `0, 1` and
  `1, 2`.
- `SplitPinning.rootSL2 x a : SL₂(k) →* G(k)` (module
  `GroupScheme.Rational.OrthogonalOrbitSpecialLinear`) is the Library's
  rank-one homomorphism of the simple root `a`. It sends the upper unipotent
  `upper t` to the point `x_a(t)` of the given parametrization, the lower
  unipotent `lower t` to the point of the negative partner
  `(x a).linked.negative` of `U_{-a}`, and `diag(u, u⁻¹)` to `a^∨(u)`
  (`rootSL2_upper`, `rootSL2_lower`, `rootSL2_diag`).

So `φ` sends the elementary matrices `1 + t E₀₁`, `1 + t E₁₀`, `1 + t E₁₂`,
`1 + t E₂₁` to `x_a(t)`, `x_{-a}(t)`, `x_b(t)`, `x_{-b}(t)`. By `hom_ext` in
the presentation module, such a `φ` is unique, because `SL₃(k)` is generated by
the two block copies of `SL₂(k)`. No condition is imposed on the
characteristic or the size of `k`.

## Intended use

Task 6836 builds the quasi-split `SU₃` rank-one coordinates of a relative root
whose Galois orbit of simple roots contains an `A₂` pair. The plan (agreed with
Worker 163) is to apply this statement over a Galois splitting field to each
`A₂` pair of the orbit, and to descend the resulting `SL₃` homomorphism, twisted
by the outer involution `SpecialLinear.TernaryPresentation.outer`, to the
Library's `SU3Root.specialUnitarySubgroup`. The presentation module provides the
comparison of `outer` with the Library's `SU3Root.unitaryTwist`.

## Provenance

Internal. This is not quoted from a source. It is the `A₂` case of a classical
fact about split reductive groups: for simple roots `a, b` spanning an `A₂`
subsystem, the derived group `D` of the Levi subgroup generated by the torus and
the root groups `U_{±a}, U_{±b}` is split semisimple of type `A₂`, hence a
central quotient of `SL₃` (`SL₃` or `PGL₃`). By the isomorphism and isogeny
theorems for pinned split reductive groups there is a central isogeny
`SL₃ → D` carrying the diagonal torus to the torus of `D`, the roots
`e₀ − e₁`, `e₁ − e₂` and their coroots to `a, b` and `a^∨, b^∨`, and the
elementary unipotents `1 + t E₀₁`, `1 + t E₁₂` to `x_a(t)`, `x_b(t)`.
Restricted to the block copies of `SL₂`, it gives homomorphisms `SL₂ → G` with
the upper unipotents going to `x_a`, `x_b`, the diagonal tori going to the
coroots, and the lower unipotents going into `U_{-a}`, `U_{-b}`. The negative
partner in such a homomorphism is uniquely determined (on rational points this
is the Library's `NormalizedRootParametrization.eq_negPoint_of_isOppositeFactorPair`
at each unit `c`, since every nonzero argument has the form `-c⁻¹`), so the
restrictions equal
`rootSL2 x a` and `rootSL2 x b`. Taking rational points gives `φ`.
