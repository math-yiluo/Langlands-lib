# Restricting a normalizing matrix to a closed group scheme

Over an algebraically closed field, conjugation by a normalizing matrix preserves the rational points of a closed representation. The proof uses the general linear group's rational-point equivalence to view the matrix as a group-scheme point. The published rational-point containment theorem then extends conjugation to a factorization of the group-scheme morphism from a reduced group locally of finite type. The inverse matrix gives the opposite factorization. Cancelling the closed embedding proves that the two factors are inverses.

This is the closed-subgroup restriction used in the proof of uniqueness of compact real forms.
