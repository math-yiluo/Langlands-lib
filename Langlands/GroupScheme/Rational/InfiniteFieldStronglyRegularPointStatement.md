# Statement: rational points with torus centralizer over infinite fields

Author: Worker 1534.

This module states one proposition and proves nothing:
`Langlands.GroupScheme.InfiniteFieldStronglyRegularPointStatement.{u}`, a
`Prop`-valued `def` with one universe parameter `u`. It is a prerequisite of the
conditional infinite-field Steinberg reduction in
`Rational/QuasiSplitCocycleTorusReductionInfinite.lean`. There it provides the
strongly regular rational element `y` of the twisted group in Steinberg's
proof (1965, §10).

## Lean statement

```lean
def InfiniteFieldStronglyRegularPointStatement : Prop :=
  ∀ (K : Type u) [Field K] [Infinite K]
    (H : GroupScheme (Spec (CommRingCat.of K))),
    IsConnectedReductive H →
    ∃ (x : 𝟙_ (Over (Spec (CommRingCat.of K))) ⟶ H.X)
      (T : GroupScheme (Spec (CommRingCat.of K))) (i : T ⟶ H),
      IsTorus T ∧ IsClosedSubgroup i ∧
        ∀ {Z : Over (Spec (CommRingCat.of K))} (c : Z ⟶ H.X),
          c * (toUnit Z ≫ x) = (toUnit Z ≫ x) * c →
            ∃ t : Z ⟶ T.X, t ≫ i.hom.hom = c
```

The module imports `Langlands.GroupScheme.Torus.Lattice` (for `IsTorus`) and
`Langlands.GroupScheme.Reductive.Basic` (for `IsConnectedReductive` and
`IsClosedSubgroup`).

## Meaning

- `K` is an arbitrary infinite field, with no perfectness or characteristic
  hypothesis. `H` is a connected reductive group scheme over `Spec K`: smooth,
  affine and connected, with trivial unipotent radical over the algebraic
  closure (`IsConnectedReductive`, module `Reductive/Basic`).
- `x : 𝟙_ ⟶ H.X` is a `K`-rational point of `H`, a morphism from the terminal
  object `Spec K` of `Over (Spec K)`. On a test `Z` it is pulled back as
  `toUnit Z ≫ x`.
- `T` is a group scheme over `K` with `IsTorus T`: it becomes a split torus
  after base change to some field extension (module `Torus/Lattice`).
  `i : T ⟶ H` is a homomorphism whose underlying morphism is a closed immersion
  (`IsClosedSubgroup i`).
- The last clause quantifies over every test `Z` over `K`, including
  nonreduced ones. Every `Z`-point `c` of `H` that commutes with the pulled-back
  `x` factors through `i`. So the scheme-theoretic centralizer of `x` is
  contained in `T`. Since `T` is commutative and contains `x` (take `c` to be
  `x` itself), the centralizer is exactly `T`.

So the proposition asserts a strongly regular rational element in the sense of
Steinberg: one whose centralizer is a torus. It is stated scheme-theoretically,
over every test scheme.

## Sources and provenance

Provenance: derived.

- R. Steinberg, *Regular elements of semi-simple algebraic groups* (1965),
  source `steinberg-1965-regular-elements-semisimple-groups`, SHA-256
  `bda20c4a4b3cf4a7e9e5b17a4eacd0147d0c9370063ab3a3778114a53c8349b9`.
  - Before 2.15 (printed p.54, PDF p.7): "We will call an element of `G`
    strongly regular if its centralizer is a maximal torus."
  - 2.15 (printed p.54, PDF p.7): "The strongly regular elements form a dense
    open set in `G`." Steinberg's §2 concerns a semisimple group. The proof of
    2.15 characterizes the strongly regular elements of a maximal torus by
    `α(t) ≠ 1` for all roots `α` and `wt ≠ t` for all `w ≠ 1` in the Weyl
    group, and then applies the proof of 2.14. It uses only the root system and
    the Borel subgroups, so it applies unchanged to connected reductive
    groups.
  - §10 (printed p.78, PDF p.31): "By 2.15 and the Rosenlicht density theorem
    there exists in `x(G)` an element `y` which is strongly regular and over
    `k`." Steinberg works over a perfect field `k`.
- J. S. Milne, *Algebraic Groups* (2017), source
  `milne-2017-algebraic-groups`, SHA-256
  `899b0f44f3c3ccfbaf5ed1cf9c180be7a8016b1c0e9709ca0c206304e5bd1cd0`,
  Theorem 17.93(b) (printed p.382, PDF p.398): a connected reductive group
  variety over `k` is unirational over `k`, "and `G(k)` is dense in `G` if `k`
  is infinite". No perfectness hypothesis is needed, and this replaces the
  Rosenlicht density theorem over imperfect fields.
- B. Conrad, O. Gabber and G. Prasad, *Pseudo-reductive groups*, second
  edition (2015), source `conrad-gabber-prasad-2015-pseudo-reductive`, SHA-256
  `1b77af44c90211db9a3efa50896a3722f4fd2df332b7ce2ec52202eb243e7f54`,
  Proposition A.8.10 (printed pp.547–548, PDF pp.573–574). The fixed-point
  scheme of a group of multiplicative type acting on a smooth affine group is
  smooth. Applied to the multiplicative-type subgroup generated by a semisimple
  element, this makes its scheme-theoretic centralizer smooth.

Derived content. Steinberg's statement is about points over an algebraically
closed field, and the classical centralizer is the reduced one. The Lean
statement asks for a rational element whose centralizer functor, on all test
schemes, lies in a `K`-torus. Over an infinite field `K` the derivation is as
follows. The strongly regular locus is a nonempty open subset of `H` defined
over `K`, by Steinberg 2.15 over `K̄` and Galois-invariance. `H(K)` is dense in
`H` (Milne 17.93(b)), so it meets this locus in a rational point `x`. The
scheme-theoretic centralizer of the semisimple point `x` is smooth (CGP
A.8.10). Over `K̄` its points form a maximal torus, so the centralizer is a
`K`-torus `T`. It is a closed subgroup, being the centralizer of a rational
point. Over a small finite field a strongly regular rational element need not exist.
The statement is therefore restricted to infinite `K`, as in Steinberg §10,
which treats finite fields separately by Lang's theorem.

## What is not stated

- Density of strongly regular rational points, and maximality of the torus
  `T`, are not stated, although both hold.
- Nothing is proved here; the existence is the obligation of a separate
  exact-target proof.
