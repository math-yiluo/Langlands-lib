# Over a finite field, a group whose cocharacters are all central is solvable

Worker 1387. This module states one proposition,
`FiniteFieldCentralCocharacterSolvableStatement.{u}`. It is not proved here.

## Statement

Fix a finite field `k : Type u` and a group scheme `G` over `Spec k` that is
smooth and affine, with connected underlying space. Assume that every
cocharacter `χ : 𝔾_m ⟶ G` over `k` is central, in the form
`Dynamic.centralizer χ = ⊤`: on every test scheme, every point of `G` has
constant conjugation orbit under `χ`, that is, commutes with `χ`. The
proposition asserts that `G` is solvable, `IsSolvableGroupScheme G`: the
abstract group of `k̄`-points of the base change of `G` to the algebraic
closure `k̄` of `k` is solvable.

The cocharacters are the actual homomorphisms over `k` from the
multiplicative group `Gm (Spec k)`, not cocharacters defined only over an
extension of `k`. Split `k`-tori of `G` are generated by the images of such
cocharacters, so the hypothesis says that every split `k`-torus of `G` is
central. The hypothesis is the same as in
`FiniteFieldCentralCocharacterEnvelopeNormalityStatement`.

## Source and derivation

The source is Conrad–Gabber–Prasad, *Pseudo-reductive groups*, second edition
(2015), proof of Proposition A.2.11, finite-field case, printed p.480
(one-based PDF p.506), registered as
`conrad-gabber-prasad-2015-pseudo-reductive`. There `H` is the smooth
connected normal `k`-subgroup generated by the subgroups `H_S` built from
the split `k`-tori `S` of `G` and their contracting subgroups `U_G(±λ)`.
The source shows that `G/H` contains no nontrivial split `k`-torus, that the
same holds for the maximal semisimple quotient `(G/H)^ss`, and that
`(G/H)^ss` contains a Borel `k`-subgroup because `k` is finite. Hence
`(G/H)^ss = 1` and `G/H` is solvable.

The present proposition is this step under the extra hypothesis that every
cocharacter is central; the proposition is derived, not quoted. If `λ` is
central then `U_G(±λ)` is trivial, so each `H_S` equals `S`, and `H` is
generated by central split tori. It is therefore a central split torus. A
central torus is solvable, and `G/H` is solvable by the source argument, so
`G` is solvable.

## Intended use

Together with `FiniteFieldSolvableEnvelopeNormalityStatement` (normality of the
torus envelope of a solvable group over a finite field), this proposition
implies `FiniteFieldCentralCocharacterEnvelopeNormalityStatement`, the base
case of the finite-field reduction in
`FiniteFieldMaximalTorusTransporterReduction`.
