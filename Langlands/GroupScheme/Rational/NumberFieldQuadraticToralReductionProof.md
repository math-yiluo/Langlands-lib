# Toral reduction of quadratic cocycles over number fields

`Langlands.GroupScheme.numberFieldQuadraticToralReduction.{u}` proves `NumberFieldQuadraticToralReductionStatement.{u}`. The representative is defined on the original quadratic field cover. The real conditions refer to its actual pullbacks to the real completions.

Write the cocycle in terms of its value at the nonidentity automorphism. At each real place with a complex upper completion, the pulled-back cover is isomorphic to the cover of that upper completion. A local trivializing cochain gives a neighbourhood of cochains for which the twisted value is close to the identity. In the real Lie group of completion points, the square map is locally invertible at the identity. The branch gives a square root inverted by the coefficient automorphism, which also inverts the quadratic cocycle value.

The geometric Weil restriction provides independent coordinates for the two embeddings. The inverse image of the strongly regular locus under the twist map projects to a nonempty Zariski open on the Weil restriction. Analytic opens there are Zariski dense, so the cochain neighbourhood at each inert real place can be refined to a nonempty open of strongly regular twists. Simultaneous approximation at the complex places of the upper field uses a dominant unirational parametrization and weak approximation in its affine coordinates. If there are no inert real places, Zariski density of the rational points of the Weil restriction supplies a strongly regular twist directly.

The full centralizer of the chosen value over the quadratic field is a maximal torus. The coefficient automorphism sends the value to its inverse and preserves the centralizer, so the torus descends to the number field. The twisted cocycle factors through it on the original cover. At an inert real place, the local square root commutes with its square, so it factors through this same torus and exhibits the torus cocycle as a coboundary. At a split real place a section of the pulled-back cover gives triviality.

The result is derived from V. Platonov and A. Rapinchuk, *Algebraic Groups and Number Theory* (Academic Press, 1994), Lemma 6.28, printed pp. 369–370. The proof uses the approximation and local triviality ideas of that lemma with a torus obtained from the chosen regular value.
