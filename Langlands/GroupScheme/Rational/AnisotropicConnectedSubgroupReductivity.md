# Reductivity of connected closed subgroups of anisotropic semisimple groups

`AnisotropicConnectedSubgroupReductivityStatement` says that a connected closed subgroup of an anisotropic affine semisimple group of locally finite type over a characteristic-zero field is reductive. Smoothness of the subgroup follows from Cartier's theorem. This supplies the reductivity of the identity component of the descended intersection in the parabolic argument of Platonov and Rapinchuk, *Algebraic Groups and Number Theory*, §6.6, Theorem 6.26, pp. 354–356, and §6.8, Lemma 6.32, p. 380. [Chapter 6](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0079816908620711)

For a tangent point of the ambient group, nilpotence of the adjoint tangent matrix implies nilpotence of its adjoint Lie operator by the coordinate comparison with the Lie bracket. The Killing form is nondegenerate by characteristic-zero Lie semisimplicity. If the tangent vector is nonzero and its adjoint operator is nilpotent, the Killing form argument produces an adjoint operator with eigenvalue one on that vector. Taking the Jordan semisimple part preserves this eigenvalue, and the resulting tangent element belongs to the Lie algebra of a ground-field maximal torus. The restriction of the adjoint representation to the torus would thus have a nonzero ground-field eigenvalue on a torus tangent point, contrary to anisotropy.

For a connected unipotent closed subgroup, all tangent matrices in the restriction of the ambient adjoint representation are nilpotent. The previous argument and injectivity of the tangent map make its tangent kernel trivial, hence the subgroup is trivial on every test scheme. Applying this to the smooth connected normal unipotent subgroups of the given connected closed subgroup gives pseudo-reductivity. Over a characteristic-zero field, the perfect-field comparison gives reductivity.
