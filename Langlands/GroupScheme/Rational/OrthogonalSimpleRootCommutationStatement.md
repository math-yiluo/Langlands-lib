# Orthogonal simple-root commutation

This module defines a proposition, with no proof asserted here. It isolates the
commutation needed when Steinberg's ordered product is permuted inside a
Galois orbit of pairwise orthogonal simple roots.

`Langlands.GroupScheme.OrthogonalSimpleRootCommutationStatement.{u}` quantifies
over every field `k : Type u`, every actual affine group scheme `G` over
`Spec k`, and a supplied `p : SplitPinning G`. A split pinning includes connected
reductivity, an actual split maximal torus and inclusion, a finite reduced root
datum realizing that inclusion, a base, and simple-root Lie trivializations.
It supplies the objects relative to which this proposition is formulated.
The statement asserts no existence of a pinning.

For distinct indices `a b : p.base.support`, assume
`p.rootDatum.IsOrthogonal a b`. Mathlib defines this as the two equalities
`p.rootDatum.pairing a b = 0` and `p.rootDatum.pairing b a = 0`. In particular,
each root is fixed by reflection in the other. Both roots are simple for the
same supplied base.

Write `U(c)` for the actual `RelativeRoot.subgroup p.inclusion c`. Its
membership on a scheme test is defined by extension of the full torus orbit
through the character `c`, with value the identity at zero. This is the
published intrinsic subgroup functor, without any chosen additive
parametrization or pointwise replacement.

For every scheme test `Z` over `Spec k`, the proposition says that every
element of the subgroup

```
U(p.rootDatum.root a)(Z) ⊔ U(-p.rootDatum.root a)(Z)
```

commutes with every element of

```
U(p.rootDatum.root b)(Z) ⊔ U(-p.rootDatum.root b)(Z).
```

Here `⊔` is the ordinary subgroup supremum in the group of actual morphisms
`Z ⟶ G.X`, whose operation is induced by the group-scheme multiplication.
It is the subgroup generated by the two displayed point subgroups. The
conclusion `Commute x y` is the equality `x * y = y * x` of these morphisms.
All tests are allowed, including nonreduced tests. No algebraic closedness,
perfectness, infinitude or characteristic restriction is imposed on the field.

## Provenance and proof boundary

R. Steinberg, *Regular elements of semi-simple algebraic groups* (1965),
Theorem 9.4 and its proof, printed pp.72–73 / PDF pp.25–26, choose the
reflection representatives in the corresponding rank-one groups. In each
Galois orbit of simple roots, orthogonality then implies that the factors
commute. Section 2.1 and 2.2, printed p.52 / PDF p.5, describe the root
subgroups and positive-root multiplication. Source:
`steinberg-1965-regular-elements-semisimple-groups`, SHA-256
`bda20c4a4b3cf4a7e9e5b17a4eacd0147d0c9370063ab3a3778114a53c8349b9`.

The proposition is a derived scheme-theoretic prerequisite of that argument.
Steinberg works with algebraic groups over an algebraically closed universal
field and imposes perfectness on the ground field in Section 9. This
proposition instead concerns supplied split data over any field and commutation
on all scheme tests. The intrinsic-root comparison and the stronger all-test
conclusion are proof obligations.

A possible proof uses the published unconditional semigroup commutator bound
and the triviality theorem for semigroups having no ambient occurring weights.
For distinct simple roots, strict mixed combinations of opposite signs cannot
be roots, by the sign and linear independence properties of a base. For
orthogonal simple roots, reflection changes the sign of just one coefficient;
thus strict combinations of the same sign cannot be roots either. The four
choices of root signs then give trivial commutators. Elementary subgroup
generation extends the resulting pairwise commutation to the two suprema.
This paragraph is a proof plan, not a claim of a completed proof.

The simplicity hypothesis matters: arbitrary orthogonal roots may have a sum
which is a root, for example the two orthogonal short roots in type B2.
The generated-subgroup hypothesis also matters. Arbitrary representatives of
the two simple reflections can have torus factors acting nontrivially on the
other root groups. The statement does not claim that all reflection
representatives commute.

No root parametrizations, root-generated reflection representatives, Galois
actions or descended cross-sections are constructed here. Their existence and
compatibility remain separate obligations of the Steinberg consumer.
