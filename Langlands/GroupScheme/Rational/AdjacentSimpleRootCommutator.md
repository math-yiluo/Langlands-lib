# Commutators of adjacent simple root subgroups

Author: Worker 90.

This helper module supports the proof of the even-chain Galois data
statement (Task 5375). That target is derived from Robert Steinberg,
*Regular elements of semi-simple algebraic groups*, Publ. Math. IHÉS 25
(1965), 49–80 (source `steinberg-1965-regular-elements-semisimple-groups`).
The Markdown of the target `EvenChainGaloisDataStatement` gives the full
source account. In a component of type `A_n` with `n = 2m` even,
Steinberg merges the middle pair of simple roots `α_m`, `α_{m+1}` into the
root `a = α_m + α_{m+1}` (notation before Theorem 9.11, printed p.74). His
construction depends on the noncommutativity of `X_{α_m}` and
`X_{α_{m+1}}`: see step 6) of the proof of 9.16 and Remark 9.17 (printed
p.77). The final paragraph of p.77, which proves 9.7 e), considers the
class of `u_m u_{m+1}` in `X_{α_m} X_{α_{m+1}} X_a / X_a`. The statements
below supply the corresponding facts in this project's setting: the
commutator of two adjacent simple root subgroups lies in the root subgroup
of their sum, and root maps are additive. They are internal derived
helpers, not numbered results of that paper.

They are proved for a field `k : Type u`, an affine group scheme `G` over
`Spec k`, and a supplied split pinning `p`. Throughout, `U(c)` denotes the
intrinsic subgroup functor `RelativeRoot.subgroup p.inclusion c`.

## Statements

- `SplitPinning.simple_add_ne_zero`: for distinct simple roots `a ≠ b` and
  natural numbers `n > 0`, `m`, the character `n • a + m • b` is nonzero.
- `SplitPinning.adjacent_root_combination`: let `a ≠ b` be simple roots
  whose mutual pairings `⟨a, b^∨⟩` and `⟨b, a^∨⟩` are both `-1`. If
  `n, m ≥ 1` and `n • a + m • b` is a root of the torus inclusion, then
  `n = m = 1`.
- `SplitPinning.adjacent_commutator_mem`: for such `a`, `b` and an index
  `α` whose root equals `a + b`, on every test `Z` and for
  `x ∈ U(a)(Z)`, `y ∈ U(b)(Z)`, the commutator `x y x⁻¹ y⁻¹` lies in
  `U(α)(Z)`.
- `SplitPinning.adjacent_mul_eq_mul_mul`: in the same situation there is
  `g ∈ U(α)(Z)` with `x y = y x g`.
- `SplitPinning.RootParametrization.point_add` and `rationalPoint_add`: a
  root parametrization `x` is additive, `x(c + c') = x(c) x(c')`, on the
  points of every test and on rational points.
- `SplitPinning.RootParametrization.rationalPoint_neg`:
  `x(-c) = x(c)⁻¹` on rational points.
- `SplitPinning.RootParametrization.rationalPoint_injective`: the map
  `c ↦ x(c)` on rational points is injective.
- `SplitPinning.RootParametrization.exists_rationalPoint_eq`: every rational
  point of `U(α)` is `x(r)` for some `r : k`.
- The imported `SplitPinning.RootParametrization.rationalPoint_mem` from
  `SteinbergRootRepresentatives`: every `x(c)` lies in
  `U(α)`.
- `SplitPinning.adjacent_rationalPoint_reorder`: for adjacent simple roots
  `a`, `b` as above, an index `α` with root `a + b`, and root
  parametrizations `xa`, `xb`, `xα` of `a`, `b`, `α`, for all `s t : k`
  there is `r : k` with `xa(s) xb(t) = xb(t) xa(s) xα(r)`.

These are the rank-two commutator relation of type `A₂` in the weak form
needed later: the commutator of `U(a)` and `U(b)` lies in `U(a + b)`. The
exact commutator value (the structure constant `±1` of the Chevalley
commutator formula) is not computed.

## Implemented proof

`simple_add_ne_zero` restricts the linear independence of the base
(`p.base.linearIndepOn_root`) to the injective pair `![a, b]` and applies
`LinearIndependent.pair_iff`.

`adjacent_root_combination` uses the closure of the root range under
simple reflections. With mutual pairings `-1`, the reflection in `a`
sends `n a + m b` to `(m - n) a + m b`, and the reflection in `b` sends it
to `n a + (n - m) b`. If `n > m`, the first image is
`m b - (n - m) a`, a strict mixed combination of two distinct simple
roots, which the published lemma
`SplitPinning.strict_mixed_simple_ne_zero_not_root` excludes from the
roots; symmetrically `m > n` is excluded. Hence `n = m`, and the given
root equals `m` times the root `a + b`, which is the root of
`reflectionPerm a b`. The two roots are linearly dependent, so
reducedness of the supplied root datum (`p.isReduced.eq_or_eq_neg`) makes
them equal or opposite. Comparing their pairings with the coroot of
`a + b` (`2m` versus `±2`) gives `m = 1`.

`adjacent_commutator_mem` follows the method of the published module
`OrthogonalSimpleRootCommutation`. The published equality
`RelativeRoot.weightFiltration_one_eq_subgroup` identifies `U(a)`, `U(b)`
and `U(α)` with the subgroups generated by the first cyclic tails of their
roots under conjugation. The theorem `RelativeRoot.semigroupCommutator`
places the commutator in the subgroup generated by the semigroup sum of
the tails of `a` and `b`, whose elements are the characters
`n a + m b` with `n, m ≥ 1`. By `RelativeRoot.semigroupSubgroup_mono_on_weightSupport`
it suffices that every such character occurring as a conjugation weight
lies in the tail of `α`. Such a character is nonzero
(`simple_add_ne_zero`), so by
`RelativeRoot.mem_actionWeightSupport_conjugation_iff` it is a root, and
`adjacent_root_combination` shows it equals `a + b = 1 • α`. Smoothness
comes from `p.isConnectedReductive.smooth`.

`adjacent_mul_eq_mul_mul` applies the commutator statement to `x⁻¹` and
`y⁻¹`, taking `g = x⁻¹ y⁻¹ x y`, and closes the identity with `group`.

`point_add` compares the scalar points of `c + c'` and of `c`, `c'` in the
vector group coordinatewise and uses that the root map is a group-object
morphism (`MonObj.mul_comp`). `rationalPoint_add` is its specialization to
the identity test, `rationalPoint_neg` follows from additivity and the
value at zero, `rationalPoint_injective` follows from additivity, `rationalPoint_neg` and
`rationalPoint_ne_one` applied to `x(c - c')`, `exists_rationalPoint_eq` transports the global section
from `RootParametrization.mem_rootSubgroup_iff` through
`Scheme.ΓSpecIso`, and the imported `rationalPoint_mem` is the converse direction of
`mem_rootSubgroup_iff`. Finally `adjacent_rationalPoint_reorder` combines
`adjacent_mul_eq_mul_mul` with `exists_rationalPoint_eq`.
