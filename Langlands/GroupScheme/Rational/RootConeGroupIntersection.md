# The identity component of a cone-root-group intersection

Let a maximal split torus lie in a connected reductive group over an arbitrary
field. For two real cones, take supplied closed groups generated by the
whole-torus centralizer and all actual relative root groups in the respective
cone slices. Their scheme-theoretic intersection is smooth. The group supplied
for the common cone slice is isomorphic to its identity component, and the
isomorphism commutes with the inclusions into the ambient group.

These statements do not require the cones to be closed. They identify the
identity component and do not assert that the entire intersection is connected.
This is an internal derivation for the later convex-root intersection proof.

The first lemma also identifies a supplied group for an indivisible-root cone
slice with the same character-semigroup subgroup. It applies the existing
root-system saturation lemma: each divisible root is twice an indivisible root,
positive dilation preserves the cone, and the doubled-root group is contained
in the corresponding indivisible-root group. The ambient relative-root-system
theorem supplies the required multiplier bound.

For smoothness, the cone-semigroup identification supplies closed presentations
of the two semigroup functors. The first supplied group is smooth by generated
root-group geometry, so the generic smooth-intersection theorem applies to the
actual pullback.

For the identity-component comparison, the cone-intersection semigroup is the
intersection of the two character semigroups. Monotonicity puts its group in
both supplied groups. The exact semigroup Lie-image theorem identifies its
ambient tangent image; the exact Lie-image theorem for the all-test intersection
gives the same span. The common cone-slice group is smooth and connected, and
the larger intersection is smooth and affine. The published
`identityComponentIsoOfLeOfHasLieImage` therefore identifies them, and its
factorization theorem gives compatibility with the ambient inclusions.

The corresponding lemmas specialize the same conclusions to two individually convex
subsets of the indivisible relative roots. Unpacking their cones and saturating
by divisible roots preserves each of the three supplied group maps. Intersecting
the cones gives the common subset, so the preceding smoothness and
identity-component comparison apply with no change of representatives.

Both the full-root cone normalizer inclusion and its indivisible-root convex
specialization are all-test consequences of this comparison. The
identity component is normal in the intersection. Its ambient image equals the
range of the common-root group, since precomposition by the comparison
isomorphism preserves ranges. Normality of the component therefore says that
every intersection point normalizes this range after every test refinement,
in both conjugation directions.
