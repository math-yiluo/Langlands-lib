# Central split tori detect nonzero rational characters

Author: Worker 1278.

The universe-polymorphic proposition
`Langlands.GroupScheme.CentralSplitTorusCharacterStatement` says that, over every
field `k`, every nonzero rational character of a connected reductive group `G`
restricts to a nonzero character of some central split subtorus of `G`.
This module defines the proposition as a `Prop`-valued `def`; it supplies no proof.

## Hypotheses and conclusion

The universe `u` is arbitrary. The proposition quantifies over every
`k : Type u` with a `Field k` instance and every
`G : GroupScheme (Spec (CommRingCat.of k))`. A group scheme here is the published
group object in schemes over `Spec k`, with its multiplication, unit and inverse.
There is no perfectness, characteristic, splitness, local-field or
algebraic-closedness hypothesis on `k` or `G`.

The hypothesis `IsConnectedReductive G` has exactly its published meaning in
[`Reductive.Basic`](../Reductive/Basic.md): `G` is smooth, affine and connected,
and its base change to `AlgebraicClosure k` has trivial unipotent radical.
The last condition says that every smooth connected unipotent normal closed
subgroup of that base change has trivial image as a subgroup functor. This is
the geometric condition; triviality of only the `k`-unipotent radical would be
the weaker pseudo-reductive condition and is not the hypothesis here.

The remaining terms are the published declarations from
[`Rational.Characters`](Characters.md):

- `χ : rationalCharacters G` is a homomorphism of group schemes over `k`
  from `G` to `𝔾_m`, written in the additive group of characters
  `X^*(G)_k`. Addition is pointwise multiplication of character values, so
  `χ ≠ 0` means that the character is not the constant identity character.
  This is nontriviality as a group-scheme morphism, rather than a condition
  solely on its values on `G(k)`.
- `A : CommGroupScheme (Spec (CommRingCat.of k))` is an actual commutative
  group scheme over `k`; `A.toGrp` is its underlying group scheme. The
  existentially quantified homomorphism `i : A.toGrp ⟶ G` presents a subgroup.
- `IsCentralSplitTorus i` is `IsSplitSubtorus i ∧ IsCentral i`.
  `IsSplitSubtorus i` requires `A.toGrp` to be isomorphic over `k` to a finite
  product of `𝔾_m` and requires the underlying scheme morphism of `i` to be a
  closed immersion. Thus `i` is a closed split subtorus of `G`.
- `IsCentral i` requires every point of `A` over every test scheme `T` over
  `Spec k` to map into the published subgroup functor `center G`. A point
  of `G(T)` lies in that centre when, after every morphism `T' ⟶ T` of test
  schemes, its restriction commutes with every point of `G(T')`. This is
  functorial centrality, stronger than merely checking the centre of `G(k)`.
- `characterRestrict i χ` is precomposition, the character `χ ∘ i` of
  `A.toGrp`. The conclusion `characterRestrict i χ ≠ 0` requires this
  group-scheme character to remain nontrivial.

The torus and its inclusion are existential conclusions and may depend on `χ`.
The proposition assumes neither a chosen split component nor its existence or
any factorization through it. It does not assert that one torus detects all
characters. If `rationalCharacters G` is trivial, the implication from `χ ≠ 0`
is vacuous, so such groups remain within the quantified scope.

Centrality uses the functorial centre and does not assume that a representing
centre is smooth or reduced. This matters in positive characteristic, where a
connected reductive group's scheme-theoretic centre can be nonsmooth.

This proposition is derived from structure facts in the following registered
sources; neither source states this exact proposition.

- David Renard, *Représentations des groupes réductifs p-adiques*, French edition
  dated 9 July 2026, source ID
  `renard-2026-representations-groupes-reductifs-padiques`, SHA-256
  `7b539996793b7c3620e72e1e389588eb42d1a8e91d6f68b2eead5c2e2ffc182c`.
  Section V.2.1, printed p.104 / PDF p.110, recalls that the radical `R(G)` of
  a connected reductive group is a central torus, that `G` is generated by
  `R(G)` and the semisimple derived group `DG`, and that their intersection is
  finite. It defines the split component as a maximal split torus in `R(G)`.
  Section V.2.2, printed p.105 / PDF p.111, explains that characters are trivial
  on `DG` and that restriction embeds the algebraic character lattice of `G`
  as a full-rank sublattice of that of `R(G)`. Its Remark 2 recalls the
  almost-direct product decomposition of a torus into its maximal split and
  maximal anisotropic subtori, with an anisotropic torus having no nonzero
  rational character. Renard works over a nonarchimedean local field `F`;
  these passages alone do not supply the arbitrary-field scope of the Lean
  proposition.

The mathematical derivation motivating the statement is as follows. A rational
character is trivial on `DG`, so a nonzero character must restrict nontrivially
to the central torus `R(G)`, since `R(G)` and `DG` generate `G`. Write `R(G)`
as the almost-direct product of its split subtorus and its anisotropic subtorus.
The character is trivial on the anisotropic factor; hence it must remain
nontrivial on the split factor. Its inclusion into `G` is a central closed
split subtorus, giving the required witness. Milne's arbitrary-field structure
facts supply the generality beyond Renard's local-field recall. This paragraph
explains the derived provenance; its argument has not been implemented as a
Lean proof in this statement module.
