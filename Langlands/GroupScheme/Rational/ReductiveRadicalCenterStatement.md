# The solvable radical of a connected reductive group

Let \(G\) be a connected reductive group over a field \(k\) with algebraic
closure \(\bar k\). A geometric solvable radical \(r:R\to G\) is a closed
subgroup that remains the solvable radical over \(\bar k\). The statement
asserts that \(R\) is a torus and \(r\) is central over \(k\). For any
closed representative \(Z\to G_{\bar k}\) of the scheme-theoretic centre,
the embedded subgroup \(R_{\bar k}\) equals \((Z_{\mathrm{red}})^\circ\).
The equality holds as subgroup functors on every test scheme over \(\bar k\).

This is the radical and centre comparison recalled in Renard,
*Représentations des groupes réductifs p-adiques*, French edition dated
9 July 2026, V.2.1, printed p. 104 (PDF p. 110), and the torus assertion in
V.2.2, printed p. 105 (PDF p. 111). Renard refers to Springer,
*Linear Algebraic Groups*, second edition, for the section. The reduced
identity component expresses the ordinary algebraic-group centre in
Renard's convention of identifying a group with its geometric points. The
underlying scheme-theoretic centre can have infinitesimal structure in
positive characteristic.
