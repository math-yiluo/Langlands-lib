# Actual root products after restriction between split tori

This internal construction works over a field `k : Type u`, at arbitrary
universe `u`. The ambient group scheme `G/k` is affine. The product and
generation theorems also require `G` to be smooth. All subgroup equalities use
the existing intrinsic subgroup functors and hold on every scheme over `k`,
including nonreduced test schemes.

`rootPointsProduct i a V x` multiplies the underlying points of the existing
root functors `subgroup i (a r)` in the order of `Fin n`. Its value lies in the
original group `G(V)`. This definition itself assumes neither smoothness nor
splitness. It also permits `n = 0`, when the product is the identity point.

For the selected-semigroup results, let `i : T.toGrp → G` be monomorphic, let
`T` be split, and retain the existing `HasOppositeFactorPairs i` hypothesis.
Let `A` be an additive subsemigroup of the actual character lattice of `T`,
with `0 ∉ A`. Let the injective list `a : Fin n → characterLattice T`
enumerate exactly `semigroupIndivisibleRoots i A`. Indivisibility here is
within the actual roots selected by `A`, as defined in
`SelectedRootSemigroupProduct`; it does not mean primitivity in the entire
character lattice.

`mem_semigroupSubgroup_iff_existsUnique_rootPointsProduct` says that a point
of `G(V)` belongs to the existing semigroup subgroup precisely when it has a
unique ordered expression with one point in each actual root functor
`subgroup i (a r)`. This is a statement about the intrinsic point subtypes,
so uniqueness does not depend on a choice of representing schemes.

`semigroupSubgroup_carrier_eq_iSup_selectedRoots` identifies this subgroup
of `G(V)` with the supremum of all `subgroup i χ` for actual roots `χ ∈ A`.
The supremum is taken in the lattice of subgroups of the original `G(V)`.
Thus it is ordinary group generation on each test scheme, with no closure
operation or sheafification in this conclusion. The theorem needs no supplied
list: finite enumeration is obtained in its proof.

For restriction, let `f : S → T` be any homomorphism between split
commutative group schemes over `k`, and take a nonzero character `α` of `S`.
The definition `characterSemigroupPreimage f (cyclicTail α 1)` selects every
`T`-character whose restriction is a positive integral multiple of `α`.
`zero_notMem_characterSemigroupPreimage_cyclicTail` proves that this
semigroup excludes zero. It uses torsion-freeness of the character lattice of
the split smaller torus and does not assume `f` is monomorphic.

With the same smooth affine `G`, monomorphic upper map `i` and upper
`HasOppositeFactorPairs i`, the remaining results apply the selected product
to this entire character inverse image:

- `subgroup_comp_torusHom_orderedRootProduct` supplies an actual group scheme
  representative `U → G` of the existing smaller-torus root functor and
  actual representatives `H r → G` of the selected larger-torus root functors.
  Each inclusion is closed, and each representing group is smooth, connected,
  unipotent and split unipotent. There is an isomorphism of schemes over `k`
  from the finite product of the `H r` to `U`, whose composite into `G` is
  exactly the existing `orderedMultiplication`. The ordered product is not
  asserted to be a homomorphism of group schemes.
- `mem_subgroup_comp_torusHom_iff_existsUnique_rootPointsProduct` gives unique
  larger-torus root coordinates for every smaller-torus root point on every
  test scheme, for every injective enumeration of the selected indivisible
  roots.
- `subgroup_comp_torusHom_carrier_eq_iSup` identifies the exact smaller-torus
  root subgroup of `G(V)` with the subgroup generated by all actual upper
  root groups whose roots restrict to positive multiples of `α`.

No assumption that `α` occurs as a root is needed: the theorems also cover a
trivial selected subgroup and the empty enumeration. There is no monomorphism
assumption on `f` or on the composite from the smaller torus.

The proof first invokes `semigroup_orderedRootProduct` from
`SelectedRootSemigroupProduct`. The finite-product point equivalence from
`OrderedProductPoints`, followed by each representing inclusion's point
equivalence, lifts an intrinsic tuple of root points to the product scheme.
The ambient ordered product agrees with its image under the constructed
scheme isomorphism and inclusion. Cancellation through that isomorphism and
the representing monomorphism proves injectivity. A point in the semigroup
subgroup lifts through its representing scheme and then through the inverse
isomorphism, proving existence. Conversely, every factor lies in the
semigroup subgroup by positive-ray containment, so its product lies there.

For the generation equality, factorization places every semigroup point in
the subgroup generated by the selected roots. In the reverse direction each
root in `A` has its positive cyclic tail in `A`, hence its entire root functor
is contained in the semigroup subgroup. Finally,
`subgroup_comp_torusHom_eq_semigroupSubgroup` from
`RootSubgroupTorusRestriction` identifies the exact restricted root functor
with the inverse-image semigroup subgroup. Substitution gives the three
restriction results above.

This is an internal consequence of the existing actual scheme-product and
split-torus restriction theorems, with no new assertion from literature.
The upper opposite-factor-pair hypothesis is retained explicitly. The unit
does not construct initial root valuations, building geometry, or a special
vertex for a supplied integral model, and does not prove
`BuildingComparisonStatement`.
