# Freudenthal's multiplicity formula for a split reductive group in characteristic zero

`Langlands.GroupScheme.FreudenthalMultiplicityStatement.{u}` is a Prop-valued
definition. This module defines the proposition and proves nothing.

## Statement

The proposition says: for every field `k : Type u` of characteristic zero
(`[CharZero k]`), every group scheme `G : GroupScheme (Spec (CommRingCat.of k))`
with `[IsAffineHom G.X.hom]`, every `p : SplitPinning G`, every `n : ℕ`, every
`r : MatrixRepresentation G (Fin n)` and every `lam : X`, where
`X = characterLattice p.torus`, if `p.IsHighestWeight r lam` then there is a
`ℤ`-bilinear form `F : X →ₗ[ℤ] X →ₗ[ℤ] ℚ` such that

1. `F x y = F y x` for all `x y : X` (symmetry);
2. `F (s_i x) (s_i y) = F x y` for every root index `i` and all `x y : X`,
   where `s_i = p.rootDatum.reflection i` is the reflection
   `x ↦ x - ⟨x, α_i^∨⟩ α_i` (Weyl invariance);
3. `0 < F x x` for every nonzero `x : X` (positive definiteness);
4. for every `μ : X`, writing `m ν = Module.finrank k (p.representationWeightSpace r ν)`
   (cast to `ℚ`) and `α_i = p.rootDatum.root i`,

   ```text
   (F lam lam + Σ_{i : α_i ∈ p.positiveRootMonoid} F lam α_i - F μ μ) * m μ
     = Σ_{i} Σ_{j : ℕ} F (μ + (j + 1) • α_i) α_i * m (μ + (j + 1) • α_i).
   ```

   Both sums are `finsum`s. The outer sum on the left runs over the root
   indices whose root lies in the monoid generated by the simple roots, that
   is, over the positive roots, each counted once (`RootPairing.root` is an
   embedding). The double sum on the right runs over all roots `α` and all
   integers `j ≥ 1`; for fixed `α` only finitely many `μ + j α` are weights
   of the finite-dimensional representation `r`, so the inner `finsum` is a
   finite sum, and `p.rootIndex` is finite.

The form `F` may depend on `r` (it is quantified after `r`). Nothing is
claimed about which form occurs; any form with properties 1–3 for which 4
holds is acceptable.

Every ingredient is an existing declaration, used without change:

- `SplitPinning G`, `p.torus`, `p.rootIndex`, `p.rootDatum`, `p.base` (from
  [`Reductive.Pinning`](../Reductive/Pinning.md)).
- `p.positiveRootMonoid`, `p.representationWeightSpace r μ` and
  `p.IsHighestWeight r lam` (from
  [`Rational.FundamentalRepresentations`](FundamentalRepresentations.md)):
  the submonoid of `X` generated by the simple roots; the weight space of
  `r` at `μ` for the action of the torus; and the conjunction "irreducible,
  sum of weight spaces is everything, the weight space at `lam` has
  dimension one, and every weight `μ` satisfies `lam - μ ∈ positiveRootMonoid`".
- `RootPairing.reflection` and `RootPairing.root` from Mathlib.

## Relation to the classical formula

Let `Φ` be the set of roots, `Φ⁺` the positive roots for the base `p.base`
and `ρ = ½ Σ_{α ∈ Φ⁺} α ∈ X ⊗ ℚ`. Extend `F` to `X ⊗ ℚ` and write
`‖x‖² = F x x`. Freudenthal's formula (Humphreys, *Introduction to Lie
Algebras and Representation Theory*, GTM 9, §22.3, Theorem; Freudenthal,
*Zur Berechnung der Charaktere der halbeinfachen Lieschen Gruppen I*,
Indag. Math. 16 (1954), 369–376) reads

```text
(‖lam + ρ‖² - ‖μ + ρ‖²) m(μ) = 2 Σ_{α ∈ Φ⁺} Σ_{j ≥ 1} F(μ + j α, α) m(μ + j α).
```

The Lean form is equivalent to it, by the following elementary computation.

- Left side. `F lam lam + Σ_{α ∈ Φ⁺} F lam α - F μ μ = ‖lam + ρ‖² - ‖μ + ρ‖² + 2 F(μ, ρ)`,
  since `Σ_{α ∈ Φ⁺} α = 2ρ`.
- Right side. The multiplicities are invariant under every root reflection
  (`SplitPinning.finrank_representationWeightSpace_root_reflection`), so
  along each `α`-string `Σ_{j ∈ ℤ} F(μ + j α, α) m(μ + j α) = 0` (the
  reflection `s_α` maps the string to itself and negates `F(·, α)`).
  Splitting the Lean sum into the terms of `α` and of `-α` for `α ∈ Φ⁺`
  and using this identity gives
  `2 Σ_{α ∈ Φ⁺} Σ_{j ≥ 1} F(μ + j α, α) m(μ + j α) + 2 F(μ, ρ) m(μ)`.

Subtracting `2 F(μ, ρ) m(μ)` from both sides gives the classical formula.
The Lean form avoids `ρ`, which need not lie in `X`.

## Provenance (derived)

The classical formula is stated for a finite-dimensional irreducible module
of highest weight `lam` over a complex semisimple Lie algebra, with `F` the
form on the dual of a Cartan subalgebra induced by the Killing form. The
statement here is its algebraic-group form for a split reductive group over
a field of characteristic zero; the derivation is not a numbered result of
a single source, for these reasons:

- Reductive rather than semisimple, with the character lattice of a split
  torus in place of the weight lattice of a Cartan subalgebra. A form
  satisfying 1–3 exists (for instance the dual of the positive definite
  form `β(y, y') = Σ_χ m'(χ) ⟨χ, y⟩ ⟨χ, y'⟩` on cocharacters, `m'` the
  weight multiplicities of a faithful representation).
- The form is existentially quantified. Any `W`-invariant form is orthogonal
  for the decomposition into the `W`-fixed part and the span of the roots,
  and on each simple factor of the root system it is a multiple of the
  Killing form; the classical formula is homogeneous of degree one in `F`
  and splits along the simple factors (an irreducible module of a product is
  a tensor product), so it holds for every such form. The existential is
  weaker and is what the proof of the Weyl character formula uses.
- The sum runs over all roots with `j ≥ 1` (see the previous section) and
  the left coefficient is written without `ρ`.

Humphreys is registered in the project literature as source
`provided-e4636f4e6257fbf54e791ed1ca16e71c5ba9c10c64f8184fd7ec2e0fe6e211e5`.

This proposition is an internal intermediate statement of the proof of
`Langlands.GroupScheme.WeylCharacterFormulaStatement`. It is proved
as `Langlands.GroupScheme.freudenthalMultiplicityStatement` in
`Langlands.GroupScheme.Rational.FreudenthalMultiplicityProof`, by the Casimir
operator of the trace form of a faithful representation together with `r`. That
operator acts by a scalar on the simple representation `r`, and the trace of
`x_α x_{-α}` on `r_μ` is computed by telescoping along the `α`-string.
