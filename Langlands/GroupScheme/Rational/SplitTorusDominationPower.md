# Prime-exponent reduction for commutative split-torus domination

This internal supporting module reduces the commutative positive-characteristic
case to the explicit remaining case of groups killed by the characteristic
prime. It proves neither that elementary case nor the exact unconditional
split-torus domination statement. The original ground field is retained,
including finite and imperfect fields.

`RelativeRoot.IsFppfQuotient.isCommMonObj` shows that an actual quasi-compact
fppf quotient of a commutative group is commutative. Every commutator in the
source is the identity and hence belongs to the supplied kernel; the preceding
structural module's fpqc commutator-descent lemma applies.

`commutative_splitUnipotent_of_splitTorusDomination_of_primeExponent` assumes
splitness for every dominated smooth connected affine commutative unipotent
group killed by the prime. It inducts on Lie dimension. The trivial group has
a series of length zero. Otherwise the published uniform all-test exponent
bound gives a last nontrivial multiplication by a power of the prime. Pointed
image generation supplies the smooth connected subgroup generated by its image.
If that subgroup were trivial, its canonical factor would make the original
power map trivial, a contradiction. Closed-kernel minimality makes the entire
subgroup killed by the prime. It is commutative and unipotent by its actual
closed inclusion. Its domination comes from applying the preceding operations
module to that pointed power map with the dominated original group as source.
The explicit elementary premise therefore gives a split series for this
subgroup.

The normal-subgroup quotient is smooth affine, connected, commutative,
unipotent, and dominated. Its Lie dimension is smaller since the kernel is
nontrivial, smooth and connected. Induction splits that quotient, and the
published fppf extension lemma concatenates the two series.
`splitSeriesOfPrimeExponentCase` selects the resulting series and length under
exactly the same premise.

Provenance: this is an internal assembly of the imported all-test exponent
bound, last-power-image construction, pointed image generation, domination
transport, quotient dimension decrease and split-series extension APIs. The
prime-exponent theorem is deliberately an explicit premise, not an assumed
axiom or an unconditional conclusion.
