# Detecting a positive vector factor with a pointed family

This internal deduction supplies an actual closed inclusion of a first group
factor into a product, a closed vector line in every positive-dimensional vector
group, and a positive-dimension test using a nontrivial pointed family. The
rational-point proposition remains an explicit premise.

The product inclusion puts the second coordinate at its unit. Its projection
back to the first factor is a retraction; that projection is separated when the
second group is separated, so the inclusion is closed. A singleton coordinate
block then supplies a closed vector line with natural coordinates on every test.

For a pointed family into a product of a vector group and a group that becomes
affine-line rigid over the separable closure, the second projection is one by
the conditional field-extension family theorem. If the vector factor had
coordinate dimension zero, its first projection would also be one. Product
extensionality and cancellation of the actual group isomorphism would make the
original family one, a contradiction.

The dimension test works over every field, with no characteristic restriction.
It assumes a smooth source group, a separated complementary group, an actual
product isomorphism, a nontrivial family whose entire basepoint section is one,
and affine-line constancy of the complementary group after extension to the
separable closure. Both the field and extension lie in the same arbitrary
universe. No group-homomorphism condition is imposed on the family.

The structural vector-factor proposition is not proved here. The unrestricted
all-field woundness criterion remains the target of the enclosing task.
