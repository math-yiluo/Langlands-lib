# Classification of simple representations of a split reductive group by highest weight

`Langlands.GroupScheme.SplitHighestWeightClassificationStatement.{u}` is a
Prop-valued definition. This module defines the proposition and proves
nothing.

## Statement

The proposition says: for every field `k : Type u`, every group scheme
`G : GroupScheme (Spec (CommRingCat.of k))` with `[IsAffineHom G.X.hom]` and
every split pinning `p : SplitPinning G`, the following three conjuncts hold.

1. Existence. For every `lam : characterLattice p.torus` with
   `p.IsDominantWeight lam`, there are `n : ℕ` and
   `ρ : MatrixRepresentation G (Fin n)` with `p.IsHighestWeight ρ lam`.
2. Isomorphism classes. For all `n m : ℕ`,
   `ρ : MatrixRepresentation G (Fin n)`, `σ : MatrixRepresentation G (Fin m)`
   and `lam mu : characterLattice p.torus` with `p.IsHighestWeight ρ lam` and
   `p.IsHighestWeight σ mu`,

   ```text
   (∃ C : Matrix (Fin m) (Fin n) k,
       ρ.Intertwines σ C ∧ Function.Bijective C.mulVecLin) ↔ lam = mu
   ```

3. Exhaustion. For every `n : ℕ` and every
   `ρ : MatrixRepresentation G (Fin n)` with `ρ.IsIrreducible`,

   ```text
   ∃! lam : characterLattice p.torus, p.IsDominantWeight lam ∧ p.IsHighestWeight ρ lam
   ```

In words: let `(G, T)` be a split reductive group over an arbitrary field
with a chosen base of its roots. Every dominant character is the highest
weight of a simple representation; two simple representations with highest
weights are isomorphic if and only if their highest weights are equal; and
every simple representation has exactly one dominant highest weight. So
taking the highest weight is a bijection from isomorphism classes of simple
representations of `G` onto dominant characters.

Every ingredient is an existing declaration, used without change:

- `SplitPinning G` (from [`Reductive.Pinning`](../Reductive/Pinning.md))
  bundles connected reductivity of `G`, a split maximal torus `p.torus` with
  its inclusion `p.inclusion`, a reduced root datum `p.rootDatum` with
  character lattice `characterLattice p.torus` that is the root datum of
  `(G, p.torus)` (`IsRootDatumOf`), a base `p.base` (a Mathlib
  `RootPairing.Base`) and trivializations of the simple-root Lie lines. The
  trivializations play no role in the statement.
- `p.IsDominantWeight lam` (from
  [`Rational.DominantHighestWeight`](DominantHighestWeight.md)) says that
  the integral pairing of `lam` with the coroot of every simple root in
  `p.base` is nonnegative.
- `p.positiveRootMonoid`, `p.representationWeightSpace ρ μ` and
  `p.IsHighestWeight ρ lam` (from
  [`Rational.FundamentalRepresentations`](FundamentalRepresentations.md)).
  The monoid is the additive submonoid of the character lattice generated by
  the simple roots, so `lam - μ ∈ p.positiveRootMonoid` says
  `lam - μ = Σ_{α ∈ Δ} m_α α` with `m_α ∈ ℕ`. The weight space is the
  character space (from
  [`MatrixRepresentation.CharacterSpaces`](../MatrixRepresentation/CharacterSpaces.md))
  of the restriction of `ρ` to the torus at the character `μ`. And
  `p.IsHighestWeight ρ lam` is the conjunction of: `ρ.IsIrreducible`; the
  weight spaces of `ρ` span `kⁿ`; the weight space at `lam` has dimension
  one; and `lam - μ ∈ p.positiveRootMonoid` for every `μ` with nonzero weight
  space.
- `MatrixRepresentation G (Fin n)` (from
  [`MatrixRepresentation`](../MatrixRepresentation.md)) is a homomorphism of group schemes
  `G ⟶ GL_n` over `k`, that is, a representation of `G` on `kⁿ` with its
  standard basis. `ρ.IsIrreducible` (from
  [`MatrixRepresentation.DenseRationalStability`](../MatrixRepresentation/DenseRationalStability.md))
  says that `kⁿ ≠ 0` and that the only subspaces stable in the sense of
  `IsStableSubspace` (from
  [`MatrixRepresentation.StableSubrepresentation`](../MatrixRepresentation/StableSubrepresentation.md))
  are `0` and `kⁿ`. Stable subspaces are the subrepresentations: the
  condition is checked on the points of `G` with values in every commutative
  `k`-algebra. So `ρ.IsIrreducible` says that `ρ` is a simple
  representation.
- `ρ.Intertwines σ C` (from
  [`Lie.RepresentationSubquotient`](../Lie/RepresentationSubquotient.md))
  says that for every commutative `k`-algebra `S` and every point `x` of `G`
  with values in `S`, `C · ρ(x) = σ(x) · C` as matrices over `S`. So the
  linear map `C : kⁿ → kᵐ` is a homomorphism of representations, and with
  `Function.Bijective C.mulVecLin` it is an isomorphism of representations.

## Provenance (derived)

Source: J. S. Milne, *Algebraic Groups* (2017), registered as
`milne-2017-algebraic-groups` (SHA-256
`899b0f44f3c3ccfbaf5ed1cf9c180be7a8016b1c0e9709ca0c206304e5bd1cd0`). Printed page
numbers are given; the PDF page is the printed page plus 16.

The statement combines three results of Chapter 22, Section a:

- Theorem 22.2 (Fundamental theorem), printed pp. 463–464: "For each
  dominant λ ∈ X, there exists a simple representation V(λ) of G, unique up
  to isomorphism, that decomposes as a representation of T into
  V(λ) = V(λ)_λ ⊕ ⊕_{μ} V(λ)_μ with V(λ)_λ of dimension 1 and all μ < λ.
  Every simple representation is isomorphic to V(λ) for a unique dominant λ."
- 22.3, printed p. 464: "Every simple representation (V, r) has a unique
  weight λ(r) (called the highest weight) such that μ < λ(r) for all other
  weights. The map (V, r) ↦ λ(r) defines a bijection from the set of
  isomorphism classes of simple representations of G to the set of dominant
  λ ∈ X."
- Theorem 22.19, printed p. 468: "Two simple representations of G are
  isomorphic if and only if their highest weights are equal."

Conjunct 1 is the existence in Theorem 22.2 (also Theorem 22.20, p. 468).
Conjunct 2 is Theorem 22.19 for representations with the highest-weight
decomposition; its direction from right to left gives the uniqueness up to
isomorphism in Theorem 22.2, and its direction from left to right says that
the map of 22.3 is well defined on isomorphism classes. Conjunct 3 is the
last sentence of Theorem 22.2 and the first sentence of 22.3. Together they
give the bijection of 22.3.

The setting is that of the opening of Chapter 22, printed p. 463: "Let
(G, T) be a split reductive group over k, and let (X, Φ, Φ∨) be its root
datum. Because T is split, every representation (V, r) of G decomposes into
a direct sum V = ⊕_{λ ∈ X(T)} V_λ of its weight spaces V_λ for the action of
T (see 12.12)." Milne fixes "a Borel subgroup B of G containing T", lets Φ⁺
be the corresponding positive roots and Δ the simple roots in Φ⁺, and
defines "μ ≤ λ if λ − μ = Σ_{α ∈ Δ} m_α α with m_α ∈ ℕ". Definition 22.1:
"An element λ of X is said to be dominant if ⟨λ, α∨⟩ ≥ 0 for all α ∈ Φ⁺."
The same page says: "Throughout, representations are finite-dimensional
unless it is specified otherwise." Further conventions:

- Chapter 21, printed p. 424: "The field k is arbitrary", and "G is split if
  some maximal torus T is split, in which case we refer to the pair (G, T) as
  a split reductive group". Reductive groups are connected (printed p. 135:
  "A connected group variety over a field k is said to be reductive if G_{k^a}
  is reductive"). Notation, printed p. 4: "After p. 162, all algebraic groups
  are affine". The Lean statement has an affine group scheme `G` whose
  connected reductivity is part of `p`.
- Definition 4.15, printed p. 90: "A representation of an algebraic group is
  simple if it is ≠ 0 and its only subrepresentations are 0 and itself."
  Proposition 4.16: "Every simple representation of an algebraic group is
  finite-dimensional."
- Theorem 12.12, printed pp. 234–235, gives the decomposition into weight
  spaces for a diagonalizable group, here the split torus `T`.

The provenance is derived for these reasons:

- Base instead of Borel subgroup. Milne fixes a Borel subgroup `B ⊇ T`; the
  Lean statement uses the base `p.base`. By Theorem 21.32 (printed p. 434)
  the Borel subgroups containing `T` correspond bijectively to the Weyl
  chambers, and Definition 21.35 (printed p. 435) attaches to `B` its system
  of positive roots `Φ⁺(B)`. The base `p.base` determines a system of
  positive roots, the roots that are nonnegative combinations of the simple
  roots, and hence a Borel subgroup `B ⊇ T` with this `Φ⁺(B)` and simple
  roots `Δ = p.base`. The order `μ ≤ λ` is membership of `λ − μ` in
  `p.positiveRootMonoid`. Relating `B` and `p.base` is an obligation of a
  proof.
- Dominance through simple coroots. `p.IsDominantWeight` asks
  `⟨λ, α∨⟩ ≥ 0` only for `α ∈ Δ`. This is equivalent to Definition 22.1,
  because the coroot of every positive root is a nonnegative integral
  combination of the simple coroots. The 2022 corrected reprint states the
  equivalence in its Definition 22.1 (see below).
- Highest-weight representations. `p.IsHighestWeight ρ lam` is the condition
  on `V(λ)` in Theorem 22.2: `ρ` is simple, `kⁿ` is the sum of the weight
  spaces, the weight space at `lam` is one-dimensional and every weight `μ`
  satisfies `μ ≤ lam`. For a simple representation this says that `lam` is
  its highest weight in the sense of 22.3.
- Coordinates. Finite-dimensional representations are given as matrix
  representations on `kⁿ`; every finite-dimensional representation is
  isomorphic to one of these, and simplicity, weight spaces and isomorphism
  classes are preserved by isomorphisms. Isomorphisms of representations
  are bijective intertwining matrices.
- Exhaustion (conjunct 3) asks for exactly one `lam` that is dominant and a
  highest weight of `ρ` in the sense of `IsHighestWeight`. Milne's 22.3 says
  more: `λ(r)` is the only weight `λ'` with `μ < λ'` for all other weights
  `μ`. This follows from conjunct 3: such a `λ'` satisfies `λ' ≤ lam`
  because it is a weight, and `lam ≤ λ'` because `lam` is a weight; as the
  simple roots are linearly independent, `λ' = lam`.
- Pinnings. The statement is quantified over split pinnings, which carry
  data that Milne does not fix: a reduced root datum identified with the
  roots of `(G, T)` and nonzero vectors in the simple-root Lie lines. The
  vectors are not used.
- `k`, `G`, `p.rootIndex` and the test algebras in `IsStableSubspace` and
  `Intertwines` lie in one universe `u`. This is a formalization convention.

Relation to the Library. The Library's
`SplitDominantHighestWeightRepresentationsExistStatement` (in
[`Rational.DominantHighestWeight`](DominantHighestWeight.md)) is conjunct 1
for algebraically closed `k` only; it has no uniqueness or exhaustion part.
The present statement is over an arbitrary field, as in Milne, and adds
conjuncts 2 and 3.

## For the proof (not part of this module)

The Library proves the proposition as `splitHighestWeightClassification` in
[`Rational.SplitHighestWeightClassificationProof`](SplitHighestWeightClassificationProof.md).

Milne's route, with `B` the Borel subgroup attached to `p.base`:

- Conjunct 3. `B` is split (21.34), hence trigonalizable (16.52), so a
  simple `V` contains a primitive vector `v` (an eigenvector for `B`,
  Definition 22.16), which generates `V`. Proposition 22.17 (printed p. 467)
  gives: the weight `λ` of `v` has multiplicity one, every other weight is
  `λ − Σ_{α ∈ Δ} m_α α` with `m_α ∈ ℕ`, and `λ` is dominant. With 12.12
  this gives `IsHighestWeight ρ λ` and `IsDominantWeight λ`. If `λ` and `λ'`
  both satisfy `IsHighestWeight ρ`, then `λ − λ'` and `λ' − λ` both lie in
  `p.positiveRootMonoid`; the simple roots are linearly independent, so
  `λ = λ'`.
- Conjunct 2. From right to left: Theorem 22.19 (printed p. 468) uses
  primitive vectors `v₁, v₂` in `V₁, V₂` (Theorem 22.18), whose weights are
  both `lam` by the argument of conjunct 3; the `G`-submodule `V` of `V₁ ⊕ V₂` generated by `v₁ + v₂` projects
  isomorphically onto `V₁` and onto `V₂`. From left to right: an isomorphism
  is `T`-equivariant, so it maps weight spaces onto weight spaces, and then
  `IsHighestWeight σ lam` and `IsHighestWeight σ mu` give `lam = mu` as in
  conjunct 3.
- Conjunct 1. Theorem 22.20: it suffices to find a representation, possibly
  infinite-dimensional, with a primitive vector of weight `λ`; the quotient
  of the `G`-submodule it generates by its largest proper `G`-stable subspace
  (22.17) is simple, hence finite-dimensional (4.16), with highest weight
  `λ`. The construction uses 22.21–22.26 and 22.12. The 2017 text of this
  construction is corrected by the errata entries for pp. 468 and 469; the
  corrected reprint gives the revised version on its printed pp. 469–471,
  reducing the general case to a product of a semisimple group and a torus
  by a central isogeny (19.25) and 22.12. The Library's proof of the
  algebraically closed case,
  `Langlands.GroupScheme.splitDominantHighestWeightRepresentationsExist`, does
  not cover an arbitrary field.

In the Lean setting, `B` must be produced from `p.base`, and submodules,
quotients and direct sums of representations must be given coordinates
`kʳ`.
