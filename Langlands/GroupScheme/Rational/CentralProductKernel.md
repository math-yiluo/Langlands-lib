# Actual factor kernels and central ordered-product descent

This internal development works over any base scheme and universe. In an
actual commuting square of group-scheme homomorphisms, suppose a specified
monomorphism represents the full scheme-theoretic kernel of the ambient map.
The kernel of the factor map maps into this represented kernel. The canonical
`factorKernelHom` is defined by the existing universal factorization through a
represented subgroup, and its composite is the original kernel inclusion
followed by the original factor inclusion.

If the original factor represents an all-test subgroup and the quotient-factor
inclusion is monic, this kernel map represents exactly the preimage of that
subgroup in the ambient kernel. Indeed a point of the preimage lifts uniquely
to the original factor. Its quotient coordinate is killed by the monic
quotient-factor inclusion, so it is the identity and the lift factors through
the actual scheme kernel. Monicity of the ambient kernel inclusion identifies
the two maps. The argument applies on every test scheme and imposes no
surjectivity on point groups of those tests.

The companion representative-invariance result replaces every factor of an
ordered product by any representative of the same actual all-test subgroup.
The canonical representing isomorphisms give a bijection of factor tuples on
every test. The original homomorphism squares identify their ordered products,
so bijectivity of the ordered point product transfers. The existing all-test
criterion gives the actual ordered-multiplication isomorphism. Empty products
are included, and no reordering or commutativity is assumed.

These results identify actual factor kernels for the central-extension step.
They do not supply semigroup intersection, semigroup realization, central
vector subgroups or the general ordered semigroup product theorem.
