# Rational points of a commutative target generated through a finite product

Author: Worker 1757.

This module is an internal helper about rational points of group schemes.
A commutative group scheme `Q` may receive a finite family of homomorphisms
`φ i : Q i ⟶ Q`. If the induced map from the product `∏ Q i` to `Q` is
surjective on rational points, then the images of the `Q i (k)` generate
`Q(k)`. This holds in particular when the map is an isomorphism. The module
proves these facts. It supplies a way to discharge the quotient hypothesis
`hQ` of the diagram chase in
`Langlands.GroupScheme.QuotientGeneration`, which is
`Langlands/GroupScheme/SimplyConnected/LocalQuotientGeneration.lean`.
That module is not imported here, and nothing here proves Theorem A or
`LocalKneserTitsStatement`.

All declarations live in `Langlands.GroupScheme.ProductGeneration`.
Rational points are the existing `rationalPoints G`, the sections of `G`
over the base. `rationalPoints.map f` is postcomposition with a
homomorphism `f`.

## The generated subgroup

Let `k` be a commutative ring, `J` any index type, `Q i` and `Q` group
schemes over `Spec k`, and `φ i : Q i ⟶ Q` homomorphisms.
`componentPoints φ` is the subgroup of `Q(k)` generated by the union of the
images of all the maps `rationalPoints.map (φ i)`. It is an `abbrev` for
this subgroup closure. This is exactly the subgroup on the right-hand side
of the hypothesis `hQ` in `QuotientGeneration`.

## The product map

From now on `J : Type u` is finite (`Fintype J`) and `Q` is commutative
(`IsCommMonObj Q.X`). The product map is the existing
`FiniteEtaleSum.sumMap φ : pi Q ⟶ Q`, the product over `i` of the
projection to `Q i` followed by `φ i`. Its definition needs no finite étale
hypothesis. Here `pi Q` is the Library's categorical product of the family
of group schemes. Its rational points are identified with families of
rational points by `piPointsEquiv`.

- `rationalPoints_map_sumMap`: for a rational point `x` of the product,
  `sumMap φ` sends `x` to the product over `i` of `φ i` applied to the
  `i`-th coordinate of `x`.
- `range_map_sumMap`: the image of the rational points of the product
  under `sumMap φ` is exactly `componentPoints φ`.
- `surjective_map_sumMap_iff`: `sumMap φ` is surjective on rational points
  if and only if `componentPoints φ = ⊤`.
- `componentPoints_eq_top_of_isIso`: if `sumMap φ` is an isomorphism of
  group schemes, then `componentPoints φ = ⊤`.
- `range_le_componentPoints_of_surjective` and
  `range_le_componentPoints_of_isIso`: under the same hypotheses, the
  image of `rationalPoints.map q` lies in `componentPoints φ` for every
  homomorphism `q : M ⟶ Q`. This is the form of `hQ`.

## Proofs

The coordinate formula expands `sumMap φ` as a finite product of
homomorphisms. Composition with a fixed rational point respects products
of homomorphisms into a commutative group object, so the image of `x` is
the product of the composites. Each composite is the image under `φ i` of
the `i`-th coordinate.

For `range_map_sumMap`, one inclusion holds because a finite product of
elements of a subgroup lies in it. For the other inclusion it suffices to
hit every generator `φ i (z)`. Take the rational point of the product whose
`i`-th coordinate is `z` and whose other coordinates are `1`. By the
coordinate formula its image is `φ i (z)`. The surjectivity criterion
restates this equality. If `sumMap φ` is an isomorphism, the point
`y ≫ inv (sumMap φ)` is a preimage of any rational point `y` of `Q`,
which gives surjectivity.

## Relation to Prasad–Raghunathan

In the proof of Theorem A of Prasad–Raghunathan (1985), §1.6, `M` is the
centralizer of a maximal `k`-split torus in a simply connected group and
`𝒢` is the derived group of `M`. The paper shows that `M/𝒢` is isomorphic
to the torus `T_{Δ−Δ₀}`, which is the direct product of tori `T_{Θ_i}`
indexed by the Galois orbits `Θ_i` in `Δ − Δ₀`. This module takes no
position on that structure. When such a product decomposition is given as
an isomorphism `sumMap φ`, `range_le_componentPoints_of_isIso` yields the
hypothesis `hQ` of the diagram chase. The quotients `Q i` there are
quotients of the factors `M_{Θ_i}`, and the chase also needs the maps `φ i`
to be compatible with them. The paper's own argument uses a different
map, into a product of quotients of Levi subgroups, together with
Theorem B. Neither the paper's isomorphism nor any compatible family of
maps is formalized here.

Source: G. Prasad and M. S. Raghunathan, *On the Kneser–Tits problem*,
Comment. Math. Helv. 60 (1985), §1.6 (proof of Theorem A, printed
pp. 109–110).

## Scope

The facts are elementary and hold over any commutative ring for any finite
family. No torus, reductivity, smoothness or finite étale assumption is
used. The isomorphism or surjectivity of `sumMap φ` stays an explicit
hypothesis. It is not proved for any actual quotient of a centralizer.
