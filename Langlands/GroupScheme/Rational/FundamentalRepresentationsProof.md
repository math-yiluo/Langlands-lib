# Proof: fundamental representations of simply connected split groups exist

Author: Worker 5063.

This module proves the published proposition
`Langlands.GroupScheme.SplitFundamentalRepresentationsExistStatement`
([`FundamentalRepresentations`](FundamentalRepresentations.md)) at its original
universe `u`:

```
theorem splitFundamentalRepresentationsExist :
    SplitFundamentalRepresentationsExistStatement.{u}
```

The theorem's type is exactly the published proposition. Let `k : Type u` be an
algebraically closed field of any characteristic and `G` an actual group scheme
over `Spec k` with affine structure morphism. Let `p : SplitPinning G` be a
supplied split pinning with `IsSimplyConnected G`. For each simple root `a` of
`p.base`, the theorem gives an element of `p.FundamentalRepresentation a`. This
is a finite actual matrix representation `G → GL_(Fin n)` with a torus character
`λ`, satisfying two conditions. First, `⟨λ, α_b∨⟩ = δ_(b,a)` for every simple
coroot `α_b∨`. Second, the full `p.IsHighestWeight` package holds at `λ`:

- actual irreducibility;
- the torus character spaces span the whole coordinate space;
- the `λ` character space has dimension one;
- for every occurring weight `μ`, `λ - μ` lies in the monoid generated by the
  simple roots.

The theorem adds no hypothesis. In particular there is no coroot-span,
highest-weight, representation-existence or geometric simple-connectedness
premise. The proof does not change the statement's Lean/Markdown pair or any
module that pair imports. `lean_check` builds the module with no errors or
warnings. The theorem depends only on the standard axioms `propext`,
`Classical.choice` and `Quot.sound`.

## Implemented proof

The proof is one application of the published conditional assembly
`splitFundamentalRepresentationsExist_of_corootSpan_of_highestWeight` from
[`FundamentalRepresentationsAssembly`](FundamentalRepresentationsAssembly.md).
That theorem derives the target from two propositions. Both are now published
theorems without hypotheses, and the proof passes them in:

- `splitSimplyConnectedCorootSpan : SplitSimplyConnectedCorootSpanStatement.{u}`,
  from [`SimplyConnected.SplitNecessity`](../SimplyConnected/SplitNecessity.md).
  For an algebraically simply connected group over any field, a split maximal
  torus and a finite reduced root datum of it, the integral coroots span the
  whole cocharacter lattice. This is the necessity half of the coroot criterion
  (Milne, *Algebraic Groups*, Proposition 23.59, split case). Its proof combines
  the published Existence Theorem, the central case of the Isogeny Theorem and
  the semisimplicity criterion.
- `splitDominantHighestWeightRepresentationsExist :
  SplitDominantHighestWeightRepresentationsExistStatement.{u}`, from
  [`DominantHighestWeightProof`](DominantHighestWeightProof.md). Over an
  algebraically closed field, every integral character of a supplied split
  pinning with nonnegative pairing against every simple coroot is the highest
  weight of a finite actual matrix representation, with the full
  `IsHighestWeight` package. The representation is the finite covariant socle of
  a coordinate realizing that character.

The assembly then runs as follows; its Markdown gives the details:

1. The first input, applied to the supplied group, `p.torus`, `p.inclusion`,
   `p.rootDatum` and the pinning's split, maximal, root-datum and reducedness
   fields, gives `p.rootDatum.corootSpan ℤ = ⊤`.
2. With this span, the published `Langlands.RootDatum.fundamentalWeightBasis`
   supplies the character `λ_a` dual to the simple-coroot basis at `a`. Each
   pairing `⟨λ_a, α_b∨⟩` is one or zero, so `λ_a` is dominant
   (`fundamentalWeightBasis_isDominant`).
3. The second input, applied to `λ_a`, gives `n` and
   `ρ : MatrixRepresentation G (Fin n)` with `p.IsHighestWeight ρ λ_a`.
4. `fundamentalRepresentationOfHighestWeight` packages `ρ` and `λ_a` as a
   `p.FundamentalRepresentation a`. The Kronecker-delta condition is
   `fundamentalWeightBasis_pairing`.

## Independence of the inputs

The two input theorems take no hypotheses. Neither can use the target
proposition, which had no proof in the Library before this module. The proof
uses neither
invariant generation nor a quotient section. It does not use the split or
quasi-split Steinberg quotient statements, whose Lean/Markdown pairs are
unchanged.

## Provenance

The statement's original source is R. Steinberg, *Regular elements of
semi-simple algebraic groups*, Publ. Math. IHÉS 25 (1965), section 2.6 and
Lemma 7.15(a)–(c), as cited in the statement's Markdown. The Lean proof does
not follow Steinberg's text. It only combines the three published results
named above: the conditional assembly and its two input theorems.
