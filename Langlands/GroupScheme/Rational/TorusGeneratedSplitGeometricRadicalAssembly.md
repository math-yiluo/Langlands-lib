retains three universal propositions as explicit arguments:
`SplitTorusDominatedSolvableQuotientStatement`,
`UnipotentSplitTorusDominationStatement`, and
`RelativeRoot.SplitUnipotentStableVectorSubgroupStatement`. It does not
prove any of those propositions or the unconditional target.

`TorusKernelDomination.representative_isSplitUnipotent_of_stableVector`

closed smooth connected unipotent full-kernel representative
`i : U ⟶ H`, and pointed split-torus domination of the smooth affine
group `H`. The all-test kernel equality in `IsFppfQuotient` gives the
canonical `KernelRepresentative.iso q i` between the full categorical
kernel and `U`. It transfers smoothness, connectedness, and unipotence
to that kernel. Surjectivity of the quotient supplies dominance.
`kernel_isSplitUnipotent_of_stableVector` then gives splitness of that
same full kernel, using the explicit domination-splitness and
stable-vector inputs. Splitness transports back along the canonical
isomorphism to the specified `U`.

`IsTorusGenerated.splitGeometricRadicalOfStructuralStatements` returns
an actual ground-field group scheme, its inclusion, and both the
geometric-radical and split-unipotence predicates. Starting from a
smooth connected affine solvable group `G` generated by its

`solvableFieldExtensionStatement` transports solvability to the actual
base-changed group. The explicit quotient-existence premise provides
an actual torus quotient there, with a closed smooth connected
unipotent full kernel. The preceding comparison lemma gives that
kernel representative a split series over the extension field.

`finiteGaloisSplitTorusQuotientDescent` then descends this supplied
quotient and kernel to `k`, retaining the actual geometric-radical
comparison and ground-field splitness. It uses the explicit
stable-vector input to descend splitness; no claim that every
unipotent group over an imperfect field is split is used.

Finally,
`torusGeneratedSplitGeometricRadical_of_structuralStatements`
forgets the chosen data into the exact published

The provenance is internal and derived: this module assembles the
