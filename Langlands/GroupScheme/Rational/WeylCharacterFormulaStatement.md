# The Weyl character formula in characteristic zero

`Langlands.GroupScheme.WeylCharacterFormulaStatement.{u}` is a Prop-valued
definition. This module defines the proposition and proves nothing.

## Statement

The proposition says: for every field `k : Type u` of characteristic zero
(`[CharZero k]`), every group scheme `G : GroupScheme (Spec (CommRingCat.of k))`
with `[IsAffineHom G.X.hom]`, every `p : SplitPinning G` and every
`rho : characterLattice p.torus` with

```text
2 • rho = ∑ᶠ (i : p.rootIndex) (_ : p.rootDatum.root i ∈ p.positiveRootMonoid),
  p.rootDatum.root i
```

the following holds. Write `X = characterLattice p.torus` and
`W = LinearAlgebra.DominantTriangular.reflectionGroup p.rootDatum`. The
statement introduces, by a local `let`, the map
`J : X → AddMonoidAlgebra ℤ X`,

```text
J μ = ∑ᶠ w : W, AddMonoidAlgebra.single (w μ) (LinearMap.det w)
```

where `w μ` applies the automorphism `w : X ≃ₗ[ℤ] X` and `LinearMap.det w` is
the determinant of its underlying `ℤ`-linear map. Then for every `n : ℕ`,
every `r : MatrixRepresentation G (Fin n)` and every `lam : X` with
`p.IsHighestWeight r lam`, there is `c : X →₀ ℤ` such that

- `c μ = Module.finrank k (p.representationWeightSpace r μ)` for every
  `μ : X`, and
- `J rho * AddMonoidAlgebra.ofCoeff c = J (lam + rho)` in
  `AddMonoidAlgebra ℤ X`.

In words: if the half sum `rho` of the positive roots lies in the character
lattice, then the character of every simple representation with highest
weight `lam` is `J(e^{lam + rho}) / J(e^rho)`.

Every ingredient is an existing declaration, used without change:

- `SplitPinning G` (from [`Reductive.Pinning`](../Reductive/Pinning.md))
  bundles `IsConnectedReductive G`, a split maximal torus `p.torus` with its
  inclusion `p.inclusion`, a finite index type `p.rootIndex`, a Mathlib
  `RootDatum` `p.rootDatum` on the character and cocharacter lattices of
  `p.torus`, a proof `p.isRootDatum : IsRootDatumOf p.inclusion p.rootDatum`,
  reducedness `p.isReduced`, a base `p.base` and trivializations of the
  simple-root Lie lines. The statement uses the torus, the root datum and
  the base; the Lie trivializations do not occur in it.
- `IsRootDatumOf i P` (from [`Reductive.RootDatum`](../Reductive/RootDatum.md))
  says that the pairing of `P` is `latticePairing T`, that the set of roots
  of `P` is the set `roots i` of roots of `G` with respect to the torus, and
  that every reflection `P.reflection α` is induced by a point of `G`
  normalizing the torus.
- `characterLattice T` (from [`Torus.Lattice`](../Torus/Lattice.md)) is
  `T ⟶ Gm`, the group of characters of `T`, written additively.
- `p.positiveRootMonoid` (from
  [`Rational.FundamentalRepresentations`](FundamentalRepresentations.md)) is
  the additive submonoid of `X` generated by the simple roots
  `p.rootDatum.root a`, `a : p.base.support`. A root `p.rootDatum.root i` lies
  in it exactly when it is a nonnegative integral combination of the simple
  roots, that is, when it is positive for the base `p.base`. Mathlib's
  `RootPairing.root` is an embedding `p.rootIndex ↪ X`, so the sum over the
  indices `i` with positive root is the sum of the positive roots, each
  counted once. `p.rootIndex` is finite (the instance
  `SplitPinning.finiteRootIndex`), so this `finsum` is a finite sum.
- `LinearAlgebra.DominantTriangular.reflectionGroup P` (from
  [`LinearAlgebra.DominantTriangularInvariants`](../../LinearAlgebra/DominantTriangularInvariants.md))
  is the subgroup of `X ≃ₗ[ℤ] X` generated by the reflections
  `P.reflection i`. Mathlib's reflection is `x ↦ x - (coroot' i x) • root i`
  (`RootPairing.reflection_apply`). The instance `finite_reflectionGroup`
  in the same module says that this group is finite when the index type is
  finite, so the `finsum` defining `J μ` is a finite sum.
- `AddMonoidAlgebra ℤ X` is Mathlib's group ring of `X` over `ℤ`, with
  multiplication `single a s * single b t = single (a + b) (s * t)`
  (`AddMonoidAlgebra.single_mul_single`). `AddMonoidAlgebra.single χ 1` is
  Milne's basis element `e^χ`, and `AddMonoidAlgebra.ofCoeff c` is the
  element `Σ_χ c(χ) e^χ` with coefficient function `c`.
- `p.representationWeightSpace r μ` (from
  [`Rational.FundamentalRepresentations`](FundamentalRepresentations.md)) is
  the character space of `r.restrict p.inclusion` at the rational character
  corresponding to `μ` under `rationalCharacters.equivCharacterLattice`, the
  eigenspace of the torus in `kⁿ` with character `μ`. So
  `Module.finrank k (p.representationWeightSpace r μ)` is the multiplicity
  of `μ` as a weight of `r`, and the element `ofCoeff c` of the conclusion is
  the character `Σ_μ dim(V_μ) e^μ`.
- `p.IsHighestWeight r lam` (from
  [`Rational.FundamentalRepresentations`](FundamentalRepresentations.md))
  says that `r` is irreducible (`MatrixRepresentation.IsIrreducible`, from
  [`MatrixRepresentation.DenseRationalStability`](../MatrixRepresentation/DenseRationalStability.md):
  `kⁿ` is nonzero and its only stable subspaces are `⊥` and `⊤`), that `kⁿ`
  is the supremum of the weight spaces, that the weight space at `lam` has
  dimension `1`, and that `lam - μ ∈ p.positiveRootMonoid` for every weight
  `μ`.
- `MatrixRepresentation G (Fin n)` (from
  [`MatrixRepresentation`](../MatrixRepresentation.md)) is a homomorphism of
  group schemes `G ⟶ GL_n`, a representation of `G` on `kⁿ` with its
  standard basis.

The witness `c` is unique when it exists, since its values are prescribed.
It exists exactly when only finitely many weight spaces of `r` are nonzero,
which holds because eigenspaces of the torus for distinct characters are
linearly independent. The existential packages this finiteness, so that the
character can be written as an element of `AddMonoidAlgebra ℤ X`.

## Provenance (derived)

Source: J. S. Milne, *Algebraic Groups* (2017), registered as
`milne-2017-algebraic-groups` (SHA-256
`899b0f44f3c3ccfbaf5ed1cf9c180be7a8016b1c0e9709ca0c206304e5bd1cd0`). Printed page
numbers are given; the PDF page is the printed page plus 16.

The statement is Theorem 22.52 (Weyl character formula), printed p. 480:
"For a simple representation V of G with highest weight χ,
ch_G(V) = J(e^{χ+ρ})/J(e^ρ) = Σ_{w∈W} det(w) e^{w(χ+ρ)} / Σ_{w∈W} det(w) e^{w(ρ)}."
(Milne writes the highest weight as `χ`; the Lean statement calls it `lam`.)
Milne's proof treats characteristic zero (through the cohomology of line
bundles on `G/B`, Theorems 22.50 and 22.51) and refers to Iversen and
Jantzen for the general case.

Its setting, printed pp. 479–480:

- Section 22d opens, printed p. 479: "Let (G, T) be a split reductive group
  over a field k, and let B be a Borel subgroup of G containing T."
- Printed p. 480: "We now assume that Pic(G) = 0. When G is semisimple, this
  condition means that G is simply connected (18.19, 18.23). Let X = X(T)
  and let W = W(G, T). We let W act on ℤ[X] on the left (see 22.36 for
  ℤ[X]). For w ∈ W, we let det(w) = det(w|X), and we define the antisymmetry
  operator J : ℤ[X] → ℤ[X], J(e^χ) = Σ_{w∈W} det(w) e^{w(χ)}. The half sum of
  the positive roots, ρ = ½ Σ{α | α ∈ Φ⁺}, lies in X – the proof of this uses
  that Pic(G) = 0 (Iversen 1976, 9.2)."
  The last assertion is false in general (`GL₂` is a counterexample; see the
  bullet on the half sum below), and the Lean statement does not rely on it.

Further conventions:

- Opening of Chapter 22, printed p. 463: "Throughout, representations are
  finite-dimensional unless it is specified otherwise." The weights of a
  representation are the characters `λ` with `V_λ ≠ 0` for the action of
  `T` (same page). Milne fixes "a Borel subgroup B of G containing T", lets
  Φ⁺ be "the corresponding system of positive roots and Δ the set of simple
  roots in Φ⁺", and defines an order relation on `X` "by setting λ ≥ μ if
  λ − μ = Σ_{α∈Δ} m_α α with m_α ∈ ℕ. Thus Φ⁺ = {α ∈ Φ | α > 0}."
- 22.3, printed p. 464: "Every simple representation (V, r) has a unique
  weight λ(r) (called the highest weight) such that μ < λ(r) for all other
  weights."
- Example 22.36, printed p. 473: "The group algebra of X is the free
  ℤ-module ℤ[X] with basis the set of symbols {e^χ | χ ∈ X} and with
  e^χ · e^χ′ = e^{χ+χ′} (cf. 12.6). The (formal) character of a
  representation (V, r) of T is ch(V) = Σ_{χ∈X} dim(V_χ) · e^χ." Printed
  p. 474: "We define the character ch_G(V) of a representation (V, r) of G
  to be its character as a representation of T."
- Opening of Chapter 21, printed p. 424: "The field k is arbitrary", and "G
  is split if some maximal torus T is split, in which case we refer to the
  pair (G, T) as a split reductive group." The Weyl group `W(G, T)` is
  `N_G(T)/T` (same page).
- Proof of Theorem 21.37, printed p. 436: "W(G, T) acts faithfully on T, and
  hence on X(T). When we identify W(G, T) with a subgroup of Aut(X(T)), the
  element s_α becomes identified with the reflection map x ↦ x − ⟨x, α∨⟩α."
  Theorem 21.37, printed p. 435: "The Weyl group W(G, T) of (G, T) is
  generated by the elements s_α, α ∈ Φ."

The provenance is derived for these reasons:

- Characteristic zero. The official errata for p. 480 (see below) says: "In
  Theorem 22.52, either assume k has characteristic zero or replace V with
  H^0(G/B′, L(χ))." The Lean statement takes the first option,
  `[CharZero k]`. (In positive characteristic the formula fails for simple
  representations in general; the corrected reprint states it for
  `H^0(G/B′, L(χ))` with `χ` dominant.)
- The half sum as a hypothesis, and a defect in the source. Milne assumes
  `Pic(G) = 0` and asserts that then `ρ ∈ X` (quoted above; the corrected
  reprint repeats the assertion). That assertion is false in general, so
  the Lean statement does not use the condition `Pic(G) = 0`.
  Counterexample: `G = GL₂` over `ℚ` with the diagonal torus `T = D₂` and
  the Borel subgroup of upper triangular matrices. By Example 21.6 (printed
  p. 426) and Example 21.36 (printed p. 435), `X = ℤχ₁ ⊕ ℤχ₂` and the only
  positive root is `χ₁ − χ₂`, so `ρ = ½(χ₁ − χ₂)` is not in `X`. Yet
  `Pic(GL₂) = 0`: the coordinate ring `ℚ[a, b, c, d, 1/(ad − bc)]` is a
  localization of a polynomial ring, hence a unique factorization domain,
  and A.79 (printed p. 584) says that `Pic(A) = 0` for such a ring `A`.
  The Lean statement instead assumes that some `rho : X` satisfies
  `2 • rho = Σ_{α ∈ Φ⁺} α`. Because `T` is split, `X ≅ ℤ^r` is torsion free,
  so such a `rho` is unique and equals Milne's `ρ`. The statement therefore
  covers exactly the split pinnings whose half sum of positive roots lies in
  `X` (for example `SL₂` with its diagonal torus, where `X = ℤ`, the
  positive root is `2` and `ρ = 1`). It says nothing about the others; in
  particular it excludes `GL₂` (for every split pinning of `GL₂` the
  positive root is `±(χ₁ − χ₂)`, and the hypothesis has no solution), even
  though `Pic(GL₂) = 0`. For the groups it covers, the formula is justified
  by the corrected reprint (see below): without the assumption
  `Pic(G) = 0`, `ρ ∈ ½X` and the formula still holds in `ℤ[½X]`. When
  `ρ ∈ X`, the elements `J(e^ρ)`, `J(e^{λ+ρ})` and `ch_G(V)` all lie in
  `ℤ[X]`, and the group ring map `ℤ[X] → ℤ[½X]` induced by the inclusion
  `X ⊆ ½X` is injective, so the formula holds in `ℤ[X]`. The formula for
  groups such as `GL₂`, in `ℤ[½X]` or with a shifted `ρ`, is a different
  statement and is not formalized here.
- Quotient written as a product. `ℤ[X]` is the ring of Laurent polynomials
  in `r` variables, an integral domain, and `J(e^ρ) ≠ 0`: its coefficient at
  `e^ρ` is the sum of `det(w)` over the `w ∈ W` with `w(ρ) = ρ`, and only
  `w = 1` fixes `ρ`, because `⟨ρ, α∨⟩ > 0` for every positive root `α`
  (for a reduced root system `⟨ρ, α∨⟩ = 1` for every simple root `α`) and
  `W` acts simply transitively on the Weyl chambers. So
  `ch_G(V) = J(e^{λ+ρ})/J(e^ρ)` is equivalent to
  `J(e^ρ) · ch_G(V) = J(e^{λ+ρ})`, which is the Lean equation.
- Weyl group. Milne's `W = W(G, T)` acts on `X` through its image in
  `Aut(X)`, which by Theorem 21.37 and its proof is the subgroup generated
  by the reflections `x ↦ x − ⟨x, α∨⟩α`, `α ∈ Φ`. By `p.isRootDatum`, the
  pairing of `p.rootDatum` is the pairing of characters and cocharacters
  and its roots are the roots of `(G, T)`, so these reflections are the maps
  `p.rootDatum.reflection i`, and the image is `reflectionGroup p.rootDatum`.
  The action is faithful, so summing over `W(G, T)` and summing over this
  subgroup give the same `J`. `det(w|X)` is `LinearMap.det` of `w` on the
  free module `X` of finite rank.
- Base instead of Borel subgroup. Milne fixes a Borel subgroup `B ⊇ T`; the
  Lean statement uses the base `p.base`, as in
  [`Rational.SplitHighestWeightClassificationStatement`](SplitHighestWeightClassificationStatement.md).
  By Theorem 21.32 (printed p. 434) the Borel subgroups containing `T`
  correspond bijectively to the Weyl chambers, and Definition 21.35 (printed
  p. 435) attaches to `B` its system of positive roots `Φ⁺(B)`. The base
  `p.base` determines a system of positive roots, the roots that are
  nonnegative combinations of the simple roots, and hence a Borel subgroup
  `B ⊇ T` with this `Φ⁺(B)`; these are the roots in `p.positiveRootMonoid`.
- Simple representations with highest weight. `p.IsHighestWeight r lam`
  implies that `r` is simple and that `lam` is its highest weight in the
  sense of 22.3 (`lam` is a weight because its weight space has dimension
  `1`, and every other weight `μ` has `μ < lam`). Conversely, a simple
  representation with highest weight `λ` satisfies
  `p.IsHighestWeight r λ`: its weight space at `λ` has dimension `1` by
  Theorem 22.2 (it is isomorphic to `V(λ)`), and it is the sum of its weight
  spaces by 12.12 (cited on p. 463). So the hypothesis describes exactly
  Milne's `V`.
- Coordinates. Finite-dimensional representations are matrix
  representations on `kⁿ`; every finite-dimensional representation is
  isomorphic to one of these, and isomorphisms preserve simplicity, highest
  weights and characters.
- Pinnings. The statement is made for the torus of a split pinning. Every
  split reductive `(G, T)` in Milne's sense carries one: for a connected
  reductive `G` and a split maximal torus `i : T.toGrp ⟶ G`,
  `SplitPinning.ofRootDatum` (in
  [`Reductive.PinningConstruction`](../Reductive/PinningConstruction.md)),
  applied to a root datum given by the theorem `rootDatumExistence` (in
  [`Reductive.RootDatumExistence`](../Reductive/RootDatumExistence.md)) and
  to the root lines given by the theorem `splitRootLieLine` (in
  [`Reductive.SplitRootLieLineProof`](../Reductive/SplitRootLieLineProof.md)),
  is a split pinning with torus `T` and inclusion `i`.
- `k`, `G`, the root index type and the test algebras in the stability and
  eigenspace conditions lie in one universe `u`. This is a formalization
  convention.

Relation to the Library. `SplitPinning.formalCharacter` (in
[`Rational.TorusFormalCharacter`](TorusFormalCharacter.md)) is the character
of a representation with coefficients in `k`; `formalCharacter_apply` in the
same module identifies its coefficient at a weight with the weight
multiplicity. The Library has no statement of the Weyl character formula.

## For the later proof (not part of this unit)

Milne's route needs the line bundles `L(λ)` on `G/B` and their cohomology
(Theorems 22.48, 22.50 and 22.51), which the Library does not have. A route
through characteristic-zero representation theory: by
`SplitHighestWeightClassificationStatement` the representation `r` is
determined by `lam`; the character is `W`-invariant (the proved theorem
`SplitPinning.finrank_representationWeightSpace_root_reflection` in
[`Rational.HighestWeightRootEndpoint`](HighestWeightRootEndpoint.md)), so
`J(e^ρ) · ch` is `W`-alternating and is an integral combination of the
`J(e^μ)` with `μ` strictly dominant and `μ ≤ λ + ρ`, with coefficient `1`
at `λ + ρ`. To show that no other `μ` occurs, the standard argument uses the
Casimir operator of the Lie algebra in characteristic zero (for instance
through Freudenthal's multiplicity formula). The witness `c` comes from the
finiteness of the set of weights.
