# Torus cocycles of anisotropic quadratic tori in simply connected groups

Module: `Langlands.GroupScheme.Rational.NumberFieldQuadraticAnisotropicToralKernel`.

The module states one proposition,
`NumberFieldQuadraticAnisotropicToralKernelStatement`, in an arbitrary universe `u`.
It contains no theorem; the statement is not proved here.

## The statement

The proposition quantifies over:

- a number field `F : Type u`;
- a group scheme `G` over `Spec F` with `IsAffineHom G.X.hom` and
  `LocallyOfFiniteType G.X.hom`, such that `IsSimplyConnected G` (so `G` is
  semisimple and simply connected);
- a field `L : Type u` with `[Algebra F L]`, `[FiniteDimensional F L]` and
  `[IsGalois F L]`, and `Module.finrank F L = 2`; write `σ` for the generator of
  `Gal(L/F)`;
- a commutative group scheme `T` over `Spec F` with a homomorphism
  `i : T.toGrp ⟶ G` such that
  - `IsMaximalTorus i`: `T` is a torus, `i` is a closed subgroup, and `i` does not
    factor through a strictly larger closed torus of `G`;
  - `IsSplitTorus` holds for the base change
    `(GroupScheme.baseChange (algebraBaseMap F L)).obj T.toGrp`, i.e. `T` splits
    over `L`;
  - `IsAnisotropic T.toGrp`: `T` contains no nontrivial split subtorus over `F`;
- a cocycle `η : Cocycle T.toGrp (GaloisCocycle.cover F L)` of `T` on the cover
  `Spec L → Spec F` that is trivial at every real place. For a real infinite place
  `w` of `F`, with `b = algebraBaseMap F (Global.placeCompletion F (.inl w))` the map
  from the spectrum of the completion `F_w`, the condition is that
  `η.baseChange b _ r` is trivial, where `r` is the counit of the pullback
  adjunction at the cover. This is triviality on the actual pullback cover
  `Spec (F_w ⊗_F L) → Spec F_w`; it is the real-place part of
  `Cocycle.LocallyTrivial` (module
  `Langlands.GroupScheme.Rational.NumberFieldHasseKernelReduction`).

The conclusion is `(η.map i).IsTrivial`: the image of `η` in `G` is the coboundary
of a point of `G` over `Spec L`.

In terms of Galois cohomology: every class in the kernel of
`H¹(L/F, T) → ∏_{w real} H¹(F_w, T)` maps to the trivial class of `H¹(L/F, G)`
(equivalently, of `H¹(F, G)`, since inflation `H¹(L/F, G) → H¹(F, G)` is injective).

Since `T` splits over `L` and is anisotropic over `F`, the group `Gal(L/F)` acts on
the character group `X(T)` without nonzero fixed vectors; as `σ² = 1`, the element
`χ + σ(χ)` is fixed and hence zero, so `σ` acts on `X(T)` as `−1`. At a real place
`w` where `L` splits, the pullback cover `Spec (F_w ⊗_F L) → Spec F_w` has a
section, and the real-place condition holds for every cocycle.

## Provenance

Derived. The statement is a special case of the Hasse principle for simply
connected groups. In V. Platonov and A. Rapinchuk, *Algebraic Groups and Number
Theory* (1994), Theorem 6.6 (printed p. 286) says that for simply connected `G` the
map `H¹(F, G) → ∏_{v archimedean} H¹(F_v, G)` is bijective. The image of `η` in
`H¹(F, G)` is trivial at every real place because `η` is, and at every complex place
because `H¹(ℂ, G) = 1`; hence it is trivial, and by injectivity of inflation in
degree one the image of `η` in `H¹(L/F, G)` is trivial, which is the Lean conclusion.

The statement is also the step of the proof of the Hasse principle for groups of
types `E_7` and `E_8` (§6.8, printed p. 386, paragraphs "Groups of type E₇" and
"Groups of type E₈") that follows Lemma 6.17 (printed p. 329) and Lemma 6.28
(printed p. 369). For such a group, a totally imaginary quadratic extension `L` over
which it splits, and a Borel `L`-subgroup `B` with `T = B ∩ σ(B)` a maximal
`F`-torus, the book shows that `σ` acts on `X(T)` as `−1` and replaces a locally
trivial cocycle by a cohomologous cocycle of `T` on `L/F` that is trivial at the
archimedean places. The hypotheses on `T` and `η` above record exactly this
situation. The `E_7` paragraph concludes with "a simply connected subgroup of `G`
of type `A_7` containing `T`": an `F`-subgroup `H` of `G` containing `T`, generated
by the root groups of a closed subsystem of type `A_7`. Over `L`, where `T` is a
split maximal torus of `H_L` and the `A_7` coroot lattice has index `2` in
`X_*(T)`, the base change `H_L` is isomorphic to `SL_{8,L}/μ_2`; hence `H` is not
simply connected, even geometrically (Library note
`notes/errata/platonov-rapinchuk-1994-e7-a7-subgroup.md`, which does the lattice
computation over an algebraically closed field). Over `F` itself `H` is not split:
`σ` acts as `−1` on the roots of `H`, so it induces the nontrivial symmetry of the
`A_7` diagram.

An argument that avoids that subgroup, and does not use the Hasse principle for
`G` itself:

1. Since `σ = −1` on `X(T)`, every root `α` of `(G_L, T_L)` satisfies
   `σ(α) = −α`, so the subgroup `G(α)` generated by the root subgroups of `α` and
   `−α` is defined over `F`. It is simply connected of type `A_1`, split by `L`,
   i.e. of the form `SL_1(Q)` for a quaternion algebra `Q` over `F` containing `L`.
2. Since `G` is simply connected, the simple coroots `α_1^∨, …, α_r^∨` of a base
   form a basis of the cocharacter group `X_*(T)` (as used in the proof of
   Lemma 6.18, printed p. 330). Each `T_i = α_i^∨(R¹_{L/F} G_m)`
   is an `F`-torus contained in `G(α_i)`, and multiplication
   `T_1 × ⋯ × T_r → T` is an isomorphism over `F`. Hence `η` is a product of
   commuting cocycles `η_i` of `T_i`, each trivial at the real places.
3. Each `η_i` is trivial in `H¹(F, G(α_i))` by the Hasse principle for `SL_1(Q)`
   (local vanishing at the finite places and the norm theorem for the reduced
   norm of `Q`).
4. The components are assembled by the componentwise induction of the proof of
   Proposition 6.25 (printed pp. 394–395): if `η_1 = g⁻¹ σ(g)` with
   `g ∈ G(α_1)(L)`, conjugation by `g` is defined over `F` on `T`, carries the
   remaining components to the analogous components for the maximal `F`-torus
   `g T g⁻¹`, and the class of `η` equals the class of the conjugate of
   `η_2 ⋯ η_r`. Equivalently, `η_k` is trivial in the inner twist of `G(α_k)` by
   the partial product `η_1 ⋯ η_{k−1}`, which normalizes `G(α_k)`, and
   `η_1 ⋯ η_r = h⁻¹ σ(h)` with `h = g_1 ⋯ g_r`.

The real-place hypothesis cannot be omitted. Let `ℍ` be Hamilton's quaternion
algebra over `ℚ`, `G = SL_1(ℍ)`, `L = ℚ(i) ⊂ ℍ` and `T = R¹_{L/ℚ} G_m ⊂ G`. Then
`H¹(L/ℚ, T) = ℚ^×/N_{L/ℚ}(L^×)`, `H¹(ℚ, G) = ℚ^×/Nrd(ℍ^×) = ℚ^×/ℚ_{>0}`, and the
class of `−1`, which is not trivial at the real place, maps to the nontrivial
element of `H¹(ℚ, G)`.
