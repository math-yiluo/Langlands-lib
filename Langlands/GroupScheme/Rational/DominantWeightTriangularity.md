# Dominant-weight triangularity in the group algebra of a root datum

Author: Worker 1804.

This module holds the combinatorial step in the proof that fundamental trace
characters freely generate class functions
(`Langlands.GroupScheme.SplitFundamentalCharacterGenerationStatement`). It does
not use group schemes. It works with an abstract Mathlib root pairing
`P : RootPairing ι ℤ X Y` with a base `b : P.Base`, and with the group algebra
`AddMonoidAlgebra k X` of the weight lattice over a commutative ring `k`.
Coefficients are read through the Mathlib API `f.coeff`. Everything is in
namespace `Langlands.RootDatum.Triangular`.

## Definitions

- `positiveCone P b : AddSubmonoid X` is the additive submonoid generated by
  the simple roots `P.root a`, `a ∈ b.support`. These are the nonnegative
  integral combinations of simple roots. It defines the usual dominance order:
  `μ ≤ ν` when `ν - μ ∈ positiveCone P b`.
- `IsDominant P b x` says that `P.toLinearMap x (P.coroot a) ≥ 0` for every
  simple index `a ∈ b.support`.
- `IsReflectionInvariant P f`, for `f : AddMonoidAlgebra k X`, says that
  `f.coeff (P.reflection i x) = f.coeff x` for every root index `i` and every
  weight `x`. The coefficients are invariant under all root reflections, and
  hence under the Weyl group.
- `IsTopWeight P b ν g` says that `g.coeff ν = 1`, and that every weight `μ`
  in the support of `g.coeff` satisfies `ν - μ ∈ positiveCone P b`. So `g` has
  leading coefficient one at `ν`, and every other weight of `g` lies below `ν`.
- `expWeight lam m = ∑ j, m j • lam j` for `lam : Fin r → X` and
  `m : Fin r → ℕ`.

## Main results

Throughout the main results:

- `c : Fin r → AddMonoidAlgebra k X`, `lam : Fin r → X` and `e : Fin r ≃ b.support`.
- `hlam1 : ∀ j, P.toLinearMap (lam j) (P.coroot (e j)) = 1`.
- `hlam0 : ∀ j a, a ≠ e j → P.toLinearMap (lam j) (P.coroot a) = 0`.

So `lam j` is the fundamental weight dual to the simple coroot indexed by
`e j`.

- `aeval_injective hlam1 hlam0 hc`: if `hc : ∀ j, IsTopWeight P b (lam j) (c j)`,
  then `MvPolynomial.aeval c : MvPolynomial (Fin r) k → AddMonoidAlgebra k X`
  is injective.
- `mem_range_aeval hlam1 hlam0 hc hinv hss f hf`: suppose in addition that
  - `hinv`: `MvPolynomial.aeval c q` is reflection invariant for every `q`;
  - `hss`: every `x : X` that pairs to zero with every simple coroot is zero.

  Then every reflection-invariant `f` lies in the range of
  `MvPolynomial.aeval c`. This theorem needs `[Fintype ι]`.
- `eq_zero_of_forall_simple_coroot hfin hx` gives `hss` when
  `X ⧸ P.rootSpan ℤ` is finite (`hfin`), with `[Fintype ι]`. For split
  semisimple groups the Library's `IsSemisimple.finite_quotient_rootSpan`
  supplies this finiteness.

## Proofs

**Pointedness and maxima.** `eq_zero_of_mem_positiveCone_of_neg_mem` shows
that the cone is pointed. If `x` and `-x` are both nonnegative combinations of
simple roots, adding the two expressions gives a vanishing combination with
natural coefficients. The simple roots are linearly independent
(`RootPairing.Base.linearIndepOn_root`), so every coefficient is zero. As a
result, the dominance order is a partial order. `exists_maximal_of_finset`
then gives a maximal element of any nonempty finite set, using
`Finset.exists_maximal` for the induced preorder.

**Maximal elements of stable sets are dominant.** Let `T` be stable under the
simple reflections, and let `ν` be maximal in `T`. If `⟨ν, α_a^∨⟩ < 0`, then
`s_a ν - ν = -⟨ν, α_a^∨⟩ α_a` is a positive multiple of a simple root. This
contradicts maximality, unless `⟨ν, α_a^∨⟩ = 0`. That is
`exists_isDominant_of_stable`.

**The canonical form.** The proofs use Mathlib's `P.RootForm` with
`[Fintype ι]`, the form `B(x, y) = ∑_i ⟨x, α_i^∨⟩⟨y, α_i^∨⟩`. From
`rootForm_self_smul_coroot` we get
`2 B(α_a, x) = B(α_a, α_a) ⟨x, α_a^∨⟩` (`two_mul_rootForm_root`), with
`B(α_a, α_a) > 0`. Hence `B(s, x) ≥ 0` whenever `s` is in the cone and `x` is
dominant. Suppose `μ ≤ ν` are both dominant. Then
`B(ν,ν) - B(μ,μ) = B(ν-μ, ν) + B(ν-μ, μ) ≥ 0`. If the two values are equal,
then `B(ν-μ, ν-μ) = 0`, so `ν = μ` by Mathlib's
`eq_zero_of_mem_rootSpan_of_rootForm_self_eq_zero` (`rootForm_self_le_of_isDominant`).

**Detecting weights by simple coroots.** `eq_zero_of_forall_simple_coroot`
first passes from simple coroots to all coroots: every coroot is in the
`ℤ`-span of the simple coroots (`RootPairing.Base.coroot_mem_span_int`).
Finiteness of `X ⧸ rootSpan` gives `n > 0` with `n • x` in the root span.
Every coroot pairs to zero with `n • x`, so `B(n • x, n • x)` is a sum of
zeros and `n • x = 0`. Then `n * ⟨x, y⟩ = 0` for every `y`, so `x` pairs to
zero with all of `Y`. The pairing is perfect, so `x = 0`.

**Top weights are multiplicative.** If `g` has top weight `ν` and `h` has top
weight `ν'`, then `g * h` has top weight `ν + ν'` (`IsTopWeight.mul`). The
coefficient of `ν + ν'` comes only from the pair `(ν, ν')`, because the cone
is pointed. The support of a product lies in sums of supports. Powers and
finite products follow (`IsTopWeight.pow`, `isTopWeight_prod`), so the
monomial `∏ j, c j ^ m j` has top weight `expWeight lam m`
(`isTopWeight_monomial`).

**Injectivity.** `pairing_expWeight` computes
`⟨expWeight lam m, α_a^∨⟩ = m (e.symm a)`, so `expWeight lam` is injective.
Take a nonzero `q` with `aeval c q = 0`. Choose a monomial `m0` of `q` whose
weight `expWeight lam m0` is maximal among the weights of the monomials of `q`.
`aeval c q` is the sum of `q.coeff m • ∏ j, c j ^ m j`. At the weight of `m0`,
only `m0` itself contributes. Any other `m` would have its weight dominate
that of `m0`, contradicting maximality and injectivity. That contribution is
`q.coeff m0 ≠ 0`, which is a contradiction.

**Surjectivity onto invariants.** The proof uses a double induction. The outer
induction is on a strict upper bound `n + 1` for `B(μ, μ)` over the weights
`μ` of `f`. The inner induction is on the number of dominant weights `ν` of
`f` with `B(ν, ν) = n`.

- If `f` has no such dominant weight, the set of weights of length `n` is
  stable under simple reflections, since `f` is invariant and `B` is
  reflection invariant. If it were nonempty, its maximal element would be
  dominant (`hdrop`), so all weights have `B < n` and the outer induction
  applies.
- Otherwise pick a dominant `ν` of `f` with `B(ν,ν) = n`. Since
  `⟨ν, α_a^∨⟩ ≥ 0`, put `m j = ⟨ν, α_{e j}^∨⟩`. Then `expWeight lam m = ν`,
  because their difference pairs to zero with every simple coroot and `hss`
  applies. The monomial `g = ∏ j, c j ^ m j` is reflection invariant (by
  `hinv`) and has top weight `ν`.
- Every weight `μ` of `g` has `B(μ,μ) ≤ B(ν,ν) = n`
  (`rootForm_le_of_isTopWeight`). The weights of `g` with the same length as
  `μ` form a set stable under simple reflections. Its dominant element lies
  below `ν`, so the comparison above applies. The difference `f - f.coeff ν • g` is
  invariant and keeps the bound. Its dominant weights of length `n` form a
  subset of those of `f` without `ν`: a dominant weight of `g` of length `n`
  equals `ν` by the equality case above, and the coefficient at `ν` cancels.
  The inner induction applies to the difference, and `f` is in the range.

## Provenance

This is an internal helper development for the task above. Bourbaki,
*Groupes et algèbres de Lie*, Chapter VI, §3.4, contains the classical
statement that Weyl-invariants of the group ring of the weight lattice are
freely generated by elements with fundamental leading terms. The termination
measure here uses the canonical form `RootForm` of Mathlib instead of the
classical induction on dominant weights. The argument was written for this
formalization, and no external text is claimed to match it line by line.
