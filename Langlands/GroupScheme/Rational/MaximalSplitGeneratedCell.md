# The generated cell of the split subgroup

This internal derived support module, in namespace
`Langlands.GroupScheme.MaximalSplitSubgroup`, is part of the proof of
`MaximalSplitSubgroupStatement` (Borel–Tits 1965, Theorem 7.2). It formalizes
Step 1 of the proof of Conrad–Gabber–Prasad, *Pseudo-reductive groups*,
Theorem C.2.29, part (i), in the split case where the group `C` is the torus.
There is one change. CGP assume rank-one subgroups `F_a` with an open cell. Here
the input is only the rank-one relation for a pair of opposite lines, so no
rank-one group is assumed.

Notation: `k` is a field, `Ga = VectorGroup.groupScheme k 1`, and `crd`, `pt`
are the coordinate and point maps of `Ga` from `RootCell`. `T` is a commutative
group scheme and `t : T ⟶ H` a homomorphism to a group scheme `H` over `Spec k`.
A *line of weight `a`* is a homomorphism `x : Ga ⟶ H` with
`VectorGroup.IsTorusEquivariant t x a`, that is, `t(s) x(c) t(s)⁻¹ = x(a(s) c)`
on every test.

## Lines (section `Lines`)

- `crd_scalarPoint`, `scalarPoint_eq_pt`, `pt_zero`: `scalarPoint` agrees with
  `pt`, and `pt 0 = 1`.
- `lineOrbitExtension x y`: for a `Z`-point `y` of `Ga`, the morphism
  `Z × 𝔸¹ ⟶ H`, `(z, s) ↦ x(s · y(z))`.
- `range_le_subgroup_of_isTorusEquivariant`: a line of weight `a` has range in
  the relative root subgroup functor `RelativeRoot.subgroup t a`, on every test.
  No smoothness or torus hypothesis is needed.

## Rank-one closure (section `Closure`, `k` algebraically closed)

- `rationalPoints H`: the rational points `Over.mk (𝟙 (Spec k)) ⟶ H.X`.
  `rangePts f`: the subgroup of rational points in the range of `f`.
- `rationalPointClosure_located_eq_univ`: let `P` be locally of finite type with
  irreducible underlying space, and `U` a nonempty open subset. Then every
  rational point of `P` lies in the closure of the rational points located in
  `U`.
- `locallyOfFiniteType_vectorGroup`: vector groups are locally of finite type.
- `planeProduct x y`: the morphism `(X, Y) ↦ x(X) y(Y)` from the plane
  `RootCell.Universal.plane k` to `H`. `comp_pt` and `comp_planeProduct`
  compute its composites with points.
- `comp_tripleCell_mem`: a rational point of `Ga × T × Ga` maps under the cell
  multiplication `Dynamic.tripleCellMultiplication y t x` into the product set
  `y(k) · t(T(k)) · x(k)`.
- `isUnit_of_mem_openU`: a rational point of the plane located in the open set
  `1 + XY ≠ 0` (`Universal.openU`) has `1 + XY` a unit.
- `comp_planeProduct_mem`: under `RootCell.RelationOver t ν x y` at the base
  test `Over.mk (𝟙 (Spec k))`, such a point maps into `y(k) · t(T(k)) · x(k)`.
- `pair_subset_rationalPointClosure`: under the same relation and
  `LocallyOfFiniteType H.X.hom`, the product set `x(k) · y(k)` lies in the
  closure `rationalPointClosure H (y(k) · t(T(k)) · x(k))`. This is the pair
  hypothesis `hpair` of `LeviGluing.dense_rationalPointImage_tripleProduct`.

## Density (section `Density`)

- `conj_mem_rangePts_of_isTorusEquivariant`: rational torus points normalize the
  rational points of a line.
- `le_normalizer_closure`: in a group, a subgroup `P` that conjugates a set `S`
  into itself normalizes the subgroup generated by `S`.
- `generatedPts x`: the subgroup generated by the rational points of a family
  of lines `x i`.
- `dense_generatedCell` (`k` algebraically closed, `H` locally of finite type):
  let `x i` be lines of weight `a i` and `y i` lines of weight `-a i`, for `i`
  in an arbitrary index type. Assume that for each `i` the relation
  `RelationOver t (ν i) (x i) (y i)` holds at the base test, and that rational
  points of `x i` and `y j` commute for `i ≠ j`. Assume also that the rational
  points of all `x i`, all `y i` and the torus generate a subgroup with dense
  locations. Then the locations of `V · t(T(k)) · U` are dense, where
  `U = generatedPts x` and `V = generatedPts y`.
- `range_le_centralizer_cocharacter`: the range of `t` lies in the dynamic
  centralizer of `λ ≫ t` for every cocharacter `λ` of `T`.
- `isRepresentedBy_dynamicFactors_of_generated` (`k` algebraically closed, `H`
  smooth and affine): under the hypotheses of `dense_generatedCell`, let `λ` be a
  cocharacter and `t` a closed subgroup. Let `n`, `u` be closed subgroups with
  ranges in `Dynamic.unipotent` of `λ⁻¹ ≫ t` and of `λ ≫ t`, containing the
  rational points of all `y i`, respectively all `x i`. Then `n`, `t`, `u`
  represent the opposite dynamic unipotent subgroup, the dynamic centralizer and
  the dynamic unipotent subgroup of `λ ≫ t`.

## The torus centralizer (section `Centralizer`)

- `torusCentralizer_le_centralizer_cocharacter`: the torus centralizer of `t`
  lies in the dynamic centralizer of `λ ≫ t`.
- `range_le_torusCentralizer`: the range of `t` lies in its torus centralizer.
- `groupHom_neg_comp`: precomposition with inversion of `Gm` turns `λ ≫ t` into
  `(-λ) ≫ t`.
- `torusCentralizer_eq_range_of_generated` (CGP C.2.29(i)): under the hypotheses
  of `dense_generatedCell`, with `H` smooth and affine over an algebraically
  closed field, `t` a closed subgroup, and a cocharacter `λ` with
  `0 < ⟨a i, λ⟩` for all `i`, the torus centralizer of `t` is the range of `t`.

## Proofs

`range_le_subgroup_of_isTorusEquivariant`: for `g = x(y)`, the extension
`lineOrbitExtension x y` composed with `a : T → 𝔸¹` is the conjugation orbit of
`g`. This is the equivariance identity on the test `Z × T`, after computing both
sides in the coordinate of `Ga` with `AffineLine.homEquiv_comp_unitInclusion`.
At `s = 0` its value is `x(0) = 1`.

`rationalPointClosure_located_eq_univ` follows the Jacobson argument of the
Library's `LeviGluing.rationalPointClosure_rationalTripleProduct_eq_univ`. Every
nonempty open set meets `U`, because the space is irreducible. The intersection
contains a closed point (`nonempty_inter_closedPoints`), which comes from a
rational point (`pointOfClosedPoint`).

`pair_subset_rationalPointClosure`: write the product `x(s) y(r)` as the image
under `planeProduct x y` of the rational point `(s, r)` of the plane. Points of
the plane located in `1 + XY ≠ 0` map into the rational cell:
`RootCell.inCell_of_relationOver` gives a point of `Ga × T × Ga`, which
`comp_tripleCell_mem` splits into its three factors. Those located points are
dense by `rationalPointClosure_located_eq_univ`, since the plane is integral.
`LeviGluing.postcomp_mem_rationalPointClosure` then transports the closure along
the morphism `planeProduct x y`.

`dense_generatedCell` applies `LeviGluing.dense_rationalPointImage_tripleProduct`
with `A i = x i (k)`, `B i = y i (k)`, `U = generatedPts x`,
`V = generatedPts y` and torus points `t(T(k))`. The normalizer hypotheses come
from `le_normalizer_closure` and `conj_mem_rangePts_of_isTorusEquivariant`. The
pair hypotheses come from `pair_subset_rationalPointClosure`. The generation
hypothesis is the supplied one, after the subgroup generated by
`⋃ A i ∪ V ∪ t(T(k))` is identified with the one generated by
`⋃ A i ∪ ⋃ B i ∪ t(T(k))`.

`isRepresentedBy_dynamicFactors_of_generated` applies
`LeviGluing.isRepresentedBy_dynamicFactors_of_dense_closed_subcell` with
`b = t`. Its density hypothesis follows from `dense_generatedCell`, because
`V ⊆ n(k)` and `U ⊆ u(k)`.

`torusCentralizer_eq_range_of_generated` takes for `n` and `u` the Library's
representatives `Dynamic.Affine.unipotentHom` of the two dynamic unipotent
subgroups. The lines lie in them by `range_le_subgroup_of_isTorusEquivariant`
and `RelativeRoot.subgroup_le_dynamicUnipotent`, using `⟨-a i, -λ⟩ = ⟨a i, λ⟩ > 0`
and `groupHom_neg_comp` for the `y i`. The previous theorem then shows that `t`
represents the dynamic centralizer of `λ ≫ t`. The torus centralizer lies in
that centralizer and contains the range of `t`.

## Provenance

The route is CGP Theorem C.2.29, Step 1 (PDF pp. 652–653): density of the cell
`V C U` by the word induction, then the dynamic factors. The word induction and
the closed-subcell argument are the Library's `LeviGluing` modules. Those modules
implement Milne, *Algebraic Groups* (2017), Proposition 23.22. The rank-one
closure condition, which CGP take from the open cell of `F_a`, is derived here
from the scheme-level rank-one relation. No statement is assumed.
