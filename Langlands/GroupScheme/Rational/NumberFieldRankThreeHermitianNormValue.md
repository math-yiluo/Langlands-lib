# A prescribed value of the rank-six quadratic norm form

Let `F` be a number field, with nonzero weights `a₀, a₁, a₂` and nonzero parameters `c, d`. The norm form has six diagonal weights `aᵢ` and `-c aᵢ`. If the scalar equation `HasNonzeroSkewNormZero` holds with the images of these parameters at every real completion, the norm form represents `-(a₀ a₁ a₂)d` over `F`.

At a real place where the diagonal weights have both signs, a suitable coordinate axis represents the target. If all the weights are positive, then `c < 0`. The nonzero skew norm zero forces `d < 0`, because a positive `d` would make every summand nonnegative and one strictly positive. Consequently the target is positive. With all the weights negative, changing their signs gives the same conclusion for `d`, and the target is negative. At a complex place the desired value is obtained by a square root on a coordinate axis. The nonzero coefficients make the form regular, and its dimension six allows the application of the number-field quadratic representation theorem with archimedean inputs.

This is the value calculation for equation (6) in Springer's appendix to Kneser, *Lectures on Galois Cohomology of Classical Groups* (1969), Theorem 5.3.3(i), printed p. 110 (PDF p. 116). The scalar hypothesis here is the first-coefficient consequence of local skew isotropy in the pure-line diagonal basis.
