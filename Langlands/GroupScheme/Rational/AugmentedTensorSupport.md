# Degree support for tensor products of augmentation kernels

Worker 1228. This internal algebraic consequence of the identity-slice
calculation implements the support argument in Conrad–Gabber–Prasad,
*Pseudo-reductive Groups*, second edition (2015), Lemma 3.3.4, printed
pp.108–109 (PDF pp.134–135), registered as
`conrad-gabber-prasad-2015-pseudo-reductive`. The grading hypotheses are
explicit. This module does not construct the grading associated with a torus
action or identify augmentation weights with Lie weights.

For a family of submodules indexed by degrees, `degreeSpan U P` is the
submodule generated by the spaces with degree in `P`. It is monotone in `P`.
For an independent family, `degree_mem_of_nonzero` proves that a nonzero
element in the space of degree `γ` can belong to `degreeSpan U P` only when
`γ` belongs to `P`. The proof uses disjointness of a homogeneous space from
the sum of all the other spaces.

`map₂_degreeSpan` distributes a bilinear image across the two selected
families. `tensor_kernels_le_sum_degreeSpan` applies it to the tensor map.
It assumes that the augmentation kernels are supported in `P` and `Q`, and
that a tensor of degrees `α` and `β` lies in the target space of degree
`α + β`. Its conclusion is the support bound for the range of the actual
tensor of the two augmentation-kernel inclusions. The support set is exactly
`Set.image2 (· + ·) P Q`: one element from each set, with no extra zero
degree inserted. The proof uses monotonicity of bilinear submodule images,
then the defining degree compatibility on each pair of homogeneous spaces.

`identity_slice_kernels_le_sum_degreeSpan` combines that support bound with
the common-kernel equality from `AugmentedTensor`.
`degree_mem_sum_of_injective_constant_slices` further applies it to a
nonzero augmentation element under an injective algebra pullback that is
constant at the augmentation on both identity slices. It also assumes an
independent family of target homogeneous spaces and membership of the
pullback element in the space of the indicated degree. Injectivity makes
the image nonzero, and independence then gives the asserted membership in
the literal sum of support sets.

In the geometric application, injectivity comes from the dominant map to
the image closure, the slice equations come from the commutator, and the
homogeneous spaces come from the torus action on coordinates. Those inputs
and the passage to a smooth connected subgroup remain separate obligations.
The full all-test semigroup commutator statement is not proved here.
