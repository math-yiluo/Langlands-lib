# Highest-weight representations of a split reductive group under extension of the base field

`Langlands.GroupScheme.SplitHighestWeightBaseChangeStatement.{u}` is a
Prop-valued definition. This module defines the proposition and proves
nothing.

## Statement

The proposition says: for every field `k : Type u`, every group scheme
`G : GroupScheme (Spec (CommRingCat.of k))` with `[IsAffineHom G.X.hom]` and
every split pinning `p : SplitPinning G`, the following four conjuncts hold.
`bK` abbreviates `algebraBaseMap k K`, the morphism `Spec K ⟶ Spec k` of a
field extension `K` of `k`, and `ρ.baseChange bK` is the representation of
`G_K` on `Kⁿ` obtained from `ρ` by extension of scalars.

1. Endomorphisms. For all `n : ℕ`, `ρ : MatrixRepresentation G (Fin n)` and
   `lam : characterLattice p.torus` with `p.IsHighestWeight ρ lam`, every
   matrix `C : Matrix (Fin n) (Fin n) k` with `ρ.Intertwines ρ C` equals
   `Matrix.scalar (Fin n) c` for some `c : k`.
2. Simplicity after extension. For every field `K : Type u` with
   `[Algebra k K]` and all `n`, `ρ`, `lam` with `p.IsHighestWeight ρ lam`,
   the representation `ρ.baseChange bK` satisfies `IsIrreducible`.
3. Highest weight after an algebraic extension. For every field `K : Type u`
   with `[Algebra k K]` and `[Algebra.IsAlgebraic k K]` and all `n`, `ρ`,
   `lam` with `p.IsHighestWeight ρ lam`,

   ```text
   (p.baseChange (F := K)).IsHighestWeight (ρ.baseChange bK)
     (characterLattice.baseChange bK p.torus lam)
   ```

   An instance `IsAffineHom` for the structure morphism of `G_K` is supplied
   by `haveI`, as the base change `pullback.snd G.X.hom bK` of an affine
   morphism; it is a proposition, so its proof term does not matter.
4. Descent of semisimple representations. For every field `K : Type u` with
   `[Algebra k K]`, every `m : ℕ` and every
   `ρ' : MatrixRepresentation ((GroupScheme.baseChange bK).obj G) (Fin m)`
   with `IsSemisimpleRep ρ'`, there is
   `ρ : MatrixRepresentation G (Fin m)` with `IsSemisimpleRep ρ` and a matrix
   `C : Matrix (Fin m) (Fin m) K` with
   `(ρ.baseChange bK).Intertwines ρ' C` and
   `Function.Bijective C.mulVecLin`.

Here `IsSemisimpleRep σ`, a local `let` in the statement whose field `F`,
group `H` and dimension `m` are implicit (so that it applies over `k` and
over `K`), says

```text
sSup {W : Submodule F (Fin m → F) | σ.IsStableSubspace W ∧ W ≠ ⊥ ∧
  ∀ U : Submodule F (Fin m → F), σ.IsStableSubspace U → U ≤ W → U = ⊥ ∨ U = W} = ⊤
```

that is, `Fᵐ` is the sum of its simple subrepresentations.

In words: let `(G, T)` be a split reductive group over `k` with a chosen base
of its roots. The only endomorphisms of a simple representation `V(λ)` of
highest weight `λ` are the scalars; `V(λ)` stays simple after any extension
of the base field; after an algebraic extension `K` it has highest weight `λ`
for `(G_K, T_K)`; and every semisimple representation of `G_K` is the
extension of scalars of a semisimple representation of `G`.

Every ingredient is an existing declaration, used without change:

- `SplitPinning G` (from [`Reductive.Pinning`](../Reductive/Pinning.md))
  bundles connected reductivity of `G`, a split maximal torus `p.torus` with
  its inclusion `p.inclusion`, a reduced root datum `p.rootDatum` on
  `characterLattice p.torus` that is the root datum of `(G, p.torus)`, a base
  `p.base` and trivializations of the simple-root Lie lines.
- `p.IsHighestWeight ρ lam` (from
  [`Rational.FundamentalRepresentations`](FundamentalRepresentations.md)) is
  the conjunction of: `ρ.IsIrreducible`; the weight spaces of `ρ` for the
  torus `p.torus` span `kⁿ`; the weight space at `lam` has dimension one;
  and `lam - μ ∈ p.positiveRootMonoid` for every `μ` with nonzero weight
  space. The monoid is generated by the simple roots of `p.base`.
- `MatrixRepresentation G (Fin n)` (from
  [`MatrixRepresentation`](../MatrixRepresentation.md)) is a homomorphism of
  group schemes `G ⟶ GL_n` over `k`. `ρ.IsIrreducible` (from
  [`MatrixRepresentation.DenseRationalStability`](../MatrixRepresentation/DenseRationalStability.md))
  says that `kⁿ ≠ 0` and that the only subspaces stable in the sense of
  `IsStableSubspace` (from
  [`MatrixRepresentation.StableSubrepresentation`](../MatrixRepresentation/StableSubrepresentation.md))
  are `0` and `kⁿ`. Stable subspaces are the subrepresentations: the
  condition is checked on the points of `G` with values in every commutative
  `k`-algebra.
- `ρ.Intertwines σ C` (from
  [`Lie.RepresentationSubquotient`](../Lie/RepresentationSubquotient.md))
  says that `C · ρ(x) = σ(x) · C` for every commutative `k`-algebra `S` and
  every point `x` of `G` with values in `S`. So `C` is a homomorphism of
  representations, and with `Function.Bijective C.mulVecLin` an
  isomorphism.
- `algebraBaseMap k K` (from
  [`Torus.LatticeGalois`](../Torus/LatticeGalois.md)) is
  `Spec.map (CommRingCat.ofHom (algebraMap k K))`.
  `MatrixRepresentation.baseChange` (from
  [`Rational.ValuedRepresentationBaseChange`](ValuedRepresentationBaseChange.md))
  is the base change of the homomorphism `G ⟶ GL_n` followed by the canonical
  isomorphism of the base change of `GL_n` with `GL_n` over `K`; its points
  are given by `pointMatrix_baseChange` in the same module.
- `SplitPinning.baseChange` (from
  [`Reductive.PinningBaseChange`](../Reductive/PinningBaseChange.md)) is the
  base change of `p` along an algebraic extension: its torus is the base
  change of `p.torus`, its inclusion the base change of `p.inclusion`, its
  root datum and base are those of `p` transported along the canonical
  bijection `characterLattice.baseChangeEquiv` (from
  [`Torus.SplitLatticeBaseChange`](../Torus/SplitLatticeBaseChange.md)). The
  map `characterLattice.baseChange` (from [`Torus.Lattice`](../Torus/Lattice.md))
  sends a character `χ` of `p.torus` to its base change, composed with the
  canonical isomorphism of the base change of `𝔾_m` with `𝔾_m`; it is the
  underlying map of that bijection.

## Provenance (derived)

Source: J. S. Milne, *Algebraic Groups* (2017), registered as
`milne-2017-algebraic-groups` (SHA-256
`899b0f44f3c3ccfbaf5ed1cf9c180be7a8016b1c0e9709ca0c206304e5bd1cd0`). Printed page
numbers are given; the PDF page is the printed page plus 16.

The statement is 22.4 and 22.5, printed p. 464:

- 22.4: "The only endomorphisms of V(λ) are the scalar multiplications by
  elements of k, i.e., End(V(λ)) ≃ k (Exercise 22-1). Therefore, V(λ) remains
  simple under extension of the base field (4.19). It follows that if (V, r)
  is a simple representation of G of highest weight λ over k, then
  (V, r) ⊗ k′ is a simple representation of G_{k′} of highest weight λ over
  k′."
- 22.5: "Let G be a split reductive group over k, and let k′ be an extension
  of k. For every semisimple representation (V′, r′) of G_{k′} over k′,
  there exists a semisimple representation (V, r) of G over k and an
  isomorphism (V, r) ⊗ k′ → (V′, r′). This follows from 22.4."

Conjunct 1 is the first sentence of 22.4, conjunct 2 its second sentence,
conjunct 3 its third sentence for algebraic extensions, and conjunct 4 is
22.5.

The setting is that of the opening of Chapter 22, printed p. 463: "Let
(G, T) be a split reductive group over k", with "a Borel subgroup B of G
containing T", its positive roots Φ⁺ and simple roots Δ, and "Throughout,
representations are finite-dimensional unless it is specified otherwise."
`V(λ)` is the simple representation of Theorem 22.2 (printed pp. 463–464):
"For each dominant λ ∈ X, there exists a simple representation V(λ) of G,
unique up to isomorphism, that decomposes as a representation of T into
V(λ) = V(λ)_λ ⊕ ⊕_{μ} V(λ)_μ with V(λ)_λ of dimension 1 and all μ < λ."
Further conventions:

- Chapter 21, printed p. 424: "The field k is arbitrary". Reductive groups
  are connected (printed p. 135). Notation, printed p. 4: "After p. 162, all
  algebraic groups are affine". The Lean statement has an affine group
  scheme `G` whose connected reductivity is part of `p`.
- Definition 4.15, printed p. 90: "A representation of an algebraic group is
  simple if it is ≠ 0 and its only subrepresentations are 0 and itself. It
  is semisimple if it is a sum of simple subrepresentations."
- Proposition 4.19(b), printed p. 91, for a finite-dimensional
  representation (V, r) of G and an extension k′ of k: "If (V, r) is simple
  and End(V, r) = k, then (V′, r′) is simple", where (V′, r′) = (V, r) ⊗ k′.
- Exercise 22-1, printed p. 482: "Let λ be a dominant character of a split
  reductive group (G, T) relative to a Borel subgroup B, and let
  E(λ) = Ind_B^G(𝔾_a(λ)). Show that End(E(λ)) ≃ k. Deduce that End(V(λ)) ≃ k
  if V(λ) is a simple representation with highest weight λ."

The provenance is derived for these reasons:

- Highest-weight representations. `V(λ)` is any simple representation with
  the decomposition of Theorem 22.2. `p.IsHighestWeight ρ lam` is that
  condition for `ρ`, with the order `μ ≤ λ` given by membership of `λ − μ`
  in `p.positiveRootMonoid` and the base `p.base` in place of the Borel
  subgroup `B` (by Theorem 21.32, printed p. 434, the Borel subgroups
  containing `T` correspond to the Weyl chambers, hence to the bases). The
  dominance of `lam` is not assumed; it follows from `IsHighestWeight` by
  Theorem 22.18 (printed p. 467). In 22.4, "a simple representation of G of
  highest weight λ" is again such a representation.
- Endomorphisms. `End(V(λ)) ≃ k` says that every endomorphism of the
  representation is multiplication by a scalar. In coordinates an
  endomorphism is a matrix intertwining `ρ` with itself, so conjunct 1 says
  that every such matrix is scalar.
- Extensions. Milne's k′ is an arbitrary extension field of k (printed p. 3).
  Conjuncts 2 and 4 hold for every field `K` with a `k`-algebra structure.
  Conjunct 3 is restricted to algebraic extensions: the Library's base change
  `SplitPinning.baseChange` of a split pinning is defined only under
  `[Algebra.IsAlgebraic k K]`. For a transcendental extension the last
  sentence of 22.4 is stated only in part: simplicity is conjunct 2, but the
  assertion about the highest weight is not stated. In conjunct 3 the weight
  `λ` over `k′` is the base change `characterLattice.baseChange bK p.torus lam`
  of `lam`. Milne's phrase "of highest weight λ over k′" implicitly
  identifies `X(T_{k′})` with `X(T)`; base change of characters is this
  identification, and it is a bijection because `p.torus` is split
  (`characterLattice.baseChangeEquiv`).
- Coordinates. Finite-dimensional representations are matrix representations
  on `kⁿ` and on `Kᵐ`; every finite-dimensional representation is isomorphic
  to one of these, and simplicity, semisimplicity, weight spaces,
  endomorphism algebras and isomorphism classes are preserved by
  isomorphisms. `(V, r) ⊗ k′` is `ρ.baseChange bK`. In conjunct 4 the
  representation `ρ` has the same dimension `m` as `ρ'`, which an isomorphism
  `(V, r) ⊗ k′ ≅ (V′, r′)` forces. Semisimplicity is Definition 4.15: the sum
  (`sSup`) of the simple stable subspaces is the whole space.
- Pinnings. The statement is quantified over split pinnings, which carry data
  that Milne does not fix: a reduced root datum identified with the roots of
  `(G, T)` and nonzero vectors in the simple-root Lie lines. The vectors are
  not used. In conjunct 4 the pinning only expresses that `G` is split
  reductive.
- `k`, `K`, `G`, `p.rootIndex` and the test algebras in `IsStableSubspace`
  and `Intertwines` lie in one universe `u`. This is a formalization
  convention.

Relation to the Library. The Library's
`SemisimpleRepresentationCharZeroStatement` (in
[`Reductive.SemisimpleRepresentationCharZeroStatement`](../Reductive/SemisimpleRepresentationCharZeroStatement.md))
uses the same formula for semisimplicity, over a fixed field. The
highest-weight predicate is the one of
`SplitDominantHighestWeightRepresentationsExistStatement` (in
[`Rational.DominantHighestWeight`](DominantHighestWeight.md)).

## For the later proof (not part of this unit)

A possible route:

- Conjunct 1 (a direct argument, not the route through `E(λ)` suggested by
  Exercise 22-1). An intertwining matrix `C` commutes with the action of
  `p.torus`, so it maps each weight space into itself. It acts on the
  one-dimensional weight space at `lam` by a scalar `c`. Then `C − c` is an
  intertwining matrix whose kernel is a stable subspace containing the
  nonzero weight space at `lam`; by simplicity the kernel is `kⁿ`, so
  `C = c`.
- Conjunct 2. Proposition 4.19(b) with conjunct 1.
- Conjunct 3. Weight spaces commute with extension of scalars (Theorem
  12.12, printed pp. 234–235, for the split torus): the weight space of
  `ρ.baseChange bK` at the base change of `μ` is `K ⊗ V_μ`, and every
  character of the base-changed torus is a base change. The base-changed
  roots generate the base-changed `positiveRootMonoid`. With conjunct 2 this
  gives `IsHighestWeight`.
- Conjunct 4. Decompose `ρ'` into simple representations. `(G_K, T_K)` is a
  split reductive group with the same root datum, so by Theorem 22.2 each
  simple summand has a dominant highest weight, which is the base change of
  a dominant `λ`; by 22.4 the extension of scalars of `V(λ)` is simple with
  the same highest weight, hence isomorphic to the summand (Theorem 22.19).
  The direct sum of the `V(λ)` is a semisimple representation of `G`. For a
  transcendental `K` this needs the split reductive structure of `G_K`, which
  the Library's `SplitPinning.baseChange` does not provide.

In the Lean setting, direct sums and subrepresentations must be given
coordinates `kʳ`.
