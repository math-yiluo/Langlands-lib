# Commutative algebra for the finiteness of rational character lattices

This helper module contains the purely algebraic part of the proof of
`Langlands.GroupScheme.RationalCharacterLatticeStatement`. Nothing in it
mentions group schemes. Its results are internal lemmas that support
`CharacterLatticeProof`; they are standard commutative algebra and are not
taken from a particular source.

## Group-like elements

`linearIndepOn_of_comul`: let `O` be a vector space over a field `k` and
`Δ : O → O ⊗ O` a `k`-linear map. Every set `S` of nonzero vectors `a` with
`Δ a = a ⊗ a` is `k`-linearly independent. The proof is an adapted copy of
Mathlib's `linearIndepOn_isGroupLikeElem`, which uses only these two
properties of group-like elements. It goes by induction on finite subsets. If
`a = ∑ c_x x`, apply `Δ` and compare with `a ⊗ a = ∑ c_x c_y x ⊗ y`. The
tensors `x ⊗ y` are independent, so at most one `c_x` is nonzero, and then
`a = x`.

## Multiplicative maps to a field

In the section `Lattice`, `k` is a field, `L` is a field with a `k`-algebra
structure, `M` is an additive abelian group, and `w : Multiplicative M →* L`
is a monoid homomorphism. `wa w m` stands for `w (ofAdd m)`, and
`wa_add`, `wa_zero`, `wa_nsmul` and `wa_sum` record that it turns sums into
products. In most results the values `wa w m` are assumed `k`-linearly
independent (`hw`).

- `isAddTorsionFree`: under `hw`, `M` has no torsion. If `m ≠ 0` had finite
  order `d`, then `w(m) ≠ 1` (by injectivity) and the geometric sum
  `∑_{j<d} w(m)^j` would vanish. That sum is a nontrivial linear relation
  among the distinct values `w(j • m)`.
- `mem_span_of_mem_adjoin`: for a subgroup `P`, the `k`-subalgebra generated
  by `w(P)` lies in the `k`-span of `w(P)`. The span already contains `1` and
  is closed under multiplication.
- `linearIndependent_out_adjoin`, `linearIndependent_out_field`: for a
  subgroup `P`, the values of `w` at the chosen representatives
  `Quotient.out c` of the cosets `c ∈ M ⧸ P` are linearly independent over
  the subalgebra generated by `w(P)`, and hence over its fraction field, the
  intermediate field `k(w(P))`. Write the coefficients as `k`-combinations of
  values on `P`. Shifting the supports by the representatives gives a
  `k`-relation among values of `w`. That relation must be trivial, and the
  shifted supports lie in distinct cosets.
- `finite_quotient`: assume every element of `M` has a positive multiple in
  `P`, and all values of `w` lie in the `k`-subalgebra generated by a finite
  set `g`. Then `M ⧸ P` is finite. Let `K = k(w(P))` and let `B` be the
  `K`-subalgebra generated by `g`. Every value `w(m)` is algebraic over `K`,
  since some power of it lies in `K`. The representatives give
  `K`-independent elements of `B`. Pick a maximal ideal `𝔪` of `B`. The
  `K`-span of the representatives consists of elements algebraic over `K`.
  So each of its nonzero elements has an inverse in `B`, and the span meets
  `𝔪` only in `0`. The representatives therefore stay independent in the
  field `B ⧸ 𝔪`. That field is finite over `K` by Zariski's lemma (Mathlib's
  `finite_of_finite_type_of_isJacobsonRing`).
- `finite_of_linearIndepOn`: under the same finite generation hypothesis,
  every `ℤ`-linearly independent subset `S` of `M` is finite. The monomial
  with exponents `e ∈ ℕ^(S)` maps to `w(∑ e_s s)`, and these group elements
  are distinct. So `hw` makes the values `w(s)`, `s ∈ S`, algebraically
  independent over `k`. Hence `#S` is at most the transcendence degree of
  the finitely generated algebra, which is finite.
- `free_and_finite`: under `hw` and the finite generation hypothesis
  (`hg : ∀ m, wa w m ∈ Algebra.adjoin k g` for a finite set `g ⊆ L`), `M` is
  a free `ℤ`-module of finite rank. Choose a maximal `ℤ`-linearly
  independent subset `S`; it is finite. The quotient by its span `P` is
  torsion, so it is finite by `finite_quotient`. Then `S` together with the
  coset representatives generates `M`. A finitely generated torsion-free
  abelian group is free.
