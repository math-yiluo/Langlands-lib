# Split closed subgroups of commutative groups containing images of split groups

This helper module serves the proof of
`Langlands.GroupScheme.RelativeRoot.SplitUnipotentStableVectorSubgroupStatement`
(task 6b07dcd2-af39-4c69-8ade-d0faa997a49f). Let G be a smooth affine
commutative group over a field k. A morphism ω : Y ⊗ S ⟶ G, with S split
unipotent, that is a homomorphism on every rational fibre {y} × S factors
through a smooth connected split unipotent closed subgroup of G, provided
rational points detect functions on Y ⊗ S. In the proof of the target this
shows that certain commutative subgroups generated by images of split groups
are split.

This is a standard kind of argument: images of split groups generate a split
subgroup. The route was worked out for this formalization. It chooses a split
closed subgroup with minimal coordinate-kernel height and shows that it already
contains every homomorphic image of S. It uses the Library's
`BorelExistence.coordinateKernel` and the height lemmas of
`Langlands.GroupScheme.Absolute.BorelExistenceCoordinates`.

Everything is in the namespace `Langlands.GroupScheme.RationalDensity`. Fix a
field k and write S₀ = Spec k. All groups are group objects of `Over S₀`.

## Split closed subgroups

- `IsSplitClosed κ`, for a homomorphism κ : I ⟶ G, is the conjunction of four
  conditions. κ is a closed subgroup (`IsClosedSubgroup κ`), I is smooth over
  S₀, the underlying space of I is connected, and I is split unipotent
  (`IsSplitUnipotent I`).
- `trivial_isSplitClosed`: the inclusion of the trivial group is split closed.
  This is the Library's `SplitUnipotentRadicalExistence.trivial_isSplitCandidate`.

## Enlargement

`IsSplitClosed.exists_enlarge`. Assume G is smooth and affine over S₀ and
commutative: a * b = b * a for all points a, b of G with values in any object
T. Let κ : I ⟶ G be split closed, let S be smooth, connected, affine over S₀
and split unipotent, and let φ : S ⟶ G be a homomorphism. Then there are a
split closed inc : D ⟶ G and homomorphisms j₀ : I ⟶ D and i₀ : S ⟶ D with
j₀ ≫ inc = κ and i₀ ≫ inc = φ.

The proof follows the first half of the inductive step of the Library's
`SplitSemidirect.isSplitUnipotent_of_surjective`
(`Langlands.GroupScheme.Rational.SplitSemidirectSeries`).

1. The range of κ is normal in G because G is commutative. So S acts on I by
   conjugation through φ (`Semidirect.conjStable_of_isNormal`), and the
   multiplication I ⋊ S ⟶ G, (a, s) ↦ κ(a) φ(s), is a homomorphism
   (`Semidirect.mulHom`). Here `Semidirect` and `SplitSemidirect` are the
   namespaces `Langlands.GroupScheme.Semidirect` and
   `Langlands.GroupScheme.RelativeRoot.SplitSemidirect`.
2. The semidirect product has underlying object I ⊗ S. It is smooth and affine
   over S₀ because I and S are. I is affine over S₀ because κ is a closed
   immersion into the affine G. I is connected and of finite type, hence
   geometrically connected (the Library's `geometricallyConnected_of_connected`),
   so the product with the connected S is connected.
3. The Library's image theorem `SplitNormalFppfProduct.homImageHypothesis`
   factors the multiplication as a surjection fac : I ⋊ S ⟶ D followed by a
   closed subgroup inc : D ⟶ G, with D smooth, connected and affine.
4. j₀ and i₀ are the two factors of I ⋊ S followed by fac. They are
   homomorphisms because their composites with the monomorphism inc are the
   homomorphisms κ and φ (`SplitSemidirect.isMonHom_of_comp_eq`). j₀ is a
   closed immersion since j₀ ≫ inc = κ is. Its range is normal in D, by
   commutativity of G after composing with inc.
5. The product map I ⊗ S ⟶ D, (a, s) ↦ j₀(a) i₀(s), equals fac, so it is
   surjective. Now `SplitSemidirect.isSplitUnipotent_of_surjective` shows that
   D is split unipotent, since I is split unipotent and S has a split series.

## Factorization

`exists_splitClosed_factor`. Let G be smooth, affine and commutative as above,
and let S be smooth, connected, affine over S₀ and split unipotent. Let
ω : Y ⊗ S ⟶ G be a morphism over S₀. Assume rational points detect functions on
Y ⊗ S, and that for every rational point y of Y the restriction
`pointSection S y ≫ ω` (the map s ↦ ω(y, s)) is a homomorphism S ⟶ G. Then
there are a split closed κ : I ⟶ G and g : Y ⊗ S ⟶ I with g ≫ κ = ω.

1. Among all split closed subgroups κ : I ⟶ G (the trivial one is one), choose
   one for which the height of `coordinateKernel κ` is minimal. This kernel is
   the kernel of Γ(G) ⟶ Γ(I). It is an ideal of finite height in the
   noetherian ring Γ(G) (`coordinateKernel_height_ne_top`), so the minimum is
   taken over natural numbers.
2. Every homomorphism φ : S ⟶ G factors through κ. Enlarge κ and φ to a split
   closed inc : D ⟶ G with j₀ ≫ inc = κ. The height of the kernel of κ is at
   most that of inc by minimality, so j₀ is an isomorphism by the Library's
   `isIso_of_coordinateKernel_height_le`. Then φ = (i₀ ≫ j₀⁻¹) ≫ κ.
3. Let x be a rational point of Y ⊗ S, with components y in Y and s in S. Then
   x = s ≫ pointSection S y, so x ≫ ω is the rational point s of S followed by
   the homomorphism of step 2. That homomorphism factors through κ, so x ≫ ω
   lifts to a rational point of I.
4. G is affine and κ is a closed immersion. Rational points detect functions on
   Y ⊗ S, so `RationalPointsDetectFunctions.exists_factor` (module
   `Langlands.GroupScheme.Rational.SplitUnipotentRationalDetection`) gives
   g : Y ⊗ S ⟶ I with g ≫ κ = ω.
