# Root subgroups generate an isotropic almost-simple group

Authors: Worker 180.

`Langlands.GroupScheme.IsotropicRootSubgroupGenerationStatement` is an
unproved proposition at arbitrary universe `u`. This module states it and
proves nothing.

## The proposition

For every field `k : Type u`, every group scheme `G` over
`Spec (CommRingCat.of k)` with `[IsAffineHom G.X.hom]`,
`IsAlmostSimple G` and `¬ IsAnisotropic G`, every commutative group scheme
`S` with `i : S.toGrp ⟶ G` and `IsMaximalSplitTorus i`, and every closed
subgroup `h : H ⟶ G` (`IsClosedSubgroup h`) such that

- `SubgroupFunctor.range i ≤ SubgroupFunctor.range h`, and
- `RelativeRoot.subgroup i a ≤ SubgroupFunctor.range h` for every
  `a ∈ relativeRoots i`,

the range of `h` is everything: `SubgroupFunctor.range h = ⊤`, on every
test scheme over `Spec k`.

## Exact objects

- `IsAlmostSimple G` (module `Reductive.AlmostSimple`): `G` is semisimple
  and noncommutative, and every smooth connected closed normal subgroup
  has trivial or full range. This is simplicity over `k`, the notion
  "presque simple sur `k`" of Borel–Tits, not absolute almost simplicity.
- `IsAnisotropic G` (module `Rational.SplitTorus`): the split rank of `G`
  is zero. Its negation says that `G` contains a nontrivial closed split
  torus.
- `IsMaximalSplitTorus i`: `S` is a split torus, `i` is a closed subgroup,
  and every factorization of `i` through a split subtorus is an
  isomorphism.
- `relativeRoots i` and `RelativeRoot.subgroup i a`: as in
  `Langlands.GroupScheme.SimpleRootSubgroupGenerationStatement`; the root
  subgroup functor of `a` consists of the points whose torus-conjugation
  orbit factors through `a` and a morphism from the affine line taking `0`
  to `1`. The root set may be nonreduced.
- `SubgroupFunctor.range h` is the functor of points of `G` that factor
  through `h`, on every test scheme. `IsClosedSubgroup h` says that the
  underlying morphism of schemes of `h` is a closed immersion.

## Source and derivation

A. Borel and J. Tits, *Groupes réductifs*, Publ. Math. IHÉS 27 (1965),
registered as `borel-tits-1965-groupes-reductifs` (printed page = PDF
page + 53):

- 4.11, printed p.89 / PDF p.36: for opposite parabolic subgroups `P`,
  `P′` of a connected reductive group, the subgroup generated by their
  unipotent radicals `R_u(P)` and `R_u(P′)` is closed, connected and
  normal in `G`;
- 3.11, printed pp.77–78 / PDF pp.24–25: for a quasi-closed unipotent set
  `ψ` of relative roots written as a disjoint union of quasi-closed
  unipotent parts, the product map from these parts onto `U_ψ` is an
  isomorphism of varieties; applied to the positive `k`-roots split into
  the rays of the indivisible ones (5.2), the unipotent radical of a
  minimal `k`-parabolic subgroup is the product of the root groups
  `U_(a)`, `a > 0` indivisible;
- 5.11, printed p.99 / PDF p.46: if `G` is almost simple over `k`, its
  relative root system is irreducible; a connected normal `k`-subgroup is
  determined by an ideal of the root system together with a split torus
  and an anisotropic part.

Derivation. Choose a generic cocharacter `ν` of `S`; the dynamic
subgroups `P(ν)` and `P(-ν)` are opposite minimal `k`-parabolic subgroups
with unipotent radicals `U(ν)` and `U(-ν)`, each generated by the root
groups of the positive, respectively negative, relative roots. By
hypothesis `H` contains both, so `H` contains the closed subgroup `N`
generated by them. By 4.11, `N` is a smooth connected normal subgroup of
`G`. Because `G` is not anisotropic and is semisimple, the maximal split
torus `S` is not central, so there is a relative root and `N` is
nontrivial. Almost simplicity over `k` gives `N = G`, hence `H = G`.

The hypothesis that `H` contains the torus `S` is not needed for this
argument, but it is harmless for the intended use and allows the closed
subgroup generated by `U(ν)`, the image of `ν` and `U(-ν)` (the Library's
`Dynamic.OppositeEnvelope`) to be used directly. The proposition is a
derived formulation of the cited results in the Library's actual objects;
it makes no splitness, perfectness or characteristic assumption on `k`
and does not assume `G` simply connected.
