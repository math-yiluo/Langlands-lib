# Canonical reflection points for arbitrary parametrized roots

Provenance: internal. This generalizes the published canonical representative
construction from normalized simple-root maps to `RootParametrization α` for
any indexed root of a supplied split pinning. It uses the published
`splitPinnedOppositeFactorPairs`, uniqueness of actual normalizing opposite
pairs, and `SplitPinning.oppositeFactorPair_weylAction`, which already applies
to every indexed root. It makes no assertion that a compatible parametrization
exists.

For a root map `x`, `RootParametrization.unitPoint` is its actual rational point
at coordinate one. This point belongs to the intrinsic root subgroup and is
different from the identity, since the root map is a monomorphism.
`oppositeFactorPair` chooses the unique ordered pair in the opposite root
subgroup whose triple around that point normalizes the torus.
`reflectionRepresentative` is this triple with its actual normalizing torus
automorphism. The published reflection theorem identifies its action on the
entire pinned character lattice with the reflection of `α`. Its membership in
the subgroup generated by the rational positive and negative root subgroups
follows from membership of the three factors.

For a normalized simple-root map, the new point, opposite pair and reflection
point agree definitionally with the corresponding published constructions.
The three `toRootParametrization` comparison lemmas record these agreements.
The earlier constructions remain available in their existing modules.

The Galois transport lemmas use a supplied identification of the pinned torus
with the base change of a ground-field torus. Their hypotheses are the actual
ordinary transport of the indexed root and equality of the conjugated
coordinate-one points. Changing torus identifies the opposite-pair predicates;
the published coefficient conjugation theorem transports the pair; uniqueness
identifies it with the pair for the image root. Multiplicativity of rational
conjugation then transports the reflection triple. No independent choice or
Hilbert 90 argument for reflection representatives is needed once compatible
coordinate-one points are supplied.

All fields are arbitrary and the field types share one arbitrary universe.
No finiteness or Galoisness assumption on the extension, characteristic
restriction, simple-connectedness assumption or Lie normalization is used.
For the consumer's merged-root construction, existence of compatible root maps
and the adjacent-simple-root commutator and absorption laws remain obligations.
