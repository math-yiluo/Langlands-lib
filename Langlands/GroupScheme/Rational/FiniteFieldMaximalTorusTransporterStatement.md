# Conjugates of a maximal torus over a finite field lie in the torus envelope

Worker 1387. This module states one proposition,
`FiniteFieldMaximalTorusTransporterStatement.{u}`. It is not proved here.

## Statement

Fix a finite field `k : Type u` and a group scheme `G` over `Spec k` that is
smooth and affine, with connected underlying space. The proposition asserts
that there are a commutative group scheme `T₀` over `Spec k` and a homomorphism
`j₀ : T₀ ⟶ G` such that

- `j₀` is a maximal torus (`IsMaximalTorus j₀`): `T₀` is a torus, `j₀` is a
  closed subgroup, and `j₀` factors isomorphically through every closed torus
  of `G` through which it factors; and
- for every scheme `U` over `Spec k` and all points `g, x : U ⟶ G.X` with `x`
  in `(SubgroupFunctor.range j₀).carrier U`, the conjugate `g * x * g⁻¹` lies in
  `(closedTorusEnvelope G).carrier U`.

The group operations are those of the group of `U`-points of `G`. The second
condition says that the conjugation morphism `G × T₀ → G`, `(g, t) ↦ g t g⁻¹`,
takes values in the closed envelope of all ground-field tori on every test
scheme, reduced or not. Equivalently, the transporter of `T₀` into the envelope
is all of `G`. The envelope is the existing `closedTorusEnvelope G`: the
intersection, on every test scheme, of the ranges of all actual closed
subgroups of `G` that contain every closed `k`-torus of `G`. No new envelope is
introduced and no representability, smoothness, or splitting hypothesis is
added.

The torus `T₀` is existentially quantified. Only one maximal `k`-torus is
asserted to have this property.

## Source and derivation

The source is Conrad–Gabber–Prasad, *Pseudo-reductive groups*, second edition
(2015), Proposition A.2.11, printed pp.477–480 (one-based PDF pp.503–506),
registered as `conrad-gabber-prasad-2015-pseudo-reductive`. For a smooth
connected affine group `G` over a field `k`, it asserts that the smooth
connected `k`-subgroup `G_t` generated by the `k`-tori of `G` is normal in `G`.
The finite-field case of its proof is on printed pp.478–480.

The present proposition is a derived consequence of that assertion, specialized
to finite fields. The source subgroup `G_t` is a closed subgroup containing
every `k`-torus and contained in every closed subgroup containing them, so its
range on every test scheme is the fixed `closedTorusEnvelope G`. Normality of
`G_t` gives `g x g⁻¹ ∈ G_t` for every point `g` of `G` and every point `x` of
`G_t` over the same test scheme. Every `k`-torus lies in `G_t`, so this holds
for points `x` of any maximal `k`-torus `T₀`; a maximal `k`-torus exists in
every smooth affine group over a field.

The weaker form, one maximal torus rather than normality, is what the
reduction module `TorusGeneratedEnvelopeAlgebraicClosureFiniteReduction`
needs. Together with `MaximalTorusBaseChangeStatement` and
`MaximalTorusConjugacyStatement` it gives
`TorusGeneratedEnvelopeAlgebraicClosureFiniteStatement`. That reduction
specializes the conjugation condition to the test scheme `Spec k̄`.

The hypotheses (finite field; smooth, affine, connected group) are those of the
finite-field case of Proposition A.2.11. No perfectness assumption is stated
separately, since finite fields are perfect. Splitness, reductivity, solvability
and characteristic restrictions are not assumed.
