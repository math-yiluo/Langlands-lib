# Field extension of the fixed closed torus envelope: the inclusion

Worker 1523. This internal module works at arbitrary universe `u` over every
field `k` and every field extension `K` of `k` in the same universe, given as an
algebra `[Algebra k K]`. The base-change morphism is
`Spec.map (CommRingCat.ofHom (algebraMap k K))`; for `K = AlgebraicClosure k`
it is the Library abbreviation `toAlgebraicClosure k`. Finite and imperfect
fields, every characteristic, and nonsplit tori are included.

## Main results

For a smooth affine group scheme `G` over `Spec k`,
`closedTorusEnvelope_baseChange_le K G` proves that the base change to `K` of
the fixed envelope `closedTorusEnvelope G` of all closed `k`-tori lies in the
fixed envelope of all closed `K`-tori of the base-changed group. The comparison
holds as subgroup functors on every test scheme over `Spec K`. Connectedness
of `G` is not assumed.

`closedTorusEnvelope_baseChange_eq_iff_containsTori K G` proves, under the same
hypotheses, that the two subgroup functors are equal exactly when the base
change of `closedTorusEnvelope G` contains every closed `K`-torus (the Library
predicate `SubgroupFunctor.ContainsTori`). The forward direction is the
containment property of the envelope over `K`. In the reverse direction, the
base change of the published smooth representative
`smoothTorusGeneratedEnvelope G` is a closed subgroup of the extended group.
Its range is the base-changed envelope by
`SubgroupFunctor.range_baseChangeHom`, and the least-closed-subgroup property
of the envelope over `K` gives the remaining inclusion. Hence the open
equality in `TorusGeneratedEnvelopeAlgebraicClosureInfiniteStatement` and
`...FiniteStatement` reduces to the containment of all `K`-tori for
`K = AlgebraicClosure k`. This module proves neither statement.

## Implemented derivation

The inclusion is proved by the minimal-height argument of the published
`Rational.SmoothTorusGeneratedEnvelope`, with one additional condition kept
along the way.

- `EnvelopeBaseChange.BaseChangeContained K i` says that the base change to
  `K` of the all-test range of `i : H ⟶ G` lies in the envelope of `K`-tori.
  `baseChangeContained_torus` proves it for every closed `k`-torus: its base
  change is a closed `K`-torus by `IsTorus.baseChange` and
  `IsClosedSubgroup.baseChange`.
- `EnvelopeBaseChange.isSchemeTheoreticallyDominant_pullback_map` transports a
  quasi-compact schematically dominant morphism of over schemes along a flat
  base change. It uses the actual pullback functor `Over.pullback`. The field
  extension is flat by `fieldExtension_fpqc`.
- `EnvelopeBaseChange.range_baseChange_le_of_points` considers a generated
  image `D` of a morphism `f : V ⟶ G.X`, with `V` affine over `k`, smooth affine
  `D.group` and a dominant ordered power map `D.powerMap n`. Let `a` be a
  closed subgroup of the extended group. The hypothesis quantifies over all
  test schemes: for every scheme `W` over `Spec K`, possibly nonreduced, and
  every morphism `v` from `W`, regarded as a scheme over `k`, to `V`, the
  `W`-valued point of the extended group induced by `v ≫ f` lies in `a(W)`.
  Containment of the `K`-rational points alone would not suffice (over `F₂`,
  the subgroup of `G_a` cut out by `X² - X` contains every `F₂`-point of `G_a`
  but not the dual-number point `ε`). Under this hypothesis the base change of
  `D.inclusion` has range inside that of `a`. The proof applies the hypothesis
  with `W` the base change of the finite power `V^n`. The base change of the
  ordered power map is then a product of such points, so it factors through
  `a`. Smoothness over a field makes `D.group` reduced, so the
  dominant power map is schematically dominant, and so is its base change.
  The published `exists_over_factor_of_schematic_precomp` then factors the
  base-changed inclusion through the closed immersion `a`.
- `EnvelopeBaseChange.baseChangeContained_generated` applies this to the
  multiplication morphism `H × T → G` of `SmoothTorusEnvelope.productMap`. If
  `H` satisfies `BaseChangeContained K` and `T` is a closed `k`-torus, the
  group generated by this product does too.
- `EnvelopeBaseChange.IsAdmissible K i` is the conjunction of the published
  `SmoothTorusEnvelope.IsAdmissible i` (closed, smooth, connected, inside the
  envelope of `k`-tori) and `BaseChangeContained K i`. A closed `k`-torus is
  admissible, so admissible subgroups exist. One of them, `H`, has minimal
  coordinate-kernel height. Given a closed `k`-torus `T`, pointed-image
  generation for `H × T → G` supplies a smooth connected generated subgroup.
  It is admissible by the preceding item and contains `H`, so the published
  coordinate-kernel height criterion makes the factorization of `H` through
  it an isomorphism. Thus `H` contains every closed `k`-torus. Its range is
  therefore the fixed envelope, and the second admissibility condition is the
  asserted inclusion.

## Provenance

The provenance is internal. The source statement is the second paragraph of
Conrad--Gabber--Prasad, *Pseudo-reductive groups*, second edition (2015),
Proposition A.2.11 (printed pp.477--480, one-based PDF pp.503--506; registered
as `conrad-gabber-prasad-2015-pseudo-reductive`). It states that the formation
of the subgroup `G_t` generated by the `k`-tori commutes with every extension of
the ground field. The inclusion proved here is the elementary half of that
compatibility: the extension of `G_t` is generated by extended `k`-tori, which
are `K`-tori. The Lean argument instead runs the Library's own minimal-height
construction of the envelope, adapted to keep track of field extension; the
source does not spell out this half. The deep half, that the extension contains
every `K`-torus, is the retained content of the two published algebraic-closure
statements. The source proves it separately for infinite fields (density of
rational points of the variety of maximal tori) and for finite fields
(Gabber's argument).
