# Standard rational parabolic subgroup functors

This file defines the shared standard subgroup functors of a group scheme `G` over
a field `k`, relative to a commutative group scheme homomorphism `i : T ⟶ G` and a
cocharacter `ν` of `T`. The construction needs only that `G` is affine. Maximal
splitness, connected reductivity and genericity belong to the subsequent
structural statements, rather than to the definitions.

`positiveRelativeRoots i ν` consists of the published relative roots `α` with
positive integer pairing with `ν`. `simpleRelativeRoots i ν` consists of those
positive roots that are not sums of two positive roots. All relative roots enter
this definition; reducedness is not assumed. These are **derived formulations**
of positivity and simplicity through a cocharacter, in place of the order used
in Borel–Tits §5.12 or the minimal parabolic used in Renard V.3.12 and V.4.2.
The definitions also apply when the relative-root set is empty.

`IsStandardCocharacter i ν J μ` includes `J ⊆ simpleRelativeRoots i ν`, the zero
pairing condition on `J`, and strict positivity on the other simple roots.
`standardParabolic`, `standardLevi` and `standardUnipotent` are pointwise
intersections of, respectively, the published dynamic parabolic, centralizer and
unipotent subgroup functors of every such `μ`. Their restriction stability follows
from that of each dynamic subgroup. This choice-free intersection construction is
**derived**, rather than a literal definition in the sources. It takes no
structure theorem as an argument. An empty family has intersection equal to the
whole point group, as usual; existence of a standard cocharacter and agreement
with each member of the family are separate structural assertions.

The three `standard…Points` abbreviations evaluate these actual subgroup
functors at `Over.mk (𝟙 (Spec k))`. Thus they are subgroups of the categorical
rational-point group `G(k)`, without auxiliary labels or an abstract group model.
`standardRelativeWeylGroup` is the image in the published quotient
`relativeWeylGroup i = normalizerPoints i / centralizerPoints i` of the subgroup
of normalizing points lying in `standardLeviPoints`. Its membership lemma gives
the precise representative condition. Its identification with the reflection
subgroup generated by `J` is a further structural theorem.

The elementary lemmas express membership in the intersections, their inclusions
in each dynamic subgroup, and the inclusions of the standard Levi and unipotent
subgroups in the standard parabolic. The whole-torus centralizer lies in the
standard Levi because it centralizes every cocharacter through the torus.
These lemmas do not assert a general Levi decomposition for the intersections.

Sources:

- Borel and Tits, *Groupes réductifs* (1965), source
  `borel-tits-1965-groupes-reductifs`, §3.13 (printed pp.78–80), §4.2
  (printed pp.85–86), §5.12 (printed p.99) and §5.17 (printed p.101).
  Sections 4.2 and 5.12 attach standard parabolic, Levi and unipotent groups
  to simple-root subsets; §5.17 identifies the Weyl group of the standard Levi.
- Renard, *Représentations des groupes réductifs p-adiques*, French version
  dated 9 July 2026, source
  `renard-2026-representations-groupes-reductifs-padiques`, V.3.8
  (printed p.111), V.3.12 (printed p.112), V.4.2 (printed p.118) and
  V.4.3 (printed p.119). Renard works over a nonarchimedean local field;
  Borel–Tits supplies the arbitrary-field scope of the intended statements.

The implemented intersections and subgroup images are internal constructions on
the already published `Rational.RelativeRoots`, `Rational.TorusCentralizer`,
`Torus.Lattice` and `Dynamic.Basic` interfaces.
