# The rank-three quaternionic skew isotropy principle

Let `F` be a number field and `D` a central quaternion division algebra over it. The quaternion conjugation is obtained from the supplied orthogonal involution, with `D` of dimension four and the involution's symmetric subspace of dimension three. An invertible rank-three antihermitian Gram matrix for quaternion conjugation has a nonzero skew-isotropic row over `F` if, over each completion of `F`, it has a skew-isotropic row with at least one invertible quaternion coordinate.

The pure quaternion norm calculation and the local symmetric isotropic planes give a hyperbolic symmetric component over the embedded quadratic field. A three-dimensional totally isotropic subspace meeting its semilinear translate supplies a skew-isotropic vector directly. In the complementary case its induced Hermitian pairing is left-separating. An orthogonal basis of the subspace extends to a quaternion basis, in which the scaled skew pairing has diagonal entries along the pure root line. At the real completions the given isotropic rows imply the scalar norm equation in that basis. The rank-six norm form represents the required determinant value over the number field. Splitting the corresponding Hermitian line and rescaling its binary complement yields the pair whose sum with its semilinear translate has skew self-value zero. Complementarity ensures that the resulting vector is nonzero.

This implements the rank-three case of Theorem 5.3.3(i) in Springer's appendix to Kneser, *Lectures on Galois Cohomology of Classical Groups* (1969), printed pp. 109–110 (PDF pp. 115–116), with the local row hypothesis stated above.
