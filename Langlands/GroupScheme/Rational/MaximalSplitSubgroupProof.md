# Proof of Borel–Tits 7.2: the split reductive subgroup with the nonmultipliable roots

This module proves `Langlands.GroupScheme.MaximalSplitSubgroupStatement` (Borel–Tits 1965,
Théorème 7.2, existence part), stated in `Rational/MaximalSplitSubgroupStatement`:

- `Langlands.GroupScheme.maximalSplitSubgroup : MaximalSplitSubgroupStatement.{u}`.

Let `k` be any field, `G` a connected reductive group over `Spec k` and `i : S ⟶ G` a maximal
split torus. Then there are a connected reductive `F`, a closed subgroup `j : F ⟶ G` and
`f : S ⟶ F` with `f ≫ j = i`, such that `f` is a maximal torus of `F` and
`roots f = {a ∈ roots i | 2 • a ∉ roots i}`.

It also contains the intermediate theorem
`MaximalSplitSubgroup.exists_maximalSplitSubgroup (K)`. This is the same conclusion for any
algebraically closed field `K` with `[Algebra k K]`, which serves as the auxiliary extension
in the proof. The main theorem applies it with `K = AlgebraicClosure k`.

No hypothesis on `k` is made: any characteristic, perfect or not, finite or infinite. The
proof does not use Borel–Tits 1965, 3.22(b). The project erratum
`notes/errata/borel-tits-1965-closed-root-intersection.md` records a counterexample to it.

## Proof of `exists_maximalSplitSubgroup`

Write `ψ = characterLattice.baseChange bc S` for base change of characters along
`bc : Spec K ⟶ Spec k`, and `R = roots i`. A subscript `K` denotes base change to `K`.

1. **A base of the nonmultipliable roots.** `exists_nonmultipliableBase`
   (module `MaximalSplitNonmultipliableBase`) gives a finite set `D` of roots with `2d ∉ R`
   and a cocharacter `lam` with the following properties. `lam` is positive on `D` and nonzero
   on `R`. No `n d - m e` (`d ≠ e` in `D`, `n, m > 0`) is zero or a root. Roots in the
   semigroup spanned by `D` are nonmultipliable. Each `d₀ ∈ D` has a cocharacter `mu` vanishing
   on `d₀` and positive on the rest of `D`. Finally, every nonmultipliable root satisfies each
   predicate that holds on `D` and is preserved by the reflections at `D` (whenever those
   reflections preserve `R`).
2. **The subgroup over `k`.** `exists_generated_lineSubgroup` (module
   `MaximalSplitSubgroupGenerated`), applied to the family `d ↦ d` on `D`, gives a smooth,
   affine, connected closed subgroup `j : F ⟶ G` with `f ≫ j = i`. `F` is generated by `f`
   and lines `x d`, `y d` of weights `±d`. The same theorem gives cocharacters `ν d` with
   `⟨d, ν d⟩ = 2`, the rank-one relations, Weyl points `n d` normalizing `f` through the
   reflections, the commutation of `x d` with `y e` for `d ≠ e`, and preservation of `R` by
   the reflections. Then `f` is a closed subgroup (`IsClosedSubgroup.of_comp`), and
   `roots f ⊆ R` (`roots_subset_comp`). By the published `relativeRootNegation`, `R` is
   stable under negation.
3. **Transport to `K`.** `baseChange_smooth_affine_connected` and
   `dense_baseChange_lineFamily` (module `MaximalSplitSubgroupBaseChange`) show that `F_K` is
   smooth, affine and connected. They also show that the `K`-points generated by `f_K` and
   the base-changed lines are dense. The module `MaximalSplitLineBaseChange` gives
   the following for the base-changed lines `lineBaseChange K (x d)` and
   `lineBaseChange K (y d)`: their weights `±ψ d`, the rank-one relations, commutation, and
   that `lineBaseChange K (x d)` is a monomorphism. The module
   `MaximalSplitSubgroupRootTransport` gives the remaining data over `K`:
   `IsNormalizingPoint.baseChange_latticeReflection` for the Weyl points, and
   `baseChange_rootData` for the combinatorial clauses with root set
   `ΦK = nonmultipliableBaseChange K R = ψ '' {c ∈ R | 2 • c ∉ R}`. The published
   `roots_baseChange` gives `roots f_K = ψ '' roots f`, and the base change of `f` is a
   closed subgroup (`IsClosedSubgroup.baseChange`).
4. **Reductivity and roots over `K`.** `isConnectedReductive_and_roots_eq_of_generated`
   (module `MaximalSplitGeneratedReductive`) shows that `F_K` is connected reductive with
   `roots f_K = ΦK`. `torusCentralizer_eq_range_of_generated` (module
   `MaximalSplitGeneratedCell`) shows that the torus centralizer of `f_K` in `F_K` is the
   range of `f_K`. Then `isMaximalTorus_of_torusCentralizer_eq` makes `f_K` a maximal torus.
5. **Descent.** `IsConnectedReductive.of_fieldExtension` gives that `F` is connected
   reductive, and `IsMaximalTorus.of_baseChange` gives that `f` is a maximal torus of `F`.
   Since `S` is split, `ψ` is injective
   (`IsSplitTorus.characterLattice_baseChange_bijective`). Hence
   `ψ '' roots f = roots f_K = ΦK = ψ '' {c ∈ R | 2 • c ∉ R}` gives
   `roots f = {c ∈ R | 2 • c ∉ R}`.

## Relation to the literature

Borel–Tits prove Théorème 7.2 (printed pp. 117–121, PDF pp. 64–68) with the closed-root
assertion 3.22. The Lean proof follows instead Conrad–Gabber–Prasad, *Pseudo-reductive
groups*, 2nd ed. (2015), Theorem C.2.29 (printed pp. 625–628, PDF pp. 651–654) and the proof
of Theorem C.2.30 (printed pp. 631–632, PDF pp. 657–658). CGP assume `k` infinite and use
Zariski density of rational points over `k`. Here every density argument is made over the
algebraic closure, and the result descends to `k` by field extension. This makes the proof
work over every field.
