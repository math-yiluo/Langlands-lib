# Highest-weight spaces under arbitrary field extension

For a supplied split pinning and an arbitrary extension of its base field, `representationWeightSpaceBaseChangeFieldEquiv` identifies the tensor extension of each actual representation weight space with the weight space for the pulled-back character and the actual pinning `baseChangeField`. It preserves all weight multiplicities and reflects nonzero weight support. Canonical character pullback also preserves and reflects dominance and membership in the positive-root monoid. The algebraic-extension specializations use the previously published `SplitPinning.baseChange` and retain the corresponding names without `Field`.

The construction first proves that scalar extension of a matrix representation commutes with restriction along a group-scheme homomorphism. It then applies the split-torus character-space equivalence to the restriction to the supplied torus. This concerns character spaces tested on all affine algebras, rather than just rational points. The positive-root monoid comparison maps the additive closure of the simple roots. The dominance comparison uses the unchanged simple-root indices and preservation of the actual integral character-coroot pairing.

The supporting theorem `IsHighestWeight.baseChangeField_of_isIrreducible` takes irreducibility of the extended representation as an explicit premise. The complete split-torus decomposition supplies the spanning condition. Weight multiplicity preservation supplies the one-dimensional highest-weight space. Surjectivity of split character-lattice pullback writes every new weight as an old weight, and preservation of nonzero support and of the positive-root monoid transports the highest-weight inequalities. `IsHighestWeight.baseChange_of_isIrreducible` is its algebraic-extension specialization. This module does not prove the separate absolute irreducibility premise.

Provenance: this is a derived construction using actual pinning and character-space base change, for the proof of Milne, *Algebraic Groups* (2017), discussion 22.5 (printed p.464), with the split-torus decomposition in Theorem 12.12 (printed pp.234–235).
