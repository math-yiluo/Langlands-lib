# Existence of a quadratic opposite-Borel torus

Start with a geometric Borel over the quadratic field, a maximal torus
inside it, and the opposite dynamic Borel for that torus. Rational
conjugacy of minimal parabolics gives a point carrying the coefficient
conjugate of the first Borel to the opposite Borel. Translating the
opposite open cell by this point gives a nonempty open subset of the
group over the quadratic field.

Density of the twisted Galois values gives a point `g` such that
`g⁻¹ σ(g)` lies in this translated cell. Its factorization is a point
of the original unipotent radical followed by a point of the opposite
Borel and the chosen translator. Conjugating the original Borel by
`g` now makes its intersection with its coefficient conjugate the
conjugate of the original maximal torus. Quadratic descent recovers
the intersection as a maximal torus over the ground field.

This proves the existence assertion of Platonov and Rapinchuk,
*Algebraic Groups and Number Theory* (1994), Lemma 6.17,
printed pp. 329–330. The nonempty open cell in the group supplies
the opposition condition used in that lemma.
