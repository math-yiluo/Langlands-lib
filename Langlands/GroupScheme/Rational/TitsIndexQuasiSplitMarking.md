# Quasi-splitness and the marking of every Tits vertex

In a quasi-split group, no absolute root vanishes on the maximal split torus in the index. Thus every vertex is distinguished. Conversely, if every vertex is distinguished, the index's positive cocharacter pairs positively with every simple root. For a root vanishing on the cocharacter, either the root or its negative belongs to the additive monoid generated by the simple roots on which the cocharacter vanishes. That set of simple roots is empty, which contradicts the root being nonzero. The extended cocharacter is therefore regular. Its centralizer is the extended maximal torus. Faithfully flat reflection identifies the centralizer of the original maximal split torus with the original maximal torus. The torus-centralizer criterion gives quasi-splitness.

This is the marking criterion for quasi-split groups in the language of the Tits index, as used in Platonov and Rapinchuk, *Algebraic Groups and Number Theory* (1994), §6.8, printed pp. 371–373.

When the star action is trivial, regularity and invariance of the positive cocharacter make the ordinary Galois action preserve the simple-root base. It is then the star action and fixes every simple root. The simple roots generate all roots, and semisimplicity makes their span have finite index in the character lattice. The entire absolute character lattice is fixed. Equivariant character descent gives an isomorphism with a split torus of the same rank.
