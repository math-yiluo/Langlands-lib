# Reduction of the finite-field maximal-torus transporter to the central-cocharacter case

Worker 1387. This module proves the conditional theorem
`finiteFieldMaximalTorusTransporter_of` and two supporting lemmas, all in
`Langlands.GroupScheme`.

## Main theorem

`finiteFieldMaximalTorusTransporter_of` takes
`hbase : FiniteFieldCentralCocharacterEnvelopeNormalityStatement.{u}` and
proves `FiniteFieldMaximalTorusTransporterStatement.{u}`: over a finite field,
every smooth connected affine `G` has a maximal torus `j₀` whose conjugates
`g x g⁻¹`, for points `g` of `G` and `x` of `range j₀` on every test, lie in
`closedTorusEnvelope G`.

## Proof

Strong induction on the actual Lie dimension
(`smoothConnectedAffineGroup_lieDimension_induction`), for the fixed finite
field.

- **Base case.** If every cocharacter of `G` is central, `hbase` makes the
  envelope normal. Any maximal torus (`TorusExistence.exists_isMaximalTorus`)
  lies in the envelope, so its conjugates do.
- **Inductive step.** Otherwise pick `χ` with `Dynamic.centralizer χ ≠ ⊤` and
  its closed representative `iZ : Z ⟶ G` from `Dynamic.Affine`. `Z` is smooth
  (`dynamicSmooth`), connected (`dynamicConnected`) and affine, and `iZ` is not
  an isomorphism, so `Lie.dimension Z < Lie.dimension G`
  (`Lie.dimension_lt_of_proper_closedSubgroup`). The induction hypothesis gives
  a maximal torus `j₀'` of `Z` with the conjugation property in `Z`.
  - `χ` factors through `Z` as a central cocharacter `χ'` of `Z`
    (`Dynamic.commute_cocharacter_of_mem_centralizer`), hence through `j₀'`
    by `IsCentral.exists_factor_of_isMaximalTorus`.
  - `j₀' ≫ iZ` is a maximal torus of `G`: a closed torus `i''` of `G` through
    which it factors also receives `χ`, so it centralizes `χ`
    (`range_le_torusCentralizer_of_factor` and
    `torusCentralizer_gm_eq_dynamicCentralizer`), factors through `Z`, and the
    maximality of `j₀'` applies.
  - Conjugation property: `U_G(χ)` and `U_G(χ⁻¹)` lie in the envelope
    (`unipotent_le_closedTorusEnvelope`, finite field). For a point `z` of
    `Z_G(χ)`, write `z = z' ≫ iZ`; the conjugate of a point of `j₀'` by `z'`
    lies in the envelope of `Z` by the induction hypothesis, and its image
    lies in the envelope of `G` by `closedTorusEnvelope_comp_mem`.
    `Dynamic.conj_mem_of_centralizer_conj_mem`, with the smooth envelope
    representative `smoothTorusGeneratedEnvelope G`, extends this from
    `Z_G(χ)` to all of `G`.

## Supporting lemmas

- `closedTorusEnvelope_comp_mem`: for a closed subgroup `iZ : Z ⟶ G`, the image
  under `iZ` of a point of `closedTorusEnvelope Z` lies in
  `closedTorusEnvelope G`. For a closed subgroup `j'` of `G` containing all
  `k`-tori, the pullback of `j'` along `iZ` in group schemes is a closed
  subgroup of `Z` (base change of a closed immersion) containing all `k`-tori
  of `Z`, since a closed torus of `Z` composed with `iZ` is a closed torus of
  `G`. The minimality `closedTorusEnvelope_le` and the pullback square conclude.
- `IsCentral.exists_factor_of_isMaximalTorus`: over a field, a central
  homomorphism `a : A ⟶ M` from a torus into a smooth affine group factors
  through every maximal torus `t`. The product homomorphism `prodHom a t` from
  the torus `A ⊗ T` factors through a closed torus
  (`torusImageClosedTorusStatement`); maximality of `t` makes the composite of
  the second inclusion with this factor an isomorphism, and inverting it gives
  the factorization of `a`. This mirrors the published split-torus version
  `IsCentralSplitTorus.exists_factor_of_isMaximalSplitTorus`.

## Provenance

The reduction is an internal derivation from published Library results and
the single explicit hypothesis `hbase`. It follows the structure of the
finite-field argument of Conrad–Gabber–Prasad, *Pseudo-reductive groups*,
second edition (2015), proof of Proposition A.2.11, printed pp.479–480: the
subgroups generated by a split torus and `U_G(±λ)` handle noncentral
cocharacters through the open cell, and the remaining case without
noncentral split tori is the content of `hbase`. The containment of
`U_G(±λ)` uses the finite-field point count instead of the source's
solvable-case argument.
