# Well-founded lower-variable remainders for actual fundamental traces

This module proves the well-founded lower-variable condition for the actual restricted fundamental trace characters. It retains any field and actual split pinning, every supplied root ordering, normalized parametrization and reflection representative, and an arbitrary supplied family of actual fundamental highest-weight representations. It asserts no existence of that family. No algebraic-closure or simple-connectedness premise is needed for this calculation.

The internal root-pairing lemma `rootForm_nonneg_of_isDominant` shows that the canonical root form is nonnegative between dominant weights. For each coroot, the base expresses that coroot or its negative as a nonnegative sum of simple coroots. Both dominant weights therefore pair with it with the same sign, so their product is nonnegative. Summing over all coroots proves the lemma. `rootForm_self_le_of_dominant_difference` expands the canonical squared length and combines this cross-term nonnegativity with nonnegativity of the squared length of the difference: adding a dominant weight cannot decrease squared length.

`fundamentalWeightLength` is the natural-number value of the nonnegative integral canonical squared length of the supplied fundamental highest weight. The strict comparison of these lengths, pulled back to the variable indices by the supplied ordering, is well founded by the natural-number measure theorem. `fundamentalTraceDiagonal` is the coefficient of the own-variable linear monomial in the actual restricted fundamental character. `fundamentalTraceRemainder` subtracts that coefficient times the corresponding variable. Its own-variable coefficient vanishes by coefficient extraction.

The main support comparison is `fundamentalWeightLength_lt_of_trace_coeff_ne_zero`. For a nonzero monomial coefficient different from the own-variable linear monomial, the actual monomial-support theorem supplies an occurring dominant weight `lam` with precisely those simple-coroot exponents. The highest-weight property places `lam` below the source fundamental highest weight in the simple-root cone. Equality of the weights would force exactly the excluded own-variable monomial, by the fundamental pairing equations. The published canonical root-form comparison in `DominantWeightTriangularity` therefore gives strictly smaller squared length for `lam`.

If a variable occurs in that monomial, its exponent is positive. Subtracting its fundamental highest weight from `lam` remains dominant, because it decreases just that positive simple-coroot pairing by one. The preceding difference comparison bounds that fundamental weight's squared length by the squared length of `lam`. Its length is consequently strictly smaller than that of the source fundamental weight. Nonnegativity allows passage from integral squared lengths to their natural-number values.

`fundamentalTraceRemainder_hasLowerRemainders` extracts a monomial containing any variable of a remainder. Its own-variable coefficient is zero, so the monomial is not the excluded linear term, and its coefficient agrees with the actual trace coefficient. The support comparison thus proves the existing `TriangularPolynomial.HasLowerRemainders` condition. Finally, `hasTriangularFundamentalRestriction_of_diagonal_ne_zero` packages these remainders, their well-founded relation, and the units formed from nonzero diagonal coefficients into the existing `HasTriangularFundamentalRestriction` conclusion. Nonvanishing of those actual diagonal coefficients is its explicit remaining input.

The provenance is internal: the proof combines the actual trace monomial-support theorem, the supplied fundamental pairing and highest-weight conditions, the published simple-root-cone root-form comparison, and finite-root and polynomial identities from Mathlib. This canonical-length order implements the lower-variable step without choosing a linear extension of the dominant-weight order. The module proves no nonvanishing claim, normalization of the diagonal coefficients, representation-existence theorem, invariant-generation theorem, or canonical quotient theorem.
