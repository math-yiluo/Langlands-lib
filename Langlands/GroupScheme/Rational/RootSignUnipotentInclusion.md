# Root signs, dynamic unipotent inclusions, and nested decompositions from root negation

Author: Worker 818.

This module proves two root-sign criteria for dynamic subgroups. It also gives a
conditional assembly of the published target `DynamicNestedParabolicDecompositionStatement`
from the single published hypothesis `RelativeRootNegationStatement`. The negation
statement stays an explicit premise and is not proved here. The unconditional target
is not claimed.

Throughout, `k` is an arbitrary field, `G` a group scheme over `Spec k`, `T` a commutative
group scheme over `Spec k`, `i : T ⟶ G` a homomorphism, and `zeta`, `eta` elements of
`cocharacterLattice T`. `⟨α, zeta⟩` denotes the published `latticePairing T α zeta`.
`U(zeta)` and `P(zeta)` denote the dynamic subgroup functors
`Dynamic.unipotent (groupHom zeta ≫ i)` and `Dynamic.parabolic (groupHom zeta ≫ i)`.
All inclusions are inclusions of subgroup functors on every test scheme over `Spec k`,
including nonaffine and nonreduced tests. Roots are the published actual relative roots
`relativeRoots i`: the nonzero characters with a nonzero weight space in the Lie kernel.

## Root-sign criteria (namespace `Dynamic`)

The first two theorems assume that `G.X.hom` is affine and locally of finite type and
that `T` is a split torus. Neither reductivity, smoothness nor connectedness is assumed.

`Dynamic.unipotent_le_of_positive_rootSigns`: if every root positive on `zeta` is
positive on `eta`, then `U(zeta) ≤ U(eta)`.

Proof. By `CocharacterWeights.unipotent_le_of_mem_nonpositiveIdeal` (module
`Dynamic.CocharacterWeightCriterion`), it suffices to show the following. Let `g` be an
augmentation eigenfunction of the conjugation action of `T` whose weight `w` has
`⟨w, eta⟩ ≤ 0`. Then `g` lies in the ideal generated by the augmentation eigenfunctions
of weights nonpositive on `zeta`. This is
`Algebra.GradedSignIdeal.mem_nonpositiveSpan_of_cotangent_signs`, applied to:

- the augmentation ideal `I`, internally graded by the augmentation weight spaces
  (second conjunct of the published `coordinateWeightDecomposition`);
- the homogeneous multiplication `augmentationGradedMul`;
- the additive maps `w ↦ ⟨w, eta⟩` and `w ↦ ⟨w, zeta⟩`.

Its cotangent hypothesis is checked as follows. Let `x` be homogeneous of weight `χ`
with nonzero class in `I/I²` and `⟨χ, zeta⟩ > 0`. By the published
`cotangentMk_mem_weightSpace`, `χ` lies in the cotangent support. The published
`cotangentLieSupport` identifies that support with the support of the conjugation action
on the Lie kernel. Since `⟨χ, zeta⟩ > 0`, `χ ≠ 0`, so `χ` is a root by
`RelativeRoot.mem_relativeRoots_iff_actionWeightSupport_conjugation_top`. The
hypothesis then gives `⟨χ, eta⟩ > 0`.

`Dynamic.latticePairing_nonneg_of_parabolic_le`: if `P(zeta) ≤ P(eta)`, every root `α`
with `⟨α, zeta⟩ ≥ 0` has `⟨α, eta⟩ ≥ 0`.

Proof. Choose a nonzero `X` in the weight space of `α`. It lies in the nonnegative
weight span of `zeta`. The published `mem_nonnegativeWeightSpan_iff_mem_parabolic`
(module `Dynamic.WeightExclusion`) puts the corresponding point of `G(k[ε])` in `P(zeta)`,
hence in `P(eta)`. The published `latticePairing_nonneg_of_weight_mem_parabolic` then gives
`⟨α, eta⟩ ≥ 0`.

For a maximal split torus of a connected reductive group, these two theorems give the
second conjunct and the forward half of the first conjunct of the published
`RelativeRootSignInclusionsStatement`. They are proved here under the weaker hypotheses
above. The first needs no root symmetry, only the published coordinate-weight
decomposition and cotangent support comparison.

`Dynamic.unipotent_le_of_parabolic_le_of_negation` assumes
`RelativeRootNegationStatement`, a connected reductive `G`, and a maximal split torus
`i`. It shows that `P(zeta) ≤ P(eta)` implies `U(eta) ≤ U(zeta)`.

Proof. Connected reductive groups are affine and smooth, hence locally of finite type,
and `T` is split. By the first theorem with the roles exchanged, it suffices that every
root `α` positive on `eta` is positive on `zeta`. If `⟨α, zeta⟩ ≤ 0`, then `-α` is a root
by the negation hypothesis, and `⟨-α, zeta⟩ ≥ 0`. By the second theorem
`⟨-α, eta⟩ ≥ 0`, contradicting `⟨α, eta⟩ > 0` by linearity of the pairing.

## Conditional assembly

`DynamicNestedParabolicDecompositionFromNegation` is the proposition
`RelativeRootNegationStatement.{u} → DynamicNestedParabolicDecompositionStatement.{u}`.
The theorem `dynamicNestedParabolicDecomposition_of_negation` proves it. For a compatible
nested pair `χ, ψ`, unpack the supplied common maximal split torus `i` and the
cocharacters `zeta`, `eta` of `T` realizing `χ`, `ψ`, together with the supplied inclusions
`P(χ) ≤ P(ψ)` and `Z(χ) ≤ Z(ψ)`. The previous theorem gives `U(ψ) ≤ U(χ)`. The published
`Dynamic.nestedLeviDecomposition_of_inclusions` (module `Dynamic.NestedDecomposition`)
turns the three inclusions into the normal complement and the three set-product
equalities of `Dynamic.NestedParabolicDecomposition` on every test scheme.

## Example: negation is needed

Without negation stability the reverse unipotent inclusion can fail. Take
`G = 𝔾_a ⋊ 𝔾_m` with `t` acting on `𝔾_a` by `t`, and `T = 𝔾_m`. Its only root is the
identity character. For the cocharacters `-1` and `1`, `P(-1) = 𝔾_m ≤ P(1) = G`, yet
`U(1) = 𝔾_a` is not contained in `U(-1) = 1`. This group is not reductive. Negation
stability is what reductivity contributes here.
