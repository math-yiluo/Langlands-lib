# Supplied normalized simple-root maps and the Steinberg ordered morphism

This internal construction works over an arbitrary field `k : Type u`, in any characteristic, with an actual affine group scheme `G` and its supplied `SplitPinning G`. It defines data and morphisms on the Library's actual group schemes and scheme tests. No existence theorem for these supplied data is asserted.

`SplitPinning.NormalizedRootParametrization p a` supplies a group-scheme homomorphism from the standard additive group to `G`. It must represent the whole intrinsic functor `RelativeRoot.subgroup p.inclusion (p.rootDatum.root a)` on every test, and it must be a closed subgroup inclusion. Its torus-equivariance field is the identity `t x_a(c) t⁻¹ = x_a(a(t)c)` on every scheme over the base, using `VectorGroup.charValue` and standard global-function coordinates. Its derivative field sends `VectorGroup.standardEpsilon k` to `p.pinning.rootVector a` via the actual `Lie.targetMapLinear`. The epsilon vector is `RelativeRoot.vectorLiePoint` with coefficient one. The family type is `NormalizedSimpleRootParametrizations p`.

`VectorGroup.scalarPoint R T c` is the actual additive-group point with global coordinate `c`. Its coordinate and precomposition lemmas expose this identification on every test. `coordinateMorphism R r j` is the actual map from the standard vector-group scheme to its `j`-th additive coordinate, and `comp_coordinateMorphism` records its all-test value. `NormalizedRootParametrization.point` evaluates the supplied root map at a scalar. Its precomposition law and `mem_rootSubgroup_iff` identify the complete intrinsic root subgroup with these scalar values.

`SimpleReflectionRepresentative p a` supplies a rational point, an actual torus automorphism, the equation `IsNormalizingPoint p.inclusion point torusAut`, and `characterAut torusAut = p.rootDatum.reflection a`. This uses the Library's convention in which the character action is induced by the inverse torus automorphism. `SimpleReflectionRepresentatives p` is the family of such data. No representative is selected from unspecified normalizer points, and no compatibility with descent or a pinning beyond the displayed data is claimed.

For an ordering `e : Fin r ≃ p.base.support` and supplied families `x` and `n`, `steinbergProductPoint e x n T c` is the product of `x_(e j)(c_j) n_(e j)`, in increasing `Fin r` order, with each rational point pulled back by `toUnit T`. The implementation uses `List.ofFn` and the actual point group law, so it does not assume that factors commute. `steinbergProductPoint_comp` proves the formula's compatibility with every morphism of test schemes, pulling back each global function.

`steinbergProduct e x n` is an actual morphism over `Spec k` from `(VectorGroup.groupScheme (CommRingCat.of k) r).X` to `G.X`. Its domain is the spectrum of the polynomial coordinate algebra in `Fin r` variables. The construction evaluates the ordered expression on this domain's global coordinate sections and multiplies the resulting scheme maps. `comp_steinbergProduct` proves the displayed ordered formula on every test using coordinate pullback. `coordinatesPoint_comp_steinbergProduct` evaluates it at any family of global functions through the inverse standard coordinate equivalence. The proofs pull precomposition through the ordered list product by list induction, use naturality of each root-map value and the test structure map, and identify pulled-back coordinate sections by `VectorGroup.coordinates_apply`. The two zero-rank lemmas give the identity point and the constant identity morphism for the empty product, by the empty-list product and then the definition of the scheme morphism. The product map is used as a scheme morphism; the construction does not supply it with a group-homomorphism structure.

The original product is Robert Steinberg, *Regular elements of semi-simple algebraic groups* (1965), §7, printed pp.66–68 (PDF pp.19–21), especially Theorem 7.1 and Lemma 7.5, registered as `steinberg-1965-regular-elements-semisimple-groups`. The present all-test formulation and derivative normalization are internal bookkeeping, rather than a literal statement of Lemma 7.14's normalization. Borel–Springer, *Rationality Properties of Linear Algebraic Groups II* (1968), §8.1–8.2, printed pp.481–482 (PDF pp.39–40), registered as `borel-springer-1968-rationality-properties-ii`, is a source for the later arbitrary-field root-parametrization existence obligation; that theorem is not proved here.

The file does not assert root-parametrization existence, regularity, immersion, character identities, a Galois action, any invariant-quotient isomorphism, or rational lifting. It applies relative to supplied split data, including over imperfect and finite fields, and does not infer that an arbitrary quasi-split group is split. The parent quasi-split Steinberg quotient statement and its imported pairs are unchanged.
