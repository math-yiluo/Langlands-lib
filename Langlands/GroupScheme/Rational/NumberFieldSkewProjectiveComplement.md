# Projective skew values in the complement of a line

`NumberFieldSkewProjectiveComplementStatement` takes an invertible skew Gram matrix for standard quaternion conjugation over a number field. At every completion it assumes an isotropic row with an invertible coordinate. For any global row `e` of nonzero skew value, it gives a nonzero row `z` orthogonal to `e` whose skew value is a ground-field multiple of the value of `e`. That scalar multiple can be zero.

Choose the embedded quadratic field generated by the value of `e`. Its symmetric component has a normalized hyperbolic pair on `e` and a scalar multiple of `j • e`. The two pairings with those vectors detect both quaternion coordinates of the pairing with `e`, so their common orthogonal complement is also orthogonal to the original quaternion line. At each completion of the embedded quadratic field, the original local skew zero gives an isotropic plane in the extended symmetric component. The orthogonal complement of the specified hyperbolic pair then contains an isotropic vector. The scalar extension of the global complement is identified by extending the projection onto it. The quadratic local-to-global theorem gives the required global nonzero vector. Its symmetric component vanishes, which makes its skew value a multiple of the chosen pure value.

This is a derived form of the projective representation and binary-block selection in Springer's appendix to Kneser's *Lectures on Galois Cohomology of Classical Groups* (1969), Proposition 5.3.2 and Theorem 5.3.3(ii), printed pp. 109–112 (PDF pp. 115–118).
