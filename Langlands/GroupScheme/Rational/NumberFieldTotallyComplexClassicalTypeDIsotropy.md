# Totally complex isotropy for classical type D

`NumberFieldTotallyComplexClassicalTypeDIsotropyStatement` applies to simply connected, absolutely almost-simple type D of rank at least four over a totally complex number field. Its actual numbered Cartan matrix has star action fixing the nonfork vertices. The conclusion is positive split rank.

The scalar branch of the spin presentation is isotropic by the dimension of its quadratic form. For the quaternionic branch it suffices to find an isotropic row with an invertible coordinate at every completion for the associated skew-hermitian Gram matrix.

At complex places the quaternion coefficient algebra is a matrix algebra, and quadratic forms of dimension greater than four are isotropic. At nonarchimedean places with split coefficient algebra the same quadratic bound holds by the local anisotropic dimension theorem. The split quaternion chart gives the required nonsingular rows in both cases.

At a nonarchimedean division place, the completed group has positive split rank by the local classification of anisotropic groups. The central isogeny preserves split rank. The identity component maps into the extended unitary Gram group, so that group also has positive split rank. A nontrivial split torus acting by inverse right multiplication has a nonzero weight on the row space. The corresponding common weight space is totally isotropic because that weight is distinct from its negative. A nonzero row in it has an invertible coordinate over the division algebra.

The higher-rank skew isotropy theorem then gives a totally singular line in the spin presentation. Its Witt index is the original group's split rank. This implements the classical type D argument in Platonov and Rapinchuk, *Algebraic Groups and Number Theory*, §6.6, Theorem 6.25, printed p. 352, with the local nonsingularity condition explained immediately before Claim 6.3 on printed p. 343.
