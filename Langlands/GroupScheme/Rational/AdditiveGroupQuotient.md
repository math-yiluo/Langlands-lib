# Quotients of a one-dimensional vector group over a field

This derived unit shows that a quotient of a one-dimensional vector group over a
field is trivial or again a one-dimensional vector group. Milne, *Algebraic
Groups* (2017), 14.58 (§14.f "Algebraic groups isomorphic to Ga", printed p.298,
PDF p.314) states: "Every nontrivial quotient of a group isomorphic to Ga is
isomorphic to Ga (Exercise 14-3a)". Exercise 14-3(a) (printed p.301, PDF p.317)
asks for an endomorphism `φ` of `Ga` with `e → H → Ga → Ga → e` exact for each
algebraic subgroup `H ≠ Ga`. Here the subgroup `H` is the scheme kernel of the
quotient map, which may also be all of `Ga`. The unit proves the statement for
the published predicates `RelativeRoot.IsVectorGroupOfDimension`,
`RelativeRoot.IsFppfQuotient` and `RelativeRoot.HasSplitSeries` over
`B = Spec k`, and it changes no published definition. The polynomial algebra
is in the companion module `AdditivePolynomial`.

## Main theorems

`trivial_or_isVectorGroup_of_flat_surjective` assumes:

- `k` is a field and `V Q : GroupScheme (Spec k)`;
- `hV : IsVectorGroupOfDimension V 1`;
- `q : V ⟶ Q` is a homomorphism whose underlying scheme morphism is flat and
  surjective;
- the structure morphism of `Q` is separated.

Its conclusion is `HasSplitSeries 0 Q ∨ IsVectorGroupOfDimension Q 1`.
`HasSplitSeries 0 Q` says that `Q` is trivial on every test scheme. No
quasi-compactness hypothesis on `q` is needed: `V` is affine, so `V ⟶ Spec k` is
quasi-compact, and since `Q` is separated over `Spec k`, `q` is quasi-compact.

`trivial_or_isVectorGroup_of_isFppfQuotient` has the same conclusion. It replaces
flatness and surjectivity by `IsFppfQuotient N q` for some subgroup functor `N`,
which includes them.

## Definitions

All of these are in namespace `RelativeRoot.AdditiveGroupQuotient`.

- `structureMap T : k →+* Γ(T, ⊤)` is the `k`-algebra structure of the global
  functions of a scheme `T` over `Spec k`. It is the inverse of `Γ(Spec k) ≅ k`
  followed by the structure morphism on global sections. `structureMap_comp`
  records its compatibility with morphisms over `Spec k`.
- From the coordinate system `e, he` of `hV` (section variables in Lean):
  `coord x` is the coordinate of a point `x : T ⟶ V`, and `ofCoord a` is the
  point with coordinate `a`.
- `coordRingHom : k[X] →+* Γ(V, ⊤)` evaluates a polynomial at the coordinate of
  the identity point of `V`, with coefficients through `structureMap V`.
- `lineIso : V ≅ 𝔸¹` is the isomorphism with the published affine line
  `AffineLine.scheme (Spec k)` given by the coordinate.
- `coordEquiv : k[X] ≃+* Γ(V, ⊤)` is `coordRingHom` as a ring isomorphism.
- `polyEndo g : V ⟶ V`, for `g : k[X]`, is the scheme endomorphism `x ↦ g(x)`,
  that is, the point with coordinate `coordRingHom g`.
- `kernelIdeal q` is the ideal of `k[X]` that defines the scheme kernel of `q`.
  It is the preimage under `coordRingHom` of the kernel of the ring map on global
  sections induced by `kernelι q`.
- `kernelPolynomial q` is the principal generator of `kernelIdeal q` divided by
  its leading coefficient, so it is monic or zero (`kernelIdeal_eq_span`,
  `kernelPolynomial_monic`).

## Coordinates

- `coord_comp`, `coord_mul`, `coord_one`, `coord_ofCoord` and `coord_injective`
  say that `coord` is natural, additive, injective and inverse to `ofCoord`.
- `appTop_coordRingHom`: pulling `coordRingHom p` back along a point `x`
  evaluates `p` at `coord x`, by `Polynomial.hom_eval₂` and naturality.
- `isAffine`: `V` is affine, being isomorphic to `𝔸¹`.
- `bijective_eval₂Hom_affineLine` and `bijective_eval₂RingHom_affineLine`:
  evaluation at the coordinate is a ring isomorphism from `k[X]` onto the global
  functions of `𝔸¹`. Mathlib's `AffineSpace.SpecIso_hom_appTop` identifies the
  `MvPolynomial PUnit` version with an isomorphism of global sections, and
  `MvPolynomial.uniqueAlgEquiv` passes to one variable.
- `coordRingHom_bijective`: `coordRingHom` is this evaluation for `𝔸¹`, followed
  by the global-sections isomorphism of `lineIso`.

## Endomorphisms from polynomials

- `coord_comp_polyEndo`: the coordinate of `x ≫ polyEndo g` is `g(coord x)`.
  `comp_polyEndo_eq_one_iff`: so `x ≫ polyEndo g = 1` exactly when
  `g(coord x) = 0`.
- `isMonHom_polyEndo`: if `g(a + b) = g(a) + g(b)` for all global functions `a, b`
  of all test schemes, then `polyEndo g` is a group homomorphism. Compare
  coordinates. The unit condition uses `g(0) = 0`, which follows from additivity.
- `appTop_polyEndo_comp` and `appTop_polyEndo`: on global sections,
  `polyEndo g` is the composition map `p ↦ p ∘ g`, transported through
  `coordEquiv`. Both sides are ring maps out of `k[X]` that agree on constants
  and on `X`.

## The kernel polynomial

Let `f = kernelPolynomial q`, and assume that `Q` is separated.

- `ker_appTop_kernelι_eq`: the ideal of the kernel in `Γ(V, ⊤)` is generated by
  `coordRingHom f`, because `coordRingHom` is surjective.
- `mem_range_kernelι_iff_eval`: a point `x` lies in the kernel of `q` exactly when
  `f(coord x) = 0`. The kernel inclusion `kernelι q` is a closed immersion, since
  `Q` is separated (`kernelι_isClosedImmersion`). If `x` factors through it,
  `f(coord x)` is the pullback of a function vanishing on the kernel. Conversely,
  if `f(coord x) = 0`, then the kernel ideal lies in the kernel of `x` on global
  sections. Mathlib's `Scheme.ker_of_isAffine` and
  `IdealSheafData.equivOfIsAffine` turn this into an inclusion of ideal
  sheaves, and `IsClosedImmersion.lift` factors `x` through the kernel.
- `eval₂_add_eq_zero`: the roots of `f` in the global functions of any test
  scheme are closed under addition, because the kernel is a subgroup.
- `taylor_mem_span`: `f(X + Y) ∈ (f(X), f(Y))` in `k[X][Y]`. Take the test scheme
  `T = Spec (k[X][Y] / (f(X), f(Y)))`. The classes of `X` and `Y` are roots of `f`
  in `Γ(T, ⊤)`, so their sum is a root. Since `Γ(Spec R) ≅ R` is injective,
  `f(X + Y)` vanishes in the quotient ring.
- `kernelι_comp_self`: the kernel inclusion of any homomorphism composes with it
  to `0`.

## Proof of the main theorem

Let `f = kernelPolynomial q`.

- `f = 0`. Every point lies in the kernel, so `q` is trivial on points. Then
  `q ≫ 0 = q = q ≫ 𝟙 Q`, and uniqueness in the published fpqc homomorphism
  descent (`FpqcHomDescent.unique`, with `q` flat, surjective and quasi-compact)
  gives `𝟙 Q = 0`. So every point of `Q` is trivial, which is `HasSplitSeries 0 Q`.
- `f ≠ 0`. Then `f` is monic. It has positive degree: if `f = 1`, the trivial
  point `1 : V ⟶ V` lies in the kernel, so `1 = f(coord 1) = 0` in `Γ(V, ⊤)`.
  This contradicts injectivity of `coordRingHom`, which sends `1` and `0` of
  `k[X]` to `1` and `0`. By `taylor_mem_span` and the companion lemma
  `taylor_eq_of_mem_span`, `f(X + Y) = f(X) + f(Y)`. So
  `eval₂_add_of_taylor_eq` makes `f` additive on every commutative `k`-algebra,
  and `φ = Grp.homMk (polyEndo f) : V ⟶ V` is a homomorphism. On global
  sections `φ` is the composition map with `f`, transported along `coordEquiv`.
  That map is faithfully flat by `compHom_faithfullyFlat`, and faithful
  flatness is invariant under ring isomorphisms. Mathlib's
  `Flat.flat_and_surjective_iff_faithfullyFlat_of_isAffine` then makes `φ` flat
  and surjective. It is quasi-compact as a morphism of affine schemes. By `mem_range_kernelι_iff_eval` and
  `comp_polyEndo_eq_one_iff`, a point lies in the kernel of `q` exactly when it
  lies in the kernel of `φ`. So `kernelι q ≫ φ = 0` and `kernelι φ ≫ q = 0`.
  Fpqc homomorphism descent along `q` and along `φ` gives `α : Q ⟶ V` with
  `q ≫ α = φ` and `β : V ⟶ Q` with `φ ≫ β = q`. Its uniqueness gives
  `α ≫ β = 𝟙 Q` and `β ≫ α = 𝟙 V`. The published
  `IsVectorGroupOfDimension.of_iso` transports `hV` along `Q ≅ V`.

## Scope

The base is the spectrum of a field. Separatedness of `Q` is used only to make
the kernel inclusion a closed immersion. The unit classifies kernels of flat
surjective homomorphisms out of a one-dimensional vector group through the
additive polynomial `f`, and it proves nothing about subgroups that are not such
kernels.
