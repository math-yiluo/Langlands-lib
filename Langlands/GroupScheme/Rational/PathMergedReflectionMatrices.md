# Supplied merged reflections on normalized path lines

This internal prerequisite works over an arbitrary field `k : Type u`, on the actual affine group scheme with a supplied split pinning. The matrix representation has a finite decidable index type in `Type`, is a supplied highest-weight representation fundamental at the first vertex of a path component, and uses supplied normalized simple-root maps. It retains a supplied adjacent merged root and an arbitrary supplied reflection representative. There is no parity, characteristic, infinitude, perfection or algebraic-closure assumption.

`ReflectionRepresentative.rationalMatrix_mem_representationWeightSpace` specializes the published normalizer transport theorem to any supplied root reflection. The adjacent Cartan pairing identifies the merged root with the reflection of the second adjacent simple root in the first. Conjugating their simple reflections then shows that the merged reflection interchanges the two endpoint weights. The endpoint vectors therefore map into their opposite one-dimensional weight spaces. A nonzero initial vector gives nonzero normalized vectors and hence endpoint scalars in the original field.

For distinct indices `i` and `l`, `pathEndpointReflectionMatrix i l a b` sends the basis vector at `i` to `a` times the vector at `l`, the vector at `l` to `b` times the vector at `i`, and fixes all other basis vectors. Its endpoint block in that order is

```text
0  b
a  0
```

The determinant theorem rewrites this matrix as a diagonal matrix with its rows permuted by the endpoint transposition. The diagonal product is `a * b` and the transposition has sign minus one, so the determinant is `-(a * b)`.

`normalizedPathMatrix_adjacentReflection_of_endpoint_scalars` assumes in addition that the representative lies in the rational subgroup generated by the positive and negative merged-root groups. The published endpoint-root stabilizer theorem fixes every other path vector. Applying the coordinates of the full normalized path basis then gives exactly this endpoint reflection matrix, with `l = i + 2`.

`exists_normalizedPathMatrix_adjacentReflection` combines the endpoint scalar existence with that complete matrix formula. The determinant-one theorem for the generated endpoint root subgroup gives `-(a * b) = 1`, and hence `a * b = -1`. In particular both scalars are nonzero, although the theorem records the product identity directly. The generated-subgroup assumption is explicit; no such membership is asserted for an arbitrary supplied representative.
