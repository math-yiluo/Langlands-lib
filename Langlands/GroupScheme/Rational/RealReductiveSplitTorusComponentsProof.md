## Helper results (namespace `RealSplitTorusComponents`)

- `ringPointHom f : RingPoint ℝ H.X ℝ →* RingPoint ℝ G.X ℝ` is postcomposition with a
  homomorphism `f : H ⟶ G` (`ringPointHom_apply`). It is the Library's `affinePointsTarget`
  read through `affinePointsRingPointMulEquiv`.
- `continuous_ringPointHom`: it is continuous (`continuous_affineSchemePointMap`).
- `ringPointHom_mem_connectedComponent_one`: it maps the identity component into the identity
  component.
- `exists_mem_connectedComponent_one_mul` (the quotient step). Let `q : Z → Q` be a
  homomorphism whose underlying morphism is smooth and which is an fppf quotient of `Z` by the
  image of `s : S → Z`. Assume `Z(ℝ)` is locally connected and `Q(ℝ)` is connected. Then every
  `z ∈ Z(ℝ)` is `c · s(t)` with `c` in the identity component of `Z(ℝ)` and `t ∈ S(ℝ)`.
  Proof: the point map of the smooth morphism `q` is open on real points (the Library's
  `isOpenMap_affineSmoothPointMap`, an inverse function theorem argument over complete
  fields). The identity component of `Z(ℝ)` is open by local connectedness, so its image is an
  open, hence also closed, subgroup of the connected group `Q(ℝ)`. That image is therefore all
  of `Q(ℝ)`. So `q(z) = q(c)` for some `c` in the identity component. Then `c⁻¹ z` lies in
  the kernel of `q`, which is the image of `s` (`mem_range_kernelι_iff` and the kernel clause
  of `RelativeRoot.IsFppfQuotient`).

## Main proof

Let `g ∈ G(ℝ)`.

1. **Centralizer.** Choose a generic cocharacter `ν` of `S`
   (`IsSplitTorus.exists_genericCocharacter`). The Library's closed dynamic centralizer
   `j : Z = Dynamic.Affine.centralizerScheme (ν ≫ i) → G` represents the torus centralizer
   `Z_G(S)` (`Dynamic.Affine.isRepresentedBy_centralizerHom` and the published
   `genericCentralizer`). Let `s : S → Z` be the canonical factor `torusCentralizerFactor`,
   so `s ≫ j = i`. By the published `BruhatTits.Cartan.actualCentralizerStructure`, `Z` is
   connected reductive. The factor `s` is a central maximal split torus of `Z`
   (`isCentral_torusCentralizerFactor`,
   `IsMaximalSplitTorus.isMaximalSplitTorus_torusCentralizerFactor`).
2. **Anisotropic quotient.** The published
   `BruhatTits.RationalPoints.exists_smooth_anisotropic_central_split_quotient` gives a smooth
   affine anisotropic `Q` and a smooth fppf quotient `q : Z → Q` by the image of `s`. By the
   published `centralQuotientReductive`, `Q` is connected reductive. By
   `realAnisotropicCompactConnected` (module
   `Langlands.GroupScheme.Rational.RealAnisotropicCompactConnectedProof`; Borel–Tits 9.4 and
   14.3), `Q(ℝ)` is connected.
3. **Local connectedness.** `Z` is smooth of some relative dimension `n`
   (`smoothConnectedRelativeDimension`), so `Z(ℝ)` is a topological manifold
   (`realSmoothAffineChartedSpace`) and hence locally connected.
4. **Covering.** By the published `exists_relativeRootGenerated_mul_centralizer` (over any
   field), the rational point corresponding to `g` is `h · z`, where `h` is in the subgroup
   `G(ℝ)⁺` generated by the relative root groups and `z` centralizes `i`. Since `j` represents
   the torus centralizer (`centralizerPoints_le_torusCentralizer`), `z = z' ≫ j` for a real
   point `z'` of `Z`. Rational points and real points are identified by
   `RealPointTopology.rationalRingPointMulEquiv`.
5. **Identity components.** `RealPointTopology.rootGenerated_le_identityComponentPreimage`
   (module `Langlands.GroupScheme.Rational.RealPointTopology`) puts `h` in the identity
   component of `G(ℝ)`. The quotient step applied to `z'` gives `z' = c · s(t)` with `c` in the
   identity component of `Z(ℝ)` and `t ∈ S(ℝ)`. The image `j(c)` is in the identity component
   of `G(ℝ)`.
6. **Conclusion.** `g = (h · j(c)) · i(t)`, using `s ≫ j = i`. The first factor lies in the
   identity component `Subgroup.connectedComponentOfOne`. Right multiplication by `i(t)` is
   continuous, so it maps the identity component into the component of `i(t)`. Hence `g` and
   `i(t)` have the same connected component, which is the statement for `s := t`.

## Relation to the source

Borel–Tits (1965), 14.4, printed pp. 146–147 (PDF pages 93–94), reduce to reductive `G` and
then use the connectedness of `(G/P)(ℝ)` for a minimal parabolic `P` (14.2, proved by a
dimension argument). This gives `G(ℝ) = G(ℝ)⁰ P(ℝ)`. They finish with the fibration of
`Z_G(S)` by `S`, whose quotient `M' = Z_G(S)/S` is anisotropic, so `M'(ℝ)` is compact (9.4)
and connected (14.3). The Lean proof replaces 14.2 by the Library's covering
`G(k) = G(k)⁺ · Z_G(S)(k)` (a consequence of the rational Bruhat decomposition) and by the fact
that real points of relative root groups are contracted to `1` along affine lines. It keeps
the source's last step, but uses openness of the smooth quotient map on real points instead of
the exact sequence `M'(ℝ) = Z_G(S)(ℝ)/S(ℝ)` (which would need Hilbert's Theorem 90 for `S`).
