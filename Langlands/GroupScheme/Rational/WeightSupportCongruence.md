# Ambient weight support and cyclic-tail commutators

These internal consequences of the published all-test semigroup constructions
support the proof of `RootSubgroupVectorStatement`. They work over an arbitrary
field at every universe. They do not prove that target or the source semigroup
commutator theorem.

`AgreeOnAmbientWeights b A A'` is the proposition that, for every character
in `actionWeightSupport b ⊤`, membership in `A` is equivalent to membership
in `A'`. It compares the two semigroups only on weights of the actual
ambient tangent action. The generator, subgroup and weight-span congruence
lemmas use this predicate as their hypothesis.

The imported `actionWeightSupport_mono` result from `SemigroupClosure` (in the
`Langlands.GroupScheme.RelativeRoot` namespace) says that inclusion of subgroup
functors preserves their actual tangent weight support. Applying it to the
inclusion into the top subgroup proves that every subgroup's support lies
in the ambient support. An ambient weight
occurs exactly when its action weight submodule is nonzero. Therefore two
additive semigroups selecting the same ambient weights have exactly the same
admissible smooth connected invariant generators. Taking the subgroup closure
of their points on each test proves equality of the generated functors. The
corresponding weight spans agree too: a weight space outside ambient support
is zero, and each nonzero summand occurs on both sides.

The sum of the positive tails indexed by `r` and `s` lies in the tail indexed
by `r + s`, directly by adding the two positive integer coefficients. The
cyclic semigroup generated by `2*a` lies in the second tail for `a`. If no
multiple `n*a` with `n >= 3` is an ambient action weight, the two semigroups
select the same ambient support, so their generated functors agree.

The final two lemmas assume the existing
`SemigroupCommutatorStatement` explicitly. Its all-test commutator bound,
followed by the tail-sum inclusion, places each commutator in the tail
indexed by `r + s`. If that actual subgroup functor is trivial, the
commutator is the identity on every test, hence the original points commute.
This supplies a centrality bridge without identifying an fppf quotient with
a quotient of individual test-point groups.

The provenance is the definitions in `WeightSemigroup`, the imported
`actionWeightSupport_mono` theorem in `SemigroupClosure`, and the exact
conditional commutator target in `WeightSemigroupStatement`; these are
derived set, subgroup, and group calculations. No additional geometric or
root-system theorem is asserted.
