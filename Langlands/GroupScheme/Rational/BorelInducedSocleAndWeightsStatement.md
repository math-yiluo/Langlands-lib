# The simple socle and the weight bounds of opposite-Borel induction

`Langlands.GroupScheme.SplitBorelInducedSocleAndWeightsStatement` is a proposition, not a proof, for Milne, *Algebraic Groups*, 2022 corrected reprint, Proposition 22.27(b,c), printed p. 471. It uses the simple-root order and dominance of §22a and Definition 22.1 (p. 464), and the induced space of Definition 22.21, including its footnote on test algebras (p. 469).

## The whole induced representation

Take a split pinning `p` of a connected reductive group scheme `G` over a field `k`, in universe `u`. The torus is `p.torus` and the Borel containing it determines `p.base`. The pinning supplies the based root datum and the actual opposite Borel inclusion `p.negativeBorelHom`; the root coordinates of the pinning are not additional hypotheses of the source. `[IsAffineHom G.X.hom]` expresses affineness, part of connected reductivity. The opposite Borel is the dynamic parabolic of the reverse of the pinning's strictly positive cocharacter. For `lam` in the character lattice of the torus, `p.borelCharacter lam` is its extension \(\chi\) to \(B^-\) through the limit map \(B^-\to T\).

Milne's \(E(\lambda)\) consists of all \(f\in\mathcal O(G)\) with \(f(xb)=f(x)\chi(b)^{-1}\) and has left regular action \((a\cdot f)(x)=f(a^{-1}x)\). The covariance equation is tested on all \(k\)-algebras, equivalently on all test schemes by restricting to affine opens. Composition with inversion, \(F(x)=f(x^{-1})\), identifies this entire space with the space satisfying \(F(bx)=\chi(b)F(x)\), and transports the action to \((a\cdot F)(x)=F(xa)\). The space equivalence is `HomogeneousQuotientFromEmbedding.rightCovariantCoordinateEquiv` at the inverse character; `HomogeneousQuotientFromEmbedding.inducedRepresentation_mul_basis` describes the translated action on its chosen basis. Thus `inducedRepresentation` is the full induced space in finite matrix coordinates, not a preselected highest-weight subspace.

`D` is a supplied `HomogeneousQuotient` for the opposite Borel inclusion. Its properness is used by the published construction of the finite basis. This is a choice of quotient model: `SplitPinning.isGeometricBorelSubgroup_negativeBorelHom` makes this a parabolic subgroup, and `smoothSubgroupQuotientExistence` and `parabolicSubgroupQuotientProper` give a proper model for the smooth opposite Borel. The proposition applies to any such model; it does not prove quotient existence.

## Conclusions

- **(b)** If `p.IsDominantWeight lam`, there is a simple stable subspace `W`, and every simple stable subspace of the whole inducer equals `W`. This is uniqueness as a subspace, not merely uniqueness of an isomorphism class as in Theorem 22.19. Stability is for the actual group representation, not just its rational points. `StableSubspaceRepresentation` gives coordinates for the actual inclusion with image `W`. In those coordinates `p.IsHighestWeight` asserts irreducibility, the full torus weight decomposition, dimension one at `lam`, and domination of every occurring weight by `lam`.
- **(c)** Each nonzero restricted torus weight space of the whole inducer, of weight `μ`, satisfies \(w_0\lambda\leq\mu\leq\lambda\). The inequalities are `lam - μ` and `μ - w.val • lam` in `p.positiveRootMonoid`, the nonnegative integral span of the **simple** roots. The lower bound applies to any Weyl element sending the positive roots to the negative roots (the characterization of \(w_0\)); the proposition does not assert existence of that element. There is no separate dominance premise for these bounds, which quantify only over occurring weights.

The 2017 printing used induction from \(B\) in Definition 22.21 (p. 468), with highest weight \(w_0\lambda\) and an upper bound by that weight in 22.27(b,c) (p. 470). Its errata snapshot of 2026-11-02, entry for p. 468, records the switch to the opposite Borel. This statement takes the opposite-Borel convention and both weight bounds from the corrected reprint; the reprint errata snapshot of 2026-11-04 has no entry for pp. 464, 469 or 471.
