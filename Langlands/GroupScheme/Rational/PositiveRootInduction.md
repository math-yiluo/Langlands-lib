# Positive-root induction and standard-cocharacter signs

This internal helper develops the combinatorial part of the general
standard-cocharacter comparison. Its provenance is derived directly from the
published definitions in `Rational.StandardParabolic` and the integer-valued
bilinear lattice pairing in `Torus.Lattice`.

`HaveSameRelativeRootSigns i zeta eta` is an internal comparison property:
for every actual relative root the pairings with the two cocharacters have
equivalent nonnegative, zero, and positive signs. It is an equivalence relation,
as follows pointwise from reflexivity, symmetry, and transitivity of logical
equivalence. The property deliberately contains no dynamic subgroup equality.

The pairing with the fixed cocharacter is a strictly positive integer on each
positive relative root. If such a root is not simple, the definition supplies
two positive roots adding to it. Each has smaller pairing. Strong induction on
the natural value of the pairing therefore reduces every positive relative root
to sums of the published simple relative roots. No root-system hypothesis,
finiteness, reducedness, genericity or reductivity is used in this induction.

For a standard cocharacter, each simple root has nonnegative pairing. Additivity
extends this to every positive relative root. A positive root has zero pairing
exactly when it lies in the additive monoid generated by `J`: at a decomposition
step the two nonnegative summands of a zero pairing must both be zero. Conversely
the pairing vanishes on `J` and hence on its generated additive monoid.

Nonnegativity and the zero characterization show that a positive root pairs
strictly positively exactly outside that monoid. Two standard cocharacters for
the same `J` therefore have the same nonnegative, zero, and positive signs on
all positive roots.

The all-root comparison and additive-group generation theorem explicitly assume
both genericity of the fixed cocharacter and negation stability of the relative
roots. Genericity makes each root either positive or negative. For a negative
root, its opposite is positive by negation stability; bilinearity negates each
pairing. This proves the sign comparison for every root and generation of the
relative roots by the simple ones as an additive group. Negation stability is
not proved here and is not implicit in these conclusions.

The theorem `IsStandardCocharacter.haveSameRelativeRootSigns` packages the
all-root comparison in the internal property. All declarations retain the
field universe and allow arbitrary fields and affine ambient group schemes;
the combinatorial results require no connectedness or reductivity.

This helper does not assert existence of standard cocharacters, negation
stability of the relative roots, or equality of dynamic subgroup functors.
Those remain separate structural obligations of the task.
