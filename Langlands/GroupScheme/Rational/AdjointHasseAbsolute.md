# Reduction of the adjoint Hasse kernel to absolutely almost-simple groups

`NumberFieldAbsolutelyAlmostSimpleAdjointGaloisHasseKernelStatement`
asserts that an everywhere locally trivial natural cocycle is trivial
for an absolutely almost-simple adjoint group over a number field. The
cocycle uses the given finite Galois field cover and the full pullbacks
of that cover at all places. All the fields lie in the same universe.

The internal reduction takes this arithmetic premise and the affine
adjoint Weil restriction statement. For an almost-simple adjoint group,
noncommutativity gives a nontrivial point on some test scheme. Both
adjointness and nontriviality pass to every geometric fibre of its
field base. The affine Weil presentation therefore expresses it as a
Weil restriction from a finite étale algebra with adjoint almost-simple
geometric fibres. The finite étale algebra over the number field is
reduced Artinian, hence the product of its actual maximal residue
fields. Those fields are finite extensions of the base number field.

The group over each residue field is adjoint by the field-point
comparison for adjointness. Its base change to an algebraic closure is
almost simple by extension from the geometric fibre. More explicitly,
the residue-field embedding into the specified field extends to a map
between algebraic closures, and almost simplicity ascends along this
map of algebraically closed fields. Field-extension reflection gives
almost simplicity on the original residue field as well.

The comparison for affine Weil restriction from a product algebra
identifies the given group with the product of the restrictions from
the individual fields. Project the cocycle to each factor, transfer
it along the appropriate Weil restriction and refine to a finite
Galois cover over the factor's number field. The arithmetic premise
gives triviality there, which the transfer reflects. Assemble the
coordinate trivializations and transport them back across the group
isomorphism. The previous adjoint product reduction then gives the
full adjoint kernel from the two stated premises.
