# Weyl stabilizers of rational character subspaces

Let `T` be a maximal split torus in a connected reductive group over an
arbitrary field. For any rational subspace `W` of the rationalized actual
character lattice, consider the integral cocharacters pairing to zero with
every integral character whose rationalization belongs to `W`.

`WeylSubspaceStabilizerStatement` asserts that the pointwise stabilizer of
these cocharacters in the actual relative Weyl group is exactly the subgroup
generated by integral root reflections in relative roots whose rationalizations
belong to `W`. `weylSubspaceStabilizer` proves this uniform proposition.
The subspace need not be generated by roots. Nonreduced relative root systems,
rank zero, the zero subspace and the whole character space are included.

The proof uses finite zero-set detection on the actual relative roots:
one integral cocharacter annihilates `W` and vanishes on a relative root exactly
when its rationalization lies in `W`. A Weyl element fixing the whole
annihilator fixes this cocharacter. The cocharacter stabilizer theorem then
expresses it using precisely the required root reflections. Conversely, each
of those reflections preserves the pairing with each annihilating cocharacter;
perfectness of the split-torus pairing identifies this with fixing the
cocharacter.

`mem_rootSubsetWeylGroup_roots_in_subspace_of_displacement_mem` gives a useful
consequence: if the rationalization of `w χ - χ` lies in `W` for every actual
character `χ`, then `w` belongs to that root-reflection subgroup. Pairing each
such displacement with an annihilating cocharacter makes all the pairings
invariant, so the preceding theorem applies.

The convex-subset corollary specializes to the rational span of the symmetric
part of a convex relative-root subset. Convex symmetric-span saturation
identifies the roots in this span with the symmetric part itself. Hence an
element fixing every integral cocharacter annihilating that part lies in its
Weyl subgroup.

This internal derivation combines the finite zero-set choice, cocharacter
stabilizer and convex symmetric-span results. It makes no assertion that the
annihilator of the span of an intersection equals a sum of annihilators, and
does not prove a group-intersection formula for arbitrary closed root subsets.
