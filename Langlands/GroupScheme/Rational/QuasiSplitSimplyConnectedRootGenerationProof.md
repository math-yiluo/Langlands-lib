## What the theorem says

Let `k : Type u` be any field. Let `G` be a group scheme over `Spec k` with an affine
structure morphism (`[IsAffineHom G.X.hom]`) such that `IsSimplyConnected G` and
`IsQuasiSplit G` hold. Let `i : S.toGrp ⟶ G` be any maximal split torus
(`IsMaximalSplitTorus i`). Then

```text
relativeRootGeneratedSubgroup i = ⊤.
```

So `G(k)` is generated by the rational points
`(RelativeRoot.subgroup i a).carrier (Over.mk (𝟙 _))` of the Library's relative-root subgroup
functors, for `a ∈ relativeRoots i`.

The theorem has no hypotheses beyond those of the proposition. It makes no assumption on the
characteristic, the cardinality or the perfectness of `k`. It applies to finite fields, to
semisimple groups that are not `k`-simple, to the trivial group and to relative rank zero. It
assumes no generation, centralizer-containment or root-group comparison premise. It uses no
unproved Library statement as a premise. Its axioms are `propext`, `Classical.choice` and
`Quot.sound`.

## Proof

The proof term is the published theorem

```lean
quasiSplitSimplyConnectedRootGeneration : QuasiSplitSimplyConnectedRootGenerationStatement.{u}
```

of module `Langlands.GroupScheme.Rational.QuasiSplitCentralizerRootGeneration`, by Worker 4348.
It is imported by its Lean name. Its type is exactly the target proposition, so the module
applies it with no further argument.

That theorem has the following route. See its own Markdown for the details.

1. The published covering corollary
   `relativeRootGeneratedSubgroup_eq_top_of_centralizerPoints_le` (module
   `Rational/RootGeneratedCentralizerCovering`) holds for every connected reductive `G`. It
   reduces `G(k)^+ = G(k)` to the inclusion `Z(k) ⊆ G(k)^+`. Here `Z(k)` is
   `centralizerPoints i`, the rational points of the centralizer of `S`. A quasi-split group
   is connected reductive (`IsQuasiSplit.isConnectedReductive`).
2. `centralizerPoints_le_relativeRootGeneratedSubgroup_of_simplyConnected` proves that inclusion
   when `G` is quasi-split and simply connected. A closed torus `T` represents the centralizer
   of the supplied maximal split torus. A finite Galois extension `K` splits `T`. A pinning of
   `G_K` compatible with a regular cocharacter carries a Galois action on the simple roots.
   Simple connectedness makes the simple coroots span the cocharacters of `T_K`. So every
   rational point of `T` is the image of a Galois-equivariant product of simple-coroot points.
   This product is decomposed over the Galois orbits of simple roots. Orthogonal orbits are
   handled by rank-one descent. Orbits of type `A_2` are handled by the quadratic fixed-field
   unit decomposition. Injectivity of scalar extension then puts the original point in
   `G(k)^+`.

## Source

V. Platonov and A. Rapinchuk, *Algebraic Groups and Number Theory* (1994), §7.2, printed
p. 406 / PDF p. 416 (registered as `platonov-rapinchuk-1994-algebraic-groups-number-theory`)
recalls `G(k)^+ = G(k)` for simply connected quasi-split groups and attributes it to Steinberg.
The statement's Markdown explains how the Lean proposition is derived from that equality. The
Lean proof above is internal. It does not follow the source's proof, and it applies directly
to semisimple simply connected groups rather than only to absolutely simple ones.
