# Brauer induction for relative Weil groups

Author: Worker 84. Statement contribution to task
`34b81620-20d7-4059-9b08-6bedd1c1c029`, revision 1. It supplies the Brauer step
of the Galois-side global functional equation (published target
`Langlands.Global.FunctionalEquationComparison.RelativeWeilTwistFunctionalEquationStatement`).

The module is in the namespace `Langlands.ClassField.Global.RelativeWeilGroup`.
It has one abbreviation for a subgroup, one short proved openness theorem,
an abbreviation for the virtual group, one set definition and two proposition
definitions. The theorem `isOpen_galoisPreimage` proves only that a subgroup is
open. No generation result is proved here and nothing is assumed.

The target is `InducedCharacterGenerationStatement`. Let `C_E` be the idele class
group of `E`. For every class family `c` and every finite Galois extension `E/F` of
number fields, it says that the classes induced from continuous characters of the
open subgroups `W_H ⊇ C_E` of `W_{E/F}` generate the exact virtual group of
continuous finite-dimensional complex representations of `W_{E/F}` over `ℤ`.

## Declarations

The variables are `c : FundamentalClass.ClassFamily.{u}`, number fields
`F E : Type u` with `[Algebra F E]` and `[IsGalois F E]`, and the published
`RelativeWeilGroup c F E`, written `W_{E/F}`. It is the published topological
extension `1 → C_E → W_{E/F} → Gal(E/F) → 1` of the class `c F E`. Here
`projection c F E : W_{E/F} →* (E ≃ₐ[F] E)` is the published projection, and the
automorphism group has the published discrete topology `relativeGaloisTopology`,
which is a local instance.

- `galoisPreimage c F E H` (abbreviation), for `H : Subgroup (E ≃ₐ[F] E)`, is
  `H.comap (projection c F E)`, i.e. the inverse image `W_H` of `H` in `W_{E/F}`.
  It contains the image of `C_E` (the published `exact`). Since `Gal(E/F)` is
  finite, its index is finite. The Mathlib instances
  `Subgroup.finiteIndex_of_finite` (for `H`) and the `FiniteIndex` instance for
  `Subgroup.comap` (in `Mathlib/GroupTheory/Index.lean`) supply `FiniteIndex`. Every subgroup of
  `W_{E/F}` containing the image of `C_E` is such an inverse image, namely of its
  own image in `Gal(E/F)`.
- `isOpen_galoisPreimage c F E H` (theorem, proved): `W_H` is open, because the
  published `continuousProjection` is continuous into a discrete group.
- `ContinuousVirtual c F E` (abbreviation) is the published
  `ExactGrothendieckGroup` of the predicate `IsContinuous` on
  `FiniteRepresentation.{0, u, max u uV} ℂ W_{E/F}`. This is the free abelian
  group on isomorphism classes of finite-dimensional complex representations
  whose action map `W_{E/F} → End(V)` is continuous (published `IsContinuous`,
  module topology on endomorphisms). It is taken modulo `[V] = [V'] + [V'']` for
  every short exact sequence `0 → V' → V → V'' → 0` of such representations.
  This is Tate's `R(W_{E/F})` (1979, §2, p.9). The carrier universe is
  `max u uV`, the carrier universe of induction from carrier universe `max u uV`.
- `inducedCharacterGenerators c F E` is the set of elements
  `continuousInduce (galoisPreimage c F E H) (isOpen_galoisPreimage c F E H)
  (characterClass θ)`, for a subgroup `H` of `Gal(E/F)` and a continuous
  homomorphism `θ : W_H →ₜ* ℂˣ`. Here `W_H` has the subspace topology.
  `characterClass θ` is the published exact class of the character line `θ` (carrier
  `ULift ℂ`, universe `max u uV`). The published `continuousInduce` is finite-index
  induction (`W_H`-equivariant functions on `W_{E/F}` with values in the
  representation of `W_H`) on continuous exact virtual groups. So each generator is
  `[Ind_{W_H}^{W_{E/F}} θ]`.
- `InducedCharacterGeneration c F E` is
  `Submodule.span ℤ (inducedCharacterGenerators c F E) = ⊤`: every element of
  `ContinuousVirtual c F E` is a finite `ℤ`-linear combination, with signs, of
  generators.
- `InducedCharacterGenerationStatement` quantifies `InducedCharacterGeneration`
  over all `c`, `F`, `E` as above. It has universe parameters `u` (fields) and `uV`.

No hypothesis is placed on `c`. The statement does not ask for
`IsArithmeticClassFamily c`, for the published restriction compatibility
`FundamentalClass.Restriction c`, or for semisimplicity of representations.

## Relation to the source

Tate (1979), Proposition (2.3.1), p.11, states that `R(W_F)` is generated by the
classes `Ind_{E'/F}[χ]` for finite extensions `E'/F` and quasi-characters `χ` of
`W_{E'}`. The second part, about the degree-zero subgroup, is not used here. Tate
works with the absolute Weil group and every finite separable `E'/F`. The target
here is a derived relative form, and differs from the source as follows.

1. **Relative group.** The group is `W_{E/F}` for one finite Galois `E/F`, not
   `W_F`, and only the subgroups `W_H`, `H ≤ Gal(E/F)`, are used. When `c`
   satisfies `FundamentalClass.Restriction c`, the published
   `range_restrictionInclusion` identifies the range of the published
   `restrictionInclusion c F E K h : W_{E/K} →ₜ* W_{E/F}`, for an intermediate
   field `K`, with `W_H` for `H = Gal(E/K)`. That inclusion is an open embedding
   (published `isOpenEmbedding_restrictionInclusion`). By the Galois
   correspondence every `H` is of this form. So under that hypothesis the
   generators are `Ind_{W_{E/K}}^{W_{E/F}} θ` for `F ⊆ K ⊆ E` and quasi-characters
   `θ` of `W_{E/K}`. These are the
   generators of (2.3.1) with `E' = K` inside `E`. The target states the
   group-theoretic form, which does not need that hypothesis.
2. **Why the source proof does not transfer verbatim.** Tate's proof uses
   (2.2.3), p.10. A primitive irreducible representation of `W_F` is
   `σ ⊗ χ`, with `σ` of Galois type. This uses Tate's [S3] lifting of a
   projective representation of `Gal(E/F)` to a linear representation of
   `G_F`, which need not factor through `Gal(E/F)`. It also uses the degree-zero
   Brauer theorem for `G_F`. Both steps leave the group `W_{E/F}`: Tate's
   generators may be induced from fields not contained in `E`. The relative form
   therefore needs the following argument, which never leaves `W_{E/F}`.
3. **Why the target is true.** Composition series reduce every class to classes of
   irreducible continuous representations `ρ`, since subquotients of continuous
   representations are continuous. Clifford's theorem (finite-dimensional `ρ`,
   abelian normal subgroup `C_E`) makes `ρ|_{C_E}` a sum of continuous characters.
   Let `η` be one of them, `H` the stabilizer of `η` in `Gal(E/F)`, and `V_η` the
   `η`-isotypic part. Then `ρ ≅ Ind_{W_H}^{W_{E/F}} V_η` and `V_η` is irreducible.
   On `V_η`, `C_E` acts by the scalar `η`. Push the image of `W_H` into the
   subgroup `G = ρ(W_H)·ℂ^×` of `GL(V_η)`. Then `G/ℂ^×` is a quotient of `H`, the
   subgroup `H₀ = G ∩ SL(V_η)` is finite, and `G = H₀·ℂ^×`. The finite Brauer
   theorem for `H₀` writes `V_η|_{H₀}` as a signed sum of representations induced
   from linear characters of subgroups of `H₀`. These subgroups may be enlarged
   to contain the finite group `μ = H₀ ∩ ℂ^×` of scalars. Now `G` is the quotient
   of `H₀ × ℂ^×` by the finite central subgroup of pairs `(ζ, ζ^{-1})`, `ζ ∈ μ`.
   Tensor the decomposition with the identity character of `ℂ^×` and keep the
   part on which that central subgroup acts trivially. This gives a decomposition
   of `V_η` as a representation of `G`, induced from characters of subgroups
   containing `ℂ^×`. Pull back along `ρ`. Every subgroup of `G` containing `ℂ^×`
   pulls back to some `W_{H'}` with `H' ≤ H`. Mackey's formula has a single double
   coset because `ρ(W_H)ℂ^× = G`. The pulled-back characters are continuous,
   since `ℂ^×` is open in each finite-index subgroup of `G` containing it.
   Transitivity of induction finishes the argument. After the negative terms are
   moved across, every identity used is an isomorphism of genuine continuous
   representations, so it holds in the exact virtual group. This is only a sketch
   of why the statement is true; the proof is a separate obligation.
4. **Coefficients and topology.** As in Tate, the coefficients are integers with
   signs. Quasi-characters are continuous homomorphisms into `ℂ^×`, with no
   unitarity condition. Representations are continuous in Tate's sense (§2,
   p.9), and no semisimplicity is assumed. The virtual group is defined by exact
   sequences, as in Tate's `R(G)`.

The local p-adic analogue of (2.3.1) is the published
`Langlands.LocalFactor.Inductive.GenerationStatement`. The finite-group input
is the published proved `Langlands.FiniteRep.BrauerTheory.finiteBrauerTheory`, whose spanning
conjunct is `IntegralBrauerSpanningStatement`.

## Intended use

In the Galois-side global functional equation, apply this statement to
`ρ = S ⊗ S'` for continuous representations `S`, `S'` of `W_{E/F}`. It writes `[ρ]`
as `Σ n_i [Ind_{W_{E/K_i}}^{W_{E/F}} θ_i]`. Each `θ_i` then corresponds, by the
published transfer reciprocity statement, to a Hecke character of `K_i`, and the
global factors of `ρ` become products of Hecke L-functions (Tate 1979, §3.5;
Henniart 2000, §2.3). That reduction is a separate obligation.
