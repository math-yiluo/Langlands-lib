# Cofiltered elements and points of generated sites

Author: Worker 265.

This internal categorical module concerns an arbitrary category `C`, a
functor `F : C ⥤ Type w`, and a precoverage `J`. Its object, morphism and
value universes are independent. All its constructions are derived from
Mathlib's category of elements, cofiltered categories and generated
Grothendieck topologies, together with the published
`Langlands.CategoryTheory.PointwiseCovering` module. No external
mathematical assertion is assumed.

`HasCommonPointedRefinements F` asks for a common source carrying a point
with both prescribed images, for any two objects and their points.
`HasEqualizingPointedRefinements F` asks that parallel arrows agreeing on
a prescribed point become equal after precomposition with an arrow
through which that point lifts. `LiftsPrecoverage F J` says that the
arrows of every presieve belonging to `J` are jointly surjective on `F`.

`isCofiltered_elements_of_refinements` combines the first two conditions
with nonemptiness of the category of elements. A common pointed
refinement gives a cone over two elements. An equalizing pointed
refinement gives a morphism of elements equalizing two parallel
morphisms; equality of their underlying arrows suffices by extensionality
in the category of elements.

`hasPointwiseLiftingSquares_of_isCofilteredElements` assumes that the
category of elements is cofiltered or empty. A compatible pair of points
and a cospan give a cospan in that category. Mathlib's cofiltered-cospan
lemma gives a commuting cone of elements. Forgetting the elements gives
the required square, and the point carried by its vertex has the two
prescribed images. Nonemptiness is unnecessary for this implication.

`isJointlySurjective_of_mem_toGrothendieck_of_lifts` uses pointwise lifting
squares and `LiftsPrecoverage` to prove joint surjectivity for every
covering sieve in the topology generated by `J`. It inducts on the
generated topology. In the generator case it uses the inclusion of the
presieve into its generated sieve. The top, pullback and transitivity
cases use the corresponding published pointwise-covering lemmas.

The final theorem combines this argument with cofilteredness of the
category of elements. In particular, the generating precoverage need
not itself have been defined from `F`: generators defined using another
point functor can be used once their surjectivity on `F` is proved.
These are conditional categorical results, not existence assertions for
analytic fibre products, equalizing neighbourhoods, or field-valued
point lifts.
