# Picard groups of braided monoidal categories

This is an internal categorical abstraction of the tensor-inverse and isomorphism-class description in the Stacks Project, Section 17.25 (01CR), Lemma 17.25.2 and Definition 17.25.9 (01CX). The source discusses modules on a ringed space, not arbitrary braided monoidal categories. This file supplies the latter construction and does not identify it with a scheme Picard group or prove a local rank-one criterion.

`TensorInvertible X` means that there exists an object `Y` and an isomorphism `X ⊗ Y ≅ 𝟙_ C`. The predicate itself only requires a monoidal category. Its unit, transport across an isomorphism, and preservation by strong monoidal functors are constructed using the unitors and the strong monoidal tensor/unit isomorphisms.

For a braided monoidal category, `tensorInvertible_iff_isUnit` identifies this predicate with being a unit in Mathlib's `Skeleton C`. In the forward direction a tensor inverse supplies an inverse skeleton class; in the reverse direction a representative of the inverse skeleton class supplies an inverse object. Braiding makes the skeleton monoid commutative, so a right inverse suffices. Closure under tensor follows from multiplication of units.

`PicardGroup C` is `Additive ((Skeleton C)ˣ)`. Its additive commutative group instance is supplied by Mathlib's constructed commutative monoid on the skeleton, the group of units, and the additive type tag. Thus the group laws follow from the category's associator, unitors and braiding through Mathlib's skeleton construction; no group laws are extra input assumptions. The object and morphism universes are arbitrary and independent. The Picard group has the object universe; no universe shrinking or essential smallness theorem is asserted.

`ofObject` sends an invertible object to its class. `toSkeletonClass` is injective, and `representative` chooses an object in a class. `representative_invertible` and `ofObject_representative` prove that all group elements come from invertible objects. `ofObject_eq_iff` characterizes equality of constructed classes by existence of an isomorphism of their objects; `eq_iff` gives the corresponding characterization using chosen representatives. Consequently the choice of inverse witness introduces no extra class data.

`ofObject_unit` and `ofObject_tensor` identify zero with the unit object and addition with tensor product. `representativeTensorNegIso` constructs an isomorphism from the tensor product of representatives of a class and its negative to the unit. All these helper proofs reduce to equality of skeleton classes and the standard units identities.

`PicardGroup.map F` is the additive version of the units homomorphism induced by `Skeleton.monoidHom F`. It requires strong monoidality, but no compatibility with the braidings. Its defining homomorphism laws are inherited from the actual monoid homomorphism. `map_ofObject` proves that the map sends the class of `X` to the class of `F.obj X`. Identity, composition and invariance under natural isomorphism are separate statement/proof targets.
