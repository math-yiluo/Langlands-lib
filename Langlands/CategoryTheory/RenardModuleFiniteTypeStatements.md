# Finite type in Renard's unital module-category example

This statement-only module gives two closed propositions and their
conjunction in namespace `Langlands.Renard.ModuleFiniteType`. It proves
neither comparison. A proof belongs to a separate successor task.

## Objects and universes

Each component binds all of its inputs internally, in this order:

```lean
∀ (R : Type uR) [Ring R] (M : ModuleCat.{uM} R), ...
```

The ring universe `uR` and the module universe `uM` are independent. All
three declarations have universe parameters in the order `uR uM`.
`ModuleCat.{uM} R` is Mathlib's actual category of ordinary unital left
`R`-modules with actual `R`-linear maps. Its Hom universe is `uM`. The
ring may be noncommutative, and it may be the zero ring. No field,
Noetherianity, finite dimension, countability or finite generation is an
input assumption.

This category is Renard's `M(A)` for a unital ring `A`. On printed5/PDF11,
`A−mod` denotes the category of left `A`-modules. On printed6/PDF12,
modules over a unital ring are not assumed unital a priori. There `M(A)` is
defined as the full subcategory of `A−mod` whose objects are the unital
modules, meaning that the unit acts as the identity (footnote on PDF11).
The remark and definition on printed7/PDF13 repeat that, for a unital
ring, `M(A)` is the category of unital left modules. This module uses
`ModuleCat R` directly. It does not construct Renard's larger category of
possibly nonunital modules or embed `ModuleCat R` into it.

## The statements

| Declaration in `Langlands.Renard.ModuleFiniteType` | Asserts, for every `R` and `M` as above |
| --- | --- |
| `FiniteTypeStatement` | `Langlands.Renard.IsFiniteTypeObject M ↔ Module.Finite R M` |
| `FiniteSubcoverStatement` | `Langlands.Renard.HasFiniteSubcoverProperty M ↔ Module.Finite R M` |
| `ModuleFiniteTypeStatement` | `FiniteTypeStatement.{uR, uM} ∧ FiniteSubcoverStatement.{uR, uM}` |

`ModuleFiniteTypeStatement` is the conjunction of the two complete closed
propositions, in that order. Each conjunct carries its own quantifiers.
`Module.Finite R M` is Mathlib's algebraic finite type: the top submodule
of the underlying module is generated by a finite set.

## The imported source-facing predicates

`IsFiniteTypeObject` and `HasFiniteSubcoverProperty` are imported unchanged
from the published `Langlands.CategoryTheory.RenardFiniteType`. Here they
are applied in the category `ModuleCat.{uM} R`. Their subobjects are
Mathlib's actual `CategoryTheory.Subobject M`, which are isomorphism classes
of monomorphisms into `M`. They are ordered by factorization of the
inclusion arrows. No submodule lattice or other surrogate order is
substituted for this order.

Both predicates begin with `HasSubobjectUnions M`: every subset of
`Subobject M` has a least upper bound, including the empty subset. Finite
type then requires that a nonempty chain of proper subobjects cannot have
the top subobject as its least upper bound. The finite-subcover property
requires that each subset with least upper bound top contains a finite
subset with the same least upper bound. That finite subset may be empty.
Properness is inequality of the subobject class with top.

The source speaks of families `(A_i)` and finite subfamilies. The predicates
quantify over subsets of `Subobject M`. A family is represented by its
range, which has the same upper bounds.

Neither component replaces a source-facing predicate by ordinary finite
generation, categorical finite presentability, compactness in the subobject
lattice or another category. The subobject–submodule correspondence of
Mathlib, `ModuleCat.subobjectModule`, is a natural proof route. It is not
part of either statement.

## Source and the two components

The governing original is David Renard, *Représentations des groupes
réductifs p-adiques*, French version dated 9 July 2026, registered source
`renard-2026-representations-groupes-reductifs-padiques`, SHA256
`7b539996793b7c3620e72e1e389588eb42d1a8e91d6f68b2eead5c2e2ffc182c`.
The retained file is
`/home/taskhub/central-library/Renard--Representations des groupes reductifs p-adiques (2026 version)--2026.pdf`.
In this file the PDF page is the printed page plus six. Appendix A.I.2–A.I.3,
printed248–249/PDF254–255, was read in full and visually checked on the
rendered pages. The module conventions on PDF11–13 were also read.

A.I.2 (PDF254) defines subobjects, their order and unions of arbitrary
families. A.I.3 assumes that the category has arbitrary unions. It calls
`X` of finite type if, for every chain of proper subobjects, the union is
again proper. The next sentences, running from printed248 onto the top of
printed249 (PDF254–255), characterize finite type in such a general
category by compactness. `X` is to be of finite type if every family of
subobjects with union `X` has a finite subfamily with union `X`, citing
[37] 4.10, Theorem 1. Next come a paragraph relating Noetherian objects to
subobjects of finite type and the one-sentence definition of a Noetherian
category.

The `Exemple` closes A.I.3 on printed249/PDF255. Its first sentence says
that a module `M` over a unital ring `A` is of finite type in the category
`M(A)` if and only if `M` is of finite type over `A` in the algebraic
sense, citing [25], Lemma 3.6.1. `FiniteTypeStatement` formalizes exactly
this sentence.

The general compactness paragraph is not a statement about modules, and this
unit does not formalize it in a general category. `FiniteSubcoverStatement`
combines that paragraph's characterization with the module example, but
only in the same actual category `ModuleCat.{uM} R`. It says that the
finite-subcover property of `M` in this category is also equivalent to
algebraic finite type. The general chain/finite-cover equivalence is
neither asserted nor used.

The second sentence of the `Exemple` says that `M(A)` is Noetherian
exactly when `A` is Noetherian. It is not stated here.

## Corrections retained in the imported predicates

The imported definition unit documents its internal repairs to the printed
wording. These repairs are diagnoses checked against the rendered
original. They are not author-issued errata. This unit adds no further
repair. Three of them affect the predicates used here.

1. **Cover equation.** At the top of PDF255 the compactness paragraph
   prints the cover hypothesis as `⋃_i A_i = A`. The object being
   characterized, and the concluding equation `⋃_j A_{i_j} = X`, use `X`.
   The hypothesis is therefore read with right side `X`. In the subobject
   order it becomes `IsLUB S ⊤`.
2. **Nonempty chains.** The finite-type predicate quantifies only over
   nonempty chains, and finite subcovers may be empty. For the zero module
   the empty union is already the top subobject. If the empty chain were
   admitted, the zero module could not be of finite type, although it is
   finitely generated. That would contradict the `Exemple`, in particular
   over the zero ring, where every unital module is zero.
3. **Properness.** The source glosses a proper subobject as one not
   isomorphic to `X`. The predicates use inequality with the top
   subobject, which is equivalent to the inclusion arrow not being an
   isomorphism. Over a nonzero ring the two readings differ for modules.
   In a free module of infinite rank, a proper direct summand can be
   abstractly isomorphic to the whole module.

The fourth repair, of the variable called maximal in the
Noetherian-object definition, concerns `IsNoetherianObject`. That predicate
is not used here.

The public note `notes/renard/appendix-a-corrections.md` was consulted. Its
three discrepancies concern A.III.1, A.IV.1–A.IV.2 and A.VI.1, not A.I.3.
No errata file for the 2026 edition, such as
`notes/errata/renard-2026-representations-groupes-reductifs-padiques.md`,
was found in the published or project notes. The supplementary registered
author errata `renard-2010-corrections-complements`, SHA256
`fdf3c88716c294c451c1f6fad67f6b99c95dae670aae0b8ad191ab0a96e69863`, were
read at PDF18–19 when the imported predicates were authored. Those pages
do not address these A.I.3 conventions. This partial reading of the errata
to the 2010 edition does not replace the 2026 text.

Renard cites [25], Lemma 3.6.1 for the example and [37], 4.10 Theorem 1
for compactness. Neither reference was read for this unit, and neither
supplies verified proof evidence here.

## Scope

These are statements about published definitions in Mathlib's actual
unital module category. They construct no alternative category and assume
no correspondence between subobjects and submodules. The following remain
separate obligations:

- the general categorical chain, finite-cover and compactness
  characterizations;
- Noetherian objects and categories, and the comparison of `M(A)` with
  left Noetherian rings;
- comparisons for arbitrary, possibly nonunital actions, and equivalences
  with additive categories of such actions;
- general action, Hom, lattice, restriction and quotient constructions.

In particular, this module does not identify all additive stable
subgroups with scalar-linear stable submodules.
