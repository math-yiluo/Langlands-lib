# Separation by twisted characters for finite-order automorphisms

`TwistedCharacterSeparationStatement` says that two irreducible admissible complex representations of a locally profinite group are equivalent when their twisted distribution characters are proportional for a continuous finite-order automorphism.

Choose nonzero vectors and a compact open subgroup fixing them. Intersect its translates under the finite cyclic action to get an invariant subgroup. The two twisting automorphisms restrict to invertible endomorphisms on the finite-dimensional spaces of fixed vectors. The twisted distribution on the Hecke corner is the trace of the corner action composed with that restriction.

If the representations are inequivalent, their nonzero fixed spaces are nonisomorphic simple modules over the corner. Jacobson density gives a corner element acting by the inverse twist on the first fixed space and by zero on the second. The first twisted trace is the dimension of the fixed space and the second vanishes. This contradicts proportionality.

The argument is the two-representation case of Arthur–Clozel, Chapter 1, Lemma 6.3, printed p. 52. It is phrased for the actual twisted distributions on Schwartz–Bruhat functions.

For general linear groups over a cyclic algebra, the entrywise automorphism has the required finite order. The relative module topology supplies its continuity.
