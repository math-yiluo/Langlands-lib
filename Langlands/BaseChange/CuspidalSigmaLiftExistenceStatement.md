# Every product of Galois conjugates of a supercuspidal is the σ-lift of a supercuspidal

This module `Langlands.BaseChange.CuspidalSigmaLiftExistenceStatement` (namespace
`Langlands.BaseChange`) states, as the proposition
`CuspidalSigmaLiftExistenceStatement.{u, v, uV, uW}`, the converse of
`CuspidalSigmaLiftStructureStatement`: for an irreducible supercuspidal representation `Π₁`
of `GL_{n₁}(E)` whose stabilizer in `⟨σ⟩` is generated by `σ^s`, the product
`Π₁ × Π₁^σ × ⋯ × Π₁^{σ^{s-1}}` is irreducible and is the σ-lift of an irreducible
supercuspidal representation of `GL_{s n₁}(F)` whose stabilizer in `K(E/F)` has `s`
elements. The statement is **not** proved here.

## Statement

`CuspidalSigmaLiftExistenceStatement` says: for

- a field `F : Type u` with `[ValuativeRel F] [TopologicalSpace F]
  [IsNonarchimedeanLocalField F] [CharZero F]`,
- a field `E : Type v` with `[ValuativeRel E] [TopologicalSpace E]
  [IsNonarchimedeanLocalField E] [Algebra F E] [IsModuleTopology F E]`,
- `σ : E ≃ₐ[F] E` and `l : ℕ` with `IsCyclicAlgebra σ l`,
- `s n₁ : ℕ`, `hn₁ : 0 < n₁`, and an irreducible supercuspidal
  `Π₁ : Representation ℂ (GL_{n₁}(E)) W` (`W : Type uW`) such that `Π₁ ≅ Π₁^{σ^i}` holds
  exactly for the `i : ℕ` with `s ∣ i`,

writing `Π := sigmaOrbitProduct σ s hn₁ Π₁`, a representation of `GL_{s * n₁}(E)`:

1. `Π` is irreducible, and
2. there are `hΠ : IsAdmissible Π`, a complex vector space `V : Type uV`, a representation
   `π : Representation ℂ (GL_{s * n₁}(F)) V` with `hπ : IsAdmissible π`,
   `A : SigmaIntertwiner σ (s * n₁) Π` and `c : ℂ` such that `π` is supercuspidal and
   irreducible, `Nat.card (normTwistStabilizer E π) = s`, and
   `IsSigmaLift σ l π hπ Π hΠ A c`.

The topology instances that `IsSigmaLift` takes for `GL_{s * n₁}(F)` and `GL_{s * n₁}(E)` are
supplied with `haveI` from the Library's `LocalFieldTopology.generalLinear_t2Space`,
`LocalFieldTopology.generalLinear_locallyCompactSpace` and
`LocalFieldTopology.generalLinear_totallyDisconnectedSpace`, applied to `F` and to `E`.

The constant `c` is existential, as in the discrete-series σ-lift statements: the Whittaker normalization of the
intertwiner `A`, which would fix `c`, is not defined in this programme.

## The stabilizer clause

When `E` is a field with `IsCyclicAlgebra σ l`, `σ` generates `Gal(E/F)`, which is cyclic of
order `l`. The stabilizer of the isomorphism class of `Π₁` is generated by some `σ^t` with
`t ∣ l`, and the hypothesis `∀ i : ℕ, (Π₁ ≅ Π₁^{σ^i}) ↔ s ∣ i` says exactly that `t = s`: HL's
"`σ^s` engendre le stabilisateur de la classe d'isomorphisme de `Ξ₁` dans `Σ`". Since
`σ^l = 1`, taking `i = l` gives `Π₁ ≅ Π₁^{σ^l} = Π₁`, so the hypothesis forces `s ∣ l`; as
`l = [E : F] ≥ 1`, this gives `s ≠ 0` (for `s = 0` the hypotheses are contradictory). In
particular `Nat.card (normTwistStabilizer E π) = s` asserts that the stabilizer is finite and
of order `s ≥ 1`.

## The group `K(E/F)`

`normTwistStabilizer E π` is the stabilizer of the class of `π` in `K(E/F)`, the group of
characters of `F^×` trivial on `N_{E/F}(E^×)`. By local class field theory `K(E/F)` is cyclic
of order `l` and its elements are automatically continuous (see `CuspidalSigmaLift.md`).

## Convention for `Π^σ`

`Π₁^{σ^i}` is `g ↦ Π₁(g^{σ^i})`, as in HL II.1.3 and `Langlands.BaseChange.TwistedCharacter`. The factors
`{Π₁^{σ^i} : 0 ≤ i < s}` form the `Gal(E/F)`-orbit of the class of `Π₁`; the other convention
(`σ⁻¹`) gives the same factors in another order, and since the factors are pairwise unlinked
(next section) the product does not depend on the order.

## The product

`sigmaOrbitProduct σ s hn₁ Π₁` is normalized parabolic induction from the standard block
upper triangular parabolic subgroup of `GL_{s * n₁}(E)` with `s` diagonal blocks of size
`n₁`, the `i`-th block carrying `Π₁^{σ^i}`; this is HL's `×`. Its carrier is the Library's
space of smooth induced functions. Here no rank transport is needed: the statement is made
directly for `GL_{s * n₁}`.

## Irreducibility

The factors `Π₁^{σ^i}`, `0 ≤ i < s`, are cuspidal and pairwise non-isomorphic (by the
stabilizer clause). No two of them are linked: the normalized absolute value `|det|_E` is
σ-invariant, so `Π₁^{σ^i} ≅ Π₁^{σ^j} ⊗ |det|_E^{±1}` would give `Π₁ ≅ Π₁ ⊗ |det|_E^m` with
`m ≠ 0`, which is impossible because the central characters of the two sides have different
absolute values. By Bernstein–Zelevinsky the product is irreducible. HL obtain the
irreducibility by showing that the product is essentially σ-discrete (Arthur–Clozel,
Lemma 2.8, converse). None of this is formalized here.

## Universes

The carrier `V` of `π` is existential, in an arbitrary universe `uV`. This is harmless: an
irreducible admissible representation of `GL_m` over a nonarchimedean local field has
countable dimension, so it has a model in every universe.

## The field `E` and `CharZero F`

HL II.4.12 is stated for a cyclic extension field `E` of `F`. The statement requires `E` to be
a nonarchimedean local field because the Library's product induction
(`LocalFieldData.ofInstances E`) needs it. `[IsModuleTopology F E]` makes the topology of `E`
the one induced from `F`, as in `Langlands.BaseChange.TwistedCharacter`. `CharZero F` is
assumed as in the statements on orbital integrals, twisted characters, pseudo-coefficients
and discrete-series σ-lifts: the concordance inside `IsSigmaLift` uses the Library's
`IsRegularSemisimple` (separable characteristic polynomial), which agrees with HL's
regularity in characteristic zero; Arthur–Clozel also work in characteristic zero.

## Prime degree

If `l` is prime, the case `s = l` of this statement produces, for every supercuspidal `Π₁` of
`GL_{n₁}(E)` with `Π₁ ≇ Π₁^σ`, a supercuspidal `π` of `GL_{l n₁}(F)` whose σ-lift is
`Π₁ × Π₁^σ × ⋯ × Π₁^{σ^{l-1}}`; together with `CuspidalSigmaLiftStructureStatement` this
describes both cases of Arthur–Clozel, Chapter 1, Lemma 6.10.

## Proof routes

HL deduce the Remarque from the Corollaire of II.4.12 and from σ-discrete descent (II.4.6,
Théorème (2): `SigmaDiscreteDescentStatement`): `Ξ₁ × ⋯ × Ξ₁^{σ^{s-1}}` is essentially σ-discrete (Arthur–Clozel,
Lemma 2.8, converse), hence irreducible, and is the σ-lift of an essentially discrete `ρ`;
the Proposition and the Corollaire applied to `ρ` (via `L(Π, Ξ̌) = ∏_κ L(π, κρ̌)`, II.4.6,
Théorème (4), and II.3.5 (ii)–(iii)), together with the uniqueness of σ-lifts, show that `ρ`
is cuspidal with stabilizer of order `s`. A later proof must not derive this statement
from a result that depends on it.
