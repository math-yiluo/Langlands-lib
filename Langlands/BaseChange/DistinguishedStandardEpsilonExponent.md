# The distinguished standard epsilon exponent

`DistinguishedStandardEpsilonExponentStatement` starts with a finite standard
pair factor choice for a cuspidal presentation and a second cuspidal
presentation in the dual slot, with the rank-one constant presentation in the
second position and irreducible admissible finite components, over a number
field with compact adelic additive quotient. The presentation in the dual slot
is arbitrary: only the epsilon factor, the additive character and the measure
of the choice enter. The local field is identified bicontinuously with the actual
completion. Assume the supplied local representation corresponds to a
representation over that completion. An unramified character of the
determinant twists the latter into the actual finite component, up to
isomorphism. Given a local standard epsilon certificate at the pulled-back
standard finite character and self-dual measure, its exponent equals that of
the finite factor choice.

The proof pushes the local certificate through the field isomorphism, using
the corresponding Haar measure and the trivial line. The unramified twist
preserves the exponent. The chosen finite additive character is the standard
component and its self-dual Haar measure is unique. The distinguished finite
factor identification then gives the claimed exponent.

Over an extension of number fields with two chosen places one above the other,
compatible field isomorphisms from a local extension to the two completions
identify the pulled-back standard characters by the actual relative trace.
The scalar map compatibility transports the trace, and the standard adelic
trace identity gives its restriction to those components.

At such a pair of completions, the pullback of an unramified character by
the actual norm is unramified. The norm multiplies the normalized order by
the inertia degree, so it carries upper order-zero units into lower
order-zero units.

These are the local identifications for the completed standard pairs at the
distinguished place in Arthur–Clozel, Chapter 1, proof of Proposition 6.9(ii),
printed pp. 69–70.
