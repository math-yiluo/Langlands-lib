# Compact open subgroups of regular centralizers

The units of a finite reduced algebra over a nonarchimedean local field have a largest compact open subgroup for the topology induced by the module topology on the algebra. The Artinian decomposition writes the algebra as the product of its residue fields. These are finite extensions of the base with the spectral local-field topology, which agrees with their module topology. The decomposition is a homeomorphism because its two directions are linear. The corresponding isomorphism on units identifies their largest compact open subgroup with the product of the integral unit groups.

For a regular semisimple matrix, its minimal polynomial divides its separable characteristic polynomial. The quotient of the polynomial ring by that minimal polynomial is finite-dimensional and reduced. In the proof, a nilpotent represented by a polynomial has a positive power divisible by the squarefree minimal polynomial, so the polynomial itself is divisible by it. Evaluation embeds the quotient into matrices. A cyclic vector shows that every matrix commuting with the regular element is a polynomial in it. For an invertible commuter, the same applies to its inverse. The induced map on units is therefore an isomorphism onto the ordinary centralizer, and the closed embedding on matrices proves that it respects the topologies. The proof covers dimension zero through the same polynomial quotient and unit-group constructions.

At an associated pair with separable ordinary characteristic polynomial, the Library's `normCentralizerEquiv` carries this subgroup to the largest compact open subgroup in the twisted centralizer. The internal argument uses the product-of-fields centralizer description in Henniart–Lemaire, *Changement de base et induction automorphe pour GLₙ en caractéristique non nulle*, Mém. SMF 124 (2011), I.2.3–I.2.4, printed pp. 13–14, PDF pp. 21–22. The volume-one prescription motivating the subgroup construction is in I.2.7, printed p. 19, PDF p. 27.
