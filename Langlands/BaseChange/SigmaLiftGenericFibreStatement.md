# Fibres of local cyclic base change at an essentially square-integrable representation

The module `Langlands.BaseChange.SigmaLiftGenericFibreStatement` states, as the proposition
`SigmaLiftGenericFibreStatement.{u, v, uV, uV', uW}`, Henniart–Lemaire's description of the
fibre of local cyclic base change for `GL_n` over an essentially square-integrable
representation, among generic representations. Suppose an irreducible generic representation
`π'` of `GL_{r+1}(F)` has the same σ-lift as an irreducible essentially square-integrable
representation `π`. Then `π'` is isomorphic to the twist of `π` by `κ ∘ det` for a character `κ`
of `F^×` that is trivial on the norms from `E^×`. The statement is **not** proved here.

Notation: `GL_n(R)` is the Library's `GeneralLinear n R`, and `det` is
`GeneralLinear.determinant`. Henniart–Lemaire are cited as HL.

## Statement

`SigmaLiftGenericFibreStatement.{u, v, uV, uV', uW}` quantifies over:

- a nonarchimedean local field `F : Type u` of characteristic zero:
  `[Field F] [ValuativeRel F] [TopologicalSpace F] [IsNonarchimedeanLocalField F] [CharZero F]`;
- a field `E : Type v` that is an `F`-algebra with its module topology:
  `[Field E] [Algebra F E] [TopologicalSpace E] [IsTopologicalRing E] [IsModuleTopology F E]`;
- `σ : E ≃ₐ[F] E` and `l r : ℕ` with `hE : IsCyclicAlgebra σ l` (module
  `Langlands.BaseChange.Norm`). Since `E` is a field, this says that `E/F` is a cyclic field
  extension of degree `l` whose Galois group is generated by `σ`.

The topological instances on `GL_{r+1}(F)` and `GL_{r+1}(E)` are introduced with `haveI`
exactly as in the Library statement `SigmaLiftCharacterIdentityStatement`: first
`Module.Finite F E` from `hE.1`, then `LocalFieldTopology.generalLinear_t2Space`,
`generalLinear_locallyCompactSpace` and `generalLinear_totallyDisconnectedSpace` for `F`, and
`generalLinear_t2Space_of_finite`, `generalLinear_locallyCompactSpace_of_finite` and
`generalLinear_totallyDisconnectedSpace_of_finite` (module
`Langlands.BaseChange.TwistedCharacter`) for `E`, all at rank `r + 1`.

The claim: for every `ψ : UnitaryAdditiveCharacter F`, all complex vector spaces
`V : Type uV`, `V' : Type uV'` and `W : Type uW`, and all

- `π : Representation ℂ (GL_{r+1}(F)) V` with `hπ : IsAdmissible π`,
- `π' : Representation ℂ (GL_{r+1}(F)) V'` with `hπ' : IsAdmissible π'`,
- `τ : Representation ℂ (GL_{r+1}(E)) W` with `hτ : IsAdmissible τ`,

if `π.IsIrreducible`, `IsEssentiallySquareIntegrable π`, `Whittaker.IsGeneric ψ r π'` and
`τ.IsIrreducible`, then for all `A A' : SigmaIntertwiner σ (r + 1) τ` and all `c c' : ℂ` with
`IsSigmaLift σ l π hπ τ hτ A c` and `IsSigmaLift σ l π' hπ' τ hτ A' c'`, there is
`κ : Fˣ →* ℂˣ` with `IsNormTrivial E κ` and
`Nonempty (π'.Equiv (twist (κ.comp GeneralLinear.determinant) π))`.

The binders are those of the task's form: the carriers `V`, `V'`, `W` and their instances are
implicit, everything else is explicit, in the order above. HL's `Π` is written `τ`, as in
`Langlands.BaseChange.TwistedCharacter`.

The declarations used are all in the Library: `IsCyclicAlgebra` (`Langlands.BaseChange.Norm`);
`SigmaIntertwiner`, `IsSigmaLift` and the three topology lemmas
(`Langlands.BaseChange.TwistedCharacter`); `IsNormTrivial`
(`Langlands.BaseChange.CuspidalSigmaLift`); `Langlands.Whittaker.IsGeneric`
(`Langlands.Representation.Whittaker.GeneralLinear`); `UnitaryAdditiveCharacter`
(`Langlands.HarmonicAnalysis.Basic`); `JacquetLanglands.IsEssentiallySquareIntegrable`
(`Langlands.JacquetLanglands.EssentiallySquareIntegrable`); `Langlands.twist`
(`Langlands.Representation.Twist.Definitions`); `GeneralLinear.determinant`; `IsAdmissible`;
and Mathlib's `Representation.IsIrreducible` and `Representation.Equiv`. The module defines
nothing else.

## Comparison with HL

**Source and scope.** The statement is HL's Lemme II.4.8 (2) for a cyclic field extension `E/F`
in characteristic zero, at rank `r + 1 ≥ 1`. HL's `π` is essentially discrete; it is the Lean
`π` (irreducible, admissible, `IsEssentiallySquareIntegrable`). HL's `Π` is a σ-lift of `π`; it
is `τ`, irreducible and admissible, with `IsSigmaLift σ l π hπ τ hτ A c`. HL's "autre
représentation lisse irréductible générique de `G(F)` se relevant en `Π`" is `π'` with
`Whittaker.IsGeneric ψ r π'` and `IsSigmaLift σ l π' hπ' τ hτ A' c'`. HL's conclusion
"isomorphe à `κπ` pour un caractère `κ ∈ K(E/F)`" is the existence of `κ` with
`IsNormTrivial E κ` and an isomorphism `π' ≅ twist (κ ∘ det) π`, where
`twist (κ ∘ det) π` is `g ↦ κ(det g) • π(g)` on the space of `π`, that is `κπ`.

`IsNormTrivial E κ` says `κ(N_{E/F}(x)) = 1` for every `x ∈ E^×`, with the norm
`Algebra.norm F`. This is HL's `κ ∈ K(E/F)` (II.1.3). The Lean `κ` is any group homomorphism
`Fˣ →* ℂˣ`, while HL's characters are continuous. This makes no difference for norm-trivial
characters, by a paper argument that is not formalized: `N_{E/F}(x) = x^l` for `x ∈ F^×`, so a
norm-trivial `κ` is trivial on the `l`-th powers of `F^×`, which contain an open subgroup of
`F^×` in characteristic zero (Hensel's lemma); hence `κ` is locally constant.

HL's representations are smooth irreducible. The Lean statement additionally asks `π`, `π'` and
`τ` to be admissible, as `IsSigmaLift` requires; smooth irreducible representations of `GL_n`
are admissible (a known theorem, not formalized), so this matches HL's hypotheses.

**σ-lifts.** `IsSigmaLift σ l π hπ τ hτ A c` (item 6 of `Langlands.BaseChange.TwistedCharacter`)
is HL's σ-relèvement (II.2.7, printed p. 67): `c ≠ 0`, and the twisted trace distribution of
`τ` against `A` equals `c` times the character distribution of `π` on every concordant pair of
test functions, for all Haar measures. In HL's Définition `Π` is irreducible and σ-stable; here
irreducibility is the hypothesis `τ.IsIrreducible`, and σ-stability follows from the existence
of `A`. The two lifts in the statement may use different intertwiners `A`, `A'` and constants
`c`, `c'`. HL fix one isomorphism of `Π` onto `Π^σ` (or the Whittaker-normalized one,
`I_σ(Π)`, II.4.1), which is not defined in the programme. Since replacing `A` by `a • A`
replaces `c` by `a * c` (HL II.2.9, Remarque (2); `twistedDistribution_smul` in
`TwistedCharacter`), and for irreducible `τ` any two intertwiners differ by a nonzero scalar
(Schur's lemma, not formalized), allowing independent `A`, `A'`, `c`, `c'` expresses the same
hypothesis "`π'` se relève en `Π`" without fixing a normalization.

**Genericity.** `Whittaker.IsGeneric ψ r π'` (module
`Langlands.Representation.Whittaker.GeneralLinear`) says that `π'` is irreducible and admissible
and has a nonzero linear functional `ℓ` with `ℓ(π'(u) v) = θ_ψ(u) ℓ(v)` for all `u` in the upper
unipotent group, where `θ_ψ(u) = ψ(u₁₂ + u₂₃ + ⋯ + u_{r,r+1})`. This is HL's definition of
genericity (II.4.1, printed pp. 81–82) for their fixed nontrivial `ψ`.
`UnitaryAdditiveCharacter F` is continuous and nontrivial by definition. For `GL_{r+1}` the
choice of `ψ` does not matter: every nontrivial `ψ'` is `x ↦ ψ(yx)` for some `y ∈ F^×`, and
conjugation by `diag(1, y, …, y^r)` exchanges the two characters (HL II.4.1, printed p. 82).
This is a paper remark; the statement fixes one `ψ` and is claimed for every `ψ`. The separate
hypothesis `hπ'` is the admissibility proof that `IsSigmaLift` takes as an argument; it is
also contained in `Whittaker.IsGeneric ψ r π'`.

**Essentially discrete.** HL call the irreducible smooth representations that are essentially
square-integrable modulo the centre "essentiellement discrètes" (II.3.2, printed p. 78); such a
`π` can be written `ωπ₀` for a character `ω` of `F^×` and a discrete series `π₀` (II.4.7, printed
p. 89). The Library's `IsEssentiallySquareIntegrable π` asks for a real `s` with
`|det|^{-s} ⊗ π` square-integrable modulo the centre (admissible, unitary central character,
square-integrable matrix coefficients on `GL_{r+1}(F)/Z`). The two classes agree, by a paper
argument that is not formalized: every character of `F^×` is a unitary character times
`|·|^s` for a real `s`, and twisting by a unitary character preserves square-integrability
modulo the centre.

**Characteristic zero, field case.** The statement assumes `[CharZero F]`, as the other
base-change statements of the programme do (`SigmaLiftCharacterIdentityStatement`,
`RegularTransferStatement`). Concordance inside `IsSigmaLift` tests regular semisimple elements
in the Library's sense (separable characteristic polynomial), which agrees with HL's regularity
(squarefree characteristic polynomial, II.1.2) in characteristic zero; and the orthogonality
relations of characters that HL's proof uses are Clozel's in characteristic zero (HL's
reference [20], cited at the start of II.4.8). `E` is a field because HL state the lemma in
II.4, where from II.4.3 on `E` is a field.

**Rank.** The rank is written `r + 1` because `Whittaker.IsGeneric ψ r` is defined for
representations of `GL_{r+1}(F)`. Every positive rank is covered.

## Relation to other statements

- Statement 5 of Task 7449, `SigmaLiftFibreStatement`, was not in the Library when this module
  was written, and this module does not import it. It asks `π'` to be essentially
  square-integrable instead of generic, and also allows rank `0`. Irreducible essentially
  square-integrable representations of `GL_{r+1}(F)` are generic (Zelevinsky 1980, Theorem 9.3
  with Theorem 9.7 for a single segment), so the present statement implies the positive-rank
  case of that one. That implication is not proved here.
- HL II.4.6, Théorème (2) (printed p. 88) contains the same fibre assertion for a σ-discrete
  series `Π`. HL note (II.4.8, printed p. 90) that the lemma holds independently of that
  theorem and gives a small part of it.

## Proof route (for a later proof task)

HL's proof (printed pp. 90–91), which is not formalized here:

1. Twisting by a character `χ` of `F^×` multiplies `Θ_π` by `χ ∘ det`, and `π` lifts to `Π`
   exactly when `χπ` lifts to `χ_E Π` (HL II.2.9, Remarque (4)); so `π` may be assumed to be a
   discrete series.
2. The central characters satisfy `ω_Π = ω_π ∘ N_{E/F} = ω_{π'} ∘ N_{E/F}` (II.2.9, Remarque
   (3)), so `ω_π` and `ω_{π'}` agree on `N_{E/F}(E^×)`; in particular `ω_{π'}` is unitary.
3. By formula (4) of II.4.8 with `π' = π`, `Θ_π` is not identically zero on the elliptic norms
   (this is Lemme (1)). Through the Shintani identity (Library statement
   `SigmaLiftCharacterIdentityStatement`), the twisted character `Θ^A_Π` is not identically
   zero on the σ-elliptic set.
4. If `π'` were not a discrete series, it would be a proper parabolic induction (Zelevinsky's
   classification of generic representations, HL II.3.2), whose character vanishes on the
   elliptic set (HL II.4.5). Then `Θ^A_Π` would vanish on the σ-elliptic set, a contradiction.
5. So `π'` is a discrete series, and formula (3) of II.4.8 (the orthogonality relations on the
   elliptic norms) gives `π' ≅ κπ` for some `κ ∈ K(E/F)`.
