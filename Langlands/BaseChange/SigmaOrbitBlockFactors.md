# Standard L-polynomials of the actual supercuspidal sigma-orbit blocks

`SigmaOrbitBlockFactorsStatement` gives full Whittaker type and separated standard L-polynomials for the blocks `P₁ ∘ entrywiseAut (σ^i)` of a supercuspidal sigma-orbit, starting with an irreducible supercuspidal `P₁` on its actual carrier. The extension field is supplied as a p-adic field with topological data. The base field and its action on the extension come with the stated `ℚ_p`-scalar tower and module topology, and the additive character has a supplied self-dual measure. The two certificates are against the trivial rank-one representation and its inverse-transpose pullback, at the original and inverse additive characters with the same measure. The conclusion is that no nonzero root of the original polynomial product is a root of the dual product after reflection `x ↦ q_E⁻¹ / x`.

`sigmaOrbitBlockFactors` transports irreducibility and supercuspidality along the homeomorphic field automorphisms and obtains full Whittaker type by the Library's supercuspidal genericity theorem and multiplicity one. The published equality of supplied and canonical topological data lets the proof use the p-adic certificates without changing the carrier or either field action.

In rank greater than one, the proof applies the Library's version of Jacquet–Piatetski-Shapiro–Shalika, *Rankin-Selberg Convolutions* (1983), Proposition (8.1)(i), printed p. 443, to the original and inverse-transpose supercuspidal blocks. Each original and dual standard polynomial is one, so there are no roots to compare. In rank one, `exists_tateCharacterModel` gives an actual character model with character `χ`; inverse transpose gives the inverse character. The proof uses the Tate rank-one pair certificates and `PadicField.AbelianTransport.lPolynomial_eq` along the restricted field automorphisms to identify the original and dual polynomials for every block with the polynomials of `χ` and `χ⁻¹`. If `χ` is ramified, the original polynomial is one. Otherwise, with `a = χ(ϖ)`, an original root satisfies `a * x = 1`. The reflected dual linear polynomial evaluates to `1 - q_E⁻¹` there, which is nonzero. Taking the finite products gives the separation assertion.

This derived interface supplies the Whittaker and L-polynomial premises of the local epsilon-product step of the conductor identity `CuspidalSigmaLiftConductorStatement`. It does not assert the block epsilon coefficients or exponents, or the conductor identity of Arthur–Clozel, Ch. 1, Proposition 6.9(ii).
