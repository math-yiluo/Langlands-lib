# Lifting twisted traces from a Jacquet module

J. D. Rogawski, *Trace Paley–Wiener theorem in the twisted case*, Trans. Amer.
Math. Soc. 309 (1988), 215–229, Proposition 7.1 (printed p. 219, PDF p. 5;
proof on pp. 220–221), states that `r*_{GM}(F_tr(M)) ⊆ F_tr(G)` for a
stable standard Levi. Here `r*_{GM}` precomposes with the normalized Jacquet
functor (§§3–4), and `F_tr` consists of twisted trace forms of compactly
supported locally constant functions (§5). The inclusion is used in the rank
induction for Proposition 11.2 (printed pp. 228–229, especially the last
paragraph of the proof on p. 229). This module states the realization for
`GL_n` in the **unnormalized** Jacquet convention; it does not prove it.

## Statement

`Langlands.BaseChange.TwistedJacquetTraceRealizationStatement.{u,v,z} : Prop`
uses the field and topology preamble of `TwistedPseudoCoefficientExistenceStatement`.
`F : Type u` is a nonarchimedean local field of characteristic zero;
`E : Type v` is a field with an `F`-algebra structure and the module topology,
and `IsCyclicAlgebra σ l` says that `E/F` is cyclic of degree `l` with
generator `σ : E ≃ₐ[F] E`. This includes the trivial extension (`l = 1`).
For `B : BlockShape n`, set `G = GL_n(E)`, `P = B.parabolic E`,
`N = B.unipotentRadical E` and `M = B.Levi E`. The Levi product has the
finite product topology. Both `G` and `M` carry their Borel σ-algebras.
Hausdorffness, local compactness and total disconnectedness of `G` and the
factors of `M` use the topology lemmas from `Langlands.BaseChange.TwistedCharacter`.

For any Haar measures `μ` on `G` and `ν` on `M` and any
`f : SchwartzBruhat M`, there exists `φ : SchwartzBruhat G` such that for
every irreducible admissible representation `ρ` of `G` on a complex vector
space in `Type z`, every `A : SigmaIntertwiner σ n ρ`, and every witness `hJ`
of admissibility of the unnormalized Jacquet representation `ρ_N`,

```
twistedDistribution μ ρ   hρ A.toLinearMap   φ =
twistedDistribution ν ρ_N hJ A_N.toLinearMap f.
```

Here `ρ_N = Parabolic.leviCoinvariants B (ρ.comp P.subtype)` is the
unnormalized product-Levi action on the Library's `B.JacquetModule ρ`, and
`A_N = jacquetSigmaIntertwiner σ B A` is the descent of `A` by `[v] ↦ [A v]`.
The order in the trace is the integrated action composed with the twisting
operator, as in Rogawski §5. One `φ` works simultaneously for all these
pairs, not a separate lift for each `ρ` or `A`. The witness `hJ` is an
argument required by `twistedDistribution`. With compatible local-field data
on `E`, `Langlands.AllField.Parabolic.isAdmissible_leviCoinvariants` gives
this admissibility from admissibility of `ρ`; the statement quantifies over
the witness rather than fixing how to construct it.

## Comparison with the sources

**Actual group and automorphism.** For `n > 0` and a nontrivial extension,
apply the source setting to the restriction of scalars of `GL_n` from `E`
to `F`. Its group of `F`-points is `GL_n(E)`. The entrywise automorphism
has order `l`, is outer (it acts nontrivially on scalar matrices), and fixes
the standard upper triangular minimal parabolic as a subgroup. The block
parabolics and Levis are stable, with the Levi product identified with the
block diagonal subgroup. For `n = 0` both groups are trivial: the equality
amounts to rescaling the sole test value by the ratio `ν(M)/μ(G)`.

**Trace functions on pairs.** Rogawski uses finite-length modules for the
semidirect product by the cyclic group generated by the automorphism. This
statement only tests the pairs with irreducible admissible underlying
`G`-representation. In positive rank, `A^l` commutes with `ρ`, so Schur's
lemma makes it a nonzero scalar. Rescaling `A` by an `l`th root of the
inverse scalar gives an extension to the semidirect product. Rescaling `A`
also rescales `A_N` by the same factor, as is seen on quotient representatives.
Both traces scale by that factor, so the source equality on the extensions
gives the stated equality for every `A` without changing `φ`. No additional
trace distribution or Grothendieck group is defined.

**Trivial extension.** Rogawski starts with an outer automorphism. For
`σ = 1`, use the untwisted trace-form inclusion of Bernstein–Deligne–Kazhdan,
Proposition 3.2(ii), applied to the ordinary traces. Their Hecke measures are
represented by test functions with respect to the given Haar measures.
Schur's lemma again makes `A` a scalar, and both traces are multiplied by
that scalar. Thus the identity case does not rely on the outerness hypothesis.

**Normalization.** In Rogawski §3, the normalized action on the same
quotient is `m ↦ δ_P(m)^(-1/2) ρ_N(m)`, with `δ_P` the modulus of the Levi
acting on `N`. The modulus is invariant under `leviAut σ B`: in block
determinant coordinates this follows from invariance of the local absolute
value under `σ`. Therefore the same `A_N` intertwines the normalized action.
Apply the normalized realization to the test `δ_P^(1/2) f`. The factor
`δ_P^(1/2)` cancels `δ_P^(-1/2)` in the integrated action, so its normalized
trace with `A_N` is the unnormalized trace at `f`. Multiplication by either
half-power preserves local constancy and compact support. The two Haar
measures are independent: scaling `μ` by `a > 0` and `ν` by `b > 0` changes
the lift by multiplying it by `b/a`. These comparisons explain the
formulation; they are not separate Lean results here.
