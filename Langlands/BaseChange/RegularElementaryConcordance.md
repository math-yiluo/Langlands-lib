# Concordance of the regular Labesse pair

`RegularElementaryConcordanceStatement` proves `AreConcordant` for the
actual upper regular elementary test and the lower test with the weight
scaled by the cyclic degree. The fields are the canonical p-adic
carriers in an unramified cyclic extension and the ambient Haar
measures give the two spherical subgroups mass one. All ranks \(r+1\)
and all strictly decreasing integer weights are allowed.

An ordinary orbit meeting the lower support is conjugate to a diagonal
whose coordinate orders are divisible by the cyclic degree. The scalar
unramified norm criterion then gives an upper diagonal witness for
that orbit. Thus every regular non-norm has zero ordinary orbital
integral.

If a regular associated twisted orbit meets the upper support, its norm
has the characteristic polynomial of the diagonal of the coordinate
field norms. The ordinary orbit is conjugate to that diagonal, whose
orders are the lower scaled weights. Accordingly, when the ordinary
orbit has no such diagonal representative both orbital integrals
vanish.

For the remaining associated pairs, conjugate the ordinary element to
the indicated diagonal and twist-conjugate the upper element so its
norm is exactly the scalar extension of that diagonal. The cyclic
norm identity shows that the upper element commutes with its norm.
Distinct diagonal orders force the upper element to be diagonal.
The relative norm order formula gives its original upper weights.

For this exact pair the orbit-map preimages of the supports are the
products of the respective centralizers with the spherical subgroups.
Each orbital integral is therefore the mass of the spherical subgroup
image in the compatible quotient. The compact-open orbit mass formula
divides the ambient spherical subgroup mass by the centralizer
intersection mass. Every measure association witness for the exact
diagonal pair is diagonal and centralizes the twisted centralizer,
so the two centralizer intersection masses agree by the scalar
extension map. This gives the required equality for every compatible
associated measure package.

This proves the strictly decreasing weight case of Henniart–Lemaire
(2011), I.4.2, Proposition, pp. 28–30.
