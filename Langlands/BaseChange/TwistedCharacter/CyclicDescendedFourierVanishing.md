# Fourier coset vanishing for the descended cyclic character

Fix an irreducible admissible representation of the cyclic matrix semidirect product and a semisimple element. Use the normalized conjugation pushforward and the tensor identity defining the descended slice distribution `T`. In the fixed algebra, `AdjointExponentialTestAgreement` means that a global additive distribution `Dexp` agrees with `T` on tests transported through every sufficiently small exponential congruence chart.

`CyclicDescendedFourierVanishingStatement` assumes exponential product straightening in the ambient matrix algebra and the fixed algebra, conjugation invariance of `Dexp` under the fixed-algebra units, and this chart agreement. Use the bicharacter from a unitary additive character and the restricted trace pairing. Given `‖d‖ > 1` and an open neighborhood `V` of the fixed-algebra nilpotent annulus, each sufficiently small slice ball has a bound `b > 0`. At a center `Z` with `‖Z‖ > b` and no nonzero scalar multiple in `V`, `Dexp` vanishes on the Fourier transform of the indicator of the dual-lattice coset centered at `Z`.

The proof first chooses a smaller neighborhood of the annulus to allow transfer from orbit points to their representatives. At each sufficiently small lattice level the orbit character theorem gives a finite-dimensional smooth slice representation with the required exponential trace formula for `Z`. A nonzero evaluation on the Fourier transform of the coset gives a nonzero slice trace by exponential chart agreement and the orbit sum identity. Compact trace localization gives an orbit frequency `Y`. Above the chosen bound `Y` is large and rescales into the smaller neighborhood with norm at most one. The transfer estimate then gives a nonzero scalar multiple of `Z` in `V`.

This gives the large-frequency vanishing argument for the cyclic semisimple slice. [Clozel, *Characters of non-connected, reductive p-adic groups*, §§3.4 and 3.7, Lemmas 6 and 12, pp. 155 and 162–165](https://doi.org/10.4153/CJM-1987-008-3)
