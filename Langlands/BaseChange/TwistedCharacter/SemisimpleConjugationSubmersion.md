# The semisimple conjugation submersion domain

Let `R` be a finite-dimensional normed algebra over a complete nontrivially normed field `F`, with `R` complete, and let `α` be an `F`-algebra automorphism. Write `S` for the fixed algebra. The normalized conjugation map is `(g,Y) ↦ g(1+Y)α(g)⁻¹` on the unit locus of the first coordinate and `S` in the second coordinate. The domain `adjointConjugationDomain α` in `S` consists of the points `Y` for which `1+Y` is a unit of `S` and the strict differential at `(1,Y)` is surjective. When `α` is twisted adjoint conjugation at a matrix `δ`, multiplying the output on the right by `δ` gives the corresponding map into the twisted component.

The strict differential at `(1,Y)` is `(X,Z) ↦ X(1+Y)+Z−(1+Y)α(X)`. This follows from the product and inversion rules. Surjectivity is open because a continuous linear map near a surjection still composes with a fixed right inverse to give an invertible endomorphism. At `Y=0`, semisimplicity gives the direct sum of the fixed subspace and the image of `1−α` by the semisimple slice theorem. The differential thus reaches the whole algebra. This proves that the domain is an open neighborhood of zero in the semisimple case.

Conjugation by a fixed unit intertwines the differentials at `Y` and at its conjugate. It preserves invertibility of `1+Y`, so the domain is invariant under the fixed-algebra units. For any unit `g`, left multiplication by `g` in the conjugator variable and multiplication by `g` and `α(g)⁻¹` on the output are continuous linear equivalences. They carry the strict differential at `(1,Y)` to the strict differential at `(g,Y)`. Hence the map is submersive at every such pair. This gives the additive-coordinate form of the open invariant submersion domain used for descent.
