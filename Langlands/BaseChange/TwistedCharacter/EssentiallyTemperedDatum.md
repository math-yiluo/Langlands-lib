# Langlands data of essentially tempered irreducible representations

Let `E` be a `p`-adic field with topological data `T`. An irreducible
representation of `GL_n(E)`, `n > 0`, is essentially tempered when some
unramified twist of it is tempered. This module shows that its Langlands
data has a single block.

- `exists_realPowerParameter_eq`: every positive real `r` is
  `realPowerParameter T s` for some real `s`. Take `s = log_q r`, where
  `q` is the normalized absolute value of the uniformizer, which is
  positive and different from one.
- `determinantUnramified_one`: the parameter `1` gives the trivial
  character.
- `exists_singleBlockDatum_of_twist_tempered`: let `π` be irreducible and
  `twist ψ π` tempered for an unramified character `ψ`. Then `π` is the
  Langlands quotient of a tempered datum with one block. The proof has
  four steps.
  1. `eq_detOrderCharacter_of_isUnramified` writes `ψ` as
     `determinantUnramified E n z`.
  2. Split `z⁻¹` as a real power parameter `realPowerParameter T s` times
     a unitary parameter `u`.
  3. The twist of `twist ψ π` by the unitary character
     `determinantUnramified E n u` is still tempered
     (`isTemperedModCenter_unitaryTwist`). Use it as the single block
     with exponent `s`.
  4. `singleBlockNormalizedProductEquiv` identifies the standard module of
     the one-block datum with that block twisted by `|det|^s`. The product
     of the three characters is trivial, so this standard module is
     equivalent to `π`.
- `shape_length_eq_one_of_twist_tempered`: every tempered datum whose
  Langlands quotient is essentially tempered has a single block. The proof
  compares the datum with the one-block datum above by
  `glRegularLanglandsDataUniqueness`, which matches the blocks by an order
  isomorphism.

In the Langlands classification, an irreducible representation is
essentially tempered exactly when its Langlands triplet `(α, Ξ, ψ)` is not
induced. In Henniart–Lemaire (2011), II.5.11, p. 109, a triplet is induced
when the partition `α` has more than one part. There the twisted
Grothendieck group is decomposed, by the Proposition of II.5.10, as
`𝒢_{e.t}(G(E),σ) ⊕ 𝒢^L_{ind}(G(E),σ)`: the subgroup generated by the
essentially tempered irreducible σ-representations, plus the subgroup
generated by the standard σ-representations of induced σ-triplets. Lemme
2, pp. 111–112, states the corresponding direct sum in the quotient
`Ḡ(G(E),σ)`. The second theorem of this module is one direction of the
characterization above: a datum whose Langlands quotient is essentially
tempered has a single block. It is the direction used in the triangular
separation.
