# Twisted character integrability from densities near semisimple points

`CyclicGLSemisimpleLocalCharacterStatement` asks for the ordinary character density near every adjoint semisimple point of the cyclic matrix semidirect group. It uses the characteristic-zero nonarchimedean local field and cyclic algebra of the twisted integrability statement. For every irreducible admissible complex representation of the cyclic group extension and each such point, there is an open neighborhood carrying a common density for all left Haar measures. The function is locally constant at the componentwise regular points of that neighborhood. The local character identity is tested on every Schwartz–Bruhat function supported in the neighborhood.

`cyclicGLCharacterRegularity_of_semisimpleLocal` proves the global character theorem on the cyclic matrix extension from this local premise. The adjoint representation is a group homomorphism, so conjugation preserves its characteristic polynomial. Since conjugation also preserves each cyclic component, the minimum multiplicity defining regularity is invariant. Every conjugacy-orbit closure meets the adjoint semisimple locus by the twisted matrix orbit-closure theorem applied to the coefficient automorphism of that component. The local density therefore globalizes by conjugation transport and clopen gluing. This is the globalization step of semisimple descent. [Clozel, *Characters of non-connected, reductive p-adic groups*, Theorem 2 and §3.4, pp. 153–156](https://doi.org/10.4153/CJM-1987-008-3)

`twistedCharacterFromSemisimpleLocal` combines globalization with the generator comparison, extension of the representation by its normalized intertwiner, restriction to the generator coset and restoration of the scalar of the original intertwiner. Its conclusion is `TwistedCharacterLocalIntegrabilityStatement` with the base field, coefficient algebra and representation carrier universes independent. The premise `CyclicGLSemisimpleLocalCharacterStatement` supplies the local analytic density calculation. Arthur–Clozel, *Simple Algebras, Base Change, and the Advanced Theory of the Trace Formula*, Chapter 1, §2.1 and Proposition 2.2, pp. 10–12, uses this cyclic extension and restriction for the twisted character theorem.
