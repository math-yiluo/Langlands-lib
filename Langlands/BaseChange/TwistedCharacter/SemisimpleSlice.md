# A local slice for semisimple twisted conjugation

`TwistedSemisimpleSliceChartStatement` takes a complete nontrivially normed field, a finite commutative coefficient algebra with its module topology, a coefficient automorphism σ, and an invertible matrix δ. Assume the adjoint operator `T` given by `X ↦ δ σ(X) δ⁻¹` is semisimple as a linear endomorphism. Write `S` for the subalgebra fixed by `T` and `q` for the image of `1 − T`. The theorem `twistedSemisimpleSliceChart` gives an open partial homeomorphism from a neighborhood of `(0, 0)` in `q × S` onto a neighborhood of δ in the matrix algebra consisting of units. On the source the matrices `k = 1 + X` and `m = 1 + Y` are units and the chart sends `(X, Y)` to `k m δ σ(k)⁻¹`. The fixed-subalgebra calculation gives `m δ = δ σ(m)`, the twisted centralizer equation.

This is the matrix specialization of the local submersion in the semisimple descent argument, using additive coordinates near the identity and a complementary subspace for the conjugator directions. [Clozel, *Characters of non-connected, reductive p-adic groups*, §3.4, p. 154](https://doi.org/10.4153/CJM-1987-008-3)

The proof first works with a semisimple algebra automorphism `α` of a finite-dimensional complete normed algebra. A complement of its fixed subspace can be taken invariant under `α`. On that complement `1 − α` is injective and, by finite dimension, surjective. Its image on the full algebra is therefore that complement. After right-translating the chart to the identity, multiplication and inversion give strict differential `(X, Y) ↦ X − α(X) + Y`. This is invertible by the direct sum of the complement and the fixed subspace, so the strict inverse function theorem supplies the local chart. The source is restricted to the open set where both additive coordinates give units. For matrices the finite algebra norm realizes the given module topology, and right multiplication by δ restores the desired output coordinate.
