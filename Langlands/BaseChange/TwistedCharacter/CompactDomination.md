# Restriction of compact domination along smooth induction

`SmoothInducedFixedVanishingStatement` asks for a compact open subgroup of
the inducing group such that vanishing of its fixed space implies vanishing
of the induced fixed space at the given ambient open subgroup. The proof
requires compactness of the left quotient by the inducing subgroup and
openness of its homomorphism to the coefficient group. The latter group is
Hausdorff, locally compact and totally disconnected.

For the ambient level `K`, take a finite cover by double cosets of the
inducing subgroup and `K`. Each coefficient stabilizer is the image of an
open subgroup under the inducing homomorphism. Take a compact open
subgroup `J` in their intersection. A zero `J`-fixed space forces every
coefficient fixed space of the finite evaluation model to be zero.
Evaluation on the cover is injective, so the induced `K`-fixed space also
vanishes.

Compact-open exactness lifts a fixed vector of an irreducible subquotient
to a nonzero fixed vector of the ambient representation. Hence if the
ambient fixed space is zero, all irreducible constituent twisted traces
of a finite-length pair belong to the kernel of a functional dominated
at that level. Twisted trace dévissage places the whole trace in the kernel.
Composing the functional with a linear map that realizes twisted trace
induction therefore gives a compactly dominated functional on the
inducing group, provided the relevant inductions are admissible and
finite length.

This gives the compact-domination part of restriction along induction,
corresponding to the support condition in Rogawski (1988), Lemma 11.1(b),
printed p. 228. The proof uses the compact-level condition of
Henniart–Lemaire (2011), II.5.7, Remarque (1), printed p. 105.
