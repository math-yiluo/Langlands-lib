# Local epsilon and conductor certificates on supplied p-adic fields

`CuspidalSigmaLiftPadicEpsilonFactorsStatement` assembles local certificates on the supplied p-adic realizations of `F` and `E`. The two additive characters and their self-dual measures are arbitrary inputs; they need not be trace-related here. The base representation and the first orbit block are irreducible supercuspidal, and the extension representation is equivalent to the full normalized `sigmaOrbitProduct` after the specified rank reindexing. Every member of the given finite set of characters is trivial on the actual norms; the set need not exhaust those characters.

`cuspidalSigmaLiftPadicEpsilonFactors` applies `LocalGalois.localNormedField` on the supplied base topology. The norm-trivial character has open kernel by `IsNormTrivial.isOpen_ker`: norms of embedded units are their extension-degree powers, and that power image contains a neighborhood of one by the inverse function theorem. The published determinant-twist and irreducibility comparisons show that each actual twist is irreducible supercuspidal. The supplied-data standard epsilon theorem then gives both a finite newvector level and the actual epsilon monomial against the trivial rank-one character, with exponent equal to the level plus rank times the additive conductor. Values assigned outside the indexing set are not asserted to have certificates.

The orbit-block theorem gives irreducible supercuspidality, full Whittaker type, original and inverse-transpose dual L-polynomial certificates, and separation of the original and reflected dual products. Apply the same standard epsilon theorem to each block, then apply the reindexed orbit-product theorem at the same measure to obtain `OrbitProductEpsilonMultiplicativity` for the supplied extension representation. No irreducibility of that representation is needed.

This is the internal local data assembly for the conductor identity `CuspidalSigmaLiftConductorStatement`, not the comparison of the epsilon monomials across the fields. In particular it does not use a base-change theorem, Arthur–Clozel Proposition 6.9(ii), or its converse.
