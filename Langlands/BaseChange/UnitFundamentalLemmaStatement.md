# The fundamental lemma for the units of the spherical Hecke algebras

Module `Langlands.BaseChange.UnitFundamentalLemmaStatement`.
`Langlands.BaseChange.UnitFundamentalLemmaStatement.{u, v}` is a proposition-valued
definition (statement 8 of Task 7424). It states an unproved obligation; it is neither
proved nor assumed in this module.

## Statement

The module repeats, as local instances declared for every `PadicField` over every prime,
the canonical topology, valuative relation and local-field structure of the field, and the
canonical topology, topological-group, Hausdorff, Borel measurable and Borel space
structures on `GL_n`, as in `Langlands/Representation/Unramified/HeckeOperators.lean`.
So the spherical Hecke algebras and the measures `sphericalHaar` of `F` and of `E` are the
Library's own, and the Borel structures on `GL_n(F)` and `GL_n(E)` are those that
`AreConcordant` requires.

For every prime `p` with `[Fact p.Prime]`, every `F : PadicField.{u} p`, every
`E : PadicField.{v} p`, every `F`-algebra structure on `E`
(`[Algebra F.Carrier E.Carrier]`) compatible with the two `ℚ_p`-algebra structures
(`[IsScalarTower (Padic p) F.Carrier E.Carrier]`), every `F`-algebra automorphism
`σ : E.Carrier ≃ₐ[F.Carrier] E.Carrier` and all `l r : ℕ`, assume:

1. `IsCyclicAlgebra σ l` (Task 7405, module `Langlands.BaseChange.Norm`): `E` is a finite
   reduced `F`-algebra of dimension `l`, `σ` has order `l`, and the elements of `E` fixed
   by `σ` are those of `F`. Since `E` is a field, this says that `E/F` is a cyclic Galois
   extension of degree `l` whose Galois group is generated by `σ`.
2. `E.canonicalTopologicalData.ramificationIndex =
   F.canonicalTopologicalData.ramificationIndex`. The Library's `ramificationIndex` of a
   `PadicField` is the valuation of `p` for its normalized valuation, i.e. its absolute
   ramification index over `ℚ_p`. Since the absolute ramification index of `E` is the
   ramification index of `E/F` times that of `F`, the hypothesis says that `E/F` is
   unramified. This is the Library's way of saying "unramified", as in
   `Langlands/Weil/Local/FiniteGaloisUnramified.lean`.

Then

`AreConcordant σ l (sphericalHaar F r) (sphericalHaar E r)
  ⇑(1 : SphericalHeckeAlgebra E r) ⇑(1 : SphericalHeckeAlgebra F r)`.

Here:

- `SphericalHeckeAlgebra F r` is the Library's spherical Hecke algebra of `GL_{r+1}(F)`
  for `K = GL_{r+1}(O_F)`, with convolution for `sphericalHaar F r`, the Haar measure
  giving `K` mass one. Its unit is the characteristic function of `K`: by the Library's
  `standardHeckeElement_zero` it is the standard element for `i = 0`, which by
  `standardHeckeElement_apply` is the characteristic function of `K · 1 · K = K`. In the
  same way `(1 : SphericalHeckeAlgebra E r)` is the characteristic function of
  `K_E = GL_{r+1}(O_E)`. These are HL's `f_e` and `φ_e`.
- `AreConcordant σ l μF μE φ f` (Task 7416, module `Langlands.BaseChange.OrbitalIntegral`)
  is HL's "`φ` et `f` concordent" for a function `φ` on `GL_{r+1}(E)` and a function `f`
  on `GL_{r+1}(F)`, with `μF`, `μE` the Haar measures on the two groups: (a) at every
  regular semisimple `γ ∈ GL_{r+1}(F)` (`IsRegularSemisimple`: separable characteristic
  polynomial) that is not a norm (no `δ ∈ GL_{r+1}(E)` whose norm
  `N(δ) = δ · σ(δ) ⋯ σ^{l-1}(δ)` is conjugate in `GL_{r+1}(E)` to `γ`), every orbital
  integral of `f`, for every right Haar measure on the centralizer of `γ` and every
  compatible quotient measure, vanishes; (b) at every associated pair `(δ, γ)` (`N(δ)`
  conjugate in `GL_{r+1}(E)` to `γ`) with `γ` regular semisimple, the σ-orbital integral
  of `φ` at `δ` equals the orbital integral of `f` at `γ`, for all associated right Haar
  measures on the σ-centralizer of `δ` and the centralizer of `γ` and all quotient
  measures compatible with `μE` and `μF`. The paired Markdown of
  `Langlands.BaseChange.OrbitalIntegral` gives the details.

In words: for an unramified cyclic extension `E/F` of `p`-adic fields with generator `σ`
of its Galois group, the characteristic functions of `GL_{r+1}(O_E)` and
`GL_{r+1}(O_F)` are concordant, for the Haar measures giving these subgroups volume one.

The names `IsCyclicAlgebra` (Task 7405) and `AreConcordant` (Task 7416) are used exactly
as Task 7424 names them.

## How this matches HL, and what is left out

- Characteristic. HL work over non-archimedean local fields of any characteristic. This
  statement is for `PadicField`s, finite extensions of `ℚ_p`, so of characteristic zero:
  this is the setting of the Library's spherical Hecke algebra and of the target of input
  (I3), Task 7375, which needs the fundamental lemma for `p`-adic fields. In
  characteristic zero HL's regular elements (characteristic polynomial a product of
  pairwise distinct irreducible polynomials) are exactly the elements with separable
  characteristic polynomial, which is the Library's `IsRegularSemisimple` used by
  `AreConcordant`.
- Measures. HL fix the Haar measures giving `K` and `K_E` volume one (I.1.1 and I.3.1).
  This is
  `sphericalHaar F r` and `sphericalHaar E r`, the Haar measures of the Library giving
  `GL_{r+1}(O_F)` and `GL_{r+1}(O_E)` mass one.
- Field case. HL's Proposition is stated for `E = E_1`, i.e. `E/F` a field extension,
  which is the case here since `E` is a `PadicField`. The extension of the Proposition to
  an unramified cyclic algebra `E = E_1 × ⋯ × E_1` (HL I.3.2, printed pp. 25-26) and the
  variant with functions transforming by an unramified central character (HL I.3.3,
  printed p. 26) are not part of this statement.
- Strong concordance. HL state strong concordance ("concordent fortement"), which also
  compares the σ-orbital and orbital integrals at associated closed elements that are not
  regular. This statement only asserts concordance at regular semisimple elements, the
  `AreConcordant` of Task 7416; strong concordance is not part of it.
- Relation to statement 9. Statement 9 (`Langlands.BaseChange.FundamentalLemmaStatement`)
  asserts the concordance of `φ` and `b φ` for every `φ` in `SphericalHeckeAlgebra E r`
  and every `b` with `IsUnramifiedBaseChangeHom F E l r b`. Since every algebra
  homomorphism `b` satisfies `b 1 = 1`, this statement is its case `φ = 1` (HL:
  `b(φ_e) = f_e`). It is stated separately because HL (after Kottwitz) and
  Arthur-Clozel prove it first, by a different argument, and deduce the general case from
  it: HL's proof of I.6.6 uses the unit case, in its central-character form I.3.3, at
  the unramified places of a global situation (I.6.7, printed p. 48), and Arthur-Clozel
  use Proposition 4.9 in the trace-formula argument that proves Theorem 4.5 (printed
  p. 42). Unlike statement 9, this statement does not involve `b`.
