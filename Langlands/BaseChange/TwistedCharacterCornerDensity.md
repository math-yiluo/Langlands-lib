# Independent actions on the finite-dimensional Hecke corner

The endomorphisms of a finite-dimensional simple complex module over an algebra are scalar. An eigenvector of an algebra-linear endomorphism supplies a nontrivial kernel for its difference with a scalar map, and simplicity forces that difference to vanish. Between two nonisomorphic simple modules every algebra-linear map is zero.

On their direct sum, any complex-linear action on the first module and zero on the second then commutes with the algebra-linear endomorphisms. Jacobson density on the two finite bases realizes this action by an element of the algebra.

This version of the density argument is used for the two twisted traces in Arthur–Clozel, Chapter 1, Lemma 6.3, printed p. 52.
