# A generic σ-stable representation detected on the σ-elliptic set is a σ-discrete series

Module `Langlands.BaseChange.GenericSigmaEllipticDiscreteStatement`, declaration
`Langlands.BaseChange.GenericSigmaEllipticDiscreteStatement` (level parameters `u v uW`). This
is a statement only. Nothing in the module is proved.

## Statement

Let `F` be a nonarchimedean local field of characteristic zero. Let `E` be a field that is a
cyclic `F`-algebra of degree `l` with generator `σ`, with its module topology over `F`. Let
`r : ℕ` and `n = r + 1`. Give `GL_n(E)` its Borel σ-algebra. Let `τ` be an admissible
representation of `GL_n(E)` on a complex vector space `W : Type uW`. Assume:

1. `τ` is generic for some unitary additive character `ψ` of `E`;
2. `τ` has a central character `χ` with `|χ(z)| = 1` for every central `z`;
3. `A` is an isomorphism of `τ` onto `τ^σ`, and `μ` is a Haar measure on `GL_n(E)`;
4. some Schwartz–Bruhat function `φ` on `GL_n(E)`, whose σ-orbital integrals vanish at every
   σ-regular element that is not σ-elliptic, satisfies `tr(τ(φ) ∘ A) ≠ 0`.

Then `τ` is a σ-discrete series representation.

In Lean, the binders are, in this order: `F : Type u` with `[Field F] [ValuativeRel F]
[TopologicalSpace F] [IsNonarchimedeanLocalField F] [CharZero F]`; `E : Type v` with
`[Field E] [Algebra F E] [TopologicalSpace E] [IsTopologicalRing E] [IsModuleTopology F E]`;
`σ : E ≃ₐ[F] E`; `l r : ℕ`; `hE : IsCyclicAlgebra σ l`; then, by `haveI`/`letI`,
`Module.Finite F E` from `hE.1`, the three topological instances on `GeneralLinear (r + 1) E`
listed below, the Borel σ-algebra `borel _` and its `BorelSpace` instance; then
`W : Type uW` with `[AddCommGroup W] [Module ℂ W]`,
`τ : Representation ℂ (GeneralLinear (r + 1) E) W` and `hτ : IsAdmissible τ`. The proposition
continues

`∀ (ψ : UnitaryAdditiveCharacter E), Whittaker.IsGeneric ψ r τ →
∀ χ : Subgroup.center (GeneralLinear (r + 1) E) →* ℂˣ, HasCentralCharacterRep τ χ →
(∀ z, ‖(χ z : ℂ)‖ = 1) →
∀ (A : SigmaIntertwiner σ (r + 1) τ) (μ : Measure (GeneralLinear (r + 1) E)) [μ.IsHaarMeasure]
(φ : SchwartzBruhat (GeneralLinear (r + 1) E)),
SigmaOrbitalIntegralsVanishOffElliptic σ l μ ⇑φ →
twistedDistribution μ τ hτ A.toLinearMap φ ≠ 0 → IsSigmaDiscreteSeries σ (r + 1) τ`.

## Library notation

- `GeneralLinear n E` is `GL_n(E)`, with the topology coming from `E`. The theorems
  `generalLinear_t2Space_of_finite`, `generalLinear_locallyCompactSpace_of_finite` and
  `generalLinear_totallyDisconnectedSpace_of_finite` (module
  `Langlands.BaseChange.TwistedCharacter`) make it a Hausdorff, locally compact, totally
  disconnected group when `E` is finite over `F`; the statement binds them with `haveI`, after
  `Module.Finite F E` taken from `hE.1`.
- `IsCyclicAlgebra σ l` (module `Langlands.BaseChange.Norm`): `E` is a finite reduced
  `F`-algebra of dimension `l`, `σ` has order `l`, and the fixed points of `σ` are `F`. Here `E`
  is a field, so `E/F` is a cyclic field extension of degree `l` with Galois group generated by
  `σ`. The degree `l` is not assumed to be prime.
- `SigmaIntertwiner σ n τ` (module `Langlands.BaseChange.TwistedCharacter`): an isomorphism of
  representations from `τ` to `τ^σ = τ ∘ entrywiseAut σ n`, that is, a linear automorphism `A`
  of `W` with `A ∘ τ(g) = τ(g^σ) ∘ A`, where `g^σ` applies `σ` to every matrix entry.
  `A.toLinearMap` is its underlying linear map.
- `twistedDistribution μ τ hτ A.toLinearMap φ` (module
  `Langlands.BaseChange.TwistedCharacter.Basic`): the twisted distribution character
  `tr(τ(φ) ∘ A)`, where `τ(φ) = ∫ φ(g) τ(g) dμ(g)` has finite rank because `τ` is admissible.
- `SchwartzBruhat (GeneralLinear n E)` is the space of locally constant compactly supported
  complex functions on `GL_n(E)`.
- `UnitaryAdditiveCharacter E` (module `Langlands.HarmonicAnalysis.Basic`): a continuous
  nontrivial additive character of `E` with values in the unit circle.
- `Whittaker.IsGeneric ψ r τ` (module `Langlands.Representation.Whittaker.GeneralLinear`): `τ`
  is irreducible and admissible and has a nonzero Whittaker functional for the standard
  character of the upper unipotent subgroup of `GL_{r+1}(E)` defined by `ψ`.
- `HasCentralCharacterRep τ χ` (module `Langlands.BaseChange.PseudoCoefficient`): every central
  `z` acts on `W` by the scalar `χ z`. Here `χ : Subgroup.center (GL_n(E)) →* ℂˣ`, and the next
  hypothesis says `‖(χ z : ℂ)‖ = 1` for every `z`.
- `SigmaOrbitalIntegralsVanishOffElliptic σ l μ φ` (module
  `Langlands.BaseChange.EllipticOrbitalIntegral`): for every σ-regular `δ` that is not
  σ-elliptic, every right Haar measure on the σ-centralizer of `δ` and every quotient measure
  compatible with it and with `μ`, the σ-orbital integral `∫ φ(x⁻¹ δ x^σ)` is `0`.
  Henniart–Lemaire call such a function "σ-elliptique" (II.4.10, printed p. 93).
- `IsSigmaDiscreteSeries σ n τ` (module `Langlands.BaseChange.PseudoCoefficient`): `τ` is
  admissible, irreducible, tempered and σ-stable, and it is not isomorphic to the unnormalized
  smooth induction, from the standard upper block parabolic subgroup of any block shape with at
  least two blocks, of a smooth σ-stable representation of the Levi product whose carrier lies
  in the universe of `W`. Since the induction is unnormalized, the inducing representation
  differs from the usual normalized inducing data by the half-modulus of the parabolic, which
  is σ-invariant, so σ-stability of the data is the same in either convention.

## Design

- **Unitary central character, not unitarizability.** The conclusion includes temperedness.
  Without a condition on the central character it fails. Twist a σ-discrete series `τ₀` that
  satisfies the hypotheses by `|det|^s` with `s` real and nonzero. The twist is still generic,
  and `A` is still an isomorphism onto its σ-conjugate because `|det|^s` is σ-invariant. Its
  twisted trace at `φ` is the twisted trace of `τ₀` at `|det|^s · φ`, and multiplication by
  `|det|^s`, a σ-conjugation invariant function, keeps the σ-orbital integrals zero off the
  σ-elliptic set (take `φ = |det|^{-s} · φ₀` for a function `φ₀` that works for `τ₀`). So the
  twist satisfies every hypothesis except the unitarity of the central character, but it is not
  tempered. A unitary central character excludes this, and it is weaker than unitarizability.
  The hypothesis is stated without the open-kernel condition of the Library's
  `UnitaryCentralCharacter` (module `Langlands.Representation.MatrixCoefficient.ModuloCenter`).
  That condition holds for every nonzero smooth representation with a central character (the
  kernel contains the intersection of the centre with the open stabilizer of a nonzero vector),
  so this is only a matter of form.
- **Genericity for some `ψ`.** The genericity hypothesis is taken for one given `ψ`; the
  conclusion does not mention `ψ`.
- **Functions with vanishing σ-orbital integrals off the σ-elliptic set.** These are more
  general than functions supported in the σ-elliptic set: σ-ellipticity is invariant under
  σ-conjugation, so a function supported in the σ-elliptic set has zero σ-orbital integrals off
  it. A twisted pseudo-coefficient of a σ-discrete series, as in the Library's
  `TwistedCuspidalPseudoCoefficientExistenceStatement` (module
  `Langlands.BaseChange.TwistedCuspidalPseudoCoefficientExistenceStatement`), has property (c),
  vanishing σ-orbital integrals off the σ-elliptic set, but it is not in general supported in
  the σ-elliptic set. The present form applies to such functions directly.
- **Hypothesis on one `A`, one `μ` and one `φ`.** All three are universally quantified in front
  of the hypothesis, so the statement needs the nonvanishing only for one choice.
- **Admissibility as a separate binder.** `Whittaker.IsGeneric` includes admissibility, but
  `twistedDistribution` takes an admissibility proof as an argument, so the statement binds
  `hτ : IsAdmissible τ` first. The binder adds no condition.
- **Field `E`, `n = r + 1`.** The sources use a cyclic field extension. The Library's
  genericity predicate has rank index `r` on `GL_{r+1}`.

## Provenance

This is a reformulation of the lemma of Henniart–Lemaire 2011, II.5.8 (printed p. 106: "Soit Π
une représentation lisse irréductible générique σ-stable de G(E). Si Π est σ-elliptique, alors
Π est essentiellement σ-discrète"), which is one direction of II.5.15, Proposition 1 (printed
p. 117), and of Arthur–Clozel 1989, Chapter 1, Lemma 2.11 (printed pp. 18–19: "Then Π is
σ-discrete modulo torsion by a character"). Henniart–Lemaire call `Π` σ-elliptic when `Θ^A_Π`
is not identically zero on the σ-elliptic set (II.4.8, Définition, printed p. 91). Two changes
are derived:

- the hypothesis is the nonvanishing of the twisted distribution on a function with vanishing
  σ-orbital integrals off the σ-elliptic set (Henniart–Lemaire's "fonction σ-elliptique",
  II.4.10, printed p. 93). By the twisted Weyl integration formula it implies that `Θ^A_τ` is
  not identically zero on the σ-elliptic set;
- the unitary central character turns "essentially σ-discrete" (a σ-discrete series twisted
  by an unramified character `ν_E^a ∘ det`, `a` real, in Henniart–Lemaire's proof) into
  σ-discrete.

Henniart–Lemaire work in positive characteristic, but the argument does not depend on the
characteristic. Arthur–Clozel work in characteristic zero.

## Why it is true (sketch; not part of the Lean)

1. By the twisted Weyl integration formula (Henniart–Lemaire II.2.10 (1), printed p. 72) and
   the local integrability of `Θ^A_τ` (Clozel 1987, as stated in the Library's
   `TwistedCharacterLocalIntegrabilityStatement`, which its module states and does not
   prove), `tr(τ(φ) ∘ A)` is a sum of integrals of `Θ^A_τ` against the σ-orbital integrals of
   `φ`. The terms of non-σ-elliptic tori vanish, so `Θ^A_τ` is not identically zero on the
   σ-elliptic set.
2. By Zelevinsky's theorem (Zelevinsky 1980, Theorem 9.7), `τ ≅ Δ₁ × ⋯ × Δ_k` for
   essentially square-integrable `Δᵢ`, and the product is irreducible in every order. The
   Library's `Langlands.JacquetLanglands.padicGenericEllipticCharacterVanishing` (module
   `Langlands.Representation.Classification.GenericEllipticCharacter`) proves the untwisted
   analogue of steps 2–3, for `GL_{r+1}` over a field presented as a `PadicField`: a generic
   representation that is not essentially square-integrable has character value `0` at every
   regular elliptic element.
3. If `τ` is induced from a σ-stable representation of a proper standard Levi subgroup, the
   formula for twisted characters of induced representations (the twisted form of van Dijk's
   formula; Henniart–Lemaire II.4.5, used in the proof of II.5.8; Arthur–Clozel, proof of
   Lemma 2.11, printed p. 19) makes `Θ^A_τ` vanish on the σ-elliptic set, against step 1.
   Grouping the `Δᵢ` by σ-orbits shows that the multiset `{Δᵢ}` is a single σ-orbit without
   repetition, so `τ` is essentially σ-discrete (Arthur–Clozel, Lemma 2.8, printed p. 17).
4. All `Δᵢ` have the same real exponent, because `|σ(x)|_E = |x|_E`. The central character of
   `τ` is the product of theirs, so its unitarity makes every `Δᵢ` square-integrable and `τ`
   tempered. Hence `τ` is a σ-discrete series.

## Related statements

In a twisted globalization of a σ-discrete series by the simple twisted trace formula, the
local component at the chosen place of the σ-stable cuspidal representation that the trace
formula produces is generic (the Library's `Langlands.Automorphic.CuspidalLocalGenericityStatement`
states the genericity of local components of cuspidal representations), and it pairs
nontrivially with a twisted pseudo-coefficient of the σ-discrete series being globalized. With
a unitary central character, this statement then makes that local component a σ-discrete
series. Henniart–Lemaire II.5.14, Corollaire 2 (printed p. 117) gives the forward implication
from essential σ-discreteness to σ-ellipticity.
