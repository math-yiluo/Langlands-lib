# The normalized σ-action on a generic unramified constituent of `GL_n(E)`

This module states, for an unramified cyclic extension `E/F` of p-adic fields with
generator `σ`, that a generic irreducible subquotient `π` of an unramified normalized
principal series of `GL_(r+1)(E)` carries one σ-intertwiner with two normalization
properties: it acts as the identity on the actual Borel Jacquet quotient of `π`, and it
fixes every `GL_(r+1)(O_E)`-fixed vector of `π`. The source is G. Henniart and
B. Lemaire, *Changement de base et induction automorphe pour `GL_n` en caractéristique
non nulle*, Mém. Soc. Math. Fr. 124 (2011), I.5.4, Lemmas 1 and 2, and I.5.5, Lemmas 1
and 2 (exact locations below). The module states the proposition; it does not prove it.

## Statement

`Langlands.BaseChange.UnramifiedGenericSigmaNormalizationStatement.{uF, uE, uV} : Prop`
quantifies over the following data.

- A prime `p`, p-adic fields `F : PadicField.{uF} p` and `E : PadicField.{uE} p` in
  independent universes, an `F.Carrier`-algebra structure on `E.Carrier` compatible with
  the two `ℚ_p`-algebra structures (`IsScalarTower (Padic p) F.Carrier E.Carrier`), an
  `F`-algebra automorphism `σ` of `E.Carrier`, and `l r : ℕ`.
- `IsCyclicAlgebra σ l`: `E` is finite of degree `l` over `F`, `σ` has order `l`, and
  its fixed elements are those of `F`. So `E/F` is cyclic of degree `l` with Galois
  group generated by `σ`; the trivial extension (`l = 1`) is included.
- `E.canonicalTopologicalData.ramificationIndex = F.canonicalTopologicalData.ramificationIndex`:
  the absolute ramification indices over `ℚ_p` agree, so `E/F` is unramified. This is
  the hypothesis used for the same purpose in `Langlands.BaseChange.FundamentalLemmaStatement`.
- A tuple `z : Fin (r + 1) → ℂˣ`. The normalized principal series
  `Langlands.Unramified.principalSeriesOfTuple E z` is the Library's normalized induction
  from the upper Borel subgroup (`Langlands.Correspondence.borelShape r`) of the
  unramified characters `Langlands.Unramified.character E (z i)` of `E^×`, whose value
  at the canonical uniformizer is `z i`.
- A complex vector space `V : Type uV`, in a universe independent of both fields, and a
  representation `π` of `GL_(r+1)(E)` on `V`.
- A nontrivial continuous unitary additive character `ψ` of `E` with
  `Whittaker.IsGeneric ψ r π`. This predicate contains irreducibility, admissibility
  for the canonical topology of `GL_(r+1)(E)`, and a nonzero Whittaker functional for
  the standard character of the upper unipotent subgroup attached to `ψ`. The premise
  says that `π` is generic for some `ψ`; for `GL_n` this does not depend on `ψ`, since
  conjugation by a diagonal matrix changes the additive character.
- `IsSubquotientOf (principalSeriesOfTuple E z) π`: some subrepresentation of the
  principal series maps onto `π` by an intertwining map.

The conclusion is the existence of `A : SigmaIntertwiner σ (r + 1) π`, a linear
automorphism of `V` with `A ∘ π(g) = π(g^σ) ∘ A`, where `g^σ` is the entrywise action
`entrywiseAut σ (r + 1) g`, such that:

1. for every `v : V`,
   `jacquetSigmaIntertwiner σ (borelShape r) A ((borelShape r).jacquetMk π v) = (borelShape r).jacquetMk π v`;
2. for every `v ∈ fixedVectors π (sphericalSubgroup E r)`, `A v = v`.

In (1), `(borelShape r).JacquetModule π` is the Library's actual Jacquet quotient of `V`
by the span of the `π(u)v − v` with `u` in the unipotent radical of the upper Borel
subgroup, `(borelShape r).jacquetMk π v` is the class `[v]`, and
`jacquetSigmaIntertwiner σ (borelShape r) A` is the descent of `A` to this quotient,
which sends `[v]` to `[A v]` (`jacquetSigmaIntertwiner_mk`). Property (1) therefore says
that the descended linear isomorphism is the identity of the whole quotient, on the
actual module, generalized weight spaces and multiplicities included, and not only on a
semisimplification. The same quotient is the carrier of the normalized Jacquet
representation `E.canonicalTopologicalData.normalizedJacquet (borelShape r) π`, so (1)
does not depend on whether the torus acts with or without the modulus normalization.
The shape `borelShape r` is definitionally `BlockShape.singletons (r + 1)`
(`Langlands.Unramified.borelShape_eq_singletons`).

In (2), `sphericalSubgroup E r` is `GL_(r+1)(O_E)` for the canonical valuation ring of
`E`. When `π` has no nonzero spherical vector, (2) holds for every `A`.

The intertwiner is a linear equivalence and `V` is nonzero because `π` is irreducible,
so `A` is a nonzero operator. Both properties are asserted for the same `A`.

## The two normalization properties and their mathematical basis

The source uses the following conventions. Henniart–Lemaire I.5.1 call `π` σ-stable
when it is isomorphic to `π^σ : g ↦ π(g^σ)`, an isomorphism being an automorphism `A` of
the space with `A ∘ π(g) = π^σ(g) ∘ A`; this is `SigmaIntertwiner`. I.5.2 descends `A`
to the unnormalized Jacquet module by passage to the quotient, as
`jacquetSigmaIntertwiner` does. The characters `character E (z i)` are unramified, and
every automorphism of `E` preserves the normalized valuation, so they are σ-stable
(I.5.3, the paragraph after the proof of the Proposition, printed p. 31).

**The canonical operator of the principal series.** Write `I` for
`principalSeriesOfTuple E z`. The identity maps are σ-intertwiners of the one-dimensional
inducing characters, and `Langlands.BaseChange.inducedSigmaIntertwiner` with these
identity maps is the operator `f ↦ (g ↦ f(g^{σ⁻¹}))` of `I`. It satisfies the
convention above. I.5.4 (printed p. 33) writes the same canonical operator `A_{σ,χ}` as
`f ↦ (g ↦ f(g^σ))`; replacing `σ` by the generator `σ⁻¹` passes between the two
formulas, and the arguments below do not depend on the choice.

**(1) Identity on the Jacquet quotient.** The proof of I.5.4, Lemma 2 (printed p. 35)
states that the canonical operator descends to the identity of the minimal Jacquet
module of the principal series "by the definition of `A_{σ,ψ}`". One argument: the
upper Borel subgroup `P` and the permutation matrices are fixed by `σ`, so the operator
preserves the filtration of `I` by supports in unions of Bruhat cells `PwP`. By the
geometric lemma (Bernstein–Zelevinsky, cited by Henniart–Lemaire as [7, 2.12]) the
Jacquet module of each graded piece is one-dimensional, detected by an integral
`f ↦ ∫ f(wn) dn` over a unipotent group isomorphic to a power of `E`; `σ` preserves its
Haar measure, so the operator acts by `1` on each graded piece. The descended operator is
therefore unipotent on the finite-dimensional Jacquet module of `I`. Its `l`-th power
is the identity, since `σ^l = 1`. A unipotent operator of finite order on a complex
vector space is the identity.

I.5.4, Lemma 1 (printed p. 33) passes to the generic constituent. By Bernstein–Zelevinsky
(cited as [7, 4.4 and 4.6]) the principal series has a unique generic irreducible
subquotient `I*`, and a one-dimensional space of maps `Φ` to the Whittaker space of a
σ-stable additive character of the upper unipotent subgroup. The canonical operator
preserves `ker Φ` and the unique irreducible subrepresentation `I*` of `I / ker Φ`, so it
induces a σ-intertwiner of `I*`. A generic irreducible subquotient `π` of `I` is
isomorphic to `I*`; transporting along this isomorphism gives `A`. Exactness of the
Jacquet functor makes the Jacquet module of `I*` a subspace of that of `I / ker Φ`,
which is a quotient of that of `I`, compatibly with the descended operators. So the
descended operator on the Borel Jacquet quotient of `π` is the identity, as the proof
of I.5.4, Lemma 2 concludes.

**(2) Spherical vectors.** If `π` also has a nonzero `GL_(r+1)(O_E)`-fixed vector, it is
isomorphic both to `I*` and to the unique spherical subquotient `I**`, and by I.5.5,
Lemma 1(3) (printed p. 36) the principal series is then irreducible, so `π` is
isomorphic to `I`. The proof of I.5.5, Lemma 2 (printed p. 39) uses that the canonical
operator acts trivially on the `K_E`-fixed vectors of `I`: these form the line of the
function `f₀(bk) = (δ^{1/2} χ)(b)`, and `f₀(g^{σ⁻¹}) = f₀(g)` because `σ` preserves the
Borel subgroup, `GL_(r+1)(O_E)`, the unramified characters and the modulus `δ`. Then the
operator `A` obtained from the canonical operator by the isomorphism `π ≅ I` satisfies
both (1) and (2). If `π` has no spherical vector, the operator of the previous paragraph
satisfies (1) and (2) holds trivially.

**Uniqueness.** These remarks are not part of the Lean statement. Two σ-intertwiners of
an irreducible admissible `π` differ by a nonzero scalar (Schur's lemma), and the Borel
Jacquet quotient of an irreducible subquotient of a principal series is nonzero, so at
most one `A` satisfies (1). By I.5.4, Lemma 1 and the proof of Lemma 2 it is the
operator `A_σ(π)` of I.5.4, normalized by `λ ∘ A_σ(π) = λ` for a Whittaker functional
`λ` of the σ-stable character `θ ∘ Tr_{E/F}` of the upper unipotent subgroup. The
statement records the normalization by its two properties instead of by the Whittaker
functional. In the source, (1) reduces the trace of a torus element composed with the
descended operator on the Jacquet quotient to the ordinary trace of that element, the
sum of the Jacquet characters counted with multiplicity (the formula of I.5.4,
Lemma 2), and (2) reduces the σ-twisted trace of a spherical `π` on σ-invariant
spherical Hecke functions to the ordinary trace (the proof of I.5.5, Lemma 2).

## Comparison with the source

- Henniart–Lemaire treat the generic constituent `I*_χ` through the canonical operator
  of the full principal series. The Lean statement takes any representation `π` that is
  generic and a subquotient of the principal series; such a `π` is isomorphic to `I*_χ`,
  and the conclusion is invariant under isomorphism. The compatibility of `A` with the
  canonical operator of the full principal series is not part of the conclusion: the two
  properties concern only `π`, its Jacquet quotient and its spherical vectors.
- The source works with all regular weights `ν` and the Jacquet modules `π_ν` for the
  minimal Levi `M_ν = A_0`. These are the Borel Jacquet quotient of this statement.
- The hypotheses `IsCyclicAlgebra σ l` and the equality of ramification indices
  describe the setting of Chapter I of Henniart–Lemaire, an unramified cyclic extension
  of degree `l` with generator `σ`. The argument above uses from them that `σ` is an
  automorphism of `E` of finite order.
