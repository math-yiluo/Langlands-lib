# Independence of σ-lifts of essentially discrete series from the generator

This module `Langlands.BaseChange.SigmaLiftGeneratorIndependenceStatement` (namespace
`Langlands.BaseChange`) states, as the proposition
`SigmaLiftGeneratorIndependenceStatement.{u, v, uV, uW}`, that for an essentially
square-integrable representation `π` of `GL_n(F)`, a σ-lift of `π` to `GL_n(E)` is a σ'-lift
of `π` for every other generator `σ'` of `Gal(E/F)`. The module contains this one
declaration. It states the proposition and does **not** prove it.

## Setting

`GL_n(R)` is the Library's `GeneralLinear n R`. The statement quantifies over:

- a nonarchimedean local field `F : Type u` of characteristic zero (`[Field F]
  [ValuativeRel F] [TopologicalSpace F] [IsNonarchimedeanLocalField F] [CharZero F]`);
- a field `E : Type v` that is an `F`-algebra with a topology making it a topological ring
  that is the `F`-module topology (`[Field E] [Algebra F E] [TopologicalSpace E]
  [IsTopologicalRing E] [IsModuleTopology F E]`);
- `σ σ' : E ≃ₐ[F] E` and `l n : ℕ` with `hσ : IsCyclicAlgebra σ l` and
  `IsCyclicAlgebra σ' l` (module `Langlands.BaseChange.Norm`). `IsCyclicAlgebra σ l` says
  that `E` is finite over `F` of dimension `l`, reduced, that `σ` has order `l`, and that the
  elements of `E` fixed by `σ` are those of `F`. For a field `E` this means that `E/F` is a
  cyclic extension of degree `l` whose Galois group is generated by `σ`. So `σ` and `σ'` are
  two generators of the same cyclic group `Gal(E/F)` of order `l`.

The binders, instance hypotheses and topological instances are those of
`DiscreteSeriesSigmaLiftStatement` (module
`Langlands.BaseChange.DiscreteSeriesSigmaLiftStatement`), with the second automorphism `σ'`
and the hypothesis `IsCyclicAlgebra σ' l` added. The first conjunct `hσ.1` of
`IsCyclicAlgebra σ l` gives `Module.Finite F E`. With it, the topologies of `GL_n(F)` and
`GL_n(E)` are made Hausdorff, locally compact and totally disconnected inside the statement
(`haveI`) by the Library's `LocalFieldTopology.generalLinear_t2Space` and companions and by
`generalLinear_t2Space_of_finite` and companions of `Langlands.BaseChange.TwistedCharacter`.
These are the instance arguments of `IsSigmaLift`, for `σ` and for `σ'` alike.

## Statement

`SigmaLiftGeneratorIndependenceStatement.{u, v, uV, uW}`: for every complex vector space
`V : Type uV` and every representation `π : Representation ℂ (GL_n(F)) V` with
`hπ : IsAdmissible π`, `π.IsIrreducible` (Mathlib) and
`JacquetLanglands.IsEssentiallySquareIntegrable π`, for every complex vector space
`W : Type uW` and every representation `ρ : Representation ℂ (GL_n(E)) W` with
`hρ : IsAdmissible ρ` and `ρ.IsIrreducible`, and for every `A : SigmaIntertwiner σ n ρ` and
`c : ℂ`: if `IsSigmaLift σ l π hπ ρ hρ A c`, then there are `A' : SigmaIntertwiner σ' n ρ`
and `c' : ℂ` with `IsSigmaLift σ' l π hπ ρ hρ A' c'`.

In words: if an irreducible admissible representation `ρ` of `GL_n(E)` is a σ-lift of an
irreducible admissible essentially square-integrable representation `π` of `GL_n(F)`, then
`ρ` is also a σ'-lift of `π`.

**The intertwiners and constants are not compared.** The statement asserts only that some
σ'-intertwiner `A'` and some constant `c'` exist. It says nothing about how `A'` is related
to `A`, nor `c'` to `c`.

Since `σ` and `σ'` enter symmetrically, the statement applied with `σ` and `σ'` exchanged
gives the converse: an irreducible admissible representation of `GL_n(E)` is a σ-lift of `π`
for some intertwiner and constant exactly when it is a σ'-lift of `π` for some intertwiner and
constant. This is the sense of HL's "la notion de relèvement est indépendante du choix de σ".

**Names.** HL write `Π` for representations of `GL_n(E)`. The letters `Π` and `Σ` are reserved
tokens in Lean, so the Lean statement writes `ρ` for HL's `Π`, and `π` as in HL.

## Universe parameters

- `u`: the carrier of `F`;
- `v`: the carrier of `E`;
- `uV`: the carrier `V` of `π`;
- `uW`: the carrier `W` of `ρ`.

All four are free, and `V` and `W` are universally quantified together with their
`AddCommGroup` and `Module ℂ` instances.

The statement can be instantiated at `.{uK, uK, uV, max uK uV}`. These are the universes of
the data of `IsSigmaLiftOnSupercuspidals` (module `Langlands.BaseChange.SigmaLiftOnSupercuspidals`):

- the base and top fields `L M : PadicField.{uK} p` of a cyclic step of p-adic fields have
  carriers in `Type uK` (`u = v = uK`);
- the supercuspidal representatives `GeneralLinear.SupercuspidalRep.{uK, uV}` are objects of
  `SmoothRep.{0, uK, uV}`, with carriers in `Type uV`;
- the lifted representations `τ : SmoothRep.{0, uK, max uK uV}` have carriers in
  `Type (max uK uV)`.

Supercuspidal representations of `GL_n` over a p-adic field are essentially
square-integrable (`JacquetLanglands.padicSupercuspidalEssentialSquareIntegrability`). So at
these universes the statement applies to a supercuspidal representative and a σ-lift `τ` of
it, when `τ` is irreducible. It makes the same `τ` a σ'-lift for any other generator `σ'`. For
a cyclic extension of prime degree, every `σ ≠ 1` of the Galois group is a generator.

## Explanations

- **σ-lifts.** `IsSigmaLift σ l π hπ ρ hρ A c` (module `Langlands.BaseChange.TwistedCharacter`)
  is HL's σ-relèvement (II.2.7, printed p. 67): `c ≠ 0`, and for all Haar measures on
  `GL_n(F)` and `GL_n(E)` and every pair `(φ, f)` of concordant test functions
  (`AreConcordant σ l` of `Langlands.BaseChange.OrbitalIntegral`), the twisted trace
  `trace(ρ(φ) ∘ A)` equals `c` times the trace `trace(π(f))`. Concordance depends on `σ`: it
  is defined through the norm `cyclicNorm σ l`, `δ ↦ δ δ^σ ⋯ δ^{σ^{l-1}}` (module
  `Langlands.BaseChange.Norm`), and the σ-centralizers and σ-orbital integrals. The
  σ-intertwiners, `A ∘ ρ(g) = ρ(g^σ) ∘ A` (`SigmaIntertwiner`), depend on `σ` too. So the
  hypothesis and the conclusion of the statement are lift relations defined through different
  data.
- **The constants.** AC's lifts use the canonical intertwining operator `I_σ` and constant `1`
  (Definition 6.1). HL show `c = 1` for the Whittaker-normalized intertwiner `I_σ(Π)`
  (II.4.6, Théorème (1)). That normalization is not defined in the Library's `IsSigmaLift`,
  which takes an arbitrary intertwiner and some constant. Replacing `A` by a nonzero multiple
  `a • A` replaces `c` by `a * c`. Accordingly the statement asserts only the existence of
  some `A'` and `c'`.
- **Form of the statement.** HL's proof of point (3) in II.6.18 shows that a σ-lift and a
  σ'-lift of the same discrete series are isomorphic. Mathematically this gives the form stated
  here, with three further facts: the existence of a σ'-lift (Théorème (1) of II.4.6 for
  `σ'`); the transport of a lift along an isomorphism of representations (in the Library,
  `IsSigmaLift.transport` of `Langlands.BaseChange.SigmaLiftTransport`); and, for
  essentially discrete `π`, the twisting of II.4.7. This derivation is not formalized here.
- **Discrete and essentially discrete.** HL's "série discrète" is an irreducible
  square-integrable representation with unitary central character, which is
  `JacquetLanglands.IsSquareIntegrable` (module `Langlands.JacquetLanglands.EssentiallySquareIntegrable`:
  admissible, unitary central character, matrix coefficients square-integrable modulo the
  centre). HL's "essentiellement discrète" (II.4.7) is a twist of a discrete series by a
  character `ω ∘ det`. The Library's `IsEssentiallySquareIntegrable` uses only the twists
  `|det|^s`, `s` real. The two classes agree because every character of `F^×` is a unitary
  character times some `|·|^s`. The twist `ω_E = ω ∘ N_{E/F}` of II.4.7 uses the field norm
  `N_{E/F}`, which does not depend on the generator, and leaves the intertwiner unchanged. So
  the reduction of II.4.7 serves `σ` and `σ'` alike. Theorem 6.2 (c) of AC is stated for
  tempered representations.
- **Field.** HL's Theorem II.4.6 and its proof by the global method (II.6.1, printed p. 118)
  assume that `E` is a field, and so does AC's Theorem 6.2. So does this statement. The degree
  `l` is not assumed prime.
- **`CharZero F`.** As in the statements on orbital integrals, twisted characters and
  pseudo-coefficients: the Library's `IsRegularSemisimple` asks for a separable characteristic
  polynomial, while HL's regular elements have squarefree characteristic polynomial. The two
  agree in characteristic zero, where HL's results and AC's apply verbatim. In characteristic
  zero the proof of the source is AC's (HL II.6.1).

## Proof

This file states the proposition and does not prove it. AC prove Theorem 6.2 (c) by the
global construction of the local lifting (§6.3, printed p. 57). HL prove point (3) by the
global method and a comparison of Rankin–Selberg L-functions (II.6.18). A later proof must not
derive this statement from a result that depends on it.
