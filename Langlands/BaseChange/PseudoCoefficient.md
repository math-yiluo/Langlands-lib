# Tempered representations, σ-discrete series and (twisted) pseudo-coefficients

Pseudo-coefficients are the local test functions on which the simple trace
formula and its twisted form are evaluated in the local comparison for cyclic
base change of `GL_n`. This module defines them, together with the notions
they are tested against. It has two parts.

- A generic part, for a topological group `G`: temperedness for every Haar
  measure on `G / Z`, the central character of a representation that is not
  bundled as a `SmoothRep`, pseudo-coefficients with a fixed central
  character, and twisted pseudo-coefficients for a group automorphism `θ` of
  `G`.
- A `GL_n` part: the entrywise action of `σ` on the standard Levi subgroups of
  `GL_n(E)`, and σ-discrete series representations of `GL_n(E)`.

Only definitions and their elementary lemmas are here. The existence of
pseudo-coefficients is stated, not proved, in
`Langlands.BaseChange.PseudoCoefficientExistenceStatement` (untwisted) and
`Langlands.BaseChange.TwistedPseudoCoefficientExistenceStatement` (twisted).

## Conventions

`GL_n(R)` is the Library's `GeneralLinear n R`. For `σ : E ≃ₐ[F] E`,
`g^σ = entrywiseAut σ n g` applies `σ` to every entry of `g`
(`Langlands.BaseChange.Norm`). `Θ_π` is the Library's distribution character
`Character.distribution μ π hπ`, `f ↦ trace π(f)` with
`π(f) = ∫ f(g) π(g) dμ(g)`. `Θ^A_Π` is `twistedDistribution μ Π hΠ A`
(`Langlands.BaseChange.TwistedCharacter.Basic`), `f ↦ trace(Π(f) ∘ A)`
for an endomorphism `A`. The symbol `Π` is reserved in Lean, so the representations
of `GL_n(E)` that HL call `Π` are named `τ` in the Lean source; this file
keeps HL's `Π`.

For an automorphism `θ` of `G`, an element `A` of
`Π.Equiv (Π.comp θ.toMonoidHom)` is a linear automorphism of the space of `Π`
with `A ∘ Π(g) = Π(θ g) ∘ A` for every `g`: an isomorphism of `Π` onto
`Π^θ = Π ∘ θ`. For `θ = entrywiseAut σ n` this is the type
`SigmaIntertwiner σ n Π` of `Langlands.BaseChange.TwistedCharacter`, which
records the comparison with HL's isomorphisms of `Π` onto `Π^σ`. HL's
condition that `A^d` be a scalar is not imposed. For irreducible admissible
`Π` it holds by Schur's lemma (not proved here).

## Generic definitions

Let `G` be a group with a topology making it a topological group, and `V`,
`W` complex vector spaces.

1. `IsTemperedRep ρ`, for `ρ : Representation ℂ G V`. Give the central
   quotient `G / Z` (`G ⧸ Subgroup.center G`) its Borel σ-algebra. Then
   `IsTemperedRep ρ` says that the Library's `IsTemperedModCenter ρ μ`
   (`Langlands/Representation/Classification/Tempered.lean`) holds for
   every Haar measure `μ` on `G / Z`: `ρ` is admissible, it has a unitary
   central character (a `UnitaryCentralCharacter ρ`), and every absolute
   smooth matrix coefficient is in `L^{2+ε}(G / Z, μ)` for every `ε > 0`.
   For `GL_n` over a `p`-adic field this is the Library's
   `PadicField.TopologicalData.IsTempered` (lemma
   `isTemperedRep_iff_isTempered` below); here `G` is any topological
   group. It is the notion "tempered" ("tempérée") of HL II.1.7 and AC
   Chapter 1, §2.3. The quantifier uses Mathlib's class
   `Measure.IsHaarMeasure`: left invariance, finiteness on compact sets and
   positivity on nonempty open sets, with no regularity condition. For a
   general topological group, two measures in this class need not be
   positive scalar multiples of each other, and the predicate asks for the
   condition at each of them. When `G / Z` is locally compact and second
   countable, two Haar measures on it do differ by a positive constant
   (Mathlib's `Measure.isMulLeftInvariant_eq_smul` and
   `Measure.haarScalarFactor_pos_of_isHaarMeasure`). Such a constant does
   not change `L^p`-membership, so quantifying over all Haar measures then
   agrees with using a single fixed one. This is the case for `GL_n(E)`
   when `E` is a finite commutative algebra over a nonarchimedean local
   field `F` with its module topology, as in lemma
   `exists_isHaarMeasure_centralQuotient_of_finite` and in the two existence
   statements. Then `GL_n(E)` is locally compact
   (`generalLinear_locallyCompactSpace_of_finite`) and second countable,
   since its topology is induced from a finite product of copies of `E`,
   and `E` is homeomorphic to `F^l` for `l = dim_F E`. Its quotient by the
   closed subgroup `Z` is also locally compact and second countable, because
   the quotient map is open. This comparison is not formalized. When `G / Z` has
   no Haar measure the predicate is vacuous, and lemma
   `exists_isHaarMeasure_centralQuotient_of_finite` below excludes this for
   `GL_n(E)`.

2. `HasCentralCharacterRep ρ χ`, for `ρ : Representation ℂ G V` and a
   character `χ : Subgroup.center G →* ℂˣ` of the centre (only the group
   structure of `G` is used): `ρ(z) v = χ(z) v` for every central `z` and
   every `v`. It is the Library's `SmoothRep.HasCentralCharacter`
   (`Langlands/Representation/Category/Basic.lean`) for a representation that
   is not bundled as a `SmoothRep`, and HL's `ω_Π = χ`. No continuity or
   unitarity of `χ` is required.

In items 3 and 4, `G` is moreover Hausdorff, locally compact and totally
disconnected with its Borel σ-algebra, and `μ` is a left-invariant measure on
`G` that is finite on compact sets, as for `Character.distribution` and
`twistedDistribution`. Test functions are in `SchwartzBruhat G`, the
locally constant compactly supported complex functions `C^∞_c(G)`.

3. `IsPseudoCoefficient μ χ π hπ f`, for `π : Representation ℂ G V`
   (`V : Type uV`), `hπ : IsAdmissible π` and `f : SchwartzBruhat G`. It is
   the conjunction of:
   - (a) `π` has central character `χ`;
   - (b) `Θ_π(f) = 1`;
   - (c) `Θ_{π'}(f) = 0` for every complex vector space `V'` in the universe
     `uV` of `V` and every admissible, irreducible, tempered
     (`IsTemperedRep`) representation `π'` of `G` on `V'` with central
     character `χ` and not isomorphic to `π` (`IsEmpty (π'.Equiv π)`).

   This is Kazhdan's Theorem K(a), `Tr π'(h_π) = δ_{π,π'}` for irreducible
   tempered `π'`, taken with a fixed central character as in AC Chapter 1,
   Corollary 2.10 and HL II.5.13 (with `E = F`, `σ = id`).

4. `IsTwistedPseudoCoefficient μ θ χ Π hΠ A φ`, for an automorphism
   `θ : G ≃* G`, `Π : Representation ℂ G W` (`W : Type uW`),
   `hΠ : IsAdmissible Π`, an isomorphism `A : Π.Equiv (Π.comp θ.toMonoidHom)`
   of `Π` onto `Π^θ`, and `φ : SchwartzBruhat G`. It is the conjunction of:
   - (a) `Π` has central character `χ`;
   - (b) `Θ^A_Π(φ) = 1`, with `A` used through its underlying linear map;
   - (c) `Θ^{A'}_{Π'}(φ) = 0` for every complex vector space `W'` in the
     universe `uW` of `W`, every admissible, irreducible, tempered
     representation `Π'` of `G` on `W'` with central character `χ` and not
     isomorphic to `Π`, and every isomorphism `A'` of `Π'` onto `Π'^θ`.

   With `θ = entrywiseAut σ n` this is HL II.5.13, Définition (printed
   p. 113), and AC Chapter 1, Corollary 2.10 (printed p. 18). In (c) a
   representation `Π'` that is not θ-stable has no `A'`, and the condition is
   empty for it; HL and AC test only σ-stable `Π'`.

## Generic lemmas

- `HasCentralCharacterRep.of_equiv`: a central character is transported along
  an isomorphism `ρ ≅ ρ'` of representations.
- `isTemperedRep_iff_of_equiv`: `IsTemperedRep` is invariant under
  isomorphism (from the Library's `isTemperedModCenter_iff_of_equiv`).
- `IsTemperedRep.isAdmissible`: if `G` is locally compact, a tempered
  representation is admissible. The central quotient is then locally compact
  and carries a Haar measure (`Measure.haarMeasure`), to which the
  definition is applied.
- `isTemperedRep_iff_isTempered`: for a `p`-adic field `F : PadicField p`
  with topological data `T`, and with the topology of `T` on `GL_n(F)`,
  `IsTemperedRep ρ ↔ T.IsTempered ρ` for every representation `ρ` of
  `GL_n(F)`. Both sides unfold to the same proposition.
- `smoothRep_hasCentralCharacter_iff`: for a bundled smooth representation
  `A : SmoothRep ℂ G`, `A.HasCentralCharacter χ` is
  `HasCentralCharacterRep A.representation χ`.
- `twistedDistribution_eq_of_equivariant`: let `ρ` on `V` and `ρ'` on `V'`
  be admissible, `A`, `A'` endomorphisms of `V`, `V'`, and `e : V ≃ V'` a
  linear equivalence with `e(ρ(g) v) = ρ'(g)(e v)` and `e(A v) = A'(e v)`
  for all `g` and `v`. Then `Θ^A_ρ = Θ^{A'}_{ρ'}`. The proof writes
  `ρ'(f) ∘ A'` as the conjugate `e ∘ (ρ(f) ∘ A) ∘ e^{-1}` (using the
  Library's `Hecke.integratedAction_intertwiner`) and uses the invariance of
  the finite-rank trace under conjugation (`FiniteRankEnd.trace_conjugate`).
  It is the twisted analogue of the Library's
  `Character.distribution_eq_of_equivariant`.
- `IsTwistedPseudoCoefficient.twistedDistribution_eq_one`: if `φ` is a
  twisted pseudo-coefficient of `(Π, A)`, then `Θ^{A'}_{Π'}(φ) = 1` for
  every admissible `Π'` on a complex vector space `W'` (in any universe),
  every isomorphism `A'` of `Π'` onto `Π'^θ`, and every isomorphism
  `e : Π' ≅ Π` with `e ∘ A' = A ∘ e`, that is, at every `(Π', A')`
  isomorphic to `(Π, A)`. This follows from (b) and the previous lemma.
- `isPseudoCoefficient_of_isTwistedPseudoCoefficient_refl`: for `θ` the
  identity automorphism (`MulEquiv.refl G`) and an `A` whose underlying
  linear map is the identity, a twisted pseudo-coefficient of `(π, A)` is a
  pseudo-coefficient of `π`. Condition (b) follows from
  `twistedDistribution_one` (`Θ^1_π = Θ_π`, in
  `Langlands.BaseChange.TwistedCharacter.Basic`), and condition (c) is applied
  to the identity isomorphism of `π'` onto `π' ∘ id`.

## `GL_n` part: definitions

In items 5–7, `F` is a commutative ring, `E` a commutative `F`-algebra,
`σ : E ≃ₐ[F] E` and `n : ℕ`. No topology on `F` is used; this is the context
of `SigmaIntertwiner` and `IsSigmaStable` in `Langlands.BaseChange.TwistedCharacter`. `B : BlockShape n` is the Library's
`Langlands.GeneralLinear.BlockShape` (`Langlands/Group/GL/Blocks.lean`): an
ordered list of positive block sizes `B.size i` summing to `n`, with
`B.length` blocks. It has the Levi product
`B.Levi E = ∏ᵢ GL_{B.size i}(E)`, the block diagonal embedding
`B.leviEmbedding E` into `GL_n(E)`, the standard upper block parabolic
subgroup `B.parabolic E` of `GL_n(E)`, and the projection
`B.leviProjection E : B.parabolic E →* B.Levi E` onto the diagonal blocks.

5. `leviAut σ B : B.Levi E ≃* B.Levi E` applies `σ` to every entry of every
   block: it is `MulEquiv.piCongrRight` of the automorphisms
   `entrywiseAut σ (B.size i)` of the factors.

6. `IsLeviSigmaStable σ B Ξ`, for a representation `Ξ` of `B.Levi E` on a
   complex vector space `X`: `Ξ` is isomorphic to `Ξ^σ = Ξ ∘ leviAut σ B`,
   that is, `Nonempty (Ξ.Equiv (Ξ.comp (leviAut σ B).toMonoidHom))`.

7. `IsSigmaDiscreteSeries σ n Π`, for `Π : Representation ℂ (GL_n(E)) W`
   (`W : Type uW`), where `E` moreover carries a topology making it a
   topological ring (this gives the topologies of `GL_n(E)` and of the Levi
   products). It is the conjunction of:
   - `Π` is admissible, irreducible and tempered (`IsTemperedRep`);
   - `Π` is σ-stable (`IsSigmaStable σ n Π`, from `Langlands.BaseChange.TwistedCharacter`: `Π ≅ Π^σ`);
   - non-induction: for every block shape `B` of `n` with at least two
     blocks, every complex vector space `X` in the universe `uW` of `W`, and
     every smooth representation `Ξ` of `B.Levi E` on `X` with
     `IsLeviSigmaStable σ B Ξ`, `Π` is not isomorphic to
     `Induction.smoothInduced (B.parabolic E) (Ξ ∘ B.leviProjection E)`, the
     Library's (unnormalized) smooth induction from the standard parabolic
     `B.parabolic E` of `Ξ` inflated through the Levi projection.

   This is HL II.4.6 (printed p. 88) and AC Chapter 1, §2.3 (printed p. 17):
   "tempered, σ-stable, and cannot be induced from a (tempered) σ-stable
   representation of a standard Levi subgroup". A block shape `B` is HL's
   sequence `α` (II.3.1): `B.length = l(α)`, `B.Levi E` is `G_α(E)` (as the
   product `∏ᵢ GL_{αᵢ}(E)`, embedded block diagonally by `B.leviEmbedding E`),
   `B.parabolic E` is `P_α(E)`, and `B.leviProjection E` is the projection
   `P_α(E) → G_α(E)` with kernel `U_α(E)`. The entrywise action
   `leviAut σ B` (item 5) is the action of `σ` on `G_α(E)` that defines
   HL's σ-stable representations of `G_α(E)`. So the block shapes with at
   least two blocks give exactly HL's `α` with `l(α) > 1`, that is, the
   proper standard parabolic subgroups and their standard Levi subgroups. HL II.1.7 (printed
   p. 59) states the definition informally with "a proper σ-stable Levi
   subgroup", and II.4.6 makes it precise with these standard subgroups. See
   "Induction" below for the comparison of the induction and of the inducing
   representations.

## `GL_n` part: lemmas

- `leviAut_apply`: `(leviAut σ B m)ᵢ = entrywiseAut σ (B.size i) (mᵢ)`.
- `entrywiseAut_leviEmbedding`: `σ` commutes with the block diagonal
  embedding, `(B.leviEmbedding E m)^σ = B.leviEmbedding E (leviAut σ B m)`.
  Proof: compare entries in block coordinates (`BlockShape.indexEquiv`);
  off-diagonal blocks are `0` and `σ 0 = 0`.
- `entrywiseAut_mem_parabolic_iff`: `g^σ ∈ B.parabolic E ↔ g ∈ B.parabolic E`,
  because membership says that certain entries vanish and `σ` is injective.
- `leviProjection_entrywiseAut`: for `p ∈ B.parabolic E`, the Levi projection
  of `p^σ` (which lies in `B.parabolic E` by the previous lemma) is
  `leviAut σ B` of the Levi projection of `p`. Both sides have the same
  entries by definition.
- `exists_isHaarMeasure_centralQuotient_of_finite`: let `F` be a
  nonarchimedean local field (`IsNonarchimedeanLocalField F`) and `E` a
  commutative `F`-algebra, finite over `F`, with a topology making it a
  topological ring that is the module topology over `F`. With the Borel
  σ-algebra on `GL_n(E) / Z`, this quotient carries a Haar measure. The
  proof uses that `GL_n(E)` is locally compact (`generalLinear_locallyCompactSpace_of_finite` in
  `Langlands.BaseChange.TwistedCharacter`), hence so is its central
  quotient. Compare the Library's
  `JacquetLanglands.exists_isHaarMeasure_centralQuotient` for `E = F`. So
  `IsTemperedRep`, and hence item 7, is not vacuous for `GL_n(E)`.
- `isSigmaDiscreteSeries_trivial`: for every `E` and `σ` as in item 7, the
  trivial representation of `GL_1(E)` on `ℂ` (Mathlib's
  `Representation.trivial`) is a σ-discrete series representation. It is
  smooth and one-dimensional, hence admissible and irreducible. `GL_1(E)` is
  commutative, so its central quotient is a point; the trivial character is a
  unitary central character and every absolute matrix coefficient is a
  constant on a point, in `L^{2+ε}` for the finite measure `μ`. The identity
  is an isomorphism onto its σ-twist. No block shape of `1` has two blocks
  (`BlockShape.length_le`). So item 7 is satisfiable.

## Remarks

**Test functions.** HL and AC take pseudo-coefficients in `C^∞_c(G, χ)`: the
functions `φ` on `G` that are locally constant, compactly supported modulo
the centre `Z`, and satisfy `φ(zg) = χ(z)^{-1} φ(g)`; for such `φ`, `Π'(φ)` is
the integral over `Z \ G` against a Haar measure `dḡ` on `Z \ G` (AC
Corollary 2.10, footnote; HL II.2.7, printed p. 67). Here test functions
are taken in `SchwartzBruhat G = C^∞_c(G)`, traces are taken for the Haar
measure `μ = dg` on `G` itself, and condition (c) of items 3 and 4 ranges
only over representations with central character `χ`. Fix a Haar measure
`dz` on `Z` and let `f_χ(g) = ∫_Z f(zg) χ(z) dz` be the average of
`f ∈ C^∞_c(G)` over `Z` against `χ`. It lies in `C^∞_c(G, χ)`, and every
function in `C^∞_c(G, χ)` is such an average (HL II.2.6, printed p. 66). HL
II.2.7 (printed p. 67) shows that if the quotient measure is
`dḡ = dg / dz`, formed with the same `dz` that defines `f ↦ f_χ`, then
`Θ^{A'}_{Π'}(f) = Θ^{A'}_{Π'}(f_χ)` for every `f ∈ C^∞_c(G)`, every
admissible `Π'` with central character `χ` and every isomorphism `A'` of
`Π'` onto `Π'^σ`, the left side computed with `dg` and the right side with
`dḡ`. HL state the untwisted analogue for `GL_n(F)` in the same place,
averaging over the norm subgroup `N_{E/F}(E^×)` of the centre `F^×`, which
is the whole centre when `E = F`; it is the case `A' = 1`. If the quotient measure is instead `c · dg / dz` with
`c > 0`, the right side is multiplied by `c`. For instance, for
`G = GL_1(F) = Z`, the trivial representation, `f` the indicator function
of the unit group `O_F^×`, `dz` giving `O_F^×` volume `1` and `dg = 2 dz`,
the trace of `f` for `dg` is `2`. The average `f_χ` is the constant
function `1` on `G`, and `Z \ G` is a point. The trace of `f_χ` is `2` for
the compatible quotient measure `dg / dz`, which gives the point mass `2`,
and `1` for the quotient measure of mass `1`. HL's proof of II.5.13 builds
its pseudo-coefficient by such an average: it takes `φ̃` locally constant
with compact support and sets `φ = φ̃_χ` (printed pp. 113–114). With
compatible measures, `φ̃` has, for `dg`, the same trace at every tested
representation (or pair) as `φ̃_χ` has for `dg / dz`. So the statements
match the sources, up to the points discussed under "Value 1" and
"Universes". This comparison is not formalized; the space `C^∞_c(G, χ)`
and integration over `Z \ G` are not defined here.

**Value 1.** HL require the value `1` at every `(Π', A')` isomorphic to
`(Π, A)`. Item 4 requires it at `(Π, A)` itself, and
`IsTwistedPseudoCoefficient.twistedDistribution_eq_one` gives it at every
isomorphic `(Π', A')`. This needs only that `Π` and `Π'` are admissible.
When `Π' ≅ Π`, item 4 says nothing about an isomorphism `A'` of `Π'` onto
`Π'^θ` that does not correspond to `A`. Item 4 assumes only that `Π` is
admissible, and then such an `A'` need not be a scalar multiple of the
corresponding one. For instance, let `G` be the infinite cyclic group with
generator `g` and the discrete topology, `θ = id`, `Π(g^m) = 2^m` on `ℂ²`
and `A = 1`. Then the operator `diag(1, 2)` is another isomorphism of `Π`
onto `Π^θ = Π`. When `Π` is irreducible and admissible,
Schur's lemma (not proved here) makes such an `A'` a scalar multiple `λ` of
the corresponding one, as recorded in `Langlands.BaseChange.TwistedCharacter`.
Then `Θ^{A'}_{Π'}(φ) = λ` by the lemma above and `twistedDistribution_smul`
of `Langlands.BaseChange.TwistedCharacter.Basic`. This is the setting of
HL II.5.13, whose `(Π, A)` is strongly irreducible (`Π` irreducible), and
of `TwistedPseudoCoefficientExistenceStatement`, where `IsSigmaDiscreteSeries`
includes irreducibility. AC normalize the operator to `I_σ` (Chapter 1,
§2.1) and require `trace(Π(φ) I_σ) = 1`.

**Induction.** Item 7 follows HL II.4.6 (printed p. 88) and AC Chapter 1,
§2.3. It uses the same parabolic subgroups and differs from these sources
only in the form of the induction and of the inducing representations. None
of the following comparisons is formalized.

- *Standard parabolic subgroups.* Item 7 tests exactly HL's standard pairs
  `(P_α(E), G_α(E))` with `l(α) > 1`, as explained after its definition, and
  AC also use standard Levi subgroups. HL II.1.7 speaks informally of "a
  proper σ-stable Levi subgroup", and II.4.6 makes this precise with the
  standard subgroups. A σ-stable Levi subgroup alone need not be a Levi
  component of a σ-stable parabolic subgroup. For example, take a quadratic field `E = F(a)` with
  `σ(a) = −a`. The subgroup of `GL_2(E)` that is diagonal in the basis
  `(1, a)`, `(1, −a)` of `E²` is σ-stable, because `σ` exchanges the two
  lines. For the same reason `σ` exchanges the two Borel subgroups that
  contain it. For a σ-stable parabolic subgroup `P` with a σ-stable Levi
  component `M`, and `E` a field, descent does work. Each subspace of the
  flag fixed by `P` and each summand of the compatible decomposition fixed
  by `M` is σ-stable, hence defined over `F`. So `(P, M)` is conjugate to a
  standard pair by an element of `GL_n(F)`, which commutes with `σ`.
- *Normalization.* Item 7 uses the Library's unnormalized smooth induction.
  HL's `ι_α` (II.3.1, printed p. 78; see also II.5.5, printed p. 104) and AC
  use normalized parabolic induction. The normalized induction of `Ξ` is the
  unnormalized induction of `Ξ ⊗ δ_P^{1/2}`, with `δ_P` the modulus
  character of `P = P_α(E)`. When `E` is a cyclic algebra over a local field
  `F`, `δ_P` is invariant under `σ`, because `σ` preserves Haar measure on
  `E`. Then `Ξ ↦ Ξ ⊗ δ_P^{1/2}` is a bijection from the smooth σ-stable
  representations of `G_α(E)` to themselves that preserves irreducibility.
  So item 7 excludes the same representations as the normalized form.
- *Inducing representations.* HL II.4.6 takes `Ξ` smooth, irreducible and
  σ-stable. Item 7 takes every smooth σ-stable `Ξ` (on a space in the
  universe `uW`, see "Universes"), irreducible or not, and this excludes
  the same representations. Suppose the irreducible representation `Π` is
  isomorphic to the induction `Ind(Ξ)` of a smooth `Ξ` from `P_α(E)`. Then
  `Ξ` is irreducible. Smooth induction from `P_α(E)` is exact. It also takes
  a nonzero `Ξ` to a nonzero representation: a section supported on
  `P_α(E) K`, for a small enough compact open subgroup `K`, is nonzero at
  the identity. So `Ξ ≠ 0`, because `Π ≠ 0`. A nonzero proper
  subrepresentation `Ξ'` of `Ξ` would give the subrepresentation `Ind(Ξ')`
  of `Ind(Ξ)`, which is nonzero and proper because its quotient
  `Ind(Ξ/Ξ')` is nonzero. AC's parenthetical "tempered" on the inducing
  representation is not imposed, and HL II.4.6 does not impose it either.

**Universes.** In items 3, 4 and 7 the quantified carriers `V'`, `W'` and `X`
are taken in the universe of the given representation (`uV` for items 3 and
`uW` for items 4 and 7). Lean cannot quantify over all universes inside one
proposition.

**Elliptic inner product.** AC's elliptic inner product (Chapter 1, (6.6))
and its orthogonality relations (Proposition 6.6) are not defined here. AC
prove Proposition 6.6 as a consequence of base change. The facts that the
comparison needs are left to a later statement task: the (twisted) orbital
integrals of a pseudo-coefficient (AC Chapter 1, Lemma 2.12; HL
II.5.14–II.5.15) and Kazhdan's Theorem K(b).
