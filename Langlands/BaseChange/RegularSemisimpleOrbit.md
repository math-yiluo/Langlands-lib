# Regular semisimple conjugacy orbits in the general linear group

A matrix with separable characteristic polynomial is determined up to conjugacy by that polynomial. The proof decomposes its polynomial module into monic cyclic quotients. The product of the defining polynomials has degree equal to the matrix dimension, and it annihilates the module. Over an algebraic closure each root of the characteristic polynomial is in the spectrum, so it is a root of this annihilating polynomial. Separability and monicity identify the product with the characteristic polynomial. Its squarefreeness gives pairwise coprime factors, and the Chinese remainder theorem identifies the module with the quotient by the characteristic polynomial. The published `matrixAEvalConjugacy` converts an isomorphism of these modules into a conjugating matrix.

For an invertible regular semisimple matrix, the algebraic result identifies its conjugacy orbit with the fiber of the characteristic-polynomial coefficient map. The coefficient map is continuous, and its target is Hausdorff when the field is Hausdorff. Therefore the fiber is closed.

The map from left cosets of the centralizer to this orbit takes `[x]` to `x⁻¹ γ x`. Two group elements give the same conjugate exactly when they represent the same left coset. The map is continuous by the quotient topology. The closed orbit in a locally compact Hausdorff group is locally compact and Baire. With a sigma-compact group, Mathlib's open mapping theorem for transitive actions applies to the conjugation action on this orbit. Precomposing the action map with inversion gives the open map `x ↦ x⁻¹ γ x`. Openness descends along the quotient projection, so the coset map is a homeomorphism. This gives the orbit homeomorphism used for ordinary orbital integrals in Henniart–Lemaire (2011), I.2.4, printed pp. 14–16.

The ordinary orbital integrand, expressed as `twistedOrbitFunction` for the identity twist, is the pullback along this homeomorphism and the closed inclusion of the orbit. Their composition is a closed embedding, so the pullback of any function with compact support has compact support. The algebraic argument applies over any field, and the topological results use the displayed Hausdorff, local compactness and sigma-compactness hypotheses.
