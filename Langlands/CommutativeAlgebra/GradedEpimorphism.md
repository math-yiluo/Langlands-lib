# Epimorphisms of connected positively graded algebras

This module is an internal derived construction for the relative dynamic
unipotent step in the reductive monomorphism problem. Its results are proved
from the existing `GradedLine.IsGrading` interface and Mathlib's ideal, category
and affine-scheme interfaces. They are not presented as a translation of a
separately numbered result in the literature.

Let `R`, `A` and `B` be commutative rings in an arbitrary common universe, with
`R`-algebra structures on `A` and `B`. Let `hA` and `hB` be gradings in the
published Laurent-polynomial sense. `PositiveElement hB` consists of a degree
`n > 0` and an element of `B` homogeneous of degree `n`. For an algebra map
`φ : B →ₐ[R] A`, `positiveImageIdeal hB φ` is the ideal of `A` generated by
the images of these elements.

`surjective_of_positiveImageIdeal` assumes that all negative components of `A`
vanish, every degree-zero component of `A` is a scalar, and `φ` sends homogeneous
elements of each degree to homogeneous elements of the same degree. If
`positiveImageIdeal hB φ` contains every positive homogeneous element of `A`,
then `φ` is surjective. This criterion does not assume positivity or
connectedness of the source, categorical epimorphy, or an augmentation.

For the epimorphism result, supply an `R`-algebra augmentation
`ε : A →ₐ[R] R`. The assumptions are:

- All negative components of both `A` and `B` vanish.
- The degree-zero component of `a : A` equals `algebraMap R A (ε a)`.
- The degree-zero component of `b : B` equals
  `algebraMap R B (ε (φ b))`.
- The map `φ` preserves homogeneous degrees.
- The underlying ring map of `φ` is an epimorphism in `CommRingCat`.

Under these hypotheses, `surjective_of_epi` proves `Function.Surjective φ`.
The zero-component assumptions explicitly express connectedness and compatibility
with the augmentation. An independent augmentation of `B` need not be supplied;
it is `ε.comp φ`. There are no field, DVR, reducedness, flatness, smoothness,
finite generation or finite presentation assumptions.

`isClosedImmersion_specMap_of_mono` replaces the epimorphism hypothesis by
monicity of the actual scheme morphism `Spec A → Spec B` associated to `φ` and
concludes that this morphism is a closed immersion. It reflects monicity through
the fully faithful spectrum functor, converts the resulting monicity in the
opposite category to ring epimorphy, and applies the algebra theorem followed by
Mathlib's closed-immersion theorem for spectra of surjective ring maps.

The implemented algebra proof has two parts. First, put
`I = positiveImageIdeal hB φ`. Modulo `I`, the image of every positive homogeneous
component of `B` vanishes. Negative components vanish by hypothesis. Summing
components therefore shows that the quotient map `q` satisfies
`q (φ b) = algebraMap R (A ⧸ I) (ε (φ b))`; this is
`quotient_image_eq_scalar`. Epimorphy of the underlying ring map cancels `φ`
from this equality of ring maps. Thus
`a - algebraMap R A (ε a)` belongs to `I` for all `a`; this is
`sub_scalar_mem_positiveImageIdeal_of_epi`. For a positive homogeneous `a`, its
scalar term vanishes by its zero-component identity, so `a` itself belongs to
`I`.

Second, the ideal-generation criterion uses induction on an integer degree,
bounded above by a natural number. Negative degrees vanish, and degree zero
consists of scalars in the range. Express a positive homogeneous element as a
finite sum of ideal generators with arbitrary coefficients. Taking its component
of degree `n` replaces a coefficient of a generator of degree `d > 0` by its
component of degree `n-d`. This degree is strictly smaller, so the coefficient
belongs to the range by induction. Each term, and hence their sum, belongs to
the range. Summing homogeneous components proves surjectivity for every element.

The module does not construct a morphism between dynamic unipotent subgroups or
verify its grading and augmentation compatibility. Those are separate consumer
obligations, as are the relative cell and reductive matrix arguments. In
particular this module does not prove the host reductive monomorphism statement.
