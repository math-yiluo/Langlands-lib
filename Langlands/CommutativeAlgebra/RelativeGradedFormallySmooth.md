# Relative square-zero lifting for graded algebras

Worker 708. This is an internal extension of the published diagonal averaging
construction in `GradedFormallySmooth`. No external source theorem is
asserted. All declarations lie in
`Langlands.CommutativeAlgebra.RelativeGradedFormallySmooth`.

Let `A → B` be an algebra map of arbitrary commutative rings, with ring maps
`cA : A → A[T,T⁻¹]` and `cB : B → B[T,T⁻¹]`. The equivariance hypothesis
`heq` says that applying `cB` to the image of `a` agrees with applying the
algebra map to every coefficient of `cA a`. The ideals `negIdeal` and
`offIdeal` are the published ones: the ideal generated by all negative
coefficients, and the ideal generated by all coefficients of `c(b) − b`,
respectively.

The constructions `negQuotientMap` and `offQuotientMap` are actual ring
homomorphisms

- `A / negIdeal cA → B / negIdeal cB`;
- `A / offIdeal cA → B / offIdeal cB`.

Their values on quotient classes are the classes of the images under the
original algebra map (`negQuotientMap_mk` and `offQuotientMap_mk`).
`map_mem_negIdeal` and `map_mem_offIdeal` prove their well-definedness by
checking ideal generators and applying span induction. These constructions
need only `heq`; the smoothness results need the further identities below.

Suppose `B` is formally smooth as an `A`-algebra.
`formallySmooth_negQuotientMap` proves formal smoothness of the first map
when both `cA` and `cB` have counits and homogeneous coefficients:
`∑ₙ c(b)ₙ = b` and `c(c(b)ₙ) = c(b)ₙ Tⁿ`.
`formallySmooth_offQuotientMap` proves formal smoothness of the second map
assuming only the homogeneous-coefficient identity for `cB` and `heq`.
No field, finite generation, characteristic, or nonzero-weight hypothesis
is imposed. The two maps are the coordinate-quotient maps relevant to
attracting and fixed loci when the ring maps come from multiplicative-group
actions; this module does not construct those scheme loci.

The lifting lemmas `exists_lift_negIdeal` and `exists_lift_offIdeal`
retain an explicitly specified map `a : A → D`. For any square-zero ideal
`J` in `D`, an `A`-compatible ring map `x : B → D/J` killing the relevant
ideal lifts to a ring map `z : B → D` which kills that ideal, agrees with
`a` on `A`, and reduces to `x` modulo `J`. The base map `a` must kill the
corresponding ideal of `A`.

For the negative ideal, the proof gives `D[X]` the base algebra structure
twisted by `cA` followed by `a`. The vanishing of negative coefficients
makes this a polynomial ring map. The compatible polynomial orbit map of
`x` is an algebra map for that structure. Formal smoothness lifts it through
`D[X] → (D/J)[X]`, whose kernel has square zero. Diagonal averaging of this
lift produces `z`. It kills the negative coefficients because a polynomial
has no coefficients in negative degrees. `diag_preserves_base` uses
homogeneity and the counit for `cA` to prove that averaging recovers `a`
on the original base ring. Homogeneity and the counit for `cB` show that
the lift reduces to `x`.

For the fixed ideal, start with an ordinary `A`-algebra lift of `x` and
take its degree-zero average using the published diagonal construction.
The square-zero calculation makes this average a ring map. Homogeneity
shows that it kills the fixed ideal. The base map kills its own fixed
ideal, so its value on the degree-zero coefficient equals its original
value; equivariance therefore preserves the specified base map.

For each quotient-map smoothness theorem, the formal smoothness criterion
reduces to a square-zero diagram. Compose its maps with the two quotient
projections, apply the corresponding lifting lemma, and factor the lift
through the quotient. Quotient induction gives its algebra compatibility
and the required commutative diagram.

This unit supplies relative algebraic infrastructure for Task 6453. It does
not prove the closedness of a parabolic orbit, affineness of a reductive
homogeneous space, or `DynamicQuotientProperStatement`. Its proof uses only
commutative-algebra lifting and the published diagonal averaging helpers,
with no regular-cocharacter/Borel or rank-one result.
