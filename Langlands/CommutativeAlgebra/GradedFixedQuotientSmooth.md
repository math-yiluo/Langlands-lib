# Smoothness of a graded fixed quotient

This is an internal algebraic construction by Worker 1345 for the proof of
`Langlands.GroupScheme.Diagonalizable.FixedSubgroupSmoothStatement`. It records
the degree-zero averaging argument independently of the geometric adapter. It
is not itself a proof of that published group-scheme statement.

Let `R` be a commutative ring, `A` a commutative `R`-algebra, and let an arbitrary
abelian group `ι` grade `A` through submodules `𝒜 i` and `GradedAlgebra 𝒜`.
`fixedIdeal 𝒜` is the ideal generated by every homogeneous element whose degree
is nonzero. No torsion-freeness or finiteness of the group is assumed.

For a square-zero ideal `J` of an `R`-algebra `B`, `averagedLift` takes an algebra
map `f : A →ₐ[R] B` carrying every nonzero homogeneous component into `J` and
composes it with the degree-zero projection. The result is an algebra map.
The proof uses homogeneous induction in each factor: factors of degree zero
commute with projection; if both degrees are nonzero, their images lie in `J`
and their product vanishes because `J² = 0`. The construction also annihilates
`fixedIdeal`, as proved by `fixedIdeal_le_ker_averagedLift`.

`map_proj_zero` proves that an algebra map killing the nonzero homogeneous
components is unchanged by the degree-zero projection. The theorem
`formallySmooth` then proves `Algebra.FormallySmooth R (A ⧸ fixedIdeal 𝒜)` when
`A` is formally smooth. Given a quotient-valued map, formal smoothness of `A`
first gives an arbitrary lift. All nonzero components of that lift land in the
square-zero ideal. Averaging gives a lift that factors through the fixed
quotient, and `map_proj_zero` verifies that it lifts the specified map.

Finally `smooth` proves `Algebra.Smooth R (A ⧸ fixedIdeal 𝒜)` when `R` is
noetherian and `A` is smooth over `R`. The quotient is finite type, and finite
type over a noetherian base gives finite presentation. This applies to every
field and to character groups with characteristic torsion. The module does
not construct a grading from a group action or identify a representative of a
fixed subgroup.
