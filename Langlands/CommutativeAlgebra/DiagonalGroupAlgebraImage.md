# Local group-algebra images of Hopf maps

For a commutative finite-type Hopf algebra over a commutative ring and a Hopf map to the group algebra of an abelian group, the image is locally a subgroup algebra. More precisely, at every prime there is an idempotent outside the prime such that after inverting it, the scalar-extended map factors as a surjection onto a subgroup algebra followed by the standard inclusion. The scalar-extension equivalence for group algebras is taken in its usual coefficient coordinates. Its inverse preserves the counit and comultiplication because it takes each standard monomial with coefficient one to a group-like element in the tensor algebra.

Take the finite union of the supports of a set of algebra generators. For each degree in that union, choose the idempotent generator of its coefficient ideal or its complement, whichever avoids the prime. Inverting their product makes each of these ideals either the unit ideal or zero. In the first case, homogeneous extraction gives the monomial with coefficient one in the image. In the second, all coefficients in that degree vanish. The monomials with coefficient one in the image form a subgroup of the degree group by multiplication and the antipode. The images of the original generators are supported in this subgroup, and tensor induction extends this containment to all elements of the scalar-extended image. The reverse inclusion follows by taking linear combinations of the subgroup monomials.

The induced algebra map onto the subgroup algebra preserves the counit and comultiplication by cancellation along the inclusion and its tensor square. For the tensor square, both group algebras are free modules over the coefficient ring. This is an internal algebraic step toward the relative torus image factorization.
