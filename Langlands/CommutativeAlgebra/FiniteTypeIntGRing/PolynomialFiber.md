# Fibres of polynomial extensions

This helper module for the proof of
`Langlands.CommutativeAlgebra.FiniteTypeIntGRingStatement` identifies the
fibre of a polynomial ring `B₀[Y_μ]` over a suitable prime with a tensor
product `K ⊗[A₀] B₀`. All declarations are in the namespace
`Langlands.CommutativeAlgebra.FiniteTypeIntGRing`. The module makes
`RingHom.ker_isPrime` a local instance, so the kernel of a ring map into a
field is used as a prime ideal.

## Statements

**Residue fields of kernels.** Let `P` be a commutative ring, `K` a field and
`ψ : P →+* K`.

- `residueFieldKerLift ψ : κ(ker ψ) →+* K` is the map induced by `ψ`, with
  `residueFieldKerLift_algebraMap`: it sends the image of `p ∈ P` to `ψ p`.
- `residueFieldKerLift_bijective ψ hψ`: it is bijective when every element of
  `K` is a quotient `ψ u / ψ v` with `ψ v ≠ 0` (hypothesis `hψ`).
- `residueFieldKerEquiv ψ hψ : κ(ker ψ) ≃+* K` is the resulting isomorphism,
  with the simp lemmas `residueFieldKerEquiv_algebraMap` (image of `p` goes to
  `ψ p`) and `residueFieldKerEquiv_symm_apply` (`ψ p` goes back to the image
  of `p`).

**Fibres.** Let `R₀` be a commutative ring, `σ` and `μ` types,
`R₀[X_σ] = MvPolynomial σ R₀` and `R₀[X_σ, Y_μ] = MvPolynomial (σ ⊕ μ) R₀`
(with `X_i = X (inl i)` and `Y_j = X (inr j)`). Let `A₀` be an
`R₀[X_σ]`-algebra, `B₀` an `A₀`-algebra, `K` a field that is an
`A₀`-algebra, and suppose `B₀[Y_μ] = MvPolynomial μ B₀` carries an
`R₀[X_σ, Y_μ]`-algebra structure. The hypotheses are:

- `ψ : R₀[X_σ, Y_μ] →+* K`, with `hψ`: on `R₀[X_σ]` (embedded by
  `rename Sum.inl`) `ψ` is the composite `R₀[X_σ] → A₀ → K`;
- `hB₁`: on `R₀[X_σ]` the structure map into `B₀[Y_μ]` is the constant
  polynomial of the composite `R₀[X_σ] → A₀ → B₀`;
- `hB₂`: the structure map sends `Y_j` to the variable `Y_j` of `B₀[Y_μ]`;
- `hsurj`: every element of `K` is a quotient `ψ u / ψ v` with `ψ v ≠ 0`;
- for the inverse map and the equivalence, `A₀` is the localization of
  `R₀[X_σ]` at a submonoid `M` (`IsLocalization M A₀`).

The declarations are:

- `polyToTensor A₀ B₀ ψ : B₀[Y_μ] →+* K ⊗[A₀] B₀`, the evaluation map that
  sends `b ∈ B₀` to `1 ⊗ b` and `Y_j` to `ψ (Y_j) ⊗ 1`;
- `polyToTensor_algebraMap`: on the image of `p ∈ R₀[X_σ, Y_μ]` it is
  `ψ p ⊗ 1`;
- `fiberToTensor ψ hψ hB₁ hB₂ hsurj : κ(ker ψ) ⊗[R₀[X_σ, Y_μ]] B₀[Y_μ] →+*
  K ⊗[A₀] B₀`, with `fiberToTensor_tmul`:
  `c ⊗ f ↦ (residueFieldKerEquiv ψ hsurj c ⊗ 1) * polyToTensor f`;
- `tensorToFiber ψ hψ hB₁ hsurj M : K ⊗[A₀] B₀ →+*
  κ(ker ψ) ⊗[R₀[X_σ, Y_μ]] B₀[Y_μ]`, with `tensorToFiber_tmul`:
  `k ⊗ b ↦ (residueFieldKerEquiv ψ hsurj).symm k ⊗ C b`;
- `fiberEquivTensor ψ hψ hB₁ hB₂ hsurj M`: the ring isomorphism
  `(ker ψ).Fiber B₀[Y_μ] ≃+* K ⊗[A₀] B₀` given by these two maps, with
  `fiberEquivTensor_tmul` recording its value on pure tensors.

Informally, adjoining the variables `Y_μ` to both sides and passing to the
fibre over the prime `ker ψ` recovers the base change of `B₀` along
`A₀ → K`. This is the step that handles finite extensions `K` of a residue
field generated by the values `ψ (Y_j)`. The module is internal to the
project's proof and does not follow a specific reference.

## Proofs

**Residue fields.** `Ideal.ResidueField.lift` applies because elements outside
`ker ψ` have nonzero, hence invertible, images in `K`. A ring map out of a
field is injective. Under `hψ`, the element `ψ u / ψ v` is the image of
`(image of u) / (image of v)`, so the map is surjective.

**`polyToTensor` on the image of `R₀[X_σ, Y_μ]`.** By
`MvPolynomial.ringHom_ext` it suffices to compare the two ring maps
`R₀[X_σ, Y_μ] → K ⊗[A₀] B₀` on constants and variables. On constants and on
the `X_i`, `hB₁` and `hψ` reduce both sides to the image of an element `a` of
`A₀`, where `1 ⊗ a = a ⊗ 1` (`Algebra.TensorProduct.tmul_one_eq_one_tmul`).
On the `Y_j`, `hB₂` gives `Y_j ↦ ψ (Y_j) ⊗ 1` directly.

**The forward map.** Give `K ⊗[A₀] B₀` the `R₀[X_σ, Y_μ]`-algebra structure
`p ↦ ψ p ⊗ 1`. Then `c ↦ residueFieldKerEquiv c ⊗ 1` is an algebra map from
`κ(ker ψ)` (by `residueFieldKerEquiv_algebraMap`), and `polyToTensor` is one
from `B₀[Y_μ]` (by the previous paragraph). `Algebra.TensorProduct.lift`
combines them, the target being commutative.

**The inverse map.** Give the fibre the `A₀`-algebra structure through
`A₀ → K ≅ κ(ker ψ)` and the left factor. Then
`k ↦ residueFieldKerEquiv.symm k ⊗ 1` is an `A₀`-algebra map from `K`.
The map `b ↦ 1 ⊗ C b` from `B₀` is also an `A₀`-algebra map: by
`IsLocalization.ringHom_ext M` this needs checking only on the image of
`p ∈ R₀[X_σ]`. There `hψ`, `residueFieldKerEquiv_symm_apply` and `hB₁` turn
both sides into the image of `rename Sum.inl p`, moved across the tensor
sign. `Algebra.TensorProduct.lift` combines the two maps.

**Inverse to each other.** `RingEquiv.ofRingHom` needs both composites to be
the identity, which is checked on pure tensors (`TensorProduct.inductionOn`;
sums by additivity).

- On `k ⊗ b` in `K ⊗[A₀] B₀`: the inverse gives
  `residueFieldKerEquiv.symm k ⊗ C b`, which the forward map sends to
  `(k ⊗ 1)(1 ⊗ b) = k ⊗ b`.
- On `c ⊗ f` in the fibre: first, the composite of `polyToTensor` with the
  inverse map is `f ↦ 1 ⊗ f`. By `MvPolynomial.ringHom_ext`, on constants
  this is `tensorToFiber_tmul`, and on `Y_j` it sends `Y_j` to
  `(image of Y_j) ⊗ 1 = 1 ⊗ (image of Y_j)`, which is `1 ⊗ Y_j` by `hB₂`.
  Hence `c ⊗ f ↦ (residueFieldKerEquiv c ⊗ 1) * polyToTensor f ↦
  (c ⊗ 1)(1 ⊗ f) = c ⊗ f`.
