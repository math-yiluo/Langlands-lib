# Factoriality of power series rings over a field

Helper module `Langlands.CommutativeAlgebra.MvPowerSeriesFactorial`, written for the proof
that every stalk of a scheme smooth over an algebraically closed field is a unique
factorization domain (task `7d63b52e-e715-47d0-a015-331cfa8a4b6d`). It proves that
`MvPowerSeries (Fin n) K` is a `UniqueFactorizationMonoid` for a field `K`, given a change of
coordinates passed as a hypothesis. The proof runs by induction on `n` with the Weierstrass
preparation theorem.

The last theorem takes one input as an explicit hypothesis, `hcoord`: a change of coordinates

## Completeness

`map_pow_smul_top_le` is `Submodule.map_smul''` followed by monotonicity in the module.
`isAdicComplete_of_linearEquiv` transports the Hausdorff and precompleteness conditions along
a linear equivalence `e`: congruences modulo `I ^ n • ⊤` map to congruences under `e` and
`e.symm` (`SModEq.map`, `SModEq.mono`).

`isMaximal_idealOfVars`: by `MvPolynomial.mem_pow_idealOfVars_iff'` with exponent one, the
ideal generated by the variables is the kernel of the constant coefficient. That map is
surjective onto the field `K`, so the kernel is maximal.

`isAdicComplete_maximalIdeal`: let `P = MvPolynomial σ K` and `I` the ideal of the variables,
finitely generated because `σ` is finite. Mathlib's
`MvPowerSeries.toAdicCompletionAlgEquiv` is a `P`-algebra isomorphism from
`MvPowerSeries σ K` to the `I`-adic completion `AdicCompletion I P`, which is `I`-adically
complete as a `P`-module (`AdicCompletion.isAdicComplete`). The transport lemma makes
`MvPowerSeries σ K` an `I`-adically complete `P`-module, and
`IsAdicComplete.map_algebraMap_iff` makes it complete for the extended ideal
`I · MvPowerSeries σ K`. It remains to identify this ideal with the maximal ideal. In the
completion the extension of the maximal ideal `I` is maximal
(`AdicCompletion.isMaximal_map_of_le`). The inverse isomorphism carries it to
`I · MvPowerSeries σ K`, because both maps are `P`-algebra maps (`Ideal.map_map`,
`AlgEquiv.commutes`). Hence that ideal is maximal and, the ring being local, equals
`maximalIdeal (MvPowerSeries σ K)`.

## Distinguished factors

`isDistinguishedAt_of_monic_dvd`: reduce `w = p * q` modulo the maximal ideal. Mathlib's
`IsDistinguishedAt.map_eq_X_pow` gives `w̄ = X ^ deg w`, so `p̄` divides a power of the prime
`X` (`dvd_prime_pow`). It is therefore associated to some `X ^ i`, and equal to it because
both are monic (`eq_of_monic_of_associated`). Reduction preserves the degree of a monic
polynomial, so `i = deg p`. Every coefficient of `p` below its degree then has zero residue,
that is, it lies in the maximal ideal.

`prime_coe_of_isDistinguishedAt`: Mathlib's
`Polynomial.IsDistinguishedAt.algEquivQuotient` (Weierstrass division) is a ring isomorphism
`A[X] ⧸ (p) ≃ A⟦X⟧ ⧸ (p)` when `A` is complete. If `p` is prime in `A[X]`, the left side is a
domain, so the ideal `(p)` of `A⟦X⟧` is prime. Since `p ≠ 0`, the element `p` is prime in
`A⟦X⟧` (`Ideal.span_singleton_prime`).

## Irreducible series are prime

`prime_of_irreducible_of_map_ne_zero`: Mathlib's Weierstrass preparation theorem
(`PowerSeries.exists_isWeierstrassFactorization`) writes `g = w * h`, with `w` a
distinguished polynomial and `h` a unit. Then `w` is irreducible in `A⟦X⟧` and is not a unit
in `A[X]`. The polynomial ring `A[X]` is factorial, so the Library lemma
`Langlands.Analytic.exists_monic_prime_factor` gives `w = p * q` with `p` and `q` monic and
`p` prime in `A[X]`. By the two lemmas above, `p` is distinguished and prime in `A⟦X⟧`. It
divides the irreducible `w`, so the two are associated, and hence `g` is prime.

## Induction

`uniqueFactorizationMonoid_of_coord`: for `n = 0`, `MvPowerSeries (Fin 0) K` is
isomorphic to the field `K` (`MvPowerSeries.isEmptyEquiv`). For `n + 1`, the ring is a
Noetherian domain (`MvPowerSeries.isNoetherianRing`, `isDomain_mvPowerSeries`). Its
divisibility is therefore well founded, and it remains to show that every irreducible `f` is
prime.

Apply `hcoord` to obtain a `K`-algebra automorphism `φ` and `k` such that the coefficient of
`X_0 ^ k` in `φ f` is nonzero. Let `A = MvPowerSeries (Fin n) K`, which is factorial by
induction, local, and complete by `isAdicComplete_maximalIdeal`. Map `φ f` to `A⟦X⟧` with Mathlib's
`MvPowerSeries.finSuccEquiv`, which uses `X_0` as the power-series variable. By
`coeff_coeff_finSuccEquiv` the `k`-th coefficient of the image has nonzero constant
coefficient. It is therefore not in the maximal ideal of `A` (`mem_maximalIdeal_iff`, from
`MvPowerSeries.isUnit_iff_constantCoeff`), so the image has nonzero reduction. The image is
irreducible, hence prime by the previous lemma, and primality transfers back along the ring
isomorphism.

## Provenance and contributions

This is an internal derivation for the target proof. It follows the classical argument for
factoriality of `k⟦X_1, ..., X_n⟧`: induction on the number of variables, a change of
coordinates, the Weierstrass preparation theorem and Gauss's lemma. The Library's proof
`Langlands.Analytic.StrictTateAlgebra.uniqueFactorization_tate`
(`Langlands.Geometry.Analytic.TateFactorial`) uses the same pattern for strict Tate
algebras. Mathlib supplies Weierstrass preparation and division, so this proof needs no
separate Gauss-norm argument. Worker 254 wrote the Lean proof and this description.
