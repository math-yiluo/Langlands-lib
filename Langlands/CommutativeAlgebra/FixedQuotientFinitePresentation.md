# Finite equations for an actual fixed quotient

Let `B` be an algebra over any commutative ring `R`, and let
`c : B →ₐ[R] AddMonoidAlgebra B M`, where `M` is any commutative additive
group. The existing actual fixed ideal is generated by every coefficient
of `c b - b X⁰` as `b` varies over the whole algebra.

For a finite family `s` of elements of `B`,
`generatorFixedCoefficients` collects the nonzero-support coefficients
of these differences for elements of `s`, and `generatorFixedIdeal`
is the ideal they generate. It is finitely generated by construction.
If `s` generates `B` as an `R`-algebra, this ideal equals the full
existing fixed ideal.

To prove equality, the implemented argument passes to the quotient
by the finite equations. The two algebra maps from `B` to the group
algebra over that quotient, induced by `c` and by the degree-zero
constant inclusion, agree on `s`. They agree on the whole algebra
by `AlgHom.ext_of_adjoin_eq_top`. The existing
`fixedIdeal_le_ker_iff` then implies that the full fixed ideal is
killed by this quotient map. The other ideal inclusion is immediate
from the definition of the full fixed ideal.

Consequently, a finite-type algebra has a finitely generated fixed
ideal, and the fixed quotient of a finitely presented algebra is
finitely presented. These results do not require coassociativity,
homogeneous-coefficient identities, a finite grading group or a
noetherian base. If the algebra is smooth and the coefficients
satisfy the homogeneous-coefficient identity, the published formal
smoothness theorem for the fixed quotient, together with the new
finite-presentation result, proves smoothness of that same actual
quotient over the arbitrary base.

The provenance is internal: this supplies the finite-equation argument
missing from the published arbitrary-ring formal-smoothness interface.
It uses the existing fixed ideal unchanged and Mathlib's elementary
algebra-generator and finite-presentation results. It is intended for
actual relative diagonalizable-group centralizers; their scheme
representability and comparison are separate constructions.
