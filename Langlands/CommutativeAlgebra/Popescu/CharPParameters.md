# Step (12) of Stacks Lemma 11.4: annihilators of `γᵢ = πᵢ tᵢ`

This module supports the proof of Stacks Project, *Smoothing Ring Maps*, Lemma 11.4
(Tag 07FJ), in `CharPResolutionAssembly`. It proves step (12) of that proof. All
declarations are in the namespace `Langlands.CommutativeAlgebra.Popescu.CharP`.

**Notation.** `K` is a Noetherian ring (in the assembly `K = k[y₁, …, y_m]`), `𝔭` a prime
of `K`, and `π : Fin d → K`. The ring `R = K[t₁, …, t_d]` is `MvPolynomial (Fin d) K`,
and `γᵢ = C(πᵢ) Xᵢ`, i.e. `πᵢ tᵢ`. Step (12) of the source reads: "If necessary modify the
choice of `πᵢ` such that for `i = 1, …, d` we have
`Ann_{R/(γ₁ᵉ, …, γ_{i−1}ᵉ)R}(γᵢ) = Ann_{R/(γ₁ᵉ, …, γ_{i−1}ᵉ)R}(γᵢ²)`".

## Difference from the source

The source applies Ogoma's lemma (Lemma 10.1, Tag 07FC) directly in `R` with
`S = K \ 𝔭`. It uses that the `γᵢ` form a regular sequence in `S⁻¹R = K_𝔭[t]`
(Algebra, Lemma 68.10). The Lean proof avoids regular sequences in polynomial rings:

1. It applies Ogoma's lemma in `K` itself, once for each subset `T ⊆ {j < i}`, to the
   module `K / (πⱼᵉ : j ∈ T)`.
2. It transfers the resulting conditions to `R` by comparing coefficients of monomials.

Ogoma's lemma is the Library statement `AnnihilatorStabilizationStatement`, received as
the hypothesis `hOg`. Its conclusion holds for all multiples `t s π` with `t ∈ S`, not only
for powers `sⁿ π` as in the source. This lets one multiply together the elements `s_T`
obtained for the different subsets `T`.

## Coefficients

- `gamma_pow_eq`: `(C(ρⱼ) Xⱼ)ᵉ = monomial (single j e) (ρⱼᵉ)`.
- `mem_span_gamma_iff ρ e T x`: `x ∈ ((C(ρⱼ) Xⱼ)ᵉ : j ∈ T)` if and only if, for every
  exponent `α`, the coefficient of `t^α` in `x` lies in the ideal of `K` generated by the
  `ρⱼᵉ` with `j ∈ T` and `e ≤ αⱼ`.
  **Proof.**
  - (⇒) Induction over the span. A product's coefficient is a sum over the antidiagonal of
    `α` (`coeff_mul`). The condition is monotone in `α`.
  - (⇐) Write `x = Σ_α monomial α (coeff α x)` (`MvPolynomial.as_sum`). Show by induction
    over the span in `K` that `monomial α c` lies in the ideal. A generator `ρⱼᵉ` with
    `e ≤ αⱼ` gives `monomial α (ρⱼᵉ) = monomial (α − single j e) 1 · (C(ρⱼ) Xⱼ)ᵉ`.
- `gamma_annihilator ρ e T i hi h x hx`. Let `i ∉ T`. Assume that for every `T' ⊆ T` and
  `y ∈ K`, `ρᵢ² y ∈ (ρⱼᵉ : j ∈ T')` implies `ρᵢ y ∈ (ρⱼᵉ : j ∈ T')`. Then the same holds
  for `γᵢ = C(ρᵢ) Xᵢ` and the ideal `(γⱼᵉ : j ∈ T)` of `R`.
  **Proof.** Apply `mem_span_gamma_iff`. The coefficient of `t^β` in `γᵢ x` is
  `ρᵢ · coeff_{β − eᵢ}(x)` if `βᵢ ≥ 1`, and `0` otherwise (`coeff_monomial_mul'`). The
  coefficient of `t^{β + eᵢ}` in `γᵢ² x` is `ρᵢ² · coeff_{β − eᵢ}(x)`. The set
  `{j ∈ T : e ≤ (β + eᵢ)ⱼ}` equals `{j ∈ T : e ≤ βⱼ}` because `i ∉ T`. Apply `h` with
  `T'` this set.

## Step (12)

- `exists_step12 hOg 𝔭 π e hreg`. Assume `hreg`: for every `T` and `i ∉ T`, `πᵢ` is a
  nonzerodivisor on `K_𝔭 / (πⱼᵉ : j ∈ T)`. Then there are `sᵢ ∉ 𝔭` such that, for every
  `i` and every `T` with all `j ∈ T` below `i`, `(sᵢπᵢ)² y ∈ ((sⱼπⱼ)ᵉ : j ∈ T)` implies
  `sᵢπᵢ y ∈ ((sⱼπⱼ)ᵉ : j ∈ T)`.
  **Proof.** Induction on `t ≤ d`. The invariant is that the condition holds for all
  `i < t`. For the step `i₀ = t`:
  1. For each `T ∌ i₀`, apply Ogoma's lemma to `M_T = K / ((sⱼπⱼ)ᵉ : j ∈ T)`, the element
     `sᵢ₀ πᵢ₀` and `S = K \ 𝔭`. Its hypothesis is `ogoma_hyp` (module `CharPRegular`),
     with `u = s` and `v = sᵢ₀`. This gives `σ_T ∉ 𝔭`.
  2. Replace `sᵢ₀` by `(∏_T σ_T) sᵢ₀` (`Function.update`). The ideals for `i < t` involve
     only `j < i < t` and do not change.
  3. For `i₀` and a given `T`, write `∏ σ = (∏_{T' ≠ T} σ_{T'}) σ_T`. Use the "for all
     `τ ∈ S`" form of Ogoma's lemma with `τ = ∏_{T' ≠ T} σ_{T'}`.
- `exists_step12_poly hOg 𝔭 π e hreg`: the same `s` gives step (12) in `R`. For every
  `i` and `x ∈ R`, `γᵢ² x ∈ (γⱼᵉ : j < i)` implies `γᵢ x ∈ (γⱼᵉ : j < i)`, where
  `γᵢ = C(sᵢπᵢ) Xᵢ`. This follows from `exists_step12` and `gamma_annihilator` with
  `T = {j : j < i}`.

In the assembly, `hreg` comes from `mem_of_mul_mem_span_pow` (module `CharPRegular`) with
`L = L' = K_𝔭`, where the `πᵢ` generate the maximal ideal of the regular local ring `K_𝔭`.
