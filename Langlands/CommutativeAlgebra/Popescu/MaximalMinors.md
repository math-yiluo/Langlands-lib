# Determinants of products and maximal minors

This module proves the inclusion that the Cauchy–Binet formula gives for maximal minors. Let
`A` be a `c × N` matrix and `B` an `N × c` matrix over a commutative ring `S`. Then
`det (A B)` lies in the ideal generated by the maximal minors `det (A.submatrix id e)` of `A`,
where `e` ranges over the order embeddings `Fin c ↪o Fin N`, that is, over the `c`-element sets
of columns in increasing order.

## Statements

- `det_submatrix_mem_span_minors`: for an injective `σ : Fin c → Fin N`, the determinant of
  `A` with columns `σ` lies in the ideal of maximal minors.
- `det_mul_mem_span_minors`: `det (A * B)` lies in the ideal of maximal minors of `A`.
- `pow_mem_span_minors_of_mul_eq`: if `A * B = t • 1`, then `t ^ c` lies in the ideal of maximal
  minors of `A`.

## Proofs

`det_submatrix_mem_span_minors`.
- Let `s` be the image of `σ`. It has `c` elements, and `e = s.orderEmbOfFin` lists it in
  increasing order.
- For each `i`, `σ i = e (π i)` for a unique `π i`. The map `π` is injective, hence a
  permutation of `Fin c`.
- So `A.submatrix id σ` is `A.submatrix id e` with its columns permuted by `π`. Its
  determinant is therefore `sign π · det (A.submatrix id e)` (`Matrix.det_permute'`).

`det_mul_mem_span_minors`.
- Expand `det (A B)` with the Leibniz formula, then expand the product of sums with
  `Finset.prod_univ_sum`. This gives
  `det (A B) = Σ_{σ : Fin c → Fin N} (∏_i B (σ i) i) · det (A.submatrix id σ)`.
- If `σ` is not injective, `A.submatrix id σ` has two equal columns, so its determinant is
  zero (`Matrix.det_zero_of_column_eq`).
- If `σ` is injective, the previous lemma applies.

`pow_mem_span_minors_of_mul_eq`: `det (t • 1) = t ^ c`.
