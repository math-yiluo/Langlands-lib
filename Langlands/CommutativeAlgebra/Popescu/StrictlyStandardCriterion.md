# A conormal criterion for strictly standard elements

`isStrictlyStandard_of_conormal` gives a sufficient condition for an element to be strictly
standard in the sense of `IsStrictlyStandard` (module `Basic`; Stacks Project, *Smoothing ring
maps*, Definition 2.3, tag 07C7). It is the matrix form of the argument in the proof of Stacks
Lemma 3.7(e) (tag 07EZ), together with the converse direction of Lemma 2.4 (tag 07ET).

## Statement

Data:

- a surjective `R`-algebra map `φ : R[x_1, …, x_N] → C` whose kernel is generated by
  `F_1, …, F_M`;
- elements `H_1, …, H_c` of the kernel, with `c ≤ N`;
- a polynomial `s` with `s · F_j ∈ (H_1, …, H_c) + (ker φ)²` for every `j`;
- an `N × c` matrix `Ψ` over `C` and `t ∈ C` such that the `c × N` Jacobian matrix
  `(φ(∂H_k/∂x_i))` satisfies `Jac(H) · Ψ = t • 1`.

Conclusion: `φ(s) · t^c` is strictly standard in `C` over `R`.

## Proof

Take the presentation of `C` whose relations are `H_1, …, H_c, F_1, …, F_M` (`Fin.append`),
with the first `c` relations distinguished.

1. **Kernel.** These relations generate `ker φ`: the `H_k` lie in the kernel, and the `F_j`
   already generate it.
2. **Minors.** By `pow_mem_span_minors_of_mul_eq` (module `MaximalMinors`), `t^c` lies in the
   ideal generated by the maximal minors of `Jac(H)`. These minors are the images under `φ` of
   the polynomial minors `det (∂H_j/∂x_{e k})` that appear in the definition
   (`AlgHom.map_det`). So `φ(s) · t^c` lies in that ideal.
3. **Condition on the other relations.** Let `τ` be a lift of `t`, and take `s · τ^c` as the
   lift required by the definition. Each later relation is some `F_j`. Then
   `s · τ^c · F_j = τ^c · (s · F_j)` lies in `(H) + (ker φ)²`, which is the ideal required
   by the definition.
