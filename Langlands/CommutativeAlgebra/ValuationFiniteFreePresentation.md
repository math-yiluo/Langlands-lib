# Finite free presentations over arbitrary valuation domains

This internal construction supplies finite free presentations and the
coherence and finite-projective-dimension conditions for the valuation
domain itself. Valuation domains have arbitrary rank and include fields.
No Noetherian or discrete valuation hypothesis is imposed.

`FiniteFreeKernelPresentation R M` records a finite free module
`Fin generators → R`, an actual surjective `R`-linear map from it to
`M`, a natural number `relations`, and an actual linear equivalence
from `Fin relations → R` to the kernel of that map.
`R` and `M` may lie in independent universes.
The structure can be used over any commutative ring; its existence is
proved here for finitely presented modules over valuation domains.
`valuationRingFiniteFreeKernelPresentation` selects such a presentation
noncomputably.

The supporting results first show that a torsion-free module over a
valuation domain is flat, and that a finitely generated torsion-free
module is free. Mathlib's valuation-ring instance supplies the Bézout
domain property. Mathlib identifies flatness with vanishing of torsion
over a Bézout domain, and identifies torsion-freeness with that same
vanishing. Since valuation domains are local, Mathlib's freeness
theorem for finite flat modules applies.

For `finiteFreeSubmoduleCoherent_of_valuationRing`, a finitely generated
submodule of a finite free module is torsion-free and therefore free by
the preceding result. A finite projective module is finitely presented,
which proves exactly `FiniteFreeSubmoduleCoherent R`.

For the presentation construction, choose a finite free surjection
onto the given finitely presented module. Its kernel is finitely
generated by Mathlib's finite-presentation kernel theorem. The kernel
is torsion-free as a submodule of a free module over a domain, so it is
finite free. A finite indexed basis supplies the recorded equivalence.

`hasProjectiveDimensionLE_one_of_finitePresentation_of_valuationRing`
then proves that every finitely presented module in the universe of
`R` has projective dimension at most one. The actual kernel sequence of
the chosen surjection is short exact and both its kernel and middle
term are free, hence projective. Mathlib's short-exact-sequence
projective-dimension bound gives the result.
`finiteProjectiveDimensionForFinitelyPresentedModules_of_valuationRing`
packages this uniform bound as
`FiniteProjectiveDimensionForFinitelyPresentedModules R`.

These statements concern the valuation domain itself. They do not
establish the corresponding properties for polynomial rings, smooth
algebras or their localizations, and assert no extension of line
bundles on schemes.
