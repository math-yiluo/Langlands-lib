# The coordinate-degree component and the identity cotangent module

This is internal, derived commutative algebra for the global root-coordinate construction.
It extends the published graded-line interfaces and the maximal-fibre detection argument
in `GradedLineGlobal`. No assertion from new external literature is used.

The first lemma proves that the degree-d component generates a finite type positively
graded algebra whose maximal fibres are universally polynomial lines in positive degree d.
For each maximal ideal separately it chooses a component with nonzero linear coefficient
on the fibre. Its powers span each fibre degree; finite-module Nakayama over all maximal
ideals puts every homogeneous component in the algebra generated by degree d.
Summing the homogeneous components proves generation of the whole algebra.

An augmentation with scalar degree-zero component kills every positive homogeneous
element. Algebra induction on the degree-d generators then proves the identity-point
Leibniz rule for the degree-d component.

Suppose in addition that an A-algebra structure on R splits the R-algebra
structure on A (the scalar-tower hypothesis), and its augmentation has the
specified degree-zero component. Give the degree-d component the A-module
structure induced through this augmentation. The Leibniz rule above makes its
projection an R-derivation. The universal property of Kähler differentials,
followed by tensor extension along A → R, gives a map from
R ⊗[A] Ω[A/R] to the degree-d component.

In the other direction, differentiate a homogeneous element and take its tensor
with 1. The first composite is the identity because homogeneous projection is
the identity on the degree-d component. For the other composite, compare two
A-linear maps out of Ω[A/R]. Their associated derivations agree on the
degree-d generators, so algebra generation makes them agree on all A.
The universal differential property and tensor induction then prove the
second identity. `degreeCotangentEquiv` packages these maps as an actual
R-linear equivalence.

The equivalence itself only assumes algebra generation in positive degree
and the augmentation condition. The preceding maximal-fibre lemma provides
generation under its separate finite-type and graded-line hypotheses.
No flatness or smoothness hypothesis is needed for this cotangent comparison.
All results retain arbitrary commutative rings in universe u, including the
zero ring. The group-scheme Lie comparison and a global root parametrization
remain separate obligations of Task 6700.
