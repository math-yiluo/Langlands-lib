# Formal smoothness of quotients cut out by a `ℤ`-grading

Let `R` be a commutative ring and `B` a commutative `R`-algebra with a ring map
`c : B → B[T, T⁻¹]`. Write `c(b)ₙ` for the coefficient of `Tⁿ` in `c(b)`.
Assume:

- (`hR`) `c(r · 1) = r · 1` is constant for `r ∈ R`;
- (`hε`) `∑ₙ c(b)ₙ = b` for every `b`;
- (`hδ`) `c(c(b)ₙ) = c(b)ₙ Tⁿ` for every `b` and `n`.

So `c` is a `ℤ`-grading of `B` with homogeneous components `b ↦ c(b)ₙ`.
All declarations are in `Langlands.CommutativeAlgebra.GradedFormallySmooth`.

- `negIdeal c` is the ideal generated by all `c(b)ₙ` with `n < 0`.
- `offIdeal c` is the ideal generated by all coefficients of `c(b) - b`,
  that is, by the `c(b)ₙ` with `n ≠ 0` and the `c(b)₀ - b`.
- `formallySmooth_quotient_negIdeal`: if `B` is formally smooth over `R` and
  `hR`, `hε`, `hδ` hold, then `B ⧸ negIdeal c` is formally smooth over `R`.
- `formallySmooth_quotient_offIdeal`: if `B` is formally smooth over `R` and
  `hR`, `hδ` hold, then `B ⧸ offIdeal c` is formally smooth over `R`.
- `formallySmooth_of_retract`: if `A` is formally smooth over `R` and
  `A' → A → A'` composes to the identity (`R`-algebra maps), then `A'` is
  formally smooth over `R`.

## Provenance

This is internal infrastructure for the proof of `DynamicSmoothStatement`.
For the `𝔾_m`-action on a smooth affine group `G` by conjugation through a
cocharacter, `B ⧸ negIdeal c` and `B ⧸ offIdeal c` are the coordinate rings of
`P_G(χ)` and `Z_G(χ)`. Conrad–Gabber–Prasad (*Pseudo-reductive groups*,
Proposition 2.1.8(3)) prove smoothness of `Z_G(χ)` geometrically, by their
Proposition A.8.10(2) on fixed points of a `GL₁`-action. The ring-theoretic
lifting argument below is derived here and is not taken from that source.

## Proof

Formal smoothness is checked through
`Algebra.FormallySmooth.of_comp_surjective`. Let `D` be an `R`-algebra and `J`
an ideal of `D` with `J² = 0`. Let `ȳ : B → D ⧸ J` be an `R`-algebra map
killing the ideal. A lift `y : B → D` killing the ideal is required.

**Diagonal maps.** For a ring map `L : B → D[T, T⁻¹]`, `diag L` is the additive
map `∑ₙ aₙ Tⁿ ↦ ∑ₙ (L aₙ)ₙ`. A Laurent polynomial `q` is `IsDiagonalMod L J` when,
for every `i`, all coefficients of `L(qᵢ)` away from degree `i` lie in `J`.
`coeff_mul_add_of_coeff_mem` shows that if `p` has all coefficients away from
degree `i` in `J`, and `p'` has all coefficients away from degree `j` in `J`,
then `(p p')_{i+j} = pᵢ p'ⱼ`: every other contribution is a product of two
elements of `J`. Expanding `q q'` into monomials then gives `diag_mul`:
`diag L (q q') = diag L q · diag L q'` for diagonal `q`, `q'`. So if every
`c(b)` is diagonal, `diagRingHom c L : b ↦ diag L (c(b))` is a ring map.

**Negative part.** Since `ȳ` kills the negative coefficients,
`b ↦ ȳ(c(b))` lands in `(D ⧸ J)[T]` (`polynomialLift`). This gives an
`R`-algebra map `θ : B → (D ⧸ J)[T]`, using `hR`. The map
`D[T] → (D ⧸ J)[T]` is surjective with kernel `J[T]`, of square zero. By
formal smoothness of `B` (`Algebra.FormallySmooth.liftOfSurjective`) we get
`Θ : B → D[T]` lifting `θ`. Put `L = Θ` viewed in `D[T, T⁻¹]`. Modulo `J`, the
`k`-th coefficient of `L(x)` is `ȳ(c(x)ₖ)`. By `hδ`, `c(c(b)ᵢ)` has only the
coefficient `c(b)ᵢ`, in degree `i`, so every `c(b)` is diagonal. Let
`y = diagRingHom c L`. It is an `R`-algebra map by `hR`. Modulo `J`,
`y(b) = ∑ₙ ȳ(c(b)ₙ) = ȳ(b)` by `hε`. For `n < 0`, `hδ` gives
`y(c(b)ₙ) = (Θ(c(b)ₙ))ₙ`. This is a coefficient of negative degree of a
polynomial, hence `0`. So `y` factors through the quotient and lifts `ȳ`.

**Off-diagonal part.** Here `ȳ(c(b)ₙ) = 0` for `n ≠ 0` and
`ȳ(c(b)₀) = ȳ(b)`. Take any lift `Y : B → D` of `ȳ`
(`Algebra.FormallySmooth.lift`) and `L = C ∘ Y`, the constant Laurent
polynomials. Then every `c(b)` is diagonal, and `diagRingHom c L` is
`b ↦ Y(c(b)₀)` (`diag_C_comp`). It lifts `ȳ`. It kills `c(b)ₙ - [n = 0] b`
because `hδ` makes `c` of that element have zero constant coefficient.

**Retracts.** Given `s : A' → A` and `r : A → A'` with `r ∘ s = id`, lift
`f ∘ r : A → D ⧸ J` to `g : A → D`. Then `g ∘ s` lifts `f`.

The module reuses `Langlands.GroupScheme.AffineLine.coeff_toLaurent_of_neg`
and `Langlands.GroupScheme.AffineLine.toLaurent_trunc` from
`GroupScheme.Dynamic.Representability`. These existing general algebra lemmas
and the new `mapRingHom_toLaurent` compare polynomials and Laurent
polynomials coefficientwise.
