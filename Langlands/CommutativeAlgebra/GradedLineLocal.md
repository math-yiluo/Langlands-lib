# Positively graded algebras over a reduced local ring that are polynomial in one variable

Namespace `Langlands.CommutativeAlgebra.GradedLine`. The ring `R` and the
`R`-algebra `A` lie in the same universe. The main results are
`exists_aeval_bijective` and `exists_aeval_bijective_of_universal`.

Let R be a reduced local ring and A a finitely generated R-algebra with a
ℤ-grading. Assume that A has no components of negative degree and that its
degree-zero part consists of scalars. Suppose that on the fibres over the
residue fields, A looks like a polynomial ring K[Y] with Y of degree d > 0, in
the sense made precise below. Then A is the polynomial algebra R[x] on a
homogeneous element x of degree d.

## Provenance

This is internal commutative algebra, written for the local-ring Isomorphism
Theorem task (ab10ec3c). It is the algebraic core of the proof that a root group
of a split reductive group over a reduced local ring is isomorphic to 𝔾ₐ. The
argument is a graded form of Nakayama's lemma, applied one homogeneous
component at a time. It replaces the étale-local argument of Conrad, *Reductive
group schemes* (2014), Lemma 4.2.2 (printed pp.117–118), which uses a section of the
root group through a point other than the identity.

## Gradings

A grading is a ring map c : A → A[T, T⁻¹] (a coaction of 𝔾ₘ).
`IsGrading R c` says that c fixes the scalars, that the coefficients of every
c(a) sum to a, and that each coefficient c(a)ₙ is homogeneous of degree n.

- `comp hc n` is the R-linear map a ↦ c(a)ₙ. `homog hc n` is the submodule of
  homogeneous elements of degree n, those with c(a) = single n a.
- Basic facts: `comp_mem_homog`, `comp_of_mem`, `comp_self`, `mul_mem_homog`,
  `pow_mem_homog`, `comp_mul_of_mem` (for a homogeneous of degree m,
  comp n (a b) = a · comp (n − m) b), `sum_comp` (a is the sum of its
  components), `comp_algebraMap` and `one_mem_homog`.

## Finite generation of the components

Assume `hneg`: there are no components of negative degree. Assume `hzero`: the
degree-zero component of every element is a scalar.

- `homog_eq_bot` (negative degrees vanish) and `homog_zero_le`
  (homog 0 ⊆ R·1).
- `posPairs c s` and `gen c`: the components of positive degree of the elements
  of a finite set s. `exists_sub_mem_span`: if s generates A, every element of A
  is a scalar plus an element of the ideal generated by these components. The
  proof is an induction over `Algebra.adjoin`.
- `homog_eq_sup`: in positive degree n, homog n is the finite sum over the
  generators g of degree e of g · homog (n − e).
- `homog_fg`: if A is of finite type over R, every homog n is a finitely
  generated R-module. The proof is strong induction on n, using the previous
  equality and `Submodule.fg_finset_sup`.

## Fibre lines

For a field K over R and an R-algebra map ψ : A → K[Y]:

- `IsFibreLine hc K d ψ` says that ψ is graded and that Y lies in the K-span of
  the image of ψ. Graded means that ψ maps homog n into multiples of Y^j with
  j·d = n.
- `IsFibreLine.coeff_one_comp`, `exists_coeff_one_ne_zero`, `eq_C_mul_X_pow` and
  `eq_zero` are the consequences used below. In particular, some homogeneous
  element of degree d has a nonzero coefficient of Y.
- `coeff_aeval_C_mul_X` computes the coefficients of f(u Y).
- `aeval_monomial_T_mul_C_X`, `coeff_aeval_T_mul_C_X` and
  `coeff_eq_zero_of_scaling`: a map ψ is graded as soon as it intertwines the
  grading with the scaling Y ↦ T^m Y. In formulas,
  `mapRingHom ψ (c a) = aeval (T^m · Y) (ψ a)` in K[Y][T, T⁻¹] for all a. This is
  how gradedness is verified geometrically.

## The main theorem

- `mem_smul_homog`: a homogeneous element of degree n that lies in 𝔪A lies in
  𝔪 · homog n, because comp n is R-linear.
- `powSpan x d n` is the R-span of the powers x^j with j·d = n.
- `le_powSpan_sup` and `homog_le_powSpan`: take ψ on the closed fibre with
  kernel contained in 𝔪A, and x ∈ homog d with ψ(x) = u Y, u ≠ 0. Then
  homog n ⊆ powSpan x d n + 𝔪 · homog n. The reason is that ψ(a) is a scalar
  multiple of Y^j; subtracting a lift of the scalar times x^j gives an element of
  𝔪A. Nakayama's lemma (`Submodule.le_of_le_smul_of_le_jacobson_bot`) together
  with `homog_fg` removes the 𝔪-term.
- `exists_aeval_bijective`: with such data at the closed point, and a fibre line
  ψ_p for every prime p, the map R[X] → A, X ↦ x, is bijective.
  - Surjectivity: every component of every element lies in a powSpan.
  - Injectivity: at every prime p, the coefficient u_p of Y in ψ_p(x) is
    nonzero; otherwise the coefficient of Y would vanish on the whole span of
    ψ_p (`coeff_one_eq_zero_of_mem_powSpan`). So if f(x) = 0, every coefficient
    of f maps to zero in every residue field, hence lies in the nilradical,
    which is zero since R is reduced.

## Universal fibre lines

- `IsUniversalLine K ψ`: for every K-algebra D, every R-algebra map A → D is ψ
  followed by evaluation at a unique element of D. This is how a fibre that is
  𝔾ₐ is recorded.
- `IsUniversalLine.X_mem_span`: a universal line spans K[Y]. Apply the
  universal property to D = K ⊗_R A to obtain a K-algebra map that is a section
  of the K-linear extension of ψ.
- `IsUniversalLine.ker_le`: at the closed point, the kernel of a universal line
  lies in 𝔪A. Apply the universal property to D = A ⧸ 𝔪A.
- `exists_aeval_bijective_of_universal`: the main theorem with these
  hypotheses. Suppose that for every field K over R there is a universal line
  ψ : A → K[Y] that is graded with Y of degree d. Then A ≅ R[x] with x
  homogeneous of degree d.

All theorems use only the standard axioms `propext`, `Classical.choice` and
`Quot.sound`.
