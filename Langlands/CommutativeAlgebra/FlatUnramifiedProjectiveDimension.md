# Projective dimension over flat unramified algebras

This is an internal algebraic construction. Let `R` and `S` be commutative
rings in the same arbitrary universe `u`, with a specified `R`-algebra
structure on `S`. All module types below also lie in `u`. No valuation,
Noetherian or module-finiteness assumption is imposed.

## Tensor induction and dimension

`algebraInductionFunctor R S` sends an actual `R`-module `M` to
`S ⊗[R] M` with its left `S`-action. Its map on morphisms is
`LinearMap.baseChange S`. The base-change identities prove the functor and
additivity laws. Tensor products preserve projectivity, giving the instance
`algebraInductionFunctor_preservesProjectiveObjects`.

If `S` is flat over `R`, `algebraInductionFunctor_shortExact` proves that
this functor preserves short exact sequences. It uses Mathlib's exactness
of left tensoring, preservation of injectivity by a flat tensor factor,
and preservation of surjectivity by tensoring. The short complexes here
are the original categorical complexes and their actual images under the
functor.

`algebraInduction_hasProjectiveDimensionLE` consequently preserves every
finite bound `n` on projective dimension. The proof inducts on `n`. The
zero case is preservation of projectives. For the successor case it chooses
a projective epimorphism onto the module, takes its categorical kernel,
and applies the short-exact-sequence dimension criterion before and after
tensoring. Thus no finite generation or finite presentation is needed for
this induction.

## The unramified diagonal and its module section

Assume now that `S` is essentially of finite type and formally unramified
over `R`. `exists_unramifiedDiagonal` produces an actual tensor
`e : S ⊗[R] S` such that

- multiplication sends `e` to `1`;
- `(1 ⊗ s) * e = (s ⊗ 1) * e` for every `s : S`.

Indeed the diagonal ideal `I`, the kernel of tensor multiplication, is
finitely generated by essential finite type. Formal unramifiedness makes
its cotangent module zero, so `I` is idempotent. Mathlib's finitely generated
idempotent ideal theorem supplies an idempotent generator `d`. Set
`e = 1 - d`. Every member of `I` annihilates `e`, and tensor multiplication
kills `d`. In particular `1 ⊗ s - s ⊗ 1` annihilates `e`. The displayed
properties follow. The conclusion records the two properties used below;
it does not include idempotency as a separate field.

Let `M` carry compatible `R`- and `S`-module structures. The genuine
`S`-linear map `diagonalTensorMap M` sends `a ⊗ b` to the `R`-linear map
`m ↦ a ⊗ (b • m)`. It is constructed with the heterogeneous tensor lift.
The lemmas `diagonalTensorMap_left` and `diagonalTensorMap_right` compute
multiplication by `s ⊗ 1` and `1 ⊗ s`, respectively, using tensor induction.

`algebraInductionCounit M` is the actual `S`-linear map
`S ⊗[R] M → M`, `a ⊗ m ↦ a • m`. Tensor induction proves that applying
this counit to `diagonalTensorMap M x m` gives `mul(x) • m`.
For any tensor satisfying the diagonal equality, `diagonalModuleSection`
is the map `m ↦ diagonalTensorMap M e m`. Its `S`-linearity follows from
the two multiplication computations and that equality.

For the tensor supplied by unramifiedness, multiplication one makes this
section split the counit. `exists_algebraInduction_retract` therefore
constructs a categorical `S`-module retract of the original `M` from
`S ⊗[R] M`. Flatness is not needed for this retract.

## Dimension transfer and scope

`hasProjectiveDimensionLE_of_flat_unramified` combines the retract with
the flat induction theorem: for every `n`, every module with its specified
compatible algebra action satisfies

`PD_R(M) ≤ n  →  PD_S(M) ≤ n`,

provided `S/R` is flat, essentially of finite type and formally unramified.
The proof uses Mathlib's projective-dimension bound for a retract. This
applies without assuming that `S` is finite as an `R`-module.

The provenance is internal: all maps and splittings are constructed here
from the imported Mathlib tensor, Kaehler ideal, flatness and homological
algebra results. This module imports no project geometric statements.
It provides a prerequisite for applying valuation polynomial dimension
bounds to etale charts. It does not prove coherence of those charts,
a local Picard theorem, line-bundle gluing, or the exact two-axis extension
target.
