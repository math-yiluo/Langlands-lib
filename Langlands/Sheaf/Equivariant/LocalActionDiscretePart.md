# The discrete part of a sheaf for a local action system

Author: Worker 26. Built on the published local action systems of
`Langlands.Sheaf.Equivariant.LocalAction` (Worker 13). Also uses Mathlib's
sheaves of modules, its abelian structure on sheaf categories, its abelian full
subcategories (`Mathlib.CategoryTheory.Abelian.Subcategory`) and its Grothendieck
abelian categories.

Let `T` be a category, `J` a Grothendieck topology on `T`, `G` a group with a
topology, and `L : LocalActionSystem T G` a local action system. A local action
of an open subgroup `H` on an object `U` is a monoid homomorphism
`H →* (End U)ᵐᵒᵖ`. Recall from `LocalAction` that a presheaf is discrete,
`L.IsDiscrete F`, when every admissible local action fixes each section on some
open subgroup. `L.discreteSheafProperty A J` is this property for sheaves. The
module answers the following question. Under a compactness hypothesis on the
site, do discrete sheaves of modules form an abelian category with enough
injectives, in which kernels and cokernels are those of all sheaves? It does
not construct any site or local action system.

## Equivariance of arrows

`IsLocallyEquivariant a b f`: here `a` is a local action of `H` on `U`, `b` a
local action of `H'` on `V`, and `f : V ⟶ U` an arrow. The proposition says
the following. For every open subgroup `K ≤ H'` there is an open subgroup
`M ≤ H` such that every `h ∈ H` with `h ∈ M` has some `k ∈ H'` with `k ∈ K` and
`(b k).unop ≫ f = f ≫ (a h).unop`. An arrow that intertwines the two actions on
a common open subgroup satisfies this with `k = h`. Berkovich's "N-morphisms"
on printed p.8 are of this kind. The condition is weaker because `k` may differ
from `h`.

`IsDiscreteSection.of_finite_family`: let `f i : V i ⟶ U` be finitely many
locally equivariant arrows, indexed by `Fin n`. Suppose they separate the
sections of `F` over `U`, i.e. two sections with equal restrictions along all
`f i` are equal. Suppose further that the restriction of a section `s` along
each `f i` is discrete for `b i`. Then `s` is discrete for `a`. Choose an open
subgroup `K i` fixing the `i`-th restriction and an `M i` given by local
equivariance for `K i`. Then `H ⊓ ⨅ i, M i` fixes `s`. For `h` in it, the
restriction of `h • s` along `f i` equals that of `s`, and separation finishes.
The coefficient category is any concrete category.

## The compactness hypothesis

`LocalActionSystem.HasFiniteEquivariantRefinements L J` is a class with one
field. Let `a` be an admissible local action of `H` on `U` and `S` a covering
sieve of `U`. Then there are `n : ℕ` and, for `i : Fin n`, arrows
`f i : V i ⟶ U` in `S` with the following properties. Each `V i` carries an
admissible local action `b i` of an open subgroup `H' i`. Each `f i` is locally
equivariant from `b i` to `a`. The sieve generated by the `f i` covers `U`.

This abstracts two facts that Berkovich uses for a compact `U` quasi-étale
over `X`. First, a covering of `U` has a finite subcovering by compact pieces.
Second, by the Key Lemma, the structure morphisms are N-morphisms for a small
open subgroup N. Both are used in step A.2 of the proof of Theorem 1.4.2. The
class is an explicit hypothesis. No instance is constructed here.

`LocalActionSystem.DiscreteSheaf L A J` is the full subcategory of `Sheaf J A`
on `L.discreteSheafProperty A J`. `L.discreteSheafInclusion A J` is its
inclusion functor.

## Modules: the discrete submodules

From here on, `R` is a commutative ring with `R : Type uR`, coefficients are in
`ModuleCat.{w} R`, and `F : Tᵒᵖ ⥤ ModuleCat.{w} R`.

- `isDiscreteSection_zero`, `IsDiscreteSection.add`, `IsDiscreteSection.smul`:
  the discrete sections for a fixed local action contain `0` and are closed
  under addition and scalar multiplication. For a sum, the intersection of the
  two fixing subgroups works.
- `isSeparated_of_isSheaf`: let `F` be a sheaf for `J` and `S` a covering
  sieve of `U`. Two sections over `U` whose restrictions along every arrow of
  `S` agree are equal. Proof: the inclusion of the submodule spanned by `x - y`
  and the zero map agree after every restriction in `S`. By the sheaf
  condition with this submodule as test object they are equal.
- `discreteSubmodule L F U` is the submodule of sections `s` over `U` with the
  following property. For every object `V`, every admissible local action `b`
  on `V` and every arrow `f : V ⟶ U`, the restriction `F.map f.op s` is
  discrete for `b`. `mem_discreteSubmodule_iff` unfolds membership.
- `map_mem_discreteSubmodule`: restriction along an arrow preserves the
  discrete submodules. `map_mem_discreteSubmodule_of_hom`: so does every
  morphism of presheaves.
- `isDiscreteSection_of_mem_discreteSubmodule`: on an object with an admissible
  local action `a`, every element of the discrete submodule is discrete for
  `a`. Take `f` to be the identity.
- `isDiscrete_iff_forall_mem`: `L.IsDiscrete F` holds exactly when every
  section lies in the discrete submodule.
- `mem_discreteSubmodule_of_covering`: assume
  `[L.HasFiniteEquivariantRefinements J]` and that `F` is a sheaf. Let `s` be a
  section over `U`, and suppose its restrictions along the arrows of a covering
  sieve `S` lie in the discrete submodules. Then `s` lies in the discrete
  submodule of `U`. Given an admissible `b` on `V` and `f : V ⟶ U`, refine the
  pulled-back sieve `S.pullback f` by a finite locally equivariant family. The
  restrictions of `F.map f.op s` along it are discrete by hypothesis. By
  `isSeparated_of_isSheaf` the family separates sections over `V`, so
  `IsDiscreteSection.of_finite_family` applies.

## The discrete part

- `discretePresheaf L F` is the presheaf `U ↦ discreteSubmodule L F U`, with
  the restricted restriction maps. `discretePresheafι L F` is its inclusion
  into `F`, injective on sections by `discretePresheafι_app_injective`.
  `discretePresheafMap L φ` is the restriction of a morphism `φ`.
  `discretePresheafFunctor L` is the resulting endofunctor of presheaves.
- `isDiscrete_discretePresheaf`: the discrete part is discrete. Use
  `IsDiscreteSection.of_injective` along the inclusion.
- `discretePresheaf_isSheaf`: under `[L.HasFiniteEquivariantRefinements J]`,
  the discrete part of a sheaf is a sheaf. The proof checks the sheaf
  condition for each test module `E` and covering sieve. Separatedness:
  compose with the injective inclusion and use uniqueness of gluing in `F`.
  Existence: glue the family composed with the inclusion in `F`. Every value
  of the gluing has restrictions in the discrete parts along the sieve, so it
  lies in the discrete part by `mem_discreteSubmodule_of_covering`. Then
  restrict the codomain.
- With `[L.HasFiniteEquivariantRefinements J]`:
  `discreteSubsheafFunctor L J` is the endofunctor of `Sheaf J (ModuleCat R)`
  taking a sheaf to its discrete part. `discreteSubsheafι L J` is the natural
  inclusion into the identity. `isDiscrete_discreteSubsheaf` says the image is
  discrete. `discreteCoreflector L J` is the corestricted functor
  `Sheaf J (ModuleCat R) ⥤ L.DiscreteSheaf (ModuleCat R) J`.
- `discreteLift hS f`: a morphism `f : S ⟶ F` from a discrete sheaf factors
  through the discrete subsheaf of `F`. `discreteLift_comp_ι` records the
  factorization.
- `discreteAdjunction L J`: the inclusion of discrete sheaves is left adjoint
  to `discreteCoreflector L J`. The hom bijection sends `f` to
  `discreteLift _ f`, and its inverse composes with the inclusion.

## Abelian structure

This section also assumes `[HasSheafify J (ModuleCat.{w} R)]`, so that
`Sheaf J (ModuleCat R)` is abelian in Mathlib.

- `injective_app_of_mono`: a monomorphism of module sheaves is injective on
  sections. The forgetful functor to presheaves is a right adjoint, so it
  preserves monomorphisms, and so does evaluation.
  `mono_discreteSubsheafι_app` says the inclusion of the discrete part is a
  monomorphism.
- `discreteSheafProperty_isClosedUnderSubobjects`: a subsheaf of a discrete
  sheaf is discrete, by `IsDiscrete.of_injective`.
- `discreteSheafProperty_of_isIso`: a sheaf is discrete if the inclusion of
  its discrete part is an isomorphism.
  `discreteSheafProperty_isClosedUnderQuotients`: a quotient of a discrete
  sheaf is discrete. An epimorphism from a discrete sheaf factors through the
  discrete subsheaf of the target. So that inclusion is an epimorphism and a
  monomorphism, hence an isomorphism.
- `discreteSheafProperty_containsZero`: the zero sheaf is discrete, being a
  subobject of its own discrete part.
- `sheaf_limit_sections_ext` (no sheafification assumption): sections of a
  finite limit of module sheaves are determined by their projections.
  `discreteSheafProperty_isClosedUnderBinaryProducts`: two open subgroups
  fixing the two projections of a section have an intersection fixing the
  section. `discreteSheafProperty_isClosedUnderFiniteProducts` follows.
- `discreteSheaf_abelian`: `L.DiscreteSheaf (ModuleCat R) J` is abelian. This
  is Mathlib's instance for full subcategories closed under kernels, cokernels
  and finite products, and containing zero.
- `discreteSheafInclusion_preservesMonomorphisms`,
  `discreteSheafInclusion_preservesEpimorphisms`,
  `discreteSheafInclusion_preservesFiniteLimits`,
  `discreteSheafInclusion_preservesFiniteColimits`: the inclusion into all
  module sheaves preserves monomorphisms, epimorphisms, finite limits and
  finite colimits, so it is exact. Kernels and cokernels are computed among
  all sheaves (Mathlib's `preservesKernels_ι`, `preservesCokernels_ι`).
- `injective_discreteCoreflector_obj`: the discrete part of an injective module
  sheaf is injective among discrete sheaves. A right adjoint of a functor
  preserving monomorphisms preserves injectives.
- `discreteSheaf_enoughInjectives`: if `Sheaf J (ModuleCat R)` has enough
  injectives, so do discrete module sheaves (Mathlib's
  `EnoughInjectives.of_adjunction`).

`discreteSheaf_enoughInjectives_of_small` instantiates this for a small site
`T : Type w` (a `SmallCategory`) and a ring `R : Type w`. Mathlib then supplies
sheafification and shows that `Sheaf J (ModuleCat.{w} R)` is a Grothendieck
abelian category with enough injectives. So under
`[L.HasFiniteEquivariantRefinements J]` the discrete module sheaves have enough
injectives, and right derived functors on them are available.

## Provenance and scope

Derived infrastructure. Sources, read in the original by Worker 26:

- Berkovich, *Etale equivariant sheaves on p-adic analytic spaces* (unfinished
  1995–1996 manuscript), §1.4, printed pp.7–8 = PDF pp.7–8. Definition 1.4.1
  calls an étale `G_d`-sheaf a `G`-sheaf when, for every quasi-étale morphism
  `U → X` with compact `U`, the action of `G_U` on `F(U)` is discrete. Here
  `G_U` is the open subgroup given by the Key Lemma, which acts canonically on
  `U` (printed p.7). The category is denoted `T_G(X_ét)`. Step A.2 of the
  proof of Theorem 1.4.2 (printed p.8) uses finite coverings of compact
  spaces, on which the structure morphisms are `N`-morphisms for small open
  `N`. Theorem 1.4.2 identifies `T_G(X_ét)` with the category of sheaves on
  the site `X(G)_ét`, from which its abelian structure and enough injectives
  follow. That theorem is neither formalized nor used here.
- Harris, *Galois properties of cohomological automorphic forms on GL(n)*
  (1999), §2, printed p.304 = PDF p.6. Harris calls `𝒯` an "étale sheaf in
  smooth `Z/ℓ^m Z[S(ẽ)]`-modules" if, for any quasi-étale morphism
  `U → U(ẽ)` with `U` compact, the stabilizer `S(ẽ)_U` acts smoothly on
  `𝒯(U)`. He adds: "in other words, `𝒯` is what Berkovich calls discrete".

Correspondence and differences. `IsDiscrete` for a local action system whose
admissible actions are the canonical `G_U`-actions on compact `U` is the shape
of Definition 1.4.1. Discreteness for an admissible `a` and any open subgroup
of `G` contained in `H` matches "the action of `G_U` on `F(U)` is discrete".
The present module does not build Berkovich's site, the Key Lemma actions or
the `G_d`-sheaf structure. It also does not show that
`HasFiniteEquivariantRefinements` holds for analytic étale sites. These belong
to the analytic specialization. The abelian structure and enough injectives
are proved directly through the discrete-part coreflector, not via Theorem
1.4.2. The class `HasFiniteEquivariantRefinements` and the weak form
`IsLocallyEquivariant` are choices made for this formalization. They are the
properties the proof needs.

Universes: `T : Type u` with `Category.{v}`, `G : Type uG`, `R : Type uR`,
modules in `ModuleCat.{w} R`. The final small-site theorem takes `T : Type w`,
`SmallCategory T` and `R : Type w`.
