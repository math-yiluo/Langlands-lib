## Purpose

Let `X` be a `G`-space. In step A.4 of the proof of his Theorem 1.4.2, Berkovich defines a
sheaf `(b_*F)^G` on `X(G)_qét` by `(b_*F)^G(V(G)) = F(V)^G`, for a quasi-étale `G`-sheaf `F`
on `X`. Step B.2 does the same for étale `G`-sheaves. `AnalyticInvariantPresheaf` constructs
this as a presheaf. This module proves that it is a sheaf for the topologies of `Qét(X(G))`
and `Ét(X(G))`, assuming one geometric hypothesis.

The hypothesis is that the forgetful functor `toQuasiEtaleSite X : Qét(X(G)) ⥤ Qét(X)` (resp.
`toEtaleSite X`) satisfies `HasImagePullbacks`. Under it, the forgetful functor is continuous,
and so is `quasiEtaleToTwisted X`. Every sheaf `F` on the twisted site therefore restricts to
a sheaf `V(G) ↦ F(V)` on `Qét(X(G))`; this is Berkovich's `b_*F`. Its `G`-invariants form a
sheaf by `NaturalActionSheaf`.

Continuity of `toQuasiEtaleSite X` is the statement that Berkovich's morphism
`b : X → X(G)` gives a morphism of sites `X_qét → X(G)_qét` in the sense that pushforward
along it preserves sheaves. The hypothesis is not proved here (see "The image pullback
hypothesis" below).

## Setting

`K : Type u` is a complete ultrametric normed field, `G : Type z` a group with a topology,
and `X : GSpace.{u, v, v} K G`. The twisted sites are those of `X.obj.ρAut : G →* Aut X.obj.V`.
`R` is a commutative ring. Each declaration below takes the hypothesis `hX` explicitly.

## Quasi-étale

Let `hX : HasImagePullbacks (EquivariantQuasiEtaleSite.toQuasiEtaleSite X)`.

- `toQuasiEtaleSite_isContinuous X hX`: `toQuasiEtaleSite X` is continuous from
  `equivariantQuasiEtaleTopology X` to `quasiEtaleTopology X.obj.V`. Proof:
  `HasImagePullbacks.isContinuous`. The condition on coverings holds by definition, since
  the coverings of `Qét(X(G))` are the families whose images are quasi-étale coverings.
- `quasiEtaleToTwisted_isContinuous X hX`: `quasiEtaleToTwisted X` is continuous from
  `equivariantQuasiEtaleTopology X` to `twistedQuasiEtaleTopology X.obj.ρAut`. It is the
  composite of `toQuasiEtaleSite X` with the continuous inclusion `ι`
  (`Functor.isContinuous_comp`).

  `(quasiEtaleNaturalAction X).sheafInvariantSheafFunctor` for the two topologies. At a sheaf
  `F` and an object `V(G)` its value is the module `F(V)^G`.
- `quasiEtaleInvariantSheafCompIso X R hX`: followed by `sheafToPresheaf`, it is isomorphic
  to the presheaf functor `quasiEtaleInvariantPresheaf X R`.

## The image pullback hypothesis

`HasImagePullbacks (toQuasiEtaleSite X)` says the following. For `G`-morphisms `V → U` and
`W → U` in `Qét(X(G))`, there is an object `P(G)` of `Qét(X(G))` with `G`-morphisms to `V` and
`W` forming a commutative square, whose underlying square is a fibre product in `Qét(X)`.
The expected witness is the fibre product `V ×_U W` of analytic spaces with the diagonal
action of `G`. Proving this needs the following, none of which is in the Library:

- fibre products in `Qét(X)`. The Library has the statement `FibreProductPullbackStatement`
  (Worker 21, Berkovich 1993 Proposition 1.4.1). It is stated without proof, and only for
  strong maps of saturated presentations with Hausdorff spaces in a single universe;
- the continuous diagonal action of `G` on the fibre product, making it a `G`-space;
- quasi-étaleness (resp. étaleness) of the composite `V ×_U W → X`.

The hypothesis is therefore an explicit argument `hX`. It is never assumed as an axiom or an
instance. No result here states that it holds for any particular `X`.

## Provenance and correspondence

Derived. Berkovich asserts without proof that `b` gives a morphism of sites and that
`(b_*F)^G` is a sheaf. Here the morphism of sites is replaced by continuity of the forgetful
functor, which is what makes `V(G) ↦ F(V)` a sheaf. That continuity is derived from the
explicit hypothesis `hX`, which isolates the geometric input that the Library lacks. The
sheaf `b_*F` itself is not given a separate name: it is the restriction of `F` along
`quasiEtaleToTwisted X` (`Functor.op_comp_isSheaf`), and only its invariants are packaged as
a functor.

As in `AnalyticInvariantPresheaf`, the construction is defined for every module sheaf on the
twisted site (Berkovich's `G^d`-sheaves) and then restricted to `G`-sheaves. `g` acts on
sections by restriction along the twisted endomorphism with twist `g⁻¹`. The isomorphisms
`b^*((b_*F)^G) ≅ F` and `E ≅ (b_*b^*E)^G` of steps A.4–A.5 are not formalized.
