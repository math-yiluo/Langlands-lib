# Module and locally discrete coordinates for twisted induction

This internal categorical development continues the actual right Kan coordinates and their transport formula. It gives a linear equivalence between an actual induced module section over U and the dependent family of ordinary sections over gU. The group universe is independent of the ring and module universe. No global categorical product hypothesis at the group universe is required: the existing limiting Kan fan realizes a coordinate family by a map from the free rank-one module R, and its uniqueness distinguishes sections by their coordinates. The coefficient ring and module carriers are in the same universe.

`coordinateMap` is the explicit linear map which, for a twisted arrow f with twist h, reads the coordinate at g h⁻¹ and applies the ordinary restriction along `transport f g`. `moduleCoordinates_map` proves that it computes the existing right Kan map. A coordinate family is `IsCoordinateDiscrete` when one open subgroup of the acting local subgroup fixes it under these explicit maps. `isDiscreteSection_iff` proves that this is exactly the existing discreteness predicate for the induced section. For a local action that lifts the base action, the twist of the arrow for h is h⁻¹, so the coordinate read is g h.

For any supplied local action system, `coordinateDiscreteSieve` consists of the arrows factoring through admissible objects where the explicitly restricted coordinate family is discrete. This is proved equal to the existing discrete sieve under the linear equivalence. With admissible refinements for the supplied topology, `smoothCoordinateSubmodule` is the image of the existing locally discrete submodule under that equivalence. Its membership theorem states exactly that the explicit coordinate sieve covers, and `smoothModuleCoordinates` identifies its elements with sections of the actual locally discrete presheaf part.

`smoothInductionCoordinates` specializes the same equivalence to the underlying sections of the existing sheaf `smoothCoreflector` applied to actual induction of an ordinary sheaf. Its value formula agrees with the actual inclusion of the locally discrete subsheaf into induction. This uses a translation-invariant topology on the ordinary site and admissible refinements on the associated twisted site.

All claims are derived from the current Library's Kan fan, twisted transport, and local-action definitions, with the free rank-one module and submodule transport from Mathlib. These are coordinate identifications. Analytic extension, support, exactness, and compact cohomological vanishing remain separate obligations.
