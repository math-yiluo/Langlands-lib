# Products have the expected augmentation order in characteristic zero

Let A be a commutative Hopf algebra over a characteristic-zero field K, and I its augmentation ideal. Suppose a finite family ξ in I is linearly independent modulo I² and spans I modulo I². If x has exact I-adic order m and y has exact order n, then xy does not lie in I^(m+n+1). Finite type of A is not a hypothesis of this lemma.

The augmentation projection shows the comultiplication defect Δ(x) − x tensor 1 − 1 tensor x lies in each of the ideals generated by I in a tensor factor. The map Ψ: A → (A/I^n) tensor (A/I²) therefore kills I^(n+1). A Taylor formula with square-zero increments applied to a homogeneous polynomial relation reduces it to a relation among its partial derivatives. Linear independence of ξ modulo I² and strong induction on degree force all those derivatives to vanish. Euler's homogeneous identity in characteristic zero then rules out a nonzero homogeneous polynomial relation in the next filtration step.

Every element of I^n is congruent modulo I^(n+1) to a homogeneous degree-n polynomial in ξ, by induction on ideal powers and the spanning assumption. Represent x and y this way. Their nonzero homogeneous polynomials have nonzero product over the field; the previous injectivity result rules out xy lying in the next power.
