# Cyclic Herbrand quotients of two full invariant lattices in one real representation

`Langlands.GroupTheory.CyclicLatticeHerbrandInvariance.{v,w}` is a
`Prop`-valued definition. It states Milne, *Class Field Theory*, Chapter VII,
Lemma 3.5, written with ordinary Mathlib group cohomology and with the
Herbrand quotient equality cross-multiplied. It states the lemma and does not
prove it.

## Exact objects and quantifiers

The proposition quantifies over the following data.

- A group `G : Type v` with `[Finite G]` and `[IsCyclic G]`. The trivial group
  is allowed, because there is no `Nontrivial G` hypothesis.
- A real vector space `V : Type w`, given by `[AddCommGroup V] [Module ℝ V]`,
  with `[FiniteDimensional ℝ V]`. The universe `w` is independent of `v`, and
  `V` may be zero-dimensional.
- A group homomorphism `ρ : G →* (V ≃ₗ[ℝ] V)`. This is an action of `G` on `V`
  by real linear automorphisms, which is the same thing as an `ℝ[G]`-module
  structure on `V`.
- Two representations `M N : Rep.{v} CoefficientScalars.{v} G`. Here
  `CoefficientScalars.{v} = ULift.{v} ℤ` is the lifted integer coefficient ring
  published in `Langlands.GroupTheory.CocycleCohomology`. This is the coefficient
  ring of the arithmetic lattice representations already in the Library.
- Additive homomorphisms `ιM : M →+ V` and `ιN : N →+ V`, with these
  hypotheses:
  - `Function.Injective ιM` and `Function.Injective ιN`;
  - equivariance: `ιM (M.ρ g x) = ρ g (ιM x)` for all `g` and `x : M`, and
    likewise for `ιN`;
  - full lattice conditions: there are a type `ι : Type w` and a real basis
    `b : Module.Basis ι ℝ V` with
    `Set.range ιM = (Submodule.span ℤ (Set.range b) : Set V)`, and likewise,
    with its own index type and basis, for `ιN`.
- The hypotheses `Finite (groupCohomology M 1)` and
  `Finite (groupCohomology M 2)`.

The conclusion is the conjunction of `Finite (groupCohomology N 1)`,
`Finite (groupCohomology N 2)` and

\[
  \lvert H^2(G,M)\rvert \cdot \lvert H^1(G,N)\rvert
  = \lvert H^2(G,N)\rvert \cdot \lvert H^1(G,M)\rvert .
\]

Every cohomology group here is Mathlib's `groupCohomology` of the given
representation, in the stated degree. Each cardinality is `Nat.card` of the
underlying type of that cohomology module. The finiteness clauses make all four
cardinalities finite. Each is also nonzero, because a group is nonempty. So the
equation is equivalent to `|H²(G,M)|/|H¹(G,M)| = |H²(G,N)|/|H¹(G,N)|`, and the
statement needs no rational division.

## Meaning of the lattice hypotheses

Injectivity and equivariance make `ιM` a `G`-module isomorphism from `M` onto
its image `ιM(M) ⊆ V`, where `G` acts on the image through `ρ`. In particular
the image is a `G`-stable subgroup of `V`, and the cohomology of `M` is that of
the `G`-stable subgroup `ιM(M)`. The same holds for `N`. The full lattice
condition says that this image is exactly the `ℤ`-submodule of `V` generated by
some real basis of `V`. The two images may use different bases, and no relation
between `M` and `N` is assumed beyond their common ambient representation
`(V, ρ)`.

On an abelian group, a `ULift ℤ`-module structure is unique: it is the
canonical `ℤ`-action, because the ring `ULift ℤ` is `ℤ`. So the cohomology here
is the usual cohomology of `G`-modules. Using the published lifted integers puts
`M`, `N`, their cohomology and `G` in the one universe `v` that Mathlib's
`groupCohomology` needs. `V` has its own universe `w`.

## Source correspondence

The source is J. S. Milne, *Class Field Theory*, v4.03 (2020), literature ID
`milne-2020-class-field-theory`. In this edition the PDF page number is the
printed page number plus nine.

- **Lemma 3.5** (Chapter VII, printed p. 209, PDF p. 218): `G` is a finite
  cyclic group, `V` is a real vector space on which `G` acts linearly (an
  `ℝ[G]`-module), and `M`, `N` are two `G`-stable full lattices in `V`. If
  either `h(M)` or `h(N)` is defined, then so is the other, and they are equal.
- **Footnote 2** (printed p. 208, PDF p. 217) defines a *full lattice*: a
  subgroup `M` of a real vector space `V` that is the `ℤ`-submodule generated by
  a basis of `V`. Equivalently, `M` is finitely generated and the canonical map
  `ℝ ⊗_ℤ M → V` is an isomorphism. The Lean condition `Set.range ιM =
  span ℤ (range b)` is the first of these forms, applied to the image of `M`.
  The second form shows that a real vector space containing a full lattice is
  finite-dimensional. This is why the Lean statement assumes
  `[FiniteDimensional ℝ V]`, as the task asks.
- **Herbrand quotient** (Chapter II, Section 3, after Proposition 3.4, printed
  p. 81, PDF p. 90): `h(M) = |H⁰_T(G,M)| / |H¹_T(G,M)|`. It is *defined* when
  both groups are finite. By the periodicity of Proposition 3.4 on the same page,
  `H⁰_T(G,M) ≅ H²(G,M)` for finite cyclic `G`, and `H¹_T = H¹`. Hence "`h(M)` is
  defined" is the hypothesis `Finite (groupCohomology M 1) ∧
  Finite (groupCohomology M 2)`, and `h(M) = h(N)` is the cross-multiplied
  equation above.
- **Symmetry**: Milne's "if either is defined" is symmetric in `M` and `N`.
  The Lean statement asserts one direction, from `M` to `N`. The hypotheses are
  symmetric, so the other direction is the same proposition applied with `M` and
  `N` exchanged.

The task also cites two lemmas used in Milne's proof of Lemma 3.5. Neither is
part of this statement.

- **Lemma 3.2** (printed p. 208, PDF p. 217): for a finite group, an infinite
  field `k` and an extension field `Ω ⊇ k`, finite-dimensional `k[G]`-modules
  that become isomorphic over `Ω` are already isomorphic over `k`.
- **Lemma 3.4** (printed pp. 208–209, PDF pp. 217–218): for finite cyclic `G`
  and finitely generated `G`-modules `M`, `N` with `ℚ ⊗ M ≅ ℚ ⊗ N`, if either
  Herbrand quotient is defined, then so is the other, and they are equal. Its
  proof uses Chapter II, Corollary 3.9 (printed p. 82, PDF p. 91): a
  homomorphism with finite kernel and cokernel preserves the Herbrand quotient.

Milne proves Lemma 3.5 as follows. The canonical maps `M ⊗ ℝ → V ← N ⊗ ℝ` are
`G`-isomorphisms, so Lemma 3.2, with `k = ℚ` and `Ω = ℝ`, gives
`M ⊗ ℚ ≅ N ⊗ ℚ`. Lemma 3.4 then applies. A proof of the present statement is
left to follow-up work.

## Derived formulation

This is the source lemma, adapted to Lean in four ways that do not change its
mathematical content.

1. Each lattice is given as an abstract representation together with an
   injective equivariant additive embedding into `V`, rather than as a subgroup
   of `V`. A `G`-stable subgroup `L` of `V` is recovered by taking for `M` the
   representation on the subtype `L` and for `ιM` the inclusion. Conversely,
   the image of an embedding is a `G`-stable subgroup isomorphic to `M`. The
   task permits this form, and it applies directly to the Library's lattice
   representations, whose carriers are subtypes of a real coordinate space.
2. Finite dimensionality of `V` is stated explicitly. Footnote 2 implies it.
3. Milne's `H⁰_T` is replaced by the ordinary `H²` through cyclic periodicity,
   because the task requires ordinary Mathlib `groupCohomology` throughout.
4. The quotient equality is cross-multiplied, and only one direction of the
   symmetric statement is stated.

## Relation to Mathlib's lattice API

Mathlib calls `L : Submodule ℤ E` a lattice when `E` is a normed real space,
`L` is discrete (`[DiscreteTopology L]`) and `L` spans `E` over `ℝ`
(`IsZLattice ℝ L`, in `Mathlib.Algebra.Module.ZLattice.Basic`). The statement
uses the topology-free basis-span form instead, so that it needs no norm or
topology on `V`. In a finite-dimensional normed real space the two notions
agree.

- From `IsZLattice ℝ L`, `ZLattice.module_free` gives a `ℤ`-basis of `L`.
  `Module.Basis.ofZLatticeBasis ℝ L` turns it into a real basis of the ambient
  space, and `Module.Basis.ofZLatticeBasis_span` says that its `ℤ`-span is `L`.
  So `L` with `L.subtype.toAddMonoidHom` satisfies the full lattice hypothesis,
  with index type `Module.Free.ChooseBasisIndex ℤ L`, which lies in the universe
  of the ambient space.
- Conversely, for a real basis `b` with finite index type (automatic in finite
  dimension, `Module.Finite.finite_basis`), `Submodule.span ℤ (Set.range b)` has
  Mathlib's `DiscreteTopology` instance (`ZSpan`) and the instance
  `instIsZLatticeRealSpan`.

Before submission the author compiled both directions privately, together with
an instantiation of the proposition at universes `.{v,v}`. That instantiation
uses, for a number field `E : Type v` with `G = E ≃ₐ[F] E` finite cyclic,
`V = InfinitePlace E → ℝ` and `ρ = fullLogPermutation F E`. Its two lattices
are `infinitePlaceCoordinateLatticeRepresentation F E` and
`augmentedFullLogUnitLatticeRepresentation F E`, with the subtype inclusions as
embeddings. Equivariance holds by `rfl`, and by
`augmentedFullLogUnitLatticeAut_apply`. These Lean checks are not part of this
candidate. The universes of the proposition are independent, so the same applies
to `V = fullLogHyperplane E : Type v`, or to any `V` in another universe.
