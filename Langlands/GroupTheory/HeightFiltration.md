# Height subgroups and their ordered coordinates

This is an internal group-theoretic part of the root-height filtration
construction. It starts with a finite ordered family of actual subgroups
`H i` of a group, with natural-number heights `w i`, and the hypothesis
that commutators of two factors lie in the subgroup generated by factors
at heights at least the sum of the two input heights.

`factor H w d i` is `H i` when `d ≤ w i`, and the trivial subgroup
otherwise. `level H w d` is the subgroup generated by these factors.
The levels descend, the zero level is the subgroup generated by all
factors, and the one level is the same subgroup if all heights are
positive. A level above every factor height is trivial.

With positive heights, the sum bound implies the strict increase required
by the published `HeightCollection` theorem. Applying it after discarding
the lower factors proves `exists_coords`: every element of a level has an
ordered factorization with coordinates trivial at every lower height.
The order of the factors is arbitrary.

The normality argument conjugates each generator of a level by an
element of one original factor. The commutator belongs to a level no
lower than that generator, so the conjugate remains in the level.
Induction on the generated subgroup then proves that each original
factor normalizes every level. Consequently the levels are normal in
the full generated subgroup and every next level is normal in the
current one. The normality results do not require positive heights.

These assertions are derived group theory supporting the weighted-root
construction in Conrad, *Reductive group schemes* (2014),
Theorem 5.4.3, pp.174–177. They do not assert representability, smoothness,
closedness, a represented quotient, or descent of a vector bundle.
