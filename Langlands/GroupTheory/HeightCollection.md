# Finite subgroup collection by commutator heights

This module proves an internal finite group calculation intended for the
positive-root collection argument. Its inputs and conclusion concern ordinary
subgroups of an arbitrary group in universe `v`.

For a finite family of subgroups, suppose the commutator of two factors lies
in the subgroup generated by factors whose heights exceed both input heights.
More precisely, the subgroups are indexed by `Fin n` and have natural-number
heights `w`. For every pair, including equal indices, their commutator subgroup
must lie in the supremum of the factors whose heights strictly exceed the
maximum of the two input heights. No monotonicity in the index is assumed.

`orderedProduct_surjective_of_iSup_eq_top` says that if the factors generate the
ambient group, every element has coordinates in their prescribed order.
The proof inducts on the number of nontrivial factors.
A nontrivial factor of maximal height is central. Passing to its quotient
reduces that number. Lifts of the quotient coordinates differ from the given
element by an element of this central factor, which can be inserted in its
original position. The implementation reuses the published ordered-product
central-correction calculation from `Rational.OrderedProductAlgebra`.

`range_orderedProduct_eq_iSup` removes the generation premise by applying the
first theorem inside the subgroup generated by the factors. Images under its
injective inclusion transport the commutator bound, and the lifted coordinates
give the range equality. `orderedProductSubgroup` packages this range as an
actual subgroup, so the ordered product is closed under multiplication and
inverses. Its equality with the supremum of the factors shows that the range
depends only on the family of subgroups, hence is independent of its ordering
whenever the displayed commutator condition holds. The empty family is included.

The definitions and proofs are derived internal content rather than a
translation of a separate source assertion. No root commutator bound,
root-system property, uniqueness of coordinates, or chamber decomposition is
asserted here.
