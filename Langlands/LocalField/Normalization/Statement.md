# Complete p-adic arithmetic normalization target

Author: Worker 27. This module defines the complete proposition target.
Acceptance of this statement does not establish acceptance of its proof.

`Langlands.PadicNormalizationStatement` quantifies every natural number `p`
with `Fact p.Prime`, every `F : Langlands.PadicField p` in an arbitrary
carrier universe, and every explicitly supplied `T : F.TopologicalData`.
It imposes no additional Galois, unramified, residue, ramification or
normalization hypothesis. Its seven conjuncts are named propositions in
`Langlands.PadicField.TopologicalData`.

1. `ResidueArithmeticLaws` says that the actual residue field has
   characteristic `p`, is finite and finite-dimensional over `ZMod p`,
   and receives an injective scalar map. Its actual cardinality `q` and
   actual dimension `f` satisfy `f > 0`, `q > 1` and `q = p^f`.
2. `DiscreteOrderLaws` specifies the surjective discrete valuation, the
   chosen uniformizer of multiplicative value `exp (-1)`, and additive
   order one on that uniformizer. The integer-valued order uses the
   explicit convention `ord 0 = 0`; additivity on products therefore
   requires nonzero factors. The additive inequality for sums explicitly
   requires both summands and their sum to be nonzero. Every integer
   occurs as the order of a nonzero element. It also records inversion and identifies
   the positive ramification index with the positive order of `p`.
3. `ResidueAbsoluteValueLaws` specifies zero at zero, one at one,
   nonnegativity, positivity away from zero, the formula `q^(-ord x)`
   for nonzero `x`, multiplicativity for all pairs, and the
   nonarchimedean inequality for all sums, and value `q^(-1)` on the
   chosen uniformizer.
4. `IntegralExtensionLaws` identifies the actual integer ring as the
   integral closure of the actual base integer ring in `F`. It records
   integrality exactly for the integer-ring elements, a finite free
   module whose rank is `[F : Q_p]`, and the equality of the integer ring
   with both absolute-value and spectral-norm unit balls.
5. `RamificationDegreeLaws` identifies the ideal generated by `p` and
   the image of the base maximal ideal with the `e`-th power of the
   actual maximal ideal. Mathlib's ramification and inertia indices
   equal `e` and `f`. Their product is `[F : Q_p]`, which is positive.
6. `NormalizedComparisonLaws` gives `ord_F(a) = e * ord_p(a)` on the
   base embedding, including zero with the stated convention. It gives
   `normalizedAbs_F(a) = norm_p(a)^[F : Q_p]` on every base element, and
   `normalizedAbs_F(x) = canonicalNorm_F(x)^[F : Q_p]` on every field
   element, including zero. In degree one it records `e = f = 1`,
   `q = p`, and equality of the two absolute values.
7. `NormalizationTopologyLaws` identifies the supplied topology with
   the canonical module topology and the spectral-norm topology.
   The positive-radius sets `{x | normalizedAbs_F(x-a) < epsilon}`
   form a topological basis, and their generated topology is both the
   supplied topology and the canonical module topology.

`PadicNormalizationBasicStatement` retains the first three groups as a
reusable projection target; it is part of this same coherent submission.
The complete target is `PadicNormalizationStatement`.

The scalar structure on integer rings is exactly `T.integerAlgebra`;
the scalar structure used to define `f` is exactly `T.residueAlgebra`.
The valuation ring and residue field are the actual ones in
`TopologicalData`. The normed-field structure appears only in an explicit
`letI`, and the normalized topology is expressed by its generating balls.
The statement installs no global norm or topology instance.

Marvin Jay Greenberg, Graduate Texts in Mathematics 67, Springer (1979).

and absolute-value conventions, including `q^(-ord)` and the independence
of the induced topology. Chapter II, section 2, Proposition 3 and

The explicit spectral-norm and module-topology formulations are internal
interface comparisons derived from those normalizations; they are not
presented as literal quotations from Serre.
