# Helper lemmas for the block normal form of Frobenius-semisimple parameters

This module supports the proof of
`Langlands.WeilDeligneRepresentation.Blocks.ParameterNormalForm` in
`Langlands/WeilDeligne/ParameterNormalFormProof.lean`. Every declaration is in
the namespace `Langlands.WeilDeligneRepresentation.ParameterNormalForm`.

The module has two parts. The first part shows that componentwise
endomorphisms of products, direct sums and the blocks `σ ⊗ ℂ^m` are
semisimple. The second part proves both directions of Tate's criterion (Tate
1979, 4.1.3, `tate-1979-number-theoretic-background`): if inertia has finite
image, a complex representation is semisimple exactly when an element of
degree one acts semisimply. Here "semisimple endomorphism" means
`Module.End.IsSemisimple`. "Semisimple representation" means
`Representation.IsSemisimpleRepresentation`, that is, every subrepresentation
has a complementary subrepresentation. The implemented proof follows Tate's
argument: an element of nonzero degree, together with the kernel, generates a
subgroup of finite index, and the finite-index restriction theorem from the
Library applies.

## Semisimple endomorphisms

Let `K` be a field.

- `isSemisimple_pi`: on a finite product `Π i, V i`, the componentwise
  endomorphism `LinearMap.pi fun i => f i ∘ₗ LinearMap.proj i` is semisimple
  when each `f i` is. In Mathlib, an endomorphism `f` is semisimple when the
  `K[X]`-module `Module.AEval' f` is a semisimple module. The proof gives a
  `K[X]`-linear equivalence (`LinearEquiv.ofAEval`) from the `AEval'` module of
  the product map to the product of the modules `Module.AEval' (f i)`. A finite
  product of semisimple modules is semisimple, and `IsSemisimpleModule.congr`
  transfers this along the equivalence.
- `isSemisimple_lmap`: the same for a direct sum `⨁ i, V i` and the
  componentwise map `DirectSum.lmap f`, with any index type. The equivalence is
  `DFinsupp.mapRange.linearEquiv` of the maps `Module.AEval'.of (f i)`. Mathlib
  shows that a direct sum of semisimple modules is semisimple.
- `isSemisimple_map_diag`: on `V ⊗ K^m`, the operator
  `TensorProduct.map f (LinearMap.pi fun j => c ^ j • LinearMap.proj j)`, that
  is `f ⊗ diag(1, c, …, c^(m-1))`, is semisimple when `f` is. The isomorphism
  `TensorProduct.piScalarRight : V ⊗ K^m ≃ (Fin m → V)` turns this operator
  into the componentwise map `(c ^ j • f)_j`. Each `c ^ j • f` is semisimple by
  `Module.End.IsSemisimple_smul`, so `isSemisimple_pi` applies.
  `LinearEquiv.isSemisimple_iff` transports semisimplicity along the
  isomorphism; the compatibility is checked on pure tensors.

## Powers of one element

Let `ρ : Representation k G V` with `V` finite-dimensional, and `Φ : G`.

- `zpow_mem_of_mem`: a subspace `p` stable under `ρ Φ` is stable under every
  `ρ (Φ ^ n)` with `n : ℤ`. Elements whose action preserves `p` form a submonoid
  that contains `Φ`. It also contains `Φ⁻¹`: the restriction of `ρ Φ` to `p` is
  injective, hence surjective because `p` is finite-dimensional.
- `isSemisimpleRepresentation_of_zpow`: if every `ρ g` equals some `ρ (Φ ^ n)`
  and `ρ Φ` is semisimple, then `ρ` is semisimple. Given a subrepresentation
  `W`, semisimplicity of `ρ Φ` (`Module.End.isSemisimple_iff`) gives a
  `ρ Φ`-stable complement `q` of `W`. By `zpow_mem_of_mem`, `q` is stable under
  all of `G`. The Library lemma
  `FiniteIndexSemisimplicity.isCompl_of_isCompl_toSubmodule` turns it into a
  complementary subrepresentation.
- `isSemisimple_of_zpow`: the converse under the same hypothesis on `ρ`. A
  `ρ Φ`-stable subspace is `G`-stable by `zpow_mem_of_mem`. A complementary
  subrepresentation gives a `ρ Φ`-stable complement
  (`FiniteIndexSemisimplicity.isCompl_toSubmodule`).
- `exists_zpow_of_mem_sup`: every `h` in `Subgroup.zpowers Φ ⊔ ker ρ` acts as
  some `ρ (Φ ^ n)`. The subgroup lies in the preimage under `ρ.asGroupHom` of
  the cyclic subgroup generated by `ρ Φ`.
- `exists_finite_transversal`: if the image of a subgroup `I` under `ρ` is
  finite, there is a finite set `F ⊆ G` such that every `i ∈ I` satisfies
  `f⁻¹ * i ∈ ker ρ` for some `f ∈ F`. Take one preimage in `I` of each element
  of the image.

## Finite index

`finite_quotient`: let `degree : G →* Multiplicative ℤ` and `Φ` with
`degree Φ = ofAdd 1`. Let `g` have nonzero degree `d`, and let `N` be a
subgroup. Suppose a finite set `F` satisfies: every `i` of degree zero has
some `f ∈ F` with `f⁻¹ * i ∈ N`. Then `G ⧸ (zpowers g ⊔ N)` is finite. The
proof shows that the map `(r, f) ↦ Φ ^ r * f`, for `r < |d|` and `f ∈ F`,
reaches every coset. Write the degree of `x` as `e = d * t + r` with
`0 ≤ r < |d|`. Then `Φ ^ (-r) * x * g ^ (-t)` has degree zero, so it equals
`f * n` with `f ∈ F` and `n ∈ N`. Hence `x` and `Φ ^ r * f` lie in the same
coset.

## Tate's criterion

Let `G : Type uG`, `V : Type uV` a finite-dimensional complex vector space,
`ρ : Representation ℂ G V`, `degree : G →* Multiplicative ℤ`, and `Φ` with
`degree Φ = ofAdd 1`. Assume that `ρ` maps the kernel of `degree` (inertia) to
a finite set.

- `isSemisimpleRepresentation_of_isSemisimple`: if `ρ Φ` is semisimple, then
  `ρ` is semisimple.
- `isSemisimple_of_isSemisimpleRepresentation`: if `ρ` is semisimple, then
  `ρ g` is semisimple for every `g` of nonzero degree.

Both proofs use the subgroup `H = zpowers g ⊔ ker ρ`, with `g = Φ` in the
first direction. `exists_finite_transversal` and `finite_quotient` show that
`H` has finite index. The Library theorem
`Langlands.Weil.representationFiniteIndexRestrictionSemisimplicity` says that
over `ℂ`, a representation is semisimple exactly when its restriction to a
finite-index subgroup is. On `H`, every element acts as a power of `ρ g` by
`exists_zpow_of_mem_sup`. Therefore `isSemisimpleRepresentation_of_zpow` and
`isSemisimple_of_zpow` relate semisimplicity of `ρ|H` to semisimplicity of
`ρ g`.
