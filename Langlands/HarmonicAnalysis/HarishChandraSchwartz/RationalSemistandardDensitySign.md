# Sign of the Plancherel density on the semistandard spectrum

`RationalSemistandardDensitySignStatement` concerns the actual scalar
`GlobalForm.componentDensity` on the intrinsic semistandard spectrum of
the rational points of a connected reductive group over a nonarchimedean
local field of arbitrary characteristic, with a chosen maximal split torus.
The indices are semistandard parabolic data and, for each inducing Levi
`M`, orbits under the unitary unramified character group `Im X(M)`. An
orbit is based at an irreducible admissible representation of `M(F)` with
unitary central character and square-integrable matrix coefficients
modulo the split center of `M`.

The density is the inverse opposite-intertwiner composition scalar times
the product of the squares of the rank-one gamma constants over reduced
roots of the split center up to sign. The target takes the inducing split
components, Borel quotient Haar measures and cocharacter presentations.
For each orbit, `OrbitPacketData` supplies the compact factorizations,
rational continuations with nonempty open convergence subsets, a nonempty
open irreducibility locus and regularity of the inverse scalar at every
unitary unramified character. The field and representation carrier have
independent universes.

The first assertion gives a nonnegative real value at every unitary orbit
point. The second asserts that the points with strictly positive real part
are dense in each orbit. This density property comes from the nonzero
rational function and the density of its nonvanishing locus on the unitary
unramified parameter group. The wave-packet and Hermitian spectral-form
integrals use this scalar as a weight against the orbit Haar measure.
With source Haar measure on `Im X(M)`, the twist map
`χ ↦ ω₀ ⊗ χ` is locally measure preserving for the orbit Haar measure.

The sign conclusion is that of Waldspurger (2003), Lemma V.2.1, printed
pp. 292–293, with unitary inverse regularity supplied by the packet data.
Dense positivity follows from nonnegativity and the rational
nonvanishing in IV.3(6), p. 287. The orbit conventions are those of
I.1, p. 239, and V.1, pp. 287–288. The integrals use the conventions of
VI.3, p. 302, and VII.2, pp. 319–320.
