# The stabilizer of the minimal determinant vector

Multiplication by an exterior vector of an independent family annihilates exactly
the vectors in the span of that family. The forward implication uses independence
of the exterior product obtained by adjoining a vector outside the span. The
reverse implication uses alternation on the original generators. Applying an
exterior algebra map and cancelling a nonzero scalar shows that preserving the
exterior line preserves the subspace. In the determinant representation this
subspace is the intrinsic tangent submodule of the represented dynamic radical.

The rational Bruhat decomposition writes a point fixing the determinant vector
as `p n q`, with `p` and `q` in the minimal parabolic and `n` normalizing the
split torus. The parabolic factors act on the vector by units, so `n` and its
inverse preserve its line. Conjugation by `n` transports the tangent submodule
to that of the conjugate radical. Preservation in both directions makes the
two tangent submodules equal. The Lie inclusion criterion for cocharacters of
the same split torus then makes the two dynamic radicals equal.

The two cocharacters have the same centralizer, namely the centralizer of the
maximal split torus. The dynamic centralizer–radical decompositions give equality
of the parabolics on every test scheme. Self-normalization puts `n`, and hence
`p n q`, in the minimal parabolic. Its intersection with the opposite dynamic
radical is trivial. This proves `RationalMinimalDeterminantLineStabilizerStatement`
for the exterior realization of the determinant vector, as used in the
height comparison of Waldspurger (2003), II.3.4.
