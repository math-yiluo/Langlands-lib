# Uniform fixed-point bounds for the actual full orbit constant terms

`HasUniformPointwiseNormBounds` requires a positive norm bound at every
point, uniform over the whole parameter family. The bound may depend on the
point. The theorem obtains this property for the chosen full standard
constant terms from fixed-point bounds and shared bi-invariance of the
original unitary coefficient functions.

At a common standard level, the full constant-term recurrence has uniformly
bounded coefficients and an invertible constant coefficient, by the finite
positive annulus of actual generalized Jacquet exponent norms. For a specified
Levi point, refine the bi-invariance level by its conjugate. The untwisted
induced fixed dimension at this refined level is one ray threshold for every
unitary twist and every coefficient tensor. Beyond that threshold, the actual
chosen constant term at each later ray point equals the original coefficient
there, divided by the original nonzero half-modulus.

Only finitely many later points occur in the inverse recurrence. Fixed-point
bounds for the original family at those points therefore bound its full chosen
constant terms at the specified point. No continuity of the constant terms,
choice of tensor representatives, or global labeling of exponents is assumed.

The actual orbit specialization covers every orbit class by a literal unitary
twist of the original source and realizes the original coefficient by a tensor
in that fiber. Compactness of the entire actual unitary orbit and the already
proved continuity of the original coefficient at each group point give its
fixed-point bounds. The shared bi-invariance level is also derived from the
original smooth orbit section. Consequently the actual chosen orbit constant
terms have a common positive bound at every specified actual Levi point,
without additional analytic premises.

The contextual original source is Waldspurger, *La formule de Plancherel pour
les groupes p-adiques, d'après Harish-Chandra* (2003), Proposition I.6.2,
printed pp.249–250, and VI.2.3, printed pp.299–300, source
`waldspurger-2003-plancherel`. The quantitative recurrence proof route is
derived project content. Positive-part decay requires further analytic inputs.
