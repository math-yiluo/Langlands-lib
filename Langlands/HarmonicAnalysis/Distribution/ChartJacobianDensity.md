# The differential of the absolute Jacobian in smooth coordinates

The derivative of a smooth chart is a continuous linear equivalence at each source point, with inverse the derivative of the inverse chart. A fixed linear identification of the model and ambient spaces makes its absolute determinant a positive smooth function on the source.

Jacobi's formula gives its directional derivative as the absolute determinant times the trace of the derivative of the Jacobian matrix composed with its inverse. Suppose a constant coordinate direction pushes forward to a variable linear combination of trace-zero ambient linear fields. Differentiate this identity in an arbitrary direction and use symmetry of the second derivative. After taking traces, the conjugate endomorphism terms have trace zero. The remaining rank-one terms are the derivatives of the weights evaluated on the coordinate fields.

This is the density correction for the local test-function identity used in real matrix conjugation descent. The calculation depends only on smooth chart derivatives and the trace-zero condition.
