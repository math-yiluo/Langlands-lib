# Local weighted covariance on zero-normal chart fibers

Restrict a chart transition to the part of a chosen open domain where the zero-normal fibers are locally preserved and whose image satisfies the corresponding condition for the inverse. A fixed slice point of the original transition stays in the restricted transition if it lies in the chosen open domain and both directions preserve the fibers near it.

Consider a smooth chart with smooth inverse and an ambient linear equivalence of unit absolute determinant. If the ambient distribution transforms under pullback by a scalar \(c\), the Jacobian-normalized distribution satisfies the weighted pullback law with the same scalar on the restriction. The proof applies the chart transition identity to a test in the restricted target and then includes its weighted pullback in the original product. A normalized distribution takes the same value on copies of a test in different open domains inside the chart.

For the coefficient calculation, assume also that the normalized product distribution factors through orbit integration. Use an additive Haar measure and a scalar orbit test of integral one for that measure. Inserting this test defines the slice distribution through which the normalized distribution factors. The orbit and normal opens contain zero. The slice distribution has transverse order at most \(i\), and the normal cutoff used to define its order-\(i\) coefficient is one near zero.

Take a symmetric multilinear coefficient test. At each slice point in its support, assume the transition fixes the point at orbit and normal coordinate zero, both directions preserve the zero-normal fibers nearby, the absolute orbit determinant is one, and the normal derivative is a fixed linear map \(M\). Precomposing the test with \(M\) in each multilinear argument multiplies its leading coefficient evaluation by \(c\). The full Jacobian at each fixed point is one by the ambient determinant identity and positivity of the chart Jacobian. The proof applies the zero-dilation covariance law and evaluates its transport kernel at the fixed points in the support of the slice test.

This internal result transfers the ambient action to the leading coefficient used in real matrix spectral descent.
