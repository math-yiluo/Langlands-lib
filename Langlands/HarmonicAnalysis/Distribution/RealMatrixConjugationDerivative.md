# Infinitesimal conjugation invariance of smooth matrix distributions

The time derivative at zero of a jointly smooth test family with
a common compact support is a test supported in that compact set. At
nonzero times the smooth difference quotient is supported in
the common compact set. At zero it has that support because the
original family vanishes off that set near zero. A continuous
functional constant on the family vanishes on its difference
quotients at all nonzero times, hence also on its derivative by
continuity.

For a real square matrix \(X\), exponential conjugation gives the
smooth family \(A\mapsto f(\exp(tX)A\exp(-tX))\). Its nearby slices
have support in the image under inverse conjugation of the product
of a compact time interval with the support of \(f\). At zero the
derivative is \(A\mapsto Df(A)(XA-AX)\). A conjugation-invariant
distribution has constant evaluation on the family and therefore
annihilates this derivative.

This internal result supplies the infinitesimal conjugation
identities used to analyze distributions along the orbits.
