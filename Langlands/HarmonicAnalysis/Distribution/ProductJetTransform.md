# Transformation of the first potentially nonzero transverse derivative

Let \(f\) be a smooth product test whose transverse derivatives of
orders strictly below \(i\) vanish along the zero section. If \(H\)
is smooth at \((x,0)\) and carries that point to \((x',0)\), the
order-\(i\) transverse derivative of \(f\circ H\) at \((x,0)\)
is the order-\(i\) transverse derivative of \(f\) at \((x',0)\)
applied to the normal components of \(D H(x,0)(0,v_k)\).
The map \(v\mapsto \operatorname{pr}_{W'} D H(x,0)(0,v)\)
is bundled as the continuous linear map `productNormalDerivative H x`.

The flat-product-jet theorem gives vanishing of the full lower
derivatives of \(f\). In the ordered-partition chain rule, only the
partition into singletons can contribute. Every other partition
applies a lower derivative of the outer function. This proves
the corresponding leading-order chain rule for arbitrary smooth maps.

For positive \(i\), differentiating the vanishing order-\((i-1)\)
derivative along the zero section shows that the order-\(i\)
derivative kills a tangential first argument. Symmetry gives the
same conclusion in every slot. Expanding each argument into its
tangential and normal components leaves only the term with all
normal components. Order zero is the evaluation identity.

This internal result supplies the change-of-coordinates formula
for a leading transverse coefficient.
