# Vanishing on a real spectral chart

Let \(D\) be a complex-valued distribution on real square matrices. Assume \(D\) is invariant under conjugation by every invertible real matrix and odd under ordinary transpose.

Take a smooth affine conjugation chart with smooth inverse, centered at a matrix \(A\), with orbit, semisimple, and normal coordinates. The semisimple directions lie in a complement to the orbit tangent, and the normal coordinate uses a complement to those directions within that complement. The chart has the local similarity recovery property. The diagonal pair \((A+z,A+z)\) is in the recovery neighborhood for every semisimple coordinate \(z\) in the slice open, and the tangent image of \(A+z\) equals that of \(A\) there.

Restrict to a product inside the chart whose orbit and normal opens contain zero. The orbit open is the continuous real-linear image of a product of copies of the same open interval. Assume the Jacobian-normalized restriction of \(D\) has finite transverse order on this product.

Conjugation invariance of \(D\) makes the normalized distribution kill constant orbit derivatives, so it factors through integration in that box. Its leading coefficient after integration is invariant under conjugation by the stabilizer of \(A\) and odd under adjusted transpose. Conjugation invariance supplies the stabilizer law and preserves the transpose sign after adjustment by a conjugation. The two actions fix the semisimple slice and carry similar matrices to similar matrices in both directions. The chart action therefore preserves the zero-normal fibers locally, and the absolute orbit Jacobian is one at the fixed points. The covariance law acts on pure covector-power tests through the corresponding normal block.

Conormal invariance and expansion of symmetric forms by pure powers force the leading coefficient to vanish on every symmetric coefficient test. Since the top jet of a test is symmetric, descending the finite transverse order kills the integrated slice distribution. The original normalized product restriction vanishes by its factorization through that distribution.

This internal local vanishing result supplies the analytic step in the real matrix distribution transpose proof.
