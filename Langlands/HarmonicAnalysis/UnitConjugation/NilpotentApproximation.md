# Small dilates of compact conjugacy saturations near the nilpotent cone

Let `F` be a complete nontrivially normed field and `R` a finite-dimensional normed `F`-algebra. For a continuous `p : X → R`, let `K` be compact in `X` and let `V` be open in `X`, containing every point of `K` whose image is nilpotent. If `ω` is compact in `R`, then there is an `ε > 0` with the following property. For `x ∈ K`, `g ∈ Rˣ`, `a ∈ ω` and `c ∈ F`, the two bounds `‖c‖ < ε` and `‖c • (g a g⁻¹) - p x‖ < ε` imply `x ∈ V`. The parameter `p` can be the inclusion of a fixed-algebra slice, with `K` a compact sphere on that slice.

The proof uses the characteristic polynomial of left multiplication. Its coefficients below the degree form a continuous conjugation-invariant map and vanish precisely on the nilpotent elements. On `(K \ V) × ω`, the coefficient comparison between `p x + e` and `c • a` is unequal at `(c,e) = (0,0)`. The tube lemma gives an open neighborhood of `(0,0)` in which the same inequality holds uniformly. Setting `e = c • (g a g⁻¹) - p x` contradicts conjugation invariance when both bounds hold. This implements the normalized nilpotent-neighborhood step for elements close to a conjugate with compactly bounded representatives. [Clozel, *Characters of non-connected, reductive p-adic groups*, §3.6 and the proof of Lemma 10, pp. 159–162](https://doi.org/10.4153/CJM-1987-008-3)
