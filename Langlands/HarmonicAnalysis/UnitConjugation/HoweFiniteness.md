# Finite-dimensional lattice restriction from decreasing test averages

Let `L` be a compact open additive subgroup of a topological ring `A`. Fix a set `B` of coset centers where the distributions vanish, a compact set `K` of centers, and a height `A → ℕ`. Assume that for each center `x` outside `B` and `K` there is a lattice-invariant test supported on points of strictly smaller height, whose value under every unit-conjugation-invariant distribution equals the distribution's value on the coset indicator at `x`.

`HoweFinitenessFromAveragesStatement` gives finite-dimensionality of the lattice restrictions of the invariant distributions with this vanishing. The image of `K` in the discrete additive quotient by `L` is finite. If all evaluations on its cosets are zero, induction on height proves zero evaluation on every coset. The induction uses the span of lattice-invariant tests supported on points of smaller height. The resulting restriction embeds into the finite-dimensional space of complex functions on the cosets meeting `K`.

The decreasing test identity is the consequence of the averaging condition used in the proof of Harish-Chandra's Theorem 11.1. The geometry that supplies these tests is a premise. [Harish-Chandra, *Admissible invariant distributions on reductive p-adic groups*, §11.1, p. 59]
