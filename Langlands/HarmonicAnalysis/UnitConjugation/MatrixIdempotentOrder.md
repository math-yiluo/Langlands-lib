# Integral idempotent generators of left ideals of matrix algebras

The module `Langlands.HarmonicAnalysis.UnitConjugation.MatrixIdempotentOrder` proves, for a
division ring `D` with a valuation `w : Valuation D Γ` (`Γ` a linearly ordered commutative group
with zero), that every left ideal of the matrix ring `M_n(D)` is generated by an idempotent matrix
whose entries have valuation at most `1`, and that idempotent generators of left ideals combine
over finite products of rings. The module is purely algebraic. All declarations are in the
namespace `Langlands.HarmonicAnalysis.UnitConjugation`. Row vectors are `Fin n → D`, a left
`D`-module, and matrices act on them on the right (`Matrix.vecMul v P = v P`).

## Integral Gaussian elimination

- `IsIntegralEchelon w U S b` (a structure) for a subspace `U : Submodule D (Fin n → D)`, a set
  `S` of coordinates and vectors `b : Fin n → Fin n → D`: for `p ∈ S`, `b p ∈ U`, every entry of
  `b p` has valuation at most `1`, and `b p q = 1` if `q = p` and `0` for the other `q ∈ S`.
- `IsIntegralEchelon.sum_apply_of_mem`: `(∑_{p ∈ S} v p • b p) q = v q` for `q ∈ S`.
- `IsIntegralEchelon.exists_succ` (the pivot step): if `u ∈ U` and
  `∑_{p ∈ S} u p • b p ≠ u`, there is echelon data with one more pivot. Proof: `u' = u -
  ∑_{p ∈ S} u p • b p` is a nonzero vector of `U` vanishing on `S`; choose a coordinate `j` where
  `w (u' j)` is maximal, so `j ∉ S`; the new vector is `(u' j)⁻¹ • u'`, with entries of
  valuation at most `1` and entry `1` at `j`, and each old `b p` is replaced by
  `b p - b p j • (u' j)⁻¹ • u'`, which keeps integrality (ultrametric inequality) and the pivot
  pattern.
- `exists_integralEchelon`: there is echelon data with `∑_{p ∈ S} v p • b p = v` for every
  `v ∈ U` (induction on `n - #S`; a set of pivots has at most `n` elements).
- `exists_integral_projection`: there is a matrix `P` with entries of valuation at most `1`
  such that `v P ∈ U` for every `v` and `v P = v` for `v ∈ U`: the matrix whose row `p` is `b p`
  for `p ∈ S` and `0` otherwise, for which `v P = ∑_{p ∈ S} v p • b p`.

## Left ideals of `M_n(D)`

- `leftIdealRows J`: for a left ideal `J` of `M_n(D)`, the subspace of row vectors `v` with
  `a ⊗ v = Matrix.vecMulVec a v ∈ J` for every column `a` (stability under `d •` uses the
  column `a d`).
- `row_mem_leftIdealRows`: the rows of matrices of `J` lie in `leftIdealRows J`, since
  `a ⊗ (M i) = (a ⊗ e_i) M`. `mem_of_rows_mem`: conversely a matrix whose rows lie there is in
  `J`, as `M = ∑_i e_i ⊗ (M i)`. `mul_row`: `(M P) i = (M i) P`.
- `exists_integral_idempotent_matrix`: for every left ideal `J` there is `P` with entries of
  valuation at most `1`, `P * P = P`, and `M ∈ J ↔ M * P = M` for every `M`. Proof: take the
  integral projection onto `leftIdealRows J`; a matrix lies in `J` exactly when its rows are
  fixed by `P`; the rows `e_i P` of `P` lie in `leftIdealRows J`, so `P ∈ J` and `P * P = P`.

## Finite products

`exists_idempotent_pi`: let `B i` (`i` in a finite type) be rings and `Q i` predicates on them.
If every left ideal of every `B i` has an idempotent generator `e` (`e * e = e` and
`z ∈ J ↔ z * e = z`) with `Q i e`, then every left ideal `J` of `∏ B i` has an idempotent
generator whose components satisfy the `Q i`. Proof: the components
`J_i = {z | Pi.single i z ∈ J}` are left ideals, `y ∈ J` exactly when `y i ∈ J_i` for all `i`
(`Pi.single i (y i) = Pi.single i 1 * y` and `y = ∑ Pi.single i (y i)`), and the generators of the
`J_i` form the generator of `J`.

## Source

André Weil, *Basic Number Theory*, second edition, Springer, 1973, source ID
`weil-1973-basic-number-theory`, Chapter II, §2, Theorem 1, printed p. 29 (PDF p. 46): a lattice
`L` in a left vector space over a `p`-field has a basis generating it over the integers and
adapted to a given flag of subspaces. For `L = R^n` (`R` the integer ring) and a flag through `U`,
the projection onto `U` along the other basis vectors is integral; the module obtains such an
integral projection directly by Gaussian elimination with pivots of maximal valuation, which
needs only the valuation and not a norm or a compactness argument.
