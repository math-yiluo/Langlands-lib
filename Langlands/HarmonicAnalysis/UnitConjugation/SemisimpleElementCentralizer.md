# Semisimple centralizers in finite-dimensional algebras

Let \(A\) be a finite-dimensional semisimple algebra over a perfect field
\(F\), and assume left multiplication by \(S\in A\) is semisimple as an
\(F\)-linear operator. `semisimpleElementCentralizer` proves that the
associative centralizer \(B=C_A(S)\) is semisimple and that
\(A=B\oplus [S,A]\). If \(S\) is noncentral, \(B\) is a proper subspace and
has strictly smaller \(F\)-dimension than \(A\).

`exists_semisimple_part` obtains a semisimple part \(S\) polynomial in
an arbitrary element \(X\) by Jordan–Chevalley decomposition in the faithful
left regular representation.
The squarefree minimal polynomial of left multiplication by \(S\) also
annihilates right multiplication by \(S\). The two operators commute, so
their difference \(\operatorname{ad}(S)\) is semisimple. Its kernel is
\(B\) and is complementary to its range.

Every \(x\in B\) has an inner inverse \(a\in A\) with \(xax=x\), since
its principal left ideal in \(A\) is generated by an idempotent. Write
\(a=y+z\) with \(y\in B\) and \(z\in[S,A]\). As \(x\) commutes with \(S\),
\(xzx\) belongs to \([S,A]\), while \(x-xyx\) belongs to \(B\).
Their equality forces both to vanish. Thus every element of \(B\) has an
inner inverse in \(B\). For \(x\) in the Jacobson radical of \(B\),
\(1-xy\) has a left inverse and \((1-xy)x=0\), so \(x=0\). Finite
dimensionality makes \(B\) Artinian, giving semisimplicity.

These algebraic properties underlie the smaller-dimensional centralizer
in Harish-Chandra's *Admissible invariant distributions on reductive
p-adic groups* (AMS, 1999), §3.3, printed p. 23. The proof here uses the
associative algebra structure of the Lie algebra of \(\mathrm{GL}_1(A)\)
and applies to split and nonsplit semisimple algebras.
