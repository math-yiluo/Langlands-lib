# Reduction of the Galois criterion to a strictly Henselian base

`Langlands.AbelianScheme.UnipotentInertiaStrictHenselianReductionStatement` is
the implication

```
StrictHenselianUnipotentInertiaSemiabelianStatement.{u} →
  NeronModelRamificationIndexOneBaseChangeStatement.{u} →
    CommutativeAlgebra.UnramifiedStrictHenselianDvrExtensionStatement.{u} →
      UnipotentInertiaSemiabelianReductionStatement.{u}
```

and `Langlands.AbelianScheme.unipotentInertiaStrictHenselianReduction` proves
it. The three premises are:

- `StrictHenselianUnipotentInertiaSemiabelianStatement`: the Galois criterion
  over a strictly Henselian discrete valuation ring `W` with fraction field `L`;
  unipotence of the finite étale fundamental group of `Spec L` on the rational
  `ℓ`-adic Tate module of an abelian variety over `L` gives semiabelian
  geometric special identity component of its Néron model;
- `NeronModelRamificationIndexOneBaseChangeStatement`: the base change of a
  Néron model along a local extension of discrete valuation rings of
  ramification index one is a Néron model of its generic fibre;
- `CommutativeAlgebra.UnramifiedStrictHenselianDvrExtensionStatement`: every
  discrete valuation ring `V` has a local homomorphism to a strictly Henselian
  discrete valuation ring `W` under which the maximal ideal of `V` generates
  that of `W`, with separable algebraic residue extension.

The modules of these three propositions state them and do not prove them.
The conclusion is the published
`Langlands.AbelianScheme.UnipotentInertiaSemiabelianReductionStatement`.

## Implemented proof

`injective_algebraMap_of_map_maximalIdeal` shows that a homomorphism
`V → W` of discrete valuation rings with
`(maximalIdeal V).map (algebraMap V W) = maximalIdeal W` is injective. The image
of a uniformizer `ϖ` of `V` is nonzero, since otherwise the maximal ideal of
`W`, generated by that image, would be zero. Every nonzero element of `V` is a
unit times a power of `ϖ` (`IsDiscreteValuationRing.eq_unit_mul_pow_irreducible`),
so its image in the domain `W` is nonzero.

In `unipotentInertiaStrictHenselianReduction`, take `V`, `K`, `B`, `N` and `ℓ` as
in the conclusion, with the hypothesis `hH` on all unramified strictly
Henselian extensions.

1. The third premise gives `W` with `hmax : (maximalIdeal V).map (algebraMap V W)
   = maximalIdeal W` and separable residue extension. Let `L = FractionRing W`.
   The composite `V → W → L` is injective, so `IsFractionRing.lift` extends it
   to `K → L`; this is the `K`-algebra structure on `L`, and
   `IsFractionRing.lift_algebraMap` gives the commutativity of the square
   `V → W → L`, `V → K → L`.
2. Let `G` be the base change of `N.group` along `Spec W → Spec V`. The second
   premise, applied with `hmax` and the separability of the residue extension,
   gives `NeronModelPropertyBundle W L G.X.hom`. The commutative square gives
   `Spec L → Spec K → Spec V = Spec L → Spec W → Spec V`. With the pullback
   comparisons `GeometricSemiabelianDescent.commBaseChangeCompIso` and
   `GeometricSemiabelianDescent.commBaseChangeEqIso`, the base change to `L` of
   the generic identification `N.genericIso` becomes an isomorphism from the
   base change of `B` to `L` onto the generic fibre of `G`. These data form a
   Néron model `N'` of `(baseChange (Spec.algebraMap K L)).obj B` over `W`.
3. `ℓ` is invertible in `W` as the image of a unit of `V`. The hypothesis `hH`
   at `W`, `hmax`, `L` and the commutative square is exactly the Tate-module
   hypothesis of the first premise for the abelian variety
   `(baseChange (Spec.algebraMap K L)).obj B` over `L`. The first premise
   therefore gives `N'.HasGeometricSemiabelianReduction`.
4. A separable field extension is algebraic, so
   `NeronModel.hasGeometricSemiabelianReduction_of_baseChange` (module
   `Langlands/AbelianScheme/GeometricSemiabelianReductionDescent.lean`), applied
   with the identity isomorphism `N'.group ≅ G`, gives
   `N.HasGeometricSemiabelianReduction`.

## Relation to the sources

Grothendieck, SGA 7 I, Exposé IX, 3.4.0, permits testing semistable reduction
of `A_K` over a trait after passage to the strict henselization, because the
formation of the Néron model commutes with this base change. The proof of the
Galois criterion, Proposition 3.5, makes only the reduction to a Henselian
trait and works with its inertia group `I`; Corollaire 3.8 adds elementwise
unipotence of the action of `I` to the equivalent conditions of 3.5. The
specialization to a strictly Henselian base is the choice made in this formal
reduction: there the finite étale fundamental group of the fraction field is
the whole inertia group (Bosch, Lütkebohmert and Raynaud, §7.4, printed
p. 183, take `I = Gal(K_s/K^sh)`), so the premise
`StrictHenselianUnipotentInertiaSemiabelianStatement` can ask its hypothesis of
that fundamental group. The compatibility of Néron models with extensions of ramification index
one is Bosch, Lütkebohmert and Raynaud, *Néron Models* (1990), §7.2, Theorem 1
(ii) and Corollary 2, printed pp. 176–177. The strict henselization of a
discrete valuation ring is a discrete valuation ring with maximal ideal
generated by that of the base and residue field a separable closure of the
residue field of the base (The Stacks Project, Tags 04GP and 0AP3). The proof
does not use the strict henselization itself, only an extension with these
properties, and it applies the hypothesis of
`UnipotentInertiaSemiabelianReductionStatement` at that single extension.
