# The dimension bound for `H¹(A, 𝒪_A)` of an abelian scheme over a field

Author: Worker 281.

`Langlands.AbelianScheme.FieldStructureSheafH1DimensionBoundStatement.{u}` is a
`Prop`-valued definition. This module states the bound and supplies no proof.
A separate exact-target proof of this proposition is still required; it is a
successor task, to be published after this statement is accepted.

## Exact interface

The only external parameter is the universe `u`. The proposition quantifies,
in this order, over:

- a type `k : Type u` with `[Field k]`. The field is arbitrary: it is not
  assumed perfect or algebraically closed, and no characteristic is fixed;
- an abelian scheme
  `A : AbelianScheme (Scheme.Spec.obj (Opposite.op (CommRingCat.of k)))` over
  the actual affine scheme `Spec k`. This is the Library bundle of
  `Langlands.AbelianScheme.Basic`: a commutative group object `A.X` in
  `Over (Spec k)`, whose structure morphism `A.X.hom : A.X.left ⟶ Spec k` is
  `Smooth`, `IsProper` and `GeometricallyConnected` (Mathlib's properties of
  scheme morphisms);
- a natural number `g`, with the single hypothesis `A.HasRelativeDimension g`.
  By definition this is Mathlib's `SmoothOfRelativeDimension g A.X.hom`: every
  point of `A.X.left` has affine open neighbourhoods upstairs and downstairs on
  which the ring map is standard smooth of relative dimension `g`.

It then sets

```text
H := (DerivedSections.relativeCohomology A.X.hom 1).obj
       (Langlands.Scheme.Modules.structureSheaf A.X.left)
```

and concludes `FiniteDimensional k H ∧ Module.finrank k H ≤ g`.

- `Langlands.Scheme.Modules.structureSheaf A.X.left` is the structure sheaf of
  `A.X.left` as a module over itself, `SheafOfModules.unit A.X.left.ringCatSheaf`
  (module `Langlands.AlgebraicGeometry.Modules.Tensor`).
- `Langlands.AlgebraicGeometry.Modules.DerivedSections.relativeCohomology A.X.hom 1`
  is the Library's degree-one cohomology of actual `𝒪_A`-modules as modules over
  `CommRingCat.of k`, whose underlying ring is `k` (module
  `Langlands.AlgebraicGeometry.Modules.DerivedSections`). It is Mathlib's
  right-derived functor of the `k`-module global sections
  `Scheme.Modules.pushforward A.X.hom ⋙ moduleSpecΓFunctor`, computed from
  chosen injective resolutions in `A.X.left.Modules`. A scalar `c : k` acts on
  sections through its image in `Γ(A.X.left, ⊤)` under the structure morphism.
  No vector space is supplied in its place.
- `FiniteDimensional` and `Module.finrank` are Mathlib's (imported from
  `Mathlib.LinearAlgebra.FiniteDimensional.Defs`), for this `k`-module
  structure.

So the proposition says: `H¹(A, 𝒪_A)` is a finite-dimensional `k`-vector
space and `dim_k H¹(A, 𝒪_A) ≤ g`. It holds for every `g` satisfying the
hypothesis. Both conclusions are stated, because Mathlib's `Module.finrank` is
`0` on an infinite-dimensional module, so the inequality alone would not give
finiteness.

The statement does not assume the bound, perfectness, algebraic closure,
projectivity, a polarization or a line bundle, and it does not assume that
any Picard functor is representable.

## Original source

B. Edixhoven, G. van der Geer and B. Moonen, *Abelian Varieties*, preliminary
version of the first chapters (literature ID
`edixhoven-vandergeer-moonen-abelian-varieties-draft`, SHA-256
`81e88e484e1e830286ffed93d4636c80e50a1c341ffe19cc198bd856be713b42`; printed
page = PDF page − 4). Chapter 6, §2 "Digression on graded bialgebras"
(printed pp.91–95, PDF pp.95–99):

- Proposition (6.15), printed p.94 (PDF p.98): "Let X be a group variety over
  a field k. Then H•(X, O_X) has a natural structure of a graded k-bialgebra.
  We have dim_k(H¹(X, O_X)) ⩽ dim(X)."
- Paragraph (6.14), printed p.94 (PDF p.98), gives the argument:
  cup product makes `H•(X, O_X) = ⊕_{n=0}^{g} Hⁿ(X, O_X)` a connected
  graded-commutative `k`-algebra. Through the Künneth isomorphism
  `H•(X ×_k X, O) ≅ H•(X, O_X) ⊗_k H•(X, O_X)`, the group law gives a
  comultiplication, and the counit is induced by the unit section. Corollary
  (6.13) then gives "the estimate dim_k(H¹(X, O_X)) ⩽ g for a g-dimensional
  group variety X over a field k". The paragraph adds: "Note that
  dim_k(H¹(X, O_X)) does not change if we pass from k to an algebraic closure;
  we therefore need not assume k to be perfect."
- Corollary (6.13), printed p.94 (PDF p.98): for `H•` as in the Borel–Hopf
  theorem (6.12), with `Hⁿ = 0` for all `n > g`, `dim_k(H¹) ⩽ g`.
- Theorem (6.12) (Borel–Hopf structure theorem), printed pp.93–94
  (PDF pp.97–98): a connected, graded-commutative bialgebra of finite type over
  a perfect field is a tensor product of bialgebras each generated by one
  element. Definition (6.10) and the conventions before it (printed p.92,
  PDF p.96) define graded bialgebras and "of finite type" (`dim_k Hⁿ < ∞` for
  all `n`).

The Lean proposition is the degree-one dimension bound of (6.15), specialized
from group varieties to abelian varieties and with `dim(X)` given as the
specified relative dimension `g`. Its finite-dimensionality conclusion is
taken from the coherent finiteness theorem cited below. The first assertion of
(6.15), the bialgebra structure, is not part of this statement. The source
proves equality `dim_k H¹(X, O_X) = g` for abelian varieties in (6.18), which
is not stated here either.

### Why this is the abelian-variety case of (6.15)

The following comparison is explanation; it is not part of the Lean statement
and it is not used by it.

- EvdGM's conventions: a variety over `k` is a separated `k`-scheme of finite
  type that is geometrically integral ((0.4), printed p.1, PDF p.5). A group
  variety is a variety with a group law, inverse and rational identity
  ((1.2), printed p.6, PDF p.10). An abelian variety is a complete group
  variety ((1.3), same page). By Examples (3.8), item 6 (printed p.32,
  PDF p.36), a group variety is the same as a geometrically integral group
  scheme over `k`.
- A Library abelian scheme over `Spec k` satisfies these conditions. Its
  structure morphism is proper, hence separated and of finite type, and a
  proper variety is complete. It is smooth and geometrically connected, hence
  geometrically integral: for every field extension `K ⊇ k` the base change
  `A_K` is smooth over `K` (Stacks tag 01VB), hence regular (056S) and normal
  (0569); it is connected, and a connected locally Noetherian normal scheme is
  integral (033O). These tags are in the registered source
  `stacks-smooth-connected-integral-2026-10-24`. So `A.X.left` with its group
  law is an abelian variety in EvdGM's sense, and in particular a group
  variety, to which (6.15) applies. Its group law is moreover commutative,
  which the Library bundle records explicitly.
- `dim(X) = g`. Locally, `A.X.hom` is given by ring maps that are standard
  smooth of relative dimension `g`, so the module of differentials
  `Ω_{A/k}` is locally free of rank `g`. By The Stacks Project, Lemma 29.35.12
  (tag 02G1), `rank_x(Ω_{X/S}) = dim_x(X_{f(x)})` for a smooth `f : X → S`
  (registered source `stacks-smooth-differential-rank-2026-10-22`, snapshot
  `01V4.html`, SHA-256
  `4327b0dc32a5b7a4fc554e6587f0d622d978e306bba4d2783a8aade3704aa3da`). Over a
  field the fibre is `X` itself, so every point of `A.X.left` has local
  dimension `g`; since `A.X.left` is irreducible, `dim(A.X.left) = g`. Thus `g`
  is a specified geometric relative dimension of the smooth structure
  morphism, and it plays the role of `dim(X)` in (6.15). A smooth morphism
  with connected source has some constant relative dimension, but the Lean
  statement does not assert this: `g` and the hypothesis are given.

### Finite dimensionality

Theorem (6.12) assumes `H•` of finite type, and (6.14) applies it to
`H•(X, O_X)` without comment. For a proper scheme over a field this is the
coherent finiteness theorem: The Stacks Project, Cohomology of Schemes,
Section 30.19 (tag 02O3), Lemma 30.19.2 (tag 02O6): "Let S = Spec(A) with A a
Noetherian ring. Let f : X → S be a proper morphism. Let F be a coherent
O_X-module. Then Hⁱ(X, F) is a finite A-module for all i ⩾ 0." (EGA III,
Théorème 3.2.1, is the cited reference.) Registered source
`stacks-very-ampleness-support-2026-10-29`, retained official HTML snapshot
`02O3.html` of 2026-10-29 (SHA-256
`ce0b5617576dbec3ff067a98e5f6b4316a4bdc98264f9efe4e7a2af3526bfcf4`). Applied
with `A = k`, `f = A.X.hom`, `F = 𝒪_A` and `i = 1`, it gives the
finite-dimensionality conclusion. The page's two comments (#2712, #2750) only
add the EGA reference and change no statement.

### Cohomology conventions

EvdGM writes `Hⁿ(X, O_X)` for ordinary sheaf cohomology. The Library's `H` is
the right-derived functor of global sections on `𝒪_X`-modules, with
`k`-module structure through the structure morphism. That cohomology of an
`𝒪_X`-module computed in `𝒪_X`-modules agrees with its cohomology as an
abelian sheaf is The Stacks Project, Cohomology of Sheaves, tag 01F1 (label
`lemma-modules-abelian`, `cohomology.tex` line 2175 at commit
`a04446e57ec1fbc252a871afcec7752fb2807b14`, registered source
`stacks-flasque-affine-vanishing-kunneth-a04446e5`, file SHA-256
`fa9747d0082afbf8b09244dcc6c260f8a122e5e3b4a3c69e2273506d76e18a42`). The
Library proves no such comparison, and this statement needs none: it is
formulated directly with the Library's cohomology.

## Statement, not proof

This module proves nothing. The source's argument, which a proof must
formalize or replace, uses: the cup product on `H•(X, O_X)`; the Künneth
formula over a field; the group law and unit section; vanishing of `Hⁿ` above
the dimension; the Borel–Hopf theorem (6.12) over a perfect field; invariance
of `dim_k H¹(X, O_X)` under extension to an algebraic closure, to remove
perfectness; and the coherent finiteness theorem above. None of these is
proved or assumed here.

## Dependencies

Library modules `Langlands.AbelianScheme.Basic` (the bundle and

(`relativeCohomology`) and `Langlands.AlgebraicGeometry.Modules.Tensor`

The statement involves no Picard functor, line-bundle vanishing or smoothness
result.

## Intended use

An upper bound for `dim_k H¹(A, 𝒪_A)` by the relative dimension, for arguments
that compare a tangent space with `H¹(A, 𝒪_A)` and need its dimension to be at
most `g`, such as proving that the identity component of the Picard scheme of
an abelian variety is reduced.
