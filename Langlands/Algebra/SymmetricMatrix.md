# Canonical finite monomial coordinates for symmetric matrix powers

For any commutative ring `R`, finite coordinate type `n`, and natural degree
`d`, `Index n d` is the finite type of finitely supported nonnegative exponent
vectors of total degree `d`. `basis R n d` is the monomial basis of Mathlib's
actual degree-`d` homogeneous-polynomial submodule. Its coordinate at an index
is the corresponding polynomial coefficient. The constructions include degree
zero, empty coordinate types, the zero ring, and rings with nilpotents.

The polynomial generators represent the original free module's basis vectors.
For `M : Matrix n n R`, `substitute M` sends generator `X j` to
`sum_i C (M i j) * X i`. This column convention gives the covariant symmetric
action on the free module generated by the variables. It preserves each
homogeneous component by homogeneity of these linear forms. `action M` is
its restriction and `matrix M d` is its matrix in the canonical monomial basis.
`matrix_mulVec` identifies this matrix action with the substitution itself.

Substitution by the identity matrix is the identity. Expanding the linear
forms shows `substitute (M * N) = (substitute M).comp (substitute N)`;
the finite symmetric matrices consequently preserve multiplication.
`hom R n d` packages the result as a multiplicative map of matrix monoids,
and `glHom R n d` takes invertible matrices to invertible symmetric matrices.

Induction on multivariate polynomials proves that substitution commutes with
an arbitrary coefficient-ring homomorphism. Reading coefficients gives
`matrix_map` and `glHom_map`, their entrywise coefficient-change laws. These
hold over arbitrary rings without a reducedness or field assumption.

This is an internal finite-coordinate implementation of the induced action
on the symmetric algebra used by the source. Mathlib's actual homogeneous
polynomial modules and canonical support-restricted monomial bases supply
its underlying modules. The companion group-scheme module algebraizes the
natural family. This module alone asserts no semisimplicity or classification.

Source: M. Demazure and P. Gabriel, *Groupes algebriques*, Tome I (1970), IV, section 3, proof of Theorem 3.6, printed p.510 (PDF p.540). The index, monomial basis and homogeneous action use `Langlands.LinearAlgebra.HomogeneousPolynomial`.
`matrix` forms `LinearMap.toMatrix` in that basis. It takes a `Fintype` on the original coordinates
and uses the chosen decidable equality on the monomial index. `glHom` lifts this matrix action to units.
