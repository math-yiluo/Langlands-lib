## The actual objects

All names below are in `Langlands.Renard.NilpotentFiltration`. The parameters
are an arbitrary `V : Type u` with `AddCommGroup V` and `Module ℂ V`, and a
tuple `a : Fin l → Module.End ℂ V` for any natural `l`. The definitions and
intrinsic laws need neither finite dimension nor nilpotence. Applying them
to the source's commuting nilpotent tuple in finite dimension gives precisely
the source objects. This wider definition domain is an internal formal choice.

- `generatedAlgebra a` is exactly `Algebra.adjoin ℂ (Set.range a)`, as a
  subalgebra of the original `Module.End ℂ V`. Its inherited action on `V`
  evaluates these original endomorphisms. The identity and scalars belong to
  the unital adjoin without adding a counted generator.
- `generator a i` is the actual endomorphism `a i` as an element of this
  algebra, using its membership in the adjoin.
- `generatorIdeal a` is `Ideal.span (Set.range (generator a))` inside that
  actual algebra. Mathlib's `Ideal` is a left ideal for general rings.
  For commuting tuples the algebra is commutative, by the provided
  `generatedAlgebra_isMulCommutative` theorem, so this is also the usual
  two-sided ideal generated by the source tuple. Commutation is concluded
  from `∀ i j, Commute (a i) (a j)`, with no supplied algebra-commutativity
  premise. The construction never takes the ideal in the full ambient
  endomorphism ring.
- `idealPowerModule a n` is `(generatorIdeal a)^n • ⊤`, an actual submodule
  of `V` over `generatedAlgebra a`.
- `idealPowerSubspace a n` restricts this submodule's scalars to `ℂ`. It is
  the source's complex vector subspace `V_n = I^n V`.

Zero-dimensional spaces and empty tuples are allowed. In a general ring the
left-ideal power and module product here are Mathlib's actual span-based
operations. Under the source commutation hypothesis they agree with the
usual ideal powers and their action on `V`; no surrogate sequence is supplied.

## Intrinsic laws

`generator_val` records that inclusion gives the original endomorphism.
`generator_mem_ideal` inserts each actual generator into its ideal.
`idealPowerModule_zero` and `idealPowerSubspace_zero` give `V_0 = V`, using
the zeroth ideal power and the action of the unit ideal.
`idealPowerModule_succ` expresses `I^(n+1) V = I (I^n V)` via the actual
ideal-power and scalar-action laws. The proof separates `n = 0`, using the
action of the unit ideal on `V`, from positive powers, where Mathlib's
left-ideal successor law and associativity of the action apply. It therefore
does not require commutation. The two `antitone` theorems prove that
the power-action modules and their complex subspaces descend, by inclusion
of ideal powers and monotonicity of the module product.
`idealPowerSubspace_stable` uses the `R`-submodule structure to show each
level is stable under every element of the actual generated algebra.
`generator_apply_mem_one` puts every `a i v` in `V_1`, by generator ideal
membership and the definition of its action on all of `V`.

These are internal construction laws implementing the source notation.
They do not prove the source's substantive nilpotence assertion
`V_(dim V) = 0`. That assertion, generation by a complement, quotient
surjectivity, restriction injection and dimension estimates, algebra splitting,
carrier transport, maximum monotonicity and the floor recurrence require
separate statements and proofs. No such assertion is a hypothesis of these
constructions. This module neither constructs a maximizing tuple nor changes
the already published maximum definition.
