# Renard C.I: vanishing at the ambient dimension

This module proves the published closed proposition
`Langlands.Renard.NilpotentFiltrationVanishingStatement.{u}` with the theorem
`Langlands.Renard.nilpotentFiltrationVanishing`. It imports the published
statement and its actual ideal-power construction unchanged.

For every finite-dimensional complex vector space `V : Type u`, every natural
`l`, and every pairwise commuting tuple `a : Fin l → Module.End ℂ V` whose
entries are nilpotent, the conclusion is exactly

`NilpotentFiltration.idealPowerSubspace a (Module.finrank ℂ V) = ⊥`.

The exponent is the ambient complex dimension, independent of the number of
generators. All mathematical assumptions occur inside the published target.

## Actual algebra, ideal and action

The proof uses the imported objects in
`Langlands.Renard.NilpotentFiltration` throughout:

- `generatedAlgebra a` is the actual unital subalgebra
  `Algebra.adjoin ℂ (Set.range a)` of `Module.End ℂ V`.
- `generator a i` is the original `a i`, regarded as an element of that algebra.
- `generatorIdeal a` is the ideal spanned by precisely these generators.
- `idealPowerModule a n` is `(generatorIdeal a)^n • ⊤` for the original
  evaluation action on `V`.
- `idealPowerSubspace a n` restricts that same submodule's scalars to `ℂ`.

The imported commutation lemma makes the actual generated algebra commutative.
Its inherited multiplication and action remain the original ones. The unital
adjoin includes identity and scalars, while the ideal is generated by the listed

## Implemented argument

`NilpotentFiltration.idealPowerSubspace_eq_bot_of_eq_succ` proves that equality
of two consecutive complex subspaces forces that level to be zero. First,
nilpotence of each `a i` lifts to `generator a i` through the injective algebra
inclusion, using `IsNilpotent.map_iff`. Their power-zero witnesses put the
generators in the radical of the zero ideal. `Ideal.span_le` therefore puts
the actual generator ideal in that radical, and
`Ideal.radical_le_jacobson` puts it in the Jacobson radical.

Every filtration level is finite-dimensional over `ℂ`, hence finitely
generated over `ℂ`. `Submodule.FG.of_restrictScalars` makes it finitely
generated over the actual generated algebra. Equality of consecutive complex
subspaces is equality of the underlying subsets of the original module.
Together with the imported successor law, it gives `N ≤ I • N` for the actual
level `N`. Applying
`Submodule.eq_bot_of_le_smul_of_le_jacobson_bot` gives `N = ⊥`; restricting
scalars gives the required complex-subspace equality. The ideal-radical containment and finite generation used by Nakayama are
proved from the original hypotheses.

`NilpotentFiltration.idealPowerSubspace_finrank_le` then proves for every
natural `n` that

`Module.finrank ℂ (idealPowerSubspace a n) ≤ Module.finrank ℂ V - n`,

where subtraction is natural-number subtraction. At `n = 0`, the imported
zeroth-level law gives all of `V`. At a successor, a zero next level has
dimension zero. Otherwise antitonicity gives inclusion in the preceding level.
If its dimension did not strictly decrease,
`Submodule.eq_of_le_of_finrank_le` would give equality of those subspaces.
The stabilization lemma would make the next level zero, a contradiction.
Natural-number arithmetic combines the strict drop with the induction bound.

The closing theorem specializes this estimate to `n = Module.finrank ℂ V`.
The level has dimension zero and is therefore bottom by
`Submodule.finrank_eq_zero`.

## Boundary cases and scope

The carrier universe `u` is arbitrary. The proof applies uniformly to a zero
vector space, an empty tuple, and zero, repeated or redundant entries. On a
zero space the endpoint index is zero, and the zeroth level is both top and
bottom. For an empty tuple the span defining the generator ideal is bottom;
the same argument applies without a positive-rank or nonempty-index premise.
No choice of basis or triangularization is needed.
