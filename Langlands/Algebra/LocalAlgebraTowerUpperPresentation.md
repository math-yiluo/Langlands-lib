# A polynomial presentation of the upper ring over the middle ring

This module supplies the presentation of `T` over `S` used to construct the upper stages
in `Langlands.Algebra.LocalAlgebraTowerConstruction`. All declarations are in the namespace
`Langlands.Algebra.LocalAlgebraTowerApproximation`. They use the presentation structure
`LocalAlgebraApproximationExistence.Pres` from `Langlands.Algebra.LocalAlgebraApproximationExistence`.

## Setting

`R`, `S`, `T` are commutative rings with algebra structures `R → S`, `S → T`, `R → T` and
`IsScalarTower R S T`. No locality is assumed in this module.

Recall the fields of `P : Pres R₀ R S e`. Here `e : R₀ ≃+* R`, and in this module `e` is
always `RingEquiv.refl`:

- `n` is the number of variables and `F` is a finite set of relations in
  `MvPolynomial (Fin n) R₀`;
- `ρ : MvPolynomial (Fin n) R₀ →+* S` satisfies `ρ (C r) = algebraMap R S (e r)`;
- `ρ` kills every element of `F`;
- (`surj`) for every `s` there are `a, t` with `ρ t` a unit and `s * ρ t = ρ a`;
- (`ker`) if `ρ a = 0`, there is `t` with `ρ t` a unit and `t * a` in the ideal generated by
  `F`.

## Results

`nonempty_pres_upper PS PT`. Let `PS : Pres R R S (RingEquiv.refl R)` and
`PT : Pres R R T (RingEquiv.refl R)` be presentations of `S` and `T` over `R`. Then
`Pres S S T (RingEquiv.refl S)` is nonempty: `T` has a presentation over `S` in the same
sense.

`nonempty_pres_upper_of_essFinitePresentation hS hT`. If
`EssFinitePresentation R S` and `EssFinitePresentation R T`, then
`Pres S S T (RingEquiv.refl S)` is nonempty. The proof gets `PS` and `PT` from
`LocalAlgebraApproximationExistence.nonempty_pres` and then applies `nonempty_pres_upper`.

These results do not prove `EssFinitePresentation S T`. That is the tower cancellation
statement `EssFinitePresentationTowerCancellationStatement`, which is still unproved in the
Library and is not used here. The tower construction needs only the `Pres` form, which is
the lemma proved here.

## Proof of `nonempty_pres_upper`

Write `φ = map (algebraMap R S)`, sending `MvPolynomial (Fin PT.n) R` to
`MvPolynomial (Fin PT.n) S`. Let `σ` be the evaluation map from `MvPolynomial (Fin PT.n) S`
to `T` that is `algebraMap S T` on coefficients and sends `X k` to `PT.ρ (X k)`. Both
`σ ∘ φ` and `PT.ρ` are ring maps that agree on constants and variables. The constants agree
by `PT.ρ_C` and the scalar tower. So `σ (φ f) = PT.ρ f` for every `f`.

For each generator `x_k = PS.ρ (X k)` of `S`, apply `PT.surj` to its image in `T`. This
gives polynomials `A k` and `B k` over `R` such that `PT.ρ (B k)` is a unit and
`x_k * PT.ρ (B k) = PT.ρ (A k)` in `T`.

The new presentation has these data:

- the variables are those of `PT`;
- the map is `σ`;
- the relations are `G = φ(PT.F) ∪ {C x_k * φ (B k) - φ (A k)}`.

So the relations are those of `T` with coefficients pushed to `S`, plus one relation for each
generator of `S`.

- `ρ_C` holds by the definition of `σ`.
- `ρ_F`: `σ` kills `φ(PT.F)` because `σ ∘ φ = PT.ρ`. It kills each relation of the second
  kind by the choice of `A k` and `B k`.
- `surj` follows from `PT.surj` after applying `φ`.

For `ker`, let `J` be the ideal generated by `G` and let `π` be the quotient map onto
`MvPolynomial (Fin PT.n) S ⧸ J`. Let `Fr` be the set of quotient elements `q` with
`π (φ c) * q = π (φ a)` for some polynomials `a, c` over `R` such that `PT.ρ c` is a unit. The
proof shows that `Fr` is a subring and that it has these properties:

- it contains every `π (φ a)`;
- it is closed under inverses that exist in the quotient. If `q ∈ Fr` and `q * q' = 1`, then
  the image of `q` in `T` is a unit, so `PT.ρ a` is a unit and `q' ∈ Fr`;
- it contains `π (C x_k)`, by the relation `C x_k * φ (B k) - φ (A k)`.

By induction on polynomials over `R`, `Fr` therefore contains `π (C (PS.ρ u))` for every
`u`. By `PS.surj`, every `s ∈ S` has the form `PS.ρ u * w`, where `w` is the inverse of a unit
`PS.ρ v`. So `π (C s) ∈ Fr` for every `s`. By a second induction, `Fr` is the whole quotient.

Now suppose `σ f = 0`. Write `π (φ c) * π f = π (φ a)` with `PT.ρ c` a unit. Applying `σ`
gives `PT.ρ a = 0`. Then `PT.ker` gives `d` such that `PT.ρ d` is a unit and `d * a` lies in
the ideal generated by `PT.F`. Mapping by `φ` puts `φ (d * a)` in `J`. Hence
`π (φ (d * c) * f) = π (φ (d * a)) = 0`, and `σ (φ (d * c)) = PT.ρ (d * c)` is a unit. This
proves the `ker` field with the multiplier `φ (d * c)`.
