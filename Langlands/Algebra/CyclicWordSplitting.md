# Coefficient-controlled splitting of cyclic word differences

The polynomials here are finite linear combinations of words in two letters. `cyclicTrace` sends each word to its cyclic rotation class, adding coefficients within each class. A product and its reversed product have equal cyclic trace, since moving the letters of the first word to the end one by one exchanges the two blocks.

`CyclicWordSplittingStatement` asserts that a homogeneous polynomial of degree `n + 1` with cyclic trace zero is the sum of two commutators, one with each letter. Over a field with a nontrivial ultrametric norm, the two degree `n` polynomials satisfy the same coefficient bound as the input. To prove this, choose a representative of each rotation class. Moving a single letter changes the polynomial by a commutator of that letter with the remaining word. A finite chain of moves builds the splitting for the difference between a word and its representative. All coefficients in the resulting degree `n` polynomials have norm at most one by the ultrametric inequality. Sum these splittings with the coefficients of the given polynomial. The representative terms cancel by the zero cyclic trace condition, and the bound follows from the same inequality.

The splitting implements the homogeneous commutator step used when straightening exponential products. Using cyclic trace gives an associative word-algebra version of the step with the same coefficient control. [Boyarchenko and Sabitova, *The orbit method for profinite groups and a p-adic analogue of Brown's theorem*, Lemma 2.3](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0608126v1)
