# Proof of the sandwich dimension bound

`Langlands.sandwichSimpleModuleDimensionBound.{uH,uM}` proves the published
`Langlands.SandwichSimpleModuleDimensionBoundStatement.{uH,uM}` without
changing its quantifiers or adding hypotheses. The algebra and module have
independent universes. Pairwise commuting generators and the sandwich-span
hypothesis yield `Module.finrank ℂ M ≤ q^(2^l)` for every compatible simple
finite-dimensional complex module. The source statement and its imports
are unchanged.

All algebraic helpers use a field `K`, with algebraic closedness for Schur
and Burnside, and independent universes for `K`, the algebra `H`, and its
simple finite-dimensional module `M`. The final theorem specializes `K`
to the complex numbers.

Schur's scalar-endomorphism assertion follows by taking an eigenvalue of
an endomorphism on the finite-dimensional vector space. Subtracting its
scalar multiple of the identity gives a noninjective module endomorphism;
Mathlib's simple-module Schur lemma forces it to be zero.

For Burnside surjectivity, every linear endomorphism commutes with those
scalar module endomorphisms, hence is linear over the endomorphism ring.
Jacobson density realizes it on the finite basis by one algebra element.
Equality on the basis gives equality everywhere.

`finrank_le_sandwich` chooses a finite basis of the middle subalgebra `C`
and sandwiches each basis element between the two indexed elements. The
resulting family has `q * q * finrank K C` members. Expanding each middle
element in this basis shows that this family spans the ambient algebra;
Mathlib's spanning-family cardinality bound gives the dimension inequality.

`sandwich_span_map` transports the spanning hypothesis along a surjective
algebra homomorphism. `actionAlgHom` packages the module action as an algebra
homomorphism, using the scalar-tower compatibility. Applying the transport
and count to the Burnside-surjective action gives `dimension_square_le`:
`m^2 ≤ q^2 D`, where `m` is the module dimension and `D` is the dimension
of the image of `C`.

`nat_dimension_bound` raises that inequality to `2^l` and combines it with
`D^(2^l) ≤ m^(2^(l+1)-2)`. Positivity of `m` permits cancellation of the
common factor `m^(2^(l+1)-2)`. Comparing squares yields `m ≤ q^(2^l)`.
The argument includes `l = 0`; it requires no separate positivity of `q`.

The assembly helper transports pairwise commutativity through the action
and identifies the image of the generated subalgebra with the subalgebra
generated by the image elements. Simplicity and finite dimension give
positive `m`. It then applies the numerical helper.

The final theorem supplies the general commuting estimate from
`Langlands.CommutingDimension.commuting_dimension_nat`; it is proved in the
included helper modules, not assumed. The helper chain is
`CommutingDimensionHelpers`, `CommutingDimensionNumerics`,
`CommutingDimensionRecurrence`, `CommutingDimensionNilpotent`, and
`CommutingDimensionPrimary`. Their paired Markdown explains the algebraic
inequalities, Nakayama step, double induction, and simultaneous generalized
eigenspace reduction. The nilpotent induction holds over any field; the
primary decomposition uses algebraic closedness.
