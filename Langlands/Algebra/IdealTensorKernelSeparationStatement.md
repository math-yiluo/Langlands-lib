# Mixed-scalar separation of ideal tensor kernels

`Langlands.Algebra.IdealTensorKernelSeparationStatement.{u,v,w}` defines a
proposition. Let \(R\) and \(S\) be commutative Noetherian rings, let \(S\)
be an \(R\)-algebra, and let \(M\) be a finite \(S\)-module with compatible
\(R\)- and \(S\)-module structures. Let \(I\) be an ideal of \(R\), and set
\(J=IS\), the ideal generated by its image in \(S\). Assume
\(J\subseteq\operatorname{Jac}(S)\) and, for every ideal \(a\subseteq R\)
and every \(n\in\mathbb N\), assume
\[
\ker\bigl(\mathrm{id}_M\otimes\iota_a:
           M\otimes_R a\longrightarrow M\otimes_R R\bigr)
 \ \subseteq\ I^n(M\otimes_R a),
\]
where \(\iota_a:a\hookrightarrow R\) is the ideal inclusion.
Then \(M\) is flat over \(R\). This module states this implication and
contains no proof of it.

The full proposition quantifies over `R : Type u`, `S : Type v`,
`M : Type w`, `[CommRing R]`, `[CommRing S]`,
`[IsNoetherianRing R]`, `[IsNoetherianRing S]`, `[Algebra R S]`,
`[AddCommGroup M]`, `[Module R M]`, `[Module S M]`,
`[IsScalarTower R S M]`, `[Module.Finite S M]`, and `(I : Ideal R)`,
with three independent universes. Its two premises are the displayed
Jacobson condition and the bound for all `a` and all `n : ℕ`.
`I.map (algebraMap R S)` is \(J\), and
`(⊥ : Ideal S).jacobson` is \(\operatorname{Jac}(S)\).
`LinearMap.lTensor M a.subtype` is \(\mathrm{id}_M\otimes\iota_a\).
The power submodule `I ^ n • ⊤` is taken over \(R\).

Finiteness is assumed over \(S\). There is no assumption that \(M\) is
finite over \(R\), that either ring is local, that the map is local, that
\(M\) is nonzero, or that \(S\) has any algebra finiteness property.
The explicit power bounds are a premise to be established separately
in applications.

This is a derived separatedness step from the proof of Stacks Project
Lemma 10.99.7, tag [00MK](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/00MK),
rather than its verbatim statement. That proof first bounds each ideal
tensor kernel by arbitrarily deep powers using the intersection/sum
diagram and Artin–Rees. Its final paragraph uses finiteness of the tensor
product over \(S\) and separation to make the kernel zero. Here the
power bounds are assumed directly, and the Jacobson condition replaces
the local hypotheses needed for this last step. Tensor symmetry and
the unit isomorphism \(M\otimes_R R\simeq M\) explain the chosen tensor
orientation and codomain.

The intended proof can obtain finiteness of \(M\otimes_R a\) over \(S\)
from finite generators of \(a\) over the Noetherian ring \(R\) and of
\(M\) over \(S\). Scalar compatibility takes \(I^n(M\otimes_R a)\) into
\(J^n(M\otimes_R a)\). Krull intersection over \(S\) then kills each
kernel, and the ideal tensor-injectivity criterion gives flatness.
This explains the formulation; no formal proof of these steps is
included here.

[00MJ](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/00MJ), provides the
finite-length Tor-vanishing input earlier in the original local
argument. That input and the derivation of the power bounds are outside
this proposition. No categorical Tor comparison is asserted.
