## Implemented proof

For a tuple `a : Fin l → Module.End ℂ V` and another finite-dimensional complex
space `U`, consider the tuple `b i = (a i, 0)` in
`Module.End ℂ V × Module.End ℂ U`. Let `B` be its actual unital adjoin. The
first projection is a unital complex algebra homomorphism, and its image on `B`
is exactly `Algebra.adjoin ℂ (Set.range a)`, by `AlgHom.map_adjoin`.
`Submodule.finrank_map_le` therefore bounds the original adjoin's dimension by
`Module.finrank ℂ B`.

The algebra homomorphism `LinearMap.prodMapAlgHom ℂ V U` sends a pair of
endomorphisms to their block diagonal endomorphism on `V × U`. The private
lemma `prodMapAlgHom_injective` proves its injectivity by evaluating at `(x,0)`
and `(0,y)` and projecting the results. The published
`Langlands.CommutingDimension.finrank_adjoin_map` then identifies the dimension
of `B` with that of the actual adjoin generated by the block diagonal tuple.
The private lemma `finrank_adjoin_prod_zero_le` combines these facts. It does
not require the original tuple to be nilpotent or commuting.

`nilpotentGeneratedMaxDimension_le_prod` applies this inequality to an actual
tuple attaining the maximum on `V`, obtained from the published
`nilpotentGeneratedMaxDimension_attained`. The product tuple commutes
componentwise. If `(a i)^n = 0`, then `(a i,0)^(n+1) = 0`; using the successor
exponent also covers any supplied zero exponent on a trivial carrier.
Mapping along the block diagonal algebra homomorphism preserves these two
properties. Thus the extended tuple is admissible on `V × U`, and the
published `finrank_adjoin_le_nilpotentGeneratedMaxDimension` bounds its
adjoin's dimension by the actual maximum there.

For spaces `V` and `W` with `dim V ≤ dim W`,
`nilpotentGeneratedMaxDimension_mono_finrank` chooses
`C = Fin (Module.finrank ℂ W - Module.finrank ℂ V) → ℂ`.
The product dimension formula gives `dim (V × C) = dim W`.
`LinearEquiv.ofFinrankEq` supplies a complex linear equivalence from `V × C`
to `W`; the published `nilpotentGeneratedMaxDimension_eq_of_linearEquiv`
transports the maximum. Combining this equality with the product inequality
proves the arbitrary-carrier conjunct. Applying that same result to
`Fin k → ℂ` and `Fin m → ℂ` proves the canonical monotonicity conjunct.

## Conventions and scope

The statement, actual maximum construction, and their imports are unchanged.
The proof retains zero-dimensional carriers, `l = 0`, and zero, repeated or
redundant tuple entries. The identity and scalars are already in each unital
adjoin and do not consume a generator. The proof compares dimensions through
an image inequality; it makes no equality claim between the original and
extended adjoin dimensions, which can differ because of the added identity
action. It needs neither positivity nor a supplied embedding, basis,
maximizing witness, monotonicity hypothesis or sharp numerical estimate.

This proof establishes the focused monotonicity assertion. The ideal-power
endpoint, complement generation and restriction, algebra splitting,
first-level estimate, floor recurrence, final contradiction and the remaining
whole-book program are separate obligations. The maximum and transport
constructions are imported published mathematics; their authors are not
