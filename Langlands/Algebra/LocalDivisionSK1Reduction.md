# Reducing the local reduced-norm kernel to commutator movement

This module isolates the last group-theoretic step of the local SK1
argument: once every reduced-norm-one unit can be moved by a commutator
into the image of a cyclic Galois subfield of full degree, the kernel of the
canonical reduced norm on the original units equals the commutator subgroup.
It contains no arithmetic of local fields beyond the observation that norm-one
units have valuation one.

Throughout, `coefficientNorm K D : Dˣ →* Kˣ` is the Library map from
`Langlands.Algebra.BinaryReducedNorm`, the canonical reduced norm
`Langlands.CentralSimple.reducedNormUnits K D K` composed with the unit map of
`a ↦ 1 ⊗ a`. Its kernel is exactly the subgroup in
`Langlands.CentralDivision.LocalSK1Statement`.

## The subgroup generated by commutators and a field

Let `K` be a field, `D` a division ring with `Algebra K D` (common universe),
`L` a field (any universe) with `Algebra K L`, and `ι : L →ₐ[K] D`.

* `commutatorFieldSubgroup ι : Subgroup Dˣ` is the supremum (the generated
  subgroup) of `commutator Dˣ` and the range of the unit map
  `Units.map ι.toMonoidHom : Lˣ →* Dˣ`.
* `mem_commutatorFieldSubgroup_iff`: a unit `a` lies in it if and only if
  there is `c ∈ commutator Dˣ` with `c * a` in the range of
  `Units.map ι.toMonoidHom`. Since the commutator subgroup is normal, the
  supremum is the product set (Mathlib's `Subgroup.mem_sup_of_normal_left`):
  from `a = y * z` take `c = y⁻¹`, and from `c * a = z` write
  `a = c⁻¹ * z`.

## Generic reduction

Now assume moreover that `D` is finite-dimensional and central over `K`, and
that `L` is finite and Galois over `K` with cyclic automorphism group
`L ≃ₐ[K] L`, with `hdeg : Module.finrank K L = reducedDegree K D`.

* `ker_coefficientNorm_eq_commutator_of_le`: if
  `(coefficientNorm K D).ker ≤ commutatorFieldSubgroup ι`, then
  `(coefficientNorm K D).ker = commutator Dˣ`.
* `ker_coefficientNorm_eq_commutator_of_movement`: the same conclusion from
  `hmove`: for every `a` in the kernel of `coefficientNorm K D` there is
  `c ∈ commutator Dˣ` with `c * a` in the range of `Units.map ι.toMonoidHom`.
  By `mem_commutatorFieldSubgroup_iff` this is the previous hypothesis.

Proof of the first. The inclusion of the commutator subgroup in the kernel is
the Library theorem `commutator_le_ker_coefficientNorm`, since the target `Kˣ`
is commutative. Conversely, for `a` in the kernel obtain `c` as above. The
product `c * a` lies in the kernel (both factors do) and in the image of `ι`.
The Library theorem `range_inf_ker_coefficientNorm_eq_range_inf_commutator`
(from `Langlands.Algebra.CyclicSubfieldReducedNorm`, which uses `hdeg` to
identify the reduced norm on the image with the field norm and then cyclic
Hilbert 90) shows that the image of `ι` meets the kernel exactly where it meets
the commutator subgroup. So `c * a` is in the commutator subgroup, and so is
`a = c⁻¹ * (c * a)`.

## Local version

Now let `K` be a nonarchimedean local field (`Field K`, `ValuativeRel K`,
`TopologicalSpace K`, `IsNonarchimedeanLocalField K`) and `D` as above.

* `localReducedNormValuation_eq_one_of_mem_ker`: if `a : Dˣ` lies in the kernel
  of `coefficientNorm K D`, then `localReducedNormValuation K D a = 1`. Indeed,
  by `localReducedNormValuation_apply` this valuation is the valuation of `K`
  applied to the reduced norm of `1 ⊗ a`, which is `1`.
* `ker_coefficientNorm_eq_commutator_of_unit_movement`: the generic reduction
  with `hmove` replaced by the stronger hypothesis that every unit `a : Dˣ`
  with `localReducedNormValuation K D a = 1` can be moved by a commutator into
  the image of `ι`. It follows from the previous two results.

## Provenance

This is internal packaging of the final step of the proof of Proposition 3.3
in Tits, *Groupes de Whitehead de groupes algébriques simples sur un corps*
(Séminaire Bourbaki, exposé 505, 1977), printed page 228: for `a` of reduced
norm one, a commutator `c` (from Lemma 3.2) puts `c a` into an unramified
maximal commutative subfield `F`, which is cyclic over `K`, so `c a` is a
commutator by 2.6 (Hilbert 90). Tits works over a complete discretely valued
field and assumes the residue algebra of `D` commutative; here the movement is
an explicit hypothesis, and the cyclic-field step is the Library result cited
above. No movement theorem, residue-degree formula or existence of the
subfield is proved in this module.
