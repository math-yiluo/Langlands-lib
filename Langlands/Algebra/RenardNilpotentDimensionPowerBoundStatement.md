## Exact statement and objects

`Langlands.Renard.NilpotentDimensionPowerBoundStatement` asserts,
for every pair of natural numbers `l` and `k`,

```text
(nilpotentGeneratedPhi l k : ℝ) ≤
  Real.rpow (k : ℝ) (2 - Real.rpow 2 (1 - (l : ℝ))).
```

Both subtractions, the inner power of two and the outer power of
the dimension are real operations. The comparison is a weak
inequality. The maximum is a natural number cast into the reals;
it is not replaced by a floor of the power function or by a
supplied numerical majorant.

The imported module
`Langlands.Algebra.RenardNilpotentDimensionMaximum` defines
`nilpotentGeneratedPhi l k` as the actual maximum on the
canonical complex space `Fin k → ℂ`. It takes the natural
supremum of exactly the set of dimensions of
`Algebra.adjoin ℂ (Set.range a)` for commuting nilpotent tuples
`a : Fin l → Module.End ℂ (Fin k → ℂ)`. That construction
proves that the dimension set is nonempty and bounded, that the
maximum belongs to it, and that it bounds every such tuple.
The identity and scalars belong to the unital adjoin without
consuming tuple entries. Zero and repeated entries are permitted.

The target contains no recurrence, induction hypothesis,
attainment assumption, numerical contradiction premise, supplied
function, or original-algebra bound as an assumption. The actual
maximum is fixed by the published construction. This purely
numerical statement has no carrier-universe parameter.
The separately published `nilpotentDimensionTransport` identifies
the canonical maximum with the one on every finite-dimensional
complex carrier of dimension `k`; that transport is not an
additional conclusion of this pair.

## Source correspondence and boundaries

For positive generator count and positive dimension, this is the
nilpotent-maximum inequality proved in the source's induction.
The strict counterexample used in that proof has `l ≥ 1` and
`k ≥ 2`. Its `k = 1` base case has maximum one. The one-generator
case is controlled by the Cayley–Hamilton bound.

The target extends the inequality to `k = 0` and `l = 0` as
elementary boundary consequences of the same published actual
objects. At `k = 0`, the endomorphism space and every generated
adjoin have complex dimension zero, so the actual maximum is zero.
The unital identity on this zero carrier is the zero endomorphism.
For `l ≥ 1` the exponent
is at least one, so the displayed upper bound is also zero.
For `l = 0`, the exponent is zero and Lean's real-power convention
gives `Real.rpow x 0 = 1`, including `x = 0`. The empty tuple
generates the scalar algebra: its dimension is zero at `k = 0`
and one at positive `k`. Thus the zero-generator bound is `0 ≤ 1`
on the zero carrier and equality on every positive-dimensional
carrier. These are extensions, not separately stated boundary
cases in Renard.

At `l = 1` the exponent is one, giving the bound
`nilpotentGeneratedPhi 1 k ≤ k`. At `k = 1` and every `l`, the
maximum and real-power upper bound are both one. The statement
uses no positivity hypothesis to exclude any of these cases.
