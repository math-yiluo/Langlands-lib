## Quantifiers, witness and actual objects

For every `V : Type u` with explicit `AddCommGroup V`, `Module ℂ V`
and `FiniteDimensional ℂ V` instances, and every natural generator
count `l`, the proposition asserts that some actual tuple
`a : Fin l → Module.End ℂ V` exists. The same tuple satisfies all of
the following:

1. Its entries commute pairwise.
2. Each entry is nilpotent.
3. `Module.finrank ℂ (NilpotentFiltration.generatedAlgebra a)` equals
   `nilpotentGeneratedPhi l (Module.finrank ℂ V)`.
4. Some `W : Submodule ℂ V` satisfies
   `IsCompl (NilpotentFiltration.idealPowerSubspace a 1) W`.
5. Every `W : Submodule ℂ V` with that complement condition satisfies
   `nilpotentGeneratedPhi l (Module.finrank ℂ V) ≤
   Module.finrank ℂ W * Module.finrank ℂ V`.

The Lean file imports `Langlands.Algebra.RenardNilpotentIdealFiltration`
and `Langlands.Algebra.RenardNilpotentDimensionMaximum` and uses their
definitions unchanged.
`NilpotentFiltration.generatedAlgebra a` is the actual unital
subalgebra `Algebra.adjoin ℂ (Set.range a)` of `Module.End ℂ V`, the
source's `R`. `NilpotentFiltration.idealPowerSubspace a 1` is the
complex subspace underlying `I • V`, where `I` is the ideal of this
algebra generated by the entries of this same tuple and the algebra
acts on `V` by evaluation. This is the source's `V_1 = I · V`. Under
the commutation conclusion the generated algebra is commutative, so
`I` is the source's ideal. No other algebra, ideal, action or
filtration is used.

`nilpotentGeneratedPhi l k` is the maximum, over commuting nilpotent
`l`-tuples of endomorphisms of the dimension-`k` carrier `Fin k → ℂ`,
of the dimension of the generated unital algebra. That this equals the
same maximum on any `k`-dimensional `V` is proved separately, by
`Langlands.Renard.nilpotentDimensionTransport` in
`Langlands.Algebra.RenardNilpotentDimensionTransportProof`.
Conclusion 3 says that the chosen tuple attains this maximum. It is a
conclusion, not a premise.

## Complement and dimension comparison

`IsCompl (NilpotentFiltration.idealPowerSubspace a 1) W` means that the
two complex subspaces meet only in zero and together span `V`.
Conclusion 4 asserts that such a `W` exists, so conclusion 5 is not
vacuous.

Write `k = Module.finrank ℂ V` and `s = Module.finrank ℂ W`. Then
conclusion 5 is exactly `φ_l(k) ≤ s k`. All the spaces involved are
finite-dimensional over `ℂ`: `V_1` and `W` are subspaces of `V`, and
the generated algebra is a subspace of the finite-dimensional space
`Module.End ℂ V`. So every `finrank` here is the actual dimension.

`W` is any complex complement of `V_1`. It need not be stable under
the generated algebra.

The published proposition
`Langlands.Renard.NilpotentComplementRestrictionStatement`, in
`Langlands.Algebra.RenardNilpotentComplementRestrictionStatement`,
bounds the dimension of each tuple's own generated algebra by
`Module.finrank ℂ W * Module.finrank ℂ V` for each complement `W` of
that tuple's `V_1`. That bound alone does not bound the maximum,
because a tuple's algebra can be smaller than the maximum. For
example, if `l ≥ 1` and `k ≥ 2`, the zero tuple generates only the
one-dimensional scalar algebra. A tuple whose first entry is a single
nilpotent Jordan block of size `k` and whose other entries are zero
generates an algebra of dimension `k`. In the
present statement, conclusion 3 ties the complement to a tuple whose
algebra dimension is the maximum. This is the step the source leaves
implicit. The present statement neither imports nor assumes the
restriction proposition.

## Boundary cases and extensions

The carrier universe is arbitrary. The statement also covers `l = 0`,
the zero space, and tuples with zero, repeated or redundant entries.
On the zero space, the maximum is zero, and both `s` and `k` are zero.
For the empty tuple on a nonzero space, the ideal is zero, `V_1` is
zero, its only complement is `V`, and the generated algebra is the
scalar algebra. The scalars and the identity always lie in the
generated algebra without being counted among the `l` generators.
The source does not treat the zero space or the empty tuple
separately. These readings are derived from the Lean definitions.
