# Finite generation from quotient stability

`Langlands.Algebra.IdealFiniteGenerationFromQuotientStabilityStatement` states
the elementary finite-generation implication used near the end of the proof of
Stacks Lemma 10.128.9 (tag 05UV). This file describes a statement, not a proof.

Let \(f:B\to F\) be a homomorphism of commutative rings, and let \(J\subset B\)
be an ideal. Assume the extended ideal \(JF\), generated by \(f(J)\), is
finitely generated. Suppose that whenever \(I,K\subset B\) are finitely
generated ideals with
\[
I\subset K\subset J,\qquad IF=JF,
\]
the canonical quotient homomorphism \(B/I\to B/K\) is injective. Then \(J\) is
finitely generated.

Here `Ideal.map f` is extension of ideals, which need not equal the set-theoretic
image under \(f\). `Ideal.Quotient.factor hIK` is the canonical quotient map
sending the class of \(b\) modulo \(I\) to its class modulo \(K\). Its
surjectivity is automatic, but only injectivity is needed in the hypothesis.
No hypothesis that \(f\) is surjective or that either ring is Noetherian is
included. The two rings have independent universe levels.

## Provenance and use

This derived statement extracts and slightly generalizes the two elementary
finite-generation steps in the proof of 05UV. In that application, \(B\) is a
localized finite-variable polynomial algebra and \(F=B/\mathfrak mB\).
The original proof uses Noetherianity of \(F\) to get finite generation of
\(JF\). It then chooses a finite subideal \(I\subset J\) with \(IF=JF\).
For any larger finite subideal \(K\subset J\), the applications of 00R7 and
046Y establish injectivity of \(B/I\to B/K\).

The elementary argument represented by this statement requires only finite
generation of \(JF\): choose finitely many elements of \(J\) whose images
generate it and let \(I\) be their span. Given any \(x\in J\), apply quotient
injectivity with \(K=I+(x)\). The class of \(x\) vanishes modulo \(K\), hence
also modulo \(I\), so \(x\in I\) and \(J=I\). This argument explains the
generalization; no Lean proof of it is supplied here.

The quotient-injectivity hypothesis is a remaining obligation for the 05UV
proof. It is not asserted as a theorem or hidden in approximation data, and the
statement makes no claim to prove either flatness criterion.
