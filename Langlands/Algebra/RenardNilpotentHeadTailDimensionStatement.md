## Quantifiers and published objects

For every complex vector space `V : Type u`, with explicit `AddCommGroup V`,
`Module ℂ V` and `FiniteDimensional ℂ V` instances, every natural `l`,
and every tuple `a : Fin (l + 1) → Module.End ℂ V`, assume
`∀ i j, Commute (a i) (a j)` and `∀ i, IsNilpotent (a i)`.
There are `l + 1` counted generators in the original algebra and `l`
generators in the tail. Thus Lean's `a 0` is the source's first generator,
and the source's `φ_(number of generators − 1)(k)` becomes `φ_l(k)`.
Scalars and the identity belong to every unital adjoin without adding
a tuple entry.

The imports supply the following actual objects unchanged:

- `NilpotentFiltration.generatedAlgebra a` is the original
  `R = Algebra.adjoin ℂ (Set.range a)`.
- `NilpotentFiltration.tailAlgebra a` is `R'`, the internal unital
  subalgebra of `R` generated by the embedded tail generators.
- `NilpotentFiltration.headIdealSubspace a` is the complex subspace
  underlying `R''`, the ideal of `R` generated by its embedded head.
  Under commutation `R` is commutative, so this left ideal is the
  source's two-sided ideal.
- `NilpotentFiltration.idealPowerSubspace a 1` is the original-action
  subspace `V_1 = I V`, with `I` the ideal generated by the entire tuple.
- `NilpotentFiltration.firstLevelAlgebra a` is the unital algebra
  generated by the original generators restricted to `V_1`, inside
  `Module.End ℂ V_1`. The published `firstLevelAlgebra_eq_range`
  identifies it with the image of the actual restriction
  homomorphism `firstLevelRestriction a`.
- `nilpotentGeneratedPhi n k` is the actual maximum on `Fin k → ℂ`,
  constructed in `Langlands.Algebra.RenardNilpotentDimensionMaximum`.
  Its carrier-independent interpretation is supplied by the separately
  published transport statement and proof.

No replacement algebra, ideal, action, restricted tuple, maximum or
dimension bound is supplied as data.

## The five conclusions

Write `k = finrank ℂ V` and `V_1 = idealPowerSubspace a 1`.
For a complement `W` write `s = finrank ℂ W`, as in the source.
The conjunction states:

1. `(tailAlgebra a).toSubmodule ⊔ headIdealSubspace a = ⊤`.
   Both summands are complex subspaces of the original `R`. Their
   supremum is their vector-space sum. This is exactly `R = R' + R''`;
   it asserts no directness or trivial intersection.
2. `finrank ℂ (tailAlgebra a) ≤ nilpotentGeneratedPhi l k`.
   This is the source's bound for the tail. The published
   `tailAlgebra_map` identifies its image in the original endomorphism
   ring with the actual algebra generated by `Fin.tail a`.
3. `finrank ℂ (headIdealSubspace a) ≤
   finrank ℂ (firstLevelAlgebra a)`.
   This is the source's comparison of `dim R''` with the dimension of
   the restriction of `R` to `V_1`. The right side is the dimension of
   the actual restriction image, rather than the dimension of its
   ambient endomorphism ring.
4. `finrank ℂ (firstLevelAlgebra a) ≤
   nilpotentGeneratedPhi (l + 1) (finrank ℂ V_1)`.
   The original tuple restricts to a commuting nilpotent tuple on
   `V_1`, using the two published restriction laws. This is the
   second part of the source's dimension comparison.
5. For every `W : Submodule ℂ V` with `IsCompl V_1 W`,
   `finrank ℂ R ≤ nilpotentGeneratedPhi l k +
   nilpotentGeneratedPhi (l + 1) (k - finrank ℂ W)`.
   This is the dimension consequence of the preceding four clauses:
   the dimension of a sum is at most the sum of dimensions, and a
   complement satisfies `finrank ℂ V_1 = k - finrank ℂ W`.
   The subtraction is natural-number subtraction. Complementarity in a
   finite-dimensional space ensures `finrank ℂ W ≤ k`, so this
   subtraction expresses the ordinary dimension difference in the
   source. The combined inequality is a derived packaging of the
   consecutive source comparisons, not a separately numbered lemma.

All dimensions are of actual finite-dimensional complex vector spaces.
The carrier of `R` is a subspace of the finite-dimensional original
endomorphism space, its head and tail are subspaces of `R`, and `V_1`
is a subspace of `V`. Its endomorphism space and first-level algebra
are finite-dimensional as well.

The carrier universe is arbitrary. Zero-dimensional spaces,
single-generator lists with empty tails, and zero, repeated or redundant
entries are included. These boundary extensions preserve the constructions
and comparisons. On the zero space all displayed dimensions and both maximum terms are zero.
When `l = 0`, the original tuple has one entry and the tail is empty.
Its unital adjoin is the scalar algebra, of dimension one on a nonzero
space and zero on the zero space. The bounds use `φ_0(k)` for this
empty tail and `φ_1(dim V_1)` for the single restricted generator; the
identity does not become an additional generator. These corner conventions
are internal extensions of the consecutive source comparisons.
The complement `W` is arbitrary and need not be invariant. Restriction
to `V_1` is an endomorphism action on that invariant space; no
endomorphism action on `W` is claimed.

## Proof and remaining obligations

Renard obtains the splitting by separating expressions in the head from
the tail-generated algebra. Since the head maps `V` into `V_1`,
multiplication by the head factors through restriction of `R` to `V_1`,
giving the head-ideal dimension comparison. The tail and first-level
restricted tuples are admissible for their respective actual maxima.
This is the source route explaining the statement; this Lean file
implements none of those proofs.

The statement assumes no splitting, image-dimension bound, complement
generation, filtration endpoint, maximizing tuple or sharp numerical
estimate. Its conclusions concern each original tuple. They do not
assert a bound for `φ_(l+1)(k)` using the complement of an arbitrary
tuple. Applying the comparison to an actual attaining tuple, the bound
`φ_(l+1)(k) ≤ (dim W) k`, monotonicity, the floor recurrence, the
source's final contradiction and the rest of the whole-book program
retain their separate statement and proof obligations.
