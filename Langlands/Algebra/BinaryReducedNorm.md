# Binary generation for the canonical reduced norm

Let `K : Type u` be a field and `D : Type u` a finite-dimensional
central simple `K`-algebra. The definitions in
`Langlands.BinaryElementary.Reduced` restrict the actual canonical
reduced norms to the original coefficient and binary matrix units:

```text
coefficientNorm K D : Dˣ →* Kˣ
binaryNorm K D : GL₂ D →* Kˣ.
```

Each restriction uses the actual unit map induced by the tensor
inclusion `a ↦ 1 ⊗ a`, followed by
`Langlands.CentralSimple.reducedNormUnits`. No norm map, finite basis,
splitting field or chart is an input to these definitions.
The matrix algebra's finite dimensionality, centrality and simplicity
are obtained from the corresponding proved matrix instances.

`ker_coefficientNorm` and `ker_binaryNorm` identify the kernels with
the actual pullbacks of the published `reducedNormOne` subgroups.
The two membership formulas identify them with the equation that the
canonical reduced norm of `1 ⊗ a` or `1 ⊗ g` is one.

For any finite Galois coefficient splitting chart with any nonempty
finite matrix indices and any finite coordinate bases,
`charted_coefficientNorm_eq` and `charted_binaryNorm_eq` identify the
earlier chart-descended characters with these canonical characters.
The second equality uses the actual binary block chart constructed
from the coefficient chart. Both follow from the all-test equality
of the charted and canonical units homomorphisms; they do not replace
the represented determinant with an arbitrary supplied character.
The splitting and coordinate universes are independent of `u`.

`elementary_le_ker` proves that the actual subgroup generated by
upper and lower elementary matrices has canonical reduced norm one.
It uses a finite Galois chart constructed from central simplicity,
the published block-determinant comparison and the proved elementary
kernel inclusion.

When `D` is a division ring, `elementary_eq_ker_iff` proves

```text
elementary D = (binaryNorm K D).ker
  ↔ (coefficientNorm K D).ker = commutator Dˣ.
```

There is no additional local-field, splitting, representation,
conjugating-unit or coefficient-kernel premise in this equivalence.
The proof supplies its own finite Galois chart and finite bases,
uses the two actual character equalities, and applies the published
binary charted generation criterion. That criterion uses the actual
Dieudonné determinant and its exact elementary kernel. Thus the
right-hand condition is the precise remaining coefficient arithmetic
obligation.

This is an internal derivation from the published binary elementary,
representation, splitting and canonical reduced-norm constructions.
It adds no external-source assertion. It does not prove the right-hand
coefficient condition over local fields, identify a group scheme with
these rational-point groups, treat other rank-one cases or prove
`LocalKneserTitsStatement`.
