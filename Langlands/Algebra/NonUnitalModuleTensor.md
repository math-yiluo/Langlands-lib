## Original actions and the actual quotient

The algebra and groups have independent universes `uA`, `uX` and `uY`.
A right module is exactly the published `NonUnitalModule Aᵐᵒᵖ X`, and a left
module is `NonUnitalModule A Y`, on their specified original `AddCommGroup`
structures. The right action of `a : A` is `MulOpposite.op a • x`. Associativity
for the opposite ring is precisely the original right-module convention.
There is no unit, unit law, nondegeneracy, commutativity, nontriviality or
finiteness assumption. In particular, zero algebra actions on nonzero groups
are retained.

`relations A X Y` is the actual `Submodule ℤ (X ⊗[ℤ] Y)` generated by the range
of the function

```text
(a,x,y) ↦ ((op a) • x) ⊗ y − x ⊗ (a • y).
```

The tensor here is Mathlib's integer tensor product of the original additive
groups, using their canonical integer scalar structures. `Tensor A X Y` is
exactly the quotient by that submodule, in `Type (max uX uY)`. The algebra
universe does not enlarge the carrier: it occurs in the predicate defining
the submodule. The named `addCommGroup` is inherited from this actual quotient.
There is no independently supplied additive structure on the tensor.

`projection` is the entire quotient additive homomorphism, obtained from
`Submodule.mkQ`. `projection_function` identifies its whole underlying function
with the actual quotient constructor. `projection_surjective` specializes the
quotient's surjectivity. `tmul` is this projection evaluated on an integer pure
tensor; `tmul_function` gives the complete two-variable function.

The zero and addition laws in each variable follow from the integer tensor
laws and additivity of the quotient map. `biadditive` packages those proved
laws into the actual `X →+ Y →+ Tensor A X Y`, with pointwise and whole-function
interfaces. `tmul_balanced` uses the membership of each displayed generator
in its span: its quotient value is zero, so the two pure tensors agree.

## Universal maps and uniqueness

For an independent additive target `Z : Type uZ`, `IsBalanced A b` is the
precise Prop-valued equation

```text
∀ a x y, b ((op a) • x) y = b x (a • y)
```

for an original biadditive map `b : X →+ Y →+ Z`. `balancedMaps` is the actual
additive subgroup of such maps. Closure under zero, addition and negation is
proved using these equations pointwise. No action of `A` on `Z` is an input.

`integerLift b` uses `TensorProduct.liftAddHom`. Integer balancing is automatic:
additivity in the first variable gives `b (n • x) y = n • b x y`, and additivity
in the second gives `b x (n • y) = n • b x y`. These are the standard
`map_zsmul` laws on the original groups. `integerLift_tmul` is the resulting
pure-tensor value formula.

`relations_le_ker` proves that the entire relation submodule lies in the kernel
of the integer lift, viewed as an integer-linear map by
`AddMonoidHom.toIntLinearMap`. It suffices to check the range generators by
`Submodule.span_le`; their images are zero by additivity, the pure-tensor
formula and the supplied exact balancing equation.

`lift A b hb` descends that integer lift through the actual quotient using
`Submodule.liftQ`, then takes its underlying additive map. `lift_tmul` and
`lift_pure_function` give the pure-tensor formula at a point and as the full
two-variable function. `lift_comp_projection` is the factorization equality
of complete additive maps.

`hom_ext` proves that additive maps from this quotient are determined by all
pure-tensor values. Surjectivity of `projection` reduces equality to the
integer tensor. Automatic integer linearity and `TensorProduct.ext'` then
reduce it to pure tensors. Thus `lift_unique` proves uniqueness of the
universal lift as equality of full additive maps, without assuming a universal
property or a replacement quotient object.

`restrict` takes any actual additive map from the quotient to its biadditive
pure-tensor restriction. Balancing follows from `tmul_balanced`.
`universalEquiv` is the actual additive equivalence between quotient additive
maps and the existing `balancedMaps` subgroup. Its inverse is the constructed
lift. The first inverse identity follows from `hom_ext`; the second and the
additive law follow by subgroup, additive-map and pointwise extensionality.
`universalEquiv_left_inv` and `right_inv` expose both identities.

`universalEquiv_function` identifies the entire forward biadditive function;
`universalEquiv_symm_pure_function` identifies every pure-tensor value of the
inverse as a complete function of the balanced map and both vectors.
`universalEquiv_symm_function` identifies the entire inverse underlying map
with the constructed lift, and `universalEquiv_symm_comp_projection` gives its
full-map factorization through the integer tensor.

## Maps, universes and zero cases

For original right and left intertwiners `f : X → X'` and `g : Y → Y'`,
`mapBiadditive` is the actual biadditive map `(x,y) ↦ tmul (f x) (g y)`.
Original intertwining in each variable and target tensor balancing prove
`mapBiadditive_balanced`. `map` is its universal additive lift between the
actual quotients. `map_tmul` and `map_pure_function` expose its values.
`map_id` and `map_comp` are equalities of complete additive maps, proved by
`hom_ext` and the pure-tensor formula. The source and target carrier universes
are independent, as are the two additional carrier universes in composition.

Complete unapplied assignments check the relation submodule, quotient carrier
and inherited group, projection and its full function, pure and biadditive
maps, balancing property/subgroup, universal lift/equivalence, complete-function
and factorization identities, and induced maps. They run at arbitrary universes
and separately at `(uA,uX,uY,uZ,uX',uY') = (0,1,0,1,0,1)` and `(1,0,1,0,1,0)`.
At arbitrary universes, further unapplied checks cover the four zero/addition
laws, `tmul_balanced`, `integerLift`, `relations_le_ker`, `hom_ext`,
`lift_unique`, `restrict`, both inverse identities, `mapBiadditive`, `map_id`
and `map_comp` with its six independent carrier universes. `lift_unique`,
`map_id` and `map_comp` are also checked at the two unequal assignments, with
`(uX'',uY'') = (1,0)` and `(0,1)` respectively.
In particular, no bound between the algebra and carrier universes and no hidden
coefficient module or unit law enters the complete telescope.

Competing-structure checks explicitly select the original ambient ring,
additive groups, right and left algebra actions and independent target group.
They also select the canonical integer modules of both groups and the named
inherited tensor group when competing integer modules and a competing quotient
group coexist. The projection and the entire universal equivalence preserve
these choices. The overlapping-instance linter is disabled only in sections
that intentionally install duplicate ring, group or action instances.

For a subsingleton target, all universal lifted maps vanish. For a subsingleton
algebra, every balancing generator is zero, so the relation submodule is bottom;
this does not assert that the tensor vanishes. For a subsingleton source or
second group, tensor induction through the surjective projection proves that
the tensor group is subsingleton. Finally, the actual zero right and left
ℤ-actions on ℤ give `tmul ℤ ℤ ℤ 1 1 ≠ 0`: ordinary integer multiplication is a
balanced biadditive pairing and its universal lift sends this tensor to `1`.
This checks a nonzero-group case that an unintended unit law would destroy.
