# Logarithmic derivatives of formal exponentials under a cyclic trace

Let `F` be a characteristic-zero field, `R` an associative `F`-algebra, and `M` an `F`-vector space. A cyclic `F`-linear trace `τ : R → M` satisfies `τ(ab) = τ(ba)`. Its coefficientwise extension preserves cyclic permutations of series products by swapping the indices in the antidiagonal sum. The Euler operator multiplies coefficient `n` by `n` and satisfies the product rule by coefficient convolution.

`FormalExponentialTraceStatement` asserts that the trace of the right logarithmic Euler derivative of a formal exponential agrees with the trace of the Euler derivative of its exponent. The exponent has constant term zero, so only finitely many factorial terms contribute to any coefficient. Cyclicity makes the `k` derivative terms from its `k`th power contribute equally after multiplication by the inverse exponential. The factor `k` cancels against `k!`, leaving the Euler derivative of the exponent after multiplication by the exponential and its inverse. Constant coefficient one makes the formal exponential a unit.

For a product of unit series the traced logarithmic derivative is additive. If two such series agree below a positive degree `n`, the difference of their logarithmic derivatives in degree `n` is `n` times their coefficient difference, provided the first series has constant coefficient one. The inverse difference vanishes below degree `n`, while the Euler derivative has zero constant term. Equality of the traced logarithmic derivatives therefore forces zero trace of the first unmatched coefficient, since the scalar `n` is invertible in `F`. This supplies the trace condition for the homogeneous correction in the straightening argument. [Boyarchenko and Sabitova, *The orbit method for profinite groups and a p-adic analogue of Brown's theorem*, Lemma 2.3](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0608126v1)
