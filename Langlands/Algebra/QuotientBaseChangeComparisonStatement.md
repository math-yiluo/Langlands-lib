# Canonical base change of a quotient comparison

`Langlands.Algebra.QuotientBaseChangeComparisonStatement` states an elementary
comparison used in the proof of the local fibrewise flatness criterion. It is a
proposition definition, not a proof.

For commutative rings R, B and T with R-algebra structures on B and T, let I and J
be ideals of B with I contained in J. If their extensions to B tensor_R T are
equal, then both of the following actual maps are bijective:

- The R-algebra homomorphism `(B/I) tensor_R T -> (B/J) tensor_R T` obtained by
  tensoring the canonical quotient comparison with the identity of T.
- The R-linear map `T tensor_R (B/I) -> T tensor_R (B/J)` obtained by applying
  `LinearMap.lTensor T` to that canonical quotient comparison.

The first map is `Algebra.TensorProduct.map` applied to
`Ideal.Quotient.factorₐ R hIJ` and `AlgHom.id R T`. The second uses the underlying
R-linear map of exactly the same `factorₐ`. These are assertions about specified
maps, not merely the existence of unrelated isomorphisms. `Ideal.map` denotes the
extended ideal, generated by the images of all its elements. The ring map in the
hypothesis is the canonical map `b -> b tensor 1`. The three universe parameters
are independent. No locality, finite generation, flatness, nontriviality, or
surjectivity assumption on R -> T is imposed.

## Provenance and role in 05UV

This is a derived generalization of the quotient-fibre comparison in the proof of
Stacks Project, Lemma 10.128.9, tag 05UV. In that proof, T is the residue field
R/m, B is a localization of a finite polynomial algebra, and I and J have the same
image in B/mB. Under the standard identification `B tensor_R (R/m) = B/mB`, this
is precisely equality of the extended ideals in the hypothesis. Thus the
canonical map on fibres is an isomorphism. If `I <= K <= J` and I and J have equal
extensions, monotonicity gives the same equality for I and K, so the proposition
also applies to the successive finite quotient comparisons in that proof.

The generalization is elementary: the tensor products `(B/I) tensor_R T` and
`(B/J) tensor_R T` identify with the quotients of `B tensor_R T` by the two
extended ideals. Equality of those ideals makes their canonical comparison an
isomorphism. Tensor symmetry gives the second orientation. Mathlib supplies the
quotient identifications through `Algebra.TensorProduct.quotientTensorEquiv`.
The second conclusion is included to match the left-tensor map in
`Langlands.Algebra.LocalFiberInjectivityStatement` without hiding that map behind
an abstract ring equivalence.

This statement does not construct finite subideals, transport a module's
flatness, lift fibre injectivity to injectivity before base change, or prove
05UV. Those are separate obligations. The source's final nested-ideal sentence
repeats J-prime where J-double-prime is intended; its immediately following
comparison of the two quotients determines the intended inclusions.
