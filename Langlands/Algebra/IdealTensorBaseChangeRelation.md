# Generator relations after a base change of the ring

`Langlands.Algebra.IdealTensorBaseChangeRelation` is a helper module for the
proof of `Langlands.Algebra.TensorKernelTransitionReductionStatement`. It
proves the relation lemma `sum_tmul_mem_relationSpan`. This is an
elementwise form of the surjectivity of
\(\mathrm{Tor}_1^R(M,R/I)\otimes_R R'\to\mathrm{Tor}_1^{R'}(R'\otimes_R M,R'/IR')\).
The proof of Stacks Project Lemma 10.99.14 (tag **00MO**) uses that
surjectivity, obtained from Lemmas 10.99.12 (tag 00MM) and 10.99.13
(tag 00MN), using flatness of \(M/IM\) over \(R/I\). The Lean statement and route are this project's own. They
avoid Tor and work with explicit relations.

## The relation lemma

Let \(R\to R'\) be a map of commutative rings, \(I\subseteq R\) an ideal
generated by \(g_1,\dots,g_r\), and \(M\) an \(R\)-module such that
\((R/I)\otimes_R M\) is flat over \(R/I\). Write \(I'=IR'\) and \(g_k\) also
for the image of \(g_k\) in \(I'\) (`mapGen`). By `span_mapGen`, these images
generate \(I'\). Let `relationSpan I g M` be the \(R'\)-submodule of
\(I'\otimes_{R'}(R'\otimes_R M)\) spanned by the elements
\(\sum_k g_k\otimes(1\otimes z_k)\) with \(z_k\in M\) and
\(\sum_k g_k z_k=0\) in \(M\).

`sum_tmul_mem_relationSpan`: if \(y_k\in R'\otimes_R M\) satisfy
\(\sum_k g_k y_k=0\), then \(\sum_k g_k\otimes y_k\) lies in
`relationSpan I g M`.

## Proof

The lemma is first proved for any presentation
\(K\xrightarrow{\iota}F\xrightarrow{\pi}M\to0\) with \(F\) flat
(`sum_tmul_mem_relationSpan_of_exact`). It is then applied to the free module
\(F=M\to_0 R\), \(\pi\) the linear combination map and \(K=\ker\pi\).

1. *Kernel modulo \(I\).* `idealPart I ι` is the submodule of those
   \(k\in K\) with \(\iota(k)\in IF\). Modulo \(I\), the kernel of
   \(K\to F\) is the image of `idealPart` (`exact_baseChange_idealPart`,
   via the pure-tensor lemmas of `IdealTensorRelation`).
   `ker_lTensor_le_range_of_flat` is a general fact. If \(K\to F\to P\to0\)
   is exact with \(P\) flat and \(K_0\to K\to F\) is exact, then for every
   \(N\) the kernel of \(N\otimes K\to N\otimes F\) lies in the image of
   \(N\otimes K_0\). Its proof uses Mathlib's
   `LinearMap.lTensor_injective_of_exact_of_flat` for the flat module \(P\),
   together with `lTensor_exact`. Applied over \(R/I\) with
   \(P=(R/I)\otimes_R M\), and transported along
   `TensorProduct.AlgebraTensorModule.cancelBaseChange`, it gives
   `ker_lTensor_le`. For every \(R/I\)-module \(N\), the kernel of
   \(N\otimes_R K\to N\otimes_R F\) lies in the image of
   \(N\otimes_R\) `idealPart`.
2. *Lift.* Lift each \(y_k\) to \(y'_k\in R'\otimes_R F\). The element
   \(v=\sum_k g_k y'_k\) maps to zero in \(R'\otimes_R M\). By right
   exactness, \(v\) is the image of some \(\kappa\in R'\otimes_R K\).
3. *Reduce modulo \(I'\).* Every \(g_k\) lies in \(I'\), so the reduction of
   \(\kappa\) in \((R'/I')\otimes_R K\) maps to zero in
   \((R'/I')\otimes_R F\). Step 1 with \(N=R'/I'\) writes it as the reduction
   of an element \(\mu\in R'\otimes_R\) `idealPart`. Then
   \(\kappa-\mu\in I'(R'\otimes_R K)\). This uses right exactness of
   \(I'\to R'\to R'/I'\) after tensoring
   (`mem_smul_top_of_rTensor_mkQ_eq_zero`). So
   \(\kappa-\mu=\sum_k g_k w_k\).
4. *Relations from `idealPart`.* `exists_relation_of_idealPart`: the image
   of \(\mu\) in \(R'\otimes_R F\) has the form \(\sum_k g_k u_k\), and
   \(\sum_k g_k\otimes\pi(u_k)\) lies in `relationSpan`. For a pure tensor
   \(c\otimes k_1\), write \(\iota(k_1)=\sum_k g_k u'_k\). The family
   \(\pi(u'_k)\) is then a relation in \(M\).
5. *Free part.* The family \(y'_k-u_k-\iota(w_k)\) is a relation with the
   \(g_k\) in the flat \(R'\)-module \(R'\otimes_R F\). So its generator
   tensor vanishes (`sum_tmul_eq_zero_of_flat`, flatness applied to the
   inclusion \(I'\to R'\)). Applying \(\pi\) kills \(\iota(w_k)\) and gives
   \(\sum_k g_k\otimes y_k=\sum_k g_k\otimes\pi(u_k)\), which lies in
   `relationSpan` by step 4.

The remaining lemmas (`lTensor_cancelBaseChange`, `rTensor_mkQ_baseChange`,
`rTensor_mkQ_smul_eq_zero`) are naturality and vanishing facts used in these
steps.
