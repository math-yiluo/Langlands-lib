# Binary elementary quotient

This internal algebraic construction continues `BinaryElementary` for actual
units of binary matrices. For an arbitrary ring, diagonal conjugation preserves
the subgroup generated by the upper and lower elementary matrices. The proof
uses induction on the generated subgroup and the ordered conjugation formulas
in `BinaryElementary`.

For every coefficient unit `a`, the identity
`diagonal a a⁻¹ * weyl = upper a * lower (-a⁻¹) * upper a` is checked on the
four matrix entries. Since the three factors and `weyl` are elementary,
`diagonal a a⁻¹` is elementary as well. Coefficient products are never commuted.

Over a division ring, `elementary_normal` proves normality of the actual
elementary subgroup. It factors a general matrix unit as
`e * diagonal a b * f`, with `e` and `f` elementary, using the proved
division-ring reduction. Conjugation first by `f`, then by the diagonal,
then by `e` preserves elementary membership.

`ElementaryQuotient D` is the actual group quotient `GL₂ D / elementary D`.
`quotientMap` is its surjective quotient homomorphism, whose kernel is precisely
the elementary subgroup. The same matrix factorization shows that every
quotient class has a diagonal representative.

The elementary inverse-pair diagonal shows that `diagonal a 1` and
`diagonal 1 a` have the same quotient class. Splitting a diagonal into these
two positions therefore shows that `diagonal a b` has the class of
`diagonal (a * b) 1`, in that displayed coefficient order.
`unitClass` is the resulting homomorphism from coefficient units to the
quotient; it is surjective by diagonal reduction.

Finally the quotient is commutative. Each of two classes has a representative
in the first diagonal position. Move the second representative to the second
position using the preceding class equality. Matrices supported in these
different diagonal positions commute by direct diagonal multiplication.
This proves `quotient_mul_comm` and supplies the quotient's commutative
group structure, without asserting that the coefficient ring is commutative.

All constructions work at arbitrary universe and characteristic. These are
internal consequences of explicit matrix identities and group laws. They
supply no reduced norm, arithmetic vanishing, comparison with group schemes
or proof of local Kneser–Tits.
