# Fixed points of a weight-scaling automorphism of an enveloping algebra: proof

Module `Langlands.Algebra.EnvelopingFixedWeightDecomposition`, namespace
`Langlands.Algebra.UniversalEnveloping`. The theorem

```lean
theorem envelopingFixedWeightDecomposition :
    EnvelopingFixedWeightDecompositionStatement.{u, v}
```

proves the statement of the module `Langlands.Algebra.EnvelopingFixedWeightDecompositionStatement`:
for a basis `b` of a Lie algebra `L` over a field `k`, indexed by `Fin n`, integer weights `d`, a
unit `c` that is not a root of unity and a Lie algebra endomorphism `T` with
`T (b a) = c ^ d a • b a`, every element of `U(L)` fixed by `T̃ = lift (ι ∘ T)` lies in
`positiveWeightGeneratorSpan b d ⊔ zeroWeightGeneratorSubalgebra b d`, and the two summands are
disjoint. Lean reports the axioms `propext`, `Classical.choice` and `Quot.sound`.

## The ordered basis

`orderedPBWBasisOf b` is the basis of `U(L)` given by the Library's Poincaré–Birkhoff–Witt theorem
`orderedPBWBasis`; its member at an exponent vector `e : Fin n → ℕ` is the ordered monomial
`orderedPBWMonomial b e = ι(b 0)^(e 0) ⋯ ι(b (n-1))^(e (n-1))`. The main argument assumes that `d`
is antitone, so that letters of larger weight come first. The general case reindexes `b` by the
permutation `Tuple.sort (-d)`; the two subspaces of the statement do not change under reindexing
(`positiveWeightGeneratorSpan_comp_equiv`, `zeroWeightGeneratorSubalgebra_comp_equiv`).

## Existence

Write `w(e) = Σ e_i d_i`. Since `T̃(ι(b a)) = c ^ d a • ι(b a)` and scalars can be collected from
an ordered product (`ofFn_prod_smul`), `T̃` multiplies the monomial of `e` by `c ^ w(e)`
(`map_orderedPBWMonomial`). Hence the coordinate of `T̃ z` at `e` is `c ^ w(e)` times that of
`z` (`orderedPBWBasisOf_repr_map`). If `T̃ z = z`, every exponent vector in the support of `z` has
`c ^ w(e) = 1`, so `w(e) = 0`. A monomial of weight zero is of one of two kinds
(`mem_sup_of_fixed_of_antitone`):

- if some letter has positive weight, the first letter has positive weight, because the weights
  decrease along the order; the monomial is `ι(b a) · v` with `d a > 0`
  (`ofFn_prod_pow_mem_span_of_pos`);
- otherwise every letter has weight at most zero and the weights add up to zero, so every letter
  has weight zero (`weight_eq_zero_of_sum_eq_zero`) and the monomial lies in the subalgebra
  generated by the weight-zero generators (`ofFn_prod_pow_mem_adjoin`).

## Uniqueness

Since `T` is a Lie algebra homomorphism, `T ⁅b a, b a'⁆ = c ^ (d a + d a') • ⁅b a, b a'⁆`, and an
eigenvector of `T` for `c ^ m` lies in the span of the basis vectors of weight `m`
(`mem_span_of_eigen`; the coordinates are scaled by `c ^ d i` by `repr_lieHom_apply`). So the
basis vectors of positive weight and those of weight zero span Lie subalgebras
(`lie_mem_span_pos`, `lie_mem_span_zero`, `basisSpanLieSubalgebra`). Such a subalgebra has the
basis `basisSpanLieSubalgebraBasis`, enumerated in increasing index order by the order embedding
`indexEmbedding`. Its ordered monomials span its enveloping algebra by `orderedPBWBasis`, and the
induced map into `U(L)` sends them to ordered monomials of `L` whose exponents vanish off the
subset (`ofFn_prod_pow_orderEmbedding`). Hence the subalgebra of `U(L)` generated by these
generators is spanned by the ordered monomials of `L` supported on the subset
(`adjoin_le_span_orderedPBWBasisOf`).

With weight zero this gives `zeroWeightGeneratorSubalgebra b d ≤ zeroLetterSpan b d`, the span of
the monomials with only weight-zero letters. For the right ideal take `d a > 0` and a monomial of
`e`. Split `e` into its positive-weight part `e₁` and the rest `e₂`; since positive weights come
first, the monomial of `e` is the product of the monomials of `e₁` and `e₂` (`ofFn_prod_pow_add`).
The element `x = ι(b a) · (monomial of e₁)` lies in the subalgebra generated by the positive-weight
generators, so it is a combination of monomials supported on positive weights. It is an
eigenvector of `T̃` for `c ^ (d a + w(e₁))` with `d a + w(e₁) > 0`, so every monomial in its
support has this positive weight and therefore a positive letter. Multiplying on the right by the
monomial of `e₂` gives the monomial of the sum of the exponents. Thus
`ι(b a) · (monomial of e)` lies in `positiveLetterSpan b d`, the span of the monomials with a
positive letter (`generator_mul_mem_positiveLetterSpan`), and so does the whole right ideal
(`positiveWeightGeneratorSpan_le`). The spans of two disjoint sets of basis vectors meet only in
`0` (`positiveLetterSpan_inf_zeroLetterSpan`), which proves disjointness
(`disjoint_of_antitone`).

## Provenance

The argument follows A. W. Knapp and D. A. Vogan, *Cohomological Induction and Unitary
Representations* (1995) (`knapp-vogan-1995-cohomological-induction`), Lemma 4.123 (a), (b),
printed p. 301 (PDF p. 323), where the decomposition of `U(𝔤)` comes from the
Poincaré–Birkhoff–Witt theorem and the action of a semisimple element on root vectors. Here the
grading comes from the scaling automorphism `T`, and the right ideal of the positive part replaces
the left ideal `U(𝔤) 𝔲`. The Poincaré–Birkhoff–Witt theorem used is the Library's
`orderedPBWBasis`.
