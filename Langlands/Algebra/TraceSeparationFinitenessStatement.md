# Finiteness from trace isolation

`Langlands.TraceSeparationFinitenessStatement.{uA,uI}` is a Prop-valued
definition. This module states the finiteness criterion and supplies no proof.
The formulation is adapted from Worker 307's shared draft.

## Statement and conventions

Let `A : Type uA` have structures `[Ring A] [Algebra ℂ A]` and
`[Algebra.FiniteType ℂ A]`. Thus `A` is a unital, possibly noncommutative
complex algebra generated by finitely many elements as an algebra. For any
`d : ℕ`, index type `I : Type uI`, and family

```text
ρ : I → A →ₐ[ℂ] Matrix (Fin d) (Fin d) ℂ,
```

the statement asserts that the trace-isolation hypothesis

```text
∀ i, ∃ a : A, Matrix.trace (ρ i a) ≠ 0 ∧
  ∀ j, j ≠ i → Matrix.trace (ρ j a) = 0
```

implies `Finite I`. Each `ρ i` is an actual unital complex algebra
homomorphism, and `Matrix.trace` is the sum of its diagonal entries.
The isolating element may depend on `i`.

The algebra and index universes are independent. No simplicity, injectivity,
finite-dimensionality of `A`, commutativity of `A`, or degree bound on the
isolating elements is assumed. The conclusion concerns the entire index
type, not a quotient of representations by equivalence. The zero-dimensional
case is included: every matrix trace is then zero, so the isolation premise
forces `I` to be empty.

## Source and derived provenance

The original source is I. N. Bernshtein and A. V. Zelevinskii,
*Representations of the group GL(n,F), where F is a non-Archimedean local
field*, Russian Mathematical Surveys 31:3 (1976), 1–68, English translation:
§4.16, printed p.41, in the proof of Theorem 4.14. In the retained PDF this
is zero-based page index 40 (the 41st page).

Registered source: `bernstein-zelevinsky-1976-representations-gln`, SHA256
`72dc22eb9991112936a88327f00d0f02a16a2bf8747518ca0ecfeeadd188e50c`.
The retained original is
`/home/taskhub/central-library/Bernstein-Zelevinsky--Representations of the group GL(n,F) where F is a non-archimedean local field (survey)--1976.pdf`.

Provenance is **derived**. The source fixes a matrix dimension for
irreducible representations of a finitely generated Hecke corner. The
representations considered there correspond to finite group representations
in the source's sense of compactly supported matrix coefficients; that use
of “finite” is distinct from finite dimension. Finite dimension and its
uniform bound are obtained earlier in the source. In §4.16 the special
Hecke-corner elements supply traces that isolate the relevant isomorphism
classes. Selecting one representative per class gives the premise above.
This statement extracts the subsequent algebraic step: once trace isolation
is supplied, it needs neither irreducibility nor the Hecke-corner setting.
Irreducibility belongs to the source's construction of isolating elements,
not to the polynomial-ideal argument after they have been constructed.

Here is the argument explaining the derivation. Choose finitely many algebra
generators of `A`. Every element has a noncommutative polynomial expression
in these generators, with complex coefficients. Taking the trace of that
expression on a tuple of `d` by `d` matrices gives a commutative polynomial
in their finitely many matrix entries. By the Hilbert basis theorem, the
ideal generated by the isolating trace polynomials is generated by a finite
subfamily of them. Evaluation at an index outside this finite subfamily
would annihilate every generator of the ideal, but would not annihilate that
index's own isolating polynomial, which lies in the ideal. This is a
contradiction. These remarks explain the source derivation and a possible
proof route; they are not an implemented Lean proof.
