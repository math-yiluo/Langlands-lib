# Elementary binary matrices: commutators, determinant one and list products

Module: `Langlands.Algebra.CentralSimple.LandherrElementary`.

For a ring `R`, `GL₂ R` is the unit group of `M₂(R)`, `upper p` and `lower q` are the
elementary matrices `[[1, p], [0, 1]]` and `[[1, 0], [q, 1]]`, and `elementary R` is the
subgroup they generate (module `Langlands.Algebra.BinaryElementary`).

## Rings

- `diagonalLeft : Rˣ →* GL₂ R` is `g ↦ diag(g, 1)`.
- `diagonal_commutator_mem`: `diag(a b a⁻¹ b⁻¹, 1)` is elementary, being the product
  `diag(a, a⁻¹) diag(b, b⁻¹) diag((ba)⁻¹, ba)` of elementary diagonal matrices
  (`diagonal_inverse_mem`, module `Langlands.Algebra.BinaryElementaryQuotient`).
- `diagonal_mem_of_mem_commutator`: if `g` lies in the commutator subgroup of `Rˣ`, then
  `diag(g, 1)` is elementary (the commutator subgroup is generated by commutators, all of
  which map into `elementary R` under `diagonalLeft`).
- `listProd l` is the product of the blocks `upper p * lower q` along a list `l` of pairs
  `(p, q)`; `listProd_nil`, `listProd_cons`, `listProd_append`, `listProd_replicate_zero`
  and `listProd_pad` (padding by `(0, 0)` changes nothing) compute with it, `listProd_mem`
  says it is elementary, and `listProd_inv` writes its inverse as a list product: the
  inverse of `upper p * lower q` is `lower (-q) * upper (-p)`, the product of the blocks for
  `(0, -q)` and `(-p, 0)`, taken in reverse order along the list.
- `exists_listProd_of_mem`: every elementary matrix is `listProd l` for some list, by
  induction on the subgroup closure (products are concatenations, inverses use
  `listProd_inv`).
- `map_listProd`: a ring homomorphism applied entrywise carries `listProd l` to the list
  product of the image list.

## Fields

For a field `L` and a finite index type `n`:

- `transvectionUnit` and `diagonalUnit` are transvections and invertible diagonal matrices
  as units; `swapUnit i k` is the permutation matrix of a transposition.
- `transvectionUnit_mem_commutator`: if `2 ≠ 0`, a transvection `T = 1 + c Eᵢⱼ` is a
  commutator: conjugating by `diag(2 at i)` gives `1 + 2c Eᵢⱼ = T²`, so
  `T = d T d⁻¹ T⁻¹`.
- `swap_mul_diagonal_mul_swap`, `diagonalUnit_pair_mem_commutator`: the commutator of
  `diag(c at i)` with the transposition `(i k)` is `diag(c at i, c⁻¹ at k)`.
- `diagonalUnit_mem_commutator`: an invertible diagonal matrix `diag(f)` with
  `∏ᵢ f i = 1` is a product of such commutators: fixing an index `i₀`, it is the product over
  `i ≠ i₀` of `diag(f i at i, (f i)⁻¹ at i₀)`.
- `mem_commutator_of_det_eq_one`: if `2 ≠ 0`, every invertible matrix of determinant one
  lies in the commutator subgroup of `GLₙ(L)`, by `Matrix.diagonal_transvection_induction`
  (every matrix is a product of transvections and a diagonal matrix of the same
  determinant).

## Provenance

Standard facts about elementary matrices and `SLₙ` over fields (Whitehead's lemma that
commutators give elementary `diag(g, 1)`, and generation of `SLₙ` by transvections).
In the proof of Landherr's theorem they turn a local solution of reduced norm one into a
product of elementary `2 × 2` matrices, which can then be approximated globally.
