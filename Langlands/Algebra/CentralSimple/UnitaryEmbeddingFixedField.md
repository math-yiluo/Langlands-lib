# Tensor coordinates from the fixed field of a quadratic involution

Let \(P\) be a field over \(F\) with an \(F\)-linear involution \(\sigma\), and put \(K=P^\sigma\). The module constructs \(K\) as the intermediate field fixed by the subgroup generated by \(\sigma\). For a nonidentity involution on a finite extension, that subgroup has order two and the fixed-field degree formula gives \([P:K]=2\).

Suppose \(\sigma\) acts on a quadratic subextension \(E/F\) by its nontrivial automorphism and \(F\) has characteristic zero. The multiplication map \(E\otimes_F K\to P\) is an \(E\)-algebra equivalence. For surjectivity, choose \(t\ne 0\) in \(E\) with \(\sigma(t)=-t\) by subtracting conjugates. Write \(x\in P\) as the sum of \((x+\sigma x)/2\) and \(t(x-\sigma x)/(2t)\), with both coefficients in \(K\). The tower formula equates the finite dimensions over \(E\) and gives injectivity.

Over a commutative coefficient algebra \(R\) over a characteristic-zero field \(F\), the map \(R\otimes_F K\to R\otimes_F P\) is injective by flatness over \(F\). Its image is the subalgebra fixed by the extended involution. The proof extends the projection \(x\mapsto (x+\sigma x)/2\) by tensors and applies it to a fixed element. A fixed unit and its inverse are both in the image. Their preimages multiply to one by injectivity, so they give a unit of \(R\otimes_F K\).

These are the fixed-field and scalar-extension identifications behind Prasad and Rapinchuk, *Local-global principles for embedding of fields with involution into simple algebras with involution*, arXiv:0806.0596v5, equations (9)–(10) and the proof of Proposition 4.2, pp. 13–15.
