# Local targets in `2 × 2` matrices over a completed algebra

Module: `Langlands.Algebra.CentralSimple.LandherrLocalTarget`.

## Comparison maps

Let `B` be a finite-dimensional `F`-algebra and `k` a field over `F`.

- `matrixBaseMap F B k : M₂(B) →ₐ[F] M₂(k ⊗_F B)` is entrywise `b ↦ 1 ⊗ b`
  (`matrixBaseMap_apply`).
- `matrixTensorMap F B k : k ⊗_F M₂(B) →ₐ[k] M₂(k ⊗_F B)`, `c ⊗ Z ↦ c · matrixBaseMap Z`
  (`matrixTensorMap_tmul`). It is surjective (every matrix is a sum of `c ⊗ Eᵢⱼ b`
  images) and both sides have the same `k`-dimension, so it is bijective
  (`matrixTensorMap_surjective`, `matrixTensorMap_bijective`).
- `matrixTensorMap_comp_injective`: for a subalgebra `K ≤ M₂(B)`, the composite
  `k ⊗_F K → k ⊗_F M₂(B) → M₂(k ⊗_F B)` is injective, since `k` is flat over the field `F`.
- `exists_mul_self_eq_tmul`: for `z ∈ K`, if `matrixBaseMap z` is the square of an element
  of the `k`-subalgebra it generates, then `1 ⊗ z` is a square in `k ⊗_F K`. The subalgebra
  generated by `matrixBaseMap z` lies in the image of `k ⊗_F K`, and the square root is
  pulled back using injectivity.

## Local targets

Let `E/F` be separable quadratic with `CharZero F`, `B` a finite-dimensional central simple
`E`-algebra, `τ : FirstKindInvolution F B` acting on `E` by the conjugation, and `k` a field
over `F` in which `d = skewSq F E` is not a square and which has the unitary splitting
property.

`exists_target_list`: if `y ∈ Bˣ` has relative reduced norm one over `k` and
`1 ⊗ y = x τ_k(x)` for a unit `x` of `k ⊗_F B`, then there is a list `l` of pairs such that
the elementary product `t = listProd l` (module
`Langlands.Algebra.CentralSimple.LandherrElementary`) satisfies
`t · diag(1 ⊗ y, 1) · ᵗτ_k(t) = 1` in `M₂(k ⊗_F B)`.

Proof: `exists_commutator_solution` (module
`Langlands.Algebra.CentralSimple.LandherrNonsplitLocal`) gives `w` in the commutator subgroup
of `(k ⊗_F B)ˣ` with `1 ⊗ y = w τ_k(w)`. Then `diag(w⁻¹, 1)` is elementary
(`diagonal_mem_of_mem_commutator`), hence equal to `listProd l` for some `l`
(`exists_listProd_of_mem`), and
`diag(w⁻¹, 1) diag(w τ(w), 1) ᵗτ(diag(w⁻¹, 1)) = 1` by a direct entrywise computation.

## Provenance

The comparison `k ⊗_F M₂(B) ≅ M₂(k ⊗_F B)` is elementary. The local target expresses, at a
nonsplit place, that `diag(y, 1)` and `1` represent the same hermitian form over `B_v` by an
elementary matrix; this is the local input to the approximation argument in the proof of
Landherr's theorem (Platonov and Rapinchuk, *Algebraic Groups and Number Theory* (1994),
§6.7, Theorem 6.27).
