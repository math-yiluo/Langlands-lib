# Binary norm equations in finite-dimensional commutative algebras over a number field

Module: `Langlands.Algebra.CentralSimple.LandherrNormGlobal`.

- `valuation_le_one_of_eval_eq_zero`: for a valuation `w` on a field, a root of a monic
  polynomial whose coefficients have `w`-value at most one has `w`-value at most one (if
  `w(x) > 1`, the leading term `xⁿ` strictly dominates the other terms).
- `localSolvable_finite`: let `π : K →ₐ[F] L` with `L` a number field over `F`, `d ≠ 0` in
  `F`, `z ∈ K`, and `P` a finite place of `L` lying over the place `v` of `F`. If `d` is a
  square in `F_v`, or `z` has a square root in `F_v ⊗_F K`, or `2`, `d` and `π z` are units at
  the respective places, then `x² - d y² = π z` is solvable in `L_P`. In the first case `d`
  is a square in `L_P` and every element is a value of the split form
  (`QuadraticNormEquation.exists_eq_of_isSquare`); in the second the image of the square
  root under `F_v ⊗_F K → L_P` gives a solution with `y = 0`; in the third use
  `exists_sq_sub_mul_sq_of_units` (module `Langlands.Algebra.CentralSimple.LandherrHensel`).
- `localSolvable_infinite`: the same at an infinite place of `L`, from the first two
  alternatives.
- `valuation_le_one_of_aeval`: a root in `L` of a monic polynomial over `F` with
  `v`-integral coefficients is integral at every place of `L` over `v`.
- `exists_sq_sub_mul_sq_of_local`: let `K` be a finite-dimensional commutative `F`-algebra,
  `d ≠ 0` and `z` a unit of `K`. Suppose that at every finite place `v` of `F` either `d` is a
  square in `F_v`, or `z` has a square root in `F_v ⊗_F K`, or `2` and `d` are `v`-units and
  `z`, `z⁻¹` are roots of monic polynomials over `F` with `v`-integral coefficients; and at
  every infinite place `w` either `d` is a square in `F_w` or `z` has a square root in
  `F_w ⊗_F K`. Then `z = X² - d Y²` for some `X, Y ∈ K`.

## Proof of `exists_sq_sub_mul_sq_of_local`

`K` is Artinian and `2` is a unit in it, so by `exists_sq_sub_mul_sq_eq_of_maximal` (module
`Langlands.Algebra.CentralSimple.LandherrArtinianNorm`) it suffices to solve the equation
modulo every maximal ideal `m`. The residue field `L = K/m` is a number field over `F`, and
`π z ≠ 0`. At each finite place `P` of `L`, apply `localSolvable_finite` to the hypothesis at
the place below; in the third alternative, `valuation_le_one_of_aeval` applied to `z` and
`z⁻¹` shows that `π z` is a `P`-unit. At infinite places use `localSolvable_infinite`.
The proof invokes `QuadraticNormLocalGlobal.exists_eq_of_local`. When the image of `d`
in `L` is a square, `QuadraticNormEquation.exists_eq_of_isSquare` solves the split norm
equation directly. When the image is nonsquare, `ClassFieldTheory.Global.cyclicHasseNorm`
gives a solution for the norm form of the quadratic field extension `L(√d)/L`.
The two entries of the solution in `L` lift along `π` to elements of `K` that solve
the equation modulo `m`.

## Provenance

The Hasse norm theorem for quadratic extensions, applied to a finite-dimensional commutative
algebra such as the algebra `F[y']` generated by one element. This is Step 1 of the proof of
Lemma 1 in Kneser, *Lectures on Galois Cohomology of Classical Groups* (1969), §5.5, printed
pp. 72–74 (PDF pp. 78–80), where `F[y']` is a product of fields (a separable algebra) and the
norm equation is solved componentwise. Here `F[y']` need not be separable; this is handled
by working modulo maximal ideals and lifting through the nilradical
(`exists_sq_sub_mul_sq_eq_of_maximal`). The three local alternatives correspond to the three
situations of Step 3 there, printed pp. 74–75 (PDF pp. 80–81): a split place, an element
close to `1`, and an integral unit at an unramified place.
