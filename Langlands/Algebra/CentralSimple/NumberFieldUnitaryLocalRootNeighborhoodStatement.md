# Unitary roots of nearby conjugate-reciprocal polynomials

Module: `Langlands.Algebra.CentralSimple.NumberFieldUnitaryLocalRootNeighborhoodStatement`.

`Langlands.CentralSimple.NumberFieldUnitaryLocalRootNeighborhoodStatement.{u}` is a
proposition; this module states it and does not prove it.

## The statement

The data are those of `Langlands.CentralSimple.NumberFieldUnitaryNormTorusStatement`: a number
field `F`, a separable quadratic extension `E/F`, a finite-dimensional central simple
`E`-algebra `B` that is an `F`-algebra through `E`, and an involution
`τ : FirstKindInvolution F B` acting on `E` by `SU3Root.conjugation F E`, the nontrivial
automorphism `e ↦ ē` of `E/F`. Moreover `n` is a natural number with
`Module.finrank E B = n ^ 2`, `z ∈ E`, `v : Global.Place F` is a place of `F` (archimedean or
finite) with actual completion `F_v = Global.placeCompletion F v`, and
`b : Module.Basis (Fin 2) F E` is an `F`-basis of `E`. Write

* `τ_v = τ.baseChange F_v`, the involution `id ⊗ τ` of `F_v ⊗[F] B`;
* `ψ : F_v ⊗[F] E → F_v ⊗[F] B` for
  `Algebra.TensorProduct.map (AlgHom.id F_v F_v) (IsScalarTower.toAlgHom F E B)`;
* `c_v` for `Algebra.TensorProduct.map (AlgHom.id F_v F_v) (conjugation F E).toAlgHom`;
* a polynomial `g : (F_v ⊗[F] E)[X]` is *conjugate-reciprocal* (for `z`) when
  `g.coeff (n - i) = (-1) ^ n * (1 ⊗ z) * c_v (g.coeff i)` for every `i ≤ n`;
* the *coordinates* of `g` are `fun (i : Fin n) (j : Fin 2) =>
  (Algebra.TensorProduct.basis F_v b).repr (g.coeff i) j`, the coordinates of the
  coefficients of `X⁰, …, X^{n-1}` in the `F_v`-basis `1 ⊗ b j` of `F_v ⊗[F] E`. The set
  `Fin n → Fin 2 → F_v` carries the product topology.

The hypotheses on `x : F_v ⊗[F] B` and `f : (F_v ⊗[F] E)[X]` are: `x * τ_v x = 1`; `f` is
monic of natural degree `n` and conjugate-reciprocal; `f.eval₂ ψ x = 0`;
`(derivative f).eval₂ ψ x` is a unit; and for every `c : Fin n → F_v ⊗[F] E`,
`∑ i, ψ (c i) * x ^ i = 0` implies `c = 0`. These are exactly the properties of the pair
`(x, f)` in the conclusion of
`Langlands.CentralSimple.NumberFieldUnitaryRegularLocalStatement`.

The conclusion asserts an open set `U` of `Fin n → Fin 2 → F_v` containing the coordinates of
`f` such that every monic conjugate-reciprocal `g : (F_v ⊗[F] E)[X]` of natural degree `n`
whose coordinates lie in `U` has an element `y : F_v ⊗[F] B` with `y * τ_v y = 1`,
`g.eval₂ ψ y = 0`, and `∑ i, ψ (c i) * y ^ i = 0 → c = 0` for every
`c : Fin n → F_v ⊗[F] E`.

## Provenance (derived)

Platonov and Rapinchuk, *Algebraic Groups and Number Theory* (1994), source
`platonov-rapinchuk-1994-algebraic-groups-number-theory`, proof of Theorem 6.28, printed
p. 364 (PDF p. 374), the unitary version of Krasner's lemma: for a regular unitary `x_v` with
characteristic polynomial `f_v`, "if `f(t) = tⁿ + a_{n-1}t^{n-1} + ⋯ + a_0` is sufficiently
close to `f_v` … and if the conditions in (6.44) are satisfied, then there exists a root
`x ∈ (L ⊗_K K_v)[x_v]` of `f` such that `(L ⊗_K K_v)[x] = (L ⊗_K K_v)[x_v]` and
`x τ(x) = 1`". The book proves it by the openness of the characteristic-polynomial map on
the unitary elements of `(L ⊗_K K_v)[x_v]` at a regular point (Lemma 6.25 and Proposition 3.3).
Kneser, *Lectures on Galois Cohomology of Classical Groups* (1969), source
`kneser-1969-galois-cohomology-classical-groups`, §5.6, Steps 1–2, printed pp. 80–81
(PDF pp. 86–87), obtains the same conclusion by Newton's method and the finiteness of the set
of involutions of a separable algebra.

The Lean statement differs from the sources' formulation as follows. The relations (6.44)
with `a_0 = (-1)ⁿ z`, in the convention `g = ∑ g_i Xⁱ`, are the conjugate-reciprocal
condition `g_{n-i} = (-1)ⁿ z ḡ_i`, here imposed in `F_v ⊗[F] E`. "Sufficiently close" is made
precise as membership in an open neighbourhood of the coefficient coordinates of `f` for the
basis induced by `b`. The regularity of `x_v` is written as the hypotheses that `f`
annihilates `x`, that `f'(x)` is invertible and that `1, x, …, x^{n-1}` are independent over
`F_v ⊗[F] E`, and the conclusion keeps the independence of `1, y, …, y^{n-1}`, which is what
the source's equality `(L ⊗_K K_v)[y] = (L ⊗_K K_v)[x_v]` is used for (an embedding of
`(L ⊗_K K_v)[t]/(g)`). The conclusion does not assert that `y` lies in the subalgebra
generated by `x`.
