# Complete reducibility for Jordan-closed linear Lie algebras

Author: Worker 1429.

This module is in the namespace `Langlands.Algebra`. Here `k` is an
algebraically closed field of characteristic zero. The algebra `h` is a Lie
subalgebra of `Module.End k V` for a finite-dimensional `k`-vector space `V`,
and `V` is a Lie module of `h` through the inclusion.

## Main theorem

`complementedLattice_lieSubmodule_of_jordanClosed` assumes two things:

- `hnil`: every Lie ideal of `h` whose elements are all nilpotent endomorphisms
  of `V` is `⊥`;
- `hjordan`: for every `x ∈ h`, the semisimple Jordan part
  `Langlands.LinearAlgebra.jordanSemisimplePart x` also lies in `h`.

It concludes `ComplementedLattice (LieSubmodule k h V)`: every `h`-invariant
subspace of `V` has an `h`-invariant complement.

### Provenance

This statement is internal. It is the Lie-algebra step in the proof of
`Langlands.GroupScheme.ReductiveCharZeroSemisimplicityStatement`, where `h` is
the image of the Lie algebra of a reductive group. The two hypotheses abstract
what the group side supplies. It combines standard facts. One is Lie's theorem,
showing that `⁅h, rad h⁆` acts nilpotently. The other is the semisimplicity
criterion for representations of reductive Lie algebras in characteristic
zero, proved in `Langlands.Algebra.WeylCompleteReducibility` (cf. Bourbaki,
*Lie Groups and Lie Algebras*, Chapter I, §§5–6).

## Declarations and proofs

- `isNilpotent_toEnd_of_mem_lie_radical`: let `L` be a finite-dimensional Lie
  algebra over `k`, and `M` a finite-dimensional Lie module. Every element of
  `⁅⊤, radical k L⁆` acts on `M` by a nilpotent endomorphism. The proof is by
  strong induction on `finrank k M`, and the case `M = 0` is trivial. Otherwise
  the radical is solvable. Lie's theorem
  (`exists_nontrivial_weightSpace_of_isSolvable`) gives a weight `χ` of the
  radical with nonzero weight space. This weight space is an `L`-submodule `N`
  (`weightSpaceOfIsLieTower`). On `N`, each `r` in the radical acts by the
  scalar `χ r`. The Leibniz rule then shows that every `⁅x, r⁆` acts by zero on
  `N`. Apply the induction hypothesis to `M ⧸ N`, whose dimension is smaller.
  It gives `b` such that `(toEnd y)^b` maps `M` into `N`. Hence
  `(toEnd y)^(b+1) = 0`.
- `complementedLattice_lieSubmodule_of_jordanClosed`, proved in three steps.
  1. Apply the previous lemma to the module `V` of `h`. Then `⁅⊤, radical k h⁆`
     is an ideal of nilpotent endomorphisms. By `hnil` it is `⊥`, so the
     radical is central. Mathlib's `hasCentralRadical_of_radical_le` then gives
     `HasCentralRadical k h`.
  2. Let `z` be central in `h` and `s` its semisimple Jordan part. By
     `jordanSemisimplePart_properties`, `s` lies in the subalgebra generated by
     `z`, and `z - s` is nilpotent. Every element of `h` commutes with `z`, and
     hence with `s` and with `n = z - s`. By `hjordan`, `n ∈ h`, so `n` is
     central in `h`. The line `k ∙ n` is therefore a Lie ideal of nilpotent
     endomorphisms, and `hnil` gives `n = 0`. Thus `z = s` is semisimple.
  3. Apply `WeylCompleteReducibility.complementedLattice_of_isSemisimple_center`.
