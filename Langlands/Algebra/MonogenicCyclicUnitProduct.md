# Cyclic unit products over a compact discrete valuation ring

A periodic automorphism of a finite free algebra over a compact Hausdorff discrete
valuation ring acts semilinearly through a continuous automorphism of the base.
If some scalar has pairwise unit differences between its designated cyclic conjugates,
every fixed unit of the algebra is a cyclic product of a unit.

The power of the maximal ideal at each stage extends to an invariant ideal of the
algebra. The quotient is finite because it is module-finite over the corresponding
finite base quotient. The induced automorphism and the scalar reduced to this quotient
satisfy the finite-ring cyclic product theorem. Lifting a quotient solution to the
algebra shows that the set of solutions to each congruence is nonempty. These sets
are nested and closed in the compact module topology. A point of their intersection
solves the product equation because the extended ideals intersect in zero, as is
seen by taking basis coordinates. The product is a unit and contains the point as a
factor, so the point is a unit.

For a monic polynomial over a base ring, extending the coefficients gives an
adjoined-root algebra with the power basis. Its coefficient automorphism fixes
the root. The relation expressing the polynomial as `X * f.divX + C (f.coeff 0)`
shows that the root is a unit when the constant coefficient is a unit. The
finite free result then applies to this root. These results are derived for
use on the integral polynomial of a matrix in the unit fundamental lemma.
