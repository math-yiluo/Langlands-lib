# Finiteness of the parabolic projection of an enveloping centre

Module `Langlands.Algebra.ParabolicCenterProjectionFiniteStatement`, namespace
`Langlands.Algebra.ParabolicCenterProjection`. The module states one proposition and
proves nothing.

## The data

`ParabolicCenterProjectionFiniteStatement.{v,w}` quantifies over

- a finite-dimensional complex Lie algebra `g : Type v`;
- a complex Lie algebra `m : Type w` with a Lie algebra map `j : m →ₗ⁅ℂ⁆ g`;
- an element `H : g`.

Its hypotheses are:

1. `LieAlgebra.radical ℂ g = LieAlgebra.center ℂ g`. This is the definition of a
   reductive Lie algebra in Humphreys, *Introduction to Lie Algebras and
   Representation Theory* (GTM 9), §19.1, printed p. 102 (PDF p. 115), and Exercise 6.5,
   printed p. 30 (PDF p. 43): the radical (Mathlib's `LieAlgebra.radical`, the largest
   solvable ideal) equals the centre.
2. `Submodule.span ℂ {Y | ∃ k : ℤ, ⁅H, Y⁆ = (k : ℂ) • Y} = ⊤`: the eigenvectors of
   `ad H` with integer eigenvalues span `g`, so `ad H` is diagonalizable with integer
   eigenvalues and `g` is the direct sum of the eigenspaces `g_k`, `k ∈ ℤ`.
3. `j` is injective and its range is exactly the centralizer `{X | ⁅H, X⁆ = 0} = g_0`
   of `H`. Thus `m` is identified with the Levi subalgebra `g_0` of the parabolic
   subalgebra `g_0 ⊕ n`, where `n = ⨁_{k>0} g_k`.

`positiveRightIdeal H` is the complex span of the products `ι(Y) u` in the universal
enveloping algebra `U(g)`, where `Y` is an eigenvector of `ad H` for a positive integer
eigenvalue `k + 1` and `u ∈ U(g)` is arbitrary. Since `ι` is linear, this is the right
ideal `n U(g)` generated by `n`.

## The conclusion

For every complex algebra map `p : Z(U(g)) →ₐ[ℂ] Z(U(m))` between the centres of the
enveloping algebras such that, for every central `z`,

```
z - U(j)(p z) ∈ n U(g),
```

where `U(j) = UniversalEnvelopingAlgebra.lift ℂ (ι ∘ j)` is the enveloping algebra
map of `j`, the map `p` is finite (`AlgHom.Finite`): `Z(U(m))` is a finitely generated
module over `Z(U(g))` through `p`.

## Relation to the sources

Both sources put the nilradical on the right, in the left ideal `𝔊n = U(g) n`; the
statement here uses the right ideal `n U(g)`. The two conventions are exchanged by the
principal antiautomorphism `τ` of `U(g)`, the antiautomorphism that is `X ↦ -X` on
`g`: for `Y ∈ n` and `u ∈ U(g)` one has `τ(u Y) = -Y τ(u)`, so `τ(U(g) n) = n U(g)`.
Since `τ` preserves `g_0`, it restricts to the principal antiautomorphism of `U(g_0)`
on the subalgebra generated by `g_0`. On the commutative centres `Z(U(g))` and
`Z(U(m))` the antiautomorphisms `τ` are algebra automorphisms.

Harish-Chandra, *Two theorems on semi-simple Lie groups*, Ann. of Math. 83 (1966),
registered as `provided-3315156dfd5120bb69df4005d1cb66c074532e8f8ab3cd2459c5990658370e91`,
Lemma 13 and its corollary, printed pp. 95–96 (PDF pp. 22–23), considers the
centralizer `m` of a split abelian subalgebra `𝔞` and the nilradical `n` of the
corresponding parabolic. Lemma 13 says that every element `u` of the enveloping algebra
commuting with `𝔞` has a unique `v` in the subalgebra generated by `m` with
`u - v ∈ 𝔊n`. Its proof uses the Poincaré–Birkhoff–Witt bijection
`q ⊗ m ⊗ p ↦ q m p` with the factor `p` from the enveloping algebra of `n` placed last.
Applying `τ` to that congruence, or ordering the PBW factors from `n` first, gives the
analogous uniqueness modulo `n U(g)`: for an ordered basis adapted to
`g = n ⊕ g_0 ⊕ (⨁_{k<0} g_k)`, the map `U(j)` is injective and its image meets
`n U(g)` only in `0`. In particular a map `p` as in the hypothesis of the present
statement is unique. Here a single element `H` with integral eigenvalues plays the role
of `𝔞`: its centralizer is the Levi factor and its positive eigenspaces form the
nilradical.

Harish-Chandra, *Automorphic Forms on Semisimple Lie Groups* (LNM 62, 1968), registered
as `provided-d0ff4732ff6b5d8ecf8562752577df639622e8aad2899ef307bc320213b534af`,
Chapter I, §6, printed p. 19 (PDF p. 29), defines through the Harish-Chandra
isomorphisms `Z(U(g)) ≅ I(𝔥_c)` and `Z(U(m_1)) ≅ I_1(𝔥_c)` (invariants of the Weyl
groups `W ⊇ W_1` in the symmetric algebra of a Cartan subalgebra of `m_1`) an injective
homomorphism `μ` which makes the centre of `U(m_1)` a free module of finite rank over
`μ(Z(U(g)))`. Write `s` for the shift `X ↦ X' = X + ½ tr_n(ad X)` of `Z(U(m_1))`; it
is an automorphism because the trace of a commutator on `n` vanishes. The source states
the congruence `z - s(μ(z)) ∈ 𝔊n` for central `z`. The map of the present statement is
the transport of `s ∘ μ` to the right ideal `n U(g)`:

```
p = τ ∘ s ∘ μ ∘ τ.
```

Indeed, applying `τ` to `τ z - s(μ(τ z)) ∈ 𝔊n` gives `z - τ(s(μ(τ z))) ∈ n U(g)`. The
maps `τ` on both centres and `s` are algebra automorphisms, and finiteness is invariant
under composition with automorphisms. In the setting of the source, the finiteness
assertion of Harish-Chandra (1968) for `μ` is therefore the proposition for `p`, without
the half-trace normalization and with `n` on the left.

The sources work with real semisimple Lie groups and the complexifications of their
Lie algebras. The statement here is purely algebraic: any finite-dimensional reductive
complex Lie algebra with any integral grading element. Its content is the classical
argument behind both sources. A Cartan subalgebra `𝔥` of `g_0` containing `H` is a
Cartan subalgebra of `g`. After the normalization by `τ` and the shift, the
Harish-Chandra isomorphisms of `g` and of `g_0` are compatible with `p`: they identify
`p` with the inclusion `S(𝔥)^{W_g} ⊆ S(𝔥)^{W_m}` of the Weyl group invariants, where `W_m ⊆ W_g`
is the Weyl group of `g_0`. Finally `S(𝔥)^{W_m}` is finite as a module over
`S(𝔥)^{W_g}`.

## Use

The proposition supplies the finiteness of the parabolic restriction of enveloping
centres used in Mœglin–Waldspurger, *Spectral Decomposition and Eisenstein Series*
(1995), I.2.17, printed pp. 37–38 (PDF pp. 20–21), for the actual archimedean Lie
algebras of a parabolic subgroup over a number field.
