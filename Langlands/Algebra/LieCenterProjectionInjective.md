# Central elements in the positive right ideal vanish

Module `Langlands.Algebra.LieCenterProjectionInjective`, namespace
`Langlands.Algebra.LieCenterProjectionInjective`.

The setting is that of `Langlands.Algebra.LieWeightCenterProjection`: a Lie algebra `L` over a
field `k` of characteristic zero with a finite basis `b : Basis I k L`, real gradings
`d : I → ℝ` with `d = ψ ∘ ev` for an additive `ψ : k →+ ℝ`, and an element `H` with
`⁅H, b a⁆ = ev a • b a`. The positive right ideal `positiveWeightRightIdeal b d` is spanned by
the products `ι (b a) * u` with `d a > 0`. Symbols are those of
`Langlands.Algebra.LieEnvelopingSymbol` for the PBW order of decreasing grading.

## Definitions and auxiliary results

- `letterWeight d a` is `0` if `d a = 0` and `1` otherwise: it counts the letters of nonzero
  grading in a monomial.
- `weightedHomogeneousComponent_ξ`, `component_adDer_X_eq_zero`: the linear forms `ξ x` have
  components only in letter weights `0` and `1`, so each `adDer y` shifts the letter weight by
  at most one, as required by `MvPolynomialDerivationGrading.eq_zero_of_derivations`.
- `ξ_injective`: `ξ b` is injective.
- `derivation_apply_eq_sum_pderiv`: a derivation `D` of `MvPolynomial I k` (`I` finite) satisfies
  `D p = ∑ i, pderiv i p * D (X i)`, by induction on `p`.

## Main results

- `center_eq_zero_of_mem_positiveWeightRightIdeal`. Assume the nondegeneracy hypothesis `hnd`:
  every nonzero polynomial all of whose monomials contain a variable of positive grading is not
  annihilated by `shiftPart (letterWeight d) (adDer b y) (-1)` for some `y`. Then every central
  element `z` of `U(L)` in the positive right ideal is zero.

  Proof. The ideal lies in the span of the ordered monomials having a positive letter
  (`positiveWeightRightIdeal_le_positivePBWSpan`), so every monomial of every symbol of `z`
  contains a positive variable. Since `z` is central, `symbol_commutator_adDer` gives
  `adDer y (symbol N z) = symbol N (ι y * z - z * ι y) = 0` for `z ∈ U_N`. The lowest-component
  argument `eq_zero_of_derivations` gives `symbol N z = 0`. By
  `mem_degreeFiltration_of_symbol_eq_zero` and induction on `N`, `z = 0`.

- `exists_shiftPart_ne_zero` derives the hypothesis `hnd` from an invariant form. The data are
  weights `wt : I → Γ` in an additive group and a nondegenerate form `B` with:
  `B (b e) (b i) ≠ 0` only if `wt e = -wt i` and `d e = -d i`; the coordinate of
  `⁅b e, b i⁆` at `c` is nonzero only if `d c = d e + d i`; for `d e < 0 < d i` and `d c = 0`,
  that coordinate is `-B (b e) (b i)` times the coordinate of a vector `T (wt i)`, nonzero when
  `d i > 0` and with coordinates only on letters of grading zero.

  Proof. On the variables, the weight-lowering part of `adDer (b e)` (`d e < 0`) is
  `X i ↦ -B (b e) (b i) • ξ (T γ)` for `d i > 0` and `0` otherwise, where `γ = -wt e`. Given a
  nonzero `t` with a monomial `α₀` containing a positive variable `i₀`, put `γ = wt i₀` and
  `μ = α₀ - single i₀ 1`. The coefficient of `μ` in `pderiv i₀ t` is nonzero in characteristic
  zero (`coeff_pderiv`). The vector `v = ∑ (coeff μ (pderiv i t)) • b i`, over positive `i` of
  weight `γ`, is nonzero, so some `B (b e) v ≠ 0`; this `e` has `d e < 0` and `wt e = -γ`. By
  `derivation_apply_eq_sum_pderiv` the lowering part of `adDer (b e)` sends `t` to
  `-(ξ (T γ) * S)`, where `S = ∑ B (b e) (b i) • pderiv i t` has coefficient `B (b e) v ≠ 0` at
  `μ`. As `ξ (T γ) ≠ 0` and the polynomial ring is a domain, the image is nonzero.

## Provenance

This is the injectivity of the Harish-Chandra homomorphism on the centre, in the form used for
the projection modulo the right ideal generated by the positive part: a central element whose
projection vanishes is zero. The classical proofs (for semisimple Lie algebras, through Verma
modules or through the Chevalley restriction theorem) are not followed. The argument here is
internal: the symbol of a central element is a polynomial annihilated by the derivations of the
adjoint action, and its lowest component in the number of non-Cartan letters is detected by the
pairing of root vectors of opposite weights under an invariant form.
