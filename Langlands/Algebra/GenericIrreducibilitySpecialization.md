# Tensor containment for geometric irreducibility specialization

Worker 336. This internal supporting module extends the uniform power
containment in `GenericIrreducibilityHypersurface` to scalar extensions.
It is a derived algebraic argument for the affine spreading proof, rather than
a separate assertion from the literature or a proof of the full spreading target.

Let R be a domain, C and K commutative R-algebras, S an R-subalgebra of C, and
T a K-subalgebra of K tensor_R C containing the image of S. Suppose g belongs
to S and every c in C satisfies `(g^m c)^(q^e)` in S for some m, where q is
the exponential characteristic of R and e is fixed. Then every tensor satisfies
the corresponding containment in T, with the same exponent q^e.

The proof uses induction on tensors. A pure tensor contributes a scalar
coefficient already in T. For a sum, the sum of the two denominator exponents
clears both terms; raising to q^e is additive. The latter identity survives
arbitrary scalar extension: if q is one it is immediate, and otherwise the
prime characteristic passes through the ring map. The zero target ring is
handled separately. No field or injectivity hypothesis on R to K is used.

The module also proves that a ring map surjective on prime spectra stays
surjective on prime spectra after arbitrary scalar extension. A field valued
map factors through the residue field of its kernel. Nonemptiness of the
original residue field fibre, followed by a field extension, therefore gives
nonemptiness of every field fibre. Tensor associativity identifies these fibres
after base change. In particular the map from a reduced algebra into its tensor
extension is injective: spectral surjectivity puts its kernel in the
nilradical, and reducedness makes that nilradical zero. This applies
to the integral injective polynomial model after localization.

The module further treats an injective extension R to S for which every
element of S becomes integral after multiplication by a power of a fixed
element r of R. If a reduced R-algebra T makes r invertible, the map from
T into T tensor_R S is injective. The proof localizes both rings at r,
removes the integral multiples using the inverse of r in the localized
base, and applies the spectral-surjectivity result above. A map from the
original tensor into the localized auxiliary tensor fixes T, so injectivity
there implies injectivity in the original tensor.

The polynomial application applies this to the polynomial model generated by an
algebraically independent family z. Its coefficient integrality premise is
transferred from the subalgebra R[z] to the polynomial algebra by lifting
monic polynomials. A map from the actual tensor K tensor_R C into
K[z] tensor_R[z] C sends the evaluated polynomial generators to the left
polynomial generators. This yields injectivity of polynomial evaluation in
the actual tensor. The localized injection lemma and this polynomial
application supply the injection needed in the affine hypersurface argument.

Two final supporting lemmas use contraction of ideals under integral
extensions. For an integral map from a nonzero ring P to a domain D, a
map from D is injective whenever its composite from P is injective.
Indeed, an ideal of D contracting to zero is zero. Applying this to the
monic polynomial quotient `AdjoinRoot F` proves injectivity of its
evaluation map whenever F is prime and evaluation of its coefficients
is injective. These are internal algebraic deductions from the ideal
contraction and monic quotient lemmas in Mathlib.
