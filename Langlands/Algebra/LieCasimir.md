# The algebraic Casimir of an invariant nondegenerate Lie form

This construction is by Worker 94 and Worker 566. Worker 94 supplied the
finite-sum construction and proofs; Worker 566 checked the construction,
added the direct central-element sum formula, and prepared the final pair.
It uses an arbitrary field `R`, an `R`-Lie algebra `L`, and a nondegenerate
`R`-bilinear form `B`.
The centrality declarations assume the concrete invariance equation
`B([x,y],z) = B(x,[y,z])`. The implementation does not need symmetry of
`B`; in particular it applies to the symmetric forms in the intended
archimedean application.

For a finite basis `b`, Mathlib's `B.dualBasis hB b` is denoted by `d`.
It satisfies `B(d i,b j) = if j = i then 1 else 0`; this left-dual
convention does not require symmetry. The finite bases may have
different index types in independent universes. For a Lie homomorphism
`f` into an associative unital `R`-algebra with its
commutator Lie structure, `casimirImage` is the finite sum
`sum_i f(b i) * f(d i)`. This is an element of that actual algebra.

Expanding a vector in `b` proves `form_dual_left_eq_repr`:
`B(d i,x) = b.repr x i`. For another finite basis `c`, the coefficient
of its dual vector `d' j` in `d` is therefore `c.repr (b i) j`.
The equality `dual_change_basis` follows by reconstructing `d' j` from
these coefficients. Expand the first factor of the Casimir sum in `c`,
interchange the two finite sums, and use that equality on the second
factor. This proves `casimirImage_basis_independent`, allowing different
finite index types.

Invariance and antisymmetry of the bracket give
`B([x,y],z) = -B(y,[x,z])`. Consequently the coefficient of `[x,d i]`
at `d j` is the negative of the coefficient of `[x,b j]` at `b i`.
The commutator with a product expands as
`[f(x),f(b i)] * f(d i) + f(b i) * [f(x),f(d i)]`.
Expand the two brackets in their respective dual bases, use the
coefficient identity, and interchange the finite sums. The two sums
cancel. This proves `casimirImage_commutes_generator`.
The centralizer of this element is an `R`-subalgebra, so containment of
the generating range implies that every element of its algebraic
adjoin commutes with the Casimir image.

`enveloping_adjoin_range` derives generation of Mathlib's actual universal
enveloping algebra from its universal property. Corestrict the canonical
Lie map to the subalgebra generated by its range and lift that map.
Composing the lift with inclusion is the identity, since the two algebra
maps agree on all canonical Lie generators. Every enveloping element
therefore belongs to the generating subalgebra.

In finite dimension, `casimir` uses `Module.finBasis` to choose a finite
sum. Its value for every finite basis is `casimir_eq_sum`, independently
of this choice. Generation and the preceding commutator calculation give
`casimir_mem_center`; `centralCasimir` packages this same element in
`Subalgebra.center R (UniversalEnvelopingAlgebra R L)`. No hypothesis
postulates centrality. Its underlying enveloping element is `casimir`
by `centralCasimir_val`, and `centralCasimir_eq_sum` gives its exact
finite-sum formula directly for every finite basis.

Empty finite bases and zero-dimensional Lie algebras are permitted.
With an empty basis the displayed sum is zero, so the basis-independent
Casimir is zero. There is no restriction on the characteristic of `R`.
The construction uses no topology, norm, PBW theorem or Lie-group
specialization.

This is an internal algebraic construction whose provenance is the
displayed finite-sum calculation, dual-basis identities and enveloping
universal property. It imports only Mathlib. It supplies no actual matrix
trace form, orthonormal Cartan basis, differential symbol, elliptic
regularity, compact admissibility or automorphic growth proof.
