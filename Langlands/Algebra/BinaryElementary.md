# Binary elementary matrices

This internal algebraic construction works with actual units of
`Matrix (Fin 2) (Fin 2) D`, for any ring `D : Type u`. It does not assume
that the coefficients commute. `upper x` and `lower x` are the respective
matrices `[1,x;0,1]` and `[1,0;x,1]`, equipped with inverses obtained by
negating `x`. `diagonal a b` is the diagonal matrix for two units of `D`.

`elementary D` is the subgroup generated by all these upper and lower
matrices. The signed interchange `weyl` is their product
`upper 1 * lower (-1) * upper 1`, with matrix `[0,1;-1,0]`.
The multiplication and diagonal conjugation identities are proved directly
on the four matrix entries, retaining the displayed multiplication order.

These are internal concrete matrix constructions, intended as foundations
for the division-algebra branch of local Kneser–Tits. They supply neither
reduced norm nor a comparison with actual algebraic group schemes.

For a division ring, `eliminate` proves the explicit ordered identity

```
lower (-c * a⁻¹) * [a,b;c,d] * upper (-a⁻¹ * b)
  = [a,0;0,d - c * a⁻¹ * b]                 (a ≠ 0).
```

Here matrix multiplication is meant on the underlying matrices, and the
coefficient order is essential. The proof expands the four entries and
uses associativity, inverse cancellation and additive commutativity.
It never commutes two coefficients multiplicatively.

`firstColumn_ne_zero` applies the inverse matrix equation to its `(0,0)`
entry to show that an invertible matrix has a nonzero entry in its first
column. `exists_diagonal_reduction_of_pivot` applies elimination when its
top entry is nonzero. The second resulting diagonal entry is nonzero:
otherwise the `(1,1)` entry of the product with the inverse would be zero
instead of one. Both diagonal entries therefore define actual units of
the division ring.

`exists_diagonal_reduction` proves that for every `g : GL₂ D` there are
`e,f ∈ elementary D` and `a,b : Dˣ` with
`e * g * f = diagonal a b`. When the top pivot vanishes, multiplication
by the elementary matrix `weyl` supplies the bottom entry as pivot.
`exists_elementary_diagonal_elementary` rearranges this to
`g = e * diagonal a b * f`, retaining elementary membership of `e,f`
by closure under inversion.

All results hold at arbitrary universe `u` and in every characteristic.
They are internal algebraic deductions from the ring and division-ring
laws, without an appeal to a determinant or Gaussian-elimination theorem
over a commutative field. They do not assert generation of all of
`GL₂ D` by the elementary subgroup, nor local Kneser–Tits, nor a
reduced-norm description of a special linear group over a division ring.
