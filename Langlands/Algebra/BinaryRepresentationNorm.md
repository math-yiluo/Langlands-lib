# Binary determinant under a coefficient matrix representation

The namespace `Langlands.BinaryElementary.Representation` starts with an
actual ring homomorphism

`f : D →+* Matrix I I R`,

where `D` is a ring, `R` is a commutative ring and `I` is a finite index
type. The three types may live in independent universes. No faithfulness
or splitting hypothesis on `f`, nonemptiness of `I`, or nontriviality of
`R` is needed.

`blockMap f` is the ring homomorphism from binary matrices over `D` to
matrices over `R` indexed by `I ⊕ I`. For `M = [a,b;c,d]`, its value is
the actual block matrix

`[f(a), f(b); f(c), f(d)]`.

The homomorphism laws follow from the two-term formula for binary matrix
multiplication and Mathlib's block-matrix identities. Coefficient products
retain their order; the construction does not assume that `D` is
commutative.

`coefficientNorm f : Dˣ →* Rˣ` maps a coefficient unit through `f` and
takes the determinant of the resulting invertible matrix.
`binaryNorm f : GL₂ D →* Rˣ` maps an actual binary matrix unit through
`blockMap f` and takes its determinant. Thus both characters are
constructed from the supplied representation rather than assumed as
extra functions or laws. The lemmas `coefficientNorm_val` and
`binaryNorm_val` give their determinant formulas on underlying values.

The represented upper and lower elementary matrices have identity
diagonal blocks and a zero block below or above the diagonal,
respectively. The determinant of each is therefore one by Mathlib's
block-triangular determinant formulas. `elementary_le_ker` extends this to
the actual subgroup generated by both families using the universal
property of subgroup closure. Direct block-diagonal evaluation also
gives

`binaryNorm f (diagonal a b) = coefficientNorm f a * coefficientNorm f b`.

These constructions and conclusions require only `Ring D`.

Now suppose `D` is a division ring. The theorem `binaryNorm_eq` proves
the precise comparison with the constructed binary Dieudonné determinant:

`binaryNorm f g = Abelianization.lift (coefficientNorm f) (Dieudonne.determinant g)`.

Its proof writes `g` as elementary times diagonal times elementary.
Both characters kill the two elementary factors, the binary determinant
of `diagonal a b` is the product of the abelianized coefficient classes,
and the lifted coefficient character evaluates that product to the
displayed block-diagonal determinant. The theorem `binaryNorm_eq_comp`
states the same equality as an equality of group homomorphisms.

Finally, `elementary_eq_ker_iff` proves

`elementary D = (binaryNorm f).ker ↔ (coefficientNorm f).ker = commutator Dˣ`.

For the forward implication, apply the assumed binary kernel equality
to `diagonal a 1` for each coefficient unit whose represented determinant
is one. The diagonal commutator criterion from `BinaryDieudonne` puts
`a` in the coefficient commutator subgroup. The reverse inclusion holds
because a character into the commutative group `Rˣ` kills commutators.

For the reverse implication, induction on coefficient abelianization
shows that the lifted coefficient character has trivial kernel: an
element in its kernel has a coefficient representative in the
commutator subgroup. The comparison theorem then sends every member of
the binary norm kernel to the identity under the Dieudonné determinant.
Its already proved exact kernel theorem puts that binary matrix in the
actual elementary subgroup. The other inclusion is `elementary_le_ker`.

This is an internal algebraic deduction from the explicit block-matrix
construction, Mathlib's ordinary determinant and abelianization, and the
imported internal binary elementary and Dieudonné determinant results.
It makes no assertion that an arbitrary representation realizes a
reduced norm. In an arithmetic application, construction of a splitting
representation, its comparison with the actual reduced norm and the
local coefficient commutator-kernel equality remain separate proof
obligations. No local SK₁ vanishing, group-scheme comparison or proof of
`LocalKneserTitsStatement` is supplied here.
