# The positive part of a ℤ-graded augmented algebra

Author: Worker 1389 designed and implemented this module and wrote this note, as
part of task `90ffbd89-a3dd-4baa-84ba-332896bbbd17` (split unipotence from the
absence of zero Lie weights). No other worker's draft was reused. It builds on
`Langlands.Algebra.GradedHopf.Quotient` and `Langlands.Algebra.GradedHopf.LaurentCoaction`
(same author) and applies the Library theorem
`Langlands.Algebra.GradedFormallySmooth.exists_homogeneous_aeval_bijective`
(module `Langlands.CommutativeAlgebra.GradedFormallySmoothPolynomial`).

Provenance: internal. The quotient constructed here is the algebraic counterpart of the
unipotent subgroup attached to a cocharacter in the dynamic method: only the
positive-degree part of `A` survives. The module does not formalize that geometric
connection. It proves the algebraic part: under the hypotheses below the quotient is a
polynomial ring on generators of positive degree. In the intended application `ε` is the
counit of a graded Hopf algebra. The hypothesis `h0` then says that the cotangent space
`I / I²` at the identity (`I = ker ε`) has no component of degree `0`.

## Regrading along an additive equivalence

Let `𝒜 : ι → Submodule k A` be a grading (`GradedAlgebra 𝒜`) of a commutative
`k`-algebra by an additive commutative monoid, and `φ : ι' ≃+ ι` an additive equivalence.

- `regradedAlgebra 𝒜 φ : GradedAlgebra (fun i' => 𝒜 (φ i'))` is the grading obtained by
  reindexing. The summands are independent and span `A` because those of `𝒜` do, so the
  family is internal (`DirectSum.isInternal_submodule_of_iSupIndep_of_iSup_eq_top`). The
  decomposition is the one chosen from this (`IsInternal.chooseDecomposition`). It is a
  `def`, not a global instance.
- `gradeProj_regrade`: for any grading instance on `fun i' => 𝒜 (φ i')`, the degree-`i'`
  projection is the degree-`φ i'` projection of `𝒜`.

The intended use is `φ = AddEquiv.neg ℤ`. Applying the results below to the reversed
grading `n ↦ 𝒜 (-n)` gives the negative part.

## The positive ideal

From now on `𝒜 : ℤ → Submodule k A` is a `ℤ`-grading and `ε : A →ₐ[k] k` is an
augmentation. Most results assume

- `hε : ∀ n ≠ 0, ∀ a ∈ 𝒜 n, ε a = 0`: `ε` kills every summand of nonzero degree.

The definitions and lemmas are:

- `positiveIdeal 𝒜 ε` is the ideal generated by the homogeneous elements of negative
  degree and the elements `a ∈ 𝒜 0` with `ε a = 0`. Its quotient `A ⧸ positiveIdeal 𝒜 ε`
  is the *positive part*.
- `mem_positiveIdeal_of_neg` and `mem_positiveIdeal_of_zero`: the generators lie in the
  ideal.
- `negSpan_le_positiveIdeal`: it contains `negSpan 𝒜` (from `LaurentCoaction`).
- `isHomogeneous_positiveIdeal`: it is homogeneous, being generated by homogeneous
  elements (Mathlib's `Ideal.homogeneous_span`).
- `apply_gradeProj_zero hε`: `ε (gradeProj 𝒜 0 a) = ε a`.
- `positiveIdeal_le_ker hε`: the positive ideal lies in the augmentation ideal
  `RingHom.ker ε`.

## Formal smoothness

The second hypothesis is

- `h0 : ∀ a ∈ 𝒜 0, ε a = 0 → a ∈ RingHom.ker ε ^ 2`: the degree-`0` part of the
  augmentation ideal lies in its square.

The results are:

- `gradeProj_zero_mul_mem hε`: if `ε b = ε c = 0`, the degree-`0` component of `b * c`
  lies in `positiveIdeal 𝒜 ε ^ 2 ⊔ negSpan 𝒜`. Write `b` as the sum of its components
  `bₙ`. Then the degree-`0` component of `bₙ * c` is `bₙ * c₋ₙ`. For `n < 0` the factor
  `bₙ` lies in `negSpan`. For `n > 0` the factor `c₋ₙ` does. For `n = 0`, both factors are
  of degree `0` and are killed by `ε` (`apply_gradeProj_zero`), so the product lies in
  the square of the positive ideal.
- `gradeProj_zero_mem_of_mem_sq hε`: the same holds for the degree-`0` component of every
  element of `RingHom.ker ε ^ 2`, by induction over products.
- `positiveIdeal_le_sq_sup hε h0`: `positiveIdeal 𝒜 ε ≤ positiveIdeal 𝒜 ε ^ 2 ⊔ negSpan 𝒜`.
  Negative generators lie in `negSpan`. A degree-`0` generator lies in
  `RingHom.ker ε ^ 2` by `h0` and equals its own degree-`0` component.
- `formallySmooth_positive hε h0`: if `A` is formally smooth over `k`, so is the positive
  part. This combines `formallySmooth_quotient_negSpan` and
  `formallySmooth_quotient_of_le_sq_sup` from `LaurentCoaction`.

## Augmentation and grading of the positive part

- `positiveCounit hε : A ⧸ positiveIdeal 𝒜 ε →ₐ[k] k` is the augmentation induced by `ε`
  (`positiveCounit_mk`). `nontrivial_positive hε` says that the positive part is nontrivial
  when `k` is.
- `positiveGrade 𝒜 ε : ℕ → Submodule k (A ⧸ positiveIdeal 𝒜 ε)` is
  `quotGrade 𝒜 (positiveIdeal 𝒜 ε) (Nat.castAddMonoidHom ℤ)`: its degree-`n` member is
  the image of `𝒜 n`.
- `not_mem_range_natCast`: an integer that is not a natural number is negative.
- `positiveGradedAlgebra : GradedAlgebra (positiveGrade 𝒜 ε)` is `quotGradedAlgebra` for
  the homogeneous ideal `positiveIdeal 𝒜 ε` and the injective map `ℕ → ℤ`. Negative
  summands lie in the ideal. It is a `def`, installed locally where needed.
- `positiveGrade_zero`: the degree-`0` member is the image of `k`
  (`LinearMap.range (Algebra.linearMap k _)`). Every `a ∈ 𝒜 0` is congruent to the scalar
  `ε a`, since `a - ε a` is a degree-`0` generator of the positive ideal.

## Polynomial structure

`exists_positive_aeval_bijective`: let `k` be a field, `A` a finitely generated
(`Algebra.FiniteType`) and formally smooth commutative `k`-algebra with a `ℤ`-grading
`𝒜`, and `ε` an augmentation satisfying `hε` and `h0`. Then there are `n`, degrees
`d : Fin n → ℕ` with every `d i > 0`, and elements `x i ∈ positiveGrade 𝒜 ε (d i)` of the
positive part such that `MvPolynomial.aeval x : MvPolynomial (Fin n) k → A ⧸ positiveIdeal 𝒜 ε`
is bijective. The proof installs `positiveGradedAlgebra` and applies
`exists_homogeneous_aeval_bijective` with `positiveGrade_zero`, `nontrivial_positive` and
`formallySmooth_positive`. The positive part is finitely generated as a quotient of `A`.
