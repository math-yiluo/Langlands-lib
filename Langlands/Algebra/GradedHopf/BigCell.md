# The big cell of a graded bialgebra

Author: Worker 1389 designed and implemented this module and wrote this note, as
part of task `90ffbd89-a3dd-4baa-84ba-332896bbbd17` (split unipotence from the
absence of zero Lie weights). No other worker's draft was reused. It builds on
`Langlands.Algebra.GradedHopf.Positive`, `Langlands.Algebra.GradedHopf.Quotient`,
`Langlands.Algebra.GradedHopf.Basic`, `Langlands.Algebra.GradedHopf.TensorGrading` and
`Langlands.Algebra.GradedHopf.Unitriangular` (same author). It also uses Mathlib's
`MvPolynomial.tensorEquivSum`, `Coalgebra.Repr` and Nakayama's lemma
(`Submodule.exists_sub_one_mem_and_smul_eq_zero_of_fg_of_le_smul`).

Provenance: internal. The geometric counterpart is the statement that multiplication
`U⁻ × U⁺ → G` from the two attracting subgroups of a `𝔾ₘ`-action is an isomorphism. This
module proves the corresponding Hopf-algebraic statement under the explicit hypotheses
below. It then deduces that the coordinate algebra is a polynomial algebra on homogeneous
generators of nonzero degree. The module does not formalize the geometric interpretation.
The proofs described below are the implemented ones.

## The reversed grading

For a `ℤ`-grading `𝒜` of a commutative `k`-algebra `A`:

- `negGrade 𝒜 n = 𝒜 (-n)`;
- `negGradedAlgebra` (instance) is its grading, `regradedAlgebra 𝒜 (AddEquiv.neg ℤ)` from
  `Positive`;
- `gradeProj_negGrade`: `gradeProj (negGrade 𝒜) n a = gradeProj 𝒜 (-n) a`.

## The big-cell map

From now on `A` is a commutative `k`-bialgebra (`Bialgebra k A`) with a grading `𝒜`. The
counit as an algebra map is Mathlib's `Bialgebra.counitAlgHom k A`.

- `plusIdeal 𝒜 = positiveIdeal 𝒜 (counitAlgHom k A)`. Its quotient `A⁺ = A ⧸ plusIdeal 𝒜`
  keeps the nonnegative degrees.
- `minusIdeal 𝒜 = positiveIdeal (negGrade 𝒜) (counitAlgHom k A)`. It is generated by the
  homogeneous elements of positive degree and the degree-`0` elements killed by the counit.
  Its quotient is `A⁻ = A ⧸ minusIdeal 𝒜`.
- `bigCellMap 𝒜 : A →ₐ[k] A⁻ ⊗[k] A⁺` is the comultiplication (`Bialgebra.comulAlgHom`)
  followed by the tensor product of the two quotient maps. `bigCellMap_apply` writes it as
  `TensorProduct.map` of the quotient maps applied to `comul a`.
- `mk_minus_of_pos` and `mk_plus_of_neg`: homogeneous elements of positive degree vanish in
  `A⁻`, and those of negative degree vanish in `A⁺`. `mk_minus_of_zero` and
  `mk_plus_of_zero`: an element `a` of degree `0` is the scalar `counit a` in both
  quotients.

## Matrix coefficients and surjectivity

`GradedMatrixCoefficients 𝒜` is a structure with the following fields:

- `n : ℕ`, `w : Fin n → ℤ` and `M : Matrix (Fin n) (Fin n) A`;
- `comul_apply`: `comul (M i j) = ∑ l, M i l ⊗ₜ M l j`;
- `counit_apply`: `counit (M i j)` is `1` if `i = j` and `0` otherwise;
- `mem`: `M i j ∈ 𝒜 (w j - w i)`;
- `adjoin_eq_top`: the entries of `M` generate `A` as a `k`-algebra.

These are the matrix coefficients of a finite-dimensional comodule with a basis of
homogeneous vectors (of weights `w`) generating the algebra. No triangularity is
required here.

`bigCellMap_surjective G`. Let `L i j = π⁻(M i j) ⊗ 1` and `U i j = 1 ⊗ π⁺(M i j)`, where
`π^±` are the quotient maps. By `comul_apply`, `(L * U) i j = bigCellMap 𝒜 (M i j)`.

- An off-diagonal entry `M i j` with `w j ≥ w i` vanishes in `A⁻`. If `w j > w i`, its
  degree is positive. If `w j = w i`, it has degree `0` and counit `0`. So `L` is lower
  unitriangular with respect to `τ i = (w i + ∑ |w|).toNat`.
- Symmetrically, `U` is upper unitriangular, and the diagonal entries map to `1`.

By `mem_of_mul_mem` (from `Unitriangular`), applied to the subring `range (bigCellMap 𝒜)`,
all entries of `L` and `U` lie in the range. Since the entries of `M` generate `A`, the
range contains `π⁻(a) ⊗ 1` and `1 ⊗ π⁺(a)` for every `a`, hence every pure tensor and
everything.

## The kernel lies in the square of the augmentation ideal

- `gradeProj_mem_sq_of_mem_positiveIdeal hε h0`. This is stated for any augmentation
  `ε : A →ₐ[k] k` killing nonzero degrees (`hε`) and with `h0`: every degree-`0` element
  of `ker ε` lies in `(ker ε) ^ 2`. Then for `x ∈ positiveIdeal 𝒜 ε` and `d ≥ 0`, the
  component `gradeProj 𝒜 d x` lies in `(ker ε) ^ 2`. The proof is span induction on the
  stronger property "for all `a` and `d ≥ 0`, `gradeProj 𝒜 d (a * x) ∈ (ker ε) ^ 2`". For a
  generator `g ∈ 𝒜 e`, that component is `gradeProj 𝒜 (d - e) a * g`. If `e < 0`, both
  factors are killed by `ε` (their degrees are nonzero). If `e = 0`, then `g ∈ (ker ε) ^ 2`
  by `h0`.
- `IsGradedBialgebra.counitAlgHom_eq_zero` and `counitAlgHom_eq_zero_neg`: the counit kills
  the nonzero degrees of `𝒜` and of `negGrade 𝒜`. `zero_neg_of_zero` transports `h0` to
  `negGrade 𝒜`.
- `minusCounit h𝒜` and `plusCounit h𝒜` are the induced augmentations of `A⁻` and `A⁺`.
- `plus_bigCellMap`, `minus_bigCellMap` and `counit_bigCellMap`: applying the augmentation
  of `A⁻`, of `A⁺`, or both, to `bigCellMap 𝒜 a` gives the class of `a` in `A⁺`, its class
  in `A⁻`, and `counit a`, respectively. These are the counit axioms, through
  `Coalgebra.Repr.arbitrary`.
- `ker_bigCellMap_le_plusIdeal`, `ker_bigCellMap_le_minusIdeal`: hence the kernel lies in
  both ideals.
- `mem_sq_of_mem_ker_bigCellMap h𝒜 h0`: every element of the kernel lies in the square of
  the augmentation ideal `RingHom.ker (counitAlgHom k A)`. Split the element into
  components. Those of degree `≥ 0` are handled by the first item for `𝒜`, those of
  negative degree by the same item for `negGrade 𝒜`.

## Bijectivity

`bigCellMap_injective` and `bigCellMap_bijective`. Here `k` is a field, `A` is a domain
that is finitely generated and formally smooth over `k`, `𝒜` is a graded bialgebra
structure (`h𝒜 : IsGradedBialgebra 𝒜`), `h0` holds for the counit, and
`G : GradedMatrixCoefficients 𝒜` is given. Then `bigCellMap 𝒜` is bijective.

- By `exists_positive_aeval_bijective` (from `Positive`), `A⁻` and `A⁺` are polynomial
  algebras, so `A⁻ ⊗ A⁺` is a polynomial algebra (`MvPolynomial.tensorEquivSum`).
- Since `bigCellMap 𝒜` is surjective, it has an algebra section `s`: send each variable to
  a preimage.
- With `P = s ∘ bigCellMap 𝒜`, every `a - P a` lies in the kernel `K`. `P` preserves the
  counit (by `counit_bigCellMap`), so it preserves the augmentation ideal `I`.
- For `y ∈ I ^ 2`, `y - P y ∈ I * K`. For `y = a b` this follows from
  `ab - P(a)P(b) = b (a - P a) + P(a) (b - P b)`.
- For `x ∈ K` we have `x ∈ I ^ 2` and `P x = 0`, so `K ≤ I • K`. `A` is Noetherian, so
  Nakayama's lemma gives `r` with `r - 1 ∈ I` and `r K = 0`. Then `r ≠ 0`, since `1 ∉ I`.
  As `A` is a domain, `K = 0`.

## Gradings of the quotients and homogeneous coordinates

- `minusGrade 𝒜` and `plusGrade 𝒜` are the `ℤ`-indexed images of `𝒜` in `A⁻` and `A⁺`
  (`quotGrade` with `e = AddMonoidHom.id ℤ`).
- `isHomogeneous_minusIdeal`: `minusIdeal 𝒜` is homogeneous for `𝒜`.
- `minusGradedAlgebra` and `plusGradedAlgebra` are the graded algebra instances
  (`quotGradedAlgebra`; no summand needs to be killed).
- `bigCellMap_gradeProj h𝒜`: `bigCellMap 𝒜 (gradeProj 𝒜 n a)` equals the total-degree-`n`
  projection `tensorProj (minusGrade 𝒜) (plusGrade 𝒜) n` of `bigCellMap 𝒜 a`. This follows
  from `IsGradedBialgebra.comul_gradeProj` and `IsGradedMap.map_comp_tensorProj` for the
  graded quotient maps (`isGradedMap_mkₐ`).

`exists_homogeneous_polynomial_coordinates`: under the hypotheses of
`bigCellMap_bijective`, there are `n₁ n₂ : ℕ`, weights `w : Fin n₁ ⊕ Fin n₂ → ℤ`, all
nonzero, and elements `z p ∈ 𝒜 (w p)` such that
`MvPolynomial.aeval z : MvPolynomial (Fin n₁ ⊕ Fin n₂) k → A` is bijective.

- The generators of `A⁻ ⊗ A⁺` are `x⁻ i ⊗ 1` and `1 ⊗ x⁺ j`, with `x⁻ i` of degree
  `-(d₁ i) < 0` and `x⁺ j` of degree `d₂ j > 0`. Each lies in the part of total degree
  `w p`.
- `z p` is the degree-`w p` component of a preimage. By `bigCellMap_gradeProj`, it is still
  a preimage.
- Therefore `bigCellMap 𝒜 ∘ aeval z` is the isomorphism `MvPolynomial → A⁻ ⊗ A⁺`. Since
  `bigCellMap 𝒜` is bijective, so is `aeval z`.
