## Quantifiers and actual objects

For every complex vector space `V : Type u` with explicit `AddCommGroup V`,
`Module ℂ V` and `FiniteDimensional ℂ V` instances, every natural `l`, and
every tuple `a : Fin l → Module.End ℂ V`, assume all entries commute and each
entry is nilpotent. Let `W` be any complex subspace complementary to the actual
first filtration level. The hypothesis
`IsCompl (NilpotentFiltration.idealPowerSubspace a 1) W` means their
intersection is zero and their sum is the whole space. It does not require
`W` to be invariant under the tuple or its generated algebra.

The independently published import
`Langlands.Algebra.RenardNilpotentIdealFiltration` provides the actual unital
subalgebra `R = Algebra.adjoin ℂ (Set.range a)`, the ideal `I` spanned by the
original entries viewed inside `R`, and the actual complex subspaces
`V_n = I^n V` through the original evaluation action. Under tuple commutation,
`R` is commutative, so the imported left ideal is also the source's two-sided
ideal. No replacement algebra, ideal, action or filtration is supplied.

## Two conclusions

The first conjunct states, for every natural `n`,

`V_n = span_ℂ {r(w) | r ∈ I^n, w ∈ W} + V_(n+1)`.

The Lean set uses `r : R`, `w : W`, membership in the actual ideal power, and
evaluation of the original endomorphism underlying `r`. Its complex linear
span expresses that these vectors generate `V_n` modulo `V_(n+1)`.
Submodule supremum is the sum of complex subspaces. This equality also records
that the generating vectors and the next level lie inside the actual `V_n`;
it does not choose a replacement quotient space or assume a dimension bound.

The second conjunct is

`Submodule.span R (W : Set V) = ⊤`.

This is the assertion that the original `R`-module `V` is generated by `W`,
which is the meaning of `R · W = V` in the source. Passing to the `R`-span
does not require a new action on `W`. These are the two successive source
assertions, grouped as one coherent statement. No conclusion about restriction
injectivity or the dimension of `R` is included.

The carrier universe is arbitrary, and the statement covers every complement;
its first conjunct covers every natural filtration index. If `V` has dimension
zero, its only subspace `W` is both bottom and top, so both equalities retain
their ordinary meanings. For an empty tuple, the commutation and nilpotence
hypotheses are vacuous, `I = 0`, every positive filtration level is zero, and
the complement condition forces `W = ⊤`. The same description applies when
all entries are zero. Repeated or redundant entries need not be discarded.
The zeroth ideal power is the unit ideal, so the first assertion at `n = 0`
uses all `r ∈ R`; it is included with its ordinary ideal-action meaning.
These boundary cases and unrestricted indices make the source notation
explicit. The unital adjoin includes the identity and scalars without
counting extra generators. There is no positivity, nontriviality, maximality,
chosen-basis, sharp-bound, supplied-generation or supplied-vanishing
hypothesis.

## Proof and scope boundary

The first assertion is expected from the complement decomposition and
successive ideal actions. The source then uses the vanishing filtration to
obtain generation of the whole module. This records the source proof route;
neither argument is implemented in this statement pair, and vanishing is not
an extra premise of the statement. A future proof may instead use Nakayama
on the same actual algebra and action, with its route documented separately.
