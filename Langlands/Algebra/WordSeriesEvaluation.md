# Evaluation of bounded homogeneous word series

Let `F` be a nontrivially normed ultrametric field and `A` a complete ultrametric normed `F`-algebra. `polynomialEval` evaluates the two letters at a pair in `A`. A word of length `n` has norm bounded by a constant times the `n`th power of the pair radius. Subtracting two evaluations telescopes one letter at a time, giving a corresponding Lipschitz estimate. The ultrametric inequality preserves the estimate over finite sums of words. Evaluating a bounded homogeneous series therefore converges on pair balls of radius less than one. The geometric tail bound also controls series with low coefficients zero.

For a formal unit with constant coefficient one and homogeneous coefficient bound one, its inverse also has bound one. At pair radius `r < 1`, `seriesEvalUnit` evaluates both series. The multiplicative Cauchy product identity makes the two evaluations mutual inverses.

`WordSeriesExponentialEvaluationStatement` further assumes characteristic zero. The input series is length-graded, has constant coefficient zero and has coefficient bound `c ≤ 1`. The pair `x` and radius `r` satisfy `‖x‖ ≤ r < 1` and `max(1, ‖1_A‖) r < 1`. The factorial bound is `‖(k!)⁻¹‖ c^k ≤ c` in the scalar norm for every `k > 0`. Since the exponent has zero constant coefficient, the finite factorial sum agrees through its truncation degree with the formal exponential. The evaluation error tends to zero by the geometric tail bound. The same evaluations are the convergent factorial partial sums of the algebra exponential.
