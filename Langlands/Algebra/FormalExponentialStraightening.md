# Degree-by-degree straightening in the formal word algebra

Let `F` be a characteristic-zero field with a nontrivial ultrametric norm. Work in formal series over the free associative `F`-algebra on two letters. `FormalExponentialStraighteningStatement` takes a nonzero scalar `s` of norm at most one for which `‖(k!)⁻¹‖ ‖s‖^k ≤ ‖s‖` for every positive `k`. It gives two formal unit series of constant coefficient one and coefficient bound one. The coefficient of each degree is supported on words of that length. Conjugate each letter by its corresponding unit, add the conjugates and multiply by `s`. The formal exponential of that sum equals the product of the two scaled letter exponentials in the original order.

The identity holds through degree one with both conjugators equal to one. Suppose it holds in all degrees below `n`. The two sides have the same traced logarithmic derivative by product additivity and invariance of the trace under conjugation. In degree `n` their traced derivative difference is `n` times the trace of their coefficient difference. Characteristic zero allows cancellation of `n`, so the coefficient difference has zero cyclic trace. The coefficient estimates bound that difference by `‖s‖`. Dividing by `s` gives a word polynomial of length `n` and bound one, and the cyclic word splitting writes it as commutators with the two letters.

Multiply each conjugator on the right by one plus its correction in degree `n − 1`. This changes the exponent in degree `n` by the required commutators and fixes all lower degrees. In that first changed degree the formal exponential has the same difference as its exponent. Each stage preserves the bound one, and the coefficients stabilize degree by degree. Taking those stable coefficients produces the two formal units and the identity. This implements the homogeneous correction of the Campbell–Hausdorff straightening argument directly on the exponential equation.
