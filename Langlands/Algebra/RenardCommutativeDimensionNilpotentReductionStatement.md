## The actual objects

`Langlands.Renard.CommutativeDimensionNilpotentReductionStatement` is a
closed proposition with arbitrary carrier universe `u`. It quantifies
over every finite-dimensional complex module `V : Type u`, every natural
generator count `l`, and every pairwise commuting tuple
`a : Fin l → Module.End ℂ V`. The original generators need not be
nilpotent or invertible.

The imported construction in `Langlands.Renard.CommutingReduction` fixes
`jointSpace a χ` as the intersection of the original generators'
generalized eigenspaces at the complex eigenvalue tuple `χ`. Its
`jointBlockIndex a` is exactly the subtype where that space is nonzero.
The unital `blockAlgebra a hcomm χ` is generated by the restrictions of
the original tuple to that actual space. The construction proves that
it is the range of the original algebra's action homomorphism there.
These spaces, supported indices and actions are determined by `a`.

`nilpotentGeneratedPhi l k` is the published actual maximum over
commuting nilpotent `l`-tuples on the canonical complex carrier
`Fin k → ℂ`. Its definition is the natural supremum of the exact set
of possible generated-algebra dimensions. The published construction
proves that this maximum is attained and bounds every such tuple.
The separately published `nilpotentDimensionTransport` identifies the
same maximum on every finite-dimensional complex carrier of dimension
`k`, including carriers in universe `u`.

## The three assertions

First, for every complex tuple `χ`, the dimension of the actual
`blockAlgebra a hcomm χ` is at most
`nilpotentGeneratedPhi l (finrank ℂ (jointSpace a χ))`.
This includes tuples outside the supported subtype. The comparison
uses the canonical maximum on the dimension of that very space,
without replacing the original restricted algebra by a supplied
nilpotent action.

The published `shiftedBlockTuple a hcomm χ` subtracts the scalar
`χ i` from each original restricted generator. Its entries commute
and are nilpotent. The construction's `shiftedBlockTuple_adjoin_eq`
identifies its unital adjoin with the original `blockAlgebra`.
Thus applying the actual maximum inequality to this tuple and then
transporting its carrier gives precisely the first assertion.
This describes the provenance of the statement, not a proof supplied
by this pair.

Second, for every `Fintype` structure on the exact supported subtype,
the dimension of the original
`NilpotentFiltration.generatedAlgebra a` is at most the sum of these
canonical maxima over the supported actual spaces. The original
algebra is `Algebra.adjoin ℂ (Set.range a)`. The asserted sum uses
each actual block's dimension as the maximum's second argument.
Its intended derivation combines the first assertion with the
comparison between the original algebra and the sum of the dimensions
of its actual restriction images.

The universal `Fintype` binder makes the sum explicit without selecting
an enumeration. The actual supported subtype is finite by the
separate joint-decomposition assertion. Neither a finite
decomposition nor its dimension comparison is a premise of the
present target. This pair does not prove those separate assertions
or treat their pending statement as a theorem.

Third, for positive `l`, set
`r = 2 - Real.rpow 2 (1 - (l : ℝ))`. If, for each supported actual
block of dimension `kχ`, its canonical maximum satisfies
`(nilpotentGeneratedPhi l kχ : ℝ) ≤ Real.rpow (kχ : ℝ) r`,
then the dimension of the original generated algebra is at most
`Real.rpow (finrank ℂ V : ℝ) r`. Only the maxima at those actual
block dimensions occur in this premise. A uniform canonical-maximum
bound for every natural dimension would supply that premise.

The source's finite-sum power comparison for `r ≥ 1`, together with
the equality between the sum of actual block dimensions and the
dimension of `V`, explains this implication. Its premises contain
neither the original algebra's desired bound nor a supplied block
decomposition or faithfulness assertion. Both subtractions and the
inner power in `r`, and the outer power in the dimension bound, are
real operations.

## Provenance, boundaries and remaining work

The first assertion makes the source's scalar-subtraction reduction
and immediately following maximum definition explicit on the published
actual objects. The second assertion is their internal linear algebra
consequence combined with faithful restrictions to the spanning
blocks. The third assertion is the source's reduction using its
displayed finite-sum power inequality. These three claims are not
three separately displayed source lemmas.

The first two assertions retain zero-dimensional carriers, empty
tuples, zero and redundant generators, and eigenvalue zero.
On a zero block its endomorphism algebra and canonical maximum both
have dimension zero, so the first assertion also covers unsupported
tuples. On a zero carrier the supported subtype is empty and the
second assertion compares zero with the empty sum. The third
assertion includes the zero carrier: positive `l` gives `r ≥ 1`,
so the corresponding real power of zero is zero.

For `l = 1`, the conditional exponent is one and the finite-sum
comparison is equality. For `l = 0`, the first two assertions remain
present and the final implication has a false positivity premise.
The empty tuple generates the unital scalar algebra; the identity
and scalar operators do not consume tuple entries. No stability
or invertibility assumption is imposed beyond the intrinsic stability
of the actual joint spaces.
