# Binary Dieudonné determinant and elementary quotient

For every division ring `D : Type u`, the namespace
`Langlands.BinaryElementary.Dieudonne` constructs a surjective homomorphism

`determinant : GL₂ D →* Abelianization Dˣ`

whose kernel is exactly `elementary D`. Here `GL₂ D` is the actual unit group
of the binary matrix ring, and `elementary D` is the subgroup generated by
all upper and lower elementary matrices. The quotient is the actual group
`GL₂ D ⧸ elementary D` from `BinaryElementaryQuotient`. There are no
characteristic, centrality, dimension, topology, splitting or supplied
determinant hypotheses.

This is an internal algebraic construction using the ordered matrix
elimination and elementary-quotient results in `BinaryElementary` and
`BinaryElementaryQuotient`, together with Mathlib's abelianization. Its
provenance is the explicit computations and group-theoretic argument below;
no arithmetic generation result is used.

Write an invertible matrix as `g = [a,b;c,d]`. Define the ordered complements

`s = d - c*a⁻¹*b`, and `t = b - a*c⁻¹*d`.

`coefficientClass x` is the image of the coefficient unit `x` in
`Abelianization Dˣ` when `x ≠ 0`, and is assigned the value `1` at zero.
The lemmas `coefficientClass_mul` and `coefficientClass_inv` express its
multiplicative and inverse laws on nonzero coefficients. The arbitrary
zero value is never used as a pivot or complementary factor in the
determinant formulas.

If `a ≠ 0`, the ordered elimination from `BinaryElementary` changes `g`
into `diag(a,s)` by multiplication by explicit elementary matrices.
The inverse-matrix equation at entry `(1,1)` shows `s ≠ 0`:
otherwise that entry of the product of this diagonal matrix with its
inverse would be zero rather than one. Applying the same argument to
`weyl*g` shows `t ≠ 0` whenever `c ≠ 0`. These are
`schur_ne_zero` and `coSchur_ne_zero`.

The function `value` uses `[a][s]` when `a ≠ 0`, and `[-c][t]`
otherwise. The first column cannot vanish, so the second formula has a
nonzero pivot whenever it is needed. On the overlap, direct division-ring
arithmetic gives `t = -a*c⁻¹*s`, hence
`(-c)*t = c*a*c⁻¹*s`. Passing to abelianized coefficient units cancels
the conjugation by `c`. Thus the two formulas agree, as
`pivot_formula_eq` proves. The lemmas `value_of_top` and
`value_of_bottom` permit either formula whenever its pivot is nonzero.
All coefficient products before abelianization retain their displayed
order.

The proof of invariance under left multiplication by `lower x` uses the
top pivot when it is nonzero: `a` and `s` are unchanged by this row
operation. When `a = 0`, the bottom-pivot formula is unchanged directly.
For left multiplication by `upper x`, the proof instead uses the bottom
pivot when nonzero: `c` and `t` are unchanged. When `c = 0`, the top-pivot
formula is unchanged directly. Closure induction extends these two
invariance statements to every member of the actual elementary subgroup.
Normality of that subgroup then also gives right invariance by conjugating
an elementary factor into the left position.

`quotientValue` therefore descends the function `value` to the actual
elementary quotient. In the other direction, the previously constructed
surjective diagonal-class homomorphism `unitClass` factors through
coefficient abelianization, since the elementary quotient is commutative.
The resulting homomorphism is `abelianizedUnitClass`.

Direct evaluation on a diagonal gives
`value (diagonal a b) = Abelianization.of a * Abelianization.of b`.
In particular, `quotientValue` recovers the class of `a` from
`unitClass a`. Induction on coefficient abelianization proves that
`quotientValue` is a left inverse to `abelianizedUnitClass`, establishing
injectivity of the latter. Its surjectivity follows from surjectivity of
`unitClass`. Inverting this bijective homomorphism constructs

`quotientEquiv : ElementaryQuotient D ≃* Abelianization Dˣ`.

`quotientEquiv_eq_quotientValue` identifies that inverse with the descended
pivot formula.

The homomorphism `determinant` is `quotientEquiv` composed with the
canonical quotient map. `determinant_eq_value` identifies it with the
explicit two-pivot formula, and `value_mul` supplies multiplicativity of
that formula. The theorem `determinant_diagonal` gives the displayed
diagonal evaluation, `determinant_surjective` proves surjectivity, and
`determinant_eq_one_iff` and `ker_determinant` identify its kernel with
`elementary D`.

Finally, `unitClass_eq_one_iff` and `diagonal_one_mem_iff` say that
`diag(a,1)` is elementary exactly when `a` lies in the commutator subgroup
of `Dˣ`. They follow by applying `quotientEquiv` and using that the kernel
of the coefficient abelianization map is its commutator subgroup.

This unit does not construct a reduced norm, compare with norm-one group
schemes, prove local division-algebra arithmetic or SK₁ vanishing, or prove
`LocalKneserTitsStatement`. Those remain separate obligations.
