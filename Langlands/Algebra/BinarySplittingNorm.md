# Binary matrix-algebra splitting and norm comparison

The namespace `Langlands.BinaryElementary.Splitting` starts with fields
`K`, `L`, a `K`-algebra structure on `L`, a possibly noncommutative
`K`-algebra `D`, a finite index type `I`, and an actual splitting chart

`e : L ⊗[K] D ≃ₐ[L] Matrix I I L`.

These objects may live in independent universes. No division-ring,
finite-Galois or basis assumption is needed for the construction of the
induced chart

`chart e : L ⊗[K] Matrix (Fin 2) (Fin 2) D ≃ₐ[L] Matrix (I ⊕ I) (I ⊕ I) L`.

The construction supplies both directions of this algebra equivalence
rather than assuming a chart for the binary matrix algebra.

First, `tensorMatrixEquiv` commutes scalar extension with binary matrices.
It composes three actual `K`-algebra equivalences: write matrices over `D`
as `D ⊗[K] Matrix₂ K`, reassociate the scalar tensor, and turn
`(L ⊗[K] D) ⊗[K] Matrix₂ K` back into matrices over `L ⊗[K] D`.
The theorem `tensorMatrixEquiv_tmul` proves that `l ⊗ M` has entries
`l ⊗ Mᵢⱼ`. Its proof expands the four matrix entries through these
equivalences.

Second, `blockEquiv` is the actual `L`-algebra equivalence from binary
matrices of `I`-indexed matrices to matrices indexed by `I ⊕ I`.
Its forward map is the four-block matrix construction from
`BinaryRepresentationNorm`; its inverse extracts the four ordinary
blocks. The block extraction identities prove the inverse laws. Ring
homomorphism laws come from the block map, and the scalar identity is
checked on the four blocks.

Apply `e` to each entry between these two equivalences. The resulting
`K`-algebra equivalence maps `l ⊗ M` to

`l • Representation.blockMap (coefficientRepresentation e) M`,

where `coefficientRepresentation e` sends `a` to `e (1 ⊗ a)`.
Linearity of `e` proves this formula. In particular, taking `M = 1`
proves compatibility with scalar multiplication by `L`, so it upgrades
to the displayed `L`-algebra equivalence `chart e`.
The theorem `chart_tmul` records the formula.

For the norm comparisons, additionally assume that `L/K` is finite
Galois, `I` is nonempty, and take finite bases
`b : Module.Basis J K D` and
`c : Module.Basis B K (Matrix (Fin 2) (Fin 2) D)`.
The index types of these bases may also live in independent universes.

`coefficientNorm b e : Dˣ →* Kˣ` is the previously constructed
`normFromGaloisUnits b e K` restricted along the canonical inclusion
`D → K ⊗[K] D`.
Likewise, `binaryNorm c e : GL₂ D →* Kˣ` is
`normFromGaloisUnits c (chart e) K` restricted along
`Matrix₂ D → K ⊗[K] Matrix₂ D`.
Thus these are the actual descended determinant laws already constructed
in `ReducedNormEvaluation`, applied to the original unit groups. No
arbitrary norm character or collection of desired laws is supplied.

The theorems `coefficientNorm_baseChange` and `binaryNorm_baseChange`
identify the images of these characters under `Kˣ → Lˣ` with
`Representation.coefficientNorm (coefficientRepresentation e)` and
`Representation.binaryNorm (coefficientRepresentation e)`, respectively.
For the coefficient comparison, naturality of the descended law first
extends `normFromGalois b e K (1 ⊗ a)` to `L`; its proved splitting
comparison then gives `det(e(1 ⊗ a))`. Applying the same argument to the
constructed binary chart and using `chart_tmul` proves the binary
comparison.

The field inclusion `K → L` is injective, hence so is its map on units.
Consequently each descended character has exactly the same kernel as
its represented determinant character. These are the precise subgroup
equalities `ker_coefficientNorm` and `ker_binaryNorm`.

Finally, if `D` is a division ring, `elementary_eq_ker_iff` proves

`elementary D = (binaryNorm c e).ker ↔ (coefficientNorm b e).ker = commutator Dˣ`.

Its proof rewrites both kernels using the base-change comparisons and
applies the represented determinant criterion from
`BinaryRepresentationNorm`. The left subgroup is the actual subgroup
generated by the two elementary binary matrix families. The right
subgroup is the ordinary commutator subgroup of the coefficient unit
group. Neither side is assumed to hold.

This unit is an internal transport of the existing descended determinant
construction through explicit matrix and tensor algebra equivalences.
It uses the previously established naturality, splitting comparison and
binary determinant criterion; it claims no new interpretation of an
original source. The coefficient splitting chart, finite-Galois
extension and finite bases remain explicit inputs to the norm section.
Unconditional construction of such data, comparison with the final
unconditional reduced-norm interface, local coefficient
commutator-kernel vanishing and the group-scheme comparisons still
remain separate obligations. This unit does not prove
`LocalKneserTitsStatement`.
