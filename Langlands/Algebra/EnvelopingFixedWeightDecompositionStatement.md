# Fixed points of a weight-scaling automorphism of an enveloping algebra: statement

Module `Langlands.Algebra.EnvelopingFixedWeightDecompositionStatement`, namespace
`Langlands.Algebra.UniversalEnveloping`. It contains two definitions and one `Prop`-valued
statement. It proves nothing; the proof is `envelopingFixedWeightDecomposition` in the module
`Langlands.Algebra.EnvelopingFixedWeightDecomposition`.

## Definitions

Let `k` be a field, `L` a Lie algebra over `k`, `b : I → L` a family of vectors and
`d : I → ℤ` an integer weight on the indices. Write `U(L)` for Mathlib's
`UniversalEnvelopingAlgebra k L` and `ι` for its generator map.

- `positiveWeightGeneratorSpan b d` is the `k`-span of the products `ι(b a) · v` with
  `0 < d a` and `v ∈ U(L)`. Since the span is stable under right multiplication, it is the right
  ideal of `U(L)` generated by the `ι(b a)` of positive weight.
- `zeroWeightGeneratorSubalgebra b d` is the subalgebra of `U(L)` generated by the `ι(b a)` with
  `d a = 0`.

## Statement

`EnvelopingFixedWeightDecompositionStatement.{u,v}` quantifies over a field `k : Type u`, a Lie
algebra `L : Type v` over `k`, a natural number `n`, a basis `b : Module.Basis (Fin n) k L`, a
weight `d : Fin n → ℤ` and a unit `c : kˣ`. It assumes that `c` is not a root of unity
(`c ^ m = 1` only for `m = 0`) and that `T : L →ₗ⁅k⁆ L` is a Lie algebra endomorphism with
`T (b a) = c ^ d a • b a` for every index `a`; such a `T` is bijective, since it is diagonal in the
basis `b` with unit eigenvalues. Let `T̃` be the algebra endomorphism
`UniversalEnvelopingAlgebra.lift k (ι ∘ T)` of `U(L)`. The conclusion is the conjunction of:

1. every `z ∈ U(L)` with `T̃ z = z` lies in
   `positiveWeightGeneratorSpan b d ⊔ zeroWeightGeneratorSubalgebra b d`;
2. `positiveWeightGeneratorSpan b d` and `zeroWeightGeneratorSubalgebra b d` are disjoint
   subspaces, so the decomposition in 1 is unique.

No characteristic, dimension or semisimplicity hypothesis is imposed beyond the finite basis.
The weights need not be ordered along the basis.

## Use

The intended application is archimedean. Let `G` be a connected reductive group over `ℝ` (or
`ℂ`), `Q = M V` a parabolic subgroup, and `λ` a cocharacter of the split centre of `M` defining
`Q`, so that the weights of `λ` on `Lie G` are integers, zero exactly on `Lie M` and positive
exactly on `Lie V`. Take `c = 2`, `t = λ(2)`, a basis of `λ`-weight vectors, its integer
`λ`-weights as `d`, and `T = Ad(t)`; then `T (b a) = 2 ^ d a • b a`, which is the scaling law of the
statement with a single base `c`. An element `z` of the centre of `U(Lie G)` is fixed by `T̃`:
`t = λ(exp(log 2))` lies on the one-parameter subgroup `r ↦ λ(exp r)`, whose generator `X` acts on
`U(Lie G)` by the commutator `ad X`, which kills `z`, so `Ad(λ(exp r)) z = exp(r · ad X) z = z`. The
statement then writes `z` uniquely as an element of `U(Lie M)` plus an element of the right ideal
`Lie V · U(Lie G)`.

Both hypotheses of the statement must be checked in each application; neither follows from
weaker conditions. Eigenvalues of `T` on `Lie V` of modulus greater than one do not give a single
base `c`: for the diagonal element `diag(6, 3, 1)` of `GL₃` over `ℂ`, the eigenvalues on the
upper-triangular radical are `2`, `3` and `6`, and no `c` has both `2` and `3` as integral powers.
Over a field of positive characteristic, central elements need not be fixed: in `U(𝔰𝔩₂)` over
`𝔽₅((s⁻¹))`, the element `f⁵` is central, but `Ad(diag(s, s⁻¹))` sends it to `s⁻¹⁰ f⁵`. Over a
general field the fixedness of the element to be decomposed is therefore an explicit hypothesis.

## Sources and provenance

The statement is derived. It is the weight argument of A. W. Knapp and D. A. Vogan,
*Cohomological Induction and Unitary Representations*, Princeton University Press, 1995
(`knapp-vogan-1995-cohomological-induction`), Lemma 4.123 (a), (b), printed p. 301 (PDF p. 323):
`U(𝔤) = U(𝔩) ⊕ (𝔲̄ U(𝔤) + U(𝔤) 𝔲)`, and a central element has its second component in
`U(𝔤) 𝔲`. Knapp and Vogan obtain (a) and (b) from the Poincaré–Birkhoff–Witt decomposition
(their (4.122)) by "using the element `h_{δ(𝔲)}` to measure effects of root vectors". The
statement replaces that semisimple element by a group-like automorphism `T` scaling the basis by
powers of `c`, keeps only the elements fixed by `T̃`, and uses the right ideal generated by the
positive part, which is the mirror image of the left ideal `U(𝔤) 𝔲` under the antipode. These
choices match the right enveloping action of the Library and the adjoint action of a point of the
split centre.
