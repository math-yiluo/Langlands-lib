# Ordered PBW word reductions

Author: Worker 1687; uses Worker 1431's ordered-word and shortlex helpers.

This is internal infrastructure for the arbitrary-characteristic ordered PBW
proof. Its definitions are derived directly from the supplied basis and Lie
bracket, and its lemmas are proved by elementary finite-sum and list arguments.

For a field `k`, a Lie algebra `L`, and a supplied basis `b` indexed by `Fin n`,
`WordModule k n` is the free module on lists of basis indices. `prepend i` is
the linear map that prepends `i` to each word. `insert b x` expands `x` in the
basis and forms the coordinate-weighted sum of the corresponding prepend
operators; in particular `insert b (b i) = prepend i`.

`Reduction b` is the relation generated by two constructors. The head rule
replaces `i :: j :: w`, when `j < i`, by the sum of `j :: i :: w` and the
basis expansion of the bracket `⁅b i, b j⁆` prepended to `w`. The cons rule
permits the same reduction after any additional prefix letter. Hence these
are exactly the intended contextual adjacent-inversion reductions.

`WordLT` is shortlex order on lists over the ordinary strict order on `Fin n`.
It is well-founded. The swapped term decreases lexicographically at equal
length, and every bracket term has shorter length. Prefixing preserves
shortlex, so induction over reduction constructors proves triangularity for
every supported term, including arbitrary cancellations in coefficients.

A reduction requires an adjacent inversion and therefore cannot start at an
ascending word. Conversely any nonascending word has an adjacent inversion;
the cons constructor extends the head rule through its prefix. Thus
`irreducible_iff_pairwise` identifies irreducibility with the usual pairwise
nondecreasing condition. No characteristic or positive-dimension hypothesis
is used. Coherence is established in the companion module.
