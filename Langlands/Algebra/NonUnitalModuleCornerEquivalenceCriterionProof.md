Assume first that the corner functor on the generated category is an
equivalence. For a generated object, its annihilator quotient is again
generated by surjective descent. The quotient projection becomes an
isomorphism on fixed parts by the published comparison lemma. Reflection
of isomorphisms therefore makes the original projection an isomorphism,
and its injectivity shows that the original annihilator is zero.

Now take a monomorphism `f : X ⟶ Y` with `Y` generated. Form the original
quotient of `Y` by the range of `AeX → X → Y`, using the published concrete
cokernel. This quotient is nondegenerate and generated by surjective
descent. For every `x` in `X`, its image in the quotient is annihilated by
`eA`: applying `ea` puts its representative in the range being quotiented
out. The preceding zero-annihilator argument makes that image zero. Thus
`f x` is the image of an element of `AeX`; injectivity of `f` puts `x`
itself in `AeX`. This proves subobject closure. Quotient closure was proved
without extra assumptions in `NonUnitalModuleCornerClosedEvaluation`.

This is the source's reduction by the generated part of a submodule,
expressed through the actual concrete cokernel of its composite inclusion.
The internal nondegenerate-descent lemma simply transports a fixing
idempotent along a surjective intertwiner.

The reverse implication applies the previously constructed equivalence
from `NonUnitalModuleCornerEquivalenceConstruction`. All carriers remain in
`max uA uM`; the original target and its import closure are unchanged.
