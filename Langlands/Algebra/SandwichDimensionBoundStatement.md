# Dimension bound for simple modules from a commuting sandwich span

`Langlands.SandwichSimpleModuleDimensionBoundStatement.{uH,uM}` is a
Prop-valued definition. This module states the bound and supplies no proof.

## Statement and conventions

Let `H : Type uH` have structures `[Ring H] [Algebra ℂ H]`, let `q l : ℕ`,
and let `x : Fin q → H` and `a : Fin l → H`. Assume that every pair of
elements of `a` commutes. Write
`𝒜 = Algebra.adjoin ℂ (Set.range a)` for the unital complex subalgebra
generated by this family. Assume that

```text
Submodule.span ℂ {y : H | ∃ i j, ∃ m ∈ 𝒜, y = x i * m * x j} = ⊤.
```

For every `M : Type uM` with `[AddCommGroup M] [Module ℂ M] [Module H M]`
and `[IsScalarTower ℂ H M]`, the statement asserts

```text
IsSimpleModule H M → FiniteDimensional ℂ M →
  Module.finrank ℂ M ≤ q ^ (2 ^ l).
```

The universes `uH` and `uM` are independent. The scalar-tower condition
expresses compatibility of the complex and `H` actions. The multiplication
in `H` need not be commutative; only the designated generators commute.
`Algebra.adjoin` includes complex scalars and the unit, so the unit is not
counted among the `l` generators. Both exponents in the Lean conclusion are
natural-number powers. Simplicity and finite dimension are hypotheses.

## Source and derived provenance

The original source is I. N. Bernshtein and A. V. Zelevinskii,
*Representations of the group GL(n,F), where F is a non-Archimedean local
field*, Russian Mathematical Surveys 31:3 (1976), 1–68, English
translation: Lemma 4.10 on printed p.39, the deduction in §4.11 on printed
pp.39–40, and the lemma's proof in §4.12 on printed p.40. The surrounding
sandwich description is §4.9 on printed p.39.

Registered source: `bernstein-zelevinsky-1976-representations-gln`.
The retained original has SHA256
`72dc22eb9991112936a88327f00d0f02a16a2bf8747518ca0ecfeeadd188e50c`.

Provenance is **derived**. The source applies the argument to the Hecke
corner `H_N` using the elements `γ̄_i` attached to coset representatives
on the two sides of a commutative subalgebra. This statement abstracts
that algebra and those elements while retaining the complex coefficients,
finite dimension, simplicity, and the same sandwich-span hypothesis.
The source obtains finite dimension separately in §4.6; the present
statement explicitly assumes it.

Here is the source argument explaining the bound. Set
`m = dim_ℂ M`; a simple module is nonzero, so `m > 0` under the
finite-dimensional hypothesis. Its action gives a complex algebra
representation `ρ : H → End_ℂ M`. Burnside's theorem gives
`ρ(H) = End_ℂ M`, hence `dim_ℂ ρ(H) = m²`.
Lemma 4.10 bounds the dimension of a commutative subalgebra generated
by `l` elements and the unit by

```text
f_l(m) = m^(2 - 2^(1-l)).
```

This displayed exponent is an ordinary real exponent; the subtraction
`1-l` is not truncated natural-number subtraction. Applying the lemma
to `ρ(𝒜)` gives `dim_ℂ ρ(𝒜) ≤ f_l(m)`. The sandwich span expresses
`ρ(H)` as the sum of at most `q²` images of this subalgebra under the
complex linear maps `b ↦ ρ(x i) * b * ρ(x j)`. Consequently,

```text
m² ≤ q² dim_ℂ ρ(𝒜) ≤ q² m^(2 - 2^(1-l)).
```

Dividing by the positive power of `m` and rearranging yields
`m^(2^(1-l)) ≤ q²` and then `m ≤ q^(2^l)`, as in §4.11.
For `l = 0`, the generated subalgebra consists of scalars, and the same
argument gives `m² ≤ q²`, hence `m ≤ q`. If `q = 0`, the span
hypothesis forces `H` to be the zero ring, which has no simple unital
module. Thus no positivity hypotheses on `q` or `l` are added.

In §4.12 the authors prove Lemma 4.10 by reducing commuting generators
to nilpotent ones on common generalized eigenspaces and then using
induction on the number of generators and the vector-space dimension.
This paragraph and the preceding derivation explain provenance; none
of these proof steps is formalized by this statement module.
