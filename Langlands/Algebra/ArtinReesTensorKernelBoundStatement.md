# Artin–Rees bounds for ideal tensor kernels

`Langlands.Algebra.ArtinReesTensorKernelBoundStatement.{u,v}` defines the
following proposition. Let \(R\) be a commutative Noetherian ring, let \(M\)
be an arbitrary \(R\)-module, and let \(I\) be an ideal of \(R\). Assume that,
for every ideal \(J\) and every \(k\in\mathbb N\) with \(I^k\subseteq J\),
the map
\[
\mathrm{id}_M\otimes J.\mathrm{subtype}:
M\otimes_R J\longrightarrow M\otimes_R R
\]
is injective. Then, for every ideal \(a\) and every \(n\in\mathbb N\),
\[
\ker\bigl(M\otimes_R a\longrightarrow M\otimes_R R\bigr)
\ \subseteq\ I^n(M\otimes_R a).
\]
The map in this kernel is induced by the ideal inclusion \(a\hookrightarrow R\).
The right side is the submodule generated by the scalar multiples \(r x\),
where \(r\in I^n\) and \(x\in M\otimes_R a\).
This module states the proposition and supplies no proof.

The complete proposition quantifies over `R : Type u`, `M : Type v`,
`[CommRing R]`, `[IsNoetherianRing R]`, `[AddCommGroup M]`,
`[Module R M]`, and `I : Ideal R`, in that order. Its sole additional
hypothesis is the displayed injectivity condition, expressed using
`LinearMap.lTensor M J.subtype`. The conclusion quantifies over all
`a : Ideal R` and `n : ℕ`. The universe levels `u` and `v` are independent.
There is no finiteness assumption on \(M\), local ring hypothesis,
second ring, nonzero hypothesis, or flatness assumption.

This is a derived elementary tensor formulation of the Artin–Rees step in
the proof of Stacks Project Lemma 10.99.7, tag
[00MK](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/00MK), rather than its verbatim
statement. The original proof uses powers of the maximal ideal of a local
ring. Lemma 10.99.6, tag
[00MJ](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/00MJ), supplies the finite-length
Tor vanishing used there to obtain tensor injectivity. The present statement
takes the needed tensor injectivity as a hypothesis and allows any ideal
\(I\) in a commutative Noetherian ring.

The intended derivation first applies Artin–Rees to \(a\subseteq R\).
For some \(c\), it gives
\(a\cap I^{n+c}\subseteq I^n a\) for every \(n\).
The injectivity hypothesis applies to \(a+I^{n+c}\), which contains
\(I^{n+c}\). The independently stated
`Langlands.Algebra.IdealTensorIntersectionKernelStatement` then identifies
a lift of each kernel element through
\(M\otimes_R(a\cap I^{n+c})\). Factoring this inclusion through \(I^n a\)
places its image in \(I^n(M\otimes_R a)\).
Artin–Rees is applied to the finite \(R\)-module \(R\), so this step does
not need \(M\) to be finite. This paragraph explains the formulation;
it is not a formal proof. The statement stops at the power bounds.
Separatedness, vanishing of the kernel, and flatness require further work.
