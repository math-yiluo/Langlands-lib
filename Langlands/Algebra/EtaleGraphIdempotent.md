# Graph idempotents of algebra maps out of an unramified algebra

Module `Langlands.Algebra.EtaleGraphIdempotent`, namespace
`Langlands.Algebra.GraphIdempotent`. It imports only Mathlib. It contains internal
commutative algebra used in
[`GroupScheme.Reductive.WeilFactorUniquenessReduction`](../GroupScheme/Reductive/WeilFactorUniquenessReduction.md)
to recover a finite étale algebra `B` from the idempotents of the rings `B ⊗_A T`.

## Provenance

Provenance: derived/internal. These are standard facts about unramified algebras: for an
unramified `A`-algebra `B`, the diagonal `Spec B → Spec (B ⊗_A B)` is open and closed, so the
kernel of multiplication `B ⊗_A B → B` is generated by an idempotent. This is the fact behind
Mathlib's `Algebra.FormallyUnramified.exists_algEquiv_prod` (`Mathlib/RingTheory/Unramified/Basic.lean`),
whose proof the existence statement here follows. The converse criterion
`exists_isGraphIdempotent_of_minimal` was written for this development. Its proof uses a
dual basis of the finite projective module `B` rather than any result on ring epimorphisms.

## Declarations

General lemmas on an idempotent `e` of a commutative ring:

- `mul_eq_zero_of_mem_span`: `x ∈ (1 - e) ⇒ x e = 0`;
- `eq_zero_of_mem_span_of_mem_span`: `(1 - e) ∩ (e) = 0`;
- `span_ne_top_of_ne_one`: an idempotent `s ≠ 1` generates a proper ideal.

For commutative rings `A`, `B`, `T`, an `A`-algebra `B`, an `A`-algebra `T` and
`φ : B →ₐ[A] T`:

- `graphMul φ : B ⊗[A] T →ₐ[A] T` is `b ⊗ t ↦ φ b * t` (Mathlib's `productMap φ id`).
- `IsGraphIdempotent φ e` (structure): `e` is idempotent, `graphMul φ e = 1`, and
  `(b ⊗ 1) e = (1 ⊗ φ b) e` for every `b`. Geometrically, `e` is the indicator of the graph of
  `Spec φ` inside `Spec (B ⊗ T)`.
- `mul_eq_of_tmul_one_mul`, `IsGraphIdempotent.mul_eq`: under the last condition,
  `x e = (1 ⊗ graphMul φ x) e` for all `x`.
- `IsGraphIdempotent.unique`: a graph idempotent of `φ` is unique.
- `IsGraphIdempotent.algHom_eq`: a graph idempotent determines `φ`.
- `IsGraphIdempotent.eq_mul_of_mul_eq`: if `f e = f` then `f = e (1 ⊗ graphMul φ f)`.
- `IsGraphIdempotent.ne_zero`: `e ≠ 0` when `T` is nontrivial.
- `IsGraphIdempotent.graphMul_map`, `IsGraphIdempotent.map`: graph idempotents are natural
  in `T`. The image of the graph idempotent of `φ` under `B ⊗ T → B ⊗ T'` (induced by
  `χ : T → T'`) is the graph idempotent of `χ ∘ φ`.
- `IsGraphIdempotent.eq_of_le`: if `f` is idempotent, `f e = f`, and the image of `f` in
  `B ⊗ T₂` is nonzero for every algebra map `T → T₂` to a nontrivial ring, then `f = e`.
  (Write `f = e (1 ⊗ s)` with `s` idempotent; if `s ≠ 1`, then `f` vanishes over `T/(s) ≠ 0`.)
- `exists_isGraphIdempotent_id`: if `B` is formally unramified and essentially of finite type
  over `A`, then `id_B` has a graph idempotent `δ ∈ B ⊗_A B`. It is `1 - e`, where `e`
  generates the kernel of multiplication (Mathlib's `KaehlerDifferential.ideal`).
- `exists_isGraphIdempotent`: then every `φ : B →ₐ[A] T` has a graph idempotent, the image of
  `δ`.
- `exists_dualBasis`: a finite projective module has a finite dual basis
  `∑ c_i(b) v_i = b`.
- `coordTensor c : B ⊗[A] T →ₗ[A] T`, `b ⊗ t ↦ c(b) t`, with `coordTensor_tmul`,
  `coordTensor_one_tmul_mul` (`T`-linearity), `coordTensor_map` (naturality) and
  `sum_tmul_coordTensor` (`x = ∑ (v_i ⊗ 1)(1 ⊗ coordTensor c_i x)`).
- `exists_isGraphIdempotent_of_minimal` (with `A B T : Type u`, `B` finite, projective,
  formally unramified and essentially of finite type). Let `e` be an idempotent of `B ⊗ T`, and
  let `D = (B ⊗ T)/(1 - e)` with image `e_D` of `e` in `B ⊗ D`. Assume (1) every idempotent `f`
  of `B ⊗ D` with `f e_D = f` that stays nonzero over every nontrivial `D`-algebra equals `e_D`,
  and (2) the image of `e` in `B ⊗ T/m` is nonzero for every maximal ideal `m`. Then `e` is the
  graph idempotent of some `φ : B →ₐ[A] T`.

## Proof of the converse criterion

Let `q : B ⊗ T → D` be the quotient map, `j : T → D` and `π : B → D` the two induced maps,
and `e_π` the graph idempotent of `π`. Since `graphMul π (e_D) = q e = 1`, `e_π ≤ e_D`. By (1),
`e_π = e_D`, so `(b ⊗ 1) e_D = (1 ⊗ π b) e_D`. With a dual basis `(v_i, c_i)` of `B` over `A`,
applying `coordTensor c_i` gives `j (c_i((x) e)) = q x · j (c_i(e))` for all `x` (here
`c_i(·)` means `coordTensor c_i`). By (2), the coordinates `c_i(e)` generate the unit ideal:
otherwise they lie in a maximal ideal `m`, and the image of `e` in `B ⊗ T/m` vanishes. Pick
`∑ a_i c_i(e) = 1`. Then `j` is surjective (`q x = j(∑ a_i c_i(x e))`) and injective
(`j t = 0 ⇒ (1 ⊗ t) e = 0 ⇒ t = ∑ a_i c_i((1 ⊗ t) e) = 0`). Put `φ = j⁻¹ ∘ π`. Then
`(b ⊗ 1 - 1 ⊗ φ b) e` lies in `(1 - e)` and is a multiple of `e`, so it is `0`. Also
`j (graphMul φ e) = q e = 1`, so `graphMul φ e = 1`.
