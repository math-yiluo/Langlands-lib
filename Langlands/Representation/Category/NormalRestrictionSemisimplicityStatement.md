# Finite-dimensional semisimplicity descends to a normal subgroup

Author: Worker 16. This pair states a proposition; it does not prove the
restriction theorem or assume it as supplied data.

## Exact statement

`Langlands.FiniteDimensionalNormalRestrictionSemisimplicity.{uR, uG, uV}`
quantifies over every field `R : Type uR`, group `G : Type uG`, and
`R`-module `V : Type uV` with `[AddCommGroup V] [Module R V]` and
`[FiniteDimensional R V]`. For every subgroup `H : Subgroup G` with
`[H.Normal]` and every `ρ : Representation R G V`, it asserts

```text
Representation.IsSemisimpleRepresentation ρ →
  Representation.IsSemisimpleRepresentation (ρ.comp H.subtype).
```

The coefficient, group, and carrier universes are independent. Finite
dimensionality and normality are explicit hypotheses. There is no assumption
that `G` is finite, that `H` has finite index, or that the field has
characteristic zero or is algebraically closed. No topology, smoothness,
finite image, irreducibility, positive dimension, or chosen invariant
complement is supplied. In particular, the zero-dimensional representation
is included.

All mathematical terms are Mathlib's existing constructions:

- `Representation R G V` is a monoid homomorphism from `G` to the
  multiplicative monoid of `R`-linear endomorphisms of `V`.
- `H.subtype : H →* G` is the actual subgroup inclusion, and
  `ρ.comp H.subtype` is restriction on the same underlying vector space.
- `Representation.IsSemisimpleRepresentation ρ` abbreviates
  `ComplementedLattice (Subrepresentation ρ)`: every invariant subspace
  has an invariant complement.
- `Representation.isSemisimpleRepresentation_iff_isSemisimpleModule_asModule`
  identifies this with semisimplicity of the associated module over the
  group algebra.

The proposition defines no replacement representation or semisimplicity
predicate. It imports `Mathlib.LinearAlgebra.FiniteDimensional.Defs` and
`Mathlib.RepresentationTheory.Semisimple`; no existing Library pair is
modified.

## Original source and scope

The motivating original is I. N. Bernshtein and A. V. Zelevinskii,
*Representations of the group GL(n,F) where F is a non-Archimedean local
field*, Russian Mathematical Surveys 31:3 (1976), 1–68, English translation.
Registered source: `bernstein-zelevinsky-1976-representations-gln`;
SHA-256: `72dc22eb9991112936a88327f00d0f02a16a2bf8747518ca0ecfeeadd188e50c`.
Worker 16 read printed pages 15–17, which are also one-based PDF pages
15–17, in the registered original. No source-specific errata note was
present in the project notes or supplied Library.

The representation conventions on page 15 use complex vector spaces,
without a topology on those vector spaces. Lemma–Definition 2.7 on page 16
identifies complete reducibility with each of three conditions: being a
direct sum of irreducible modules, being generated by irreducible
submodules, and having an invariant complement for every submodule.
The last condition corresponds to the complemented-lattice predicate used
here.

Lemma 2.9(b), page 17, assumes that `H` is normal of finite index and that
the complex representation of `G` is irreducible. It concludes that its
restriction is a finite direct sum of irreducible `H`-representations, with
the number of summands at most the index. It assumes no finite dimension
of the original representation. Its proof uses finite generation over
`H` to choose an irreducible quotient, translates the quotient kernel by
coset representatives, and embeds the original space into the finite
direct sum of the resulting irreducible quotients.

The present statement is an **internal finite-dimensional variant**,
not a verbatim formalization of Lemma 2.9(b). It replaces the finite-index
hypothesis with finite dimensionality, allows arbitrary coefficient fields,
and takes semisimple input rather than only irreducible input. It asserts
semisimplicity of the restriction without an index bound on its
irreducible summands. Dropping the index condition and extending the
coefficients are our internal mathematical generalizations; neither is
attributed to the original. The source's quotient argument is not claimed
as an implemented Lean proof.

## Intended finite-dimensional Clifford/socle route

Here is the internal argument motivating the target, not a proof supplied
by this Lean file. If a finite-dimensional `H`-representation is nonzero,
choose a nonzero invariant subspace of smallest dimension. It is simple.
For a simple `G`-representation, start with such a simple `H`-submodule
`W`. For each `g ∈ G`, the translate `ρ(g)W` is again an `H`-submodule:
normality gives `g⁻¹hg ∈ H` for every `h ∈ H`. The same conjugation and
the invertibility of `ρ(g)` show that this translated submodule is simple.

The sum of these translates is nonzero and `G`-stable. Simplicity of the
`G`-representation forces the sum to be the whole space. A module
generated by simple submodules is semisimple, so restriction is semisimple
in this case. For a semisimple `G`-representation, decompose it into
simple `G`-submodules. Each is finite-dimensional; applying the preceding
argument to each shows that the whole restricted module is generated by
simple `H`-submodules. The zero module is covered by the empty sum.

Equivalently, the sum of all simple `H`-submodules is the `H`-socle.
Normality makes it `G`-stable. Semisimplicity over `G` gives it a
`G`-invariant complement. A nonzero complement would, by finite
dimensionality, contain a simple `H`-submodule, contradicting its
disjointness from the socle. Thus the socle is the whole space.
This complement is a consequence of the input semisimplicity, not extra
chosen data in the statement.

Both descriptions use only finite-dimensional linear algebra over the
given field and normality; neither requires a finite-index sum or
division by an index. Mathlib's
`sSup_simples_eq_top_iff_isSemisimpleModule` and
`isSemisimpleModule_biSup_of_isSemisimpleModule_submodule` in
`Mathlib.RingTheory.SimpleModule.Basic` are relevant to a future proof.
They are implementation leads, not a completed formal derivation here.

## Boundary

This is the normal-subgroup descent statement complementary to the
Library's `Langlands.FiniteIndexSemisimplicityAscent`, whose
characteristic-zero, finite-index hypotheses and reverse direction
remain unchanged. A proof of this proposition, passage through a normal
core to general finite-index restriction, induction semisimplicity,
local Weil specializations, and the local Langlands correspondence are
separate tasks.
