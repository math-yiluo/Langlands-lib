# Every nonzero smooth representation has an irreducible subquotient

Implementation and documentation: Worker 379 (task
`0a1bd7fb-66c2-4dcc-a3c8-6619f6fa10b4`, support part of the general Aubert
program `46df6a68-5db4-4428-8044-05151d6ce1f6`). This is a generic lemma: it holds
for every topological group and uses no reductive structure.

## Statement

`Langlands.SmoothRep.exists_isIrreducible_isSubquotient_of_nontrivial`: let `G` be
a group with a topology and `A : SmoothRep.{0, uG, uV} ℂ G` a smooth complex
representation with `[Nontrivial A]` (its carrier has a nonzero vector). Then
there is `D : SmoothRep.{0, uG, uV} ℂ G` with `D.IsIrreducible` and
`SmoothRep.IsSubquotient D A`.

`IsIrreducible` is the Library's irreducibility (simplicity of the lattice of
subrepresentations, so `D ≠ 0`). `IsSubquotient D A` means that `D` is isomorphic
to an actual quotient of an actual subrepresentation of `A`. `D` has the same
carrier universe as `A`. No finite length, admissibility or finite generation is
assumed.

The Library already had the finite-length case,
`SmoothRep.exists_irreducible_subquotient`. This lemma removes the finite-length
hypothesis. It is the remark of Renard, *Représentations des groupes réductifs
p-adiques*, end of III.1.4, printed p. 60, for smooth representations of a totally
disconnected group: "toute représentation lisse de G admet toujours un
sous-quotient irréductible, si elle est de type fini, elle admet un quotient
irréductible". Renard leaves nonzeroness implicit; the Lean statement assumes it.
The proof below is the usual one: a nonzero cyclic representation has an
irreducible quotient. It uses no topology on `G`, so the lemma holds for any
topological group.

## Proof

1. Choose `v ≠ 0` in `A` and let `S` be the subrepresentation spanned over `ℂ` by
   the orbit `{A g v}` (`CompactSplitting.orbitSpan`). Let `B` be the smooth
   subrepresentation `SmoothRep.subrepresentation A S`, and `v'` the vector `v`
   of `B`. Then `v' ≠ 0`, so `B` is nontrivial.
2. Every subrepresentation `T` of `B` that contains `v'` is all of `B`: by
   induction on the span (`Submodule.span_induction`), the orbit vectors are
   `B g v' ∈ T`, and `T` is closed under sums and scalars.
3. Hence `B` is generated by the finite set `{v'}`. The Library's
   `CuspidalSupport.QuasicuspidalEmbedding.exists_isCoatom_subrepresentation`
   gives a maximal proper subrepresentation `T` of `B`.
4. The quotient `SmoothRep.quotient B T` is irreducible by the Library's
   `CuspidalSupport.QuasicuspidalEmbedding.isIrreducible_of_isCoatom`, applied to
   the quotient map, whose kernel is `T`.
5. This quotient is literally `SmoothRep.subquotient A S T`, so the identity
   isomorphism witnesses `IsSubquotient`.

The theorem uses no axioms beyond Lean's standard ones.

## Use

The support category `Alg({I})` of Aubert's erratum constrains only irreducible
subquotients. This lemma shows that a nonzero smooth representation always has
one, so membership in `Alg({I})` is never vacuous for a nonzero representation.
It is used in the reduction of detection of zero by Jacquet modules on `Alg({I})`
to the support-theory targets.
