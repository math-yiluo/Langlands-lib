# The monomial case of Brauer's theorem

`Langlands.Representation.Finite.MonomialTrace`. If a finite group `G` has a normal commutative
subgroup `A` such that every element has a `p`-power in `A` (for a prime `p`), then the trace of every
complex matrix representation of `G` is a sum of class functions induced from linear characters of
subgroups. It is applied to hyperelementary groups in `BrauerTrace`. Its bookkeeping (`Entry`,
`lsum`, `lift`) and its group lemma are also used by the retained-subgroup variant
`DegreeZeroMonomial`.

## Statement

In namespace `Langlands.FiniteRep.MonomialTrace`, with `open scoped Classical MatrixGroups`:

```lean
theorem exists_trace_eq_sum_inducedClassFunction_linearCharacter
    {G : Type u} [Group G] [Fintype G] {p : ℕ} (hp : p.Prime) (A : Subgroup G) (hA : A.Normal)
    (hcomm : ∀ a ∈ A, ∀ b ∈ A, a * b = b * a) (hquot : ∀ g : G, ∃ n : ℕ, g ^ p ^ n ∈ A)
    {n : ℕ} (ρ : G →* GL (Fin n) ℂ) :
    ∃ (k : ℕ) (H : Fin k → Subgroup G) (ψ : (i : Fin k) → (H i →* ℂˣ)),
      ∀ g : G, ((ρ g : GL (Fin n) ℂ) : Matrix (Fin n) (Fin n) ℂ).trace =
        ∑ i : Fin k, inducedClassFunction (H i)
          (fun x => if hx : x ∈ H i then (((ψ i) ⟨x, hx⟩ : ℂˣ) : ℂ) else 0) g
```

In words: the trace of `ρ` is `∑ᵢ Ind_{H i}^G ψ i`, an unsigned finite sum (repetitions allowed) of
class functions induced from linear characters `ψ i : H i →* ℂˣ`, each extended by zero outside
`H i`. The rank `n` may be zero (then `k = 0`).

## Proof

- Bookkeeping, for a finite group `K`: `extChar L ψ` extends a character of `L` by zero; an
  `Entry K` is a pair `⟨L, ψ⟩`; `lsum l` is the sum over a list of entries of
  `inducedClassFunction L (extChar L ψ)` (`lsum_nil`, `lsum_cons`, `lsum_append`, `lsum_replicate`,
  `lsum_flatten_replicate`). `lift H e` turns an entry of a subgroup `H` into an entry of `K`, and
  `inducedClassFunction_lsum_eq` (by induction in stages, `val_lift`, `extChar_lift`) says that inducing
  from `H` the extension by zero of `lsum l` gives `lsum (l.map (lift H))`.
- `exists_normal_comm_not_central` (for `f : K →* Γ`): if `K / A` is a `p`-group, `f` has commuting
  values on the normal subgroup `A` and noncommuting values somewhere, then some normal `B ≥ A` with
  commuting values contains an element whose value is not central. If `A` itself has such an element,
  `B = A`. Otherwise `A` lies in the preimage `Z` of the centre of the image, `K / Z` is a nontrivial
  `p`-group, and `B` is generated by `Z` and an element `z` mapping to a nontrivial central element of
  `K / Z`.
- `Monomial K W σ`: the trace of `σ` is `lsum l` for some `l`. `engine` proves it, by induction on a
  bound `m ≥ |K| + dim W`, for every `σ` of a group `K` with a normal commutative `A` with `p`-group
  quotient:
  - `dim W = 0`: the empty list (`monomial_of_finrank_zero`);
  - `σ` has commuting image: the weight spaces of `⊤` (`WeightSpaces`) give
    `trace σ g = ∑_χ χ g · dim wt σ ⊤ χ`, and each nonzero weight is a linear character of `K`, its own
    induction from `⊤` (`monomial_of_comm`, with `inducedClassFunction_top_of_conj_invariant`);
  - otherwise `exists_normal_comm_not_central`, applied to `σ` with values in the units of
    `End W`, gives `B`; it has two different weights. If `K` permutes the nonzero weights of `B`
    transitively, the stabilizer `H₁` of a weight `χ₁` is proper, the induction hypothesis applies to
    `stabRep` on `wt σ B χ₁` with `A.subgroupOf H₁`, and the trace of `σ` is induced from `H₁`
    (`monomial_of_transitive`, by `trace_eq_sum_fixed` and `trace_restrict_conj`). If not, the sum of
    the weight spaces in the orbit of `χ₁` and the sum of the others are complementary nonzero
    `K`-stable subspaces, and the induction hypothesis applies to both (`monomial_of_isCompl`).
- `repOfGL ρ` is the matrix representation on `Fin n → ℂ` (`trace_repOfGL`); the end theorem applies
  `engine` to it and numbers the list by `Fin`.

## Literature

Jean-Pierre Serre, *Linear Representations of Finite Groups*, Springer GTM 42 (1977) (literature
`serre-1977-linear-representations`), read in the library copy:

- §8.5, Lemma 4 and Theorem 16, printed pp.66–67: a supersolvable group is monomial. The proof is an
  induction on the order: a nonabelian supersolvable group has a normal abelian subgroup not contained
  in the centre (Lemma 4; here `exists_normal_comm_not_central`), and Proposition 24 of §8.1 (p.61)
  then induces a faithful irreducible representation from a proper subgroup. `engine` follows this
  pattern for groups with a normal commutative subgroup with `p`-group quotient, and for the trace of
  any (not necessarily irreducible) representation: the non-transitive case is split off as a direct
  sum (`monomial_of_isCompl`) instead of being excluded by irreducibility.
- §7.1, Proposition 19, printed p.55: a sum of subspaces permuted transitively is induced from the
  stabilizer (`monomial_of_transitive`).
- §10.5, printed pp.78–79: Theorem 20 derives Brauer's theorem in the monomial form from Theorem 19 and
  the monomiality of elementary groups (nilpotent, hence supersolvable, by Theorem 16). In the Library
  the same role is played by this file for hyperelementary groups (`BrauerTrace`).
