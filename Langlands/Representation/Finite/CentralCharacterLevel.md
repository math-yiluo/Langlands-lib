# Quotients for a prescribed central character

Given a homomorphism `i : A →* G`, a character `ω : A →* C` and a subgroup
`U` of `G`, `centralCharacterLevel i ω U` is the subgroup generated by `U`
and the image of `ker ω`. If `i` has central image and `U` is normal, this
subgroup is normal. When `G` is a topological group and `U` is open, the
subgroup is open because it contains `U`.

The finite-index lemma assumes that `i` has central image, `U` is normal,
`i.range ⊔ U` has finite index in `G`, and the character has finite image.
Write `K = centralCharacterLevel i ω U`. Normality of `K` and the equality
`K ⊔ i.range = i.range ⊔ U` identify the relative index of `K` in
`i.range ⊔ U` with its relative index in `i.range`. The latter is the index
of the preimage of `K` under `i`, which contains `ker ω`. The kernel has finite
index by the first isomorphism theorem. Multiplicativity of indices now gives
finite index for `K` in `G`.

A complex-valued function transforming by `ω` under left translation by
`i` and invariant under right translation by `U` descends to `G ⧸ K`.
`descendCentralCovariantFunction` uses the quotient lift. Every element of
`K` is a product of an element in `i(ker ω)` with an element of `U` when `U`
is normal. Translation by the first factor preserves the function by covariance
and centrality, and right translation by the second preserves it by hypothesis.
These constructions give finite-level functions for the character expansion of
smooth representations of groups compact modulo their centre.
