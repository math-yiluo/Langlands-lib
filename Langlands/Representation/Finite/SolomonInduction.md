# Solomon's induction theorem

`Langlands.Representation.Finite.SolomonInduction`. For a finite group `G`, the unit class function is
an integral combination of the permutation characters `Ind_H^G 1` of hyperelementary subgroups `H`.
The induction is the function-level `Langlands.FiniteRep.inducedClassFunction` of
`ClassFunctionInduction`. The theorem is one of the two inputs of Brauer's theorem in trace form
(`BrauerTrace`); the degree-zero Brauer argument (`DegreeZeroBrauerProof`) uses its complex form.

## Statements

In namespace `Langlands.FiniteRep.Solomon`, for `{G : Type u} [Group G] [Fintype G]`:

```lean
theorem exists_one_eq_sum_zsmul_inducedClassFunction_one :
    ∃ (k : ℕ) (H : Fin k → Subgroup G) (a : Fin k → ℤ),
      (∀ i, ∃ q : ℕ, q.Prime ∧ ∃ C : Subgroup G, C ≤ H i ∧ IsCyclic C ∧ (Nat.card C).Coprime q ∧
        (∀ h ∈ H i, ∀ c ∈ C, h * c * h⁻¹ ∈ C) ∧ (∀ h ∈ H i, ∃ n : ℕ, h ^ q ^ n ∈ C)) ∧
      ∀ g : G, (1 : ℚ) = ∑ i, (a i : ℚ) * inducedClassFunction (H i) (fun _ => (1 : ℚ)) g
```

In words: there are finitely many subgroups `H i` and integers `a i` such that every `H i` is
hyperelementary (for some prime `q` it contains a cyclic subgroup `C` of order prime to `q`, normalized
by `H i`, with a `q`-power of every element of `H i` in `C`, so `H i / C` is a `q`-group), and
`1 = ∑ a i · Ind_{H i}^G 1` as rational-valued functions on `G`. The coefficients may be negative.

`exists_one_eq_sum_zsmul_inducedClassFunction_one_complex` is the same statement with `ℂ` in place
of `ℚ` (the bound variable of the power condition is named `m`).

The predicate `IsHyperelementary H` names the condition on each `H i`; the statements write it out.

## Proof

- `permCharacter H g` is the number of cosets in `G ⧸ H` fixed by `g`, as an integer.
  `card_mul_permCharacter` counts `{x | x⁻¹ g x ∈ H}` as `|H| · permCharacter H g`, so
  `inducedClassFunction_one_eq_permCharacter` identifies `inducedClassFunction H 1` with it;
  `permCharacter_top` gives `permCharacter ⊤ = 1`.
- `permCharacter_mul_permCharacter_eq_sum`: `permCharacter H · permCharacter K` is the permutation
  character of `G ⧸ H × G ⧸ K`, the sum over the `G`-orbits of the permutation characters of the
  stabilizers of orbit representatives. Each such stabilizer conjugates into `K`
  (`conj_mem_of_mem_stabilizer`); `IsHyperelementary.of_le_conj` shows that it is hyperelementary
  when `K` is. Hence `permLattice`, the `ℤ`-span of all permutation characters, is a subring
  (`permSubring`, with `mul_mem_span_of_gen`, `mul_mem_permLattice`, `one_mem_permLattice`), and
  `hyperelementaryLattice`, the span of those of hyperelementary subgroups, is an ideal of it
  (`mul_mem_hyperelementaryLattice`).
- `exists_isHyperelementary_not_dvd` (for a prime `q` and `x : G`): let `C` be generated by the part
  of `x` of order prime to `q`, `N` its normalizer (which contains `x`), `Q` a Sylow `q`-subgroup of `N`
  and `H` the image of `C · Q`. Then `H` is hyperelementary. The elements `y` with `y⁻¹ x y ∈ H` lie in
  `N`, so `permCharacter H x` is computed in `N`; there `x` acts on `N ⧸ (C · Q)` as its `q`-part,
  which generates a `q`-group, so the number of fixed points is congruent modulo `q` to the index of
  `C · Q` in `N`, which is prime to `q`.
- `one_mem_hyperelementaryLattice`: otherwise the ideal lies in a maximal ideal `𝔪` of the finite
  `ℤ`-algebra `permSubring`. The residue field has prime characteristic `p` (the contraction of `𝔪` to
  `ℤ` is maximal, by integrality). By going up, `𝔪` lies under a maximal ideal `𝔐` of `G → ℤ`, and
  `𝔐 = {f | p ∣ f x₀}` for some `x₀`. The hyperelementary `H` with `p ∤ permCharacter H x₀` gives an
  element of the ideal outside `𝔐`.
- `exists_one_eq_sum_zsmul_inducedClassFunction_one` writes `1 ∈ hyperelementaryLattice` as an integral
  combination indexed by the finite set of hyperelementary subgroups, renumbered by `Fin k`;
  `exists_one_eq_sum_zsmul_inducedClassFunction_one_complex` casts it from `ℚ` to `ℂ`.

## Literature

Solomon's theorem is not stated in Serre's book, and no text of it was found in the literature
library. The proof follows patterns of Serre's proof of Brauer's theorem, read in the library copy of
Jean-Pierre Serre, *Linear Representations of Finite Groups*, Springer GTM 42 (1977) (literature
`serre-1977-linear-representations`):

- Exercise 7.2, printed p.57: the character of the permutation representation on `G/H` is
  `Ind_H^G(1)` (here `inducedClassFunction_one_eq_permCharacter`).
- §12.6, printed pp.97–98: `Γ_K`-`p`-elementary subgroups `C · P` (a `p`-group `P` acting on a cyclic
  `C` of order prime to `p` through `Γ_K`). For `K = ℚ` they are exactly the semidirect products, the
  `p`-hyperelementary subgroups of this file. Theorems 27 and 28 (p.98): induction from them is
  surjective onto `R_K(G)`; Proposition 36 (p.98) uses the resulting identity `1 = ∑ Ind_H^G f_H`.
  Solomon's theorem is the sharper statement with `f_H` multiples of the unit character.
- §12.7, Lemma 17, printed p.100: for a `p'`-element `x`, a Sylow `p`-subgroup `P` of `N(x)` gives the
  `Γ_K`-`p`-elementary subgroup `C · P`; for `K = ℚ`, `N(x)` is the normalizer of `C = ⟨x⟩`. This is the
  subgroup of `exists_isHyperelementary_not_dvd`. Compare §10.1, p.74, and §10.3 Lemma 8, p.77, for
  `p`-elementary subgroups.
- §11.4, printed pp.86–87: the prime ideals of `A ⊗ R(G)` lie under those `{f | f(c) ∈ M}` of the ring of
  class functions (Propositions 30, 30'), and Remark (1), p.87: an ideal is the whole ring if it lies
  in none of them, "the approach taken in the proof of Brauer's theorem" (also Exercise 11.7, p.89).
  `one_mem_hyperelementaryLattice` argues in this way in the ring of permutation characters.
