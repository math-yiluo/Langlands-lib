# Support-theory targets for the support categories `Alg({I})`

Implementation and documentation: Worker 379 (task
`0a1bd7fb-66c2-4dcc-a3c8-6619f6fa10b4`, the complex-and-targets unit of the
general Aubert program `46df6a68-5db4-4428-8044-05151d6ce1f6`).

This module records, as separate proposition targets, the facts about cuspidal
supports that the corrected Theorem 3.6 of Aubert's erratum and its consequences
use. They connect the predicates of `SupportCategory` (cuspidality, the cuspidal
series `𝔈(I)` and the support category `Alg({I})`) to the general
cuspidal-support theory of Renard, Chapter VI. The Library has no general
(non-`GL`) cuspidal-support theory, so each fact is a target here. None is
asserted, and nothing in this module is proved.

## Data and conventions

All targets are `Prop`-valued definitions in namespace `Langlands.Aubert.General`
of the following explicit data (the generic layer of `SupportCategory`):

* `G` with `[Group G] [TopologicalSpace G] [LocallyCompactSpace G]
  [NonarchimedeanGroup G]`, and an index type `ι` of simple roots;
* families `P M U : Finset ι → Subgroup G` (standard parabolics, standard Levis
  `L_I = M I`, unipotent radicals), with `h : ∀ I, IsSemidirectDecomposition
  (P I) (M I) (U I)` and `hU : ∀ I, IsClosed (U I)`;
* `hM : ∀ I, IsClosed (M I)` and the Levi-in-Levi decompositions
  `hL : ∀ I (K : Finset I), IsSemidirectDecomposition ((P (leviIndex I K)).subgroupOf (M I)) …`,
  given for every `I` (in `SupportCategory` they are given for one `I` at a time);
* the association relation `IsAssociate : Finset ι → Finset ι → G → Prop`.

Representations of `G` have carriers in `Type (max uG uV)`, as in
`IsInSupportCategory`.

"`L_J` is associated to `L_I` through `n`" always means the pair of conditions
used by `IsInSupportCategory`: `IsAssociate I J n` and
`∀ x, x ∈ M J ↔ n⁻¹ * x * n ∈ M I` (that is, `M J = n (M I) n⁻¹`). Both
conditions appear in every hypothesis and conclusion about association. This keeps
the targets meaningful for both intended instantiations of `IsAssociate` described
in `SupportCategory.md`: the Weyl relation "`n` represents `v ∈ W` with
`v(I) = J`", and the trivial relation, for which association means
`G`-conjugacy of the standard Levis.

"Zero" for a representation or a Jacquet module means that its carrier is a
subsingleton. `r_J` is `jacquetFunctor (h J) (hU J)`, the normalized Jacquet
functor of Worker 23's adapter, and `i_K ∘ r_K` is
`inductionJacquetFunctor (h K) (hU K)`.

Aubert's "cuspidale" (vanishing of all proper Jacquet modules, p. 2181) is
Renard's "supercuspidale" (VI.2). The targets below translate Renard's statements
accordingly.

## Targets

### `CuspidalSeriesExistenceTarget`

Every irreducible `π` lies in some cuspidal series: there is `I` with
`IsInCuspidalSeries P M U h hU I (hM I) (hL I) π`.

Source: Renard, Corollaire VI.2.1, printed p. 139. For irreducible `π` there is a
standard parabolic `P = MN` and an irreducible supercuspidal `τ` of `M` with `π`
a subrepresentation of `i^G_P(τ)`; a subrepresentation is a subquotient. Aubert's
Notation 1.6 (p. 2181) describes `Irr(G)` as partitioned by the `𝔈(I)` up to
association, which uses this fact.

### `CuspidalSupportAssociationTarget`

If an irreducible `π` is a subquotient of `i^G_{L_I}(σ)` and of
`i^G_{L_{I'}}(σ')`, with `σ`, `σ'` irreducible and cuspidal for their standard
Levis, then there are `n : G` and `hn : M I' = n (M I) n⁻¹` with
`IsAssociate I I' n` and `σ' ≅ (conjugationFunctor n hn).obj σ`.

Source: Renard, Théorème VI.5.4, implication (iv) ⇒ (i), printed pp. 173–174:
the cuspidal data `(M, ρ)` and `(M', ρ')` are associated, that is
`g M g⁻¹ = M'` and `ρ^g ≅ ρ'` for some `g ∈ G`. Since `ρ^g` is a
representation of `M' = g M g⁻¹`, it is `x ↦ ρ(g⁻¹ x g)`, which is
`conjugationFunctor g`. For the trivial
instantiation of `IsAssociate` the target is exactly Renard's (i) for standard
Levis. For the Weyl instantiation it also asks that `g` can be taken to represent
a Weyl element with `v(I) = I'`. This holds because conjugate standard Levis are
`W`-associated and because `N_G(L_I)` is generated by `L_I` and representatives
of Weyl elements. These are structural facts about the reductive group (ledger
requirement A02); for `Ad(m)σ ≅ σ` with `m ∈ L_I` the intertwiner is `σ(m)`.

### `SupportCategoryAssociationInvarianceTarget`

If `L_{I'}` is associated to `L_I` through `n`, every member of `Alg({I})` is a
member of `Alg({I'})`.

Source: erratum, p. 4687: "`Alg({I})` ne dépend que de la classe d'association
de `L_I`".

### `SupportCategoryIrreducibleJacquetDetectionTarget`

If `π` is irreducible and lies in `Alg({I})`, there are `J` and `n` such that
`L_J` is associated to `L_I` through `n` and `r_J(π)` is nonzero.

Source: the proof of Renard, Théorème VI.5.4, printed p. 174: for an irreducible
subquotient `π₀` of `i^G_P(ρ)`, Corollaire VI.2.1 gives a standard `Q = LU` with
`r^G_Q(π₀)` nonzero and supercuspidal, and Proposition VI.5.3 (ii) shows that `L`
and `M` are conjugate. Only some associated `J` is asserted. The Jacquet module
along a particular associated standard Levi may vanish.

### `SupportCategoryZeroDetectionTarget`

If `E ∈ Alg({I})` and `r_J(E)` is zero for every `J` and `n` such that `L_J` is
associated to `L_I` through `n`, then `E` is zero.

Source: erratum, p. 4687, the first sentence of the proof: "Par définition de la
catégorie `Alg({I})`, l'exactitude de la suite (∗) est équivalente à celle, pour
tout `I` dans `{I}`, de la suite" obtained by applying `r^G_{L_I}`. The case of a
single object is this target. The companion module `SupportCategoryReductions`
proves that it follows from `SupportCategoryIrreducibleJacquetDetectionTarget`
when every `U J` is a limit of compact subgroups.

### `SupportCategoryExactnessDetectionTarget`

For intertwiners `f : A ⟶ B` and `g : B ⟶ C` of smooth representations with
`B ∈ Alg({I})`, `Function.Exact (hom f) (hom g)` holds if and only if, for every
`J`, `n` such that `L_J` is associated to `L_I` through `n`,
`Function.Exact` holds for the images of `f` and `g` under `r_J`.

Source: the same sentence of the erratum, p. 4687, for a single position of the
sequence (∗); exactness of a sequence is exactness at each of its terms. The
direction "exact ⇒ exact after `r_J`" is exactness of the Jacquet functor; the
converse uses zero detection for the homology at `B`, which is a subquotient of
`B` and hence in `Alg({I})`. This deduction is not formalized here.

### `SupportCategoryInductionJacquetTarget`

For every `I`, `K` and `E ∈ Alg({I})`, `i_K r_K (E) ∈ Alg({I})`. Here
`i_K r_K (E)` is `(inductionJacquetFunctor (h K) (hU K)).obj E`, the term `E_K`
of Aubert's complex.

Source: Renard, Théorème VI.7.2 (Bernstein decomposition, printed p. 182) and
Proposition VI.7.3 (printed p. 185): `r^G_P` maps the block `M(G)_t` into the
blocks of `M` over `t`, and `i^G_P` maps `M(M)_s` into `M(G)_{i_{MG}(s)}`.
`Alg({I})` is the union of the blocks whose cuspidal Levi is associated to `L_I`.

### `SupportCategoryJacquetVanishingTarget`

If there are no `J ⊆ K` and `n` such that `L_J` is associated to `L_I` through
`n`, then `r_K(E)` is zero for every `E ∈ Alg({I})`.

Source: Renard, Proposition VI.5.3 (i), printed p. 173 (`r^G_Q i^G_P ρ = 0` when
`L` has no Levi subgroup conjugate to `M`), together with Proposition VI.7.3
(printed p. 185) to pass from induced representations to all members of the block.
The standard Levis of `L_K` are the `L_J` with `J ⊆ K` (Renard V.3.11, as
recalled in the proof of Corollaire VI.2.1).

### `SupportCategoryDecompositionTarget`

Every smooth `E` has subrepresentations `S I`, one for each `I : Finset ι`, such
that `SmoothRep.subrepresentation E (S I)` lies in `Alg({I})` for every `I` and
the carrier of `E` is the internal direct sum of the `S I`
(`DirectSum.IsInternal` of the submodules `(S I).toSubmodule`, with classical
decidable equality on `Finset ι`). Summands may be zero, and no uniqueness is
asserted.

Source: Renard, Théorème VI.7.2, printed p. 182: every smooth representation is
the direct sum of its components `V(Ω)` over the connected components `Ω` of the
variety of cuspidal data, and `M(G)_Ω` is the full subcategory of representations
all of whose irreducible subquotients lie in `Irr(G)_Ω`. Grouping the components
by the association class of the cuspidal Levi, and choosing a standard
representative `L_I` of each class, gives summands in `Alg({I})`; the other
summands are zero. This is the support decomposition used to extend the class
formula of Corollary 3.9 (a) from one support type to every finite-length
representation.

## Faithfulness and scope

* The targets quantify over all smooth representations, with no finite-length or
  admissibility condition, as in the erratum's full smooth scope.
* For an arbitrary family of the stated shape the targets can be false. They are
  meant for the standard families of the rational points of a connected reductive
  group over a nonarchimedean local field (any characteristic, nonsplit and
  non-quasisplit groups, semisimple rank zero included), with the Weyl or trivial
  association relation. For such data each target is a theorem of the cited
  sources, given the structural identifications of ledger requirements A01–A02.
* The cited proofs (Renard VI.2–VI.7) use the geometric lemma, Jacquet's
  finiteness results and the Bernstein decomposition, not a classification of
  irreducible representations.
