# The whole-group term of the Aubert operator

This internal endpoint comparison is derived from the published definitions of normalized induction, normalized Jacquet restriction and the exact finite-length Grothendieck group. It applies to every locally compact nonarchimedean group, with independent group and coefficient-carrier universes, when the actual semidirect decomposition has `P = M = top`, `U = bot`, and a supplied proof `hU : IsClosed (U : Set G)`. Closedness of this trivial radical is automatic in a Hausdorff group; the Lean classes `LocallyCompactSpace` and `NonarchimedeanGroup` alone impose no separation condition. The closedness proof is required for the actual normalization characters in both `endpointInductionJacquetIso` and the exact-K0 identity.

Conjugation on the trivial subgroup is the identity, so its Haar modulus and both normalizing characters equal one. The coinvariant relation submodule is zero, identifying the actual quotient with the original coefficient module. The semidirect projection at the endpoint is the identity on ambient elements. These identifications give `endpointInducingEquiv`. Postcomposition of smooth induced functions by that equivalence, followed by evaluation at the identity (`Induction.smoothInducedTopEquiv`), gives `endpointInductionJacquetIso` for every smooth representation. No finite-length premise is needed for that isomorphism.

`endpoint_inductionK0_comp_jacquetK0` applies the published exact-functor class formulas to this isomorphism. `FiniteLengthGrothendieckGroup.hom_ext_object` extends the equality to the whole exact K0 group, which retains all actual short exact sequence relations, including nonsplit ones. The supplied preservation premises have precisely the scope required to define the K0 maps.

The argument is an internal implementation lemma for the full-subset term in Aubert's alternating operator. It does not prove general involutivity. It uses no classification, character independence, geometric lemma, adjointness, or cuspidal-support result. The generic evaluation comparison resides in the published `Induction.SingleBlock` module; its declaration is over arbitrary topological groups and modules, so its use here imposes no general linear group specialization.
