# Twisted root support under actual point-Coxeter comparisons

This internal construction uses the actual character lattice and the actual
point normalizer/full-centralizer Weyl quotient. Work over an arbitrary field
`k : Type u` with an affine group scheme `G`, a commutative group scheme `T`,
and a monomorphism `i : T.toGrp ⟶ G`. The normalizer and centralizer point
comparisons `hN` and `hZ` are explicit. The cocharacter `ν` determines all
positive relative roots and the indecomposable positive roots used as the
simple labels. The full relative-root set may be nonreduced.

The definition `pointTwistedStandardPositiveZeroSupport` is the additive
submonoid of characters in the monoid generated by `rootSet i ν J` whose
image under the actual point Weyl action of `w` pairs to zero with `ζ`.
Additivity of the action and pairing proves closure under addition.

The remaining results take an actual Coxeter system `cs` with those simple
labels and the explicit `RootLengthComparison`. For a minimum representative
`w` of the `I,J` double coset, `point_weyl_simple_positive_of_isMinimal`
deduces positivity of the images of the `J` simple roots from the published
minimum-length criterion.

Given a standard cocharacter `ζ` for `rootSet i ν I`,
`point_weyl_inv_positive_of_pairing_eq_zero` proves that any positive root
with zero `ζ` pairing has positive inverse image under `w`. The proof
inducts on positive roots using the published positive-root induction.
For a simple root, zero pairing forces membership in `I`, and inverse
minimality gives positivity. For a sum of positive roots, nonnegativity of
both standard pairings forces each pairing to vanish; the two strictly
positive inverse pairings then add.

`point_weyl_simple_pairing_eq_zero_iff` proves that the image of a `J` simple
root has zero pairing precisely when it equals a simple root in `I`.
For the forward direction, a decomposition of the image into two positive
roots would, by the preceding inverse-positivity result, decompose the
original simple root into two positive roots. Indecomposability excludes
this. The standard cocharacter then forces the resulting simple image
into `I`. The converse uses its defining vanishing on `I`.

With a finite simple-label type and the explicit
`PositiveSimpleReflectionComparison`,
`point_mem_intersectionSubset_iff_pairing_eq_zero` identifies membership in
the intrinsic Coxeter `intersectionSubset cs I J w` with membership in `J`
and zero transported pairing. A conjugate simple reflection in the left
standard subgroup is a left simple reflection at a minimum representative;
the supplied comparison identifies its actual root label.

`point_pairing_weyl_nonneg_of_mem_closure` extends transported nonnegativity
to every element of the right-face positive monoid. The theorem
`point_mem_closure_intersectionSubset_iff` uses additive-monoid closure
induction and this nonnegativity to split a vanishing sum into vanishing
summands. Finally, `pointTwistedStandardPositiveZeroSupport_eq` identifies
the defined monoid with the monoid generated by the intrinsic intersection
subset. These claims concern all monoid elements, including those that are
not roots.

The proof route was developed by Workers 181 and 1186. It uses the published
positive-root induction and intrinsic Coxeter minimum/conjugacy results;
the monoid packaging follows the published scheme-action root-support
construction. It requires neither a scheme Coxeter realization, genericity
of `ν`, linear independence of the simple roots, nor a root-system or
connected-reductive hypothesis. The finite simple-label instance is needed
only for the intrinsic intersection identification and its consequences.
Both point comparisons and both root-sign comparisons remain explicit
where used. This unit supplies no dynamic subgroup equality, no existence
proof for those comparisons, and no proof of the final rational Aubert
compatibility proposition.
