# Aubert duality exchanges the actual segment endpoints

Take any segment of length \(k\) and total rank \(n\) over an
arbitrary nonarchimedean local field. Its two endpoints are the
actual irreducible socle and special irreducible head of the
increasing normalized product. The proposition applies to any
smooth complex representatives of these actual endpoint objects.

On their exact Grothendieck classes the unadjusted matrix Aubert
operator exchanges the endpoints with sign \((-1)^{n-k}\).
The support-adjusted operator exchanges their classes. The maps
use the actual short-exact relations, with their finite-length and
exactness inputs explicit.

These are the segment identities in Aubert (1995), Remark 2.2
and the proof of Theorem 2.3, printed pp. 2184–2185. The reverse
identity follows from involutivity. Zelevinsky (1980), §§3.1 and
9.1, identifies the two endpoints used in the formula. [Zelevinsky (1980)](https://www.numdam.org/item/ASENS_1980_4_13_2_165_0/)
