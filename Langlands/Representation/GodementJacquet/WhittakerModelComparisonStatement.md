# Matrix-coefficient and Whittaker integrals on the actual coefficient model

Author: Worker 902.

This module defines the closed proposition
`Langlands.GodementJacquet.WhittakerModelComparisonStatement.{uF,uV}`.
It states the comparison `I₂ = I₃` of JPSS Section (5.2) for the actual
right-translation representation on the Whittaker coefficient image of a
representation of Whittaker type. It neither proves nor assumes the
comparison. It is a substantive analytic statement, not a definitional
equality, and it does not follow from any functional-equation premise (none
is present).

## The source comparison

Printed 420, (5.2): "As before σ is a representation of `G_t` of Whittaker
type. We also assume that σ acts on `W(σ; ψ)`." Besides the integrals (1)
`Ψ(s, W)` over `F^×`, JPSS consider

- (2) `Z(Φ, s, f) = ∫_{G_t} Φ(g) f(g) |det g|^s dg`, where `Φ` is a
  Schwartz–Bruhat function on `M(t × t, F)` and `f ∈ 𝒜(σ)`, the space of
  bi-K-finite coefficients of σ;
- (3) `Z(Φ, s, W) = ∫_{G_t} Φ(g) W(g) |det g|^s dg` with `W ∈ W(σ; ψ)`.

Printed 421: `I₁`, `I₂ = I₂(σ)`, `I₃ = I₃(σ)` are the fractional ideals of
`ℂ[X, X⁻¹]`, `X = q^(-s)`, generated by the integrals (1), by
`Z(Φ, s + (t−1)/2, f)`, and by `Z(Φ, s + (t−1)/2, W)` respectively. JPSS
recall `I₁ = I₃` from [J-P-S I] (Theorem 4.3 and Remark 4.2) and
`I₃ ⊂ I₂` from [J-P-S I], page 186, and then prove `I₂ ⊂ I₃`: for an open
compact subgroup `Ω` and `h ∈ G_t`, the form `μ(W) = ∫_Ω W(hω) dω` on
`W(σ; ψ)` is Ω-invariant, these forms span the K-finite linear forms, so
each element of `𝒜(σ)` is a finite sum of functions
`f(g) = ∫_Ω W(hωg) dω`; for `Re(s)` large a change of variables gives
`Z(Φ, s, f) = ∫ W₁(g) Φ₁(g) |det g|^s dg` with `W₁(g) = W(gh)` in
`W(σ; ψ)` and `Φ₁(x) = ∫_Ω Φ(ωh⁻¹xh) dω` again a Schwartz–Bruhat function
on the full matrix space (the scan prints `S(r × r, F)`; the matrices are
`t × t`). Hence `I₂ ⊂ I₃` and `I₁ = I₂`.

In [J-P-S I], (3.1.3)–(3.1.4) on printed 186 give the reverse averaging: the
function `f(g) = ∫_Ω W(ωg) dω` is a matrix coefficient and
`Z(Φ, s, W) = Z(Φ, s, f)` for suitable `Ω` (stated there for irreducible
generic representations, with the measure `d^×g`). Remark 2.2.5 (printed
183) extends the convergence and rationality results to any space of
right-smooth `(N, ψ)`-equivariant functions stable under right translation
on which the representation is finitely generated and admissible; Remark 4.2
(printed 190) does the same for (4.1.4) and Theorem 4.3, with "g.c.d." in
place of `L`.

This module states the identity of the last two Laurent modules,
**`I₂ = I₃`, for σ replaced by the representation on its coefficient
model**, which is exactly JPSS's standing assumption that σ acts on
`W(σ; ψ)`. The comparison `I₁ = I₃` is a separate sibling statement.

## Quantifiers and hypotheses

The proposition quantifies over every prime `p`, every
`F : PadicField.{uF} p` with its canonical topology
`F.canonicalTopologicalData.topology` (installed by `letI`), every bundled
`ψ : HarmonicAnalysis.UnitaryAdditiveCharacter F.Carrier` for that topology
(the structure includes nontriviality, as JPSS require of ψ), every
`n : ℕ`, an independent carrier `V : Type uV` with `[AddCommGroup V]
[Module ℂ V]`, and every actual
`σ : Representation ℂ (Langlands.GeneralLinear (n + 1) F.Carrier) V`.

The hypotheses are exactly:

1. `RankinSelberg.IsOfWhittakerType n (Whittaker.standardCharacter ψ n) σ`,
   the Library's full JPSS Section 2.1 predicate: `IsAdmissible σ`
   (smoothness and finite-dimensional fixed vectors for every open
   subgroup), finite generation of `σ.asModule` over
   `MonoidAlgebra ℂ (GL_(n+1)(F))`, and a linear equivalence of the space of
   standard Whittaker functionals with `ℂ`;
2. `ℓ : Whittaker.glFunctionals ψ n σ` with `ℓ.val ≠ 0`.

Reducible σ is allowed. There is no irreducibility premise, no chosen
equivalence between σ and its coefficient model (the published
`glModelEquiv` needs irreducibility and is not used), no admissibility or
finite-generation premise on the model, no equality of modules, no
convergence or rationality premise, and no existence of a g.c.d. or
L-factor.

JPSS's rank `t` is the Library's actual rank `n + 1`. The case `n = 0`, the
actual `GL(1)` case with trivial upper unipotent group and trivial standard
character, is included and not treated separately.

## The conclusion

After installing the canonical topology by `letI`, the conclusion is

```text
Padic.jpssI2 F (n := n) (V := (glCoefficientModel ψ n σ ℓ).toSubmodule)
    (glModelRepresentation ψ n σ ℓ)
  = Padic.jpssI3 F ψ σ ℓ
```

an equality of `LaurentCoefficients`-submodules of `RationalFunctions`. The
named arguments only guide elaboration of the subtype carrier's instances;
the term is `Padic.jpssI2 F (glModelRepresentation ψ n σ ℓ)`.

Both sides are the published definitions of
`Langlands.Representation.GodementJacquet.Zeta`, with no new data:

- **Left side (`I₂`).** `glCoefficientModel ψ n σ ℓ` is the actual image of
  `v ↦ (g ↦ ℓ(σ(g)v))` in the `(N, ψ)`-equivariant functions, and
  `glModelRepresentation ψ n σ ℓ` is right translation on it. Its carrier is
  a space of functions on `GL_(n+1)(F.Carrier)`, so this side is
  `jpssI2.{uF,uF}`. `Padic.jpssI2` is the Laurent span `zetaSpan` of the
  rational values coming from `biSmoothMatrixCoefficients` of that model
  representation: complex spans of `g ↦ λ(ρ(g)w)` with `λ` a smooth
  functional and `w` a smooth vector of the model. This is JPSS's `𝒜(σ)`
  after σ is replaced by its action on `W(σ; ψ)`.
- **Right side (`I₃`).** `Padic.jpssI3 F ψ σ ℓ` (here `jpssI3.{uF,uV}`) is
  the Laurent span of the rational values of the integrals of the actual
  functions `glModelFunction ψ n σ ℓ w` in the same coefficient image. This
  is JPSS's `W(σ; ψ)`.

In both wrappers a value is a rational function `R` for which some
full-matrix Schwartz–Bruhat function `Φ` (`MatrixTestFunction n F.Carrier`,
singular matrices included) and some function `f` of the relevant space
(a bi-smooth coefficient of the model, respectively a model function)
satisfy `HasShiftedRationalValue`: the integral `∫ Φ(g) f(g) |det g|^(s + n/2) dg`
converges absolutely on some right half-plane in `s`, and
`s ↦ ∫ Φ(g) f(g) |det g|^(s + n/2) dg` has rational continuation `R` in
`X = q^(-s)`. Here the integral has exponent `s` as in (2),(3), the shift is
`jpssShift n = (n : ℂ)/2 = (t−1)/2`, the measure is the shared normalized
`generalLinearHaar F.Carrier n` on the Borel space of `GL_(n+1)(F.Carrier)`,
`|·|` is `normalizedAbsoluteValue F.canonicalTopologicalData`, and
`q = F.canonicalTopologicalData.residueCardinality`. JPSS's unnormalized
Haar measure `dg` only rescales every value by one positive constant, which
does not change a Laurent span. No quotient integration measure, additive
Fourier or self-dual measure is involved, and no functional equation is
asserted.

## Why the model and not the original σ

For reducible σ the coefficient space of σ itself can be larger than that
of its coefficient model, so `I₂(σ)` and `I₃(σ, ℓ)` need not agree. JPSS
avoid this by assuming that σ acts on `W(σ; ψ)`. Printed 422 (PDF 57), in
(5.3), makes the distinction explicit: with `σ₀` the representation of
`G_t` on `W(σ; ψ)`, "since `σ₀` is a quotient of σ, we have
`𝒜(σ₀) ⊆ 𝒜(σ)` and therefore `I₂(σ) ⊇ I₂(σ₀)`", and (5.2) is applied to
`σ₀`. This statement therefore does **not** assert `I₂(σ) = I₃(σ, ℓ)` for
the original carrier `V`; the matrix-coefficient side is the representation
`σ₀ = glModelRepresentation ψ n σ ℓ` on the actual coefficient model.

## Provenance and derived features

The statement follows JPSS (5.2) with these Library-level formulations:

- **Laurent spans instead of g.c.d. ideals.** JPSS call `I₂`, `I₃` the
  ideals generated by the integrals and immediately use that they have a
  "g.c.d.". Here both sides are only the Laurent spans of the actual
  rational values; no common denominator, fractional ideal, or normalized
  generator is asserted.
- **Rational continuations with a convergence witness**, through the
  published `HasShiftedRationalValue`, rather than formal identities of
  integrals.
- **Bi-smooth coefficients** packaging JPSS's bi-K-finite coefficients with
  explicit smoothness witnesses, as documented in the `Zeta` module.
- **Equality for every nonzero standard functional `ℓ`**, rather than for
  the single model `W(σ; ψ)` of the source; under the Whittaker-type
  premise all such `ℓ` are proportional.
- **Canonical p-adic normalizations** as described above.

## What a proof must supply

A separate exact-target proof must establish both inclusions on the actual
model, for instance by the source's route:

- `I₂ ⊆ I₃` by the averaging argument of printed 421, which uses that the
  forms `W ↦ ∫_Ω W(hω) dω` span the smooth dual of the model (an
  admissibility argument), a change of variables, and the transformed
  full-matrix test function `Φ₁`;
- `I₃ ⊆ I₂` by the averaging of [J-P-S I] (3.1.3)–(3.1.4);
- in both directions, absolute convergence on a common right half-plane
  and rationality of the integrals of the functions in the model, as in
  [J-P-S I] Remarks 2.2.5 and 4.2, so that equal integrals transfer to
  equal rational continuations.

The complete original premise supplies the needed model properties without
new hypotheses. The published `Whittaker.modelMap` is a surjective
intertwiner from σ onto the coefficient model (`modelMap_surjective`).
Admissibility can descend along it by
`Langlands.Unramified.SphericalExistence.isAdmissible_of_surjective`, with
compact-open cofinality in `GL_(n+1)` from
`Langlands.Unramified.SphericalExistence.exists_compactOpenSubgroup_le`
(in `Langlands.Representation.Unramified.SphericalConstituentExistenceProof`).
Finite generation over the group algebra can descend by
`Module.Finite.of_surjective` after converting the intertwining map to a
module map. These are future proof routes; this module proves none of
these specializations.

Conversely, this statement by itself asserts no nonzero value, common
denominator, normalized generator, product-ideal identity, functional
equation, generic-socle theorem, multiplicativity, or `I₁ = I₃`.
