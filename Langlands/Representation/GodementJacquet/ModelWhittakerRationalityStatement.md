# Convergence and rationality of each Whittaker matrix integral on the model

## Statement

`Langlands.GodementJacquet.ModelWhittakerRationalityStatement` is a
proposition. It states the following.

- Let `p` be a prime, `F` a canonical p-adic field with its canonical topology,
  `ψ` a nontrivial unitary additive character of `F`, and `n` a natural number,
  including `n = 0`.
- Let `σ` be a representation of `GL_(n+1)(F)` on a complex vector space `V` in
  an arbitrary universe. Assume `σ` is of Whittaker type for the standard
  character attached to `ψ`, in the Library sense `IsOfWhittakerType`: it is
  admissible, finitely generated over the group algebra, and its space of
  standard Whittaker functionals is one-dimensional. It may be reducible.
- Let `ℓ` be a nonzero standard Whittaker functional. Its coefficient model
  `glCoefficientModel ψ n σ ℓ` is the space of functions `g ↦ ℓ(σ(g) v)`.

Then for every Schwartz–Bruhat function `Φ` on the full space of
`(n+1) × (n+1)` matrices, singular matrices included, and every function `W` in
that coefficient model, two things hold:

1. **Convergence.** There is a real `a` such that for every complex `s` with
   `Re s > a`, the function `g ↦ Φ(g) W(g) |det g|^(s + n/2)` is integrable for
   the normalized Haar measure `generalLinearHaar` of `GL_(n+1)(F)`. This is
   `Padic.MatrixConverges F Φ (s + jpssShift n) W`.
2. **Rationality.** There is a rational function `R` such that
   `HasRationalContinuation q (fun s => Padic.matrixIntegral F Φ (s + n/2) W) R`
   holds, where `q` is the residue cardinality. That is, on some right
   half-plane the denominator of `R` does not vanish at `q^(-s)` and the integral
   equals `R(q^(-s))`.

The half-planes and `R` may depend on `Φ` and `W`. The shift `n/2` is the complex
number `jpssShift n`. The absolute value is the canonical normalized one. The
statement adds no premise of irreducibility, model admissibility, convergence or
factor existence.

Together the two parts give
`HasShiftedRationalValue q abv (generalLinearHaar F n) Φ W R` from
`Langlands.Representation.GodementJacquet.Zeta`, for the canonical
normalizations. So every pair `(Φ, W)` contributes a value to
`Padic.whittakerRationalValues`.

## Source

Jacquet, Piatetski-Shapiro and Shalika, *Rankin–Selberg convolutions* (1983),
Section (5.2), printed pages 420–421. Section (5.1), printed page 420, says that
(5.2) completes certain results of their earlier paper [J-P-S I]. In (5.2), `σ`
is a representation of `G_t = GL_t(F)` of Whittaker type acting on its Whittaker
model `𝒲(σ; ψ)`. Equation (3) defines
`Z(Φ, s, W) = ∫ Φ(g) W(g) |det g|^s dg` for a Schwartz–Bruhat function `Φ` on
`M(t × t, F)` and `W ∈ 𝒲(σ; ψ)`. The text right after it, on printed page 421,
says: "These integrals converge for Re(s) large, define rational functions of
`X = q^(-s)` and admit a "g.c.d."". The ideal `I₃(σ)` is then generated by the
shifted integrals `Z(Φ, s + (t − 1)/2, W)`.

JPSS Section (2.1), printed page 385, defines "Whittaker type": admissible, of
finite type, and with a one-dimensional space of `θ`-equivariant linear forms.
The Library's `IsOfWhittakerType` formalizes exactly these three conditions, so
no irreducibility is assumed.

[J-P-S I] is Jacquet, Piatetski-Shapiro and Shalika, *Automorphic forms on
GL(3) I* (1979). There, for an irreducible generic `π` (Proposition (3.1),
printed pages 185–186), convergence in a half-space is obtained by majorizing
`W` by a gauge (Proposition (2.3.6), printed page 184). Identity (3.1.4),
printed page 186, then gives `Z(Φ, s, W) = Z(Φ, s, f)` for `Re s` large. Here
`f(g) = ∫_Ω W(ωg) dω` is a matrix coefficient, so the right side is a
Godement–Jacquet integral. Remark (2.2.5), printed page 183, extends the
conclusion of their Proposition (2.2) to finitely generated admissible spaces
of Whittaker functions.

Formalization choices:

- The source's Whittaker model `𝒲(σ; ψ)` is the Library's coefficient image of
  the given functional `ℓ`, not an abstract model. The integrals depend only on
  the function `W`, so the statement does not need `σ` to act on its model.
- `t = n + 1`. The integral is stated with the shift `s + n/2 = s + (t − 1)/2`,
  the form in which it generates `I₃` (`Padic.jpssI3`). The unshifted form is
  the same assertion after replacing `s` by `s − n/2`. This moves the half-plane
  by `n/2` and multiplies the variable `q^(-s)` by the constant `q^(n/2)`.
- "Rational function of `q^(-s)`" uses the Library's
  `HasRationalContinuation`, which requires agreement and denominator
  nonvanishing on a right half-plane.
- The source also says that the integrals admit a "g.c.d.". That assertion is
  omitted, and so is any half-plane or denominator common to all `Φ` and `W`.
  The proposition asserts only convergence and rationality of each integral.

## Use

In the proof of the inclusion `I₂ ⊆ I₃` in JPSS Section (5.2), printed page 421,
a bi-`K`-finite matrix coefficient of `σ` is a finite sum of compact-open
averages of right-translated Whittaker functions. Convergence of the integral of
the sum does not give convergence of the integral of each summand. To split the
integral and add the rational continuations of the summands, each summand's
integral must converge on its own and be rational. This proposition provides
exactly that. It is intended as a premise for a proof of
`Langlands.GodementJacquet.WhittakerModelComparisonStatement` (`I₂ = I₃` on the
coefficient model).
