# Finite character sums under a change of variables

Let `A` be a finite abelian group, `K` a finite group, and `E : A ≃ K` a bijection of their underlying sets. Let `f` be the sum of a finite set of distinct complex additive characters of `A`. `FiniteOrbitCharacterStatement` assumes that `f ∘ E⁻¹` is conjugation invariant and that a bijection `Φ : A × A ≃ A × A` preserves both values of `f` and satisfies `E ((Φ(x,y)).1 + (Φ(x,y)).2) = E(x) E(y)`. It states that some finite-dimensional representation of `K` has character `f ∘ E⁻¹`.

The proof of `finiteOrbitCharacter` expands the additive convolution of `f` with itself. Orthogonality makes its value at `z` equal to `|A| f(z)`. The bijection carries each product fiber to the corresponding addition fiber, so the same formula holds for the finite sum defining group convolution. Thus `|K|⁻¹ (f ∘ E⁻¹)` is the coefficient function of a central idempotent in `ℂ[K]`. Applying `centralIdempotentCharacter` and taking the dual representation supplies the required character.

The finite-sum comparison is the product-fiber argument used to transport additive orbit idempotents to class functions on compact groups. In applications where the conjugators come from a larger group, the finite set of additive characters can be an orbit for that larger group. Its orbit sum has the two required invariances whenever the change of variables acts on each coordinate by one of those conjugators. [Boyarchenko and Sabitova, *The orbit method for profinite groups and a p-adic analogue of Brown's theorem*, Theorem 2.6 and Proposition 2.7(b)](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0608126v1)
