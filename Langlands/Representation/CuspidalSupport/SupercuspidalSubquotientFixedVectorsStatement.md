## The statement

In the represented-parabolic setting of the published VI.10.4 statements
(nonarchimedean local field `k` with `T2Space`, connected reductive `G`, canonical
topology `RationalPointFamily.pointTopology`, `LocallyCompactSpace` and
`NonarchimedeanGroup` binders), for every compact open subgroup `K` of `G(F)` and
every `V : SmoothRep.{0, u, v} ℂ G(F)` whose `K`-fixed vectors generate it,
every subquotient `D` of `V` (published `SmoothRep.IsSubquotient`) that is
supercuspidal and nontrivial has `fixedVectors D K ≠ ⊥`.

## Formalization choices

- **Generation.** "`V^K` engendre `V`" is: every subrepresentation whose
  underlying subspace contains `V^K` is `⊤`. The published VI.9.4 statement
  `StandardFixedVectorSubquotientGenerationStatement` uses the same encoding.
- **The hypothesis on `K`.** Renard assumes `K` contained and normal in the
  maximal compact `K₀`. His proof uses only that `K` is compact, hence
  contained in `⁰G`: it decomposes `V` by (VI.3.3.1) and restricts `V(τ)` to `⁰G`.
  The statement is therefore made for every compact open `K`, which is more
  general than the source. With the source's hypothesis it is the source
  Proposition.
- **Supercuspidality** is the published represented predicate: every normalized
  Jacquet module along a proper actual rational parabolic vanishes. "Non nul" is
  `Nontrivial D`.

## Why the statement is true

Following Renard. Let `τ` be an irreducible supercuspidal subquotient of `V`.
Decompose `V = V(τ) ⊕ W` by (VI.3.3.1), where every irreducible subquotient of
`V(τ)` is in the inertia class `[τ]` and none of `W` is. Then `V(τ) ≠ 0`, and it is
generated by its `K`-fixed vectors. Its restriction to `⁰G` is a direct sum of
copies of the compact irreducible components `E_i` of `τ|_{⁰G}`, which form one
`G`-orbit (VI.3.2). Hence some `E_i^K ≠ 0`, and so `τ^K ≠ 0` because `K ⊆ ⁰G`.
A nonzero supercuspidal subquotient `D` of `V` has an irreducible subquotient,
which is supercuspidal and a subquotient of `V`. Exactness of `K`-fixed vectors
(III.1.5) then gives `D^K ≠ 0`.
