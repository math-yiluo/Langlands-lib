# A common cuspidal-induction subquotient forces association

Authors: Workers 72 and 120. Worker 72 extracted the published direction;
Worker 120 supplied its canonical-object interface in the private parent assembly.

`Langlands.CuspidalSupport.CuspidalInductionReverseAssociationTheorem`
separates the common-subquotient-to-association direction of the published
`CuspidalInductionAssociationTheorem`. It is a proposition definition,
with no proof asserted by this pair.

For every prime, actual finite p-adic extension `F`, compatible topological
data `T`, and natural rank `n`, it considers two irreducible smooth cuspidal
representations of actual block Levis. If their canonical normalized
inductions have a common actual irreducible subquotient, their inducing
representations are associated. The field and two inducing-carrier universes
are independent. The assertion includes rank zero and adds no length,
dimension or extra theorem hypothesis.

`HaveCommonIrreducibleSubquotient` is the published predicate with an actual
invariant subspace and quotient in each induction, irreducibility of the
first quotient, and an actual representation equivalence to the second.
`RepresentationsAssociated` requires an actual coordinate permutation
conjugating the embedded Levis and a representation equivalence after the
corresponding inverse-conjugation transport.

The normalized induction is the existing smooth right-translation action
on functions with upper-parabolic covariance twisted by the canonical
positive half-modulus. `normalizedParabolicInduced` unfolds to the
induction used by the original association statement. Thus this interface
isolates precisely the reverse implication of that statement's third
equivalence. It does not replace the other implications or establish the
full association theorem.

The motivating original is Bernstein and Zelevinsky, *Induced
representations of reductive p-adic groups I* (1977), Theorem 2.9(a),
implication (iv) to (i), printed p.448/PDF9, with its proof in
section 2.15(1) using section 2.14(1), printed p.449/PDF10.
