# Normal restriction and inverse-conjugation covariance

This derived helper module implements the covariance used in Renard VI.3.2(i). Its main construction is `Langlands.NormalRestrictionOrbit.translateEquiv`, and `covariance` packages it together with preservation of irreducibility. It does not assert that a restriction has an irreducible subrepresentation. The group and representation universes are independent; no finite-index or dimension hypothesis is used.

The module uses the published `FiniteIndexRestriction.translate` and `translateOrderIso`, whose definitions and order equivalence apply to every normal subgroup. Translation there is preimage under the action. Thus translation with parameter `g⁻¹` is the image under `ρ(g)`. `translateEquiv` sends `w` to `ρ(g)w` and identifies the inverse-conjugate action `h ↦ σ(g⁻¹hg)` with the action on that image. Its inverse is the action of `g⁻¹`. The order equivalence preserves atoms, and the canonical module equivalence for a subrepresentation identifies atoms with irreducibility.

The preliminary `subrepresentationAsModuleEquiv` identifies the module of the restricted action on an invariant subspace with the corresponding submodule of the ambient group-algebra module, by the identity on vectors. `isIrreducible_iff_isSimpleModule` transports simplicity across that equivalence, and `isAtom_of_isIrreducible` uses the published order isomorphism between subrepresentations and submodules. The first three helpers work over any field. The translation helpers use complex scalars and a topological group only to match the existing inverse-conjugation API; smoothness and continuity of the representation are unnecessary.

The source motivation is David Renard, *Représentations des groupes réductifs p-adiques*, French edition dated 9 July 2026, VI.3.1(ii) and VI.3.2(i), printed pp.142–144 / PDF pp.148–150, registered source `renard-2026-representations-groupes-reductifs-padiques`, SHA-256 `7b539996793b7c3620e72e1e389588eb42d1a8e91d6f68b2eead5c2e2ffc182c`. The module generalizes the covariance argument to an arbitrary normal subgroup and supplies internal module bridges for its proof. It does not prove finite restriction, finite orbits, or constituent existence.
