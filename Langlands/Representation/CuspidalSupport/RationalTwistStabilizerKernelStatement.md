## Source

David Renard, *Représentations des groupes réductifs p-adiques*, French version dated
9 July 2026, registered as `renard-2026-representations-groupes-reductifs-padiques`, SHA256
`7b539996793b7c3620e72e1e389588eb42d1a8e91d6f68b2eead5c2e2ffc182c`. Worker 1222 read VI.4.4,
the preliminary paragraphs on complex tori and on `T` and the Remark that follows them, printed
pp.156–157 / PDF pp.162–163.

The preliminaries recall that for a complex torus `U` and a finite subgroup `J`, the character
lattice of `U/J` is the sublattice `L` of `X^*(U)` of characters trivial on `J`, and that the
algebra of polynomial functions on `U/J` is `ℂ[L] ≃ ℂ[X^*(U)]^J`. They apply this to the torus
`X(G)`, with character lattice `Λ(G) = G/⁰G` (V.2.4), and its finite subgroup (V.2.7)
`X(G)(π) = {ω ∈ X(G) | π ⊗ ω ≃ π}`:

> Le groupe des caractères algébriques du tore `X(G)/X(G)(π)` est donc un sous-réseau de
> `Λ(G) = G/⁰G`, que nous pouvons voir comme un sous-groupe de `G` contenant `⁰G`. Plus
> explicitement, il s'agit du groupe : `T = ⋂_{ω ∈ X(G)(π)} ker ω`.
>
> Si `ω ∈ X(G)(π)` alors la restriction de `ω` à `Z(G)` est triviale, d'où `⁰G Z(G) ⊂ T` et
> donc `T` est d'indice fini dans `G`.
>
> **Remarque.** La dualité entre tores et réseaux montre que `X(G)(π)` consiste exactement en
> les éléments de `X(G)` dont la restriction à `T` est triviale. De plus l'algèbre des
> fonctions polynomiales sur `X(G)/X(G)(π)` est alors `ℂ[T/⁰G] ≃ ℂ[Λ(G)]^{X(G)(π)}`. D'autre
> part, rappelons (remarques VI.3.3) que le tore `X(G)/X(G)(π)` s'identifie à la variété
> `Irr(G)[π]`. L'algèbre des fonctions polynomiales sur `Irr(G)[π]` s'identifie donc à
> `ℂ[T/⁰G]`.

Here `G` is the group of rational points of a connected reductive group over a
nonarchimedean local field `F` and `π` is an irreducible smooth representation of `G`.

The Library note `notes/renard/supercuspidal-block-center-progress.md` records the finite index
of `T` as unstated. The published `RationalSummandEndCenterStatement`,
`RationalSummandCenterGroupAlgebraStatement` and `RationalInertiaBlockCenterEvaluationStatement`
use the same `T` (an infimum of kernels) and replace the functions on `Irr(G)[π]` by
`ℂ[T/⁰G]` through this Remark. This module states the paragraph and the Remark themselves.
The finiteness of `X(G)(π)` is the published V.2.7 Lemma `RationalTwistStabilizerFiniteStatement`.

## Statement

`RationalTwistStabilizerKernelStatement` quantifies over a nonarchimedean local field
`F : Type uF` and a group scheme `G` over `Spec F` with `hG : GroupScheme.IsConnectedReductive G`,
with the instances of `RationalTwistStabilizerFiniteStatement`, and over every smooth complex
representation `A : SmoothRep.{0, uF, uV} ℂ (GroupScheme.rationalPoints G)` with
`A.IsIrreducible`. Write `X` for `X(G)`, `Stab(ω)` for `Nonempty (rationalUnramifiedTwist G ω A ≅ A)`
(Renard's `ω ∈ X(G)(π)`), and

```lean
T = ⨅ (ω : X) (_ : Nonempty (rationalUnramifiedTwist G ω A ≅ A)), ω.val.toMonoidHom.ker
```

(Renard's `T = ⋂_{ω ∈ X(G)(π)} ker ω`). The statement asserts the conjunction of:

1. `rationalCommonNormKernel G ≤ T` (`T` contains `⁰G`);
2. `Subgroup.center (GroupScheme.rationalPoints G) ≤ T` (`Z(G) ⊂ T`);
3. `T.FiniteIndex` (`T` has finite index in `G`);
4. for every `ω : X`, `Stab(ω) ↔ T ≤ ker ω` (first sentence of the Remark);
5. for every finitely supported `f : G(F) ⧸ ⁰G →₀ ℂ`: `f` is invariant under every `ω` with
   `Stab(ω)`, written `ω(g) · f([g]) = f([g])` for all `g`, exactly when `f([g]) ≠ 0`
   implies `g ∈ T` (that is, `ℂ[Λ(G)]^{X(G)(π)} = ℂ[T/⁰G]` inside `ℂ[Λ(G)]`);
6. there is a `ℂ`-algebra homomorphism
   `Θ : MonoidAlgebra ℂ (T ⧸ (rationalCommonNormKernel G).subgroupOf T) →ₐ[ℂ] (X → ℂ)` that is
   injective, sends the class of every `t ∈ T` to `unramifiedEvaluation ν t`, and whose range is
   exactly the set of `φ ∈ unramifiedRegularFunctions ν` with `φ (χ * s) = φ χ` for all `χ`
   and every `s` with `Stab(s)` (second sentence of the Remark: `ℂ[T/⁰G]` is the algebra of
   polynomial functions on `X(G)/X(G)(π)`).

## Correspondence and derived choices

- **Action on `ℂ[Λ(G)]` (item 5).** An element `λ = [g]` of `Λ(G)` is the character
  `χ ↦ χ(g)` of the torus `X(G)`. The translation action of `ω` on functions,
  `(ω · φ)(χ) = φ(χ ω)`, sends `λ` to `ω(g) λ`. An element of `ℂ[Λ(G)]` is encoded as a
  finitely supported function on `G(F)/⁰G`, and `ω · f` has coefficient `ω(g) f([g])` at
  `[g]`; item 5 says that `f` is fixed by every `ω ∈ X(G)(π)` exactly when its support lies in
  `T/⁰G`.
- **Polynomial functions on the quotient torus (item 6).** The quotient map
  `X(G) → X(G)/X(G)(π)` identifies polynomial functions on the quotient with the
  `X(G)(π)`-invariant polynomial functions on `X(G)`, the convention of the published V.2.7
  Corollary. Item 6 identifies `ℂ[T/⁰G]` with this algebra, through the evaluation map
  `t ↦ (χ ↦ χ(t))` that underlies Renard's identification `ℂ[L] ≃ ℂ[X^*(U)]^J`. The group
  algebra `ℂ[T/⁰G]` is `MonoidAlgebra ℂ (T ⧸ ⁰G)`, where `⁰G` is viewed as a subgroup of `T`
  by item 1.
- **`Z(G)` (item 2).** The statement uses the centre of the abstract group `G(F)`. It contains
  the rational points of the centre of the group scheme, so item 2 implies the source's
  `Z(G) ⊂ T` for either reading. The source's argument applies to the whole centre of `G(F)`:
  by Schur's lemma every central element `z` acts on the irreducible `π` by a scalar `c ≠ 0`,
  so `π ⊗ ω ≃ π` forces `c ω(z) = c`, that is, `ω(z) = 1`.
- **Irreducible `π`.** The paragraph is stated in VI.4.4 for the cuspidal `π` of that section,
  but neither the paragraph nor the Remark uses cuspidality. The statement assumes only that
  `π` is irreducible, as the published V.2.7 Lemma does. As there, the statement quantifies
  over representatives of the class `π`, and `X(G)(π)` depends only on the class.
- **Isomorphisms as relations.** `π ⊗ ω ≃ π` is `Nonempty` of an isomorphism of smooth
  representations, as in the published V.2.7 Lemma.
- **Sizes.** The carrier universe `uV` is independent of `uF`.

## Not stated here

- The last sentence of the Remark, `ℂ[Irr(G)[π]] ≃ ℂ[T/⁰G]`, combines item 6 with the
  identification of `X(G)/X(G)(π)` with `Irr(G)[π]` of V.2.7. The published V.2.7 Corollary
  `RationalInertiaOrbitTorusStatement` states that identification, and the VI.4.4 variety
  statement `RationalSummandCenterVarietyStatement` uses the same algebra of `Stab`-invariant
  polynomial functions on `X(G)` as the functions on `Irr(G)[π]`.
- The general facts on complex tori and finite subgroups (`X^*(U/J) = L`) are recalled by the
  source as background and are used here only in the special case of `X(G)` and `X(G)(π)`.

## Remaining obligations

The proof is a separate obligation. Items 1 and 4 (forward direction) and item 5 follow from the
definition of `T` and the fact that unramified characters are trivial on `⁰G`. Item 2 needs
Schur's lemma for irreducible smooth representations of `G(F)`. Item 3 follows from item 1 and
the finiteness of `X(G)(π)` (the published V.2.7 Lemma) once each `ker ω` with `ω ∈ X(G)(π)`
has finite index; Renard obtains it from `⁰G Z(G) ⊂ T`. The reverse direction of item 4 is the
double-annihilator property for the finite subgroup `X(G)(π)` of characters of `Λ(G)`. Item 6
uses the linear independence of the distinct characters `χ ↦ χ(g)`, `g ∈ G/⁰G`, of `X(G)` and
the fact that unramified characters separate the points of `G/⁰G`.
