# Algebra for the norm-kernel twist proof

This is internal algebra for the proof of Renard VI.3.2(iii), in the French
edition dated 9 July 2026, printed pages 143–144 / PDF pages 149–150.
The implemented route uses the finite-dimensional span of a single nonzero
restricted intertwiner's orbit instead of finite-dimensionality of the whole
restricted Hom space. All carrier universes remain independent.

The conjugation action is Mathlib's `Representation.linHom`, restricted to
the invariants of the normal subgroup. Thus its value is
`σ(g) ∘ f ∘ ρ(g⁻¹)`, with the source and target actions in that order. Its operators
commute when the subgroup contains the commutator subgroup, since the action
descends to the commutative quotient. Scalar central actions on both
representations make the centre act scalarly on this Hom representation, and
the subgroup itself acts trivially.

For any representation with a scalar normal subgroup of finite index, its
orbit span is generated by the finitely many chosen coset translates.
The span and its inherited action are the existing
`Langlands.FiniteVectors.orbitSubrepresentation`; the vector's membership
uses `Langlands.FiniteVectors.self_mem_orbitSpan`.
Restriction to this span and
`EndomorphismBound.exists_common_eigenvector` give a simultaneous eigenvector.
`EndomorphismBound.exists_character_of_forall_eigen` packages the eigenvalues
as a unit-valued character. Evaluating the conjugation eigenvector equation at
`ρ(g)v` gives an intertwiner from `twist χ ρ` to `σ`, with the character in
the direction required by the published statement.

These are conditional algebraic helpers. They do not assert the actual
rational-point structural inputs or the final equivalence.

The exported helper
`exists_twistIntertwiningMap_of_restrictedHom_nonzero` combines these steps.
Mathlib's `invariantsEquivIntertwiningMap` converts the given nonzero
restricted intertwiner to a nonzero invariant vector. The common eigenvector
construction is applied to its orbit span using the scalar action of
`H ⊔ Subgroup.center G`. Since `H` fixes the resulting nonzero vector, scalar
cancellation shows that its eigencharacter is trivial on `H`. The twisted
intertwiner has that vector as its underlying linear map, so it is nonzero.
This is an internal derived lemma, with explicit finite-index, commutator and
scalar-centre assumptions; it does not add these assumptions to the published
rational-point equivalence target.
