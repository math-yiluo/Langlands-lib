## The source

The theorem reads: let `(τ, E)` be a smooth representation of finite type of `G`. Then `(τ, E)` is `ℨ(G)`-admissible, i.e. for every compact open subgroup `K` of `G`, `E^K` is a finitely generated `ℨ(G)`-module. Renard remarks that this generalizes admissibility of irreducible representations: if `τ` is irreducible, `ℨ(G)` acts on `E^K` by scalars, so `ℨ(G)`-admissibility forces `E^K` to be finite-dimensional.

## The Lean statement

`FiniteTypeCenterAdmissibleStatement` is a statement unit: nothing is proved. It quantifies over:

- a nonarchimedean local field `k : Type u`;
- a connected reductive group scheme `G` over `Spec k`, with `G(F)` locally compact and nonarchimedean in the canonical topology `RationalPointFamily.pointTopology`;
- a smooth representation `τ : SmoothRep.{0, u, v} ℂ (rationalPoints G)` of finite type: `Module.Finite (MonoidAlgebra ℂ (rationalPoints G)) τ.representation.asModule`, the notion of the published VI.1.3 and VI.7.5 statements;
- a compact open subgroup `K : OpenSubgroup (rationalPoints G)` with `IsCompact (K : Set _)`.

The conclusion is that there are `n : ℕ` and vectors `s : Fin n → τ` that are `K`-fixed (`τ.representation g (s i) = s i` for `g ∈ K`) such that every `K`-fixed vector `x` can be written `x = ∑ i, SmoothRep.hom ((z i).app τ) (s i)` for some `z : Fin n → CatCenter (SmoothRep.{0, u, v} ℂ (rationalPoints G))`.

Here `CatCenter C = End (𝟭 C)` is Mathlib's centre of a category, Renard's `ℨ(G)`. An element `z` acts on `τ` by the `G`-equivariant endomorphism `z.app τ`, which preserves `E^K`. The conclusion says that the `ℨ(G)`-module `E^K` is generated by `s₁, …, s_n`. The module structure is not packaged as a `Module` instance; the explicit expansion is equivalent to finite generation.

## Remarks

- The centre is that of the category of smooth representations with carriers in the universe `v` of `τ`. Renard's `ℨ(G)` does not depend on such a choice; no comparison between universes is formalized.
- The VI.10.7 Corollary (the Hecke algebra `H(G, K)` is a finitely generated `ℨ(G)`-module) follows by applying the theorem to the cyclic representation `𝒟(G/K)`. It is not stated here.
- The VI.10.7 Lemma and Proposition involve Bernstein components and are separate units.
- The proof (reduction to a single component, the VI.4 and VI.10.1 progenerators, and finiteness of `ℨ[ρ]` over `ℨ_𝔰`) is not formalized here.
