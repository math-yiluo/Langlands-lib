# Sandwich structure of compact-open Hecke corners of `G°`

Authors: Worker 323 and Worker 277. Worker 277 supplied the initial statement
and provenance draft; Worker 323 checked the original source and completed the
paired documentation. This pair belongs to task
`e29c21ae-4529-4663-b834-01b770f19fee`, revision 2.

`Langlands.CuspidalSupport.DeterminantUnitCornerStructureStatement.{uF}` is a
Prop-valued definition with the single universe parameter `uF`. It states the
following assertion; it does not prove it.

## Statement and conventions

For every prime `p`, actual `F : PadicField.{uF} p`, compatible topological data
`T : F.TopologicalData`, natural number `n`, and `B : BlockShape n`, set
`H = determinantUnitSubgroup T B`. This is the actual subgroup of the product
Levi whose determinant has normalized absolute value one in every block. The
statement installs `T.topology` and `T.leviTopologicalGroup B`, uses the inherited
subgroup topology on `H`, and installs its Borel measurable structure and the
corresponding `BorelSpace` instance.

Every open subgroup `K` of `H` with compact underlying set contains an open
subgroup `N` with compact underlying set, witnessed by `hN`, such that for every
Haar measure `μ` on `H` there are natural numbers `q` and `l`, elements
`x : Fin q → Hecke.HeckeAlgebra.Corner μ N hN`, and pairwise commuting elements
`a : Fin l → Hecke.HeckeAlgebra.Corner μ N hN` satisfying

```text
span_ℂ { x i * m * x j | i,j ∈ Fin q, m ∈ Algebra.adjoin ℂ (Set.range a) }
  = the whole corner.
```

The same finite family `x` occurs on both sides. The middle factor ranges over
the actual unital complex subalgebra generated by the `a` family, and `Commute`
asserts pairwise commutation in the corner's convolution multiplication. The
span is a complex linear span, expressed by `Submodule.span ℂ ... = ⊤`, rather
than an assertion that every element is a single such product.

The subgroup `N` and its compactness witness are chosen before `μ`; the finite
families and their sizes may depend on `μ`. No particular Haar normalization is
assumed. The corner uses the published `Ring` and `Algebra ℂ` instances; its unit
is the normalized indicator of `N`. There are no added restrictions on `F`, `T`,
`n`, or `B`, and no structure or conclusion is supplied as a hypothesis. The
rank-zero empty-product case is included; its group is trivial, and the claimed
corner structure specializes to the one-dimensional complex algebra.

## Source and derived provenance

Provenance: **derived**. The original is I. N. Bernshtein and A. V. Zelevinskii,
*Representations of the group GL(n,F) where F is a non-Archimedean local field*,
Russian Mathematical Surveys 31:3 (1976), 1–68, English translation. The registered
source ID is `bernstein-zelevinsky-1976-representations-gln`, with SHA256
`72dc22eb9991112936a88327f00d0f02a16a2bf8747518ca0ecfeeadd188e50c`.
The retained source is
`/home/taskhub/central-library/Bernstein-Zelevinsky--Representations of the group GL(n,F) where F is a non-archimedean local field (survey)--1976.pdf`.

Lemma 4.9 and the description immediately following it, printed page 39, treat
`G = G_α` and a congruence subgroup `N`. With
`ḡ = ε_N * ε_g * ε_N`, part (a) spans `H_N` by
`γ̄_i * δ̄ * γ̄_j`, where the `γ_i` represent the finite quotient `Γ/N` and `δ`
belongs to the dominant diagonal monoid `Δ`. Part (b) identifies
`δ̄₁ * δ̄₂` with `(δ₁δ₂)̄`. Thus finitely many monoid generators `δ_k` give
pairwise commuting elements `a_k = δ̄_k`, and the span of the `δ̄` is precisely
the unital subalgebra they generate. The finite family `x_i = γ̄_i` supplies the
two outer factors. Under the identification of distributions with functions
using `μ`, these barred elements are normalized indicators of their double
cosets; since `N` is normal in `Γ`, the `γ_i` double cosets are the cosets
`γ_i N`. Sections 1.24–1.30, printed pages 13–14, give the convolution and
normalization conventions.

For the present determinant-unit form, use `Δ° = Δ ∩ G°`. Section 3.20, printed
page 34, defines `G°` block by block, includes `Γ` in it, and states that it is
open and normal. The Cartan decomposition of 3.2 and its product version in 3.4,
printed pages 29–30, therefore restrict to `G° = Γ Δ° Γ`: determinants of both
outer factors are units, so the diagonal factor has determinant valuation zero
in each block. The monoid `Δ°` is finitely generated: its ordered integral
exponent vectors with zero sum in each block are the lattice points of a
rational polyhedral cone. Apply the support and multiplication argument of
Lemma 4.9 to this submonoid, using the same congruence subgroup and its
factorization from 3.11, printed page 32. This produces the required sandwich
span, with `a` drawn from finitely many generators of `Δ°`.

Every compact open `K` in `G°` is also open in `G`, since `G°` is open. The
congruence subgroups form a neighborhood basis at the identity by 3.1, printed
page 28, and the product construction in 3.4. Hence one of them lies in `K`.
These subgroups are compact, open, and normal in `Γ`, and lie in `Γ ⊆ G°`.
This explains the existential level `N` for every input `K`. The source uses
normalized distributions; their densities with respect to any Haar measure
give the stated function-algebra form. Rescaling that measure rescales these
densities and preserves the resulting assertion.

Remark 4.13, printed pages 40–41, states the corresponding dimension bounds for
`G°`, and 4.16, printed page 41, explicitly applies the finite-generation
argument of 4.9 to `H_N(G°)`. These motivate this derived statement. This module
asserts neither those dimension bounds nor finiteness of representations. The
Cartan restriction, finite generation of `Δ°`, existence of sufficiently small
congruence subgroups, normalization argument, and rank-zero specialization above
explain the provenance; they are not separately formalized or proved here.
