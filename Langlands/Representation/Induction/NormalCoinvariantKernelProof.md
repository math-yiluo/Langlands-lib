# The normal-coinvariant relation kernel of compact induction

Worker 121 proves

```lean
theorem Langlands.Induction.compactInductionNormalCoinvariantKernel :
  Langlands.Induction.CompactInductionNormalCoinvariantKernel.{uG,uV}
```

The proof imports the unchanged statement from

All helpers in this file are private derived results.

## Exact scope

The target retains independent group and coefficient universes. It quantifies
over every Hausdorff topological group with the stated compact open subgroup
neighborhood basis, closed subgroups `H` and `N`, normality of `N`, containment
`N ≤ H`, every complex coefficient module, every smooth representation `σ` of
`H`, and every actual `f : CompactFunctions H σ`. Its conclusion is

```lean
(f ∈ Representation.Coinvariants.ker
  ((compactInduced H σ).comp N.subtype)) ↔
(∀ g : G, eval H σ f.val g ∈ Representation.Coinvariants.ker
  (σ.comp (N.subgroupOf H).subtype))
```

Both kernels are Mathlib's actual submodules generated by action differences.
The functions, evaluations and right translations are the published compact
induction model. No coefficient topology or finiteness assumption is used.
There are no additional lifting, exactness, averaging, exhaustion or comparison
hypotheses. Closedness of `N` remains in the exact target even though the
implemented argument does not need it.

The stated compact open subgroup basis supplies a `NonarchimedeanGroup`
instance directly: an arbitrary identity neighborhood contains an open
neighborhood, hence one of the stipulated subgroups. Applying the basis to the
whole group gives a compact identity neighborhood, which supplies
`LocallyCompactSpace G`. These are consequences of the target's hypotheses.

## Implemented proof

Write `R` for the global relation submodule in compact induction and `R₀` for
the coefficient relation submodule. The private linear map `evaluation` is
evaluation at one group element. The private linear map `cutoff` bundles the
published `compactQuotientCutoff` construction.

For the forward implication, evaluation of the global generator
`ρ(n)s - s` at `g` is

```text
σ(g n g⁻¹)(s(g)) - s(g).
```

Normality puts the conjugate in `N`, and containment puts it in `H`.
Covariance proves this formula. It is a coefficient generator, so
`evaluation_relation` and `evaluation_mem` extend it to all of `R` by
Mathlib span induction.

For the reverse implication, `exists_relation_evaluation` proves that each
`v ∈ R₀` is the value at `g` of some `r ∈ R`. For a coefficient generator
`σ(p)w - w`, take the existing `compactLocalSection` with value `w` at `g`,
using the whole quotient as the allowed support neighborhood. Conjugating
`p` by `g⁻¹` gives `n = g⁻¹ p g ∈ N`. The actual global generator
`ρ(n)s - s` has value `σ(p)w - w` at `g`. The zero, addition and scalar
steps build the required relation for every element of the coefficient span.
This uses only finite algebraic operations.

For any compact clopen subset `C` of `H \ G`, normality and `N ≤ H` give
`H g n = H g`. Consequently cutoff by `C` commutes with right translation
by `n`. The private lemma `cutoff_mem` proves this first on generators,
then by span induction proves that every such cutoff preserves `R`.

Given the pointwise hypothesis for `f` at `g`, choose a relation `r` with
`r(g) = f(g)`. The smooth covariant error `e = f - r` has closed projected
nonzero support, by the existing `isClosed_quotientSupport`. The existing
`quotientMap_preimage_quotientSupport` shows that the coset of `g` lies
outside this support. Thus its open complement contains a compact clopen
quotient neighborhood `C`, using
`exists_compactClopen_quotientNeighborhood`. On `C` the functions agree,
so their cutoffs agree; the cutoff of `f` belongs to `R`.
This is `exists_local_relation_cutoff`. Local agreement is established
from the actual support and covariance, without a coefficient topology.

Finally, `mem_of_local_cutoffs` proves a finite patching lemma for any
submodule stable under these cutoffs. Choose the preceding neighborhood at
each quotient point and extract a finite subcover of the compact projected
support of `f`. To combine overlapping neighborhoods use the identity

```text
T_(C ∪ D)(f) = T_C(f) + T_D(f) - T_C(T_D(f)).
```

Finite unions stay compact and clopen. Induction therefore puts the cutoff
by the finite union in `R`. The union contains the entire projected support,
so this last cutoff is `f` itself. This proves membership in the actual
finite relation span, rather than membership in a completion or closure.
No comparison-equivalence theorem enters the argument.

In Case IV₁ the source's normal subgroup `V` acts trivially on `M \ G`;
its `M` corresponds to `H`, and its `V` corresponds to our `N`. Its
complementary subgroup called `N` is different. The source uses the sheaf
associated to the coinvariant module and commutation with neighborhood
localizations to identify its fiber. The diagram factors Case IV through
`(M ∩ N)V` into Cases IV₁ and IV₂.

As explained in the statement Markdown, the exact pointwise kernel
formulation here is internal to this project. The implemented elementary
local-section and finite-cover proof replaces the source's sheaf and
filtered-limit argument, and introduces no complementary subgroup.
The finite-union cutoff identity also occurs in Worker 121's published
[`LocalizationProof`](../../HarmonicAnalysis/Distribution/LocalizationProof.md);
here it is proved directly for the compact-induction cutoff. The section,
cutoff and quotient-support constructions are reused from the published
Library by Lean name. This file adds only their derived proof applications.
