# Conjugation of a finite spanning family of compact types

This internal algebraic construction turns a supplied finite family of irreducible compact-coefficient representations of a normal subgroup into a finite set of classes preserved by ambient conjugation. The group has its actual topology and is a topological group. The representatives embed by supplied injective intertwiners into the restriction of one smooth ambient representation, and their ranges span that restriction. For each selected class, its intrinsic isotypic part and annihilator in this restricted object must be complementary. The representative carrier, ambient carrier, group and finite-indexing universes are independent. The indexing family may contain repeated isomorphism classes.

First, every supplied range lies in the intrinsic isotypic part of its own class. Spanning therefore makes the selected intrinsic sum the whole space. Selected complementarity then makes the common annihilator zero. These two auxiliary lemmas work for any topological group and do not require the supplied maps to be injective.

For ambient conjugation by `g`, pull back a representative along inverse conjugation of the normal subgroup. Its original embedding, followed by the action of `g`, is an injective intertwiner into the original restriction. Since its image is disjoint from the zero common annihilator, the published finite-family type-detection theorem supplies an equivalence with a member of the original family. Thus conjugation maps the finite class set into itself. The class transport is injective, so its image has the same finite cardinality; the inclusion is an equality. This gives `mem_classesOfFiniteFamily_conjugation_iff` and the actual subtype permutation `finiteFamilyConjugationEquiv`. Its value formula is the published class pullback, with no new quotient or choice of representatives.

The provenance is internal: the proof uses the existing intrinsic range inclusion, selected-complement lemma, type detection, group pullback and finite-set cardinality laws. It supplies the conjugation-membership hypothesis needed by the selected ambient projector. It does not construct the finite spanning family, prove analytic complementarity, establish semisimplicity, or prove the final determinant-unit block-isolation theorem. Only complements in the one restricted ambient object are assumed here, not complements for every smooth representation.
