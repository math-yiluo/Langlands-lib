# Compact-coefficient isotypic subrepresentations and annihilators

Worker 249 and Worker 199 construct two intrinsic invariant subspaces of an
actual smooth complex representation. Fix a class `c` of irreducible smooth
representations with compact smooth-dual coefficients. The first subspace is
generated by all copies of `c`. The second is killed by all maps to `c`.
This unit proves only their elementary lattice laws, representative
independence and naturality. It asserts no complement, projector, direct-sum
decomposition, semisimplicity, compact coefficients of these subspaces, or
finiteness statement.

## Setting and universes

`G : Type uG` is a group with a topology; no topological-group axiom,
unimodularity or countability is assumed. A class is
`c : CompactCoefficientClass.{uG, uV} G` from the published module
`CompactCoefficient.Classes`. Its representatives are actual objects
`D : IrreducibleCompactCoefficientRep.{uG, uV} G` with carriers in `Type uV`,
and `ofRep D` is the class of `D`. The ambient representation is an actual
smooth complex representation `A : SmoothRep.{0, uG, uW} ℂ G` with carrier in
`Type uW`. In the naturality laws, the target `B : SmoothRep.{0, uG, uX} ℂ G`
has carrier in `Type uX`. The universes `uG`, `uV`, `uW` and `uX` are
independent. In particular, consumers may instantiate `uV` and `uW` with the
same universe, such as `max uF uV`, without identifying either with `uG`; a
development check elaborated this specialization.

All maps involving `A` or `B` are actual `Representation.IntertwiningMap`s,
equivariant `ℂ`-linear maps between different carriers. Categorical morphisms
are not used for them, because a hom-set of `SmoothRep` would force both
carriers into one universe. Categorical isomorphisms occur only between two
representatives in the same universe `uV`, as provided by `ofRep_eq_iff`.

The supremum and infimum below use the complete lattice of `Subrepresentation`
supplied by the helper module `Langlands.Representation.SubrepresentationLattice`
(see its paired Markdown); Mathlib gives only a lattice and a bounded order.
Index types such as all representatives and all intertwiners are large. This
needs no resizing, because an indexed supremum or infimum is the supremum or
infimum of its range in the fixed lattice of subrepresentations of `A`. No
skeleton or choice of representatives is used in the definitions.

## Definitions

All declarations are in the namespace `Langlands.CompactCoefficientClass`.

- `isotypicSubrepresentation c A` is
  \[
  \bigsqcup_{D,\ \mathrm{ofRep}\,D = c}\ \bigsqcup_{f : D \to A}
  \operatorname{range} f,
  \]
  the supremum, over every representative `D` of `c` with carrier in
  `Type uV` and every intertwiner `f` from `D` to `A`, of the range of `f`.
- `annihilatorSubrepresentation c A` is
  \[
  \bigsqcap_{D,\ \mathrm{ofRep}\,D = c}\ \bigsqcap_{f : A \to D}
  \ker f,
  \]
  the infimum, over the same representatives, of the kernels of all
  intertwiners from `A` to `D`.
- `totalIsotypicSubrepresentation.{uG, uV, uW} A` is the supremum of
  `isotypicSubrepresentation c A` over all `c : CompactCoefficientClass.{uG, uV} G`.
- `totalAnnihilatorSubrepresentation.{uG, uV, uW} A` is the infimum of
  `annihilatorSubrepresentation c A` over the same classes.

The representative universe `uV` does not occur in the types of the two total
definitions, but it is a universe parameter of both and must be given
explicitly, for example as `totalIsotypicSubrepresentation.{uG, uV} A`.
Different choices of `uV` give a priori different subrepresentations; no
comparison between universes is asserted.

## Elementary laws

Let `D` be a representative with `hD : ofRep D = c`.

- `range_le_isotypicSubrepresentation D hD f`: the range of every intertwiner
  `f : D → A` lies in the isotypic part. This is the upper-bound law of the
  indexed supremum.
- `isotypicSubrepresentation_le_iff S`: the isotypic part lies in a
  subrepresentation `S` exactly when the range of every intertwiner from every
  representative of `c` lies in `S`. It is the least subrepresentation
  containing these ranges. The proof is `iSup_le_iff`.
- `annihilatorSubrepresentation_le_ker D hD f`: the annihilator lies in the
  kernel of every intertwiner `f : A → D`. This is the lower-bound law of the
  indexed infimum.
- `le_annihilatorSubrepresentation_iff S`: a subrepresentation `S` lies in the
  annihilator exactly when it lies in every such kernel. The annihilator is the
  greatest such subrepresentation. The proof is `le_iInf_iff`.
- `mem_annihilatorSubrepresentation_iff v`: `v` lies in the annihilator exactly
  when `f v = 0` for every representative `D` of `c` and every intertwiner
  `f : A → D`. The proof uses the helper's `mem_iInf`.
- `toSubmodule_isotypicSubrepresentation` and
  `toSubmodule_annihilatorSubrepresentation` give the underlying submodules as
  the submodule supremum of `LinearMap.range f.toLinearMap` and the submodule
  infimum of `LinearMap.ker f.toLinearMap` over the same indices. They follow
  from the helper's `toSubmodule_iSup` and `toSubmodule_iInf`.

## Representative formulas

For one fixed representative `D` with `ofRep D = c`:

- `isotypicSubrepresentation_eq_iSup_range D hD` states that
  `isotypicSubrepresentation c A = ⨆ f : D → A, f.range`.
- `annihilatorSubrepresentation_eq_iInf_ker D hD` states that
  `annihilatorSubrepresentation c A = ⨅ f : A → D, f.ker`.

In both proofs, one inequality holds because `D` itself is among the
representatives. For the other, let `D'` be any representative of `c`.
`ofRep_eq_iff` gives a categorical isomorphism `e : D' ≅ D`. The proof maps it
along the full-subcategory inclusion and applies `SmoothRep.equivOfIso`. This
gives an actual representation equivalence `ε` from the representation of `D'`
to that of `D`.

- For the isotypic part, an intertwiner `f : D' → A` has the same range as
  `f ∘ ε⁻¹ : D → A`: a vector `f v` equals `(f ∘ ε⁻¹)(ε v)` by
  `symm_apply_apply`.
- For the annihilator, an intertwiner `f : A → D'` has kernel containing that
  of `ε ∘ f : A → D`. If `ε (f v) = 0`, then `f v = ε⁻¹ (ε (f v)) = ε⁻¹ 0 = 0`.

## Naturality

Let `φ` be an actual intertwiner from `A` to `B`.

- `mapsTo_isotypicSubrepresentation c φ`: `φ` maps the `c`-isotypic part of
  `A` into that of `B`. The helper's `mapsTo_iSup` reduces this to one
  generating range. For `f : D → A`, the vector `φ (f v)` lies in the range of
  `φ ∘ f : D → B`, which is in the isotypic part of `B` by
  `range_le_isotypicSubrepresentation`. The proof does not claim that each
  vector of the supremum lies in one generating range.
- `mapsTo_annihilatorSubrepresentation c φ`: `φ` maps the `c`-annihilator of
  `A` into that of `B`. If `v` is killed by every intertwiner from `A` to a
  representative, then for each `f : B → D` the composite `f ∘ φ` kills `v`, so
  `f (φ v) = 0`.

The statements use `Set.MapsTo` of the underlying function of `φ` between the
underlying sets. No functor on `SmoothRep` is constructed.

## Total parts

- `isotypicSubrepresentation_le_totalIsotypicSubrepresentation A c` and
  `totalAnnihilatorSubrepresentation_le_annihilatorSubrepresentation A c` are
  the bound laws over classes.
- `totalIsotypicSubrepresentation_eq_iSup_range A` states that the total
  compact part is the supremum of the ranges of all intertwiners from all
  representatives `D : IrreducibleCompactCoefficientRep.{uG, uV} G` to `A`.
  `totalAnnihilatorSubrepresentation_eq_iInf_ker A` states that the total
  annihilator is the infimum of the kernels of all intertwiners from `A` to
  all representatives. Both proofs compare bounds: each class contribution is
  bounded through `isotypicSubrepresentation_le_iff` or
  `le_annihilatorSubrepresentation_iff`, and each representative `D` appears in
  the contribution of its own class `ofRep D`.
- `mem_totalAnnihilatorSubrepresentation_iff A v`: `v` lies in the total
  annihilator exactly when every intertwiner from `A` to every representative
  kills `v`.
- `mapsTo_totalIsotypicSubrepresentation φ` and
  `mapsTo_totalAnnihilatorSubrepresentation φ` give the same naturality for the
  totals at a fixed `uV`. The first uses `mapsTo_iSup` with the per-class
  naturality. The second uses the membership formula and composition with `φ`.

## Original source and scope

The registered original is I. N. Bernshtein and A. V. Zelevinskii,
*Representations of the group GL(n,F) where F is a non-Archimedean local field*,
Russian Mathematical Surveys **31**:3 (1976), 1–68, English translation.

- Literature ID: `bernstein-zelevinsky-1976-representations-gln`.

- Retained original: `/home/taskhub/central-library/Bernstein-Zelevinsky--Representations of the group GL(n,F) where F is a non-archimedean local field (survey)--1976.pdf`.
- Locators: §§2.39–2.40, printed/PDF p.26, define matrix coefficients and the
  finite (compact-coefficient) representations. §2.42, pp.26–28, assumes a
  unimodular l-group countable at infinity and, for a finite irreducible `ω`,
  constructs distributions `ε_N^ω` indexed by the open compact subgroups `N`.
  §2.43, p.28, defines `π(ε^ω)ξ` as the common value of the vectors
  `π(ε_N^ω)ξ` for all sufficiently small `N`. The resulting projector
  `π(ε^ω)` commutes with `G` and with every morphism, and its image is a
  direct sum of copies of `ω`. §2.44(a), p.28, gives
  `E = E_ω ⊕ E_ω^⊥` with `E_ω = π(ε^ω)E` and `E_ω^⊥ = Ker π(ε^ω)`, where
  `E_ω^⊥` has no subfactor isomorphic to `ω`. §2.44(b), p.28, states that the
  submodule `E_f` generated by all `E_ω` is completely reducible and has
  compact smooth-dual coefficients (the source's “finite”).

The compact-coefficient condition and classes, with their comparison to the
source's term “finite”, are inherited unchanged from the published `Classes`
pair.

The two lattice formulas are internal definitions suggested by §§2.43–2.44.
The source does not define `E_ω` or `E_ω^⊥` by these formulas; it defines them
through the projector `π(ε^ω)`. `isotypicSubrepresentation` and
`annihilatorSubrepresentation` are intended as intrinsic targets for a later
theorem stating that, under the source's hypotheses, such a natural projector
exists with exactly this range and kernel. The total compact part corresponds
in the same sense to `E_f`. The total annihilator has no named counterpart in
the source. This unit proves no such identification. In particular, being killed by every map to a
representative of `c` is not asserted to imply the source's stronger property
of having no subfactor isomorphic to `ω`. No splitting, complete reducibility
or finiteness conclusion is generalized to the weaker setting here. The laws
proved above are elementary lattice facts valid for any group with a topology.
