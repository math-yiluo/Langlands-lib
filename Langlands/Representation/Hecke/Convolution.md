# Actual Hecke convolution and its nonunital complex algebra

This module constructs convolution on the published `HarmonicAnalysis.SchwartzBruhat G` carrier and the `Langlands.Hecke.TestFunction` operations, the complex vector space of locally constant compactly supported functions. Its convention is

`convolutionIntegral μ f g x = ∫ y, f y * g (y⁻¹ * x) ∂μ`.

For arbitrary functions and measures this expression uses Mathlib's Bochner integral, including its nonintegrable convention. For test functions, with the Borel sigma algebra on a topological group and `IsFiniteMeasureOnCompacts μ`, the module proves integrability at every x. Its algebra additionally requires `Measure.IsMulLeftInvariant μ`. Every genuine left Haar measure supplies these two measure properties. Thus the construction applies to every locally compact Hausdorff totally disconnected group with a supplied left Haar measure. Neither global sigma-finiteness, second countability nor unimodularity is assumed. The implementation actually proves the construction under weaker topological hypotheses: local compactness, Hausdorffness and total disconnectedness are not needed once the input test functions and finite-on-compacts measure are supplied.

The integrand is continuous and supported in the compact support of its first argument, so its Bochner integral exists. For local constancy at x, joint local constancy of g(y⁻¹z) and g(y⁻¹x), together with the compact tube lemma over the support of f, supplies one neighborhood of x on which all integrands agree. Outside that support they vanish. The convolution support lies in `support f * support g`: if x is outside this set product then every integrand is zero. Compactness of the product proves compact support. These closure statements need no measure-invariance hypothesis; indeed the closure proofs still work for the raw integral of an arbitrary measure.

`TestFunction.convolution` bundles the resulting function into the existing test-function space. Additivity and complex homogeneity of the integrals give `TestFunction.convolutionBilinear`. This definition chooses no finite coset decomposition, representatives, or compact-open subgroup, so there is no decomposition or refinement choice on which its value could depend.

For associativity the proof writes the left-associated convolution as a double integral with kernel

`(z,y) ↦ (f y * g (y⁻¹ * z)) * h (z⁻¹ * x)`.

This continuous kernel is supported in the compact rectangle
`(support f * support g) × support f`. Mathlib's compact-support Fubini theorem swaps the two integrals without global sigma-finiteness. For each fixed y, the left-invariant substitution `z = y*w` changes the inner integrand to `f y * (g w * h (w⁻¹ * (y⁻¹ * x)))`. Pulling out the constant `f y` gives the right-associated convolution. This proves `TestFunction.convolution_assoc` pointwise, not only almost everywhere.

`HeckeAlgebra μ` is a type synonym for precisely this test-function space, with the measure recorded in the type so its convolution multiplication has an unambiguous typeclass instance. `HeckeAlgebra.toTestFunction` is the identity complex linear equivalence. The inherited additive group and complex module, the actual convolution multiplication, `HeckeAlgebra.nonUnitalRing`, `IsScalarTower ℂ (HeckeAlgebra μ) (HeckeAlgebra μ)` and `SMulCommClass ℂ (HeckeAlgebra μ) (HeckeAlgebra μ)` give the nonunital complex algebra in Mathlib's typeclass vocabulary. Multiplication evaluates to the displayed integral by `HeckeAlgebra.mul_apply`; it is not pointwise multiplication. The scalar compatibility, distributive, zero and associative laws are all proved from the integral. A global multiplicative unit is not asserted.

The source for the Hecke space and convolution-algebra interpretation is Flath, *Decomposition of representations into tensor products* (1979), section 1, printed p.179, PDF p.175 of Corvallis Part I, registered `flath-1979-tensor-products`, SHA256 `3b9fe0145da81d870ef8967be83635cd440a2581fe043e64e63324e22dec4db5`. The explicit integral convention is Folland, *A Course in Abstract Harmonic Analysis*, second edition (2015), section 2.5, printed pp.54–56/PDF pp.67–69, especially formula (2.36), registered `folland-2015-abstract-harmonic-analysis-second`, SHA256 `c82467bfa77428c32d3e1a5d1cbdc11129511e0c61da2f9d8fbc717776b9e542`. Folland's discussion on printed p.49/PDF p.62 explains localization for Fubini on groups that need not be sigma-compact. The local-constancy proof and the explicit compact rectangle are internal implementation choices. No errata entry for either source was present in the available project notes when these passages were read.

This is the convolution-algebra construction unit. Normalized compact-open indicators, their averaging formulas and idempotence, the unital bi-invariant corner, and local-unit/refinement identities are subsequent units of the same task. Representation actions, Satake and factorization are outside this module.
