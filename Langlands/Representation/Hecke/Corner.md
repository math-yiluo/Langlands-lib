# Bi-invariant convolution and compact-open unital corners

This module constructs the compact-open corner of the actual Haar Hecke algebra. It imports the integral convolution and normalized subgroup indicator, and reuses `TestFunction.biInvariant` on the existing compactly supported locally constant functions. For a compact open subgroup `K`, `HeckeAlgebra.Corner μ K hK` consists exactly of the actual bi-K-invariant functions. Its multiplication is convolution and its unit is `normalizedIndicator μ K hK`. The module proves that its image in the full Hecke algebra is exactly `e_K H(G) e_K`.

All constructions use a group with a topology making it a topological group and a measurable space. Left translation and the algebra constructions use `BorelSpace G`. The arbitrary-subgroup nonunital algebra requires a measure finite on compact sets and invariant under left multiplication. The compact-open unital corner requires `μ.IsHaarMeasure`, an actual `OpenSubgroup G`, and an actual proof `hK : IsCompact (K : Set G)`. These hypotheses apply to every locally compact Hausdorff totally disconnected group with a supplied left Haar measure. Local compactness, Hausdorffness and total disconnectedness are not extra Lean parameters here: the imported test functions, Haar data and supplied compact open subgroup suffice. No second countability, global sigma-finiteness or unimodularity is required.

The source is Flath, *Decomposition of representations into tensor products* (1979), section 1, printed p.179 / PDF p.175 of Corvallis Part I, registered `flath-1979-tensor-products`, SHA256 `3b9fe0145da81d870ef8967be83635cd440a2581fe043e64e63324e22dec4db5`. Flath defines the Hecke algebra, the normalized indicator `e_K`, and the subalgebra `H(G,K) = e_K H(G) e_K`. The identification with bi-K-invariant functions, the explicit projection and the inclusion maps below are derived from that definition using the already constructed Haar convolution and averaging formulas. They are internal constructions, rather than assumptions drawn from Flath's representation or factorization theorems. The original page was read; no source errata entry was present in the available project notes.

The integral convention remains `(f * g)(x) = ∫ y, f(y) g(y⁻¹ x) dμ(y)`. With `leftTranslate a f x = f(a⁻¹ x)` and `rightTranslate a g x = g(x a)`, the lemmas `TestFunction.convolution_leftTranslate` and `convolution_rightTranslate` give `(L_a f)*g = L_a(f*g)` and `f*(R_a g) = R_a(f*g)`. The first proof substitutes `y = a*z` using left invariance of the measure. The second is pointwise associativity inside the integral and requires neither Borel compatibility nor measure invariance. Neither identity requires finite mass on compact sets, since they also hold for the raw integral convention.

`convolution_leftInvariant` and `convolution_rightInvariant` pass the corresponding one-sided invariance to the convolution. The left proof uses translation by the inverse subgroup element and evaluates the resulting equality; the right proof evaluates right-translation covariance. `convolution_mem_biInvariant_of_left_right` combines these conclusions. Thus `convolution_mem_biInvariant` takes bi-(K,L)-invariant and bi-(M,N)-invariant inputs to a bi-(K,N)-invariant output for arbitrary subgroups; no relation between L and M is needed. Under finite mass on compact sets, `biInvariantConvolution μ K L M N` restricts the imported complex bilinear convolution map to precisely these subspaces. Its linearity fields are proved by the corresponding integral linearity identities and subtype extensionality.

`HeckeAlgebra.biInvariantSubalgebra μ K` is a `NonUnitalSubalgebra ℂ (HeckeAlgebra μ)`. Its vector space is the existing bi-invariant submodule pulled back along the identity equivalence `toTestFunction`; its multiplication closure is the preceding convolution theorem. `biInvariantSubalgebra_antitone` and `biInvariantInclusion` restrict the invariance hypotheses when `L ≤ K`. The latter is the ordinary injective inclusion of functions and preserves complex scalars and convolution.

For compact open K, `sandwich_mem_biInvariant` proves that `e_K*f*e_K` is bi-K-invariant: the first indicator supplies left invariance and the last supplies right invariance. Conversely, the imported one-sided averaging identities fix any bi-K-invariant function on both sides. This gives `mem_biInvariant_iff_sandwich`, expressing invariance as `e_K*f*e_K = f`, and `mem_biInvariant_iff_exists_sandwich`, expressing invariance as membership in the actual image of `g ↦ e_K*g*e_K`. The forward direction chooses `g = f`; the reverse uses the proved invariance of every such product. Normalization positivity and finiteness are the proved Haar-mass facts from `NormalizedIndicator`; no unit or averaging law is a new hypothesis.

`Corner μ K hK` is a type synonym for the subtype belonging to `biInvariantSubalgebra μ K.toSubgroup`. It retains the compactness proof as a type parameter so the unit instance can use it. It inherits the nonunital ring, complex module and scalar compatibility from that subalgebra. Its `One` instance is the normalized indicator together with the indicator's proved bi-invariance. `Corner.ring` adds the two unit laws by the imported left/right averaging identities. Natural and integer casts are their respective multiples of this unit. The `Algebra ℂ` instance uses `Algebra.ofModule` and the inherited convolution scalar compatibility, so the algebra map sends c to c times the normalized indicator. All associative and distributive laws come from the actual convolution algebra.

`Corner.toHeckeAlgebra` is an injective nonunital complex algebra homomorphism. `Corner.toBiInvariant` is the identity complex linear equivalence with the original bi-invariant function subspace. The evaluation instance, `Corner.mul_apply`, `leftInvariant` and `rightInvariant` expose the actual functions and their integral multiplication. `Corner.toHeckeAlgebra_one` identifies the corner unit with the normalized indicator in the ambient algebra.

`Corner.project` is the complex linear map `f ↦ e_K*f*e_K`, taking its values in the corner by `sandwich_mem_biInvariant`. Its additivity and scalar compatibility follow by distributivity and bilinearity of convolution. `project_toHeckeAlgebra` proves that this map fixes the embedded corner, while `toHeckeAlgebra_project` is its defining sandwich formula. In particular the resulting ambient double-average is idempotent. `range_toHeckeAlgebra` states the exact equality between the embedding's image and the set of all sandwiches. Only linearity is claimed for this projection.

For compact open `L ≤ K`, `Corner.inclusion μ K hK hL hLK` is the injective nonunital complex algebra map from the K corner to the L corner. Its composite with the ambient embedding is the original embedding, by `toHeckeAlgebra_inclusion`. `inclusion_refl` and `inclusion_trans` prove identity and composition pointwise. These maps preserve the original functions and products. The K corner's unit maps to `e_K` in the L corner; the L corner's own unit is `e_L`, so general refinement is expressed as a nonunital homomorphism. The normalized-indicator refinement identities remain in the imported module.

Representation actions, fixed-vector modules, common compact-open invariance for finite families, Satake, commutativity assertions and factorization are separate constructions. They are not assumptions of this corner algebra.
