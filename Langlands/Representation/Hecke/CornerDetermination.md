# Corner annihilators of fixed vectors determine irreducible representations

This module proves an annihilator form of Bernstein–Zelevinsky (1976),
Proposition 2.10(b), printed page 17, with proof on printed page 18 (source
`bernstein-zelevinsky-1976-representations-gln`; PDF page number equals printed
page). The source says that two irreducible smooth representations with nonzero
`N`-fixed vectors are equivalent when their `ℋ_N`-modules are. The Lean result
`Langlands.Hecke.nonempty_equiv_of_cornerAction_eq_zero_iff` assumes less: for
irreducible smooth complex representations `ρ` on `V` and `σ` on `W` and nonzero
`K`-fixed vectors `a` and `b`, if every element `e` of the actual corner
`HeckeAlgebra.Corner μ K hK` satisfies `e·a = 0 ↔ e·b = 0`, then
`Nonempty (ρ.Equiv σ)`. The group is an arbitrary topological group with its
Borel structure and a supplied Haar measure, `K` an open subgroup and `hK` its
compactness, as for the published `cornerAction`. Irreducibility is Mathlib's
`Representation.IsIrreducible`. No admissibility, Hausdorff or total
disconnectedness assumption is used.

## Definitions

- `translateCornerElement μ K hK g` is the corner projection of the left translate
  by `g` of the normalized indicator `μ(K)⁻¹ 1_K`. The same term occurs in the
  proof of the published `exists_cornerAction_eq_fixedProjection`.
- `translateCombination μ K hK : (G →₀ ℂ) →ₗ[ℂ] Corner` sends a finite formal
  combination `Σ c_g g` to `Σ c_g · translateCornerElement g`.
- `orbitCombination ρ v : (G →₀ ℂ) →ₗ[ℂ] V` sends `Σ c_g g` to `Σ c_g ρ(g) v`.

## Proof

`cornerAction_translateCornerElement`: on a `K`-fixed vector `v`, the translate
element acts as `fixedProjection (ρ g v)`. This rewrites with the published
`cornerAction_project`, `integratedAction_leftTranslate` and
`heckeAction_normalizedIndicator_of_fixed`. By linearity (`Finsupp.lhom_ext`),
`cornerAction_translateCombination` says that `translateCombination c` acts on `v`
as the fixed projection of `orbitCombination ρ v c`.

`orbitCombination_mapDomain` says that left translating the formal combination by
`h` corresponds to applying `ρ h`. Hence ranges and images of submodules of
formal combinations are invariant. `submodule_eq_bot_or_eq_top_of_isIrreducible`
restates Mathlib's simplicity of the subrepresentation lattice for invariant
submodules. `orbitCombination_surjective` follows: the range is invariant and
contains `v ≠ 0`.

`ker_orbitCombination_le` is the core of the argument. Suppose every corner element
killing `a` also kills `b ≠ 0`. The image under `orbitCombination σ b` of the
kernel of `orbitCombination ρ a` is invariant, so it is `⊥` or `⊤`. If it were
`⊤`, some `c` with `Σ c_g ρ(g) a = 0` would have `Σ c_g σ(g) b = b`. Then
`translateCombination c` kills `a`, because the fixed projection of `0` is `0`.
But it sends `b` to the fixed projection of `b`, which is `b ≠ 0`
(`fixedProjection_fixed`), a contradiction. So the image is `⊥`, which is the
kernel inclusion.

For the main theorem, the hypothesis applied in both directions gives equal
kernels of the two surjective orbit maps. The linear equivalence `φ : V ≃ W` is
`V ≅ (G →₀ ℂ)/ker ≅ W` (`quotKerEquivOfSurjective`, `Submodule.quotEquivOfEq`). It
sends `Σ c_g ρ(g) a` to `Σ c_g σ(g) b`. By `orbitCombination_mapDomain` it commutes
with every group element, so it is a `Representation.Equiv`.

This replaces the source's subspace `{(x, f x)}` of `E₁^N ⊕ E₂^N` and the
projections of the submodule it generates by the kernel comparison of the
two orbit maps. The mathematical content is the same: the corner module
structure generated by one nonzero fixed vector determines the representation.
