## The statement

`RenardIwahoriHeckeSubalgebraFinitenessStatement` asserts the following for
all such data. Let `A = Algebra.adjoin ℂ (a '' (j '' Cplus))`, generated by
the `a_z`, `z ∈ C_∅^+`. Let
`M = Algebra.adjoin ℂ (a '' (j '' (Cplus * F₀)))`, Renard's `M`, generated
by the `a_{zf}`, `z ∈ C_∅^+`, `f ∈ F₀`. Then:

1. `A ≤ M`. So `M` is a left `A`-module under multiplication in `H(G, K)`.
2. There are `n` and `D : Fin n → H(G, K)` with every `D k ∈ M`, such that
   every element of `M` lies in the complex span of the products `u * D k`,
   `u ∈ A`. That is, `M = ∑ₖ A D_k`: `M` is a finitely generated `A`-module,
   with generators `D_k` (Renard's `{D_j}`).

## Corrections and readings

- **Correction: `A` is generated by the `a_z`, `z ∈ C_∅^+`.** Renard prints
  "engendrée par les `a_z`, `z ∈ C_∅`". With that definition the Lemma
  fails, because `M` is not stable under `A`. Example, checked by the
  author: split `GL₂` over `F`, the diagonal torus, `K₀ = GL₂(𝒪_F)`,
  `K = 1 + ϖ Mat₂(𝒪_F)`, `F_∅ = {1}` (here `Λ(Z) = Λ(S)`), and
  `d = diag(ϖ, 1)`. Choose `ν` so that `d ∈ C_∅^+` (with Renard's
  conventions, the upper triangular Borel). `d` pairs nontrivially with the
  positive root, so `d⁻¹` is not dominant.
  1. The second Lemma gives `a_{z₁} a_{z₂} = a_{z₁z₂}` on `C_∅^+`. So `M`
     is the span of the `a_z`, `z ∈ C_∅^+`. The indicators of distinct
     double cosets are linearly independent. Hence `a_{d⁻¹} ∈ M` only if
     `K d⁻¹ K` contains some `z ∈ C_∅^+`.
  2. Let `z = diag(ϖ^a, ϖ^b) ∈ K d⁻¹ K`. Then
     `ϖz ∈ K diag(1, ϖ) K`, and every element of that double coset has
     reduction `diag(1, 0)` modulo `ϖ`. So `a = -1` and `b ≥ 0`. The
     determinant has valuation `-1`, so `b = 0` and `z = d⁻¹`, which is not
     dominant.
  3. So `a_{d⁻¹}` lies in the printed `A` but not in `M`. It equals
     `a_{d⁻¹} * e_K`, a product of an element of the printed `A` with the
     unit `e_K = a_1 ∈ M`, and lies outside `M`.

  Renard's own uses are dominant. The Corollary and its source, the second
  Lemma, concern `z ∈ C_∅^+`, and the proof of the Lemma multiplies by
  `a_{z₁}`, `z₁ ∈ C_∅^+`. The published `IwahoriHeckeDecompositionStatement`
  makes the same correction (its "Formal choices"). This statement adopts
  it.
- **The transversal is Renard's.** `F₀` is required to be
  chamber-compatible, as V.3.23 assumes "dans la suite". So the generators
  of `M` are literally Renard's `a_{zf}`. This differs from the published
  `IwahoriHeckeDecompositionStatement` and `IwahoriHeckeSubalgebraFinitenessStatement`,
  which allow any finite lattice transversal and generate by the `a_t`,
  `t ∈ (C_∅ F₀) ∩ dom`. For a compatible `F₀` the two generating sets
  coincide (`C̃_∅^+ = C_∅^+ F₀`, above). For an arbitrary transversal they
  can differ. Example (from the review of an earlier version), with the
  `GL₂` data of the previous item: `F₀ = {d}` is a transversal, since
  `Λ(Z) = Λ(S)`. Then `(C_∅ F₀) ∩ dom = C_∅^+`, while `C_∅^+ F₀ = C_∅^+ d`
  contains only strictly dominant elements. The generated algebras differ
  too. By the second Lemma, the algebra generated by the `a_t`,
  `t ∈ C_∅^+ d`, is spanned by `e_K` and these `a_t`. It does not contain
  `a_{ϖ·1}`: the double coset `K ϖ K = ϖ K` contains no strictly dominant
  element of `C_∅` and is not `K`. But `a_{ϖ·1}` is a generator of the other
  algebra. The Lemma for arbitrary transversals is a different, derived
  assertion, which this file does not state.
- **Compatible transversals need not exist.** V.3.23 asserts "On peut
  s'arranger pour que `C̃_A^+ = C_A^+ F_M`" without proof. Example, checked
  by the author: `G = Res_{E/F} SL₃` with `E/F` ramified quadratic, `S` the
  diagonal split torus.
  1. `Z(F)` is the diagonal torus of `SL₃(E)`, and `Λ(Z)` is the lattice of
     triples `(x, y, z)` of `E`-valuations with sum `0`. In the coordinates
     `p = x - y`, `q = y - z`, it is `{(p, q) ∈ ℤ² : p ≡ q mod 3}`.
  2. For a suitable choice of positive roots, the dominant chamber is
     `p, q ≥ 0`. Elements of `F` have even `E`-valuation, so
     `Λ(S) = 2Λ(Z)`.
  3. `(0, 3)` and `(4, 1)` are dominant and lie in the same class modulo
     `Λ(S)`. A compatible representative `(p₀, q₀)` of that class must
     satisfy `p₀ ≤ 0` and `q₀ ≤ 1`, since both points lie in its translate
     by `C_∅^+`. It must also be dominant, so `p₀ = 0`. `q₀` is odd, since
     it is congruent to `3` modulo `2`, so `q₀ = 1`. But `(0, 1)` is not in
     `Λ(Z)`.

  For such groups no `F₀` satisfies the hypothesis, and the statement
  asserts nothing. This is the scope of Renard's Lemma, which is made only
  under his arrangement.
- **The hypothesis on `K`.** Renard's "admettant une décomposition
  d'Iwahori selon les sous-groupes paraboliques standards" is read, as in
  the published statement, through the published predicate
  `StandardParabolicBasisMember B.i ν J q K` for every standard parabolic
  (every `J`), with a compatible split component `q`. Unfolded, this
  predicate is `HasIwahoriIntersectionDecomposition` together with
  `ContractionAndCofinality`, whose six clauses are:
  1. `LeviIntersectionNormalization`: `C_A` normalizes `K_M = K ∩ M`;
  2. `WeakChamberContraction`: `C_A^+` contracts `K_N = K ∩ N`, and
     `(C_A^+)⁻¹` contracts `K_N̄ = K ∩ N̄`;
  3. `StrictChamberExhaustion`: for `m ∈ C_A^{++}`,
     `N = ⋃ₙ m⁻ⁿ K_N mⁿ` and `N̄ = ⋃ₙ mⁿ K_N̄ m⁻ⁿ`;
  4. `UniformCompactContainment`: every compact subset of `N` (of `N̄`) lies
     in `m⁻¹ K_N m` (in `m K_N̄ m⁻¹`) for all `m ∈ C_A^+(ε)`, for some
     `ε > 0`;
  5. `StrictChamberNeighborhoodBases`: for `m ∈ C_A^{++}`, the
     `mⁱ K_N m⁻ⁱ` form a neighborhood basis of `1` in `N`, and the
     `m⁻ⁱ K_N̄ mⁱ` one in `N̄`;
  6. `UniformCompactOpenCofinality`: every compact open subgroup of `N` (of
     `N̄`) contains `m K_N m⁻¹` (`m⁻¹ K_N̄ m`) for all `m ∈ C_A^+(ε)`, for
     some `ε > 0`.

  The factorization and clauses 1–2 are V.5.2 (ii). Renard's proofs of
  the second Lemma and of the Lemma use them for the minimal parabolic.
  Clauses 3–6 are V.5.2 (iii)–(iv). V.5.3 does not mention them. They are
  kept so that the data are exactly those of the published
  `IwahoriHeckeDecompositionStatement`, whose module form (item 5) a proof
  of this statement can use. For a valid assignment, the published theorem
  `availableSimultaneousStandardBasis` (V.5.2) provides a neighborhood basis
  of `1` consisting of such `K`. So the extra clauses do not exclude the
  `K` that V.5.3 is applied to later in the book.
- **`A` and `M` are unital subalgebras** (`Algebra.adjoin`). `H(G, K)` is the
  unital algebra with unit `e_K = a 1`. Renard does not say whether
  "sous-algèbre" contains the unit. The two readings agree: `1 ∈ C_∅^+`, so
  the unit `a 1` is a generator of `A`, and it lies in `M` by item 1.
- **Item 1 is part of the conclusion.** It makes `M` an `A`-module, which
  "le `A`-module `M`" presupposes. It is not immediate from the definitions:
  `F₀` need not contain `1`. Step 1 below proves it.
- **Generators in `M`.** The `D_k` must lie in `M`, as Renard's "système de
  générateurs fini du `A`-module `M`" requires.

## Relation to the published statements

- On a chamber-compatible `F₀` that satisfies (P), this statement and the
  published `IwahoriHeckeSubalgebraFinitenessStatement` have the same
  conclusion. This statement drops (P). The published one allows every
  finite lattice transversal. Neither implies the other.
- On a chamber-compatible `F₀`, the module form, item 5 of
  `IwahoriHeckeDecompositionStatement`, says that the left `A`-module
  generated by the individual generators `a_{zf}` is finitely generated.
  This statement is about the algebra they generate. The two can differ: in
  Reviewer 1385's example, quoted in the published statement's Markdown
  (`G_m` over `ℚ₃`, `K = 1 + 3ℤ₃`, `F₀ = {-1}`), the module is `Aε` and the
  algebra is `A ⊕ Aε`.

## Status of the assertion

Write `K_N = K ∩ N_∅`, `K_M = K ∩ M_∅` and `K_N̄ = K ∩ N̄_∅` for the minimal
standard parabolic `P_∅ = M_∅ N_∅`, with `M_∅ = j(Z)`. Write `Z^+` for the
dominant elements of `Z(F)` (`dom`) and `Z(F)^1` for the norm kernel. The
following is known to the author. It is not used by the statement.

**What the printed proof covers.** On printed p.124 / PDF p.130, Renard
computes `a_{z₁} a_{z₂ f}` for `z₁, z₂ ∈ C_∅^+` and `f ∈ F_∅`. He shows that
it equals `a_{z₁z₂f}` when `z₂⁻¹ K_N̄ z₂ f K = z₂⁻¹ z₁⁻¹ K_N̄ z₁ z₂ f K`. This
controls the left `A`-span of the individual generators `a_{zf}`, that is,
the module form. It does not treat products `a_{z₁f₁} a_{z₂f₂}` of two
generators, which the generated algebra contains. The finiteness argument on
printed p.125 / PDF p.131 transports a function to `ℕ^d` through V.3.24,
which asserts `C_∅^+ / C_{A_G} ≅ ℕ^d`. That assertion is false in general.
For `SL₃`, in the difference coordinates above, the monoid is
`{(p, q) ∈ ℕ² : p ≡ q mod 3}`. Its indecomposables `(3, 0)`, `(1, 1)` and
`(0, 3)` satisfy `(3, 0) + (0, 3) = 3 (1, 1)`, so it is not free. The module
form for all data is the published item 5 of
`IwahoriHeckeDecompositionStatement`. Worker 127 proved it in Task 5575
(`IwahoriHeckeDecompositionProof`) without V.3.24, by Dickson's lemma in floor
coordinates.

**Step 1: `A ≤ M` holds.** Let `f₀ ∈ F₀` represent the trivial class of
`Λ(Z)/Λ(S)`. Write `f₀ = c₀ u₀` with `c₀ ∈ C_∅` and `u₀ ∈ Z(F)^1`. Let
`z ∈ C_∅^+`. Then `z u₀ = (z c₀⁻¹) f₀ ∈ C_∅ F₀`. It is dominant, since
dominance depends only on the valuation, and `z u₀` has the valuation of `z`.
By compatibility, `z u₀ ∈ C̃_∅^+ = C_∅^+ F₀`, so `a_{z u₀} ∈ M`. In
particular, `a_{u₀} ∈ M` (`z = 1`). `j(u₀) ∈ K₀`, as in the published proof
of `IwahoriHeckeDecompositionStatement`. `K` has finite index in `K₀`, so
`u₀^m ∈ K` for some `m ≥ 1`. The First Lemma (published item 2) gives
`a_{z u₀} a_{u₀}^{m-1} = a_{z u₀^m} = a_z`. So `a_z ∈ M`.

**Step 2: a sufficient condition.** Consider the condition

> (C) every `t ∈ Z^+` satisfies `t K_N t⁻¹ ⊆ K_N` and `t⁻¹ K_N̄ t ⊆ K_N̄`.

Under (C), the Lemma follows:

1. By the Iwahori decomposition `K = K_N K_M K_N̄`,
   `e_K = e_{K_N} ∗ e_{K_M} ∗ e_{K_N̄}`. Let `t, s ∈ Z(F)`, where `t`
   contracts `K_N` and `s⁻¹` contracts `K_N̄`. Renard's computation for the
   second Lemma then gives `a_t a_s = e_K ∗ δ_t ∗ e_{K_M} ∗ δ_s ∗ e_K`. This
   is the average of the `a_{t m s}` over `m ∈ K_M`, a finite combination.
   Here `t m s ∈ Z(F)`, and `t m s ∈ Z^+` when `t, s ∈ Z^+`: `K_M` is
   compact, so its elements have valuation `0` in `Λ(Z)`. The normalization
   of `K_M` is not needed.
2. By induction on the length of products, (C) puts `M` inside
   `V = span{a_s : s ∈ Z^+}`.
3. Let `s ∈ Z^+`. Since `C_∅ F₀` lifts `Λ(Z) = Z(F)/Z(F)^1`,
   `s = t u` with `t ∈ C̃_∅^+` and `u ∈ Z(F)^1`, and `j(u) ∈ K₀`. By the
   First Lemma, `a_s = a_t a_u`. Item 5 gives finitely many `t_k ∈ C̃_∅^+`
   such that every `a_t`, `t ∈ C̃_∅^+`, lies in `∑_k A a_{t_k}`. Hence
   `V ⊆ ∑_{k, u} A a_{t_k} a_u`, where `u` runs over representatives of the
   finite set `Z(F)^1/(Z(F)^1 ∩ j⁻¹K)`.
4. `A` is commutative (published item 4) and Noetherian: it is a quotient of
   the monoid algebra of the finitely generated monoid `C_∅^+` (Gordan's
   lemma). `M` is an `A`-submodule of a finitely generated `A`-module, by
   Step 1. So `M` is finitely generated by finitely many of its own elements.

(C) is the contraction half of (P). Every `u ∈ Z(F)^1` normalizes `K_N` and
`K_N̄`: `j(u) ∈ K₀` normalizes `K`, and `Z` normalizes `N_∅` and `N̄_∅`.
So (C) for `t` depends only on the class of `t` in `Λ(Z)`. Hence (C) is
equivalent to the `WeakChamberContraction` part of (P), and the
`LeviIntersectionNormalization` part of (P) is not needed.

**What remains open.** Clause 2 of the hypothesis on `K`, for the minimal
parabolic (`J = ∅`, whose split component is `S`), says that every
`z ∈ C_∅^+` contracts `K_N` and that `z⁻¹` contracts `K_N̄`. The published
proof of `IwahoriHeckeDecompositionStatement` uses it in this form. So (C) holds for `t^k` with `k = [Λ(Z) : Λ(S)]`. It also
holds for every `t ∈ Z^+` when `Λ(Z) = Λ(S)`, for example when `G` is split.
The author knows no argument for (C) itself when the class of `t` in `Λ(Z)`
is not in `Λ(S)`. The author also knows no route to the Lemma that avoids
(C), and no counterexample.
