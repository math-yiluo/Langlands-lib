# The finiteness Lemma for the generated subalgebra `M` (Renard V.5.3): statement

Module `Langlands.Representation.Hecke.IwahoriHeckeSubalgebraFinitenessStatement`.
Namespace `Langlands.Hecke`. Universe parameter `u`.
The module contains one **statement**, the `Prop`-valued definition
`IwahoriHeckeSubalgebraFinitenessStatement`; it proves nothing and constructs
nothing.

It complements the published
`IwahoriHeckeDecompositionStatement`, whose Markdown leaves the subalgebra form
of the finiteness Lemma out of that statement ("Not stated").

## Framework and published objects

All data and hypotheses up to the Haar measure are copied verbatim from the
published `IwahoriHeckeDecompositionStatement` (same binders, same order): the
nonarchimedean local field `F`, the connected reductive `G` with the canonical
topology on `G(F)`, available corrected Kaletha–Prasad data `𝔅` with base
datum `B`, the generic cocharacter `ν`, the special vertex `x` with special
compact `K₀`, the compact open `K ≤ K₀` normal in `K₀` with the V.5.2 (ii)
properties for every standard parabolic (`StandardParabolicBasisMember`), the
finite lattice transversal `F₀` and the Haar measure. The local definitions
`j`, `dom`, `Cplus` (`C_∅^+`), `Ctilde` (`C̃_∅^+ = (C_∅ F₀) ∩ dom`) and `a`
(`a_g = e_K ∗ δ_g ∗ e_K`, the mass-one indicator of `K g K`) are the published
statement's. See its Markdown, "Framework and published objects".

The additional hypothesis uses published predicates of
`Langlands.BruhatTits.SpecialCompact.ContractionPredicates` for the minimal
standard parabolic (`J = ∅`), with
`M_∅ = standardLeviPoints B.i ν ∅`, `N_∅ = standardUnipotentPoints B.i ν ∅`
and `N̄_∅ = standardOppositeUnipotentPoints B.i ν ∅`:

- `WeakChamberContraction K N_∅ N̄_∅ (j '' Ctilde)`: for every `t ∈ C̃_∅^+`,
  `t (K ∩ N_∅) t⁻¹ ⊆ K ∩ N_∅` and `t⁻¹ (K ∩ N̄_∅) t ⊆ K ∩ N̄_∅`;
- `LeviIntersectionNormalization K M_∅ (j '' Ctilde)`: for every
  `t ∈ C̃_∅^+`, `t (K ∩ M_∅) t⁻¹ = K ∩ M_∅`.

These are the clauses that V.5.2 (ii) gives for `C_A^+`, now required for
`C̃_∅^+`. Call this hypothesis (P).

## The statement

`IwahoriHeckeSubalgebraFinitenessStatement` asserts, for all such data
satisfying (P), with `A = Algebra.adjoin ℂ (a '' (j '' Cplus))` (Renard's
`A`, generated by the `a_z`, `z ∈ C_∅^+`, as in the published statement) and
`M = Algebra.adjoin ℂ (a '' (j '' Ctilde))` (Renard's `M`):

1. `A ≤ M`. So `M` is a left `A`-module under multiplication in `H(G, K)`.
2. There are `n` and `D : Fin n → H(G, K)` with every `D k ∈ M`, such that
   every element of `M` lies in the complex span of the products `u * D k`,
   `u ∈ A`. That is, `M = ∑ₖ A D_k`: `M` is a finitely generated `A`-module,
   with generators `D_k` (Renard's `{D_j}`).

## Formal choices and departures from the source

- **Hypothesis (P) is added.** Renard's Lemma has no hypothesis beyond those
  of V.5.3. His proof computes only the products `a_{z₁} a_{z₂ f}` with
  `z₁, z₂ ∈ C_∅^+`, which gives the module form (item 5 of the published
  statement). It does not treat products `a_{t₁} a_{t₂}` of two generators
  with `t₁, t₂ ∈ C̃_∅^+`. Without control of `t₁ (K ∩ N_∅) t₁⁻¹`,
  `t₂⁻¹ (K ∩ N̄_∅) t₂` and `t₁ (K ∩ M_∅) t₁⁻¹` for the non-lattice factors
  `f ∈ F₀`, such a product need not be a single `a_t`, and the argument
  below does not bound `M`. The hypotheses on `K` control these conjugates
  only for elements of `C_A`. The unconditional form
  is therefore not asserted here, and it is not claimed to be false here.
  The Markdown of `RenardIwahoriHeckeSubalgebraFinitenessStatement`, which
  states it without (P) for Renard's chamber-compatible transversals, gives
  a counterexample on paper.
- **When (P) is automatic.** If `G` is split, `Λ(Z) = Λ(S)`, so `F₀` is a
  single element `c₀ u₀` with `c₀ ∈ C_∅` and `u₀ ∈ ⁰Z`, so `j(u₀) ∈ K₀`
  (see step 2 below). Then every
  `t ∈ C̃_∅^+` is `c u₀` with `c ∈ C_∅^+`. Conjugation by `u₀` preserves `K`
  (normal in `K₀`) and `N_∅`, `N̄_∅`, `M_∅` (which `Z` normalizes), and `c`
  satisfies the clauses of V.5.2 (ii) that give the second Lemma for
  `C_∅^+`. This remark is not used by the statement.
- **`A` and `M` are unital subalgebras** (`Algebra.adjoin`). `H(G, K)` is the
  unital algebra with unit `e_K = a 1`. Renard does not say whether
  "sous-algèbre" contains the unit. The two readings agree: `1 ∈ C_∅^+`, so
  the unit `a 1` is a generator of `A`, and it lies in `M` by item 1.
- **Item 1 is part of the conclusion.** The transversal `F₀` has an
  arbitrary element in the trivial class of `Λ(Z)/Λ(S)`. So `C̃_∅^+` need not
  contain `C_∅^+`, and `A ≤ M` is not immediate from the definitions. Step 3
  below derives it from the First Lemma, without (P). It makes `M` an
  `A`-module, which "le `A`-module `M`" presupposes.
- **Generators in `M`.** The `D_k` are required to lie in `M`, as Renard's
  "système de générateurs fini du `A`-module `M`" requires. The published
  Theorem (item 6) accepts any such family.

## Why the statement is true

Write `K_N = K ∩ N_∅`, `K_M = K ∩ M_∅`, `K_N̄ = K ∩ N̄_∅`.

1. **Products of generators.** For `t₁, t₂ ∈ Z(F)` with
   `t₁ K_N t₁⁻¹ ⊆ K_N`, `t₁ K_M t₁⁻¹ ⊆ K_M` and `t₂⁻¹ K_N̄ t₂ ⊆ K_N̄`,
   Renard's computation for the second Lemma gives
   `a_{t₁} a_{t₂} = a_{t₁ t₂}`. It uses `e_K = e_{K_N} ∗ e_{K_M} ∗ e_{K_N̄}`
   (Iwahori decomposition along `P_∅`; Library: `IwahoriSandwich`,
   `massIndicator_mul`). These conditions are stable under products. By (P)
   and induction, `a_{t₁} ⋯ a_{t_r} = a_{t₁ ⋯ t_r}` for `t_i ∈ C̃_∅^+`.
2. **Reduction to `C̃_∅^+ · ⁰Z`.** The product `t = t₁ ⋯ t_r` is dominant.
   Since `C_∅ F₀` lifts `Λ(Z) = Z(F)/⁰Z`, `t = c f u` with `cf ∈ C̃_∅^+` and
   `u ∈ ⁰Z` with `j(u) ∈ K₀`; dominance only depends on the class modulo
   `⁰Z`. Here `⁰Z = Z(F)^1` is the full norm kernel, and `j(Z(F)^1) ⊆ K₀`
   holds directly (`KPApartmentData.IsOmegaCompatible.map_mem_compactStabilizer`
   applied to `B.isOmegaCompatible`, as in the published proof of
   `IwahoriHeckeDecompositionStatement`). In general `j(⁰Z)` is **not**
   contained in the parahoric of `x`: a parahoric meets `Z(F)` only in
   `Z(F)^0` (Kaletha–Prasad, Definition 7.4.1(4) and Lemma 7.4.3), which can
   be strictly smaller than `Z(F)^1`, e.g. for a ramified norm-one torus.
   Only the inclusion into `K₀` is used. By the First Lemma
   (published item 2), `a_t = a_{cf} a_u`. By the module form (published
   item 5), `a_{cf}` is an `A`-combination of finitely many `a_{t_k}`. The
   group `⁰Z/(⁰Z ∩ K)` is finite (`⁰Z` is compact and `K` is open). Hence `M`
   lies in the finitely generated `A`-module `∑_{k, u} A a_{t_k} a_u`, with
   `u` running over representatives of `⁰Z/(⁰Z ∩ K)`.
3. **`A ≤ M`.** Let `f₀ = c₀ u₀ ∈ F₀` represent the trivial class, with
   `c₀ ∈ C_∅` and `u₀ ∈ ⁰Z`. Then `u₀ = c₀⁻¹ f₀ ∈ C̃_∅^+`, and
   `z u₀ ∈ C̃_∅^+` for `z ∈ C_∅^+`. Take `m ≥ 1` with `u₀^m ∈ K`; it exists
   because `⁰Z ∩ K` is a normal subgroup of finite index in `⁰Z`. Since
   `u₀ ∈ K₀`, the First Lemma (published item 2) gives
   `a_{z u₀} a_{u₀}^{m-1} = a_{z u₀^m} = a_z`. So every generator of `A` lies
   in `M`. This step does not use (P).
4. **Finite generation.** `A` is commutative (published item 4). It is a
   quotient of the monoid algebra of `C_∅^+`, a finitely generated monoid
   (Gordan's lemma, as in the published proof of item 5). So `A` is
   Noetherian. `M` is an `A`-submodule (by 3, since `M` is a subalgebra) of
   the finitely generated module of step 2. Hence `M` is finitely generated,
   by finitely many of its own elements.
