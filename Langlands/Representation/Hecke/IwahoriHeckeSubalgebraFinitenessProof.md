## Route, and how it differs from the statement's sketch

The statement's Markdown sketches a proof whose step 4 uses that `A` is Noetherian (a
quotient of the monoid algebra of the finitely generated monoid `C_∅^+`, by Gordan's lemma).
**This proof does not use that step**, nor Gordan's lemma or any Noetherian argument. It
produces explicit generators instead:

- every element of `M` is a linear combination of `a (j t)`, `t` in the monoid generated by
  `C̃⁺`;
- each such `a (j t)` is **exactly** a product `a (j (σ z)) * D k` with `σ z ∈ C⁺` and `D k`
  one of finitely many elements of `M`.

The finiteness comes from a Dickson-type argument (the Library's
`exists_finite_stable_representatives`) applied to the lattice points of the monoid generated
by `C̃⁺`, with finitely many labels `(f, ℓ) ∈ F₀ × a''K₀`. Step 3 of the statement's sketch
(`A ≤ M`) is implemented as described there. The proof uses
`set_option maxHeartbeats 1000000`, a finite limit, because the single main declaration
elaborates the whole reductive setting.

## The helper lemma `IwahoriLatticeData.exists_finite_left_of_subset`

Setting: `L : IwahoriLatticeData K Λ ι` (Library, `IwahoriHeckeCore`), a lattice `Λ` with
`c : Λ → G`, finitely many pairings `pair α : Λ →+ ℤ`, and the Iwahori factorization of `K`
with the contraction clauses for dominant `y` (`0 ≤ pair α y` for all `α`).

Statement. For `g ∈ G`, shifts `e : ι → ℝ` and a set `S ⊆ Λ` with
`0 ≤ pair α y + e α` for all `y ∈ S` and all `α`, there is a finite `T ⊆ S` such that
every `y ∈ S` has some `t ∈ T` with `y - t` dominant and

`a (c (y - t)) * a (c t * g) = a (c y * g)`.

It is a version of the Library's `IwahoriLatticeData.exists_finite_left` in which the
admissible points are restricted to a given subset `S`, and the finite set lies inside `S`.

Proof.

1. `exists_finite_stable_representatives` is applied to `S`, with:
   - the floor coordinates `y ↦ (⌊pair α y + e α⌋₊)_α`;
   - the order relation "`y - t` dominant", which follows from comparing floor coordinates by
     `int_le_of_natFloor_add_le`;
   - the sets `V y = {(c y)⁻¹ nb (c y) g k | nb ∈ K ∩ N̄, k ∈ K}`, with
     `V y ⊆ B t = (c t)⁻¹ K (c t) g K` (compact, the image of `K × K`) whenever `y - t` is
     dominant, because `(c (y - t))⁻¹ (K ∩ N̄) c (y - t) ⊆ K ∩ N̄` (`L.conj_Knb`);
   - right `K`-invariance of the `V y`, so that finitely many tests separate them
     (`exists_finset_test_rightInvariant`).

   It returns a finite `T ⊆ S` such that each `y ∈ S` has `t ∈ T` with `y - t` dominant and
   `V t = V y`.
2. With `c (y - t) * (c t * g) = c y * g` (`L.c_sub_mul`), `massIndicator_mul` reduces the
   product formula to `K (c (y - t)) K (c t g) K ⊆ K (c y g) K`. This follows from
   `TestFunction.mul_mem_doubleCosetSet_of_factor_left` with the factorization
   `K = K_N K_M K_N̄`:
   - conjugation by `c (y - t)` keeps `K ∩ N` and `K ∩ M` inside `K` (`L.conj_Kn`,
     `L.conj_Km`);
   - for `nb ∈ K ∩ N̄`, `V t = V y` gives `(c t)⁻¹ nb (c t) g = (c y)⁻¹ nb' (c y) g k`, hence
     `c (y - t) nb (c t g) = nb' (c y g) k ∈ K (c y g) K`.

## The main theorem

Notation as in the published proof of `IwahoriHeckeDecompositionStatement` (its Markdown,
"Notation in the proof"): `j = map B.centralizer.j`, `c y = i(y(ϖ))`, `σ y = s(y(ϖ))`,
`Φ⁺ = positiveRelativeRoots i ν`, `a = massIndicator K hK haar`. Write `Q` for the submonoid of
`Z(F)` generated by `C̃⁺` (`Submonoid.closure Ctilde`), `A = Aalg`, `M = Malg`.

### Setup (copied from the published proof of `IwahoriHeckeDecompositionStatement`)

The following are reproduced with the same proofs:

- compactness of `K₀`;
- the basis member for `J = ∅`: the Iwahori factorization `hIw`, the contraction clauses
  `hcon`, `hchamber`, `hconjM`, `hconjN`, `hconjNb`, and the lattice data
  `L : IwahoriLatticeData K X_*(S) Φ⁺`;
- `hjs : j (σ y) = c y` and the valuation formula `hval`;
- `hCt` (`t' ∈ C̃⁺` iff `t' = σ y · f` with `f ∈ F₀` and `0 ≤ ⟨α, y⟩ + e α f` for all
  `α ∈ Φ⁺`) and `hCp` (`z ∈ C⁺` iff `z = σ y` with `y` dominant);
- `hjK₀ : j(Z(F)^1) ⊆ K₀` (`IsOmegaCompatible.map_mem_compactStabilizer`);
- the dominant decomposition `hdecomp`: every dominant `d` is `t' u` with `t' ∈ C̃⁺` and
  `u ∈ Z(F)^1`.

New elementary facts:

- `hfactor`, the factorization `k = l m n` with `l ∈ K ∩ N̄`, `m ∈ K ∩ M`, `n ∈ K ∩ N`, read
  off `hIw` directly;
- `hdom1`, `hdommul`: the dominant set contains `1` and is closed under products (the
  centralizer valuation is a homomorphism);
- `h2`: `a g * a x₁ = a (g x₁)` for `x₁ ∈ K₀` (`massIndicator_mul_of_normalizes`, using
  normality of `K` in `K₀`);
- `h1eq`: for `x₁, x₂ ∈ K₀`, `a x₁ = a x₂` implies `x₁⁻¹ x₂ ∈ K` (`massIndicator_eq_iff` and
  normality).

### Step 1: products along the positive monoid

`P = iwahoriPositiveMonoid K N M N̄` (Library, `IwahoriSandwich`) is the submonoid of `p ∈ G`
with `p (K ∩ N) p⁻¹ ⊆ K ∩ N`, `p⁻¹ (K ∩ N̄) p ⊆ K ∩ N̄` and `p⁻¹ (K ∩ M) p ⊆ K ∩ M`.

- `hPmul`: for `p, p' ∈ P`, `a p * a p' = a (p p')`, by `massIndicator_mul` and
  `TestFunction.mul_mem_doubleCosetSet_of_iwahori` with `hfactor`.
- `hCtP`: hypothesis (P) puts `j t` into `P` for every `t ∈ C̃⁺`. The first two clauses are
  `WeakChamberContraction`. The third follows from `LeviIntersectionNormalization`
  (`t (K ∩ M) t⁻¹ = K ∩ M`, so `t⁻¹ m t ∈ K ∩ M` for `m ∈ K ∩ M`).
- `hQ`: by `Submonoid.closure_induction`, every `t ∈ Q` is dominant, has `j t ∈ P`, and has
  `a (j t) ∈ M`. The product case uses `hdommul`, the submonoid `P` and `hPmul`.
- `hMspan`: every element of `M` lies in the span of `a (j t)`, `t ∈ Q`.
  `Algebra.adjoin_eq_span` writes `M` as the span of the monoid generated by the
  `a (j t)`, `t ∈ C̃⁺`. By `hPmul` that monoid is contained in `{a (j t) | t ∈ Q}`
  (`a 1 = 1` by `massIndicator_one`).

### Step 2: `A ≤ M`

This follows step 3 of the statement's Markdown and does not use (P).

1. `hdecomp` at `d = 1` gives `w ∈ C̃⁺` and `u₀ ∈ Z(F)^1` with `w u₀ = 1`. So
   `w = u₀⁻¹ ∈ Z(F)^1`, `j w ∈ K₀`, and the character valuation of `w` is trivial
   (`rationalCharacterValuation_ker`).
2. `Λ' = a '' K₀` is finite (`finite_image_massIndicator`, `K₀` compact). The map
   `n ↦ a ((j w)^n) ∈ Λ'` from `ℕ` is not injective
   (`Finite.exists_ne_map_eq_of_infinite`). With `h1eq` this gives `N₀ > 0` with
   `(j w)^{N₀} ∈ K`.
3. `hpow`: `a (g (j w)^n) = a g * (a (j w))^n`, by induction with `h2`.
4. For `z ∈ C⁺`:
   - `z w ∈ C̃⁺`. Write `w = c' f` with `c' ∈ C_∅` and `f ∈ F₀`; then `z w = (z c') f` with
     `z c' ∈ C_∅`, a subgroup. Dominance of `z w` follows from that of `z` by
     `dominantCentralizer_mem_iff_of_characterValuation_eq`, since `w` has trivial
     valuation.
   - `a (j z) = a (j (z w)) * (a (j w))^{N₀ - 1}`. By `hpow`, the right side is
     `a (j z (j w)^{N₀})`, which equals `a (j z)` by `massIndicator_eq_of_mem`, since
     `(j w)^{N₀} ∈ K`.
   - Both factors lie in `M`, since `w ∈ C̃⁺` and `z w ∈ C̃⁺`.
5. `Algebra.adjoin_le` gives `A ≤ M`.

### Step 3: the finite family `D`

1. **Labels.** For each `ℓ ∈ Λ'` choose `xℓ ℓ ∈ K₀` with `a (xℓ ℓ) = ℓ`. For
   `p = (f, ℓ) ∈ F₀ × Λ'` (a finite type) put:
   - `fe p = j f * xℓ ℓ`;
   - `S p`, the set of `y ∈ X_*(S)` such that `0 ≤ ⟨α, y⟩ + e α f` for all `α ∈ Φ⁺`, and
     some `u ∈ Z(F)^1` has `a (j u) = ℓ` and `σ y · f · u ∈ Q`.
2. **`hgen`.** If `u ∈ Z(F)^1` and `a (j u) = ℓ`, then
   `a (c y * fe p) = a (j (σ y · f · u))`. The proof applies `h2` twice, with `xℓ ℓ ∈ K₀` and
   `j u ∈ K₀`, together with `hjs`.
3. **Finite sets.** The helper lemma, applied for each `p` with `g = fe p`, shifts `e · f`
   and the set `S p`, gives finite `T p ⊆ S p`. The finite set
   `E = ⋃ₚ {a (c t * fe p) | t ∈ T p}` is enumerated as `D : Fin n → H(G, K)` with range `E`
   (`Set.Finite.fin_embedding`).
4. **`D k ∈ M`.** Each element of `E` is `a (c t * fe p)` with `t ∈ T p ⊆ S p`. By `hgen` it
   equals `a (j (σ t · f · u))` with `σ t · f · u ∈ Q`, which lies in `M` by `hQ`.
5. **Spanning.** By `hMspan` and `Submodule.span_le`, it suffices to show that `a (j t)` lies
   in the span for each `t ∈ Q`:
   - `t` is dominant (`hQ`), so `hdecomp` gives `t = t' u` with `t' ∈ C̃⁺` and
     `u ∈ Z(F)^1`;
   - `hCt` gives `t' = σ y · f`;
   - with `ℓ = a (j u) ∈ Λ'`, the point `y` lies in `S (f, ℓ)`;
   - the helper lemma gives `t₀ ∈ T (f, ℓ)` with `y - t₀` dominant and
     `a (c (y - t₀)) * a (c t₀ * fe (f, ℓ)) = a (c y * fe (f, ℓ))`.

   Hence
   `a (j t) = a (c y * fe (f, ℓ)) = a (j (σ (y - t₀))) * D k`, where `D k = a (c t₀ * fe (f, ℓ))`
   and `σ (y - t₀) ∈ C⁺` by `hCp`. So `a (j t)` is itself one of the products `u * D k` with
   `u ∈ A`.

The theorem returns `⟨A ≤ M, n, D, D k ∈ M, spanning⟩`.
