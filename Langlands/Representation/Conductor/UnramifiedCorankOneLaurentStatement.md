# Laurent numerators of the corank-one integrals of `π × I(x)`

Author: Worker 8. This module defines one proposition,
`Langlands.Conductor.UnramifiedCorankOneLaurentStatement` (universes `uF`,
`uV`). It is a statement only: nothing here proves it.

## Statement

The binders start as those of the published
`Langlands.Conductor.SphericalLFactorGenerationStatement`
([`SphericalLFactorGenerationStatement`](SphericalLFactorGenerationStatement.md)),
in the same order: a nonarchimedean local field `F : Type uF` of any
characteristic, `n : ℕ`, the measurable and Borel structures, `q`, `ψ`, `abv`,
`ϖ`, `μ`, `h : IsJacquetNormalization q ψ abv ϖ μ`, `V : Type uV`, a
representation `π` of `GL_(n+2)(F)` on `V` with `IsGeneric ψ (n + 1) π`, and
`ℓ : glFunctionals ψ (n + 1) π` with `ℓ.val ≠ 0`. They continue with

- `P : ℂ[X]` with `IsCharacterLPolynomial q ψ abv (rankOneQuotientHaar F) 1 π P`
  (`L(s, π × 1) = P(q^(-s))⁻¹`, `P(0) = 1`);
- an arbitrary `x : Fin (n + 1) → ℂˣ` (no ordering is assumed);
- `ℓ' : glInverseFunctionals ψ n ρ` with `ℓ'.val ≠ 0`, where
  `ρ = principalSeries h.uniformizer n h.residueCardinality x` is the published
  normalized unramified principal series `I(x)` of `GL_(n+1)(F)`
  ([`UnramifiedPrincipalSeries`](UnramifiedPrincipalSeries.md)). `ℓ'` is a
  Whittaker functional for the inverse character `ψ̄`;
- `W` in `coefficientModel π ℓ` and `W'` in `coefficientModel ρ ℓ'`;
- the hypothesis that `Ψ(s, W, W')` converges absolutely on a right
  half-plane: some `a : ℝ` with `CorankOneConverges ψ abv μ W W' s` for every
  `s` with `a < Re s`.

Write `Ψ(s, W, W')` for `corankOneIntegral ψ abv μ W W' s`, `X = q^(-s)` for
`spectralPoint q s`, and `Q_x = ∏ i, P.comp (C (x i) * X)`, the polynomial
`∏_i P(x_i X)`. The conclusion says that there are `a : ℝ`, `N : ℤ` and
`Q : ℂ[X]` with `Ψ(s, W, W') · Q_x(q^(-s)) = q^(-Ns) Q(q^(-s))` for every `s`
with `a < Re s`.

With `x_i = q^(-t_i)`, `P(x_i q^(-s))⁻¹ = L(s + t_i, π)`. So the statement
says that every corank-one integral of `π × I(x)` lies in
`∏_i L(s + t_i, π) · ℂ[q^(-s), q^s]`. The identity concerns the published
`corankOneIntegral` values on the half-plane.

The convergence hypothesis is an addition of this formalization. In JPSS,
Theorem (2.7)(i) proves that every integral converges on a right half-plane,
and the ideal statements concern the resulting functions. Here convergence is
assumed rather than asserted: `SphericalCorankOneConvergenceStatement`
([`SphericalCorankOneConvergenceStatement`](SphericalCorankOneConvergenceStatement.md))
supplies it for the integrals the reduction uses, and the published zeta-value
sets (`unequalRankZetaValues`) contain only continuations of convergent
integrals. Without it, the published `corankOneIntegral` of a non-integrable
pair is a junk value, about which the statement would say nothing meaningful.

## Source

H. Jacquet, I. I. Piatetski-Shapiro, J. A. Shalika, *Rankin-Selberg
convolutions*, Amer. J. Math. 105 (1983), 367-464, literature ID
`jpss-1983-rankin-selberg`. Theorem (2.7) (printed pages 390-391) says that
every integral lies in the fractional ideal generated by `L(s, π × I(x))`.
Theorem (3.1)(ii) (printed pages 404-405) says that the L-factor of a
representation induced from two blocks of Whittaker type lies in the product
of the block L-factors times `ℂ[q^(-s), q^s]`. Applied to `I(x)` induced in
stages from the unramified characters `χ_i` with `χ_i(ϖ) = x_i`, and with
`L(s, π × χ_i) = P(x_i q^(-s))⁻¹`, these give the statement. JPSS (3.1)
places no ordering condition on the blocks, so `x` is arbitrary here.

Jacquet 2012 (*A correction to Conducteur des représentations du groupe
linéaire*, Pacific J. Math. 260 (2012), 515-525, literature ID
`jacquet-2012-conductor-correction`), section 1, printed pages 517-519, uses
this divisibility for norm-ordered `x`. Together with Lemma 3 there
([`OrderedUnramifiedCorankOneGenerationStatement`](OrderedUnramifiedCorankOneGenerationStatement.md)),
it identifies `∏_i L(s + t_i, π)` as the generator of the ideal of `π × I(x)`.

The Lean statement quantifies over every nonzero `ψ̄`-Whittaker functional
`ℓ'` on `I(x)`, as JPSS's statement concerns the Whittaker model of `I(x)`
for any such functional.
