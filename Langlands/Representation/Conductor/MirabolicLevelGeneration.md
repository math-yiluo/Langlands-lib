# Integral mirabolic generation of the newvector subgroup

This module defines the integral groups used in the nonnegative-level
generation step of Jacquet, Piatetski-Shapiro and Shalika, *Conducteur des
representations du groupe lineaire*, Math. Ann. 256 (1981), Sections 5.1–5.3,
printed pages 211–213. The retained source is `jpss-1981-conductor`,
SHA256 `986b06d6c16221fd55f04480604a1b17bf11004a704ab16b78acdd131ae6d49d`.
The definition specializes the source's Section 5.2 group to integral ideals;
the generation proof below is a direct algebraic derivation, extended from
integer rings to commutative local rings.

For a commutative ring `R`, an ideal `I`, and matrices of size `n + 1`,
`integralJacquetMirabolic n I` is the subgroup of matrices of block form
`[[h,u],[0,1]]`, with `h` invertible and every coordinate of `u` in `I`.
It is defined as the last-row mirabolic intersected with the inverse image of
the last-column mirabolic under reduction modulo `I`. The entrywise theorem
`mem_integralJacquetMirabolic` identifies this condition exactly.
At the unit ideal it is the whole integral last-row mirabolic.

`integralJacquetMirabolic_map_inverseTranspose` proves that its image under
inverse transpose is
`integralCongruence n I ⊓ oppositeMirabolic n R`.
Here `integralCongruence` is the published actual invertible-matrix subgroup
whose final row is the final identity row modulo `I`. Inverse transpose has
the same subgroup image as transpose, because the original set is a subgroup.
Thus this image is the transpose group used on printed page 213.

When `R` is a local ring and `0 < n`,
`integralJacquetMirabolic_sup_inverseTranspose` proves

```text
H(top) ⊔ inverseTranspose(H(I)) = integralCongruence n I.
```

The supremum is in the subgroup lattice of the actual integral general linear
group. It is the subgroup generated by the two displayed groups. For the
integer ring of a nonarchimedean local field and `I = maximalIdeal ^ a`,
this is the nonnegative-level generation assertion of Section 5.3.
At `a = 0`, `I = top`, so the right side is the whole integral general linear
group, in agreement with Section 5.1's level-zero convention.

The proof first handles any congruence matrix `g` whose final diagonal entry
`d` is a unit. Right multiplication by a lower shear with coordinates
`-d⁻¹ * g(last,j)` clears its other final-row entries. Those coordinates lie
in `I`, so the shear belongs to the congruent opposite mirabolic.
Choose a first coordinate distinct from the final one. On these two
coordinates the product

```text
lower(d - 1) * upper(1) * lower(-(d - 1) * d⁻¹)
```

has final row `(0,d)`. Each lower transvection belongs to the congruent
opposite mirabolic and the upper transvection belongs to the last-row
mirabolic. Two invertible matrices with the same final row differ by a
last-row mirabolic element on the left. This proves
`mem_mirabolic_sup_of_isUnit_last_entry`.

For a proper ideal of a local ring, reduction to the residue field shows that
`d` is a unit; the proof reuses `isUnit_last_entry`. This gives
`mirabolic_sup_congruent_opposite_of_le_maximalIdeal`.
For the unit ideal, the matrix identity `g * g⁻¹ = 1` and locality imply that
some final-row entry is a unit. If the final diagonal entry is not already a
unit, an upper transvection adds such an entry to it. A unit plus a nonunit
in a local ring is a unit, so the previous argument applies.
`integral_mirabolic_sup_opposite_eq_top` proves this case, and
`mirabolic_sup_congruent_opposite` combines the two cases.

These are auxiliary group identities. They establish no existence,
uniqueness, support, functional equation, fixed-vector, or epsilon-factor
claim for representations. The rank assumption excludes rank one. No
negative ideal exponent or generation of the full field general linear
group from integral subgroups is asserted. In particular, the printed
negative-exponent full-group assertion on page 213 is not used. The
essential-vector existence argument from the 1981 paper is also not used.
