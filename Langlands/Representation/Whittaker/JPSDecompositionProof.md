# JPS function-space decomposition (proof)

Author: Worker 625.

The theorem
`Langlands.Whittaker.jpsDecomposition : JPSDecompositionStatement.{uF}` in
`Langlands.Representation.Whittaker.JPSDecompositionProof` proves the entire
published proposition from
`Langlands.Representation.Whittaker.JPSDecompositionStatement`. Its only
universe parameter is the unrestricted field universe `uF`.

## Scope and quantifiers

The proof introduces every prime `p` with its `Fact p.Prime` instance,
`F : PadicField.{uF} p`, supplied `T : F.TopologicalData`, rank `n : ℕ`,
and actual translation-stable `W : JPSFunctionSpace.FunctionSpace F n`.
It uses exactly the target's three hypotheses:

- `IsLocallyConstantSpace W T`: every scalar function in `W` is locally
  constant.
- `HasCoordinateUpperSupport W T`: each function vanishes when any given
  coordinate has sufficiently large normalized absolute value, with its
  bound uniform in all other coordinates.
- `HasFiniteOperatorSpans W T`: each actual coordinate-germ action
  `sigma W T i` has finite-dimensional complex operator span.

The conclusion chooses one finite set `X` of functions on
`Torus F n = Fin n → F.Carrierˣ` before every `f : W.toSubmodule`.
Every member of `X` satisfies `IsFiniteFunction T`: it is continuous and
its left translates span a finite-dimensional complex space. For each
`f`, there is a total coefficient map
`Φ : (Torus F n → ℂ) →
  HarmonicAnalysis.SchwartzBruhat (Fin n → F.Carrier)` such that, at every
torus point `a`,

```text
f.val a = ∑ χ ∈ X, Φ χ (fun i => (a i : F.Carrier)) * χ a.
```

Thus each coefficient is locally constant and compactly supported on the
whole additive space `Fⁿ`, including its coordinate hyperplanes. The
topology throughout is the supplied `T.topology`, its induced units
topology, and the finite product topologies. Thresholds use
`T.normalizedAbs`, the residue-cardinality normalization.

## Implemented proof

For each `i : Fin n`, apply the published theorem
`Langlands.Whittaker.jpsUniformDilation` to `p`, `F`, `T`, `n`, `W`,
`i`, local constancy, and `hspan i`. The latter is precisely
`HasFiniteCoordinateOperatorSpan W T i`, by the definition of
`HasFiniteOperatorSpans`. Classical choice collects its witnesses into
families `Y i` and `A i`, before any `f` is introduced.

The chosen data have four properties. Every `η ∈ Y i` is continuous and
translation-finite. Each `A i η` lies in the span of actual coordinate
dilations with normalized absolute value at most one. The expansion
holds in the actual germ quotient for every dilation. Finally, for every
`f`, one positive threshold precedes the entire torus tuple and every
bounded dilation, and the expansion holds pointwise throughout that
small-coordinate region. These are the local data of the published
`JPSUniformDilationStatement`; this proof derives them from its exact
published proof.

The last line applies the published
`Langlands.Whittaker.jpsDilationAssembly` to `Y`, `A`, the original local
constancy and upper support, the first property `hY`, and the fourth
property `hexpand`. The bounded-operator and quotient properties
(`hA`, `hgerm`) are part of the chosen dilation witnesses but are not
needed by the assembly interface.

Assembly chooses finite spanning sets of the one-variable translate
spaces generated by `1` and the `Y i`, then takes their finite products.
Consequently its `X` is fixed before `f`. Its induction over unprocessed
coordinates constructs coefficients directly as functions on clopen
boxes in `Fⁿ`; upper support supplies compact support. The result of that
theorem is exactly the remaining conclusion of the target, with the
same `W`, `T`, and rank. The current file only composes these published
results and introduces no further mathematical prerequisite.

The argument includes `n = 0`: choice over `Fin 0` is vacuous, and the
assembly theorem already covers the empty coordinate set and the
one-point additive space. No positive-rank assumption or representation
smoothness assumption is added.

## Dependencies

The three direct imports are the target statement and these two proofs:

- `Langlands.Representation.Whittaker.JPSUniformDilationProof`,
  theorem `Langlands.Whittaker.jpsUniformDilation`.
- `Langlands.Representation.Whittaker.JPSDilationAssemblyProof`,
  theorem `Langlands.Whittaker.jpsDilationAssembly`.

Both prerequisite proofs were independently accepted and published
before this main proof was attempted. The dilation proof in turn uses
the published proofs of coordinate-unit invariance, the bounded
dilation recurrence, and the bounded operator-span equality. The
statement pair and all imported pairs remain unchanged. No
Whittaker asymptotic statement or result is used.

## Source provenance

H. Jacquet, I. I. Piatetski-Shapiro and J. Shalika, *Automorphic forms on
GL(3) I*, Ann. of Math. (2) 109 (1979), 169–212, registered source
`jps-1979-gl3-i`, SHA256
`6446822f267f9f95156ce7a193409209e56ce7858ed6d3d350480e73f35b1aa5`.
Printed pp.181–183 (one-based PDF14–16) were checked in text and page
images. Page 181 defines finite functions and discusses finite operator
algebras. Lemma (2.2.1) on p.182 is the target decomposition; its proof
uses a finite dilation family followed by induction. Remark (2.2.5)
on p.183 concerns the later Whittaker application and supplies no extra
hypothesis here.

The implementation uses the published uniform-region dilation result
and the coordinate-set assembly described above. It does not use the
later display on p.182 whose `η(b)` has an unbound `b`, or the source's
implicit assertion about choosing slice families uniformly. The
available project literature index, central-library filenames, and
errata locations were rechecked; no source-specific correction was
found. Jacquet's nearby 2012 correction concerns the different 1981
*Conducteur* article.
