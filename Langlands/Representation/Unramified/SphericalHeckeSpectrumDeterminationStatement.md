# Standard spherical Hecke scalars determine the representation

`Langlands.Unramified.SphericalHeckeSpectrumDeterminationStatement.{uF, uV, uW}`
is an unproved proposition-valued definition. It states: for every prime `p` with
`[Fact p.Prime]`, every `F : PadicField.{uF} p`, every `r : ℕ` (including zero),
every `A : AutomorphicObject.{uF, uV} F r.succPNat`, every
`B : AutomorphicObject.{uF, uW} F r.succPNat` and every
`s : Eigenvalues (r + 1)`, if `HasSphericalHeckeSpectrum F A s` and
`HasSphericalHeckeSpectrum F B s`, then
`Nonempty (A.obj.representation.Equiv B.obj.representation)`.

The objects are actual irreducible admissible smooth complex representations of
`GL_(r+1)(F)`. The carrier universes `uV` and `uW` are independent of each
other and of `uF`. The field and group topologies are the canonical ones, through
the local instances `heckeFieldTopology` and `heckeGeneralLinearTopology` of
`HeckeOperators`. The relation `HasSphericalHeckeSpectrum F A s` is the published
one. The spherical fixed submodule of `A` for `GL_(r+1)(O_F)` is nonzero. Writing
`n = r + 1`, `q` for the residue cardinality and `T_i` for the indicator of the
double coset of `diag(ϖ,...,ϖ,1,...,1)` with `i` uniformizers (Haar measure giving
the spherical subgroup mass one), every `T_i`, `0 ≤ i ≤ n`, acts on every fixed
vector by

`(sqrt(q))^(i*(n-i)) * (-1)^i * coeff_i (∏_{z in s} (1-zX))`.

So the statement says that two such objects on whose spherical vectors all
standard Hecke operators act by the same scalars are equivalent. The conclusion is
an actual equivalence of the original representations, not of equivalence
classes or replacement carriers.

## Provenance

This is a derived statement. It combines two standard facts.

1. Bernstein–Zelevinsky, *Representations of the group GL(n,F) where F is a
   non-Archimedean local field* (1976), Proposition 2.10(b), printed page 17,
   proof on page 18 (source `bernstein-zelevinsky-1976-representations-gln`, SHA-256
   `72dc22eb9991112936a88327f00d0f02a16a2bf8747518ca0ecfeeadd188e50c`): two
   irreducible smooth representations with nonzero `K`-fixed vectors are equivalent
   when their modules over the Hecke algebra `H(G, K)` are isomorphic.
2. The spherical Hecke algebra of `GL_n` is generated by `T_1, ..., T_n` and the
   inverse of `T_n`. See Gross, *On the Satake isomorphism* (1998), section 2,
   Proposition 2.6 and (2.7)–(2.9), section 3, Proposition 3.6 and (3.14),
   retained PDF pages 4–8 (source `gross-1998-satake-isomorphism`, SHA-256
   `9bd0077b2057edd32885e46fd38872f423a4d45fef6bec0aa181af32dee062b3`). The
   published `SphericalHeckeGenerationStatement` states this generation fact up to
   powers of `T_n`.

On a spherical irreducible representation the fixed space is a line, and the
`H(G, K)`-module structure is the character given by the scalars of the
generators. Hence equal standard scalars give isomorphic Hecke modules, and fact 1
gives the equivalence. For the class-1 formulation (the spherical representation
is determined by its Hecke character, equivalently its Satake class) see Borel,
*Automorphic L-functions* (1979), sections 7.1–7.2, printed pages 38–39, PDF pages
47–48 (source `borel-1979-automorphic-l-functions`, SHA-256
`c890894fe19783890cc4def2c5bf92a18d71619acf8ba7005aefce4984fe70d6`). Stating it
for one common multiset `s` and arbitrary carrier universes is a formalization
choice.

## Intended use

This proposition is the shared bridge of three tasks. Spherical constituent
uniqueness `SphericalConstituentUniquenessStatement` follows from it and
`SphericalConstituentHeckeScalarStatement` at both carrier universes. Spherical
exhaustion and the principal-series/Hecke comparison also use it. Its intended
proof uses only the generation statement and the corner annihilator form of
fact 1. It needs no fixed-line theorem, Satake transform or principal series. This
module asserts no existence or uniqueness of a spectrum, and it does not prove the
generation statement.
