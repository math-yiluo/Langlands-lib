# Generation of the spherical Hecke algebra by the standard elements

`Langlands.Unramified.SphericalHeckeGenerationStatement` is a proposition-valued
definition. It states an unproved obligation; it is not proved or assumed in this
module.

## Statement

The module repeats, as local instances, the canonical topology, valuative
relation and local-field structure of `F.Carrier`, and the canonical topology,
topological-group, Hausdorff, Borel measurable and Borel space structures on
`GL_n(F)` used by `Langlands.Representation.Unramified.HeckeOperators`, so that
the algebra operations below are those of the Library's spherical Hecke algebra.

For every prime `p` with `[Fact p.Prime]`, every finite extension
`F : PadicField.{uF} p`, every `r : ℕ` (rank `n = r + 1`) and every element
`f : SphericalHeckeAlgebra F r`, there is `m : ℕ` with

`standardHeckeElement F r (Fin.last (r + 1)) ^ m * f ∈
  Algebra.adjoin ℂ (Set.range (standardHeckeElement F r))`.

Here `SphericalHeckeAlgebra F r` is the actual unital complex corner algebra of
compactly supported locally constant `K`-bi-invariant functions on `GL_n(F)`,
`K = GL_n(O_F)`, under convolution for the Haar measure giving `K` mass one;
`standardHeckeElement F r i`, for `0 ≤ i ≤ n`, is the characteristic function of
`K diag(ϖ,...,ϖ,1,...,1) K` with `i` uniformizers. The last one, `i = n`, is the
characteristic function of `ϖK`, for the central scalar matrix `ϖ·1`. The
membership is in the complex subalgebra generated by all the standard elements
`T_0 = 1, T_1, ..., T_n`. Equivalently, since `T_n` is invertible with inverse the
characteristic function of `ϖ⁻¹K`, the spherical Hecke algebra is generated by
`T_1, ..., T_n` and `T_n⁻¹`; the statement avoids naming that inverse.

## Original-source derivation

The statement is derived (provenance: derived) from:

- Benedict H. Gross, *On the Satake isomorphism* (1998), section 2, PDF page 4,
  Proposition 2.6 and (2.7): the Cartan decomposition into the disjoint double
  cosets `K λ(ϖ) K` for dominant coweights `λ` and the resulting basis `c_λ`
  of the spherical Hecke algebra; PDF pages 4-5, (2.8)-(2.9):
  `c_λ c_μ = c_{λ+μ} + Σ_{ν < λ+μ} a_ν c_ν`.
- Gross, section 3, PDF page 6, Proposition 3.6: the Satake transform is an
  isomorphism onto the Weyl-invariant group algebra of the coweight lattice;
  PDF page 7, (3.7)-(3.12): its triangularity with respect to dominance; PDF
  page 8, (3.14): for `GL_n`, the image of the `i`th standard element is
  `q^(i(n-i)/2)` times the `i`th elementary symmetric function.
- Armand Borel, *Automorphic L-functions* (Corvallis Part II, 1979), sections
  7.1-7.2, printed pages 38-39 / PDF pages 47-48: the same identification of the
  spherical Hecke algebra.

For `GL_n` the Weyl-invariant Laurent polynomials are generated by the
elementary symmetric functions `e_1, ..., e_n` and `e_n⁻¹`. Multiplying a finite
combination of basis elements by a large power of the central element `T_n`
shifts every coweight to one with nonnegative entries, whose transforms are
ordinary symmetric polynomials and hence polynomials in `e_1, ..., e_n`. This
algebraic step and the representation-theoretic facts above form the proof
obligation; the statement itself asserts only the displayed membership.
