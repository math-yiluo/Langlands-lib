# Positive two-block Jacquet-to-derivative comparison

Worker 321 assembles the comparison using Worker 593's normalization and
right-hand quotient constructions and the left-hand quotient adapter of
Worker 321. Worker 1004 contributed the organization of the latter adapter.

The theorem `Langlands.Derivative.partialDerivativeJacquetComparison` has
exactly the published proposition
`Langlands.Derivative.PartialDerivativeJacquetComparisonStatement.{uF,uV} F T`
as its conclusion. Its outer parameters are

```
{p : ℕ} [Fact p.Prime] (F : PadicField.{uF} p) (T : F.TopologicalData).
```

The theorem uses the given topology `T.topology`, every bundled unitary
additive character for that topology, and all positive block sizes `k,m`.
The universes `uF,uV` are independent. Both functors act on the entire
category `SmoothRep.{0,uF,uV} ℂ (GeneralLinear (k+m) F.Carrier)`, with
quotient carriers in `Type uV`, and land in the analogous category for
`GL_k`. There is no finite-length, admissibility, irreducibility,
genericity, dimension or comparison premise.

The asserted isomorphism is between the unchanged functors

```
generalLinearDerivative T ψ (k+m) m (Nat.le_add_left m k) ⋙
  generalLinearRankCast T (Nat.add_sub_cancel k m)
```

and

```
positiveTwoBlockNormalizedJacquet T k m hk hm ⋙
  partialDerivative T ψ k m.
```

The proof constructs invertible equivariant linear maps and proves
naturality for every actual ambient intertwiner. `positiveIso` bundles
these maps into an isomorphism in the functor category. The final theorem
places that isomorphism in `Nonempty`, as required by the target.

## The common quotient and the component maps

Write `m = j+1`, and fix a smooth representation `A` of `GL_(k+m)`.
Let `D_A` be the actual carrier
`(generalLinearDerivativePositive T ψ k j).obj A`, and let `R_A` be the
actual carrier of the right-hand composite. The common intermediate
space is

```
Q_A =
  Jacquet.Localization.Coinvariants A.representation
    (PartialLocalization.partialUpper k m F.Carrier)
    (PartialLocalization.character ψ.complex k m).
```

This is the algebraic relation quotient by the span of
`A.representation u v - (character u : ℂ) • v`.
The concrete localizing subgroup consists of the block matrices
`[[I_k,a],[0,u]]` with `u` upper unipotent in the final block. The
character is `ψ` of the sum of its final block's adjacent upper entries.
`PartialLocalizationData` proves that this subgroup is generated by the
actual `(k,m)` parabolic radical and the final-block upper-unipotent
image. Its character is trivial on that radical and restricts to the
original entrywise character on the final-block subgroup.
`PartialLocalizationCharacter` proves the compatible character
restrictions in the successive mirabolic decomposition. These are
proved matrix and character identities, not assumptions of the comparison.

The two imported carrier equivalences are

```
eL_A = JacquetLHS.generalLinearLinearEquiv T ψ k j A : D_A ≃ₗ[ℂ] Q_A
eR_A = JacquetRHSComparison.linearEquiv T ψ k m hk hm A : R_A ≃ₗ[ℂ] Q_A.
```

The left adapter starts with the actual terminal `Ψ⁻` quotient and
inducts through the actual normalized `Φ⁻` steps. It specializes the
published supplied-twist localization tower using the actual radicals,
successor embeddings, recursive subgroup and character identities. Its
twist-triviality obligation on the second localizing subgroup is proved
from determinant one. The tower has proved inverse maps, so this supplies
an equivalence of the full carriers.

The right adapter builds both linear maps by quotient universal
properties. The actual Jacquet double-quotient map satisfies covariance
on the concrete generated subgroup, giving the map from `Q_A` to `R_A`.
For the other direction, the direct class map kills the first radical
relations and then satisfies the second character relations on the
actual normalized first quotient. The Jacquet scalar is proved to be
one on final-block upper unipotents. Surjectivity of the quotient maps
proves both inverse laws on all vectors.

Put

```
q_A = JacquetNormalization.generalLinearDerivativePositiveMk T ψ k j A
r_A = JacquetRHS.rhsMk T ψ k m hk hm A.
```

These are the composite quotient maps into the existing objects.
Both are surjective. The imported representative formulas say
`eL_A (q_A v) = [v]` and `eR_A (r_A v) = [v]`.
`componentLinearEquiv` is literally

```
eL_A.trans eR_A.symm.
```

It therefore comes with both inverse laws. The theorem
`componentLinearEquiv_mk` proves the literal formula
`componentLinearEquiv ... A (q_A v) = r_A v`. No injectivity,
surjectivity or equality of quotient kernels is supplied by the caller.
The intermediate `Q_A` is only used as a complex module; the final
components have the actual smooth representation objects as their source
and target.

## The actual normalization and equivariance

Let `B = Shahidi.twoBlocks k m hk hm` and
`e = Shahidi.twoBlockLeviContinuousEquiv hk hm F.Carrier`. For `g : GL_k`
the exact scalar is

```
λ(g) =
  Jacquet.inverseHalfCharacter (T.leviModulus B) (e.symm (g,1)).
```

This formula uses the inverse continuous Levi equivalence, as does the
unchanged `positiveTwoBlockNormalizedJacquet` functor. Pullback along
that inverse retains the normalized Jacquet carrier and maps.
The formula `T.leviModulus_twoBlocks` gives
`δ(g0,g1) = |det g0|^m / |det g1|^k`. Hence `λ(g)` is the canonical
positive inverse square root of `|det g|^m`, usually denoted
`|det g|^(-m/2)`. The first-factor scalar remains in the comparison.

The imported normalization module proves this identification on the
actual derivative quotient maps. Determinants are preserved by the
actual Levi and successor embeddings. Restricting each `Φ⁻` twist
through the successor tower therefore gives the same half-determinant
character, and its Levi restriction is the actual `Ψ⁻` character.
Induction on the composite quotient map collects the `j` Phi factors,
and the terminal Psi step contributes the last factor. The positive
square-root and real-power identities identify the resulting
`(psiMinusCharacter T k g)^(j+1)` with `λ(g)`. The actual ambient acting
matrix is proved equal to `diag(g,I_m)`.

In particular,
`generalLinearDerivativePositive_action_mk_eq_inverseHalf` proves

```
ρ_D(g) (q_A v) = (λ(g) : ℂ) • q_A (ρ_A(diag(g,I_m)) v).
```

`JacquetRHS.rhs_action_mk` proves the same formula with `ρ_R,r_A`.
The partial derivative contributes no additional scalar.
The auxiliary `lhs_action_mk` records the left formula after applying
`eL_A`.

To construct `componentEquiv`, the Lean proof uses
`Representation.Equiv.mk` on `componentLinearEquiv`. For each `g`, linear
map extensionality reduces equivariance to an arbitrary vector of `D_A`.
Surjectivity of `q_A` supplies an original representative `v`. Rewriting
both action formulas, moving the scalar through the linear map, and
applying `componentLinearEquiv_mk` proves

```
C_A (ρ_D(g) x) = ρ_R(g) (C_A x)
```

for every `x`, where `C_A = componentLinearEquiv ... A`.
Thus the component is an equivalence of the actual normalized
representations, with the inverse already present in its linear
equivalence.

These calculations compare the published explicit determinant-normalized
functors. They do not require an additional identification of a supplied
character with a Haar conjugation modulus.

## Naturality and the natural isomorphism

For every ambient intertwiner `f : A ⟶ A'`, `lhs_natural` proves that
the actual mapped derivative intertwiner commutes with `eL` and the
direct quotient map `Jacquet.Localization.mapLinear ... (SmoothRep.hom f)`.
Its proof reduces an arbitrary derivative vector by surjectivity of
`q_A`, uses the imported representative formula and the actual
`generalLinearDerivativePositiveMk_natural`, then closes by the
definition of the induced quotient map.

`componentLinearEquiv_natural` combines this equality with
`JacquetRHSComparison.linearEquiv_symm_natural`. It proves, for every
vector `x : D_A`,

```
SmoothRep.hom (R.map f) (C_A x) =
  C_A' (SmoothRep.hom (D.map f) x).
```

Here `D` and `R` are the unchanged positive derivative and right-hand
composite functors. In particular both sides use their actual images of
`f`.

`positiveIso` applies `SmoothRep.mkIso` to each `componentEquiv`, then
`NatIso.ofComponents`. Extensionality for the full subcategory,
representations, intertwiners and linear maps reduces the categorical
naturality field to the preceding all-vector equality. This supplies a
genuine natural isomorphism of smooth-representation-valued functors.

## Rank transport and the closing theorem

The helper `derivative_add_succ` proves the functor equality

```
generalLinearDerivative T ψ (k+(j+1)) (j+1)
    (Nat.le_add_left (j+1) k) ⋙
  generalLinearRankCast T (Nat.add_sub_cancel k (j+1)) =
    generalLinearDerivativePositive T ψ k j.
```

The definition of the ordinary positive-order derivative contains an
input rank transport to the positive derivative at remaining rank
`k+(j+1)-(j+1)`. A local equality lemma substitutes this remaining rank
using its equality with `k`. Both transport equalities then have equal
endpoints; `generalLinearRankCast_refl`, `Functor.id_comp` and
`Functor.comp_id` remove them. This argument also handles the proof
terms of the rank equalities without changing a carrier or mapped
intertwiner.

The closing theorem installs `T.topology` and introduces exactly the
target's `ψ,k,m,hk,hm`. The zero case of `m` contradicts `hm`. In the
successor case it rewrites by `derivative_add_succ` and supplies
`positiveIso` as the required `Nonempty` witness. The proof of the target
uses no extra hypothesis.
