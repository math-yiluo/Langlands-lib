Authors: Worker 185 and Worker 242. The modular-lattice instance and equivariant
second-isomorphism construction are adapted from Worker 242's earlier draft;
Worker 185 supplies the interval-relation argument, comparison, and transitivity.

`HaveSameJordanHolderFactorsTransStatement.{uK,uG,uA,uB,uC}`.

## The actual quotient and action

The proof imports `LengthCriterionProof.quotientRep` and
`LengthCriterionProof.hasSimpleFactors_iff_covBy` from the independently
published `Langlands.Representation.Filtration.LengthCriterionProof`.
For invariant subspaces `S` and `T`, `quotientRep S T` acts on
`T.toSubmodule ⧸ (S.toSubmodule.comap T.toSubmodule.subtype)`.
For successive steps of a supplied filtration `s`, it is definitionally the
action of `s.gradedRepresentation i`, including its quotient carrier and module
instances. No change to a merely isomorphic label is made.

The helpers in `Langlands.JordanHolderProof` establish the quotient/action
correspondence required by lattice Jordan–Hölder:

1. `subrepresentationIsModularLattice` proves the modular law by viewing meets,
   joins, and order of invariant subspaces as those of their underlying
   submodules. Mathlib then provides the Jordan–Hölder lattice structure,
   whose maximality relation is the covering relation `⋖`.
2. `quotientSecondIso x y` gives an actual `Representation.Equiv` from
   `y / (y ∩ x)` to `(y + x) / x`. Its underlying linear equivalence is
   `LinearMap.quotientInfEquivSupQuotient`. The map sends the class of a vector
   in `y` to the class of that same vector included in `y + x`. To verify
   equivariance, `Submodule.linearMap_qext` reduces equality of quotient linear
   maps to representatives; `LinearMap.ext` then reduces it to one vector.
   Both compositions apply the ambient action and take the quotient class,
   so the final equality is definitional.
3. `quotientEquiv_of_intervalIso` applies `JordanHolderLattice.Iso.rel` to the
   relation of admitting an actual equivariant quotient isomorphism. Reflexivity,
   symmetry, and transitivity use the corresponding `Representation.Equiv`
   operations. Every generating lattice relation is realized by the inverse
   of `quotientSecondIso`, after commuting the meet and join. Consequently the
   full equivalence relation generated by those lattice relations also gives
   actual quotient equivalences.

Thus the proof uses a lattice theorem together with an explicit verification of
its representation-theoretic interpretation. It does not infer equivariance
from a bare lattice isomorphism.

## Comparison of two supplied series

Let `s : ObjectRepFiltration A n` and `t : ObjectRepFiltration A m` have simple
factors. The imported covering-step characterization turns their consecutive
invariant subspaces into covering pairs. The proof builds Mathlib
`CompositionSeries (Subrepresentation A.ρ)` values `S` and `T` with exactly
those steps and lengths `n` and `m`. Their heads are both bottom and their last
steps are both top, by the supplied filtrations' endpoint laws.

`CompositionSeries.jordan_holder S T` yields a bijection `e : Fin n ≃ Fin m`
and a lattice interval equivalence between the consecutive steps at `i` and
`e i`. Applying `quotientEquiv_of_intervalIso` at every index produces the
required equivalence of actual graded representations. The bijection matches
individual occurrences of factors: distinct indices have distinct images even
when their factors are isomorphic. It therefore preserves multiplicities, not
only constituent sets or lengths.

## Transitivity and independent universes

The first hypothesis supplies series `sA`, `sB` of length `n`, an index
permutation `e`, and factor equivalences. The second supplies series `tB`, `tC`
of length `m`, a permutation `f`, and factor equivalences. The two series of `B`
need not agree.

The comparison theorem is instantiated at the middle carrier universe `uB`.
It yields `g : Fin n ≃ Fin m` matching `sB` and `tB`. Taking finite cardinalities
shows `n = m`; the proof substitutes that equality. The resulting witness
uses `sA`, `tC`, and the permutation `e.trans (g.trans f)`. For every index,
the equivalence from `sA` to `sB`, the comparison from `sB` to `tB`, and the
equivalence from `tB` to `tC` are composed as `(p.trans q).trans r`.
All three carrier universes remain independent.

Length zero needs no separate argument: the construction of the lattice
series, endpoint comparisons, empty index bijections, and universally quantified
factor equivalences apply to it as written. Zero representations are allowed;
there is no ambient nontriviality hypothesis.

## Provenance and scope

This is derived general module theory for representations of an arbitrary
monoid over a field. Its mathematical engine is Mathlib's lattice
Jordan–Hölder theorem and the second isomorphism theorem for modules. The
finite heartbeat allowance on the comparison theorem supports elaboration of
the actual graded quotient types; it changes neither its assumptions nor its
kernel proof.

The motivation and original-source locators are documented in the imported
`JordanHolderStatement.md`: BZ77 uses Jordan–Hölder factors with multiplicities
for smooth complex representations, and chains such comparisons in its
association argument. The arbitrary-field, arbitrary-monoid result here is an
internal algebraic generalization, not a theorem attributed verbatim to BZ77.
This proof contains no p-adic induction, graded refinement, or adjacent-block
argument. It uses no assumed comparison theorem, new axiom, or proof hole.
