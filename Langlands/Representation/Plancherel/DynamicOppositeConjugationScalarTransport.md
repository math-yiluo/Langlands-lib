# Transport of the continued opposite scalar in fixed compact pictures

This module supplies the algebraic step in the proof of Waldspurger, *La formule de
Plancherel pour les groupes p-adiques (d'après Harish-Chandra)*, IV.3 (3), printed
p. 286 (`waldspurger-2003-plancherel`). The helper lemmas derive this step from the
published continuation and canonical scalar characterizations.

For an isomorphism of the actual parameter group schemes, the reverse character
restriction pulls back target parameters to source parameters. The proof
constructs each transported continuation by transferring its regular denominator
and conjugating the global polynomial numerator by the two fixed linear
equivalences. The operator identity on the full convergence domain proves the
clearing equation for the transported continuation. On the common regular region of two
transported continuations the composite coefficient is the source coefficient at
the inverse parameter, using the transported vector and the functional
precomposed with the inverse compact equivalence. The transported numerator and
denominator of its regular presentation thus present the same coefficient as
the target presentation on this nonempty open region. Uniqueness of the
fraction and compatibility of function-field transport with regular functions
give equality of the fractions.

The compact comparison theorem takes the compact equivalences, the
correspondence of the two full convergence domains, the operator identities, and
the pointwise irreducibility equivalence as inputs. Continuation independence
allows the target scalar to be computed with the transported data, using the
target's given forward and reverse convergence opens separately. The proof
transfers the source irreducibility locus by taking its preimage under the
reverse character restriction. The inverse parameter identities give
nonemptiness, and continuity gives openness. Pointwise irreducibility
correspondence and locus independence allow this open to be used for the
target scalar. The proof extracts the source and target scalars using a
normalized compact coefficient and its transport, which retains its
normalization. Applying the coefficient comparison to the actual parameter
isomorphism gives the stated conjugation equality of the canonical scalars.
