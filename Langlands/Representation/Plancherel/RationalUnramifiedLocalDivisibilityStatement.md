# Local hypersurface divisibility for actual unramified regular scalars

`RationalUnramifiedLocalHypersurfaceDivisibilityStatement` is an internal
supporting proof target for the denominator-cancellation route to conditional
standard intertwining. This file states the proposition and supplies no proof.
It is not attributed to an additional theorem in the original
representation-theoretic source.

The proposition quantifies over an arbitrary nonarchimedean local field, an
actual connected reductive group, and a topology on its rational points for
which the actual valuation-image projection is continuous. The character space
uses the full rational-character norm family and its existing compact-open
subspace topology. The scalar ring is exactly the existing
`unramifiedRegularFunctions` algebra of functions on that space, generated by
actual character evaluations. No alternate coordinate ring is substituted.

Let `U` be an open subset of that full actual character space, and let `p` and
`a` belong to the actual scalar algebra. If `p` is irreducible in this ring,
its zero locus meets `U`, and `a` vanishes at every zero of `p` in `U`, the
conclusion is `p ∣ a` in the same ring. Thus there must exist one actual
regular scalar `c` with `a = p * c`, with equality in the function algebra.
The vanishing hypothesis is only on the part of the hypersurface in the
specified open region; no global vanishing premise is imposed. Merely having
one common zero is insufficient and is not the hypothesis.

The published `rationalUnramifiedLaurentAlgebraEquiv` identifies the scalar
algebra with a finite Laurent polynomial algebra over the complex numbers.
The inverse of `rationalImageUnramifiedContinuousEquiv` followed by
`rationalReductiveImageCharacterCoordinates` identifies the actual character
space with the matching finite complex torus, preserving its topology.
These comparisons motivate a proof through local uniqueness on irreducible
complex algebraic hypersurfaces. Unique factorization and density of the
ambient nonzero locus alone do not prove the stated local vanishing-to-
divisibility implication; that implication is the substantial missing input.
The statement includes every finite rank, including zero, and has no
characteristic, splitness, quasisplitness, or smooth-point restriction.

For the intended conditional cancellation argument, a denominator factor
`p` that has a zero in the open convergence region forces all scalar numerator
coefficients to vanish along its zero locus. This proposition would make those
coefficients divisible by `p`. A separate continuity-and-density argument must
then extend the divided identity through those zeros, and a finite
factorization argument must choose one surviving denominator before all input
vectors. None of those cancellation constructions, a generic regular
numerator identity for the actual integral, convergence, or the whole
conditional standard intertwining theorem is proved by this statement.
