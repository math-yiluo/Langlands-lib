# Weak stable limits of polynomial endomorphism families

Author: Worker 1615.

This module is a supporting construction for the host task proving
`Langlands.Plancherel.ConditionalParabolicIntertwiningStatement`. It proves an
algebraic denominator principle. If the iterates of an endomorphism, applied to
a starting vector, converge weakly, then an annihilating polynomial of the form
`(X - 1)^k r` gives `r(1) • limit = r(Φ) start`. For a polynomial
endomorphism family on a finite-dimensional space, over the concrete regular
algebra `unramifiedRegularFunctions ν`, this gives one nonzero regular scalar
that works for every starting vector.

The module states and proves no intertwining-operator result. It does not
construct the operators, the truncated integrals or their convergence. Those
are the inputs of the intended application described at the end.

## Weak limits along a stable functional space

Let `E` be a complex vector space and `Φ` an endomorphism of `E`. Let `D` be
a submodule of the algebraic dual. Two hypotheses about `D` are used
throughout:

- `hD`: precomposition with `Φ` maps `D` into itself;
- `hsep`: a vector on which every functional of `D` vanishes is zero.

Neither finite dimensionality of `E` nor a topology on `E` is assumed in this
section. "Weak convergence" means convergence of each scalar sequence
`l(xₙ)` for `l ∈ D`.

- `comp_pow_mem` and `comp_aeval_mem`: precomposition with every power of
  `Φ`, and with `aeval Φ p` for every complex polynomial `p`, preserves `D`.
  The second proof expands `aeval Φ p` as the finite sum
  `∑ coeff i • Φ^i`.
- `eq_zero_of_unipotent_of_tendsto_zero`: if `(Φ - 1)^k y = 0` and
  `l(Φⁿ y) → 0` for every `l ∈ D`, then `y = 0`. The proof is by induction
  on `k`. The vector `z = (Φ - 1)^(k-1) y` is fixed by `Φ`. Since
  `l ∘ (Φ - 1)^(k-1)` again lies in `D` and commutes past `Φⁿ`, the constant
  sequence `l(Φⁿ z) = l(z)` tends to zero. Hence `l(z) = 0` for every `l ∈ D`,
  so `z = 0` by separation, and the induction hypothesis applies.
- `stableLimit_fixed`: if `l(Φⁿ β₀) → l(β)` for every `l ∈ D`, then
  `Φ β = β`. Apply the hypothesis to `l ∘ Φ ∈ D` and compare with the shifted
  sequence `l(Φⁿ⁺¹ β₀)`. Uniqueness of limits in `ℂ` and separation give the
  claim.
- `aeval_apply_of_fixed`: on a fixed vector, `aeval Φ p` acts by `p(1)`. For
  a nonzero vector this is Mathlib's eigenvector evaluation
  `Module.End.aeval_apply_of_hasEigenvector`.
- `eval_one_smul_eq_aeval_of_stableLimit`: assume the weak convergence of the
  previous item and `aeval Φ ((X - C 1)^k * r) = 0`. Then
  `r.eval 1 • β = aeval Φ r β₀`. Put `y = r(Φ)(β₀ - β)`. The annihilation
  identity gives `(Φ - 1)^k y = 0`. Since `β` is fixed and `r(Φ)` commutes
  with `Φⁿ`, `l(Φⁿ y) = (l ∘ r(Φ))(Φⁿ β₀) - (l ∘ r(Φ))(β)`, which tends to
  zero because `l ∘ r(Φ) ∈ D`. Hence `y = 0`, and `r(Φ) β = r(1) β` because
  `β` is fixed.

The identity holds at each individual parameter. It requires neither
`r(1) ≠ 0` nor continuity in a parameter.

## Pairs of endomorphisms and bilinear forms

For complex spaces `W` and `Y` in independent universes,
`bilinearPrecomp T S` is the endomorphism `β ↦ β.compl₁₂ T S` of the space
of bilinear forms `W →ₗ[ℂ] Y →ₗ[ℂ] ℂ`. Thus
`bilinearPrecomp T S β w y = β (T w) (S y)` (`bilinearPrecomp_apply`), and
its `n`-th power evaluates as `β (Tⁿ w) (Sⁿ y)`
(`bilinearPrecomp_pow_apply`).

`bilinearEval w y` is evaluation of a form at the pair `(w, y)`, and
`bilinearEvalSpan` is the span of all these evaluations. It satisfies the two
standing hypotheses: precomposition with `bilinearPrecomp T S` sends the
generator at `(w, y)` to the generator at `(T w, S y)`
(`bilinearEvalSpan_comp_mem`), and a form on which all evaluations vanish is
zero (`bilinearEvalSpan_separates`). Pointwise convergence
`β₀ (Tⁿ w) (Sⁿ y) → β w y` for all pairs gives weak convergence along the
whole span (`bilinearEvalSpan_tendsto`), by induction over the span.

## Regular families

The section `Families` uses the actual norm-character family `ν`, the
existing algebra `B = unramifiedRegularFunctions ν` generated by character
evaluations, and an arbitrary parameter subset `Ω`. It reuses the published
predicate `UnramifiedOperatorFamily.IsPolynomialOn`: a finite expansion
`∑ bᵢ(χ) • vᵢ` with `bᵢ ∈ B` and fixed vectors `vᵢ`. Applied to the vector
space `Module.End ℂ E`, it expresses a polynomial endomorphism family.

- `regularEval χ` is evaluation at `χ`, as a complex algebra homomorphism on
  `B`.
- `isPolynomialOn_zero`, `isPolynomialOn_finset_sum`: closure under zero and
  finite sums.
- `IsPolynomialOn.endApply`, `IsPolynomialOn.pow_apply`, `IsPolynomialOn.mul`:
  applying a polynomial endomorphism family to a polynomial vector family,
  iterating it on a fixed vector, and composing two families are polynomial.
  The proofs multiply the finite expansions.
- `IsPolynomialOn.bilinearPrecomp`: if `T` and `S` are polynomial families,
  so is `χ ↦ bilinearPrecomp (T χ) (S χ)`. It is the product of the families
  `bilinearPrecomp (T χ) 1` and `bilinearPrecomp 1 (S χ)`, each polynomial
  with the original coefficients.
- `IsPolynomialOn.aeval_map_apply`: for `q ∈ B[X]`, the specialization of `q`
  at `χ`, evaluated at `Φ χ` and applied to a fixed vector, is polynomial. It
  is `∑ qᵢ(χ) • (Φ χ)ⁱ v` over a range bounded by `natDegree q + 1`.
- `IsPolynomialOn.exists_monic_annihilator`: on a finite-dimensional `E` there
  is a monic `P ∈ B[X]` whose specialization at every `χ ∈ Ω` annihilates
  `Φ χ`. Write `Φ χ = ∑ cᵢ(χ) • Tᵢ`. In a fixed basis, the matrix
  `∑ cᵢ • (matrix of Tᵢ)` has entries in `B`. Its characteristic polynomial
  is `P`. By Mathlib's `Matrix.charpoly_map`, specializing `P` gives the
  characteristic polynomial of the specialized matrix. This is the matrix of
  `Φ χ`, so `LinearMap.charpoly_toMatrix` and Cayley–Hamilton
  (`LinearMap.aeval_self_charpoly`) finish the proof.

## The regular numerator theorems

`IsPolynomialOn.exists_regular_stableLimit_numerator` assumes a polynomial
endomorphism family `Φ` on a finite-dimensional `E` and one functional space
`D` that is stable under every `Φ χ` and separates points. It produces
`b ∈ B` with `b ≠ 0` before any starting vector is chosen. For every
`β₀ ∈ E` and every family `β : Ω → E` with
`l((Φ χ)ⁿ β₀) → l(β χ)` for all `χ ∈ Ω` and `l ∈ D`, the family
`χ ↦ b(χ) • β χ` is polynomial.

Proof. Factor the monic annihilator as `P = (X - 1)^k q` with `k` the root
multiplicity of `1`, so that `X - 1` does not divide `q`
(`Polynomial.exists_eq_pow_rootMultiplicity_mul_and_not_dvd`). Put
`b = q(1)`. Then `b ≠ 0` by `Polynomial.dvd_iff_isRoot`. This uses only that
`B` is a nontrivial commutative ring, which holds because the trivial
character exists. Specializing the factorization at `χ` gives an annihilator
`(X - 1)^k q_χ` of `Φ χ`. The pointwise identity gives
`b(χ) • β χ = q_χ(Φ χ) β₀`, using `Polynomial.eval_one_map`. The right side
is polynomial by `IsPolynomialOn.aeval_map_apply`.

`exists_regular_bilinear_stableLimit_numerator` is the same statement for
forms `W →ₗ[ℂ] Y →ₗ[ℂ] ℂ` on finite-dimensional `W` and `Y`, with
`Φ χ = bilinearPrecomp (T χ) (S χ)` for polynomial families `T` and `S`. Its
convergence hypothesis is pointwise:
`β₀ ((T χ)ⁿ w) ((S χ)ⁿ y) → β χ w y` for all `χ`, `w`, `y`. One `b ≠ 0`
precedes every starting form. Arbitrary `Ω`, including the empty set, and
zero-dimensional spaces are allowed. In the latter case the annihilator is
`1` and `b = 1`.

## Intended application and scope

For the actual standard compact operator, the intended application is as
follows. `W` is a space of compact-picture vectors fixed by an
Iwahori-factorized compact open subgroup, and `T χ` is the Casselman
compression of right translation by a central contracting element. `Y` is a
finite-dimensional space of smooth functionals, and `S χ` is the inverse of the
normalized central action. `β₀` is the truncated integral over a compact piece
of the target radical quotient, and `β χ` is the full standard integral. The
module proves none of these identifications, the weak convergence of the
truncations, the finite dimensionality or polynomiality of the actual
families, or uniformity over all source vectors. They remain separate
obligations of the host task.

No literature theorem is invoked. The use of Cayley–Hamilton and of a factor
coprime to `X - 1` is an internal algebraic substitute for the generalized
eigenspace and resultant construction of Waldspurger, *La formule de
Plancherel pour les groupes p-adiques d'après Harish-Chandra* (2003), IV.1,
printed pp.280–282. That construction requires the Jacquet-module
geometric filtration, which this module does not use or assert.
