# Prime divisibility and multiplicative differences of fractions

This algebraic lemma is derived for the rank-one product used in the opposite scalar problem. Each step in an iterated multiplicative difference divides a translated fraction by the preceding fraction. `MultiplicativeDifferencesVanish` says that the result is one for every choice of one automorphism from each set in the list.

Suppose a prime divides exactly one of the numerator and denominator. If a translation makes the product of their two translates free of the prime divisor, the new fraction still has the prime in exactly one side and the side switches. The proof applies this step inductively. After the final step, the prime divisibility of exactly one side contradicts equality to one. Consequently some set in the list has a nonzero ring element whose every translate is divisible by the prime. The application uses sets of translations by ambient unramified characters of the rank-one intermediate Levis.
