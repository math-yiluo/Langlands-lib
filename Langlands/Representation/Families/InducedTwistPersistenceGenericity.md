# Genericity at one rank-one wall

Author: Worker 5120.

This module produces, for one rank-one wall and one representative of `N_G(L)(k)/L(k)`,
a nonzero polynomial on `X(M)` off whose zero set the relevant induced representations are
irreducible. All declarations are in `Langlands.InducedTwistPersistence`. The unramified
characters `X(H)` of a group `H` are the published `Plancherel.unramifiedCharacters`
of the rational-character norms. Polynomials live in the canonical coordinates
`rationalImageRank`.

## Pullback of polynomials (`section Pullback`)

- `comp_restrict_mul_mem_unramifiedRegularFunctions`: for `f₁ : L → M`, `f₂ : L → M₁`,
  `φ₀ ∈ X(L)` and `χ₀ ∈ X(M₁)`, a regular function `b` on `X(L)` pulls back to a regular
  function `ψ ↦ b(φ₀ · ψ|_L · χ₀|_L)` on `X(M)`. The proof uses an explicit algebra map
  on the published regular-function algebras and `Algebra.adjoin_le`.
- `exists_polynomial_restrict_mul_mem` (polynomial pullback across a wall). Suppose
  `(ψ, χ) ↦ ψ|_L · χ|_L` maps `X(M) × X(M₁)` onto `X(L)`, and let `f ≠ 0` be a polynomial
  on `X(L)` and `φ₀ ∈ X(L)`. Then there is a polynomial `g ≠ 0` on `X(M)` such that every
  `ψ` off the zero set of `g` admits `χ ∈ X(M₁)` with `φ₀ · ψ|_L · χ|_L` off the zero set
  of `f`. The proof picks a point `φ₁` where `f` is nonzero (density), writes
  `φ₀⁻¹φ₁ = ψ₁|_L · χ₁|_L`, and pulls back `f` along `ψ ↦ φ₀ · ψ|_L · χ₁|_L`. It then
  takes the published `rationalRegularPolynomial`, which is nonzero at `ψ₁`.

## Divisibility (`section Divisibility`)

`unramified_apply_map_eq_of_splitComponent`. Let `f : L → M`, `φ : L → L`, and let `b_L`
be a split component of `L`. Suppose `ψ(f(φ x)) = ψ(f x)` for every `ψ ∈ X(M)` and every
`x` in the image of `b_L(k)`. Then the same holds for every `x ∈ L(k)`.

The two sides agree on the subgroup `H` generated by the common norm kernel of `L(k)` and
the split component. `H` has finite index, by the published
`finiteIndex_splitValuationImage_of_injective`, `finite_rationalCentralQuotient` and the
character restriction of V.2.1. Write `ψ = ψ'^{[L(k):H]}`; this is possible because
`X(M) ≅ (ℂˣ)^n` is divisible (`exists_pow_eq_of_mulEquiv`). Then evaluate at
`x^{[L(k):H]} ∈ H`.

## Twists (`section Twist`)

- `nontrivial_of_isIrreducible`: an irreducible complex representation has nonzero

- `twistEquivOfEquivScalar`: suppose `A ≃ (g ↦ a(g)ρ(g))` and `B ≃ (g ↦ b(g)ρ(g))`, and

## The wall lemma (`section Wall`)

`exists_polynomial_mul_ne_of_ne` is the published two-point lemma
`exists_polynomial_ne_of_two_points`, for a connected reductive `M` not presented as a
Levi.

`exists_polynomial_isIrreducible_wall` takes these inputs:

- Renard VI.8.5 (`GenericInducedIrreducibilityStatement.{u, max u v}`) as hypothesis
  `hVI`;
- a parabolic `Q₁ = L V₁` of `M₁`, a map `f : L → M` and an automorphism `φ` of `L`;
- the surjectivity of `X(M) × X(M₁) → X(L)` (`hsurj`);
- a split component `b_L` of `L` and an irreducible `σ` of `L(k)`.

It gives a polynomial `g ≠ 0` on `X(M)` with the following property. For every `ψ` off its
zero set and every twist character `θ_L` of `L(k)` with open kernel and
`θ_L(z) = ψ(f z)`, take any irreducible supercuspidal `C` that is equivalent to the
pullback along `φ` of `σ ⊗ θ_L` and shares an exponent of `b_L` with `σ ⊗ θ_L`. Then
`i^{M₁}_{Q₁}(C)` is irreducible.

Proof by cases:

1. **`φ` fixes every `ψ ∈ X(M)` on the split component.** By divisibility, `φ` fixes
   `ψ|_L` on all of `L(k)`. If no `ψ_*` and no irreducible supercuspidal `C_*` equivalent
   to the twisted pullback exist, take `g = 1`: the conclusion is vacuous. Otherwise
   VI.8.5 for `C_*` gives a polynomial `F` on `X(L)`, and the polynomial pullback with
   `φ₀ = (ψ_*|_L)⁻¹` gives `g`. For `ψ` off the zero set of `g`, choose `χ₁` with
   `C_* ⊗ (φ₀ ψ|_L χ₁|_L)` generic. `twistEquivOfEquivScalar` and the published
   `leviInductionEquivOfEquiv` identify `i(C ⊗ χ₁)` with this generic induced
   representation. Twist invariance (`leviInduction_isIrreducible_unramifiedTwist_iff`)
   removes `χ₁ ∈ X(M₁)`.
2. **Some `ψ₀` and `a₀` have `ψ₀(f(φ(b_L a₀))) ≠ ψ₀(f(b_L a₀))`.** Put `x = φ(b_L a₀)` and
   `y = b_L a₀`. Comparing the central scalars of `C` and of `σ ⊗ θ_L` at `a₀`
   (`apply_eq_smul_of_isGeneralizedEigencharacter`) shows two things: `σ(x)` and `σ(y)` act
   by scalars `s`, `t`, and `s ψ(f x) = t ψ(f y)`. If such scalars exist,
   `exists_polynomial_mul_ne_of_ne` gives `g` with `s ψ(f x) ≠ t ψ(f y)` off its zero set.
   That makes the hypotheses contradictory there. Otherwise the hypotheses are never
   satisfiable, and `g = 1`.

## Provenance and boundary

This is the genericity step of the derived second-adjointness route to Konno 2003,
Theorem 4.2. The VI.8.5 statement is Renard, *Représentations des groupes réductifs
p-adiques*, VI.8.5. It enters only as the hypothesis `hVI`, a published proof target and
not a theorem. The wall surjectivity enters as the hypothesis `hsurj`. It is supplied
later from the published proof target `Plancherel.WallUnramifiedTwistSurjectivityStatement`.
No other unproved input is used.
