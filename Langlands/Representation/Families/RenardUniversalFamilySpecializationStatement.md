On PDF p. 194, for a `(G, B)`-module `V` and `χ ∈ S = SpecMax(B)`, Renard writes
`Ψ_χ : B → ℂ` for the corresponding algebra morphism, `I_χ` for its kernel, and
defines the specialization `V_χ = V ⊗_B B/I_χ`. Writing `ℂ_{Ψ_χ}` for `ℂ` with
`b · z = Ψ_χ(b) z`, he notes `V_χ = V ⊗_B ℂ_{Ψ_χ}`.

The Example on PDF p. 195 takes a smooth representation `(π, V)` of `G`,
`B = ℂ[Λ(G)]` the algebra of polynomials on the variety `X(G)` of unramified
characters, and `χ_un : G → B^×` the evaluation at points of `G`. It sets
`V_B = V ⊗_ℂ B` with `π_B(g)(v ⊗ b) = π(g) v ⊗ χ_un(g) b`. The last paragraph reads:
"Si `χ ∈ X(G)`, la spécialisation de `π_B` en `χ` est isomorphe à `π ⊗ χ` :
`V_B ⊗_B B/I_χ = (V ⊗ B) ⊗_B ℂ_{Ψ_χ} = V ⊗_ℂ χ`, où `ℂ_χ` est `ℂ` vu comme espace
de la représentation `χ` de `G`." The verification of the action uses
`Ψ_χ(χ_un(g)) = χ(g)`, the identity (V.2.4.1).

## Lean model

The statement is made for an arbitrary group, as in the published universal-twist
module `UnramifiedUniversalTwist`:

- `G` is a group with a topology, and `ν : I → (G →* ℝ≥0ˣ)` an arbitrary family of
  norm characters. The unramified characters are the published
  `Plancherel.unramifiedCharacters ν`: the continuous characters `G → ℂˣ` trivial
  on the common kernel of the `ν i`. With `ν` the rational norm characters of a
  reductive group, this is the model of Renard's `X(G)` used by the published
  VI.8.4, VI.8.5 and VI.8.6 statements.
- `(π, V)` is a `ℂ`-linear representation `π : Representation ℂ G V`.
- `B` is the published `Plancherel.unramifiedRegularFunctions ν`: the `ℂ`-algebra
  of functions on the unramified characters generated by the evaluations
  `ψ ↦ ψ(g)`, `g ∈ G`.
- `(π_B, V_B)` is the published `Plancherel.universalUnramifiedRepresentation ν π`
  on `B ⊗[ℂ] V`. It acts by `g · (b ⊗ v) = χ_un(g) b ⊗ π(g) v`, where `χ_un(g)` is
  the evaluation at `g` (`unramifiedEvaluationRegular ν g`).
- For an unramified character `χ`, the morphism `Ψ_χ` is the published
  `Plancherel.UnramifiedNormalized.evaluation ν χ : B →+* ℂ`, `b ↦ b(χ)`. The
  statement makes `ℂ` a `B`-algebra through it, so that `ℂ` is the module
  `ℂ_{Ψ_χ}`.
- The specialization of `V_B` at `χ` is `ℂ ⊗[B] (B ⊗[ℂ] V)`, with the action
  `baseChange ℂ (universalUnramifiedRepresentation ν π)` of the published
  [RenardFamilyParabolicFunctors](RenardFamilyParabolicFunctors.md):
  `g · (c ⊗ x) = c ⊗ π_B(g) x`.
- `π ⊗ χ` is the published `Langlands.twist χ.val.toMonoidHom π` on `V`:
  `g · v = χ(g) π(g) v`.

The statement installs the additive group and `B`-module structures of
`B ⊗[ℂ] V` as Mathlib's `TensorProduct.addCommGroup` and
`TensorProduct.leftModule` with `letI`. These are the structures that the
published universal-twist module uses; they do not change the meaning.

## Declaration

`UniversalSpecializationStatement.{uG, uI, uV}`: for every group `G : Type uG` with
a topology, every family `ν` indexed by `I : Type uI`, every representation
`π : Representation ℂ G V` on `V : Type uV` and every unramified character `χ`,
with `ℂ` a `B`-algebra through evaluation at `χ`, there is a `ℂ`-linear
equivalence `e : ℂ ⊗[B] (B ⊗[ℂ] V) ≃ₗ[ℂ] V` such that

1. `e` intertwines the base-changed action and the twist: for all `g ∈ G` and
   all `x`, `e (g · x) = χ(g) π(g) (e x)`;
2. `e (c ⊗ (b ⊗ v)) = (c b(χ)) v` for all `c ∈ ℂ`, `b ∈ B` and `v ∈ V`.

The second clause determines `e`, since the pure tensors span the source.

## Correspondence notes

1. Generality. The source's `G` is the group of rational points of a reductive
   group, `B = ℂ[Λ(G)]` and `χ ∈ X(G)`. The statement holds for any group, any
   family `ν` of norm characters and any representation `π`; smoothness of `π`,
   which the source assumes, is not needed for the identification and is not
   assumed.
2. The algebra `B`. The source's `B = ℂ[Λ(G)]` is the coordinate ring of `X(G)`.
   The statement uses the published algebra of evaluation functions on the
   unramified characters, as do the published VI.8.4, VI.8.5 and VI.8.6
   statements. For the rational norm characters of an affine group scheme of
   locally finite type over a nonarchimedean local field, with the canonical topology on its rational points,
   the published `Langlands.Plancherel.rationalCanonicalUnramifiedCoordinateAlgebraEquiv`
   (`RationalUnramifiedCoordinateAlgebra`) identifies this algebra with the complex
   group algebra of the discrete valuation image `rationalDiscreteValuationImage G`,
   and `rationalCanonicalUnramifiedLaurentAlgebraEquiv` identifies it with a Laurent
   polynomial algebra when the group is connected reductive. The statement does not
   use these comparisons.
3. Tensor order. The source writes `V_B = V ⊗_ℂ B` and `V_χ = V_B ⊗_B ℂ_{Ψ_χ}`.
   The published universal family is `B ⊗[ℂ] V`, and the published base change of
   a family is `C ⊗[B] V`. So the specialization is `ℂ ⊗[B] (B ⊗[ℂ] V)`, which the
   symmetry of the tensor product identifies with the source's
   `(V ⊗_ℂ B) ⊗_B ℂ_{Ψ_χ}`.
4. Specialization. The source defines `V_χ = V ⊗_B B/I_χ` and identifies `B/I_χ`
   with `ℂ_{Ψ_χ}`. The statement uses `ℂ_{Ψ_χ}`, the form in which the source
   carries out the computation.
5. The twist. The source's `π ⊗ χ` acts on `V ⊗_ℂ ℂ_χ` by `π(g) v ⊗ χ(g) z`. The
   statement uses the twist on `V` itself, through `V ⊗_ℂ ℂ ≃ V`.
6. The map. The source's chain of identifications sends
   `(v ⊗ b) ⊗ z` to `v ⊗ Ψ_χ(b) z`. Clause 2 records this map in the published
   order. The identity (V.2.4.1), `Ψ_χ(χ_un(g)) = χ(g)`, holds by definition for
   the published model: `(unramifiedEvaluationRegular ν g).val χ = χ(g)` is the
   published simp lemma `unramifiedEvaluationRegular_apply`.
7. Only the last paragraph of the Example is stated. That `V_B` is a free, hence
   flat, `B`-module, and that `V_B^K = V^K ⊗ B`, are the progress note's rows
   (Ex-a) and (Ex-b).
