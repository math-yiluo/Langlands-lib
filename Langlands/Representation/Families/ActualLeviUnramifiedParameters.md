# Unramified parameters on an actual rational Levi

This internal adapter uses the published topological group equivalence
`leviPointsEquiv p l hp hl` between the rational points of an actual rational
Levi and their literal image in the ambient rational-point group. The base field
is a nonarchimedean local field in an arbitrary universe. The ambient group and
the Levi have affine structure morphisms, and `hp` and `hl` assert that the supplied
morphisms are a parabolic and a rational Levi. All point topologies are the
published canonical topologies. The explicit affine instance for the Levi is
available from `hl.isConnectedReductive.isAffineHom`. No root presentation is chosen.

`normFamily` transports every rational-character norm of the Levi along the
inverse point equivalence. Its value at an image point is exactly the original
norm at the inverse image. Each transported norm is continuous: the canonical
rational norm is continuous, and the inverse Levi point equivalence is
continuous. Affineness of the Levi follows from its connected reductivity.

`parameterEquiv` is the published unramified-character transport applied to
this point equivalence and the full norm family. It is a multiplicative
homeomorphism. A transported character is evaluated by taking the inverse
image of the point, and the inverse character is evaluated at its image.

`regularAlgebraEquiv` is the corresponding complex-algebra equivalence between
the algebras generated by all actual evaluations. Its forward map precomposes
with the inverse parameter equivalence, and its inverse map precomposes with
the forward parameter equivalence. The named evaluation elements correspond
at the original point and its Levi image.

The constructions and all evaluation identities are specializations of the
published `UnramifiedCharacterTransport` and `UnramifiedOperatorTransport`
APIs. This derived adapter adds no representation-theoretic assertion. In
particular, it does not supply compactness of the parabolic quotient,
admissibility, a detecting subgroup, or the induced irreducibility proposition.
