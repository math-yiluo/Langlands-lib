## The statement

**`StandardIwahoriCornerInductionEquivalenceStatement`.** The group data and the hypotheses
on `K` are copied word for word from the published
`StandardIwahoriFixedVectorHeckeEquivalenceStatement` (module
`Langlands.Representation.Jacquet.StandardIwahoriDecompositionStatements`, whose Markdown
explains them):

- `F` is a nonarchimedean local field and `G` a connected reductive group scheme over
  `Spec F`. `rationalPoints G` carries `rationalPointTopology F G.X` and the topological
  group structure `rationalPoint_isTopologicalGroup F G`;
- `i : S.toGrp ⟶ G` is a maximal split torus, `ν` a generic cocharacter, and `_hstandard`
  gives a standard cocharacter for every `J ⊆ simpleRelativeRoots i ν`;
- `K₀` is a compact subgroup with the Iwasawa factorization
  `G(F) = M_∅(F) N_∅(F) K₀` (V.5.1 (1));
- `K` is a compact open subgroup with `K ≤ K₀`, `K₀ ≤ normalizer K`, and the Iwahori
  decomposition `HasIwahoriIntersectionDecomposition` along every standard parabolic
  (`standardLeviPoints i ν J`, `standardUnipotentPoints i ν J`,
  `standardOppositeUnipotentPoints i ν J`).

In addition the statement takes a measurable structure `[MeasurableSpace (rationalPoints G)]`
with `[BorelSpace (rationalPoints G)]`, and a left Haar measure `haar`
(`[haar.IsHaarMeasure]`). It then sets:

- `P`: the property of `V : SmoothRep.{0, uF, max uF uV} ℂ (rationalPoints G)` that every
  subrepresentation whose underlying submodule contains `fixedVectors V.representation K` is
  `⊤`. This is "`V^K` engendre `V`", as in the published VI.9.4 statements, and
  `P.FullSubcategory` is the source's `M(G)_K`;
- `e : Algebra.Idempotent (Hecke.HeckeAlgebra haar)`: the published normalized indicator
  `Hecke.HeckeAlgebra.normalizedIndicator haar K hK = μ(K)⁻¹ 1_K`, which is the source's
  `e_K`, with the published idempotence proof `normalizedIndicator_idempotent`.

The conclusion is: there is an equivalence of categories
`E : P.FullSubcategory ≌ ModuleCat.{max uF uV} (Algebra.Idempotent.Corner e)` such that

1. `E.functor = P.ι ⋙ Hecke.heckeNondegenerateModuleFunctor haar ⋙
   Algebra.NonUnitalModuleCorner.nondegenerateFunctor e`, that is, `E` is the functor
   `V ↦ V^K = e_K · V` with its `H(G, K)`-module structure; and
2. there is a natural isomorphism
   `E.inverse ⋙ P.ι ⋙ Hecke.heckeNondegenerateModuleFunctor haar ≅
   Algebra.NonUnitalModuleCornerInduction.nondegenerateFunctor (Hecke.Homomorphism.isIdempotented haar) e`,
   that is, the `H(G)`-module underlying the representation `E.inverse M` is naturally
   isomorphic to `H(G) ⊗_{H(G,K)} M`.

Clause 1 says that `V ↦ V^K` is an equivalence: a functor equals the functor of an
equivalence only if it is itself an equivalence. Clause 2 identifies the inverse, which is
the content of (ii) beyond the VI.9.4 Proposition.

## Published objects used and how they match the source

- **`H(G)`.** `Hecke.HeckeAlgebra haar` is the algebra of locally constant compactly
  supported functions with convolution `(f ∗ g)(x) = ∫ f(y) g(y⁻¹ x) d haar(y)`. This is the
  identification of the III.1.3 Remark.
- **`M(H(G))` and III.1.4.** `Algebra.NondegenerateModuleCat (Hecke.HeckeAlgebra haar)` is
  the category of nondegenerate `H(G)`-modules. `Hecke.heckeNondegenerateModuleFunctor haar`
  sends a smooth representation to its carrier with the integrated action
  `heckeAction haar`, `f · v = ∫ f(g) ρ(g) v d haar(g)`, and an intertwiner to the same
  additive map. The published theorem `Hecke.heckeNondegenerateModuleEquivalence` proves that
  it is an equivalence (III.1.4).
- **`H(G, K)`.** `Algebra.Idempotent.Corner e` is the corner `e_K H(G) e_K`, as a unital ring
  with unit `e_K`. As a set it is the published `Hecke.HeckeAlgebra.Corner haar K hK` of
  bi-`K`-invariant test functions: an element `f` of `H(G)` is bi-`K`-invariant iff
  `e_K ∗ f ∗ e_K = f` (`Hecke.HeckeAlgebra.mem_biInvariant_iff_sandwich`). The abstract
  corner is used because it is the ring of the published I.3.2 functors.
  `ModuleCat (Idempotent.Corner e)` is the category of unital `H(G, K)`-modules,
  `M(H(G, K))`. A unital module is a complex vector space through `ℂ e_K ⊆ H(G, K)`.
- **`j_K`.** `Algebra.NonUnitalModuleCorner.nondegenerateFunctor e` is Renard's
  `j_e : M ↦ e · M` of I.3.1 on nondegenerate modules. For the module of a smooth
  representation, `e_K · V = V^K` (published `Hecke.heckeAction_normalizedIndicator_mem` and
  `Hecke.heckeAction_normalizedIndicator_of_fixed`), which is III.1.5 Proposition (i).
- **`i`.** `Algebra.NonUnitalModuleCornerInduction.nondegenerateFunctor hA e` is the
  induction functor `Z ↦ A ⊗_{eAe} Z` of the I.3.2 Lemma, landing in nondegenerate
  `A`-modules (here `A = H(G)`). The right action of the corner on `A` has no unit law, as
  the source remarks after the Lemma. `hA` is the published proof
  `Hecke.Homomorphism.isIdempotented haar` that `H(G)` is idempotented.

The instances needed by these objects are supplied inside the statement from published
theorems: `T2Space F` (`Langlands.BruhatTits.localField_t2Space`),
`T2Space (rationalPoints G)` (`rationalPoint_t2Space`), `LocallyCompactSpace`
(`rationalPoint_locallyCompactSpace`) and `NonarchimedeanGroup`
(`Langlands.Plancherel.rationalPoints_nonarchimedeanGroup`). The topology and group
structure are the same as in the published VI.9.4 statements.

## Formalization choices

- **Universe.** The induced module `H(G) ⊗_{H(G,K)} M` of a module `M` in universe `w` lives
  in `max uF w` (`Algebra.NonUnitalModuleCornerInduction.nondegenerateFunctor`). An
  equivalence needs both categories in one universe, so the representations and modules
  have carriers in `max uF uV`, as in the published I.3.2 equivalence
  `NonUnitalModuleCornerSimpleProof.generatedEquivalence`. Every universe `w ≥ uF` has this
  form (take `uV = w`), so only carrier universes smaller than that of `F` are excluded. The
  source does not discuss universes.
- **The equivalence is data under `∃`.** Asking for `E.functor` to *equal* the composite
  functor is the strongest way to say that `V ↦ V^K` is the equivalence. It is not
  stronger than the source: given that the composite is an equivalence,
  `Functor.asEquivalence` produces an `E` whose functor is the composite by definition.
- **The inverse up to natural isomorphism.** Renard's "dont l'inverse est
  `M ↦ H(G) ⊗_{H(G,K)} M`" combines the III.1.4 identification of representations with
  nondegenerate `H(G)`-modules and the I.3.2 inverse. Clause 2 states it after applying the
  III.1.4 functor, since the induced module is an `H(G)`-module, not a representation. A
  quasi-inverse is unique only up to natural isomorphism, so a natural isomorphism is the
  right form.
- **Measure.** `H(G)`, `e_K` and `H(G, K)` depend on the choice of the Haar measure, and the
  statement holds for every left Haar measure. Renard's `H(G)` consists of distributions and
  does not depend on a measure; the III.1.3 Remark identifies it with test functions once a
  Haar measure is fixed.

## Expected proof route

The statement is not proved here. This section describes the expected Lean proof; it is
not a proof.

1. **III.1.4.** `Hecke.heckeNondegenerateModuleEquivalence` makes
   `heckeNondegenerateModuleFunctor haar` an equivalence.
2. **Bridge.** For a smooth `V` with module `M`, `P V` holds iff `M` satisfies the published
   `NonUnitalModuleIdempotentGenerated.generatedProperty e` (every vector lies in
   `A · (e · M)`). Indeed `e · M = V^K` by the two `heckeAction_normalizedIndicator` lemmas,
   and the `H(G)`-submodules of `M` are exactly the subrepresentations of `V` (smoothness and
   III.1.4). So the equivalence of step 1 restricts to an equivalence
   `R : P.FullSubcategory ≌ GeneratedModuleCat e` whose functor composed with the inclusion
   is `P.ι ⋙ heckeNondegenerateModuleFunctor haar`.
3. **Subobject closure.** The open VI.9.4 Corollary
   `StandardIwahoriFixedVectorSubquotientGenerationStatement` (same hypotheses on `K`) says
   that every subquotient of a representation generated by its `K`-fixed vectors is again
   generated by them. Transported by step 1 and step 2 (a monomorphism of nondegenerate
   modules is an injective intertwiner, so its source is isomorphic to a subrepresentation),
   it gives `(generatedProperty e).IsClosedUnderSubobjects`.
4. **I.3.2.** The published `NonUnitalModuleCornerSimpleProof.generatedEquivalence hA e hsub`
   is an equivalence `GeneratedModuleCat e ≌ ModuleCat (Idempotent.Corner e)` with functor
   `cornerFunctor e = inclusion e ⋙ NonUnitalModuleCorner.nondegenerateFunctor e` and inverse
   `generatedFunctor hA e`, the lift of `NonUnitalModuleCornerInduction.nondegenerateFunctor
   hA e`.
5. **Assembly.** `E := R.trans (generatedEquivalence hA e hsub)`. Its functor is
   `P.ι ⋙ heckeNondegenerateModuleFunctor haar ⋙ nondegenerateFunctor e` (clause 1). Its
   inverse is `generatedFunctor hA e ⋙ R.inverse`. Composing with `P.ι ⋙
   heckeNondegenerateModuleFunctor haar ≅ R.functor ⋙ inclusion e` and the counit of `R`
   gives clause 2.

The only open input is the VI.9.4 Corollary, whose proof is a separate open task.
