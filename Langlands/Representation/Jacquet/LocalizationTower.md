# Transitivity of character localization, with supplied twists

This module constructs the canonical transitivity equivalence for the published
character-localized quotients of `Langlands.Representation.Jacquet.Localization`.
Localizing at `(U, θ)`, then at the actual copy of `(W, η)` inside `M`, and
restricting to the actual copy of `H`, is identified with localizing once at
`(U ⊔ W, Θ)`. On classes the comparison is `[[v]] ↦ [v]`. The module proves
`H`-equivariance, naturality in every ambient intertwiner, and the smooth
natural isomorphism of functors. It then adds supplied character twists, and
specializes them to the inverse-half twists of supplied positive characters.

All declarations are in `Langlands.Jacquet.Localization`, inside namespace
`TowerDatum` except the three join lemmas of the first section. Author: Worker
1139. The published quotient `Coinvariants`, the quotient map `mk`, the
universal property (`lift`, `lift_mk`, `hom_ext`), the descended `action`
(`action_mk`), the induced maps `map` (`map_mk`) and `smoothFunctor` are used
unchanged. So are `Langlands.twist`, `Langlands.twistIntertwining`,
`SmoothRep.twistFunctor` and `Langlands.Jacquet.inverseHalfCharacter`.

The coefficient ring `R` is an arbitrary commutative ring. Complex
representations are the case `R = ℂ`, where the coefficient universe is `0`.
The group lives in `Type uG`, and the representation carriers in independent
universes `uV`, `uW`, `uN`. Each quotient keeps its full carrier in the
universe of the original module. No finite length, admissibility,
irreducibility, finite dimension, compact quotient, parabolic shape, rank
parameter, semidirect decomposition or trivial-intersection hypothesis
`U ⊓ W = ⊥` (or `M ⊓ U = ⊥`) appears anywhere.

## Join lemmas

For subgroups `U W : Subgroup G`, write `ιU` for
`Subgroup.inclusion le_sup_left : U →* ↥(U ⊔ W)` and `ιW` for
`Subgroup.inclusion le_sup_right : W →* ↥(U ⊔ W)`.

- `sup_eq_top_of_inclusion_mem`: a subgroup `S` of `↥(U ⊔ W)` containing
  `ιU u` for all `u : U` and `ιW w` for all `w : W` is `⊤`. Its image in `G`
  contains `U` and `W`, hence `U ⊔ W`.
- `sup_hom_ext`: two homomorphisms `↥(U ⊔ W) →* N` (any group `N`) that agree
  after composing with `ιU` and with `ιW` are equal. This applies the previous
  lemma to the equalizer `MonoidHom.eqLocus`.
- `covariant_sup`: for a representation `ρ` of `G`, a character
  `Θ : ↥(U ⊔ W) →* Rˣ` and a linear map `F : V →ₗ[R] X`, if
  `F (ρ u v) = Θ (ιU u) • F v` for all `u : U` and
  `F (ρ w v) = Θ (ιW w) • F v` for all `w : W`, then
  `F (ρ x v) = Θ x • F v` for every `x : ↥(U ⊔ W)`. The set of `x` satisfying
  the identity for all `v` is a subgroup, by multiplicativity of `ρ` and `Θ`;
  the first lemma makes it `⊤`.

These lemmas use only that `U ⊔ W` is the subgroup generated by `U` and `W`.
No product decomposition `U ⊔ W = U W` or uniqueness of factorization is used.

## Input data

`TowerDatum R G` is a structure, with constructor `TowerDatum.ofData`, whose
fields are concrete subgroups, characters and compatibility facts:

- `U M W H : Subgroup G`;
- `W_le_M : W ≤ M` and `H_le_M : H ≤ M`;
- `M_le_normalizer : M ≤ Subgroup.normalizer U` and
  `H_le_normalizer : H ≤ Subgroup.normalizer W`;
- characters `θ : U →* Rˣ` and `η : W →* Rˣ`;
- `θ_invariant : IsInvariant U M M_le_normalizer θ` and
  `η_invariant : IsInvariant W H H_le_normalizer η`, the published literal
  conjugation invariance `θ (conjugate U M _ m u) = θ u`, respectively
  `η (conjugate W H _ h w) = η w`;
- `Θ : ↥(U ⊔ W) →* Rˣ` with `Θ_comp_left : Θ.comp ιU = θ` and
  `Θ_comp_right : Θ.comp ιW = η`.

The structure contains no quotient equivalence, transitivity statement or
functor isomorphism. Fix `D : TowerDatum R G` below. The evaluated forms of the
last two fields are `Θ_inclusion_left` and `Θ_inclusion_right`.
`Θ_unique` proves that any character of `U ⊔ W` with the same two restrictions
equals `D.Θ`, by `sup_hom_ext`. So `Θ` is determined by `θ` and `η`. Where it
exists, it is the character `Θ(uw) = θ(u) η(w)` of the source.

Facts proved from the data:

- `H_le_normalizer_sup : D.H ≤ Subgroup.normalizer (D.U ⊔ D.W)`. An element of
  `H` normalizes `U` (as `H ≤ M`) and `W`. Mathlib's
  `Subgroup.inf_normalizer_le_normalizer_sup` then gives the join.
- `Θ_invariant : IsInvariant (D.U ⊔ D.W) D.H D.H_le_normalizer_sup D.Θ`. For
  `h : H`, conjugation by `h` is a homomorphism `c` of `↥(U ⊔ W)`. The
  composites `Θ ∘ c ∘ ιU` and `Θ ∘ c ∘ ιW` equal `θ` and `η`, by `θ_invariant`
  at `⟨h, H_le_M h.2⟩ : M` and `η_invariant` at `h`. Hence `Θ ∘ c = Θ` by
  `sup_hom_ext`.

## Actual subgroup copies inside `M`

The second localization is a localization of a representation of the subtype
group `↥M`. The module uses the following copies with explicit types:

- `innerSubgroup := D.W.subgroupOf D.M : Subgroup ↥D.M`;
- `actingSubgroup := D.H.subgroupOf D.M : Subgroup ↥D.M`;
- `innerCharacter : ↥D.innerSubgroup →* Rˣ` is `D.η` composed with Mathlib's
  canonical isomorphism `Subgroup.subgroupOfEquivOfLe D.W_le_M`. By
  `innerCharacter_apply`, it sends `w` to `D.η ⟨((w : D.M) : G), w.2⟩`.
- `toActing : ↥D.H →* ↥D.actingSubgroup` is the inverse
  `(Subgroup.subgroupOfEquivOfLe D.H_le_M).symm`. By `coe_coe_toActing`, its
  underlying element of `G` is unchanged.
- `acting_le_normalizer` proves that the copy of `H` normalizes the copy of `W`
  in `↥M`, from `H_le_normalizer`.
- `innerCharacter_invariant` proves `IsInvariant` for the copies, from
  `η_invariant`.

No two distinct subtype groups are identified silently: every passage between
`W` and its copy, or `H` and its copy, goes through `subgroupOfEquivOfLe`.

## The algebraic tower equivalence

Let `ρ : Representation R G V`.

- `firstAction ρ := action ρ D.U D.θ D.M D.M_le_normalizer D.θ_invariant` is
  the published first localization with its descended `M` action.
- `IteratedCoinvariants ρ := Coinvariants (D.firstAction ρ) D.innerSubgroup
  D.innerCharacter` is the actual iterated quotient.
- `iteratedMk ρ := mk (D.firstAction ρ) D.innerSubgroup D.innerCharacter ∘ₗ
  mk ρ D.U D.θ` is `v ↦ [[v]]`.
- `iteratedAction ρ : Representation R ↥D.H (D.IteratedCoinvariants ρ)` is the
  published descended action of `actingSubgroup` on the second quotient,
  composed with `toActing`. `iteratedAction_iteratedMk` gives
  `iteratedAction ρ h [[v]] = [[ρ h v]]`.
- `directAction ρ := action ρ (D.U ⊔ D.W) D.Θ D.H D.H_le_normalizer_sup
  D.Θ_invariant` is the direct localization with its descended `H` action.

The maps:

- `firstToDirect ρ : Coinvariants ρ D.U D.θ →ₗ[R] Coinvariants ρ (D.U ⊔ D.W) D.Θ`
  is `lift` of `mk ρ (D.U ⊔ D.W) D.Θ`. It is well defined because
  `Θ (ιU u) = θ u`, and sends `[v]` to `[v]` (`firstToDirect_mk`).
  `firstToDirect_inner` shows it is `innerCharacter`-covariant on the copy of
  `W`, using `Θ (ιW w) = η w`.
- `forward ρ` is `lift` of `firstToDirect ρ` along the second quotient. It
  sends `[[v]]` to `[v]`.
- `iteratedMk_covariant` proves
  `iteratedMk ρ (ρ x v) = Θ x • iteratedMk ρ v` for every
  `x : ↥(D.U ⊔ D.W)`. The `U` case is the first quotient relation; the `W` case
  is the second quotient relation at the copy of `w`. `covariant_sup` extends
  them to the join.
- `backward ρ` is `lift` of `iteratedMk ρ` along the direct quotient, sending
  `[v]` to `[[v]]`.
- `equiv ρ : (D.iteratedAction ρ).Equiv (D.directAction ρ)` is Mathlib's
  equivalence of representations. Its linear equivalence is
  `LinearEquiv.ofLinearMap (forward ρ) (backward ρ)`. Both inverse laws, and
  `H`-equivariance, are proved by `hom_ext` on representatives: once on the
  direct quotient, twice on the iterated quotient.

Representative formulas, all by definition: `equiv_mk_mk` and
`equiv_iteratedMk` give `equiv ρ [[v]] = [v]`, and `equiv_symm_mk` gives
`(equiv ρ).symm [v] = [[v]]`. `equiv_equivariant` states
`equiv ρ (iteratedAction ρ h x) = directAction ρ h (equiv ρ x)` for every
`h : H` and every `x`.

## Naturality

For an ambient intertwiner `f : ρ.IntertwiningMap σ` (with `σ` on a carrier in
an independent universe):

- `actingMap` restricts an intertwiner of representations of the copy of `H`
  along `toActing`; the linear map is unchanged.
- `iteratedMap f` is `actingMap` of the published
  `map D.innerSubgroup D.innerCharacter D.actingSubgroup ...` applied to the
  published `map D.U D.θ D.M ... f`. `iteratedMap_iteratedMk` gives
  `iteratedMap f [[v]] = [[f v]]`.
- `directMap f := map (D.U ⊔ D.W) D.Θ D.H D.H_le_normalizer_sup D.Θ_invariant f`
  is the published direct induced map.
- `equiv_natural f` proves the square
  `(directMap f).comp (equiv ρ) = (equiv σ).comp (iteratedMap f)` of
  intertwining maps, by `hom_ext` on iterated representatives.

## The smooth natural isomorphism

With `[TopologicalSpace G]`, subgroups carry their induced (subtype)
topologies, as in the published `smoothFunctor`.

- `continuous_toActing` proves that the copy transport
  `toActing : ↥D.H →* ↥D.actingSubgroup` is continuous. It is a double subtype
  restriction of the inclusion of `H` into `G`.
- `actingFunctor : SmoothRep.{uR, uG, uV} R ↥D.actingSubgroup ⥤
  SmoothRep.{uR, uG, uV} R ↥D.H` pulls a representation back along
  `toActing`. Smoothness of the output is the published `isSmooth_comp` with
  `continuous_toActing`. Morphisms go to `actingMap`. The identity and
  composition laws hold by definition.
- `iteratedSmoothFunctor := smoothFunctor D.U D.θ D.M ... ⋙
  smoothFunctor D.innerSubgroup D.innerCharacter D.actingSubgroup ... ⋙
  D.actingFunctor`.
- `directSmoothFunctor := smoothFunctor (D.U ⊔ D.W) D.Θ D.H
  D.H_le_normalizer_sup D.Θ_invariant`.
- `smoothIso : D.iteratedSmoothFunctor ≅ D.directSmoothFunctor`, as functors
  `SmoothRep.{uR, uG, uV} R G ⥤ SmoothRep.{uR, uG, uV} R ↥D.H`, is built by
  `NatIso.ofComponents`. The components are `SmoothRep.mkIso (D.equiv _)` and
  naturality is `equiv_natural`. Output smoothness is that of the published
  `smoothFunctor` and of `actingFunctor`.

For `D : TowerDatum ℂ G` this is a natural isomorphism of functors
`SmoothRep.{0, uG, uV} ℂ G ⥤ SmoothRep.{0, uG, uV} ℂ ↥D.H`, with `uG` and `uV`
independent. `smoothIso_hom_app_mk_mk` and `smoothIso_inv_app_mk` give the
component formulas `[[v]] ↦ [v]` and `[v] ↦ [[v]]`.

## Supplied twists

Recall that `Langlands.twist χ ρ` acts by `g ↦ (χ g : R) • ρ g` on the same
module, and that `twistIntertwining` keeps the underlying linear map. Fix
`α : ↥D.M →* Rˣ` with `hα : ∀ w : ↥D.innerSubgroup, α w = 1` (trivial on the
actual copy of `W`), and `β : ↥D.H →* Rˣ`.

- `restrictedProduct α β := α.comp (Subgroup.inclusion D.H_le_M) * β`, the
  character `α|_H * β`. `restrictedProduct_apply` evaluates it as
  `α h * β h`.
- `twistedFirstAction α ρ := twist α (D.firstAction ρ)` twists the `M` action
  on the first quotient by `α`, before the second quotient.
- `TwistedIteratedCoinvariants α ρ` is the second quotient of that twisted
  representation at `(innerSubgroup, innerCharacter)`. `twistedIteratedMk α ρ`
  is `v ↦ [[v]]`.
- `twistedIteratedAction α β ρ := twist β ((action (twistedFirstAction α ρ)
  D.innerSubgroup D.innerCharacter D.actingSubgroup ...).comp D.toActing)`
  twists the final `H` action by `β`. `twistedIteratedAction_twistedIteratedMk`
  gives `twistedIteratedAction α β ρ h [[v]] = β(h) • α(h) • [[ρ h v]]`.

The second quotient really is of the twisted representation. Its relations are
`α(w) • [ρ w v] - η(w) • [v]`, which `hα` reduces to the untwisted relations.
`twistedForward α hα ρ` is `lift` of `firstToDirect ρ` and uses `hα` for
well-definedness. `twistedIteratedMk_covariant` proves `Θ`-covariance of
`twistedIteratedMk` on the join, again using `hα` on the copy of `W`.
`twistedBackward α hα ρ` is the corresponding `lift` along the direct quotient.

`twistedEquivOfEq α hα β γ hγ ρ :
(D.twistedIteratedAction α β ρ).Equiv (twist γ (D.directAction ρ))` takes any
character `γ : ↥D.H →* Rˣ` with `hγ : D.restrictedProduct α β = γ`. The equation
parameter lets a consumer present the product character in another form, as
the positive specialization does. The linear equivalence is
`LinearEquiv.ofLinearMap (twistedForward α hα ρ) (twistedBackward α hα ρ)`, with
both inverse laws by `hom_ext`. Equivariance follows from the action formula
above and `α h * β h = γ h`. `twistedEquiv` is the case
`γ := restrictedProduct α β`, `hγ := rfl`.

Laws:

- `twistedEquivOfEq_mk_mk`: `[[v]] ↦ [v]`; `twistedEquivOfEq_symm_mk`:
  `[v] ↦ [[v]]`.
- `twist_directAction_mk`: the action on the single class is exactly
  `twist γ (D.directAction ρ) h [v] = (α h * β h) • [ρ h v]`, where `α h` means
  `α (Subgroup.inclusion D.H_le_M h)`.
- `twistedEquivOfEq_equivariant`: equivariance for every `h : H`.
- `twistedIteratedMap α β f := twistIntertwining β (actingMap (map
  D.innerSubgroup ... (twistIntertwining α (map D.U D.θ D.M ... f))))`, built
  from the published induced maps.
- `twistedEquivOfEq_natural`: `(twistIntertwining γ (directMap f)).comp
  (twistedEquivOfEq ... ρ) = (twistedEquivOfEq ... σ).comp
  (twistedIteratedMap α β f)` for every ambient intertwiner `f`.

The algebraic statements above use no topology.

### Smooth twisted version

With `[TopologicalSpace G]` and open-kernel hypotheses
`hαo : IsOpen (α.ker : Set ↥D.M)` and `hβo : IsOpen (β.ker : Set ↥D.H)`:

- `twistedIteratedSmoothFunctor α β hαo hβo := smoothFunctor D.U D.θ D.M ... ⋙
  SmoothRep.twistFunctor α hαo ⋙ smoothFunctor D.innerSubgroup
  D.innerCharacter D.actingSubgroup ... ⋙ D.actingFunctor ⋙
  SmoothRep.twistFunctor β hβo`.
- `twistedSmoothIsoOfEq α hα β hαo hβo γ hγ hγo` is a natural isomorphism from
  this functor to `D.directSmoothFunctor ⋙ SmoothRep.twistFunctor γ hγo`, given
  `hγo : IsOpen (γ.ker : Set ↥D.H)`. Its components are `SmoothRep.mkIso` of
  `twistedEquivOfEq`, and naturality is `twistedEquivOfEq_natural`.
  `twistedSmoothIsoOfEq_hom_app_mk_mk` and `twistedSmoothIsoOfEq_inv_app_mk`
  give the component formulas.
- `twistedSmoothIso` is the case `γ := restrictedProduct α β`.

With additionally `[IsTopologicalGroup G]`, `isOpen_ker_restrict_mul` proves
that `χ.comp (Subgroup.inclusion D.H_le_M) * ψ` has open kernel when
`χ : ↥D.M →* N` and `ψ : ↥D.H →* N` (any commutative group `N`) do. The kernel
contains the open subgroup `ker (χ ∘ inclusion) ⊓ ker ψ`, and
`Subgroup.isOpen_mono` applies.

## Supplied positive characters

Let `D : TowerDatum ℂ G`, `δ₁ : ↥D.M →* ℝ≥0ˣ` and `δ₂ : ↥D.H →* ℝ≥0ˣ`.

- `restrictedProduct_inverseHalfCharacter` proves
  `D.restrictedProduct (inverseHalfCharacter δ₁) (inverseHalfCharacter δ₂) =
  inverseHalfCharacter (δ₁.comp (Subgroup.inclusion D.H_le_M) * δ₂)`. At each
  `h`, this is `(√a)⁻¹ (√b)⁻¹ = (√(a b))⁻¹` for nonnegative reals.
- `inverseHalfCharacter_inner`: if `δ₁` is trivial on the actual copy of `W`,
  so is `inverseHalfCharacter δ₁` (published `inverseHalfCharacter_eq_one`).
- `normalizedSmoothIso δ₁ δ₂ hδ₁ hδ₁o hδ₂o`, for `[TopologicalSpace G]
  [IsTopologicalGroup G]`, `hδ₁ : ∀ w : ↥D.innerSubgroup, δ₁ w = 1` and open
  kernels `hδ₁o`, `hδ₂o`, is the natural isomorphism of functors
  `SmoothRep.{0, uG, uV} ℂ G ⥤ SmoothRep.{0, uG, uV} ℂ ↥D.H` from
  `twistedIteratedSmoothFunctor (inverseHalfCharacter δ₁)
  (inverseHalfCharacter δ₂) ...` to `directSmoothFunctor ⋙
  SmoothRep.twistFunctor (inverseHalfCharacter (δ₁.comp (Subgroup.inclusion
  D.H_le_M) * δ₂)) ...`. It is `twistedSmoothIsoOfEq` with the previous
  identity as `hγ`. The open kernels come from the published
  `isOpen_ker_inverseHalfCharacter` and `isOpen_ker_restrict_mul`.

## Modulus boundary

`δ₁` and `δ₂` are arbitrary supplied positive characters. This module does
not identify them with Haar conjugation moduli such as `mod_U` on `M` or
`mod_W` on `H`. It does not prove the source's modulus identity
`mod_{U ⊔ W} = mod_U · mod_W` on `H`, and it establishes no GL determinant
identity. Those identifications remain obligations of the comparison
consumer or of an existing modulus owner, for instance the separate normalizer
Haar modulus task `2e58251c-65b9-4d52-af36-b6c21e2a4e8b`. No unpublished
general normalized semidirect functor definition is used.

Source: Bernstein–Zelevinsky, *Induced representations of reductive p-adic
groups I*, Ann. Sci. ENS (4) 10 (1977), registered
`bernstein-zelevinsky-1977-induced-i`, SHA256
`8424600228ab72c5937e45edceefcae410a2a0b8f5ebf282185691542aa89092`. The
retained original was read at §1.8(b), printed pp. 444–445 (PDF pp. 5–6), and
Proposition 1.9(c), printed p. 445 (PDF p. 6). The project literature index
and the central library list no erratum for this paper.

In §1.8(b), `E(U, θ)` is the span of `π(u)ξ - θ(u)ξ`, and the θ-localization
is `E / E(U, θ)` with `M` acting by `mod_U^{-1/2}(m) · (π(m)ξ + E(U, θ))`
(bottom display of printed p. 444). The positive exponent `mod_U^{1/2}`
occurs instead in the induction of §1.8(a), condition (1); it plays no role
here. Proposition 1.9(c) states, for subgroups `N, V` of `M` and a character
`θ'` of `V`, with `θ°(uv) = θ(u) θ'(v)` on `U° = UV`, that
`r_{V,θ'} ∘ r_{U,θ} = r_{U°,θ°}`. The source refers to [1, chap. I] for the
proof, whose definitions omit the normalizing modulus factors, and notes that
the compatibility of these factors (for the localizations, the `-1/2` powers)
must be checked.

This unit is an internal algebraic quotient construction along that route, not
a formalization of the source's normalized functor. The published
`Coinvariants` relations are exactly the source's `E(U, θ)`. The published
`action` is unnormalized. In the source's notation `(U, θ, M, V, θ', N)`
become `(U, θ, M, W, η, H)` here, and the source's `U° = UV` becomes
`U ⊔ W`. The source's standing hypotheses `M ∩ U = {e}` and closedness of `MU`
are not needed for the quotient argument and are not assumed. The source's
localization normalizations, `mod_U^{-1/2}` on `M` and `mod_V^{-1/2}` on `N`
(here `mod_W^{-1/2}` on `H`), are replaced by arbitrary supplied characters
`α` and `β`, or by the inverse-half twists `inverseHalfCharacter δ₁` and
`inverseHalfCharacter δ₂` of supplied positive characters, whose values are
`(√δ₁)⁻¹` and `(√δ₂)⁻¹`. Thus the negative exponent of §1.8(b) is matched;
identifying `δ₁`, `δ₂` with the actual moduli remains outside this module (see
the modulus boundary above).
The smooth case with locally constant (open-kernel) characters is a
specialization of these constructions. This module is not a formal proof of the
source's Haar-modulus identification, nor of any Jacquet–derivative
comparison.
