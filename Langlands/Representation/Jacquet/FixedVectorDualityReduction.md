# Fixed-vector generation and smooth duality from a splitting

Author: Worker 146. This is an internal reduction for Renard VI.10.6,
Proposition (iii), printed p.214 / PDF p.220 of the French version of
9 July 2026. It does not prove the splitting premise or the exact global
`FixedVectorCategoryStatements` target.

For any topological group, `fixedVectorSplitting K` asserts that every smooth
complex representation is a sum of a subrepresentation generated by its
`K`-fixed vectors and a subrepresentation with zero `K`-fixed submodule.
Disjointness is not required. The group and representation universes are
independent.

The proof first uses compact-open fixed duality: a representation without
`K`-fixed vectors has a smooth dual without such vectors. Given an
intertwiner from such a representation `B` to the smooth dual of a
fixed-vector-generated representation `A`, transpose it by evaluation into
the smooth bidual. The resulting map from `A` to the smooth dual of `B`
vanishes on every fixed vector, and therefore vanishes. Evaluating this
identity proves that the original intertwiner is zero. This argument also
allows independent universes for `A` and `B` and uses no admissibility or
finite-dimensionality assumption.

Apply the splitting premise to the smooth dual of `A`. The inclusion of
its summand without fixed vectors is zero by the preceding argument.
The other summand is consequently the whole dual, so the dual is generated
by fixed vectors. For the reverse implication, apply this result again
and use the injective smooth bidual evaluation, followed by the separately
assumed closure under subobjects. Cofinality of compact open subgroups
supplies the bidual injection.

The source uses the Bernstein decomposition to supply the splitting and
VI.9.4 to supply subobject closure. Both remain explicit inputs here.
The implemented transposition proof replaces the source's finite expansion
and averaging computation with published compact-open fixed duality and
evaluation. All declarations are internal derived content.
