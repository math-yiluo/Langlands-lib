# Cyclic selection and full-tail fixed vectors

This internal construction and its defining laws assemble commuting cyclic
refinements. They are preparatory algebra for the Flath existence proof. The
conditional theorem explicitly assumes local extraction and a nonzero vector
fixed by a reference tail; it does not prove either extraction, the topological
existence of such a tail, or the published Flath selection or factorization
statement. No exact external theorem is claimed for this API.

## Finite selection

`GoodCyclic` is the conjunction of admissibility and irreducibility for the
coordinate cyclic representation generated by a given vector. Its carriers are
subspaces of the ambient complex vector space, so the index, group and carrier
universes remain independent.

`goodCyclic_of_commuting_refinement` preserves this property under a nonzero
refinement in a commuting coordinate's orbit span. The published
`Cyclic.exists_equiv_of_mem_commuting_orbitSpan` supplies an equivalence,
`isAdmissible_of_equiv` transfers admissibility, and the simple-module criterion
transfers irreducibility along the bijective module map.

`finite_selection` performs finitely many such refinements inside any supplied
linear subspace invariant under the refining coordinates. Induction on the
finite exceptional set chooses one refinement, keeps it in the invariant
subspace by orbit-span minimality, and uses commuting transport to retain the
previously established factors. The empty finite set is included.

## Full reference tails

For the actual cofinite restricted product, `tailFixedSubmodule H π E` consists
of vectors fixed by every group element that equals the identity on `E` and
belongs to the reference subgroup at every coordinate outside `E`. These group
elements may have infinitely many nonidentity coordinates. This condition is
linear; it is not replaced by individual coordinate fixedness. The theorem
`mem_tailFixedSubmodule` records exactly this membership condition.

`tailFixedSubmodule_mono` says that enlarging the exceptional set enlarges the
fixed subspace, since the corresponding tail gets smaller. The proof sets the
new exceptional coordinates to the identity, which belongs to each reference
subgroup. `single_mul_eq_mul_single` checks commutation coordinatewise when a
group element is trivial at the singleton coordinate. It gives both the
commutation of distinct singleton actions and the invariance of the full-tail
fixed subspace under each exceptional-coordinate action.

`mem_fixedVectors_of_mem_tailFixedSubmodule` embeds a reference-subgroup element
at a coordinate outside the exceptional set. The resulting restricted-product
element lies in the full tail, so full-tail invariance implies the corresponding
local fixedness. The converse is neither used nor asserted. None of these
full-tail algebraic lemmas requires compactness or openness of the reference
subgroups or a topology on the ambient carrier.

## Conditional assembly

`select_of_extraction` takes an initial exceptional set `E₀` and a nonzero vector
fixed by its full reference tail. It assumes prescribed-vector cyclic refinement
at every coordinate. It also assumes that, at almost every coordinate, every
nonzero reference-subgroup-fixed vector generates an admissible irreducible
cyclic representation whose reference-subgroup fixed space has finrank one.
These hypotheses are explicit; no factorization theorem is assumed implicitly.

The proof enlarges `E₀` by the finite exceptional set of the second assumption,
uses `finite_selection` inside the enlarged full-tail fixed submodule, and then
applies the spherical extraction assumption to the resulting vector at every
remaining coordinate. It returns an exceptional set containing `E₀`, a common
nonzero vector fixed by its entire tail, good cyclic factors at all coordinates,
and local fixedness with finrank one outside that set. Admissibility makes each
stated fixed space finite dimensional whenever the reference subgroup is open;
this additional openness is not required for the conditional algebraic lemma.

The proofs derive directly from the existing cyclic-span, commuting-transport,
admissibility-equivalence and restricted-product APIs, together with finite-set
induction and the definition of the cofinite filter. The exact selection target
still needs the independently supplied topology and two-factor extraction
prerequisites, and tensor evaluation and bijectivity remain further obligations.
