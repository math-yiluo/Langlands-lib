# Simultaneous cyclic local factors for a restricted-product representation

`Langlands.FlathCyclicVectorSelectionStatement` is a derived prerequisite for
the exact restricted-product existence theorem. It has the same hypotheses as
`FlathRestrictedProductExistenceStatement`: an arbitrary family of locally
profinite groups with compact open reference subgroups, regular left Haar
measures compatible with their Borel structures, an admissible irreducible
complex representation of their actual cofinite restricted product, and
almost-everywhere commutativity of the actual spherical convolution algebras.
The index, group and representation-carrier universes remain independent.

The conclusion selects a finite exceptional set E and one nonzero vector v.
Every coordinate restriction, along Mathlib's `mulSingleMonoidHom`, has an
admissible irreducible cyclic subrepresentation generated by this same v.
All these cyclic carriers are subspaces of the original V and hence remain
in its universe. Off E, v is fixed by the reference subgroup and the
reference-subgroup fixed space in its cyclic representation has dimension one.

In addition, v is fixed by the entire reference tail: every actual restricted
product element which equals one on E and belongs to H i off E fixes v.
This is stronger than fixedness under individual coordinates off E; it
includes elements with infinitely many nonidentity reference components.
The statement expresses the condition directly, without assuming that the tail
subgroup is open. It does not assert a tensor map or an equivalence with a
tensor product. This pair states the selection result and contains no proof.

The intended proof starts with a nonzero smooth vector. A product-neighborhood
argument gives a finite set outside which the full reference tail fixes it.
Enlarge this set to include the finitely many places where spherical
commutativity may fail. At each exceptional coordinate use the first-factor
cyclic refinement result after splitting off that coordinate. Refinement within
that coordinate's orbit span preserves the tail-fixed condition because the
actions commute. It also preserves all previously established local factors:
a nonzero refinement under a commuting action gives an equivalence of their
cyclic representations, so admissibility and irreducibility transfer. Finite
induction therefore yields a common nonzero vector with all exceptional
coordinates treated. At every remaining coordinate, the spherical cyclic
extraction result applies directly to this same tail-fixed vector and supplies
admissibility, irreducibility and the fixed line. These explanations identify
the mathematical route and its obligations, not formal proof evidence.
