# Generation and length from compact-open detection

Worker 1210, in coordination with Worker 189.

`Langlands.CompactOpenDetectionConsequencesStatement.{uG,uV}` is an internal
reduction for an arbitrary topological group and a smooth complex
representation `A`. The group and representation-carrier universes are
independent. A supplied open subgroup `K` is compact. The representation
theoretic input says that every irreducible actual smooth subquotient of `A`
has a nonzero `K`-fixed vector.

The conclusions are:

1. Every nonzero actual smooth subquotient of `A` has a nonzero `K`-fixed
   vector.
2. Every actual smooth subquotient `D` is generated by its `K`-fixed vectors:
   every invariant submodule containing those vectors is all of `D`.
3. If `A` has a finite-dimensional `K`-fixed space, its actual composition
   length is at most that dimension, and its actual group-algebra module
   has finite length.

All subquotients use the existing `SmoothRep.IsSubquotient`, with actual
invariant submodules, actual module quotients and equivariant isomorphisms.
The carrier universe is the same for `A` and the quantified subquotients.
No finite-length premise or global admissibility premise is present. The
zero representation is included.

The provenance is internal: combine the published existence of an
irreducible actual subquotient of every nonzero smooth representation,
actual subquotient transitivity, compact-open fixed-vector surjectivity,
and the published fixed-vector strict-inclusion length criterion.
For generation, take the actual quotient of `D` by an arbitrary invariant
submodule containing its fixed vectors. Exactness makes all fixed vectors
in that quotient zero, whereas detection makes a nonzero quotient have a
nonzero fixed vector. These implications justify the stated reduction.

This abstracts the detection-to-generation and chain arguments in Renard
VI.6.3, French 9 July 2026 edition, printed pages 178–179 / PDF184–185;
it does not assert the full source corollary without its structural input.
In particular it neither constructs a compact open subgroup on reductive
rational points nor proves the irreducible detection premise there. The
unrestricted Howe implication, its converse, the source's other intermediate
assertions, and the stronger bound for the source's specially chosen
subgroup remain separate obligations.
