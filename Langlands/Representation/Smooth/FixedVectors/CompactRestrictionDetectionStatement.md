# Fixed-vector detection from a finite compact restriction

Worker 189. This internal proposition isolates the fixed-vector detection
argument needed for the supercuspidal proposition in Renard VI.6.3,
printed pages 178–179 / PDF184–185.

`Langlands.CompactRestrictionFixedVectorDetectionStatement.{uG,uV,uI}`
uses a topological group `G`, a normal subgroup `N` with its subtype
topology, and smooth complex representations `A` and `D` of `G` in the
same carrier universe. The group, carrier, and finite-family index
universes are independent. The subgroup `N` is Hausdorff, locally compact,
and totally disconnected, with a Borel measurable space and a supplied
Haar measure.

The fixing subgroup `K` is contained in `N`. The ambient representation
`A` is generated by `fixedVectors A.representation K`, expressed by the
condition that every subrepresentation containing those vectors is top.
The restriction of `D` to `N` has a supplied nonempty finite family of
actual irreducible subrepresentations whose underlying submodules span the
whole restriction. Every member has compact coefficients against all
smooth linear functionals. The family need not consist of distinct types
or form a direct sum.

If `D` is an actual smooth subquotient of `A`, the conclusion supplies
`v : D` with `v ≠ 0` fixed by `K`. No irreducibility hypothesis is imposed
on `D` as a representation of `G`. The nonempty irreducible family already
forces it to be nonzero.

This is a derived generalization of the algebraic detection step in that
proposition. The implementation can use the published compact-coefficient
class projectors and their invariant selected annihilator, instead of
assuming an inertial decomposition. Compact-coefficient complementarity
is available for the stated topology and Haar measure; no block-splitting
conclusion is added as a premise. Compactness and openness of `K`,
admissibility, and a special compact subgroup are unnecessary once the
listed inputs are supplied.

The proposition does not establish this finite compact-restriction input
for actual reductive rational points, identify `N` with the source's
`⁰G`, prove the source's assertion for every supercuspidal subquotient, or
prove Howe's finite-length conclusion. Those remain separate obligations.
