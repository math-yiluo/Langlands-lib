# Exact Grothendieck maps through natural isomorphisms

This internal helper by Worker 585 transfers the two preservation properties
required by the existing exact finite-length Grothendieck functor. Coefficient
rings are arbitrary commutative rings. The source and target coefficient,
group, and representation carrier universes remain independent. Each group
has an arbitrary topology; no local compactness, admissibility, or
finite-dimensionality hypothesis occurs.

`hasFiniteLength_of_iso` sends finite group-algebra length along the surjective
module map underlying the given smooth isomorphism. It uses Mathlib's
`Representation.IntertwiningMap.equivLinearMapAsModule` and
`IsFiniteLength.of_surjective` directly. It imports no classification module.

`isShortExact_of_iso` uses three actual smooth isomorphisms and two commuting
squares. Injectivity transfers by the first two bijections, surjectivity by the
last two, and equality of range and kernel by the middle bijection and the two
squares. The proof explicitly transports vectors and their preimages. All six
objects in this helper lie in the same smooth category.

`PreservesFiniteLength.of_iso` and
`PreservesFiniteLengthShortExact.of_iso` apply these results to the components
and naturality squares of a natural isomorphism of functors. Their assumptions
are exactly the respective preservation property of the source functor; neither
result assumes the preservation property it constructs for the target.

`FiniteLengthGrothendieckGroup.mapOfIso` is the existing exact-K0 `map` for the
target functor with the transported preservation proofs. The group is unchanged:
it is generated by actual finite-length smooth isomorphism classes modulo all
actual short-exact-sequence relations. `mapOfIso_eq` applies the existing
`map_eq_of_iso` to identify this map with that of the source functor on the
whole exact group.

The provenance is internal algebraic transport through actual isomorphisms,
not a new representation-theoretic preservation theorem. In the GL Aubert
application the natural isomorphisms compare actual general and block
functors, while the block preservation theorems remain separate inputs.
