# Admissibility of extensions and of finitely filtered representations

Author: Worker 285. The design was coordinated with Worker 240 on task
`18476e24-f842-458b-9a71-0fb03f270b2c`.

This module proves three internal lemmas in namespace `Langlands`. They hold
for representations of a group `G` with a topology. The coefficients form a
commutative ring `R`. The carriers live in independent universes. Neither
continuity of the group operations nor compactness is needed. No carrier is
assumed finite-dimensional. Admissibility is the Library predicate
`IsAdmissible`. It means smoothness together with finite generation of the
fixed vectors under **every** open subgroup.

## Statements

- `isAdmissible_of_extension`. Assume `R` is Noetherian. Let `i` intertwine
  `ρ` with `σ`, and let `q` intertwine `σ` with `τ`. Assume `i` is injective
  and every vector killed by `q` lies in the image of `i` (hypothesis
  `hexact`, the one-way inclusion `ker q ⊆ range i`). Assume `σ` is smooth
  and `ρ` and `τ` are admissible. Then `σ` is admissible. Neither `q ∘ i = 0`
  nor surjectivity of `q` is assumed. So `0 → ρ → σ → τ` need not be exact
  at `σ`. For example, take the trivial one-dimensional representation for
  all three and `i = q = id`. All hypotheses hold, but `ker q = 0` while
  `range i` is the whole line. When in addition `q ∘ i = 0`, the hypotheses
  say exactly that `0 → ρ → σ → τ` is exact at `ρ` and at `σ`. That is how
  the lemma is used below.
- `isSmooth_subrepresentation`. Every subrepresentation of a smooth
  representation is smooth.
- `isAdmissible_of_objectRepFiltration`. Assume `R` is Noetherian. Let `A`
  be a `Rep R G` with smooth action. Let `s` be a finite filtration of `A`
  by subrepresentations, of the Library type `ObjectRepFiltration A n`. If
  every graded representation `s.gradedRepresentation i` is admissible, then
  `A` is admissible.

## Implemented proofs

`isAdmissible_of_extension`. Smoothness of `σ` is a hypothesis. Fix an open
subgroup `K`. Let `qK` and `iK` be the restrictions of `q` and `i` to
`K`-fixed vectors, given by the Library construction `fixedVectorsMap`.

1. The kernel of `qK` lies in the range of `iK`. Let `v` be `K`-fixed with
   `q v = 0`. The hypothesis `hexact` gives `u` with `i u = v`. For `k` in `K`,
   equivariance gives `i (ρ k u) = σ k (i u) = σ k v = v = i u`.
   Injectivity of `i` gives `ρ k u = u`, so `u` is `K`-fixed and `iK u = v`.
2. Mathlib's `Submodule.fg_of_fg_map_of_fg_inf_ker` is applied to `qK` and
   the top submodule. It needs two finiteness facts.
   - The image of the top submodule lies in the `K`-fixed vectors of `τ`.
     That module is finitely generated by admissibility of `τ`. Over a
     Noetherian ring it is therefore Noetherian, so every submodule is
     finitely generated.
   - The kernel of `qK` equals the image under `iK` of its preimage, by
     `Submodule.map_comap_eq_of_le` and step 1. The preimage is a submodule
     of the `K`-fixed vectors of `ρ`. These are finitely generated and hence
     Noetherian. So the preimage and its image are finitely generated.

   Hence the `K`-fixed vectors of `σ` form a finitely generated module.

The argument uses only injectivity of `i` (in step 1) and the inclusion
`ker q ⊆ range i`, never `q ∘ i = 0`. Over a field this is the inequality
`dim σ^K ≤ dim ρ^K + dim τ^K`, which holds under that inclusion alone. In
the special case `q ∘ i = 0` the maps `0 → ρ^K → σ^K → τ^K` form an exact
sequence, and this is its familiar dimension bound. The argument needs no
averaging over `K` and no surjectivity of fixed vectors. So it applies to
every open subgroup, compact or not.

`isSmooth_subrepresentation`. An open subgroup fixing a vector in the
ambient representation fixes it in the subrepresentation, by subtype
extensionality.

`isAdmissible_of_objectRepFiltration`. Write `A_k` for
`s.subrepresentation k`, for `k = 0, …, n`. Induction on `k` shows that
each `A_k`, as a representation, is admissible.

- `k = 0`. The Library lemma `subrepresentation_zero` makes `A_0` the zero
  subrepresentation. Its fixed vectors under any subgroup form a
  subsingleton module, hence a finite one. It is smooth by
  `isSmooth_subrepresentation`.
- `k → k + 1`. The inclusion of `A_k` into `A_{k+1}` comes from
  `monotone_subrepresentation`. It is an injective intertwiner. The quotient
  map is `s.gradedProjection k`. Its source is `A_{k+1}` and its target is
  the graded representation of index `k`. It is the quotient map by the
  preimage of `A_k`, so its kernel lies in the image of the inclusion. Only
  this inclusion is checked in the proof. In fact the kernel equals the
  image, and the map is surjective (Library `gradedProjection_surjective`,
  not needed here). So each step is a genuine short exact sequence.
  `isAdmissible_of_extension` applies with the induction hypothesis and the
  hypothesis on the graded piece. Smoothness of `A_{k+1}` comes from
  `isSmooth_subrepresentation`.

Finally `subrepresentation_last` makes `A_n` the top subrepresentation. The
corestriction of the identity of `A` into `A_n` is an injective
intertwiner. The published `isAdmissible_of_injective` therefore transfers
admissibility from `A_n` to `A`.

## Provenance

These are internal lemmas for the proof of Bernstein–Zelevinsky 1977,
*Induced representations of reductive p-adic groups I*, Theorem 2.9(a),
implication (i)⇒(ii) (printed p. 448). The proof of that implication in
§2.15(2) (printed p. 449) applies 2.4(b) to the Jacquet module of an induced
cuspidal representation. Theorem 2.4(b) needs that Jacquet module to be
admissible. BZ77 obtains this from 2.3(e), Jacquet's theorem that the
Jacquet functor preserves admissibility. The formal proof instead uses the
filtration of the geometric lemma 2.12, whose graded pieces are Weyl
transports of an admissible representation. These lemmas show that such a
filtered representation is admissible. The extension argument is an
elementary fixed-vector finiteness argument. It generalizes the familiar
left exactness of fixed vectors for short exact sequences: it uses only
injectivity of `i` and the one-way inclusion `ker q ⊆ range i`, together
with Noetherianity of `R`. The filtration lemma applies it only to the
genuine short exact sequences `0 → A_k → A_{k+1} → gr_k → 0`. The argument
is not taken from a cited source, and no literature theorem is claimed
beyond it.
