# Strict rank-one extension Laurent-span comparison

Author: Worker 719.

This module defines the closed proposition
`Langlands.RankinSelberg.RankOneExtensionSpanComparisonStatement.{uF,uV,uW}`.
It states the integral comparison at the core of JPSS Lemma (5.5), in that
lemma's original strict rank range, as an inclusion of Laurent spans of
actual rational zeta values. It neither proves nor assumes the inclusion,
and it is not a divisibility or L-factor statement.

## The source comparison

For `W ∈ W(π; ψ)` and `f` in the space `U` of the induced representation,
the proof sets `W_f(g) = λ(σ(g) f)`, with `λ` the limit formula (1) on
printed 424, and rewrites the `j = 0` integral `Ψ(s, W, W_f; 0)` over
`N_t \ G_t` as the integral (4) over `N_{t₁,t₂} \ G_t`. Given
`W₁ ∈ W(σ₁; ψ)`, printed 424–425 constructs `φ ∈ C_c^∞(Ū_Q)` with
`∫ φ = 1` (5) and a compactly supported section `f ∈ U` with
`f(u; h) = W₁(h) φ(u)` (6), using `F = β ⊗ φ ⊗ W₁` supported in `QŪ_Q`;
the support of `φ` is shrunk so that (7) holds. In the coordinates
`g = diag(m, a) [[1, 0], [x, 1]] [[1, y], [0, 1]]` of printed 426 the integral
becomes (8), with weights `|det m|^(s-(r-t₁)/2)` and
`|a|^(s-(r-1)/2) σ₂(a)`. For any `W₂ ∈ W(π; ψ)` and `ε > 0`, a `W` satisfying
the scalar cutoff (9) — equal to `W₂ σ₂⁻¹(a)` on `|a - 1| < ε` and zero
otherwise — gives

`Ψ(s, W, W_f; 0) = Ψ(s, W₂, W₁; 1)`,

so every block-one integral of `π × σ₁` is a block-zero integral of `π × σ`.
The source then concludes that `L(s, π × σ₁)` lies in the ideal generated by
`L(s, π × σ)`. The cutoff `W` is produced on printed 426–427 by Fourier
smearing: `W(g) = ∫ φ₁(u) W₂(g n(u)) du`, where `n(u)` is the unipotent with
scalar entry `u` at position `(t₁+1, t₁+2)` as displayed, and `φ̂₁ = σ₂⁻¹` on
`|a - 1| < ε`, zero elsewhere.

This module states exactly the span-level content of that identity:
**the Laurent span of the block-one values of `π × σ₁` is contained in the
Laurent span of the block-zero values of `π × σ`.** It does not state the
L-factor relation of the lemma.

## Quantifiers and hypotheses

The proposition quantifies over every prime `p`, `F : PadicField.{uF} p`,
unitary additive character `ψ` for `F.canonicalTopologicalData.topology`,
and supplied `ν : LocalFactor.PadicAbelian.SelfDualHaar F.canonicalTopologicalData ψ`.
Existence of the self-dual measure is not asserted. It quantifies over
`r t : ℕ`, independent carriers `V : Type uV` and `W : Type uW` (complex
modules), actual representations `π` of `GL_(r+1)(F)` on `V` and `σ₁` of
`GL_(t+1)(F)` on `W`, and an arbitrary `χ : Quasicharacter F.Carrier` for the
canonical topology (no unitarity or ramification restriction).

The hypotheses are exactly:

1. `hrt : t + 3 ≤ r + 1`;
2. `IsOfWhittakerType r (standardCharacter ψ r) π`;
3. `IsOfWhittakerType t (standardCharacter ψ t) σ₁`.

The Whittaker-type predicates are the Library's full ones; they allow
reducible representations and assume no irreducibility. There is no
Whittaker-type hypothesis on the induced representation, no polynomial or
L-factor existence premise, and no analytic comparison premise.

### Index translation and strict range

The Library indexes ranks by predecessors. With JPSS rank `R` for `π` and
`t₁` for `σ₁`: `R = r + 1`, `t₁ = t + 1`, and the induced rank
`t₁ + t₂ = t₁ + 1` is `(1 + t) + 1`, of predecessor `1 + t`. The JPSS
hypothesis `R ≥ (t₁ + 1) + 1` is exactly `r + 1 ≥ t + 3`, i.e. `hrt`.

Both value sets use the same trailing index `k = r - t - 2`. The two rank
equations, introduced by `let` and proved by `omega` from `hrt`, are

- `hrBase : r = t + (1 + (r - t - 2) + 1)` (block `j = 1`);
- `hrInd : r = (1 + t) + (0 + (r - t - 2) + 1)` (block `j = 0`).

They match the published `unequalRankZetaValuesAt` rank equation
`r = n + (j + k + 1)`. The common identity tail has rank `k + 1 = r - t - 1`,
which is JPSS's `R - t₁ - 1 = R - (t₁ + 1)`, the size of `1_{r-t}` in source
notation on printed 424 and 426. Without `hrt` the truncated subtraction
would not make these equations true (for `r = t + 1` the right-hand sides
are `r + 1`), so natural-number subtraction does not silently extend the
range. The equal-rank endpoint `r + 1 = t + 2` (JPSS `R = t₁ + 1`), where
the induced side would need an equal-rank integral with a Schwartz variable,
is excluded; it belongs to Worker 465's separate task
`211d2363-4999-41ed-af96-2c277a4505a7`.

## Lower induction and carriers

The rank-one representation is
`χlift := (ULift.moduleEquiv.symm.conjRingEquiv.toMonoidHom).comp
  (rankOneCharacter (χ : F.Carrierˣ →* ℂˣ))`
on `ULift.{uW} ℂ`, exactly as in the published
`RankOneExtensionLDivisibilityStatement`. It acts on `ULift.up z` by
`ULift.up (χ(det g) z)`. Only this rank-one carrier is lifted, so it lives in
the universe of `W`; `V` and `W` keep their own universes.

The induced representation is the published actual
`rankOneExtensionLowerInduced F t χlift σ₁` from
`Langlands.Representation.RankinSelberg.RankOneExtensionTransport`. It is
the canonical normalized **opposite (lower)** parabolic induction on the
reversal of the upper shape `Shahidi.twoBlocks 1 (t+1)`; its lower ordered
blocks have sizes `(t+1, 1)` and factors `(σ₁, χlift)`, matching JPSS's
`Ind(G_t, Q; σ₁, σ₂)` with `σ₂ = χ`. Its rank `1 + (t + 1)` is definitionally
`(1 + t) + 1`, so it is used without casts. No carrier is reconstructed, no
abstract representation is substituted, and the two upper block orders are
not identified. The lower-to-upper transport is
`rankOneExtensionLowerToUpperEquiv`; it is not used here.

## Measures and value sets

After the rank equations the statement installs the canonical topology,
valuation, local-field instance, and Borel measurable structures on
`F.Carrier`, on `GL_(t+1)`, `upperUnipotent (t+1)`, `LeftUnipotentQuotient t`,
and on the corresponding objects at rank `(1 + t) + 1`. It universally
quantifies the right-invariance instances of `unipotentHaar F.Carrier t` and
`unipotentHaar F.Carrier (1 + t)`, then two measures:

- `μbase` on `LeftUnipotentQuotient t F.Carrier` (`N_{t+1} \ GL_{t+1}`);
- `μind` on `LeftUnipotentQuotient (1 + t) F.Carrier` (`N_{t+2} \ GL_{t+2}`).

Each must satisfy `QuotientIntegration.IsCompatible` with the
`generalLinearHaar` and `unipotentHaar` of its own rank. The quotient groups
differ, and the two measures are independent inputs.

The conclusion is

```text
zetaSpan (unequalRankZetaValuesAt (r := r) (n := t) 1 (r-t-2) hrBase
  q ψ abv μbase (rectangularMeasure ν.val 1 (t+1)) π σ₁)
≤
zetaSpan (unequalRankZetaValuesAt (r := r) (n := 1+t) 0 (r-t-2) hrInd
  q ψ abv μind (rectangularMeasure ν.val 0 ((1+t)+1)) π
  (rankOneExtensionLowerInduced F t χlift σ₁))
```

with `q = F.canonicalTopologicalData.residueCardinality` and
`abv = normalizedAbsoluteValue F.canonicalTopologicalData`. The order is
`≤` of submodules of `RatFunc ℂ` over `LaurentPolynomial ℂ`, with Laurent
variable acting as `X = q^(-s)`. The base side uses the one-row rectangular
measure, the product of the supplied `ν` over a `1 × (t+1)` matrix (the
variable `x` of `Ψ(s, W₂, W₁; 1)`); the induced side uses the zero-row
measure, a unit point mass. The supplied additive Haar measure is unchanged.

Each value is the rational continuation of an actual unequal-rank integral
`∫∫ W([g,0,0; x,1_j,0; 0,0,1_(k+1)]) W'(g) |det g|^(s - (j+k+1)/2)` over the
quotient and the rectangular variable `x`, with `W` a coefficient in `whittakerSet ψ r π`
and `W'` in `inverseWhittakerSet` of the smaller representation, and with
convergence on some right half-plane required. The exponents are
`s - (r-t)/2` (base) and `s - (r-t-1)/2` (induced), which are JPSS's
`s - (R-t₁)/2` and `s - (R-t)/2`. The Library pairs `ψ` with `π` and the
inverse character with the smaller member on both sides; the source's
printed 424–426 notation writes the small coefficient spaces as `W(σ; ψ)`
and `W(σ₁; ψ)`. Both value sets range over coefficients of every
appropriate Whittaker functional, so on the induced side the proof must
exhibit `λ` as an actual inverse-character functional on the Library carrier.

## Derived provenance

The statement is an internal derived formulation, not a verbatim statement
of JPSS:

- **Spans instead of L-factor ideals.** The source compares ideals generated
  by L-factors; here only the Laurent spans of the two actual value sets are
  compared. Relating the `j = 1` span to the certified factor of `π × σ₁`,
  and the `j = 0` span to that of the induced representation, belongs to the
  successor chain. The published
  `LaurentDivisibilityProof.normalizedGeneratorSpanDivisibility` converts an
  inclusion of normalized-generator spans into `Pbase ∣ Pind`; it does not
  prove this analytic inclusion.
- **Predecessor-rank indexing** and explicit rank equations, as above.
- **ULift tensor carrier** for the rank-one factor only.
- **Rational continuations.** Values are rational functions obtained by
  `HasRationalContinuation` at the canonical `q`, through
  `unequalRankZetaValuesAt`, not formal Laurent series of integrals.
- **Compatible quotient measures.** The source uses unnamed Haar measures and
  the decomposition (4) over `N_{t₁,t₂} \ G_t`. Here `μbase` and `μind` are
  arbitrary compatible measures on different quotients, with the same `ν`.
  The coordinate change on printed 426, the normalization (5) of `φ`, the
  modulus factors, and the relation of `μind` with `μbase`, `ν`, and a
  measure on `F^×` produce a positive constant. The successor proof must
  track it and remove it honestly by invariance of a Laurent span under a
  nonzero complex scalar. **No exact equality of individual integrals is
  asserted here**; only the span inclusion is.

## Remaining obligations and dependencies

The proof must construct the compact section and the functional `λ` on the
actual induced carrier, choose supports, establish the scalar cutoff by
Fourier smearing, prove absolute convergence on a common half-plane and the
integral identity with the actual measures, and transfer it to rational
continuations. Concrete data definitions are split into separate tasks.

The dependency direction is Lemma (5.5) → Section 7 → Section 9.3. The
module imports only `Langlands.Representation.RankinSelberg.RankOneExtensionTransport`;
its import closure contains no two-block or full L-multiplicativity module
and no Section 9.3 tail statement or proof (in particular not
`InducedTailSpanStatement`, whose measure-binder style is mirrored).
The published parent `RankOneExtensionLDivisibilityStatement` and its
imports are unchanged.
