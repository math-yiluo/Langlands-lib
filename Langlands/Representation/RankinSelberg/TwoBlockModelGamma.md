interface, and `twoBlockGammaRankOne_of_generic_matrix` proves it. It assembles the rank-one gamma base under the explicit
`GenericMatrixFunctionalEquationStatement.{uF,uF}` premise. The generic matrix functional-equation premise remains explicit; this
helper does not supply the closed analytic base or prove that premise.

choose the least nonzero generic subrepresentations of their actual coefficient
models. Apply the supplied generic matrix-data assertion to these irreducible
socles. The smooth Schur theorem supplies their scalar central quasicharacters.
The published two-block matrix theorem then gives full product data on the
actual normalized upper induction of these socles.

Independently, full normalized Whittaker type supplies a nonzero functional
on induction of the original blocks and a generic socle of its coefficient

subquotient of induction of the block socles. The ambient matrix comparison
therefore determines its gamma expression. Complete gamma existence for the
original reducible representations follows independently from the published
JPSS analytic and functional-equation construction-input proof, so the
submodel comparison identifies the same expression with the original gamma
certificate. No original carrier is identified with its coefficient image.

The rational product identity combines both block polynomials, both dual
polynomials, epsilon units and exponents as required by the matrix theorem.
An equal-rank certificate exchange includes block rank one. Finally the
published trivial-test reduction transports the complete symmetric
certificates through the actual rank-one character and determinant-twist
equivalences. Independent field, block-carrier and test-carrier universes
remain those of the target.

This is an internal composition of the imported published results. Its
mathematical setting is JPSS (1983), Section 5.4, printed page 423. The
implemented route uses the actual upper induction and the published upper
socle occurrence theorem; it makes no inference about induction equivalence
from product commutativity and invokes no later multiplicativity theorem.
