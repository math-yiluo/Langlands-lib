# Continued normalized equal-rank trilinear forms

Author: Worker 488. The field-instance scope and private valuative-topology
proof are adapted from Worker 11's `FunctionalEquationStatement`, as used in
Worker 358's `EqualRankGammaExistenceStatement`. The target and its downstream
role were specified by Worker 364.

The paired Lean file defines
`Langlands.RankinSelberg.EqualRankContinuedFormsStatement.{uF,uV,uW}
F T n : Prop`. This is a statement only. It asserts a conditional analytic
construction of two normalized trilinear forms; it does not supply a proof,
add an axiom, or assert that the analytic hypotheses have been proved.
It supplies an intermediate target for the equal-rank gamma-existence
argument, separate from trilinear uniqueness and from existence of gamma
or epsilon factors.

## Data and conditional scope

The arguments are `{p : ℕ} [Fact p.Prime]`,
`F : PadicField.{uF} p`, a supplied `T : F.TopologicalData`, and `n : ℕ`.
The group is `G = Langlands.GeneralLinear (n+1) F.Carrier`, so rank one
is included. The field and the two representation carriers have independent
universes `uF`, `uV`, and `uW`. Set `q = T.residueCardinality` and
`abv = normalizedAbsoluteValue T`.

The definition installs the same field instances as
`EqualRankGammaExistenceStatement`: `T.topology`, `T.valuativeRel`,
`T.localField`, the additive right uniform space and uniform additive-group
instance, the derived `NonarchimedeanAddGroup` instance, and the Borel
measurable structures and `BorelSpace` instances on `F.Carrier`, `G`,
`upperUnipotent (n+1) F.Carrier`, and
`LeftUnipotentQuotient n F.Carrier`. The private instance proof uses the
basis of open submodules supplied by `IsLinearTopology.hasBasis_open_submodule`;
it introduces no mathematical assumption.

With these instances, the proposition quantifies over:

1. Every `ψ : UnitaryAdditiveCharacter F.Carrier`, hence a continuous,
   nontrivial, unitary additive character, and every instance
   `Measure.IsMulRightInvariant (unipotentHaar F.Carrier n)`.
2. Every `μ : Measure (LeftUnipotentQuotient n F.Carrier)` satisfying
   `IsCompatible (upperUnipotent (n+1) F.Carrier)
   (generalLinearHaar F.Carrier n) (unipotentHaar F.Carrier n) μ`.
   This is the existing compatibility predicate for the actual quotient
   and the normalized Haar measures; in particular it includes right
   invariance of `μ`.
3. Every supplied scalar `ν : AdditiveHaarMeasure F.Carrier`.
   Self-duality of `ν` is not assumed.
4. Every `V : Type uV` and `W : Type uW`, each equipped with
   `AddCommGroup` and `Module ℂ` instances, and every representation
   `π : Representation ℂ G V` and `σ : Representation ℂ G W`.
5. The hypotheses `IsOfWhittakerType n (standardCharacter ψ n) π` and
   `IsOfWhittakerType n (inverseStandardCharacter ψ n) σ`, and every
   `ℓ : glFunctionals ψ n π` and `ℓ' : glInverseFunctionals ψ n σ`
   with `ℓ.val ≠ 0` and `ℓ'.val ≠ 0`.

The Whittaker-type and nonzero-functional hypotheses have exactly the
parent gamma-existence scope. Whittaker type means admissibility, finite
generation over the complex group algebra, and an exactly one-dimensional
space of the specified functionals. It permits reducible representations
and infinite-dimensional carriers.

The construction is conditional on these three further explicit hypotheses:

```text
EqualRankAnalyticConclusion q ψ abv μ π σ ℓ ℓ'

EqualRankAnalyticConclusion q (ψ.scale (-1)) abv μ
  (inverseTransposePullback π) (inverseTransposePullback σ)
  (firstDualFunctional ψ π ℓ) (secondDualFunctional ψ σ ℓ')

RowFourierClosed ψ ν (n+1).
```

Each analytic conclusion includes convergence on a right half-plane for
each actual test triple, rational continuation, a common denominator, and
existence of a normalized reciprocal-polynomial generator. The dual
conclusion is on the actual inverse-transpose models, with the existing
Weyl-translated functionals; it does not identify those representations
with contragredients by definition. `RowFourierClosed` says the actual
plus-sign row Fourier integral of each row Schwartz–Bruhat function is
again represented by a row Schwartz–Bruhat function. Fourier inversion,
Fourier equivariance under matrices, trilinearity, twisted invariance,
trilinear uniqueness, and gamma or epsilon existence are not hypotheses.

## The two forms and their invariance

For every pair of polynomials `P Pdual : Polynomial ℂ`, assume

```text
IsNormalizedGenerator (equalRankRationalValues q ψ abv μ π σ ℓ ℓ') P
IsNormalizedGenerator (dualEqualRankRationalValues q ψ abv μ π σ ℓ ℓ') Pdual.
```

These refer to the actual rational continuations of the two integral
families. They mean `P(0) = Pdual(0) = 1` and that the respective Laurent
spans are generated by `1/P` and `1/Pdual`. They do not mean that `P`
or `Pdual` generates the span itself. The existing theorem
`dualEqualRankRationalValues_eq` relates the dual value set to the
inverse-transpose analytic conclusion.

Put

```text
M  = (coefficientModel π ℓ).toSubmodule
M' = (coefficientModel σ ℓ').toSubmodule
S  = SchwartzBruhat (Fin (n+1) → F.Carrier).
```

The conclusion first supplies

```text
B Bdual : ℂ → (M →ₗ[ℂ] M' →ₗ[ℂ] S →ₗ[ℂ] ℂ).
```

Thus for each complex parameter these are genuine curried complex-linear
maps in all three test variables. Both are on the original coefficient
models; the dual transforms and Fourier transform enter the specification
of `Bdual`. The families are chosen after the two normalized generators
and before any spectral parameter or test triple.

For every `s : ℂ`, every `g : G`, `f : M`, `f' : M'`, and `Φ Φg : S`
such that `∀ x, Φg x = Φ (Matrix.vecMul x g.val)`, the conclusion requires

```text
B s (modelRepresentation π ℓ g f) (modelRepresentation σ ℓ' g f') Φg
  = determinantWeight abv g (-s) * B s f f' Φ.
```

It requires the identical formula with `Bdual` in place of `B`.
Here the row action is `Φg(x) = Φ(xg)` and the determinant twist is
`|det g|^(-s)`. There is no exceptional spectral set and no denominator
condition in either invariance assertion.

## Normalization, Laurent evaluation, and regular points

For every `f : M`, `f' : M'`, `Φ Φhat : S` with
`IsRowFourierPair ψ ν Φ Φhat`, and every `R Rd : RationalFunctions`
satisfying

```text
HasRationalContinuation q
  (equalRankModelIntegral ψ abv μ π σ ℓ ℓ' f f' Φ) R
HasRationalContinuation q
  (dualEqualRankModelIntegral ψ abv μ π σ ℓ ℓ' f f' Φhat) Rd,
```

the statement specifies the following regular-point formulas. `Rd` is a
continuation in its own argument `t`;
it is subsequently evaluated at `t = 1-s`. It is not a continuation of
the already reflected family as a function of `s`.

```text
R.denom.eval (spectralPoint q s) ≠ 0 →
  B s f f' Φ =
    P.eval (spectralPoint q s) *
      RatFunc.eval (RingHom.id ℂ) (spectralPoint q s) R

Rd.denom.eval (spectralPoint q (1-s)) ≠ 0 →
  Bdual s f f' Φ =
    Pdual.eval (spectralPoint q (1-s)) *
      RatFunc.eval (RingHom.id ℂ) (spectralPoint q (1-s)) Rd.
```

The two implications are separate conjuncts. The original identity does
not require regularity of `Rd`, and the dual identity does not require
regularity of `R`. Neither requires `P` or `Pdual` to be nonzero at the
evaluation point. The displayed identities are explicit conclusions,
not replaced by a lift-based formulation.
The statement never uses totalized rational evaluation at a pole to
define a linear form.

The intended construction defines the forms for every `s` by Laurent
evaluation after denominator clearing. For a continuation `R`, the theorem
`IsNormalizedGenerator.clearsDenominators` gives a Laurent lift of `P*R`;
the dual generator similarly gives a lift of `Pdual*Rd`. Injectivity of
`laurentToRatFunc` makes each lift unique. The spectral point is nonzero
because `q > 1`, so evaluation of these Laurent polynomials is defined even
where an individual rational continuation has a pole. The original lift
is evaluated at `q^(-s)` and the dual lift at `q^(-(1-s))`. On regular
points, evaluation bridges must give exactly the two displayed formulas.
The statement records the resulting linear forms, their invariance at
every `s`, and the direct regular-point identities; it does not include
chosen Laurent lifts as additional data. Constructing the lifts and
proving those evaluation bridges remain work for the proof.

## Source and derived formulation

The registered original is H. Jacquet, I. I. Piatetski-Shapiro, and
J. A. Shalika, *Rankin–Selberg Convolutions*, American Journal of Mathematics
105 (1983), 367–464, source ID `jpss-1983-rankin-selberg`. The retained file is
`/home/taskhub/central-library/Jacquet-Piatetski-Shapiro-Shalika--Rankin-Selberg Convolutions--1983.pdf`,
SHA256 `d9feed6388ca5acf02ecc6a8cce984871a7c28f062ff441e45a71fa2d8962245`.
Printed pp.391 and 396 were read visually, respectively one-based PDF
pages 26 and 31. No source erratum note was found in the checked project,
workspace, or supplied Library `notes/errata` locations.

Theorem 2.7(ii), at the top of printed p.391, gives the fractional ideal
with generator `L(s) = 1/P(q^(-s))`, with `P(0)=1`, and gives the analogous
result for the inverse-transpose pair. On p.396, §2.10 recalls the
equal-rank integral, obtains right-translation equivariance in (2.10.2),
and uses the Fourier change-of-variables formula to obtain the matching
dual equivariance in (2.10.3). The Proposition there concerns uniqueness
of twisted invariant trilinear forms outside finitely many values of
`q^(-s)`. That uniqueness proposition is a separate target and is not
assumed here.

The scan of (2.10.3) has `1-s` on its left, but the right-hand integral
appears to use `s`. The intertwining calculation uses the same reflected
parameter `1-s` on both untransformed dual sides. Accordingly this
statement continues the actual dual integral in `t` and then evaluates
at `t=1-s` throughout. This convention is a derived interpretation of
the displayed calculation, not a claim that the scan prints that formula.

The present formulation also differs from (2.10.2)–(2.10.3) by normalizing
with `P(q^(-s))` and `Pdual(q^(-(1-s)))` and using Laurent evaluation.
Those scalar normalizations preserve the determinant twist and extend
the forms to every `s`, including individual poles. The absence of
spectral exceptions in their construction is separate from the finite
exceptional set in the source's uniqueness proposition. The source works
over non-Archimedean local fields; this target is restricted to the
Library's `PadicField` scope.

The intended proof uses uniqueness of rational continuations and
linearity on common convergent right half-planes, denominator clearing,
and linearity of Laurent evaluation. Original invariance uses
`IsCompatible.right_invariant` and last-row right multiplication. Dual
invariance additionally needs the actual Fourier matrix change of
variables, inverse-transpose model action, and reciprocal determinant
scaling. These are proof obligations, not additional assumptions or
implemented results of this statement module. The existing
`PadicRectangularHaarScalingProof` supplies the determinant Haar-scaling
ingredient. No construction of gamma or epsilon factors is claimed.
