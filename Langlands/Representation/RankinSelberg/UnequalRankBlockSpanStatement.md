# Equality of adjacent unequal-rank JPSS Laurent spans

Author: Worker 46. Worker 12 specified the focused interface for the
common-generator proof. The module defines
`Langlands.RankinSelberg.UnequalRankAdjacentBlockSpanStatement.{uF,uV,uW} F T n r`,
a proposition target, using only the published definitions imported through
[AnalyticStatement](AnalyticStatement.md). It supplies no proof of the target.

## The assertion

Fix all the data and hypotheses of
`UnequalRankCommonGeneratorStatement F T n r`. For every `j k : ℕ` and
`hr : r = n + (j + k + 2)`, the conclusion is

```text
zetaSpan (unequalRankValuesAt (j+1) k … q ψ abv μ
            (rectangularMeasure ν (j+1) (n+1)) π σ ℓ ℓ')
  =
zetaSpan (unequalRankValuesAt j (k+1) … q ψ abv μ
            (rectangularMeasure ν j (n+1)) π σ ℓ ℓ'),
```

where `q = T.residueCardinality` and `abv = normalizedAbsoluteValue T`.
Each omitted argument is the equality of natural-number ranks induced by
`hr`. The two arithmetic proofs only transport types through the existing
`unequalRankValuesAt`; they assert no equality of integrals or matrices.

`unequalRankValuesAt a b` is the actual set of rational functions continuing
the corresponding coefficient-model integrals. Membership means that there
are functions in `coefficientModel π ℓ` and `coefficientModel σ ℓ'` whose
`unequalRankModelIntegral` has that rational continuation.
`HasRationalContinuation` requires agreement with evaluation at `q^(-s)` on
some right half-plane and nonvanishing of the rational denominator there.
Defining this set does not assert that every integral has a continuation.

`zetaSpan` is the `LaurentCoefficients = ℂ[X,X⁻¹]` submodule generated by
that set in `RationalFunctions = ℂ(X)`, with `X = q^(-s)`. The assertion
compares these submodules, not the unspanned sets of values. It neither
requires nor concludes `HasCommonDenominator`, so it does not package them
as fractional ideals.

Write `R = r+1` and `t = n+1` for the source's representation ranks. Its
allowed block indices are `0 ≤ a ≤ R-t-1`. Here the adjacent indices are
`a = j+1` and `a-1 = j`, and `R-t = j+k+2`. Thus all adjacent pairs occur,
including `j=0` and `k=0`. At rank gap one, `r=n+1`, there is no such
`j,k,hr`; the conclusion has no adjacent comparison to make.

## Quantifiers and representations

The telescope before the adjacent-block conclusion is copied from
`UnequalRankCommonGeneratorStatement`, without strengthening its assumptions.
It fixes every prime `p` with `[Fact p.Prime]`, every
`F : PadicField.{uF} p`, every supplied `T : F.TopologicalData`, and natural
`n,r`. It installs `T.topology`, `T.valuativeRel`, and `T.localField`, the
Borel measurable structure on `F.Carrier`, and the Borel measurable
structures and certificates on `GL_(n+1)`, its upper-unipotent subgroup,
and `LeftUnipotentQuotient n F.Carrier`. It does not restrict `T` to the
canonical topological data.

Under `n < r`, the statement quantifies over every
`ψ : UnitaryAdditiveCharacter F.Carrier`, the right-invariance instance
`Measure.IsMulRightInvariant (unipotentHaar F.Carrier n)`, every compatible
quotient measure `μ`, and every `ν : AdditiveHaarMeasure F.Carrier`.
The character is the published nontrivial continuous unitary additive
character, used with opposite signs for the two representations.

The carriers `V : Type uV` and `W : Type uW` have independent universes,
each with its own `AddCommGroup` and `Module ℂ` instances. The statement
quantifies over every representation `π` of `GL_(r+1)` on `V` and `σ`
of `GL_(n+1)` on `W`, satisfying respectively
`IsOfWhittakerType r (standardCharacter ψ r) π` and
`IsOfWhittakerType n (inverseStandardCharacter ψ n) σ`.
As defined in AnalyticStatement, Whittaker type means admissibility
(including smoothness), finite generation over the group algebra, and a
one-dimensional space of the specified Whittaker functionals. It does not
require irreducibility or finite-dimensional representation carriers.

Finally, every `ℓ : glFunctionals ψ r π` and
`ℓ' : glInverseFunctionals ψ n σ` with `ℓ.val ≠ 0` and `ℓ'.val ≠ 0` is
covered. All this data is fixed before `j,k,hr` are quantified, and is
identical on both sides of the equality.

## Measures and normalization transport

The two Haar measures in the compatibility hypothesis are the published
`generalLinearHaar F.Carrier n` and `unipotentHaar F.Carrier n`, normalized
to give their integral subgroups mass one. The quotient is the actual left
quotient `N_(n+1) \ GL_(n+1)`. The hypothesis is exactly
`QuotientIntegration.IsCompatible` for these measures and `μ`: it includes
nonzero regularity, right-translation invariance and the Weil integration
formula for continuous compactly supported complex functions. Its explicit
unipotent right-invariance instance is retained.

The rectangular measure is the published product of the supplied scalar
additive Haar over all entries. For `a` rows it is
`rectangularMeasure ν a (n+1)`; at zero rows it is the unit point mass.
The same scalar measure `ν` and the same quotient measure `μ` occur in
both adjacent families.

JPSS 1983, printed p.378, chooses self-invariant Haar for the Fourier
transform used in the proof. The present span equality is independent of
that scalar normalization. If `ν = c ν₀` for a positive real constant
`c`, the measure on `a × (n+1)` matrices scales by `c^(a*(n+1))`.
The corresponding integrals and rational continuations scale by that
nonzero constant. The reverse rescaling gives the inverse correspondence.
Multiplication by a nonzero complex constant leaves a Laurent submodule
unchanged, since both the constant and its inverse lie in its coefficient
ring. Each block's span is therefore unchanged separately, even though the
scaling exponents of adjacent blocks differ. For zero rows the exponent is
zero. This is the normalization transport from the source's Fourier
convention to arbitrary supplied additive Haar, not an added self-duality
hypothesis or a formal scaling theorem proved in this module.
