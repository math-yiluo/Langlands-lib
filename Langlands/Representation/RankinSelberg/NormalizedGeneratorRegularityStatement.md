# Regularity of normalized reciprocal generators

The proposition `Langlands.RankinSelberg.NormalizedGeneratorRegularityStatement`
quantifies a set `values` of complex rational functions, a complex polynomial
`P`, and a nonzero complex point `z`. If `P` is an `IsNormalizedGenerator` of
the Laurent module generated by `values`, and the reduced denominator of
every member of `values` is nonzero at `z`, then `P.eval z` is nonzero.

This is an internal algebraic interface derived from the published definitions
in `RationalZeta` and `RationalEvaluation`. It does not restate a separate
assertion from the Rankin–Selberg literature and has no source citation.
`IsNormalizedGenerator` requires `P.coeff 0 = 1` and identifies the generated
Laurent module with the span of the reciprocal of `P`. `RegularAt R z` means
that the reduced denominator of `R` does not vanish at `z`; totalized rational
evaluation at a pole cannot satisfy this condition.

The reason for the nonzero-point hypothesis is that Laurent polynomials can
have poles at zero. Away from zero their images are regular, and regularity is
closed under addition and multiplication. Thus regularity of the generating
set passes to its Laurent span and to the reciprocal of `P`. Evaluating the
product of `P` and its reciprocal excludes a root of `P`. At zero the separate
normalization `P.coeff 0 = 1` already excludes a root.

The Lean file defines only the proposition. It supplies no proof of this
implication, no existence theorem for a normalized generator, and no analytic
regularity theorem for zeta integrals. Those are separate proof obligations.
