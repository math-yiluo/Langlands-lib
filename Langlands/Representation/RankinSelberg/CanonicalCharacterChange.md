# Canonical character change for actual p-adic zeta modules

Author: Worker 43.

This internal construction instantiates the existing equal- and unequal-rank
character-change interfaces with the actual normalized unipotent quotient
measure. In unequal rank it uses the actual rectangular product of a supplied
shared scalar additive Haar measure. The measure laws, determinant power law,
and required finiteness are proved or supplied internally. The conclusions are
Laurent-span equality and equivalence of normalized-generator relations and
existence predicates under `ψ ↦ ψ.scale a`.

## Data and normalization

The field is any `K : PadicField p` with `Fact p.Prime` and arbitrary supplied
`T : K.TopologicalData`. Every topology and valuation in the measure results
comes from this same `T`. The field carrier and the two complex representation
carriers have independent universes `uF`, `uV`, and `uV'`. The representations
are arbitrary algebraic complex representations; smoothness, admissibility,
irreducibility, genericity, and multiplicity one are not hypotheses.

Write `q = T.residueCardinality`, `|.| = normalizedAbsoluteValue T`, and
`d_a = characterChangeElement a n = diag(a^n, …, 1)` for `a : K.Carrierˣ`.
The integer exponent is the actual `e = T.ord (det d_a)`. The shared
normalization proves `|det d_a| = q^(-e)`, with `q > 1`.

The equal-rank pair has ranks `n+1` and `n+1`. For unequal rank the smaller rank
is `n+1`, the gap is `d=j+k+1`, and the larger rank is `n+d+1`. Every natural
`n`, `j`, and `k` is allowed, including zero. Thus rank one and the zero-row
rectangular case are retained.

`canonicalCharacterChangeLaurentUnit` specializes the published equal-rank
unit to `T` and `e`. Its rational image is `cq X^(-e)`, where
`cq = |a|^(sum_{i<l}(l-i))`, with indices in `Fin (n+1)`.
`canonicalUnequalRankCharacterChangeLaurentUnit` similarly specializes the
unequal-rank unit. Its rational image is `c X^(-e)`, where

`c = cq cr / D(-d/2)`,

`cr = |a|^((n+1) sum_{i : Fin j}(i.val+1))`, and
`D(s) = determinantWeight |.| d_a s`.

The half-gap is cast into the complex numbers before division. These are actual
Laurent units: their imported constructors prove all scalar nonzeroness fields.
Their interpretation follows the imported forward and reverse integral
identities, with spectral variable `X(s)=q^(-s)`. The unit definitions do not
choose an analytic continuation or a normalized generator.

## Canonical measures and finiteness

The measurable structures on the general linear group, upper unipotent
subgroup, and left quotient are their Borel structures in the supplied topology.
`unipotentQuotientHaar_characterChange` applies the published
`characterChangeCompatibleMeasure` to `unipotentQuotientHaar_isCompatible`.
It establishes the actual measure equality encoded by
`HasCharacterChangeMeasureLaw`: pushforward by the quotient character-change
map is the positive-root modulus times the canonical quotient measure.

`unipotentQuotientHaar_sigmaFinite_normalized` uses the published certificate
`T.generalLinearSigmaCompactSpace (n+1)`. The quotient is sigma compact as the
surjective continuous image under `Quotient.mk`. The canonical quotient
measure's published regularity gives finiteness on compact sets. Mathlib then
supplies sigma finiteness, hence S-finiteness. No measure-finiteness assumption
is exposed to consumers.

In unequal rank, the scalar measurable structure is `borel K.Carrier`. The
matrix measurable structure is the iterated finite product structure used by
`rectangularMeasure η j (n+1)`; second countability from `T` makes it a Borel
structure. Here `η : AdditiveHaarMeasure K.Carrier` is the shared actual Haar
measure bundle. It is arbitrary, so a self-dual measure can be supplied when
available, but no self-duality assertion is used. The proof installs
`T.sigmaCompactSpace`; the scalar Haar measure is sigma finite and the
published finite-product instance supplies sigma finiteness of the rectangular
measure. `characterChangeRectangularProductStatement` supplies its scaling law
for this exact product measure. In particular, `j=0` retains the imported unit
point mass, without a positive-dimension hypothesis.

## Consumer arguments

`zetaSpan_equalRank_characterChange_canonical` applies the normalized
conditional equal-rank theorem to the canonical quotient law. The unequal-rank
analogue applies `zetaSpan_unequalRank_characterChange` with the actual quotient
and product measures, both proved scaling laws, internal S-finiteness, and
`T.normalizedAbs_of_ne_zero` for the determinant. These are equalities of the
existing intrinsic Laurent submodules generated by rational zeta values.
The imported transport results retain actual representation coefficient
witnesses, half-plane convergence, and rational continuation; the unequal-rank
transport uses the corrected large function `W(D_a g R⁻¹)`.

For each rank regime, `isEqualRankLPolynomial_characterChange_canonical` or
`isUnequalRankLPolynomial_characterChange_canonical` applies
`isNormalizedGenerator_congr` to span equality. The two
`normalizedGeneratorExists_*_characterChange_canonical` results apply
`normalizedGeneratorExists_congr`. The latter predicate includes existence
and uniqueness, and the conclusion is an equivalence: neither existence
predicate is independently asserted here.

## Provenance and scope

The module derives these constructions from the published pairs
`CharacterChangeMeasure`, `UnipotentQuotient`,
`RectangularCharacterChangeMeasureProof`, `Measures`,
`EqualRankCharacterChange`, `UnequalRankCharacterChange`, and
`Group.GL.CountabilityProof`, with the shared local-field topology and
normalization API and Mathlib quotient and measure instances. The mathematical
normalizations and transport arguments are those of these imported
constructions; this module introduces no separate external-source assertion.

This completes the canonical measure specialization of character-change span
and generator transport. It does not prove analytic continuation or generator
existence, a functional equation, an epsilon or gamma formula, or a conductor
identity. Selecting factors from existence witnesses and transporting their
values remain consumers of these interfaces.
