# Algebraic normalization of rational gamma equations

Worker 351, with the shared interface proposed by Worker 103.

This module defines
`Langlands.RankinSelberg.GammaNormalizationStatement : Prop`.
It states an internal algebraic prerequisite shared by the equal-rank and
unequal-rank normalized functional equations. It contains no proof of this
proposition and makes no assertion that an analytic functional equation holds.

## Exact statement

For every natural number `q` with `1 < q`, every `ζ : ℂˣ`, natural number `e`,
rational function `G : RationalFunctions`, sets `S T : Set RationalFunctions`,
and polynomials `P Pdual : Polynomial ℂ`, assume:

1. `IsNormalizedGenerator S P`.
2. `IsNormalizedGenerator T Pdual`.
3. Every `R ∈ S` has some `Rdual ∈ T` satisfying
   `GammaFunctionalEquation q ζ e G R Rdual`.
4. Every `Rdual ∈ T` has some `R ∈ S` satisfying that same gamma equation.

Then there exist `c : ℂˣ` and `m : ℤ` such that, for every pair of rational
functions `R Rdual`, the implication

```text
GammaFunctionalEquation q ζ e G R Rdual →
NormalizedFunctionalEquation q P Pdual ζ c e m R Rdual
```

holds. The constants are chosen once for the preceding data, before `R` and
`Rdual`. The conclusion does not require these rational functions to belong
to `S` and `T`. There are no representation carriers or rank parameters.

All three predicates are the existing Library definitions. In
`RationalZeta`, `RationalFunctions` is `RatFunc ℂ`, the coefficient ring is
`LaurentCoefficients = LaurentPolynomial ℂ`, and its scalar action uses the
injective ring homomorphism `laurentToRatFunc`. The generator hypothesis says
`P.coeff 0 = 1` and that `zetaSpan S` equals the Laurent span of the rational
function `1/P`; likewise for the dual polynomial and `T`. In particular, both
polynomials and both generated modules are nonzero.

Write `x = spectralPoint q s = q^(-s)` and
`y = spectralPoint q (1 - s) = q⁻¹/x`. The gamma equation says

\[
 R_{\mathrm{dual}}(y)=\zeta^eG(x)R(x)
\]

for every complex `s` where the reduced denominators of `R` at `x`,
`Rdual` at `y`, and `G` at `x` are nonzero. The normalized equation concludes

\[
 P_{\mathrm{dual}}(y)R_{\mathrm{dual}}(y)
   =\zeta^e(c x^m)\bigl(P(x)R(x)\bigr)
\]

for every complex `s` where just the reduced denominators of `R` at `x`
and `Rdual` at `y` are nonzero. There is no additional nonvanishing condition
on `G`, `P`, or `Pdual` in this conclusion. In particular, zeros canceled
from a displayed gamma quotient do not remove points from the conclusion.

## Derived provenance and algebraic rationale

The original source is H. Jacquet, I. I. Piatetski-Shapiro and J. A. Shalika,
*Rankin-Selberg Convolutions*, American Journal of Mathematics 105 (1983),
367–464, §2.9, printed pp. 395–396. The registered source is
`jpss-1983-rankin-selberg`, SHA256
`d9feed6388ca5acf02ecc6a8cce984871a7c28f062ff441e45a71fa2d8962245`;
these are PDF pages 30–31 in the retained scan, counting from one.
That section assumes existence of the functional equation, applies its dual
version, and obtains inverse Laurent factors, hence monomials. The present
statement is an algebraic generalization of that normalization argument,
not a verbatim statement of an analytic theorem from the source.

The following derivation explains the proposition's formulation; it is not
an implemented Lean proof.

Put `A = ℂ[X, X⁻¹]`, embedded in `K = ℂ(X)` by `laurentToRatFunc`, and let
`ρ` be the substitution `X ↦ q⁻¹/X`. The existing
`Langlands.LocalFactor.RationalReflection` namespace in
`Langlands.LocalFactor.WeilDelignePair` supplies this involutive field
automorphism. It preserves the actual embedded Laurent ring: it takes
`a Xⁿ` to `a q⁻ⁿ X⁻ⁿ`, and applying it twice is the identity.
Thus reflection takes an `A`-generated module to the `A`-module generated
by the reflected generators, although its action on `A` is not trivial.

For each pair satisfying the gamma equation, equality on infinitely many
regular spectral points first gives the rational identity

\[
 \rho(R_{\mathrm{dual}})=aR,\qquad a=\zeta^eG\in K.
\]

Here `q > 1` ensures that real spectral parameters give infinitely many
distinct nonzero points; only finitely many are excluded by the three
nonzero reduced denominator polynomials. This is the same rational-identity
method used in `PairFactors` for the forward normalized-to-gamma direction.

The two incidence hypotheses give both inclusions between the generated
modules, hence

\[
 \rho(\operatorname{zetaSpan}T)
     =a\,\operatorname{zetaSpan}S.
\]

The left side is nonzero by its normalized generator, so `a ≠ 0`. Substituting
the two specified generators shows that the nonzero principal modules
generated by `1/ρ(Pdual)` and `a/P` coincide. Their generators therefore
differ by a unit `u` of the actual Laurent coefficient ring:

\[
 a/P=u/\rho(P_{\mathrm{dual}}).
\]

Over `ℂ`, every Laurent unit is a nonzero monomial. The sign is a constant
Laurent unit as well, so `ε = ζ^{-e}u = cX^m` for some `c : ℂˣ` and `m : ℤ`.
It follows that

\[
 G\,\rho(P_{\mathrm{dual}})=\varepsilon P.
\]

For any rational pair satisfying the gamma equation, regardless of membership
in `S` or `T`, its rational identity now yields

\[
 \rho(P_{\mathrm{dual}})\rho(R_{\mathrm{dual}})
       =\zeta^e(cX^m)P R.
\]

This last identity is evaluated only after the rational algebra has been
performed. At every point in the normalized equation's domain, `R` and
the reflected `Rdual` are regular, the polynomial factors are regular, and
`cX^m` is regular since `x ≠ 0`. The `RationalEvaluation` API gives precisely
these evaluation and reflection laws. Thus no gamma-denominator condition or
nonvanishing of either displayed polynomial is needed, including when a pole
of `G` is canceled by other factors. `LaurentTransport` already constructs
monomial units and their images; it does not by itself supply this converse
normalization or the classification of every Laurent unit.

## Scope

The source's analytic input is represented here by the two explicit incidence
hypotheses on actual rational-function sets. Neither epsilon-factor existence,
a Laurent-unit witness, nor a normalized functional equation is assumed.
No task status or review receipt supplies mathematical hypotheses. Proving this
proposition, and establishing its hypotheses for the analytic integral families,
remain separate obligations. The imported Lean/Markdown pairs are unchanged.
