# Norm comparison on represented Levi central torus points

Author: Worker 1682.

This internal development applies split-component lifts modulo
the common norm kernel to full-center characters of represented Levis.
It does not prove the full Casselman criterion.

The presentation's ambient maximal torus maps into its represented Levi:
the standard Levi contains this torus, and its all-test image equals the
given Levi image. Pulling back the abstract Levi center gives the actual
subgroup of central torus points. Continuity of the torus point map and
closedness of the abstract center show this subgroup is closed.

A smooth full-center character restricts continuously to this closed
torus subgroup. The compact-kernel character comparison then gives equal
norms at a central torus point and any split-component point representing
the same class modulo the common norm kernel.

For a point and its component representative modulo the norm kernel, the
actual torus valuations agree. Naturality of the valuation and of the
character-cocharacter pairing identifies the restricted-character pairing
on the component with the original point's integer character order.

The final theorem assumes the represented-parabolic positive-cone
condition for all normalized Jacquet exponents. Given a central torus
point with zero selected-root orders and nonnegative simple-root orders,
at least one of which is positive, it constructs the component
representative. The positive simple root lies outside the selected set
because every selected-root order is zero. The pairing comparison therefore
gives nonnegative valuations for all restricted roots and a positive one.
The cone-to-norm bound applies to every full-center exponent at the
component representative, and the norm comparison transfers the bound
to the original central torus point.

The field and representation carrier universes remain arbitrary. The
zero selected-root order and positive simple-root order hypotheses remain
explicit. The result neither constructs a presentation nor supplies the
general reductive geometry and analytic implications of Casselman's
criterion. It is an internal deduction from the Library constructions;
no new source equivalence is asserted.
