# Normalized-exponent temperedness under an ambient scheme isomorphism

This internal construction transports the Library's normalized-exponent
condition through an arbitrary isomorphism of the actual ambient group schemes.
It is derived from the existing group transport of the Haar modulus, actual
coinvariant transport, generalized-eigencharacter invariance, admissibility
transport and ambient transport of compatible root presentations. It asserts no
new comparison with Renard's raw logarithmic convention.

The restricted normalized quotient equivalence retains each vector
representative. The closed inverse half Haar characters agree under the ambient
topological group equivalence, and corresponding maps from the same test group
give the intertwining identity. For an actual ambient scheme isomorphism, the
point equivalence uses its actual rational-point maps and their continuity for
the canonical affine point topologies. Membership in the literal radical and
Levi images transports along that equivalence.

Consequently every normalized Jacquet exponent of the transported parabolic
agrees with the exponent of the pulled-back representation. The original Levi,
radical, split component and character remain fixed. Transporting compatible
root presentations in both directions preserves nonempty coverage and all
presentation tests, because the actual nonnegative cones agree. Admissibility
transports through the same point equivalence. These facts prove that an actual
ambient scheme-isomorphism pullback of a normalized-exponent tempered
representation is normalized-exponent tempered.

All field and carrier universes remain independent. Both actual point groups
carry their canonical topology and retain local compactness and the
nonarchimedean group assumptions; the field hypotheses of the published
normalized-exponent predicate are unchanged. The group statement needs no
admissibility assumption. The final theorem assumes only the original
normalized-exponent temperedness and the connected reductivity of the two
ambient groups, beyond those topology and field hypotheses. It neither proves
square-integrability nor the final VII.2.6 classification statements.

Integration uses module-specific names for local instances. Their types, values,
and local instance scopes are unchanged.
