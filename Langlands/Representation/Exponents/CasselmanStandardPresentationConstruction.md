# Fixed-torus Casselman presentations from actual geometric witnesses

Author: Worker 1682.

This internal consumer construction supplies the exact fixed-torus
presentation record used by the sufficiency assembly. It retains the
geometric properties of the canonical standard parabolic, Levi and
radical maps and their multiplication isomorphism as explicit inputs.
Generic dynamic minimality and `StandardCocharacterStatement` are also
supplied. It does not prove these structural properties or the full
Casselman criterion.

The construction uses the published canonical standard group-scheme
representatives and maps. A supplied rational Levi is connected
reductive, so the proved general split-component existence theorem gives
an actual split component of that same Levi. The existing standard-Levi
factorization theorem supplies its factor through the prescribed maximal
split torus, using the actual all-test Levi representation.
The definition uses classical choice to select a record from the resulting
existence proof.

The supplied standard-cocharacter proposition gives a cocharacter and
the three dynamic comparisons at the same torus, original generic
cocharacter and selected root set. The published relative-root sign
inclusion theorem shows that the original generic dynamic parabolic is
contained in this standard parabolic: every positive original root has
nonnegative pairing with the chosen standard cocharacter. This supplies
the compatible presentation's minimal-parabolic containment field.

The canonical all-test parabolic and Levi equalities give the remaining
presentation fields and simultaneous dynamic realization. Evaluating the
canonical unipotent equality on the field gives the exact radical
point-image equality. Thus the output retains precisely the prescribed
torus and standard index, rather than making a new torus or face choice.

The argument works over an arbitrary field and uses no topology. The
geometric parabolic, rational Levi, full geometric radical,
multiplication and generic-minimality inputs remain obligations with
their structural owners. The construction is an internal deduction from
the Library APIs, and asserts no new original-source equivalence.
