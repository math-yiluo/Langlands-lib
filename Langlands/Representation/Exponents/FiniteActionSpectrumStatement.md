# Finite spectrum of a commutative action with finite operator span

This module states one proposition,
`Langlands.FiniteActionCommutativeSpectrumStatement.{uA,uV}`. It is a
statement only; this module contains no proof of it.

## Quantifiers and hypotheses

The proposition quantifies over

- a commutative group `A : Type uA` with an arbitrary topology
  (`[CommGroup A] [TopologicalSpace A]`; no topological-group axiom is assumed),
- a complex vector space `V : Type uV` (`[AddCommGroup V] [Module ℂ V]`), with
  no finiteness or topology on `V`,
- an actual representation `ρ : Representation ℂ A V`.

The group and carrier universes `uA` and `uV` are independent. The two
hypotheses are exactly:

1. `Module.Finite ℂ (Submodule.span ℂ (Set.range ρ))`: the complex span of the
   action operators `ρ a`, a subspace of `Module.End ℂ V`, is finite
   dimensional. This concerns the operators, not the carrier: `V` itself may be
   infinite dimensional (for instance an infinite direct sum of copies of one
   character, or the full algebraic dual of such a module).
2. `IsLocallyConstant (fun a : A => ρ a)`: every fibre of the operator map
   `A → Module.End ℂ V` is open, the pointwise local-constancy predicate. No
   topology on `V` or on its endomorphisms is used.

The zero vector space and the trivial group are allowed.

## Conclusions

Call a character `χ : A →* ℂˣ` *occurring* when
`IsOpen (χ.ker : Set A) ∧ commutativeCharacterSpace ρ χ 1 ≠ ⊥`: its kernel is
open and it has a nonzero ordinary eigenspace
`{v | ∀ a, ρ a v = χ a • v}`. A nonzero generalized eigenspace alone does not
count as occurrence. The three conclusions use the existing objects of
`Langlands.Representation.Exponents.DualJacquet`:

1. The set of occurring characters is finite (`Set.Finite`).
2. Every occurring character satisfies
   `commutativeCharacterNilpotenceIndex ρ χ < ⊤`. This index is the existing
   least `n : ℕ` such that the whole generalized component
   `generalizedCommutativeCharacterSpace ρ χ` lies in
   `commutativeCharacterSpace ρ χ n`, or `⊤` when no such `n` exists. A finite
   index therefore gives one power `n` with `(ρ a - χ a • 1) ^ n v = 0` for every
   `a : A` and every vector `v` of the generalized component. It is neither a
   bound for a single vector, nor a dimension, nor a composition multiplicity.
3. The supremum of the underlying submodules
   `(generalizedCommutativeCharacterSpace ρ χ).toSubmodule`, indexed by the
   occurring characters `χ` together with proofs that they occur, is
   `⊤ : Submodule ℂ V`. Membership in a generalized component keeps the existing
   quantifier order: some `k : ℕ` with `(ρ a - χ a • 1) ^ k v = 0` for all `a`.

Nothing else is assumed: there is no hypothesis of finite dimensionality of `V`
or of any component, no finite generation as an `A`-representation, and no
supplied spectrum, eigencharacter, projector, decomposition, uniform annihilator
or scalar quotient.

## Intended proof route (not part of this statement)

The intended proof works in the finite-dimensional commutative unital algebra
`B ⊆ Module.End ℂ V` generated by the operators `ρ a`. Because each product
`ρ a * ρ b = ρ (a * b)` and the identity `ρ 1` are action operators, the
underlying space of `B` is the operator span of hypothesis 1. As a
finite-dimensional commutative algebra over `ℂ`, `B` is Artinian: it has
finitely many maximal ideals, each the kernel of an algebra homomorphism
`φ : B → ℂ`, and its Jacobson radical is nilpotent. Elements of `B` attached to
these finitely many `φ`, each annihilated by one fixed power of `ker φ` and with
a unit sum, split every vector of `V` into pieces lying in generalized
components, with one order for all group elements and all vectors. The
candidate characters are `a ↦ φ (ρ a)`, and local constancy of `ρ` gives them
open kernels. The finiteness used here belongs to the algebra `B`, not to `V`,
which can be infinite dimensional.

## Provenance

The statement is project-internal and derived: a generic algebraic
prerequisite for the full-dual Jacquet finiteness statement
`Langlands.GLDualJacquetFinitenessStatement`, whose full algebraic dual
carrier can be infinite dimensional. It is not a verbatim translation of any
literature statement. Its mathematical content is the standard structure of
finite-dimensional commutative algebras over an algebraically closed field,
described in the route above. The proposition body is the one prescribed by
