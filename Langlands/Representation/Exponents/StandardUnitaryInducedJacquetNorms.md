# Finite exponent norms for the original actual standard induced family

This derived module specializes the full represented geometric-lemma norm
bound to the original literal standard parabolic, original rational norm
family, and original compatible split component. The source is a smooth
irreducible admissible complex representation of the original standard Levi.
The comparison preserves this representation and its carrier.

Canonical standard representatives have exactly the original parabolic, Levi
and radical point subgroups. Their Levi point homeomorphism, followed by the
subgroup equality, is the homeomorphism used to define the original Levi
rational norm characters. Composing the original twist with that
homeomorphism gives a continuous unitary unramified complex character of the
canonical Levi's rational points. The rational algebraic characters index the
norms defining the common kernel.

Induction across equal actual subgroups is compared by identity on the source
carrier and the canonical induced equivalence. Applying Jacquet functoriality,
then equal-subgroup comparison on the unchanged component, gives the actual
standard normalized Jacquet representation. Its ordinary coinvariant
realization is the same action used by the chosen standard constant term.
Consequently, at each fixed point of the specified component, a single finite
set contains the norms of all generalized exponents over all unitary
unramified twists of the given source.

No square-integrability, generic locus, distinctness of specialized exponents,
or final coefficient bound is assumed. This finite-norm result does not by
itself show that nonunitary exponents contract.

The contextual original source is Waldspurger, *La formule de Plancherel pour
les groupes p-adiques, d'après Harish-Chandra* (2003), proofs of VI.2.1 and
VI.2.3, printed pp.297–300, source `waldspurger-2003-plancherel`. The implemented
proof is a derived comparison of the project's canonical standard models
with its actual represented geometric-lemma spectral theorem.
