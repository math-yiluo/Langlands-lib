# A positive form identifies an irreducible real module with its finite dual

This internal construction supplies the Hermitian comparison for real algebraic `(𝔤, K)`-modules. It uses the infinitesimal unitarity identity in Knapp–Vogan, *Cohomological Induction and Unitary Representations* (1995), display (0.5), p. 6 (PDF p. 28, `knapp-vogan-1995-cohomological-induction`) and the Library's proved real finite contragredient.

A conjugate-linear equivariant map carries the compact orbit span to the orbit span of its image. The image of a finite-dimensional span under complex conjugate-linear mapping is finite-dimensional. This places `b(v, -)` in the finite dual `kDual ρ` for every compact-finite `v`. The form's invariance intertwines the compact action, and definiteness makes this map injective.

For a real matrix `X`, the equation relating `b(π(X)v,w)` to `−b(v,π(X)w)` is exactly the intertwining identity with the dual Lie action. The image is a complex subspace because conjugation on `ℂ` is onto. It is stable under the real Lie directions and therefore under their complex-linear span. The finite contragredient is irreducible for an irreducible admissible real module, so the nonzero image is the whole finite dual. The surjectivity theorem uses this algebraic argument on the actual finite dual.
