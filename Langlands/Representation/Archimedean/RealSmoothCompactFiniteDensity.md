# Compact-finite density for real smooth vectors

`RealSmoothCompactFiniteDensityStatement` says that the orthogonal-finite
vectors of a jointly continuous real general linear group representation
on a complex Banach space are dense in its genuine smooth carrier,
with the word-derivative topology. Compact finiteness here refers to
finite-dimensional span under the actual orthogonal matrices.

The compact smooth orbit integral, viewed as a map in the weight, intertwines
left translation of the weight and the actual orthogonal action. This
follows from left invariance of Haar measure and passage of a bounded
operator through the Banach integral in the empty word coordinate.
Each matrix entry has finite left orbit span, since matrix multiplication
expresses every left translate as a linear combination of entries. The
finite-orbit weights form a star subalgebra, so all polynomial weights
produce compact-finite smooth vectors.

If the closure of the compact-finite subspace omits a smooth vector,
Hahn–Banach separates that vector from the closed subspace by a continuous
complex functional. A real-part bound on a complex subspace forces the
functional to vanish there by scaling. Commutation of the functional with
smooth compact integration shows that its coefficient on the orthogonal
orbit of the omitted vector pairs to zero against every polynomial.
Polynomial density on the orthogonal group then gives zero for the
whole coefficient. Its value at the identity contradicts separation.

This is an internal density argument used for smooth extension of the
covariance of real Whittaker coefficients.
