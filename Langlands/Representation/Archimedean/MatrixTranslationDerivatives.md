# Matrix translation derivatives and the trace Casimir

The derivatives of left and right translations are the
Fréchet derivatives in the vectors `X * g` and `g * X`.
Differentiating these fields once more gives a Hessian term
and a term from the derivative of the linear field. Symmetry
of the second Fréchet derivative cancels the Hessian terms
in the commutator, leaving the field of `Y * X - X * Y`.

Summing two left-translation derivatives against the real
trace dual families defines the quadratic trace Casimir.
At an invertible matrix, conjugation by its inverse changes
left tangent vectors into the right tangent vectors. The
real trace contraction is invariant under that conjugation
and under exchanging its two arguments. Applying these
identities to the Hessian and first-order terms proves that
the same operator is obtained with right translations.

The chain rule exchanges left and right fields under a
linear anti-automorphism. Trace contraction then gives
commutation with transpose followed by fixed matrix
conjugation, including long-Weyl conjugation.

This gives the actual smooth matrix differential operators
used for the Casimir in Shalika (1974), §2.
