# Irreducibility and compact admissibility of the Frobenius Hilbert closure

Start with a real GK module and a Jacquet functional satisfying the
compact and Lie algebra character equations. For a closed invariant
subspace of the Frobenius Hilbert closure, the preimage under the
algebraic Frobenius map is a GK submodule. Compact
stability is immediate from equivariance. The derivative of an
exponential orbit in the closed subspace again belongs to that subspace,
which follows by applying orthogonal projection and comparing derivatives.
The genuine Frobenius derivative and tensor-product induction give full
Lie stability.

For an irreducible algebraic module and a nonzero Jacquet functional,
the Frobenius map is injective and the closure is nontrivial. Algebraic
irreducibility makes the preimage either zero or the whole module.
If the preimage is the whole algebraic module, norm density gives the
whole Hilbert closure. If the preimage is zero, the polynomial averages
of every vector in the closed subspace lie in the intersection with the
algebraic image and hence vanish. Polynomial separation at the identity
then gives the zero subspace. This proves topological irreducibility.

The compact orbit of every vector in the range of a finite-dimensional
intertwiner spans a subspace of that finite-dimensional range. Compact
recovery therefore puts the entire range of the intertwiner in the
algebraic image. The intertwiner comparison assumes injectivity of the
Frobenius map. Its linear left inverse
pulls the intertwiner back to the original algebraic module. This
pullback is linear, equivariant and injective. For an irreducible module
with a nonzero Jacquet functional, the same injectivity applies. With
algebraic compact admissibility, it bounds the dimension of each Hilbert
intertwiner space and yields Hilbert compact admissibility.

This internal proof uses compact-finite recovery and the genuine
exponential derivative for Frobenius vectors.
