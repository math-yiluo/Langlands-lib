# Compact-finite intertwiner into the inverse-transpose twisted continuous dual

The declarations are in `Langlands.Archimedean`. Write `θ(g) = (g⁻¹)ᵀ` for the published `Langlands.GeneralLinear.inverseTranspose n F`. For the matrix model over `F : Type` with `RCLike F` and a bounded-operator representation `R` on a complex normed space `E`, `inverseTransposeDualRepresentation F n R` is the composition of `continuousDualRepresentation R` with `θ`. Its space is `StrongDual ℂ E`, the continuous complex-linear dual, and its action is

`S(g)L = L ∘ R(θ(g)⁻¹) = L ∘ R(gᵀ)`.

This is restriction along the actual group automorphism, not along transpose alone. The dual is complex linear, not conjugate linear.

`RealSmoothHilbertDualHarishChandraStatement.{uH}` is an unproved proposition. Fix `n = r + 1` over `ℝ` and an `R` on a complete complex Hilbert space `H : Type uH` satisfying `IsIrreducibleAdmissibleHilbertRepresentation ℝ n R`. Give `V = smoothHilbertKFinite ℝ n R` actions `ρ` and `π` satisfying `IsRealGKModule n ρ π`, `IsIrreducibleGKModule ρ π`, and `IsAdmissibleGKModule ρ`. Require that the inclusion of `V` into the genuine smooth carrier intertwine the compact actions and derivatives in the sense of [IsSmoothGKIntertwiner](SmoothCasselmanWallachStatement.md). The conclusion is a nonzero complex-linear `f` from `V` into `DerivativeSmoothVectors (matrixModel ℝ n) S` with the same intertwining property for `S`. The image is automatically compact finite by equivariance and finite-dimensionality of the compact orbit spans in `V`. There is no continuity assertion on `f` with an ambient topology on `V`, and no extension to all smooth vectors of `R` is part of this conclusion.

## Provenance and remaining comparison

Aizenbud–Gourevitch, *Generalized Harish-Chandra descent and applications to Gelfand pairs*, arXiv:0803.3395v7, registered as `aizenbud-gourevitch-2009-generalized-harish-chandra-descent-v7` (SHA256 `a789936fffe560b9295c666f420a3cf7c69a475192bace2e5c2c4c3d975d7dfd`), Definition 8.1.1 and Corollary 7.6.3 (p.22), Theorem 8.2.1 and Remark 8.2.2 (p.23). With ordinary transpose as the anti-automorphism, Theorem 8.2.1 identifies the smooth contragredient with the inverse-transpose twist. This proposition isolates a derived compact-finite consequence expressed inside the smooth vectors of the *Banach continuous dual*. To obtain it from the source one must justify the Harish-Chandra or smooth-category comparison, in particular the realization of the contragredient compact-finite module by smooth continuous functionals on `H`, respecting the actions and their derivatives. Equality of characters and the corresponding rigidity theorem are substantial inputs to the source's identification; neither is supplied by the dual representation definition. An abstract algebraic dual would not alone furnish the required realization.

The statement does not assume or prove a real Hilbert globalization of an arbitrary algebraic module, nor does it use the final algebraic transpose-pairing target. It supplies the compact-finite input that the [reduction](SmoothHilbertTransposePairingReduction.md) would extend by Casselman–Wallach.
