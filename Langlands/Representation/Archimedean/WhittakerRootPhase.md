# The infinitesimal upper-root character

Restrict a unitary additive character to the real line
and to the imaginary line. The real-line exponential
parameter theorem gives two real frequencies. Combining
the two lines gives the phase of the whole character.
The trace-dual coefficient from these frequencies is
nonzero by nontriviality of the character.

On a real line in a scalar direction, differentiation of
the exponential gives the purely imaginary derivative
of the character. In the actual upper-unipotent group,
the transvection contributes to the first-superdiagonal
sum precisely when its root is simple. Thus the standard
character is trivial on every other positive-root
transvection, as used in Shalika (1974), Proposition 2.10.
