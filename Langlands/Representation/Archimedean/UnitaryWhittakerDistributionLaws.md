# Casimir and translation identities of the Whittaker coefficient

The representation is jointly continuous and unitary, and the two
smooth-vector functionals satisfy finite word-seminorm bounds. The
matrix group carries Borel Haar measure invariant under inversion.

The trace Casimir on matrix tests agrees with the sum of two-fold
right-translation derivatives, by left and right trace contraction
on the open matrix units and by vanishing of tests on the
complement. For an adjoint kernel, the right words become the actual
kernel derivatives that pass through the Gårding integral. The Riesz
pairing thus transfers the smooth-vector Casimir scalar to the
regularization, with conjugation from the first entry of the Hilbert
inner product. Applying the other functional gives the distribution
eigenvalue.

For a right-invariant Haar measure and functionals with the same
standard Whittaker character, consider a test pulled back by
\(x \mapsto u x v\) with both factors upper unipotent. The coefficient
translation formulas give the character at \(u^{-1}\) and the
conjugate character at \(v\). Both are values of the standard
character for \(x \mapsto \psi(-x)\). These are the two actual
distribution identities used in applying the Weyl transpose symmetry
of Shalika (1974), Theorem 2.1, to the coefficients of Theorem 3.1.
