# Lowest weight vectors in `Hom(W, V)` and covariants

Module `Langlands.Representation.Archimedean.GLTensorLowest`, namespace
`Langlands.Archimedean.GLReynolds`. It imports `GLBurnside` and the Library module
`BGChaseReps` (the representation `BGChase.homLie` of `𝔤𝔩_n(ℂ)` on `Hom(W, V)`, and
`BGChase.coord_comp_of_isWt` for coordinates with respect to a weight basis).

This module is part of a planned proof of Kostant's multiplicity bound
(`VermaGL.KostantMultiplicityStatement`, Library module `LowerVerma`). It contains no Library
statement and proves only the results listed below; no new axioms are used.

## Notation

Families `e` satisfying `IsGlFamily e`, weight vectors (`IsWeightVec e μ v`), lowest weight
vectors (`IsLowest e μ v`), generated subspaces `genSub`, the Casimir operator
`cas e = Σ_{p,q} e_pq e_qp`, the scalar `casVal μ` and the height
`ht μ = Σ_r r · μ_r` (`r` read as a natural number) are those of `GLReynolds` and
`GLLowestWeight`. For `q < p`, the lowering operator `e_pq` raises the height of a weight by
`p - q > 0` (`ht_shift`).

## Results

### Filtration kill

* `aeval_cas_apply_comm`, `isWeightVec_aeval_cas`, `isStable_ker_aeval_cas`: a polynomial
  `Q(c)` in the Casimir operator commutes with every `e_pq`. Hence it preserves weight vectors,
  and its kernel is stable.
* `cas_sub_mem`: let `G` be a stable subspace and `x` a weight vector of weight `μ` with
  `e_pq x ∈ G` for all `q < p`. Then `c x - casVal μ · x ∈ G`. The image of `x` in `V/G` is a
  lowest weight vector of the quotient family (`qFam`, `GLReynolds`), so `cas_apply_lowest`
  applies there.
* `aeval_prod_apply_eq_zero` (**filtration kill**): let `x_i` be weight vectors of weights
  `ν_i` and `J` a finite set of indices with
  `e_pq x_j ∈ span {x_i : i ∈ J, ht ν_j < ht ν_i}` for `j ∈ J` and `q < p`. Then
  `∏_{i ∈ J} (c - casVal ν_i)` kills every `x_j` (`j ∈ J`).
  Proof by strong induction on `J`. Remove an index `j₀` of minimal height. The induction
  hypothesis for `J \ {j₀}` gives that `Q'(c) = ∏_{i ≠ j₀} (c - casVal ν_i)` kills the `x_j`
  with `j ≠ j₀`. For `x_{j₀}`, every `e_pq x_{j₀}` (`q < p`) lies in the span of the `x_i` of
  larger height, so in the stable subspace `ker Q'(c)`. By `cas_sub_mem`,
  `(c - casVal ν_{j₀}) x_{j₀} ∈ ker Q'(c)`.
* `ht_sub`, `ht_zero`: `ht` is additive.

### The family on `Hom(W, V)`

* `homFam s e`: for families `s` on `W` and `e` on `V`, the family
  `φ ↦ e_pq ∘ φ - φ ∘ s_pq` on `W →ₗ[ℂ] V`. By `homFam_eq` it equals
  `famOf (BGChase.homLie (lieOf s hs) (lieOf e he))`, so it satisfies the relations
  (`isGlFamily_homFam`).
* Let `b` be a finite basis of `W` consisting of weight vectors, `b_i` of weight `μ_i`.
  `coord_apply_eq_sum` expands `b_i^*(T w)` in coordinates. `coord_diag` gives
  `b_i^*(s_pp w) = μ_i(p) · b_i^*(w)`. By `wt_eq_of_coord_ne_zero`, if `p ≠ q` and
  `b_j^*(s_pq b_i) ≠ 0`, then `μ_j = μ_i + ε_p - ε_q`.
* `xs b v₀ i = b_i^* ⊗ v₀`, that is, `w ↦ b_i^*(w) · v₀`. `homFam_xs` computes
  `homFam s e p q (xs i) = xs(e_pq v₀) i - Σ_j b_i^*(s_pq b_j) · xs j`.
  By `isWeightVec_xs`, `xs i` has weight `λ - μ_i` if `v₀` has weight `λ`.
* `homFam_xs_mem`: if `v₀` is a lowest weight vector of weight `λ` and `q < p`, then
  `homFam s e p q (xs i)` lies in the span of the `xs j` with `μ_i = μ_j + ε_p - ε_q`.
* `negIdx μ`: the indices `i` with `ht μ_i < 0`. `polR μ λ`: the polynomial
  `R = ∏_{i ∈ negIdx μ} (X - casVal (λ - μ_i))`. `ys b s e μ λ v₀ k = R(c) (xs k)`, with `c` the
  Casimir operator of `homFam s e`.
* `aeval_polR_xs`: `R(c)` kills `xs j` for every `j ∈ negIdx μ`. This is the filtration kill
  with `J = negIdx μ` and `ν_j = λ - μ_j`. The lowering operator sends `xs j` into the span of
  the `xs i` with `ht μ_i = ht μ_j - (p - q) < 0` (so `i ∈ negIdx μ`) and
  `ht (λ - μ_i) > ht (λ - μ_j)`.
* `isLowest_ys`: if `μ_k = 0`, then `ys k` is a lowest weight vector of weight `λ` for
  `homFam s e`. It has weight `λ` because `R(c)` preserves weights. For `q < p`,
  `e_pq (ys k) = R(c)(e_pq (xs k))`. Here `e_pq (xs k)` lies in the span of `xs i` with
  `ht μ_i = -(p - q) < 0`, which `R(c)` kills.

No separation of Casimir eigenvalues is needed for these statements; it enters later, when
the `ys k` are shown to be linearly independent (`GLVermaInject`).

### Maps out of a module generated by a lowest weight vector

* `prodFam e₁ e₂`: the diagonal family on `V₁ × V₂`; `isGlFamily_prodFam`.
* `exists_hom_of_lowest` (**the `Γ`-trick**): let `V₁` and `V₂` be finite-dimensional. Suppose
  `V₁ = genSub e₁ {v₀}` for a nonzero lowest weight vector `v₀` of weight `λ`, and let
  `y ∈ V₂` be a lowest weight vector of weight `λ`. Then there is a linear map
  `Φ : V₁ → V₂` commuting with the families and with `Φ v₀ = y`.
  Proof: `(v₀, y)` is a lowest weight vector of weight `λ` in `V₁ × V₂`. Let
  `Γ = genSub {(v₀, y)}` with the restricted family. By `eq_top_of_lowest` (`GLLowestWeight`),
  `Γ` is irreducible, since it has integral weights (`intWeights_resFam_genSub`) and is
  generated by `(v₀, y)` (`genSub_resFam_eq_top`). The first projection `π₁ : Γ → V₁` commutes
  with the families. Its kernel is stable and does not contain `(v₀, y)`, so it is zero. Its
  image is stable and contains `v₀`, so it is `V₁`. Set `Φ = π₂ ∘ π₁⁻¹`.
  (Irreducibility of `Γ` requires `V₂` to be finite-dimensional, which is why both modules
  are.)

### Covariants

* `exists_covariant`: let `W` and `V` be finite-dimensional, `V = genSub e {v₀}` for a nonzero
  lowest weight vector `v₀` of weight `λ`, and `y ∈ Hom(W, V)` a lowest weight vector of weight
  `λ` for `homFam s e`. Then there is a linear map `g : W → End V` with
  `g(s_pq w) = e_pq g(w) - g(w) e_pq` for all `p, q, w` and `g(w) v₀ = y(w)`.
  Apply the `Γ`-trick with `V₁ = V` and `V₂ = Hom(W, V)` and take
  `g(w)(v) = Φ(v)(w)` (`LinearMap.flip`).

The construction of lowest weight vectors in `W^* ⊗ L` by a polynomial in the Casimir operator
(a projection onto a generalized eigenspace, here made explicit by the filtration kill) is the
standard way of producing `𝔤`-maps out of modules generated by a lowest weight vector; it is
derived content written for this proof, not a quotation from a specific source.
