# The ideal of the nilpotent cone of `∏ᵢ 𝔤𝔩_{n i}(ℂ)`

Setting: `ι : Type` finite, `n : ι → ℕ`, and the polynomial ring `ℂ[X_c]`,
`c = ⟨b, p, q⟩ ∈ Coord n` (block `b`, row `p`, column `q`), with the generic blocks
`genericBlock n b` from module `Kostant.Coordinates`.

## Statement

- `charpolyIdeal n` is the ideal of `ℂ[X_c]` generated by the coefficients of `t^k`,
  `k < n i`, of the characteristic polynomials of the generic blocks `genericBlock n i`.
- `NilpotentConeIdealStatement n`: every polynomial `f` that vanishes at all tuples
  `(M i)_i` of nilpotent matrices `M i ∈ M_{n i}(ℂ)` lies in `charpolyIdeal n`. Here
  `f` is evaluated at the point `c = ⟨i, p, q⟩ ↦ M i p q`.

This is Kostant's theorem for `∏ᵢ 𝔤𝔩_{n i}`: the ideal of the nilpotent cone is generated by
the invariants without constant term (B. Kostant, *Lie group representations on polynomial
rings*, Amer. J. Math. 85 (1963)). For `𝔤𝔩_n` the basic invariants are the characteristic
polynomial coefficients. That source is not in the project literature. The proof below is
self-contained and does not rely on it.

`nilpotentConeIdeal : NilpotentConeIdealStatement n` proves it. It is
`nilpotentConeIdeal_of_charpolyLeading` applied to `charpolyLeading n`
(module `Kostant.CharpolyLeading`, by Worker 109).

## Proof (`nilpotentConeIdeal_of_charpolyLeading`)

The proof is not Kostant's. It uses a Gröbner basis argument for the monomial order `ord n` of
module `Kostant.CharpolyOrder` and Hilbert's Nullstellensatz. It assumes
`CharpolyLeadingStatement n`: for `k < n b`, the coefficient of `t^k` of the characteristic
polynomial of block `b` has leading monomial `antidiag n b k` and a nonzero leading
coefficient.

1. **Zero locus.** `isNilpotent_of_charpoly_coeff`: a square complex matrix `A` of size `N`
   whose characteristic polynomial has vanishing coefficients of `t^k` for all `k < N` is
   nilpotent. The characteristic polynomial is monic of degree `N`, hence equal to `t^N`,
   and Cayley–Hamilton (`Matrix.aeval_self_charpoly`) gives `A^N = 0`.
   `isNilpotent_of_mem_zeroLocus`: if `x` is a zero of `charpolyIdeal n`, then every block
   `(x ⟨i, p, q⟩)_{p,q}` is nilpotent. Evaluating `genericBlock n i` at `x` gives this block,
   and evaluation commutes with taking the characteristic polynomial (`Matrix.charpoly_map`).
2. **Normalized generators.** `Gen n` indexes the pairs `⟨b, k⟩` with `k < n b`.
   `charCoeff n ⟨b, k⟩` is the coefficient of `t^k` of the characteristic polynomial of
   block `b`. `gen n j` is `charCoeff n j` divided by its leading coefficient (nonzero by
   `CharpolyLeadingStatement`, `leadingCoeff_charCoeff_ne_zero`).
   - `degree_gen`: the leading monomial of `gen n ⟨b, k⟩` is `antidiag n b k`.
   - `monic_gen`: `gen n j` is monic for `ord n`.
   - `degree_gen_disjoint`: for `j ≠ j'` the leading monomials have disjoint supports. A
     variable `⟨b, p, q⟩` occurs in `antidiag n b k` only if `p + q = n b - 1 + k`, which
     determines `k` from the variable.
   - `degree_gen_le_one`: the leading monomials are squarefree.
   - `charpolyIdeal_eq_span_gen`: `charpolyIdeal n` is the ideal generated by the `gen n j`,
     since each generator is a nonzero scalar multiple of the other.
3. **Radicality.** `isRadical_charpolyIdeal`: `charpolyIdeal n` is radical, by
   `CoprimeGroebner.isRadical` (monic generators with pairwise disjoint squarefree leading
   monomials generate a radical ideal).
4. **Nullstellensatz.** Let `f` vanish at all tuples of nilpotent matrices. By step 1 it
   vanishes on the zero locus of `charpolyIdeal n`, so it lies in the vanishing ideal of
   that zero locus. `Coord n` is finite, so Mathlib's Nullstellensatz
   (`MvPolynomial.vanishingIdeal_zeroLocus_eq_radical`) identifies this ideal with the
   radical of `charpolyIdeal n`. By step 3 this is `charpolyIdeal n`.

## Provenance

The statement is Kostant's (1963), in the form used by Worker 381 for Task 6748. The proof route (squarefree initial ideal, so radical;
then the Nullstellensatz) is the standard one for `𝔤𝔩_n`. Its leading-term input is
`CharpolyLeadingStatement` (module `Kostant.CharpolyOrder`). Its Gröbner input is
Buchberger's first criterion and Cox–Little–O'Shea, *Ideals, Varieties, and Algorithms*,
Ch. 2 §9 (module `Kostant.CoprimeGroebner`).
