# Topological irreducibility of the genuine smooth action

A jointly continuous topologically irreducible complex Banach representation of the units of a finite-dimensional real Banach algebra has a topologically irreducible genuine smooth-vector action. The subspaces tested for closedness are in the genuine word-derivative topology. The result applies to the actual real and complex general linear groups with their real matrix charts.

The empty coordinate embeds the smooth carrier into the Banach space with dense range. For a closed invariant submodule of the smooth action, close its image in the Banach norm. This gives an invariant closed submodule for the original action. If the closure is zero, injectivity gives a zero smooth submodule. If the closure is the entire Banach space, compact smooth averaging sends every original vector into the smooth submodule. Indeed, averaging is continuous from the Banach norm into the genuine derivative topology and preserves the smooth submodule on its original vectors, and the submodule is closed in that topology.

For a prescribed finite family of word coordinates, take a nonnegative normalized Haar kernel supported where the translated coordinate tuple of a given vector is close to the original tuple. The Bochner formula for all coordinates bounds the norm of the error in the finite product by half the prescribed error. Finite projections test closure in the genuine topology, so the averaged outputs approximate every smooth vector there. This gives the entire smooth carrier in the second case. Norm density also transfers nontriviality. The proof uses the published normalized bump and the full-group averaging construction.

The top case is also available as `eq_top_of_invariant_of_normDense`. It takes norm density of the empty-coordinate image of the closed invariant smooth submodule as a hypothesis and concludes that the submodule is the entire smooth carrier. The proof uses the same finite-coordinate approximation by compact smooth averaging.
