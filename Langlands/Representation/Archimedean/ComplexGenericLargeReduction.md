# Generic `(𝔤𝔩_n(ℂ), U(n))`-modules are large, given Kostant's theorem for `𝔤𝔩_n(ℂ)²`

Lean module: `Langlands.Representation.Archimedean.ComplexGenericLargeReduction`, namespace
`Langlands.Archimedean.ComplexGenericLarge`.

## Statement

`ComplexGenericLargeReductionStatement.{uV}` is the implication

> `GLProductWhittakerMaximallyFaithfulStatement.{uV} → ComplexGenericLargeStatement.{uV}`.

* `GLProductWhittakerMaximallyFaithfulStatement` (module `GLProductWhittaker`) is Kostant's
  theorem (Kostant 1978, Theorem 3.9 = Theorem D) for a finite product `∏ᵢ 𝔤𝔩_{nᵢ}(ℂ)`. Take
  a Lie algebra map `π` with an infinitesimal character, a nonzero functional `ℓ`, and
  coefficients `a i p` with `a i p ≠ 0` for `p + 1 < nᵢ`. Suppose
  `ℓ(π(X) v) = (∑ᵢ ∑ₚ a i p · X i p (p+1)) ℓ(v)` for all blockwise strictly upper triangular
  `X` and all `v`. Then `π` is maximally faithful.
* `ComplexGenericLargeStatement` (module `ComplexGenericStandardInjectiveReduction`) says
  that every irreducible admissible generic `(𝔤𝔩_n(ℂ), U(n))`-module `(ρ, π)` is large
  (`IsComplexLargeGKModule`). Here `π` is an action of `ℂ ⊗_ℝ 𝔤𝔩_n(ℂ)`.

So the statement says: Kostant's theorem for finite products of `𝔤𝔩_n(ℂ)`, applied with two
factors, implies that generic modules are large. It is an internal reduction step, not a
result from the literature. The theorem `complexGenericLargeReduction` proves it. It uses
only the axioms `propext`, `Classical.choice` and `Quot.sound`.

The proof has three ingredients. The first is the isomorphism of complex Lie algebras
`ℂ ⊗_ℝ 𝔤𝔩_n(ℂ) ≅ 𝔤𝔩_n(ℂ) × 𝔤𝔩_n(ℂ)`. The second is the infinitesimal character of the
transported action. The third is the transport of the Whittaker identity. The
infinitesimal-character argument adapts the proof of
`RealGKModuleInfinitesimalCharacterStatement` in `RealGKInfinitesimalCharacter`
(Worker 379), whose generic lemmas this module imports: `envInduction`, `envMap`,
`envMap_fixes_center`, `unitConj`, `idempotent_unit_mul` and
`exists_eigenvector_of_commute`.

Throughout, `attribute [local instance 100] LieRing.ofAssociativeRing` makes the bracket of
`Fin 2 → M_n(ℂ)` the commutator of the product ring, as in `GLProductWhittaker`.

## 1. Transport along a Lie algebra isomorphism (section `Transport`)

* The algebra isomorphism `U(L) ≃ U(L')` induced by a Lie algebra isomorphism `e : L ≃ L'`
  is the Library's `Langlands.Algebra.LieEnvelopingEquiv.enveloping e`.
* `envelopingAction_enveloping`: `(π ∘ e⁻¹)(enveloping e u) = π(u)` for `u ∈ U(L)`, by the
  uniqueness part of the universal property.
* `isMaximallyFaithful_of_comp`: if `π ∘ e⁻¹` is maximally faithful, so is `π`. Let `u`
  act by `0` under `π`. Then `enveloping e u` acts by `0` under `π ∘ e⁻¹`. So it lies in the
  left ideal generated by the central elements of `U(L')` acting by `0`. The inverse
  isomorphism maps the center onto the center. It maps central elements acting by `0`
  to central elements acting by `0`, and left ideals to left ideals. This is an induction
  on `Submodule.span`.

## 2. The isomorphism `ℂ ⊗_ℝ 𝔤𝔩_n(ℂ) ≅ 𝔤𝔩_n(ℂ)²` (section `Iso`)

* `conjMat X` is the entrywise complex conjugate `X̄`.
* `pairHom : M_n(ℂ) → M_n(ℂ)²`, `X ↦ (X, X̄)`, is an `ℝ`-algebra map.
* `prodFinTwo : A × A ≃ (Fin 2 → A)` (for `A = M_n(ℂ)`) is the evident `ℂ`-algebra
  isomorphism, from Mathlib's `RingEquiv.piFinTwo`.
* `tensorEquiv : ℂ ⊗_ℝ M_n(ℂ) ≃ M_n(ℂ)²` is the Library's algebra isomorphism
  `Langlands.Shimura.ComplexMatrix.complexEquiv n` (module
  `Langlands.Shimura.Unitary.ComplexMatrixSplitting`) followed by `prodFinTwo`.
  `tensorEquiv_tmul`: it maps `z ⊗ X` to `z · pairHom X = (zX, zX̄)`, by the Library's
  `complexEquiv_tmul`. Here `ℂ` acts on the left factor.
* `tensorInv (A, B) = ½ (1 ⊗ A - i ⊗ iA + 1 ⊗ B̄ + i ⊗ iB̄)` is mapped to `(A, B)`
  (`tensorEquiv_tensorInv`, a computation with `tensorEquiv_tmul`). So the inverse of
  `tensorEquiv` is `tensorInv` (`tensorEquiv_symm_apply`), and it maps `(X, X̄)` to `1 ⊗ X`
  (`tensorEquiv_symm_pairHom`).
* `lie_eq_mul_sub`: the Library's bracket on `ℂ ⊗_ℝ 𝔤𝔩_n(ℂ)`
  (`LieAlgebra.ExtendScalars`) is the commutator of the algebra `ℂ ⊗_ℝ M_n(ℂ)`. On pure
  tensors, `[a ⊗ X, b ⊗ Y] = ab ⊗ (XY - YX)`.
* `tensorLieEquiv` is `tensorEquiv` as an isomorphism of complex Lie algebras. An algebra
  isomorphism preserves commutators.

## 3. Conjugation fixes the center of `U(𝔤𝔩_n(ℂ)²)` (sections `Assoc`, `Matrices`)

Let `B = M_n(ℂ)²`, with the commutator bracket.

* `conjLieB w` is conjugation `x ↦ w x w⁻¹` by a unit `w` of `B`, as a Lie algebra map.
* `envMap_conjLieB_idempotent`: let `e ∈ B` be idempotent, `c ≠ 0` and
  `w = 1 + (c - 1) e`. Then the induced map `envMap (conjLieB w)` of `U(B)` fixes every
  central element. Proof: `B` is the sum of the four Peirce pieces `e B e`, `e B (1-e)`,
  `(1-e) B e` and `(1-e) B (1-e)`. On these, `ad e` acts by `0, 1, -1, 0` and `Ad w` by
  `1, c, c⁻¹, 1`. So `envMap_fixes_center` applies.
* `ConjFixesCenter M` says: conjugation by every unit with underlying element `M` fixes the
  center of `U(B)`. It is stable under products with an invertible left factor
  (`conjFixesCenter_mul`), and holds for `1 + (c - 1) e` (`conjFixesCenter_idempotent`).
* `PairFixesCenter X` is `ConjFixesCenter (X, X̄)`, for `X ∈ M_n(ℂ)`.
  `pairHom_idempotent` writes `(1 + (d - 1)E, conj(1 + (d - 1)E))` as the product of
  `1 + (d - 1)(E, 0)` and `1 + (d̄ - 1)(0, Ē)`. If `E` is idempotent, both factors have
  idempotent `e` (`pairFixesCenter_idempotent`).
* Invertible diagonal matrices are products of matrices `1 + (d - 1) E_ii`
  (`pairFixesCenter_diagonal`). For `i ≠ j`, let `E = E_jj` and `Q = E_jj - (c/2) E_ij`.
  Both are idempotent, and the transvection `1 + c E_ij` equals `(1 - 2E)(1 - 2Q)`
  (`pairFixesCenter_transvection`).
* `pairFixesCenter_of_det_ne_zero`: Mathlib's induction
  `Matrix.diagonal_transvection_induction_of_det_ne_zero` now shows that `PairFixesCenter X`
  holds for every invertible complex matrix `X`.

## 4. Compatibility with `U(n)` (section `Compat`)

* `pairUnit k` is the unit `(k, k̄)` of `B`, for `k ∈ U(n)`. Its inverse is
  `(k*, conj k*)` (`pairUnit_inv_val`).
* `prodAction π = π ∘ tensorLieEquiv⁻¹` is the action of `𝔤𝔩_n(ℂ)²` on `V`.
* `rho_mul_pi`: the last clause of `IsComplexGKModule` is
  `ρ(k) π(1 ⊗ X) = π(1 ⊗ kXk*) ρ(k)`. By complex linearity and tensor induction it gives
  `ρ(k) π(x) = π((1 ⊗ k) x (1 ⊗ k*)) ρ(k)` for all `x ∈ ℂ ⊗_ℝ M_n(ℂ)`.
* `rho_mul_envelopingAction_prod`: `ρ(k) πB(u) = πB(Ad(k, k̄) u) ρ(k)` for all
  `u ∈ U(B)`, by induction on `u` (`envInduction`). On generators, `tensorEquiv⁻¹` maps
  `(k, k̄) Y (k, k̄)⁻¹` to `(1 ⊗ k) tensorEquiv⁻¹(Y) (1 ⊗ k*)`.

## 5. The infinitesimal character (section `InfChar`)

`prodAction_hasInfinitesimalCharacter`: let `(ρ, π)` be an irreducible admissible
`(𝔤𝔩_n(ℂ), U(n))`-module. Then `prodAction π` has an infinitesimal character. Let `z` be
central in `U(B)` and let `Z` be its action.

* `Z` commutes with every `π(x)`, because `π(x)` is the action of `ι(tensorEquiv x)` and `z`
  is central.
* `Z` commutes with every `ρ(k)`. By step 4, `ρ(k) Z` is the action of `Ad(k, k̄) z`
  composed with `ρ(k)`. By step 3, applied to `k` (`det k ≠ 0`), `Ad(k, k̄) z = z`.
* `exists_eigenvector_of_commute` uses `K`-finiteness, continuity and admissibility. It
  gives an eigenvector of `Z`, with eigenvalue `c`. The eigenspace `ker(Z - c)` is stable
  under `ρ` and `π`, and it is nonzero. By irreducibility it is all of `V`, so `Z = c`.

## 6. The Whittaker identity (section `Whittaker`)

* `whitSum a X = ∑ₚ a_p X_{p,p+1}` (the superdiagonal sum `∑ₚ ∑_q [q = p + 1] a_p X_{p,q}`,
  with the natural-number test `(q : ℕ) = p + 1`). It satisfies
  `whitSum a (cX) = c · whitSum a X` (`whitSum_smul`),
  `whitSum a X̄ = conj(whitSum ā X)` (`whitSum_conj`) and
  `whitSum (c a) X = c · whitSum a X` (`whitSum_const_mul`).
* `prodAction_whittaker`: suppose `ℓ(π(1 ⊗ X) v) = i Re(whitSum a X) ℓ(v)` for all strictly
  upper triangular `X`. This is the identity in `IsComplexGenericGKModule`. Then for
  blockwise strictly upper triangular `(Y₀, Y₁)`,
  `ℓ(πB(Y₀, Y₁) v) = ((i/2) whitSum a Y₀ + (i/2) whitSum ā Y₁) ℓ(v)`.
  Proof: `πB(Y₀, Y₁) = ½(π(1 ⊗ Y₀) - i π(1 ⊗ iY₀) + π(1 ⊗ Ȳ₁) + i π(1 ⊗ iȲ₁))`, by
  `tensorInv`. The four matrices `Y₀, iY₀, Ȳ₁, iȲ₁` are strictly upper triangular. Write
  `s₀ = whitSum a Y₀` and `s₁ = whitSum ā Y₁`, so `whitSum a Ȳ₁ = s̄₁`. The hypothesis gives
  the coefficient `½ i (Re s₀ - i Re(i s₀) + Re s̄₁ + i Re(i s̄₁))`. Since
  `Re(iw) = -Im w`, this equals `½ i (s₀ + s₁)`. The last step compares real and imaginary
  parts.

## 7. The reduction

`complexGenericLargeReduction`: assume Kostant's theorem `hK`, and let `(ρ, π)` be an
irreducible admissible generic module, with functional `ℓ ≠ 0` and coefficients `a`.
Apply `hK` with `ι = Fin 2`, `n = (n, n)` and the action `prodAction π`. Its hypotheses
hold:

* `prodAction π` has an infinitesimal character (step 5);
* the coefficients are `a' 0 p = (i/2) a_p` and `a' 1 p = (i/2) ā_p`. They are nonzero
  whenever `a_p ≠ 0`, that is, whenever `p + 1 < n`;
* the Whittaker identity holds, by step 6 and `Fin.sum_univ_two`.

So `prodAction π = π ∘ tensorLieEquiv⁻¹` is maximally faithful. By step 1, `π` is
maximally faithful. `IsMaximallyFaithful π` is by definition the same proposition as
`IsComplexLargeGKModule n π`: `Ideal.span` is `Submodule.span` over the ring itself.

## Provenance

The statement is an internal reduction step, and every step of its proof is internal. The
identification `ℂ ⊗_ℝ 𝔤𝔩_n(ℂ) ≅ 𝔤𝔩_n(ℂ) × 𝔤𝔩_n(ℂ)`, `z ⊗ X ↦ (zX, zX̄)`, is the standard
description of the complexification of a complex Lie algebra viewed as a real one. The
Whittaker coefficients `(i/2) a_p` and `(i/2) ā_p` are computed in step 6. The
infinitesimal-character argument adapts Worker 379's proof for `(𝔤𝔩_n(ℝ), O(n))`-modules.
That proof is accepted, and this module imports its generic lemmas.
