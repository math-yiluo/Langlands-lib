# Splitting Vogan's irreducibility statement for `GL_n(ℂ)` along the linkage criterion

Lean module: `Langlands.Representation.Archimedean.ComplexLargeStandardIrreducibleSplit`.

This module splits `Langlands.Archimedean.ComplexLargeStandardIrreducibleStatement` (statement V
of `ComplexLargeStandardIrreducibleReduction.md`) into two statements. It proves the implication
`complexLargeStandardIrreducibleSplit` (the two statements imply V). No hypothesis is added to V.
The two statements are only stated here and still need proofs. The split is the complex
counterpart of `RealLargeStandardIrreducibleReduction`, which splits the real statement
`RealLargeStandardIrreducibleStatement` along Speh's criterion in the same way.

All declarations are in the namespace `Langlands.Archimedean`.

Notation as in `ComplexLargeStandardIrreducibleReduction.md`: for a list `ds` of complex
characters (`ComplexCharacterData`, the character `z ↦ (z/|z|)^l |z|^{2t}` of `ℂˣ` with weight
`l ∈ ℤ` and exponent `t ∈ ℂ`, `|z|` the usual absolute value), `I(ds)` is the
`(𝔤𝔩_n(ℂ), U(n))`-module `(complexStandardRep n ds, complexStandardLie n ds)` on the
`U(n)`-finite functions of `complexStandardModule n ds`. `IsGKSubmodule`,
`IsMaximallyFaithfulModulo` and `IsIrreducibleGKModule` are the Library's definitions (modules
`RealLargeLanglandsQuotientReduction` and `GKModule`). `IsComplexLanglandsOrdered ds` says
`Re t₁ ≥ ⋯ ≥ Re t_n`.

## Linked characters

Tadić 2009, §3 (p. 11), parametrizes the characters of `ℂˣ` in two ways. A character is
`δ(β, n)(z) = |z|^{2β} (z/|z|)^n` for unique `β ∈ ℂ` and `n ∈ ℤ`, "`| |` the usual absolute
value on `ℂ`". For the unique `x, y ∈ ℂ` with `x + y = 2β` and `x - y = n` he writes
`δ(β, n) = γ(x, y)`, so that `γ(x, y)(z) = |z|^{x+y} (z/|z|)^{x-y}`. The Library's character
with weight `l` and exponent `t` (`ComplexCharacterData.character_eq`) is `δ(t, l)`, that is,
`γ(x, y)` with

  `x = t + l/2`,  `y = t - l/2`.

Tadić calls `δ₁, δ₂` linked if `δ₁ × δ₂` is reducible, and states (p. 11, after [JL]) that this
holds if and only if there are `p, q ∈ ℤ` with `p q > 0` and `(δ₁ δ₂⁻¹)(z) = z^p z̄^q = γ(p, q)(z)`;
in the second parametrization, `γ(x₁, y₁)` and `γ(x₂, y₂)` are linked if and only if
`x₁ - x₂ ∈ ℤ` and `(x₁ - x₂)(y₁ - y₂) > 0`.

Definitions in Lean:

* `IsComplexLinked d e`: there are integers `p, q` with `0 < p * q`,
  `(t_d + l_d/2) - (t_e + l_e/2) = p` and `(t_d - l_d/2) - (t_e - l_e/2) = q`. With
  `x = t + l/2`, `y = t - l/2` this says `x_d - x_e = p` and `y_d - y_e = q`, which is Tadić's
  condition: `x_d - x_e ∈ ℤ` forces `y_d - y_e = (x_d - x_e) - (l_d - l_e) ∈ ℤ`, and conversely.
  The quotient character `δ_d δ_e⁻¹` is `γ(p, q)`. The relation is symmetric (replace `p, q` by
  `-p, -q`), and a character is never linked to itself (`p = q = 0`).
* `IsComplexUnlinked ds`: `ds.Pairwise fun d e => ¬ IsComplexLinked d e`. No two entries of the
  list at distinct positions are linked; by symmetry the order within a pair does not matter.

### The criterion

Let `a = (δ₁, …, δ_n)` be written in standard order, `e(δ₁) ≥ ⋯ ≥ e(δ_n)` with
`e(δ(β, n)) = Re β` (Tadić, pp. 7, 11, 12). For the Library's characters `e = Re t`, so the
standard order is `IsComplexLanglandsOrdered`. Tadić writes `λ(a) = δ₁ × ⋯ × δ_n` (the
Harish-Chandra module parabolically induced from the block upper triangular parabolic subgroup,
p. 5; here the Borel subgroup) and `L(a)` for its unique irreducible quotient (p. 7). He defines `c ≺ a` when `c` is obtained from `a` by replacing a
linked pair `γ(x_i, y_i), γ(x_j, y_j)` by `γ(x_i, y_j), γ(x_j, y_i)`, and `≤` as the order
generated by `≺` (p. 11). Then:

* Lemma 3.2 (pp. 12–13): `λ(a)` contains `L(b)` as a subquotient if and only if `b ≤ a`;
* Lemma 3.3 (i) (p. 13): in the Grothendieck group, `λ(a) = Σ_{b ≤ a} m^a_b L(b)` with
  `m^a_a = 1`;
* Lemma 3.1 (iii) (p. 12): `b < a` implies `e(b) < e(a)`, so `b ≠ a`.

If no two members of `a` are linked, there is no `c ≺ a`, so `b ≤ a` only for `b = a`, and
`λ(a) = L(a)` is irreducible. If two members are linked, there is `c ≺ a`, so `λ(a)` contains
both `L(a)` and `L(c)` with `c ≠ a` (`a ↦ L(a)` is a bijection, p. 7), and `λ(a)` is reducible.
So `λ(a)` is irreducible if and only if no two members of `a` are linked. The source states
the two-character case on p. 11 and the lemmas above; the `n`-character criterion is this short
derivation from them.

## The two statements

1. `ComplexStandardIrreducibleCriterionStatement` (Sp_C). Let `ds` be a list of complex
   characters with `ds.length = n`, `IsComplexLanglandsOrdered ds` and `IsComplexUnlinked ds`.
   Then `IsIrreducibleGKModule (complexStandardRep n ds) (complexStandardLie n ds)`: `I(ds)` is
   nonzero and has no `(𝔤, K)`-submodules other than `0` and itself.

   Source: the "if" direction of the criterion above (Tadić 2009, p. 11 and Lemmas 3.2–3.3).
   The criterion holds for lists in any order (irreducibility of a principal series does not
   depend on the order of its characters: `σ₁ × σ₂ = σ₂ × σ₁` in the Grothendieck group,
   Tadić 2009, (1-2) on p. 6, and Badulescu–Renard 2010, Remark 2.3 and Proposition 2.4). Sp_C keeps only the Langlands-ordered case, which is all the reduction
   needs, so Sp_C is weaker than the source.

2. `ComplexLinkedLanglandsQuotientNotLargeStatement` (NL_C). Let `ds` be a list of complex
   characters with `ds.length = n` and `IsComplexLanglandsOrdered ds`, and suppose
   `¬ IsComplexUnlinked ds`, so two of the characters are linked. Let `R` be a largest proper
   `(𝔤, K)`-submodule of `I(ds)`, with the same three properties as in V. Then
   `¬ IsMaximallyFaithfulModulo (complexStandardLie n ds) R`: the Langlands quotient
   `I(ds) / R` is not maximally faithful.

   Why NL_C is true. By the "only if" direction of the criterion, `I(ds)` is reducible. The
   irreducible quotient `I(ds) / R` is the Langlands quotient `L(a)` of `a = ds`
   (`ComplexLargeStandardIrreducibleReduction.md`, statement L, and the conventions of
   `ComplexLanglandsQuotientReduction.md`: in the Library's left-equivariant model the quotient
   form of the classification uses non-increasing real parts). Vogan 1978, Theorem 6.2 (p. 89),
   (a) ⇒ (f), with its proof (p. 93), says that the standard module of a large irreducible
   Langlands quotient is irreducible. `ComplexLargeStandardIrreducibleReduction.md` explains why
   maximal faithfulness of the irreducible quotient `I(ds) / R` is Vogan's "large", and why
   irreducibility of Vogan's standard module of `I(ds) / R` amounts to irreducibility of
   `I(ds)` (Knapp 1994, Theorem 4(c); Badulescu–Renard 2010, Proposition 2.4). So `I(ds) / R` is
   not maximally faithful. NL_C is thus Vogan's theorem restricted to reducible standard
   modules, with reducibility detected by the linkage criterion. A proof of NL_C needs a route
   that does not go through V itself.

As with V, proofs of Sp_C and NL_C must identify the Library's `complexStandardModule n ds` and
its `U(n)`-finite part with the classical principal series `λ(a)` and its Harish-Chandra module,
as described in `ComplexLargeStandardIrreducibleReduction.md`.

## The reduction

`ComplexLargeStandardIrreducibleSplitStatement` is the implication Sp_C → NL_C → V. The theorem
`complexLargeStandardIrreducibleSplit` proves it. It assumes the hypotheses of V for `n`, `ds`
and `R`, including `IsMaximallyFaithfulModulo (complexStandardLie n ds) R`, and splits on
`IsComplexUnlinked ds`:

* If it holds, Sp_C makes `I(ds)` irreducible. `R` is stable under `complexStandardRep` and
  `complexStandardLie` (the two halves of `IsGKSubmodule`), so the second half of
  `IsIrreducibleGKModule` gives `R = ⊥` or `R = ⊤`. Since `R ≠ ⊤`, `R = ⊥`.
* If it fails, NL_C says that `R` is not maximally faithful, which contradicts the hypothesis.

The proof uses neither the maximality of `R` nor the ordering, beyond passing them to Sp_C and
NL_C.

## Provenance

Derived. `IsComplexLinked` and `IsComplexUnlinked` transcribe Tadić's linkage condition
(p. 11) in his parameters `x = t + l/2`, `y = t - l/2`, translated to the Library's
`ComplexCharacterData` as above. Sp_C is the "if" direction of the irreducibility criterion
derived above from Tadić's p. 11 and Lemmas 3.1–3.3, restricted to Langlands-ordered lists.
NL_C combines the "only if" direction of that criterion with Vogan 1978, Theorem 6.2,
(a) ⇒ (f). The split into Sp_C and NL_C and the proof of the implication are internal to this
module.
