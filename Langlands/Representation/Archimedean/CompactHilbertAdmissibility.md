# Compact admissibility and recovery in a dense completion

Let a second-countable compact topological group act isometrically and jointly
continuously on a complete normed complex space. Suppose an algebraic
compact-finite admissible representation embeds equivariantly with dense image.
`compactIntertwinerMap_bijective` identifies every intertwiner from a continuous
finite-dimensional matrix representation to the completion with a unique
intertwiner to the original carrier. `CompactHilbertAdmissibilityStatement`
asserts admissibility of the completed action and identifies its compact-finite
vectors with the image of the original carrier.

The proof averages a linear map from a finite-dimensional matrix
representation by conjugation with probability Haar measure. The average is
an intertwiner and fixes intertwiners. On columns from the algebraic carrier,
all conjugate values lie in the image of a finite sum of original orbit spans.
This subspace is finite-dimensional and closed, so the average still takes
values in the carrier. For each test vector the average depends continuously
on the columns by the bounded scalar-weighted orbit integrals. The image of
the space of original intertwiners in the column space is finite-dimensional
by admissibility and therefore closed. Column density puts the average of
every map in that image, including each map fixed by averaging.

Surjectivity on intertwiners transfers the finite-dimensionality in the
definition of admissibility to the completion. For a compact-finite vector in
the completion, the orbit span carries a continuous matrix representation in
its chosen basis. Each coordinate is continuous by finite-dimensionality and
continuity of the orbit maps. Its inclusion is an intertwiner, so it lifts to
the original carrier and gives a preimage for the original vector. Equivariance
ensures that all images of original vectors are compact-finite.

This internal result uses the Library's compact probability Haar measure,
weighted isometric integral, algebraic compact finiteness and admissibility.
It applies to the compact subgroup of a local Hilbert realization once stability
of that realization under the local group has been established.
