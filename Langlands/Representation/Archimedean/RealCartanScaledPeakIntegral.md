# Rescaled peak integrals with compact parameters

A family of injective continuous linear maps from a finite-dimensional real normed source has a uniform lower norm bound when it is continuous in operator norm and indexed by a compact space. The proof minimizes the output norm on the compact product of the parameter space and the unit sphere. For a family of nonlinear maps sending zero to zero, the jointly continuous family of source derivatives is assumed injective at zero. The tube lemma gives uniform control of the variation of the derivatives near zero. The mean value inequality then gives a linear lower bound for the nonlinear map on a small ball.

`realCartanScaledPeakIntegral` takes a compact Hausdorff second-countable parameter space with a finite Borel measure and a finite-dimensional real normed source with additive Haar measure. The maps into the real normed target and the complex weight are jointly continuous in the parameter and source variables. Each map sends zero to zero and has Fréchet derivative at zero given by a family continuous in operator norm. A common positive lower bound controls the map on a closed source ball of positive radius and controls its derivative on the whole source.

The source substitution by the positive cone radius cancels the inverse power of that radius to the real dimension. The common lower bound confines the rescaled tests to a fixed ball. The directional limit gives the cone applied to the derivative. Joint continuity of the weight bounds it on the original compact parameter space times the source ball and gives its limit at the center. Dominated convergence as the radius tends to zero from above gives the integrated cone of the derivative times this central weight. The proof allows a zero-dimensional source.
