# Exponent shape of intertwinings between complex principal series

Worker 729 wrote this module for Task 7055 (`fbfc1fa1-a863-4424-800e-92f30996a1be`), whose
target is the Library's `Langlands.Archimedean.ComplexLanglandsQuotientUniqueStatement`. The
module states one definition and one statement (E). It proves nothing. All declarations are in
the namespace `Langlands.Archimedean`. It imports only
`Langlands.Representation.Archimedean.PrincipalSeriesGKModule`.

The Library names `ComplexCharacterData`, `kFiniteFunctions`, `complexStandardModule`,
`unitaryCompact`, `complexStandardRep`, `complexStandardLie` and
`IsComplexStandardIntertwining` are described in `StandardModule.md`,
`PrincipalSeriesGKModule.md` and `ComplexGenericParameterReduction.md`. In short:

* a `ComplexCharacterData` `d` is the character `z ↦ (z/‖z‖)^{l} ‖z‖^{2t}` of `ℂˣ` with
  weight `l = d.weight : ℤ` and exponent `t = d.exponent : ℂ` (Knapp's `[z]^l |z|_ℂ^t`,
  `knapp-1994-archimedean-llc`, (4.1), p. 405);
* for a list `ds` of `n` such characters, `X(ds)` below is
  `kFiniteFunctions (complexStandardModule n ds) (unitaryCompact ℂ n)`, the `U(n)`-finite
  functions `f` on `GL_n(ℂ)` with `f(diag(a) u g) = ∏ᵢ χᵢ(aᵢ) |aᵢ|_ℂ^{(n-1)/2 - i} f(g)`
  (upper triangular unipotent `u`, `0`-indexed `i`). This is normalized induction from the upper
  triangular Borel subgroup `B`, with `GL_n(ℂ)` acting by right translation;
* `complexStandardRep n cs` and `complexStandardLie n cs` are right translation by `U(n)` and
  the right-derivative action of `ℂ ⊗_ℝ 𝔤𝔩_n(ℂ)` on `X(cs)`;
* `IsComplexStandardIntertwining n (complexStandardRep n cs) (complexStandardLie n cs) ds T`
  says that the linear map `T : X(ds) → X(cs)` commutes with right translation by every
  `k ∈ U(n)` and with the right derivative along every real direction `X ∈ 𝔤𝔩_n(ℂ)`. So `T` is
  a `(𝔤, K)`-module map.

For `d : Fin n → ComplexCharacterData`, `List.ofFn d` is the list `[d 0, …, d (n-1)]`.

## Definition: `IsNonnegSimpleRootCombination`

For `u : Fin n → ℤ`, `IsNonnegSimpleRootCombination u` is the conjunction of

1. for every `k : ℕ`, `0 ≤ ∑_{j : Fin n, j < k} u j` (every prefix sum is nonnegative), and
2. `∑_j u j = 0`.

**Meaning.** These two conditions say exactly that `u = ∑_{k=0}^{n-2} m_k (e_k - e_{k+1})`
with integers `m_k ≥ 0`, i.e. that `u` is a nonnegative integer combination of the simple roots
`e_k - e_{k+1}` of `𝔤𝔩_n` for the upper triangular Borel subgroup. Indeed, writing `u` in that
form forces `m_k = u_0 + ⋯ + u_k` (the prefix sum through `k`). For `k ≥ n` the prefix sum in
clause 1 is the total sum, which clause 2 makes zero. Every positive root `e_i - e_j` (`i < j`)
is such a combination, so the set is the monoid generated by the positive roots.

**Provenance.** Internal: a standard notion (the positive cone `ℕ·Δ` of the root lattice),
written in prefix-sum form so that the reduction in the follow-up module can compare prefix sums
directly.

## Statement E: `ComplexStandardHomExponentStatement`

For every `n : ℕ`, all `d c : Fin n → ComplexCharacterData` and every `ℂ`-linear map
`T : X(List.ofFn d) → X(List.ofFn c)` such that `T ≠ 0` and
`IsComplexStandardIntertwining n (complexStandardRep n (List.ofFn c))
(complexStandardLie n (List.ofFn c)) (List.ofFn d) T`, there are a permutation
`σ : Equiv.Perm (Fin n)` and `u v : Fin n → ℤ` with `IsNonnegSimpleRootCombination u`,
`IsNonnegSimpleRootCombination v`, and for every `j : Fin n`

* `(c j).exponent = (d (σ j)).exponent + ((u j + v j : ℤ) : ℂ) / 2`, and
* `(c j).weight = (d (σ j)).weight + (u j - v j)`.

**Meaning.** Write a character as `z ↦ z^p z̄^q` with `p = t + l/2` and `q = t - l/2` (so
`p - q = l ∈ ℤ` and `p + q = 2t`; here `z^p z̄^q` means `‖z‖^{2t} (z/‖z‖)^l`). The two
equations say `p(c_j) = p(d_{σ j}) + u_j` and `q(c_j) = q(d_{σ j}) + v_j`. So: if there is a
nonzero `(𝔤, K)`-map `X(d) → X(c)`, then `c` is obtained from `d` by one permutation `σ`,
followed by a shift of the holomorphic coordinates `p` by a nonnegative combination `u` of
simple roots and an independent shift of the antiholomorphic coordinates `q` by a nonnegative
combination `v`. The permutation is the same for both coordinates. The statement gives a
necessary condition only; it does not say which such `c` occur.

**Provenance.** Derived. E is an intermediate statement of the uniqueness proof for Task 7055
and no single cited source states it in this form. It is the complex-group form of the classical
description of the exponents of a principal series. The standard argument runs as follows.

1. *Frobenius reciprocity.* A `(𝔤, K)`-map `T : V → X(c)` is determined by the functional
   `λ(v) = (T v)(1)`, which is `𝔫`-invariant and transforms under the diagonal torus by the
   character `c·δ_B^{1/2}`. So `c·δ_B^{1/2}` is a weight of the torus on `V/𝔫V`, an exponent of
   `V` in the sense of Wallach (`wallach-1979-representations-reductive-lie-groups`: 4.12
   (evaluation at `1` kills `𝔫V`), 4.16–4.17 (the map `C` built from a quotient of `V/𝔫V`)
   pp. 77–78 (PDF 80–81); 5.4 (exponents `E(P, V)` as weights on `V/𝔫V`), p. 78 (PDF 81)).
   Wallach uses left translation and `f(kp) = δ_P(p)^{-1/2} σ(p)^{-1} f(k)`. The map
   `f ↦ (g ↦ f(g⁻¹))` turns this into the Library's right-translation model.
2. *Bruhat filtration.* For `V = X(d)`, after extending `T` to smooth vectors, such
   functionals are filtered by the support of the corresponding `B`-equivariant distribution
   on the double cosets `B w B`. On the cell of `w`
   the functional is a transversal derivative of the delta distribution of the cell, of some
   finite order. The torus acts on the transversal derivatives through positive roots. In
   `GL_n(ℂ)`, viewed as a real group, the complexified Lie algebra is `𝔤𝔩_n ⊕ 𝔤𝔩_n` and its
   Cartan subalgebra is `𝔥 ⊕ 𝔥`. Holomorphic and antiholomorphic derivatives contribute
   positive roots in the first and second copy respectively. The result is
   `(p(c), q(c)) = (w·p(d) + u, w·q(d) + v)` with the same `w` in both coordinates and
   `u, v ∈ ℕ·Δ`. That is E with `σ` the permutation of `w`.

The sign convention can be checked on the closed cell `B`. There the functional is
`λ(f) = (R(Y) f)(1)` for `Y` in the enveloping algebra of the lower triangular nilradical. If
`Y = E_{ji}` (`i < j`) then `Ad(a⁻¹) Y = (z_i / z_j) Y` for `a = diag(z)`. So
`λ(R(a) f) = d δ_B^{1/2}(a) · (z_i/z_j) · λ(f)`, and `c = d + (e_i - e_j)` in the
holomorphic coordinate. This is a positive root, which matches the sign in E.

**Consistency checks.**

* The identity map of `X(d)` (`c = d`): take `σ = 1`, `u = v = 0`.
* The long intertwining operator. Let the exponents of `d` have strictly decreasing real
  parts. Knapp's operator `A(S̄ : S : σ : ν)` (here `S = B`) is a nonzero map from the standard
  module to the principal series induced from the opposite Borel subgroup `S̄`; its image is
  the Langlands quotient (`provided-40cace9c…`, Knapp 1986, Theorem 7.24 and the remark after
  it, p. 200 (PDF 214)). Conjugating by the antidiagonal permutation matrix identifies the
  target with `X(c)` for the reversed list `c_j = d_{n-1-j}`. E holds with `σ` the reversal
  and `u = v = 0`.
* `GL_2(ℂ)` (`n = 2`). Let `d = δ_B^{-1/2}`: exponents `t = (-1/2, 1/2)` and weights `0`, so
  `p = q = (-1/2, 1/2)`. Let `c = δ_B^{1/2}`: `p = q = (1/2, -1/2)`. The trivial
  representation is a submodule of `X(d)` (the constant functions) and the unique irreducible
  quotient of `X(c)` (by `f ↦ ∫_K f`). The other composition factor `Y` of `X(c)` is a
  quotient of `X(d)`. So there are nonzero maps `X(d) → X(c)` (through `Y`) and
  `X(c) → X(d)` (through the trivial representation). E holds for both: with `σ` the swap and
  `u = v = 0`, and also, for the first map, with `σ = 1` and `u = v = e_0 - e_1`. The module
  `Y` is isomorphic to `X(e)` for `e = ((t, l) = (0, 1), (0, -1))`, i.e. `p = (1/2, -1/2)`,
  `q = (-1/2, 1/2)`. The embedding `X(e) ≅ Y → X(c)` needs a positive shift: with `σ = 1` it
  has `u = 0`, `v = e_0 - e_1`, and with `σ` the swap it has `u = e_0 - e_1`, `v = 0`. With
  the opposite sign convention (shifts by `-ℕ·Δ`) neither choice of `σ` would work. This is a
  second check of the sign.

## Use

The follow-up module `ComplexStandardOppositeOrderHomReduction` (Worker 729) proves that E
together with `ComplexStandardHomTransposeStatement` (module `ComplexStandardHomTranspose`)
implies `ComplexStandardOppositeOrderHomStatement`. That statement is from Worker 727's
`ComplexLanglandsQuotientUniqueReduction`. The proof applies E to `T` and to its transpose. It
then shows that all prefix sums of `u + v` vanish. The key step is that reversing the order of
a sorted list reverses the dominance order on prefix sums. Hence `u = v = 0` and `c = d ∘ σ`.
