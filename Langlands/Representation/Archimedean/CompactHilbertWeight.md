# Compact coefficient weights and nonzero square trace

`compactCoefficientWeights` takes the matrix coefficient
\(k\mapsto\langle R(k)v,i(u)\rangle\), linear in the finite-dimensional
input \(u\). The original Hilbert action is jointly continuous. If its
restriction to the compact subgroup preserves the inner product and
\(i\) intertwines the finite matrix action \(\tau\), translating the weight
on the left is equivalent to acting on \(u\) by \(\tau\).

For a compact subgroup with a finite Borel measure invariant under
inversion, an average with weight satisfying
\(\overline{w(k^{-1})}=w(k)\) is self-adjoint under the same inner-product
invariance hypothesis. The assertion follows by evaluating against two
vectors and changing variables by inversion. The diagonal weight with
\(v=i(u)\) satisfies this condition. If \(v\ne0\) and the finite Borel
measure is positive on nonempty open sets, this diagonal weight gives a
nonzero bounded average. Its diagonal at \(v\) is the integral of the
squared modulus of the ordinary coefficient, positive near the identity.

Finally, a nonzero self-adjoint bounded operator \(P\) on a complex
Hilbert space with finite-dimensional range has
\(\operatorname{tr}(P^2)\ne0\). Trace cyclicity through the range computes
the trace as the sum of \(\|Pb_j\|^2\) on an orthonormal basis of the range.
If the sum is zero, \(P\) vanishes on its range and \(P^2=0\).
Self-adjointness then forces \(P=0\).

These internal lemmas supply the nonzero identity trace for compact
compression of the Hilbert character.
