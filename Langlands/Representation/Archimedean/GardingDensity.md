# Gårding averages are dense in the genuine smooth-vector topology

For a continuous Hilbert representation the classical Gårding space
is spanned by averages of orbits against smooth, compactly supported
kernels. The theorem gives derivative-topology density of actual
averages of smooth vectors of a Banach representation. Every
approximating vector comes with one such kernel and the exact
whole-group Bochner integral.

Fix a compact identity neighborhood and pull Haar measure back to
its subtype. The smooth vector's orbit family has jointly smooth
orbits, by multiplication in the Lie group. Compact integration
therefore commutes with its genuine derivative words. For a given
smooth-vector neighborhood, the compact approximation theorem gives
an identity neighborhood small enough for the finitely many
derivative seminorms in that neighborhood.

Choose a nonnegative smooth bump supported inside that neighborhood
and the interior of the compact set, then divide by its positive
Haar integral. The normalized weight and its norm both have integral
one. Restriction to the compact subtype gives the required
approximation. Its integral equals the whole-group average because
the weight vanishes off the compact set.

The smoothing theorem also allows an arbitrary vector of a continuous
representation. Left Haar substitution moves the orbit parameter to
the translated kernel. Near a given group element, all the translated
kernels are supported in one compact set. The integrand is smooth in
the group parameter and in a pair consisting of the integration point
and the orbit value. The compact integration theorem applies to the
continuous family of those pairs and gives smoothness of the orbit.
It also differentiates this parameter integral. At the identity the
result is the genuine Lie derivative of the averaged vector, given by
the same integral with the left-translated kernel differentiated at
the identity.

This is an internal approximation step for the use of a Gårding
space in Shalika's continuous Whittaker uniqueness argument,
*The multiplicity one theorem for GL(n)* (1974), Theorem 3.1.
