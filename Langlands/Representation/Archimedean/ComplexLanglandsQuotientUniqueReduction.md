# Reduction of `ComplexLanglandsQuotientUniqueStatement` (LU)

The target of this module is the Library's `Langlands.Archimedean.ComplexLanglandsQuotientUniqueStatement` (LU, module
`Langlands.Representation.Archimedean.ComplexStandardSurjectionInjectiveReduction`). LU says:
if two lists `ds`, `es` of `n` complex characters in the Langlands order both have nonzero
intertwining maps from the `U(n)`-finite functions of their standard modules into the same
irreducible admissible `(𝔤𝔩_n(ℂ), U(n))`-module, then `ds` is a permutation of `es`. Its
source and meaning are explained in `ComplexStandardSurjectionInjectiveReduction.md`
(section "Statement LU"; Knapp 1994, Theorem 4 (a) and (c), due to Želobenko and Naimark).

This module states one new statement, Orth (`ComplexStandardOppositeOrderHomStatement`), and
proves that the Library's `ComplexLanglandsSubrepresentationStatement` (S1) together with Orth
implies LU (`complexLanglandsQuotientUniqueReduction`). It also proves two small lemmas about
embeddings into standard modules. All declarations are in the namespace
`Langlands.Archimedean`.

## Notation

For a list `ds` of complex characters and `n : ℕ`, `X(ds)` is the space
`kFiniteFunctions (complexStandardModule n ds) (unitaryCompact ℂ n)` of `U(n)`-finite functions
of the standard module, with `U(n)` acting by right translation (`complexStandardRep n ds`) and
`ℂ ⊗[ℝ] 𝔤𝔩_n(ℂ)` by right derivatives (`complexStandardLie n ds`); see
`PrincipalSeriesGKModule.md`. The functions satisfy `f(b g) = χ(b) δ_B(b)^{1/2} f(g)` for the
upper triangular Borel subgroup `B`, where `χ(diag(a) u) = ∏ᵢ χᵢ(aᵢ)` for the characters
`χᵢ(z) = (z/|z|)^{lᵢ} |z|^{2tᵢ}` of `ds` (`lᵢ` = field `weight`, `tᵢ` = field `exponent`).
`ComplexGenericParameterReduction.md` explains why `X(ds)` is the Harish-Chandra module of
Knapp's principal series `I(σ₁, …, σ_n)` (Knapp 1994, (4.2)), and defines
`IsComplexLanglandsOrdered ds` (real parts of the exponents do not increase along the list,
Knapp's condition (4.3)) and `IsComplexStandardIntertwining n ρ π ds A` (`A : X(ds) → V`
carries right translation by `k ∈ U(n)` to `ρ k` and the right derivative along a real
direction `X ∈ 𝔤𝔩_n(ℂ)` to `π (1 ⊗ X)`). `IsComplexStandardEmbedding n ρ π cs E` (the same two
conditions for a map `E : V → X(cs)`) is defined in `ComplexLanglandsQuotientReduction.md`.

A `ComplexCharacterData` is determined by its character, which determines the weight and the
exponent; so `ds.Perm cs` means that the characters of `cs` are a rearrangement of those of
`ds`.

## Statement Orth: `ComplexStandardOppositeOrderHomStatement`

For every `n`, all lists `ds`, `cs` and every linear map `T : X(ds) →ₗ[ℂ] X(cs)`: if
`ds.length = n`, `cs.length = n`, `IsComplexLanglandsOrdered ds` (real parts of the exponents
of `ds` non-increasing), `cs.Pairwise (fun d e => d.exponent.re ≤ e.exponent.re)` (real parts of
the exponents of `cs` non-decreasing), `T ≠ 0`, and
`IsComplexStandardIntertwining n (complexStandardRep n cs) (complexStandardLie n cs) ds T`
(`T` intertwines the `U(n)`-actions and the right derivatives on both sides), then
`ds.Perm cs`.

In words: a nonzero `(𝔤, K)`-map from a principal series in the Langlands order to a
principal series in the opposite order exists only between rearrangements of the same
characters. The statement has no universe parameter.

### Provenance: derived from Badulescu–Renard 2010

Orth is not stated in this form in the literature. It is derived below from the Langlands
classification of `GL_n(ℂ)` and the Bruhat `G`-order, in the form recorded by Badulescu and
Renard (BR: A. I. Badulescu and D. Renard, Unitary dual of GL(n) at archimedean places and
global Jacquet–Langlands correspondence, Compositio Math. 146 (2010), 1115–1164; journal PDF
page = printed page − 1113). The facts used are these.

* *Conventions* (§1.1, pp. 1116–1117; §2.2–2.4 and Definition 2.2, pp. 1122–1123). BR work in the
  category `M(G)` of Harish-Chandra modules of `G = GL(n, A)`, where `A = F = ℂ` is allowed
  and `K = U(n)`. `|·|_ℂ` is the normalized absolute value, so `|z|_ℂ = |z|²`, and
  `ν = |det|_ℂ`. `R(G)` is the Grothendieck group of finite-length objects of `M(G)`, the free
  `ℤ`-module on the set `Irr(G)` of irreducible classes. `π₁ × ⋯ × π_s` is normalized
  parabolic induction of `π₁ ⊗ ⋯ ⊗ π_s` from the parabolic subgroup generated by the
  block-diagonal subgroup and the upper triangular Borel subgroup. It is formed for
  finite-length `π_i` and defines an element of `R`.
* *Characters* (§3, p. 1124, and §9.1, p. 1132). For `GL_n(ℂ)` the essentially
  square-integrable representations are the characters of `GL_1(ℂ) = ℂˣ`: `D = D₁ = Irr₁`,
  `δ(z) = |z|_ℂ^β (z/|z|)^m`. Writing `δ = ν^{e(δ)} δ^u` with `δ^u` unitary gives
  `e(δ) = Re β`. The character of a `ComplexCharacterData` with weight `l` and exponent `t` is
  `(z/|z|)^l |z|^{2t} = (z/|z|)^l |z|_ℂ^t`, so `e = Re t`. Hence a list is written in BR's
  *standard order* `e(d₁) ≥ ⋯ ≥ e(d_l)` (Definition 3.2, p. 1124) exactly when it is
  `IsComplexLanglandsOrdered`. For a list `ds` of length `n`, `ds₁ × ⋯ × ds_n` is the
  principal series induced from the upper triangular Borel subgroup. Its Harish-Chandra module
  is `X(ds)` (`ComplexGenericParameterReduction.md` identifies `X(ds)` with Knapp's
  `I(σ₁, …, σ_n)`).
* *Commutativity* (Remark 2.3, p. 1123; Proposition 2.4, p. 1124). `(R, ×)` is a commutative
  graded ring. So `π₁ × π₂ = π₂ × π₁` in `R`, although the two are in general not isomorphic
  as modules.
* *Langlands classification* (Theorem 3.3, pp. 1124–1125). Let `d ∈ M(D)` be a multiset of
  elements of `D`, written in a standard order `(d₁, …, d_l)`. (i) `λ(d) = d₁ × ⋯ × d_l` has a
  unique irreducible quotient `Lg(d)`, and `Lg(d)` is also the unique irreducible
  subrepresentation of the reversed product `d_l × ⋯ × d₁`. (iii) `d ↦ Lg(d)` is a bijection
  from `M(D)` onto `Irr`. The displayed hypothesis reads `e(d₁) ≥ e(d₂) > ⋯ ≥ e(d_l)`. The
  strict sign is a misprint, because the theorem is stated for every standard order and
  Definition 3.2 uses `≥` throughout. "Unique subrepresentation" can only mean unique
  irreducible subrepresentation.
* *Bruhat `G`-order* (§6 and Proposition 6.1, pp. 1128–1129). `≤` is a partial order on
  `M(D)`. For every `a ∈ M(D)`, `λ(a) = Σ_{b ≤ a} m(b, a) Lg(b)` in `R`, where the `m(b, a)`
  are nonnegative integers and `m(a, a) = 1`.

*Argument.* Let `n`, `ds`, `cs` and `T` satisfy the hypotheses of Orth. Let `a ∈ M(D)` be the
multiset of the characters of `ds`, and `a'` the multiset of the characters of `cs`. Since
`ds` is in a standard order, `X(ds)` is (the Harish-Chandra module of) `λ(a)`. The list
`(a'₁, …, a'_n) = rev cs` has non-increasing real parts, so it is a standard order of `a'`.
Therefore `X(cs) = cs₁ × ⋯ × cs_n = a'_n × ⋯ × a'₁` is the reversed product of
Theorem 3.3(i) for `a'`. Both modules have finite length. The image `W = T(X(ds))` is a
`(𝔤, K)`-submodule of `X(cs)`, because `T` intertwines. It is nonzero because `T ≠ 0`, and
it is isomorphic to the quotient `X(ds) / ker T`. So `W` is a nonzero module of finite length.

1. `W` has an irreducible quotient `Q`. Composing `X(ds) → W → Q` shows that `Q` is an
   irreducible quotient of `λ(a)`, so `Q ≅ Lg(a)` by Theorem 3.3(i). `Q` is a subquotient of
   `X(cs)`, so `Lg(a)` occurs in a Jordan–Hölder series of `X(cs)`.
2. In `R`, `[X(cs)] = cs₁ × ⋯ × cs_n = a'₁ × ⋯ × a'_n = λ(a')`, by commutativity
   (Proposition 2.4). The classes `Lg(b)` for `b ∈ M(D)` are distinct elements of the basis
   `Irr` of `R` (Theorem 3.3(iii)). So by Proposition 6.1, the coefficient of `Lg(a)` in
   `[X(cs)]` is `m(a, a')` if `a ≤ a'` and `0` otherwise. By step 1 it is positive, so
   `a ≤ a'`.
3. `W` has an irreducible submodule `S`. Then `S` is an irreducible submodule of `X(cs)`,
   the reversed product for `a'`, so `S ≅ Lg(a')` by Theorem 3.3(i). `S` is a subquotient of
   `X(ds) = λ(a)`, so `Lg(a')` has a positive coefficient in `[λ(a)]`. Proposition 6.1 gives
   `a' ≤ a`.
4. By antisymmetry of the partial order, `a = a'`: the characters of `cs` are a rearrangement
   of those of `ds`. A `ComplexCharacterData` is determined by its character (see Notation),
   so `ds.Perm cs`.

The argument uses only the BR results listed above. It uses nothing from the formal
route described next.

### Formal route (not provenance)

The Library proves Orth in Lean by a different route: the module
`ComplexStandardOppositeOrderHomReduction` derives Orth from two separate statements.
`ComplexStandardHomExponentStatement` (E) is a Bruhat-cell description of the characters of a
nonzero intertwining map between principal series: a common permutation `σ` and shifts by
nonnegative integer combinations of simple roots on the two factors of the Cartan subalgebra.
`ComplexStandardHomTransposeStatement` (TP) transposes such a map into a nonzero map
`X(cs⁻¹) → X(ds⁻¹)`. Orth then follows from a comparison of prefix sums of the sorted real
parts. E and TP have their own modules with their own provenance; the Library proves them
(`ComplexStandardHomExponentProof`, `ComplexStandardHomTransposeProof`), and
`ComplexLanglandsQuotientUniqueProof` derives Orth from them as `complexStandardOppositeOrderHom`.
This module does not import them, and the truth of Orth as stated here does not
depend on them.

### Checks

(i) The hypotheses can hold. For every `ds` in the Langlands order, BR Theorem 3.3(ii)
(p. 1125) gives a nonzero intertwining operator `J : ds₁ × ⋯ × ds_n → ds_n × ⋯ × ds₁`, whose
image is the Langlands quotient `Lg`. Here the target is `X(rev ds)`, whose real parts are non-decreasing, and
`rev ds` is a permutation of `ds`, as Orth requires. For strictly decreasing real parts, the
same operator is Knapp 1986, Theorem 7.24, p. 200: a nonzero intertwining operator from
`U(S, σ, ν)` onto the Langlands quotient inside `U(S̄, σ, ν)`, the principal series induced
from the opposite Borel subgroup. Conjugation by the antidiagonal permutation matrix
identifies `U(S̄, σ, ν)` with the principal series of the reversed list (see
`ComplexGenericParameterReduction.md`). (ii) The order on `ds` cannot be dropped. For
`GL_2(ℂ)`, the principal series of exponents `-1/2, 1/2` (increasing) maps onto its
infinite-dimensional quotient. That quotient is equivalent to a tempered principal series
(`ComplexGenericParameterReduction.md`), whose exponents have real part `0`, and those
characters are not a rearrangement of the original ones. (iii) The trivial representation of
`GL_2(ℂ)` is the quotient of the principal series of exponents `1/2, -1/2`. It sits in the
principal series of `-1/2, 1/2` (functions on `B \ G`). The composite map is nonzero, and the
two lists are rearrangements of each other.

## Statement of the reduction: `ComplexLanglandsQuotientUniqueReductionStatement.{uV}`

`ComplexLanglandsSubrepresentationStatement.{uV} → ComplexStandardOppositeOrderHomStatement →
ComplexLanglandsQuotientUniqueStatement.{uV}`.

S1 (`ComplexLanglandsSubrepresentationStatement`, Library module
`ComplexLanglandsQuotientReduction`, source Wallach 1979, Theorems 4.18 and 6.7; see
`ComplexLanglandsQuotientReduction.md`, section "Statement S1") says: every irreducible
admissible `(𝔤𝔩_n(ℂ), U(n))`-module `V` (on a type in universe `uV`) has a nonzero
`E : V → X(cs)` with `IsComplexStandardEmbedding n ρ π cs E`, for some list `cs` of `n`
characters whose exponents have non-decreasing real parts.

## Auxiliary lemmas

* `IsComplexStandardEmbedding.map_rep`: if `IsComplexStandardEmbedding n ρ π cs E`, then
  `E (ρ k v) = complexStandardRep n cs k (E v)` for all `k ∈ U(n)` and `v`. Proof: both sides
  are elements of `X(cs)`, a subtype of functions; by the first clause of the embedding
  property, applied to the unit `Unitary.toUnits k` of `GL_n(ℂ)`, the function of the left side
  is the right translate of `E v` by `k`, and so is the function of the right side
  (`principalSeriesRep_apply_coe`).
* `IsComplexStandardEmbedding.map_lie_one_tmul`: `E (π (1 ⊗ X) v) = complexStandardLie n cs
  (1 ⊗ X) (E v)` for every `X ∈ 𝔤𝔩_n(ℂ)`. Proof: the same, using the second clause of the
  embedding property and `principalSeriesLie_one_tmul_apply_coe`.

## Proof of the reduction: `complexLanglandsQuotientUniqueReduction`

Assume S1 and Orth, and let `n`, `V`, `ρ`, `π`, `ds`, `es`, `A`, `B` satisfy the hypotheses of
LU. S1 gives a list `cs` of length `n` with non-decreasing real parts and a nonzero
`E : V → X(cs)` with `IsComplexStandardEmbedding n ρ π cs E`.

The core step (`key` in the Lean proof) is: for every list `ds'` of length `n` in the Langlands
order and every nonzero `C : X(ds') → V` with `IsComplexStandardIntertwining n ρ π ds' C`,
`ds'` is a permutation of `cs`. Indeed:

1. `C` is surjective (`surjective_of_isComplexStandardIntertwining`, Library: its range is
   invariant and nonzero, and `V` is irreducible).
2. `E ∘ C` satisfies `IsComplexStandardIntertwining n (complexStandardRep n cs)
   (complexStandardLie n cs) ds' (E ∘ C)` by the Library's `IsComplexStandardIntertwining.comp`,
   whose two compatibility hypotheses are the auxiliary lemmas above.
3. `E ∘ C ≠ 0`: otherwise, for every `v ∈ V` write `v = C f` by surjectivity; then
   `E v = (E ∘ C) f = 0`, so `E = 0`, a contradiction.
4. Orth, applied to `ds'`, `cs`, `E ∘ C`, gives `ds'.Perm cs`.

Applying the core step to `(ds, A)` and to `(es, B)` gives `ds.Perm cs` and `es.Perm cs`, so
`ds.Perm es` by transitivity and symmetry of `List.Perm`.

The hypotheses `IsComplexGKModule n ρ π`, `IsIrreducibleGKModule ρ π` and
`IsAdmissibleGKModule ρ` are used only to apply S1; irreducibility is also used in step 1.
