# `U(n)`-finite continuous functions on `U(n)` are polynomial in the entries and their conjugates

Lean module: `Langlands.Representation.Archimedean.UnitaryFinitePolynomial`.

Worker 140 wrote this module for Task 7063 (`f2d2e257-dd4b-4820-96ab-faa9c832ffb8`), the proof of
`ComplexStandardLanglandsQuotientStatement`. It is the unitary counterpart of the Library module
`OrthogonalFinitePolynomial` (Worker 5220, for `O(n)`), and its proof follows that module step by
step.

The module proves that a continuous complex-valued function on the unitary group `U(n)` whose
right translates span a finite-dimensional space is the restriction to `U(n)` of a polynomial in
the `n²` matrix entries `k_{ij}` and their `n²` complex conjugates `conj k_{ij}`. It states this
as `Langlands.Archimedean.UnitaryFinitePolynomialStatement` and proves it as
`Langlands.Archimedean.unitaryFinitePolynomial`. It also proves the form for functions on
`GL_n(ℂ)` used later: if a continuous function on `GL_n(ℂ)` is `U(n)`-finite under right
translation, then on every coset `g · U(n)` it is such a polynomial in the `U(n)` variable
(`exists_mvPolynomial_of_mem_kFiniteVectors`).

Notation: `U(n)` is `Matrix.unitaryGroup (Fin n) ℂ`, the complex `n × n` matrices `k` with
`k k* = 1`, with the subspace topology from the matrices. `GL_n(ℂ)` is
`Langlands.GeneralLinear n ℂ`. `Unitary.toUnits` maps `U(n)` into `GL_n(ℂ)`, with image the
subgroup `unitaryCompact ℂ n`. Right translation by `s` sends a function `F` to `x ↦ F(x s)`
(`Whittaker.rightTranslation`). Declarations without a prefix are in the namespace
`Langlands.Archimedean.UnitaryFinitePolynomial`.

## The statement

`UnitaryFinitePolynomialStatement`: for every `n : ℕ` and every continuous `f : U(n) → ℂ` such
that `Submodule.span ℂ (Set.range fun k => fun x => f (x * k))` is finite-dimensional, there is a
polynomial `P : MvPolynomial ((Fin n × Fin n) ⊕ (Fin n × Fin n)) ℂ` with

`f k = MvPolynomial.eval (Sum.elim (fun ij => k_{ij}) (fun ij => star k_{ij})) P`

for every `k ∈ U(n)`. So the variable `Sum.inl (i, j)` is replaced by the entry `k_{ij}` and the
variable `Sum.inr (i, j)` by its complex conjugate (`star` on `ℂ` is complex conjugation).

Provenance. Derived content: it is the standard fact that the representative functions of a
compact linear group (matrix coefficients of its finite-dimensional continuous representations)
are polynomials in the matrix entries and their complex conjugates. No source is cited for this
exact form. The proof is self-contained; it uses neither the Peter–Weyl theorem nor the
representation theory of `U(n)`, and its analytic input is the Stone–Weierstrass theorem. In the
proof of `ComplexStandardLanglandsQuotientStatement` it gives the polynomial factor of the
polarized Gram formula (`ComplexGramFormula`) and hence the analyticity of `U(n)`-finite matrix
coefficients (`ComplexMatrixCoefficientAnalytic`). The statement holds trivially for `n = 0`.

## Definitions

* `coord n a`, for `a : (Fin n × Fin n) ⊕ (Fin n × Fin n)`: the continuous function
  `k ↦ k_{ij}` for `a = Sum.inl (i, j)` and `k ↦ conj k_{ij}` for `a = Sum.inr (i, j)`, as an
  element of `C(U(n), ℂ)`. `coordValue k` is the family of their values at `k`
  (`coord_apply`).
* `polynomialStar n`: the star subalgebra of `C(U(n), ℂ)` generated by the entries
  `coord n (Sum.inl (i, j))`. Since `star` of an entry is the conjugate entry
  (`star_coord_inl`), its underlying subalgebra is the subalgebra generated by all coordinates
  (`polynomialStar_toSubalgebra`, via `range_coord`).
* `polynomialFunctions n`: the subalgebra of all functions `U(n) → ℂ` generated by the
  coordinates. Its elements are exactly the functions `k ↦ P(coordValue k)`; the direction used
  is `exists_mvPolynomial_of_mem_polynomialFunctions`.
* `translateSpan f`: the span of the right translates `x ↦ f (x * k)`, `k ∈ U(n)`.
* `separated n`: the span of the functions `(x, k) ↦ α(x) β(k)` on `U(n) × U(n)` with
  `α ∈ polynomialFunctions n` and `β ∈ C(U(n), ℂ)`.

The instance `compactSpace_unitary` records that `U(n)` is compact (a closed subset of a product
of closed unit balls, by `entry_norm_bound_of_unitary`).

## Proof

Fix a Haar measure `μ` on `U(n)`; continuous functions are integrable since `U(n)` is compact.

1. **Density** (`eq_zero_of_integral_polynomial_mul_eq_zero`). If `g ∈ C(U(n), ℂ)` and
   `∫ φ g dμ = 0` for all `φ ∈ polynomialStar n`, then `g = 0`. The entries separate the points
   of `U(n)` (`polynomialStar_separatesPoints`), so `polynomialStar n` is dense by the
   Stone–Weierstrass theorem; the functional `φ ↦ ∫ φ g dμ` is continuous (through `L¹`), so it
   vanishes at `φ = conj g`, giving `∫ |g|² dμ = 0` and `g = 0` because Haar measure charges
   nonempty open sets.
2. **Point evaluations** (`exists_sum_eval`) and **continuity of coefficients**
   (`continuous_functional_translate`): on the finite-dimensional `translateSpan f`, every linear
   functional is a finite combination of point evaluations, so `k ↦ ℓ(f(· k))` is continuous.
3. **Averages stay in the span** (`integral_translate_mem_translateSpan`): for continuous `φ`,
   `F_φ(x) = ∫ φ(k) f(x k) dμ(k)` lies in `translateSpan f` (expand the translates in a basis).
4. **Separation of variables** (`mul_mem_separated`, `inv_mul_mem_separated`): `separated n` is
   closed under products; for `φ` in the algebra generated by the coordinates, `(x, k) ↦ φ(x⁻¹ k)`
   lies in `separated n`. For a generator this is the identity `x⁻¹ = x*`:
   `(x⁻¹ k)_{ab} = Σ_c conj(x_{ca}) k_{cb}` and `conj((x⁻¹ k)_{ab}) = Σ_c x_{ca} conj(k_{cb})`,
   sums of products of a coordinate of `x` and a coordinate of `k`.
5. **Integration in the second variable** (`integral_separated_mem_polynomialFunctions`): for
   `h ∈ separated n` and continuous `f`, `x ↦ ∫ h(x, k) f(k) dμ(k)` is polynomial.
6. **Averages are polynomial** (`convolution_mem_polynomialFunctions`): by left invariance of
   `μ`, `F_φ(x) = ∫ φ(x⁻¹ k) f(k) dμ(k)`, polynomial in `x` by steps 4 and 5.
7. **Main theorem** (`mem_polynomialFunctions_of_finiteDimensional`): let `S` be the span of the
   `F_φ`, `φ ∈ polynomialStar n`; then `S ⊆ translateSpan f` and `S` consists of polynomial
   functions. If `f ∉ S`, a functional `ℓ` vanishing on `S` with `ℓ(f) ≠ 0` is
   `Σ_j c_j ev_{p_j}` on `translateSpan f`; the continuous `g(k) = Σ_j c_j f(p_j k)` is
   orthogonal to `polynomialStar n`, so `g = 0` by step 1, and `ℓ(f) = g(1) = 0`, a
   contradiction.

`exists_mvPolynomial_of_finiteDimensional_span` turns this into the polynomial form, and
`unitaryFinitePolynomial` is the statement.

## The form on `GL_n(ℂ)`

`exists_mvPolynomial_of_mem_kFiniteVectors`: let `f : GL_n(ℂ) → ℂ` be continuous with
`f ∈ kFiniteVectors (Whittaker.rightTranslation ℂ GL_n(ℂ)) (unitaryCompact ℂ n)`. Then for every
`g ∈ GL_n(ℂ)` there is `P` with `f(g · Unitary.toUnits k) = P(coordValue k)` for all `k ∈ U(n)`.
The function `f'(x) = f(g x)` on `U(n)` is continuous, and its right translates are images of
right translates of `f` under `F ↦ (x ↦ F(g x))`, so `translateSpan f'` is finite-dimensional and
the main theorem applies.
