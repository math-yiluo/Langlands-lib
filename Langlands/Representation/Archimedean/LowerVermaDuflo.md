# Duflo's theorem for lower Verma modules of `𝔤𝔩_n(ℂ)`

Module `Langlands.Representation.Archimedean.LowerVermaDuflo`, namespace
`Langlands.Archimedean.VermaPBW`.

The main result is `lowerVermaDuflo : VermaGL.LowerVermaDufloStatement`. The statement is in
the Library module `LowerVerma`. For every `n` and `λ : Fin n → ℂ`, the representation
`VermaGL.action n λ` of `𝔤𝔩_n(ℂ)` on the lower Verma module `M⁻(λ)` is maximally faithful
(`IsMaximallyFaithful`, from `RealGenericLargeReduction`). That is, every `u ∈ U(𝔤𝔩_n(ℂ))`
acting by zero on `M⁻(λ)` lies in the left ideal generated by the central elements acting by
zero. This is Duflo's theorem `Ann M_χ = U · ker θ` (Bernstein–Gelfand 1980, 1.13(b)), for
lower Verma modules of `𝔤𝔩_n(ℂ)`.

The proof does not follow Duflo's original argument. It applies the Library's proved form of
Kostant's theorem for Whittaker modules, `Kostant.glProductWhittakerMaximallyFaithful`
(statement `GLProductWhittakerMaximallyFaithfulStatement`): a representation with an
infinitesimal character and a nonzero Whittaker functional for a non-singular character is
maximally faithful. Both hypotheses are verified for `M⁻(λ)` with the Poincaré–Birkhoff–Witt
tools of `LowerVermaPBW`.

## Declarations

* The Library's `BGChase.envelopingAction_action`: the algebra map `U → End(M⁻(λ))` induced by
  `VermaGL.action n λ` is the module structure, `u ↦ (m ↦ u • m)`.
* `hasInfinitesimalCharacter_action`: the center acts on `M⁻(λ)` by scalars. For central `z`
  the vector `z • gen` has weight `λ`, since `E_pp z = z E_pp` and `gen` has weight `λ`
  (`unitU_smul_gen`). By `lowerVermaWeight` (`LowerVermaPBW`), `z • gen = c • gen`. Every
  element of `M⁻(λ)` is `u • gen` (`mk_eq_smul_gen`), and
  `z • u • gen = u • z • gen = c • u • gen`.
* `oneCoeff n` is the constant function `1`. It gives the superdiagonal coefficients
  `a_p = 1` of the non-singular character `η(X) = Σ_p X_{p,p+1}`.
* `whitFun n λ : M⁻(λ) → ℂ` is the functional `Ψ_{vVal λ 1}` of `LowerVermaPBW`, descended
  to the quotient. It is defined through `Submodule.liftQ` for the ideal viewed as a
  `ℂ`-submodule, composed with `Submodule.Quotient.restrictScalarsEquiv`. It is well defined
  because `Ψ` vanishes on the defining ideal (`Psi_mul_mem_lowerVermaIdeal` with `u = 1`).
  `whitFun_mk`: its value on the class of `u` is `Ψ(u)`. `whitFun_gen`: `whitFun(gen) = Ψ(1) = 1`.
* `whitFun_action` (Whittaker property): for strictly upper triangular `X` (`X p q = 0` for
  `q ≤ p`) and every `m`,
  `whitFun(X • m) = (Σ_p Σ_q [q = p + 1] · 1 · X p q) · whitFun(m)`. Write `m` as the class of
  `u`. Then `X • m` is the class of `X u`, and `Ψ(X u) = μ_U(X) Ψ(u)` by the left
  `𝔫⁺`-equivariance `Psi_ι_mul`. Here `μ_U = muUp n 1` is the superdiagonal character.
* `toUnitProd n`: the Lie algebra isomorphism `𝔤𝔩_n(ℂ) ≅ ∏_{Unit} 𝔤𝔩_n(ℂ)`, `X ↦ (_ ↦ X)`.
  The Library's Kostant theorem is stated for finite products of `𝔤𝔩`'s.

## Proof of `lowerVermaDuflo`

By `Kostant.isMaximallyFaithful_of_comp` (`Kostant/Transport`), it suffices that the transported
representation `action ∘ toUnitProd⁻¹` of `∏_{Unit} 𝔤𝔩_n(ℂ)` is maximally faithful. Apply
`Kostant.glProductWhittakerMaximallyFaithful` with index type `Unit`, block size `n`, and the
following inputs:

* the infinitesimal character transported by `Kostant.hasInfinitesimalCharacter_comp`;
* the functional `whitFun`, nonzero because `whitFun(gen) = 1`;
* the coefficients `a = 1`, which are nonzero;
* the Whittaker property `whitFun_action`, applied to the single block `X ()`.

## Provenance

The statement is BG 1.13(b) (J. N. Bernstein and S. I. Gelfand, *Tensor products of finite
and infinite dimensional representations of semisimple Lie algebras*, Compositio Math. 41
(1980); literature id `bernstein-gelfand-1980-tensor-products`), for lower Verma modules of
`𝔤𝔩_n(ℂ)`. The route through Kostant's Whittaker theorem is the standard observation that
Verma modules are free over `U(𝔫⁺)` and therefore carry nondegenerate Whittaker functionals.
Here that freeness is replaced by the explicit functional `Ψ` of `LowerVermaPBW`.
