# Products of scalar forms on genuine smooth vectors

`SmoothProjectiveProductFunctionalStatement` takes a finite family of
finite-dimensional real Banach algebras and jointly continuous representations
of their groups of units on complex Banach spaces. Give each smooth carrier
its genuine word-derivative topology. For continuous complex-linear scalar
forms on the individual carriers, there is a continuous complex-linear form
on the genuine smooth vectors of the completed Banach projective tensor
product. The latter smooth structure uses the action of the units of the
product algebra. The form on a pure smooth tensor is the product of the
individual evaluations.

The coordinate unit-group maps are smooth since they are continuous linear
projections in the open units charts. This gives genuine product-group
smoothness of each pure tensor of individual smooth vectors. Differentiate
along the exponential line of a direction in a single algebra factor. All
other coordinates of that one-parameter subgroup are the identity, so the
derivative changes just one entry of the pure tensor. Concatenation of the
embedded words then gives exactly the individual word derivatives on a
pure tensor.

Each supplied scalar form factors through a bounded form on finitely many
derivative coordinates. Split that form by the coordinate injections. Each
product of the resulting bounded forms lifts to the Banach projective
completion. Compose the lifted forms with their corresponding mixed word
derivatives and add the finite family. The pure tensor derivative formula
and expansion of the finite product of sums give the prescribed evaluations.

Jacquet uses the continuous product of Whittaker evaluations on a smooth
projective tensor product in the proof of Lemma 14.1, p. 106, using the
smooth forms of §2, pp. 8–9. The finite word construction above realizes
the product on genuine smooth vectors of a completed Banach representation.
[H. Jacquet, *Archimedean Rankin–Selberg Integrals* (2009), author-posted edition](https://www.math.columbia.edu/~hj/PerfectRankinSelberg.pdf#page=106).
