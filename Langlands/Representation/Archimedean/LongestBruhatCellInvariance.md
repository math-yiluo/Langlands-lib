# Weyl-transpose invariance on the longest Bruhat cell

`longestBruhatCell_transpose_invariant` is the open-cell step of the proof of archimedean Whittaker multiplicity one (Shalika 1974, Theorem 3.1; `UnitaryWhittakerMultiplicityOneStatement`). Let `B` be the open longest Bruhat cell in square matrix space over an `RCLike` field `F`, in size `n + 1`, and let `W` be any open set containing `B`. A complex distribution `A` on `W` is assumed to transform by `χ(u)χ(v)` under test pullback by `x ↦ u x v`, for a continuous multiplicative character `χ` of the actual upper unipotent group that is fixed by Weyl-conjugated transpose. If `f` is supported topologically in `B` and `g(x) = f(τ x)` for all ambient matrices, then `A g = A f`. The more general `longestBruhatCell_transpose_invariant_of_covariant` uses the product action `(u, v) • x = u x v⁻¹` and a character of the product group fixed by its transpose automorphism. The two-sided version uses the product character `(u, v) ↦ χ(u)χ(v⁻¹)`. Neither version assumes a Casimir equation or proves any invariance on tests not supported in the longest cell.

This implements the scope of the open-cell argument in Shalika (1974), Proposition 2.11, printed pp. 182–183 (PDF pp. 13–14), which refers to Proposition 1.8 for the averaging step. The integral `longestBruhatFiberAverage` is an internal construction for the ambient real matrix-test formulation, not a source-defined object. It is the pointwise Bochner integral `β(k)(x) = ∫ χ(p) k(p⁻¹ • x) dμ(p)` for the product action and its character. The proof takes regular left Haar measure on the product group. The lift `σ(u, v) = (τ(v⁻¹), τ(u⁻¹))` is an involutive group automorphism; its positive Haar scaling factor has square one, so the measure is preserved. Left invariance gives `β(k)(p • x) = χ(p) β(k)(x)`. Change of variables by `σ` identifies `β(k ∘ τ)(x)` with `β(k)(τ x)`. On `B`, these average values at `x` and `τ x` coincide, because each orbit has a representative fixed by `τ` and `χ(σ p) = χ(p)`.

For the distribution step the proof uses two smooth families with common compact spatial supports, built from tests `k` and `ρ` supported compactly in `B`. The first is `k(x) ρ(p • x)` and the second is `χ(p) ρ(x) k(p⁻¹ • x)`. The compact transporter bounds the parameter support of each family. Joint continuity of all spatial derivatives follows by treating the matrix factors and scalar weight as variables in a jointly smooth function and then restricting those parameters along continuous maps. The unweighted second slice, pulled back by `p`, is the first slice. Covariance and complex linearity therefore give identical distribution values on the two families. The fixed-support integration theorem commutes `A` with each integral. In particular a test whose values are the integral of the first family has the same distribution value as the test with values `ρ(x) β(k)(x)`; the latter is supported within the window `ρ`.

To represent the given `f` as that first integral, choose a nonnegative smooth real cutoff `r` compactly supported in `B` and positive on `tsupport f`. The proof obtains a cutoff on a relatively compact open thickening of this support and adds its transpose pullback, making `r` invariant under `τ`. The denominator `J(x) = ∫ r(x) r(p • x) dμ(p)` is smooth on the whole matrix space by differentiation of the compactly supported family. At each point of `tsupport f` it is strictly positive because the integrand is nonnegative and is positive at the identity. Taking `ρ = r` with complex values and `k(x) = ρ(x) f(x) / J(x)` gives a smooth test supported within `tsupport f` and the required integral formula; off the support smoothness follows because `f` is locally zero. The same formula for `g` uses `k ∘ τ` and the same window by changing variables with `σ`. Thus the two windowed character averages agree on the cell and vanish elsewhere, proving equality of their distribution values and hence of `A g` and `A f`.
