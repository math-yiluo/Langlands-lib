# Real standard modules are generic

Module `Langlands.Representation.Archimedean.RealStandardGenericProof`.

## Main results

- `Langlands.Archimedean.isRealGeneric_realStandardLie`: for every `n` and every list `ds` of real
  blocks with `realRank ds = n`, `IsRealGenericGKModule n (realStandardLie n ds)`. That is, the
  `O(n)`-finite functions of `realStandardModule n ds` carry a nonzero linear functional `ℓ` and
  real numbers `aᵢ`, nonzero for `i + 1 < n`, with
  `ℓ(π(1 ⊗ X) v) = (i ∑ᵢ aᵢ X_{i,i+1}) ℓ(v)` for every strictly upper triangular real `X`. No
  irreducibility is assumed.
- `Langlands.Archimedean.realIrreducibleStandardGeneric` proves the Library statement
  `RealIrreducibleStandardGenericStatement` (module `RealIrreducibleStandardGenericStatement`) by
  forgetting its irreducibility premise.

## Auxiliary results (namespace `Langlands.Archimedean.RealStandardGeneric`)

- `isRealGeneric_of_surjective`: let `T` be a surjective intertwiner (`IsRealStandardIntertwiner`)
  from the standard module of `es` onto that of `ds`, and `ℓ` a nonzero Whittaker functional on
  the source, for coefficients `aᵢ` as above, vanishing on `ker T`. Then the target is generic.
- `isRealGeneric_of_module_eq`: if `realStandardModule n es = realStandardModule n ds` and the
  module of `es` is generic, so is that of `ds`.
- `mem_span_center_of_whittaker_on`: let `π` be a representation of `ℂ ⊗_ℝ 𝔤𝔩_n(ℝ)` with an
  infinitesimal character, `K` a `π`-stable subspace and `ℓ` a Whittaker functional on `K` (for
  coefficients `aᵢ` as above) that is nonzero at a vector of `K`. Every `u ∈ U(ℂ ⊗_ℝ 𝔤𝔩_n(ℝ))`
  annihilating `K` lies in the ideal generated by the central elements annihilating the whole
  space.
- `whittaker_eq_zero_of_slowKernel`: let `T` be an intertwiner of standard modules, `n ≥ 2`, and
  suppose that the operators of degree `≤ k` of `U(𝔤)` restricted to `ker T` span a space of
  dimension at most `C (k + 1)^(n (n - 1) - 1)` for all `k`. Then every Whittaker functional
  as above on the source vanishes on `ker T`.
- `isRealGeneric_rankZero`, `isRealGeneric_characters`: the rank-zero case and lists of
  character blocks.
- `tailParity d = (|m| + 1) mod 2` and `tailParity_spec`: the parity condition of the tail map
  with first parity `0`.
- `isRealGeneric_append`: for every list `ds`, every list `cs` of real character data and every
  `n`, if `realRank (cs.map character ++ ds) = n` then the standard module of
  `cs.map character ++ ds` is generic.

## Proof

**Descent.** In `isRealGeneric_of_surjective`, `ℓ` vanishes on `ker T`, so it factors through
the quotient by `ker T` (`Submodule.liftQ`), and composing with the inverse of
`T.quotKerEquivOfSurjective` gives `ℓ'` on the target with `ℓ'(T v) = ℓ(v)`.
It is nonzero because `ℓ` is, and the Whittaker identity transfers along
`IsRealStandardIntertwiner.map_realStandardLie`. In `isRealGeneric_of_module_eq` the linear
equivalence of equal submodules (`exists_linearEquiv_coe_eq`) is the identity on functions, so it
commutes with the right derivatives `kFiniteLie_one_tmul_apply_coe`.

**Kostant's theorem on a subspace.** In `mem_span_center_of_whittaker_on`, restrict `π` to `K`
(`BGChase.restrictLie`). The restriction has an infinitesimal character, since every central
element acts on `V` by a scalar (`envelopingAction_intertwine` compares the two enveloping
actions), and `ℓ` restricted to `K` makes it generic. The Library's
`Kostant.realWhittakerMaximallyFaithful` makes the restriction maximally faithful, so `u` lies in
the ideal generated by the central elements annihilating `K`. Such an element acts on `V` by a
scalar that kills the nonzero vector `w₀ ∈ K`, so it annihilates `V`.

**Slowly growing kernels.** Suppose `ℓ(v) ≠ 0` for some `v ∈ ker T`. The kernel is stable under
the Lie action because `T` intertwines it. The Library's `realStandardCentralGrowth` gives
`c > 0` with `c (k + 1)^(n (n - 1)) ≤` the dimension of the image of the degree-`≤ k` part of
`U(𝔤)` under any linear map whose kernel on that part lies in `realStandardCentralIdeal`. Apply it
to `u ↦ (π(u) restricted to ker T)`: by `mem_span_center_of_whittaker_on`, an element of
`U(𝔤)` annihilating `ker T` lies in that ideal. With the assumed upper bound,
`RealLinkedQuotientGrowth.not_forall_le` gives a contradiction, since `n (n - 1) ≥ 1`.

**Induction.** `isRealGeneric_append` is proved by recursion on `ds`, keeping the blocks
already processed as a list of characters `cs`.

- `ds = []`: `cs.map character` is a character list. In rank `0`, the standard module is
  nontrivial (`realStandardNontrivial`), so some linear functional is nonzero, and the Whittaker
  identity is trivial because the only matrix is `0`. In rank `r + 1`,
  `RealPrincipalSeries.exists_characterList_of_map_character` writes the list as
  `characterList p z`, and `isRealGeneric_characterList` (module
  `RealCharacterStandardGeneric`) applies.
- A character block `c` moves into `cs`.
- An induction block of weight `0`: by `realStandardModule_append_expand` its standard module is
  that of its two characters `sgn |·|^t`, `|·|^t`, which move into `cs`, and
  `isRealGeneric_of_module_eq` transfers genericity.
- An induction block `d` of weight `m ≠ 0`: with `sb = 0` and `sa = tailParity d`, the
  ascending list `pre ++ [cb d 0, ca d sa] ++ ds` (`pre = cs.map character`) is again of the form
  `cs'.map character ++ ds`, so it is generic by recursion. The tail map
  `blockFrobMap (data_tail hn hl hs)` is surjective (`tail_surjective`) and an intertwiner
  (`modulo_tail`, `isRealStandardIntertwiner_of_modulo_bot`). Its kernel grows slowly
  (`RealTailKernel.tailKernel_growth`, module `RealTailKernelGrowth`), and `n ≥ 2` because the
  block has two positions. So the Whittaker functional vanishes on the kernel
  (`whittaker_eq_zero_of_slowKernel`) and descends (`isRealGeneric_of_surjective`).

`isRealGeneric_realStandardLie` is the case `cs = []`.

## Provenance

For irreducible real standard modules, genericity follows from the converse direction
"irreducibly induced from a generic limit of discrete series ⇒ generic" of Badulescu–Renard,
*Unitary dual of GL(n) at archimedean places and global Jacquet–Langlands correspondence*
(2010), Theorem 8.1 and the paragraph after it, p. 1131 (PDF p. 18,
`badulescu-renard-2010-unitary-dual`), which they attribute to Kostant and Vogan; the provenance
of `RealIrreducibleStandardGenericStatement` explains how the Library's standard models fit that
theorem. That source covers only the irreducible case. The proof in this module establishes
algebraic genericity for arbitrary real standard modules, reducible ones included, by the
induction over blocks and the kernel-growth argument above.

The Lean proof follows a route that uses only results in the Library.

- The base case is the continued Jacquet integral on principal series (Jacquet,
  *Archimedean Rankin–Selberg Integrals* (2009), §7.2, p. 47 of the author-posted version,
  `jacquet-2009-archimedean-rankin-selberg`), as implemented by `canonicalJacquetMap`.
- Maximal faithfulness of modules with a Whittaker functional is Kostant, *On Whittaker vectors
  and representation theory*, Invent. Math. 48 (1978), Theorem 3.9, p. 142 (PDF p. 42), in the
  form proved in the Library by `Kostant.realWhittakerMaximallyFaithful` along Matumoto,
  *Whittaker vectors and associated varieties*, Invent. Math. 89 (1987), Theorem 1, pp. 220–221
  (both in the folder source
  `provided-7fc98c3cf1cf4a28ee355a474ea2850645ab4bb5a37e25028414c938744c0cdf`).
- The growth comparison uses two Library bounds: `realStandardCentralGrowth`, the growth
  `(k + 1)^(n (n - 1))` of `U(𝔤)` modulo the central ideal of a nonzero standard module, and
  `realLeftPolynomialGrowth`, the smaller growth on functions that are polynomial along a lower
  root group. The induction on the induction blocks along the Library's ascending tail maps is
  internal to this module.
