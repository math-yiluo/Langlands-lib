# Rearrangement and the weights of a product of minors

These internal lemmas describe the finite combinatorics of the minor products
used in the scalar-commutant proof. The weight at a position counts the sets of
columns containing that position. Expanding its square counts ordered pairs
of sets and yields the sum of the cardinalities of all pairwise intersections.
A nested family attains the termwise upper bound given by the two set sizes.
Equality forces every pair in the other family to attain that bound, so that
family is nested too. For nested families with the same set sizes, the
indices at which a position occurs form final segments of the same order by
size. Their lengths therefore determine the column sets.

For the spectral comparison, equal multisets give a permutation by matching
finite fibers of equal cardinality. On each fiber of the class map, the
rearrangement inequality compares the pairing of two oppositely ordered
families with a permutation of that pairing. If the perturbed weights have
squared norm at most that of the original weights, expanding their squared
norm shows that the sum of squares of the perturbations vanishes.
