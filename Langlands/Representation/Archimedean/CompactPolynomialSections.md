# Nonzero smooth compact sections from polynomial averages

A compact subgroup embedded in a finite-dimensional real algebra sees a separating self-adjoint algebra of coordinate polynomials. The restrictions are dense in the continuous functions by Stone–Weierstrass. For a nonzero matrix coefficient of the inducing action, this gives a polynomial whose Haar pairing with that coefficient is nonzero.

The corresponding vector-valued average gives a covariant function on the algebra. It is nonzero at the identity, and the finite separated expansion of a polynomial of a product shows that the average is a vector-valued polynomial. Such a polynomial is smooth and has a finite-dimensional right translate span. Restricting it to the larger compact subgroup gives a nonzero vector in the compact picture of the archimedean induction.

This derived construction supplies the nonzero smooth compact-finite vectors needed for the proof of Langlands, *On the notion of an automorphic representation* (1979), Lemma 1 and Proposition 2, printed pp. 203–205, Corvallis Part I PDF pp. 198–200.
