# Reduction of the injectivity of generic Langlands quotient maps to Kostant and Vogan

Lean module: `Langlands.Representation.Archimedean.RealGenericLargeReduction`.

This module splits `Langlands.Archimedean.RealGenericLanglandsQuotientInjectiveStatement`
(S_B in `RealGenericParameterReduction.md`) into three statements. It also proves the
implication `realGenericLargeReduction` that S_B follows from them. No new hypothesis enters
S_B. The three statements are only stated here and remain to be proved.

Throughout, `𝔤 = ℂ ⊗_ℝ 𝔤𝔩_n(ℝ)` (the Lean type `ℂ ⊗[ℝ] Matrix (Fin n) (Fin n) ℝ`), which is
the complex Lie algebra `𝔤𝔩_n(ℂ)`. `U(𝔤)` is its universal enveloping algebra (Mathlib's
`UniversalEnvelopingAlgebra ℂ 𝔤`) and `Z(𝔤)` is the center of `U(𝔤)`
(`Subalgebra.center ℂ (UniversalEnvelopingAlgebra ℂ 𝔤)`).

## Definitions

The three definitions below work for any complex Lie algebra `L` and any complex vector space
`V` with a Lie algebra map `π : L → End(V)`.

* `envelopingAction π` is the algebra map `U(L) → End(V)` that extends `π`, given by
  `UniversalEnvelopingAlgebra.lift ℂ π`.
* `HasInfinitesimalCharacter π`: for every `z ∈ Z(L)` there is a scalar `c ∈ ℂ` such that `z`
  acts on `V` as `c · id`. This is Kostant's "V admits an infinitesimal character" (Kostant
  1978, §3.9, p. 142, and §3.1). If `V ≠ 0` the scalar is unique and `z ↦ c` is the central
  character `ξ`. If `V = 0` the condition holds trivially.
* `IsMaximallyFaithful π`: every `u ∈ U(L)` that acts on `V` by `0` lies in
  `Ideal.span Z_V`, where `Z_V = {z ∈ Z(L) | z acts by 0}`. In Kostant's notation
  `U_V ⊆ U Z_V`, where `U_V` is the annihilator of `V` and `Z_V = Z ∩ U_V` ((3.1.1)). The
  reverse inclusion `U Z_V ⊆ U_V` always holds. So this is exactly Kostant's definition
  "V is maximally faithful if `U Z_V = U_V`" (§3.9, p. 142; also p. 105). `Ideal.span` is the
  left ideal generated by `Z_V`. Because `Z_V` is central, this left ideal is the two-sided
  ideal `U Z_V`.

  If `V` has infinitesimal character `ξ`, then `Z_V = Z_ξ = ker ξ`. Then `U Z_ξ` is the
  minimal primitive ideal with infinitesimal character `ξ` (Duflo–Dixmier, as recalled by
  Kostant on p. 142: "ideals of the form `U Z_ξ` have been characterized as the minimal
  primitive ideals in U"). So maximal faithfulness of an irreducible module with an
  infinitesimal character says that its annihilator is the minimal primitive ideal. That is
  Vogan's definition of a *large* module (Vogan 1978, Definition 6.1, p. 89: "The irreducible
  (𝔤, K) module X is called large if its annihilator is the minimal primitive ideal"; the
  smallest annihilator `I_1` for a given infinitesimal character is introduced on
  pp. 85–86).

## The three statements

1. `RealGKModuleInfinitesimalCharacterStatement`. If `(ρ, π)` satisfies
   `IsRealGKModule n ρ π`, `IsIrreducibleGKModule ρ π` and `IsAdmissibleGKModule ρ`, then
   `HasInfinitesimalCharacter π`. This is the standard fact that an irreducible admissible
   Harish-Chandra module has an infinitesimal character. It is recalled without proof by
   Kostant (p. 142, "an irreducible module admits an infinitesimal character") and by Vogan
   (p. 86, "irreducible (𝔤, K) module with infinitesimal character").

   Expected proof: `Z(𝔤)` commutes with `π(𝔤)`. It also commutes with `ρ(O(n))`, because
   `Ad(k)` fixes `Z(𝔤𝔩_n(ℂ))` for every `k ∈ GL_n(ℂ)`. So `Z(𝔤)` preserves the
   finite-dimensional `O(n)`-isotypic components. An eigenspace of `z` on one of them generates
   a nonzero `(𝔤, K)`-submodule on which `z` is scalar, so by irreducibility `z` is scalar on
   all of `V`.

2. `RealWhittakerMaximallyFaithfulStatement`. If `π` (any `𝔤`-module, with no `K`-action)
   satisfies `HasInfinitesimalCharacter π` and `IsRealGenericGKModule n π`, then
   `IsMaximallyFaithful π`.

   Source: Kostant 1978, Theorem 3.9 (p. 142; Theorem D of the introduction, p. 104). For a
   complex semisimple Lie algebra, if `V` admits an infinitesimal character and either `V` or
   its full (algebraic) dual `V'` has a nonzero Whittaker vector, then `U_V = U Z_ξ`.
   Whittaker vectors are taken with respect to a non-singular character `η` of the nilradical
   `𝔫` of a Borel subalgebra, see (3.1.2) and §2.3. The functional `ℓ` in
   `IsRealGenericGKModule` satisfies `ℓ(π(1 ⊗ X) v) = (i ∑ aᵢ X_{i,i+1}) ℓ(v)` for strictly
   upper triangular `X`, with every `aᵢ ≠ 0`. With Kostant's dual action
   `(x·ℓ)(v) = ℓ(x̌ v) = -ℓ(x v)` from (3.8.4) and (3.9.4), `ℓ` is a nonzero Whittaker vector
   in `V'` for the character `η(E_{i,i+1}) = -i aᵢ`. This character is non-singular.

   Kostant's theorem is stated for semisimple `𝔤`, so the proof must pass from `𝔤𝔩_n(ℂ)` to
   `𝔰𝔩_n(ℂ)`. We have `U(𝔤𝔩_n) = U(𝔰𝔩_n)[I]`, and the identity matrix `I` acts by a scalar
   `c`. An element `∑ u_k I^k` (with `u_k ∈ U(𝔰𝔩_n)`) annihilates `V` exactly when
   `∑ c^k u_k` does. Then `u = ∑ u_k (I^k - c^k) + ∑ c^k u_k`, which lies in
   `U Z_V(𝔤𝔩_n)` once `∑ c^k u_k ∈ U(𝔰𝔩_n) Z_V(𝔰𝔩_n)`. For `n ≤ 1` the algebra `𝔤` is
   abelian and the statement is immediate.

3. `RealLargeLanglandsQuotientInjectiveStatement`. This is S_B with `IsRealGenericGKModule n π`
   replaced by `IsMaximallyFaithful π`. All other hypotheses are verbatim those of S_B:
   `IsRealGKModule`, `IsIrreducibleGKModule`, `IsAdmissibleGKModule`, `realRank ds = n`,
   `IsRealLanglandsOrdered ds`, `B ≠ 0` and `IsRealStandardIntertwiner n ρ π ds B`. The
   conclusion is `Function.Injective B`.

   Source: Vogan 1978, Theorem 6.2 (p. 89). It is stated for a quasisplit `G` that is "linear
   and reductive, with abelian Cartan subgroups; it need not be connected", which includes
   `GL_n(ℝ)` with `K = O(n)`. Its proof of (a) ⇒ (f) (p. 93) shows the following: if `X` is
   large and is the Langlands quotient of the standard module `Ind(δ ⊗ ν ⊗ 1)`, then that
   standard module is irreducible. (Translation reduces to nonsingular infinitesimal
   character, and a reducible standard module would make the τ-invariant of `X` nonempty, by
   [11], Theorems 6.15–6.16.) Casselman–Shahidi 1998, p. 562, report this for the generic
   case: for `F = ℝ`, if `J(ν, σ)` is generic then `I(ν, σ)` is irreducible.

   The proof of this statement must also identify the Library's `realStandardModule n ds` and
   its `O(n)`-finite part with the classical standard module and its Harish-Chandra module. It
   must also use the uniqueness of Langlands data to see that `(ρ, π)`, the irreducible image
   of `B`, is the Langlands quotient of that standard module. Then irreducibility of the
   standard module makes the nonzero map `B` injective.

## The reduction

`RealGenericLargeReductionStatement` is the implication
(statement 1) → (statement 2) → (statement 3) → `RealGenericLanglandsQuotientInjectiveStatement`.
The theorem `realGenericLargeReduction` proves it. Given the hypotheses of S_B, statement 1
gives an infinitesimal character. Statement 2 then turns genericity into maximal
faithfulness. Statement 3 then gives injectivity of `B`.

## Provenance

The definitions and statements 2 and 3 come from the cited sources, adapted as described
above. The adaptations are: `𝔤𝔩_n` in place of semisimple `𝔤`; Whittaker vectors in the
algebraic dual expressed through the Library's `IsRealGenericGKModule`; and Vogan's large
modules expressed through Kostant's maximal faithfulness. The split into three statements is
internal to this module.
