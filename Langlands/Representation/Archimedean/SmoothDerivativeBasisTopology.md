# Basis words generate the genuine smooth-vector topology

This internal comparison applies to the existing genuine smooth-vector carrier and Lie derivatives for any real Lie group action by bounded complex linear operators. It proves that words in any chosen real algebraic basis of the model Lie algebra generate the same topology as words in all real Lie directions. No continuity or completeness assumption on the ambient representation is added, and no derivative is replaced by a supplied action.

Genuine differentiation is real linear in its direction, since it is evaluation of the actual manifold derivative. Composing before and after this operator gives linearity in any fixed word slot. Expanding one direction in its finite basis representation expands that slot as a finite linear combination of basis directions. A list induction replaces each non-basis direction in turn. It shows that for an arbitrary source topological space, continuity of all basis-word derivatives implies continuity of all word derivatives.

`basisDerivativeCoordinates` selects the existing derivative coordinates at basis words. It is injective because the empty word is the original vector. The continuity comparison proves that the published derivative topology equals the topology induced by these basis coordinates, giving an actual topological embedding into their product. A countable basis therefore makes the smooth-vector topology metrizable; in particular this holds for every finite-dimensional real Lie algebra, including the real matrix model used for both real and complex general linear groups.

This is the countable-topology portion of comparison with the usual smooth Fréchet category. It does not prove completeness: the image in the coordinate product is not shown closed. It also does not assert joint continuity or smoothness of the transported group action, moderate growth, irreducibility transfer, character rigidity, contragredient comparison, or pairing existence. Those are separate analytic obligations. The provenance is internal multilinearity and topology reasoning from the published genuine derivative definitions.
