# Finite generation over `U(𝔫)` and the two halves of Casselman's algebraic theorem

Lean module: `Langlands.Representation.Archimedean.RealJacquetFiniteDimensional`
(namespace `Langlands.Archimedean`, universe `uV` for the module carrier).

## Setting

The objects are as in the Library module `RealJacquetReduction`. A
`(𝔤𝔩_n(ℝ), O(n))`-module is a representation `ρ` of `Matrix.unitaryGroup (Fin n) ℝ`
(`O(n)`) on a complex vector space `V`, together with a complex Lie algebra map
`π : ℂ ⊗[ℝ] Matrix (Fin n) (Fin n) ℝ → End_ℂ V`, satisfying `IsRealGKModule n ρ π`.
`IsIrreducibleGKModule` and `IsAdmissibleGKModule` are the Library's irreducibility and
admissibility. `𝔫₀` is the space of strictly upper triangular real matrices (`X i j = 0`
whenever `j ≤ i`), the Lie algebra of the upper unipotent group `N₀`. Its complex span is
`𝔫 = ℂ ⊗_ℝ 𝔫₀`. `realJacquetSubspace n π` is `𝔫V`, the span of the vectors `π(1 ⊗ X) v`
with `X ∈ 𝔫₀`.

As in `RealJacquetReduction`, the minimal parabolic is the upper triangular Borel subgroup
`B = M₀A₀N₀`, where `M₀ = B ∩ O(n)` is the finite group of diagonal sign matrices, `A₀` the
positive diagonal matrices (Lie algebra `𝔞₀`, the real diagonal matrices) and `N₀` the upper
unipotent matrices. The Library's `RealJacquetModuleFiniteStatement` (Casselman's theorem in
algebraic form, Wallach 1979, 4.14–4.16, for this minimal parabolic) asks that `V/𝔫V` be
finite-dimensional and nonzero for every irreducible admissible module. This module splits it
into its two halves, which have different proofs. It also states a stronger form of the first
half.

## Statements

All three statements quantify over `n : ℕ`, a complex vector space `V : Type uV` and maps
`ρ`, `π` as above. Each assumes `IsRealGKModule n ρ π`, `IsIrreducibleGKModule ρ π` and
`IsAdmissibleGKModule ρ`, the same hypotheses as `RealJacquetModuleFiniteStatement`.

* `RealJacquetFiniteGenerationStatement`: there is a subspace `F` of `V` with
  `FiniteDimensional ℂ F` such that every subspace `S` with `F ≤ S` and `π(1 ⊗ X) S ⊆ S` for
  every strictly upper triangular real `X` equals `⊤`. The intersection of all such `S` is
  the `U(𝔫)`-submodule generated by `F`, so this says `V = U(𝔫) F`: `V` is finitely generated
  as a `U(𝔫)`-module. The statement is phrased with stable subspaces so that it needs no
  universal enveloping algebra.

* `RealJacquetFiniteDimensionalStatement`: `FiniteDimensional ℂ (V ⧸ realJacquetSubspace n π)`.
  This is the finite-dimensionality half of Wallach's 4.16. Wallach's notation differs from the
  one above: his minimal parabolic is `P₀ = M₀^W N₀` with `M₀^W` the centralizer of `A₀` in
  `GL_n(ℝ)`. Here `M₀^W` is the full diagonal group `(ℝ^×)^n`, which is our `M₀A₀` and has Lie
  algebra `𝔪₀ = 𝔞₀`; its compact part `⁰M₀^W = M₀^W ∩ K` is the sign group `{±1}^n`, which is
  our `M₀`. Wallach's 4.14 (2) says that `V/𝔫₀V` is a finitely generated
  `(𝔪₀, M₀^W ∩ K)`-module. 4.15 notes that for the minimal parabolic `⁰M₀^W = M₀^W ∩ K`, and
  concludes that a finitely generated admissible `(𝔪₀, M₀^W ∩ K)`-module is finite-dimensional.
  4.16 applies this to `V/𝔫₀V`: `1 ≤ dim V/𝔫₀V < ∞` for a nonzero finitely generated admissible
  `V`.

* `RealJacquetNonvanishingStatement`: `realJacquetSubspace n π ≠ ⊤`, that is `V ≠ 𝔫V`.
  This is Wallach's 4.14 (1) for `(P₀, A₀)`. It is the hard half of Casselman's theorem:
  Wallach (4.13) proves it with the subquotient theorem and Casselman's asymptotic expansions of
  matrix coefficients.

## Proved reductions

The proofs reuse `mem_realJacquetSubspace` from `RealJacquetReduction`:
`π(1 ⊗ X) v ∈ realJacquetSubspace n π` for strictly upper triangular `X`, by the
definition of `realJacquetSubspace` as a span.

* `finiteDimensional_quotient_realJacquetSubspace`: if `F` is finite-dimensional and every
  `𝔫`-stable subspace containing `F` is `⊤`, then `V/𝔫V` is finite-dimensional. Proof:
  `F ⊔ 𝔫V` contains `F` and is `𝔫`-stable, because `π(1 ⊗ X) v ∈ 𝔫V` for every `v`.
  Hence `F ⊔ 𝔫V = ⊤`. So the composite `F → V → V/𝔫V` is surjective: write `v = f + m`
  with `f ∈ F`, `m ∈ 𝔫V`, and then `v` and `f` have the same image. A quotient of the
  finite-dimensional `F` is finite-dimensional (`Module.Finite.of_surjective`).

* `realJacquetFiniteDimensional_of_finiteGeneration`:
  `RealJacquetFiniteGenerationStatement → RealJacquetFiniteDimensionalStatement`, by the
  previous lemma applied to the `F` the hypothesis provides.

* `realJacquetModuleFinite_of_finiteDimensional_of_nonvanishing`:
  `RealJacquetFiniteDimensionalStatement → RealJacquetNonvanishingStatement →
  RealJacquetModuleFiniteStatement`. The conclusion is the conjunction of the two statements,
  under the same hypotheses.

So proofs of `RealJacquetFiniteGenerationStatement` and `RealJacquetNonvanishingStatement`
give `RealJacquetModuleFiniteStatement`. With the Library's
`realPrincipalSeriesSubrepresentationJacquetReduction` and `RealJacquetFrobeniusStatement`,
they then give Casselman's subrepresentation theorem `RealPrincipalSeriesSubrepresentationStatement`.

## Provenance

`RealJacquetFiniteDimensionalStatement` and `RealJacquetNonvanishingStatement` are the two
conjuncts of the Library's `RealJacquetModuleFiniteStatement`, with the same hypotheses. They
are Wallach 1979, 4.14–4.16 for `GL_n(ℝ)`, `K = O(n)` and the upper triangular Borel subgroup.

`RealJacquetFiniteGenerationStatement` is an intermediate statement of our own. It
strengthens Wallach's 4.14 (2) / 4.15 for this case to finite generation over `U(𝔫)`, which is
what the standard argument gives. Write `U(𝔤) = U(𝔫) U(𝔞₀) U(𝔨)` (Iwasawa decomposition
`𝔤 = 𝔫 ⊕ 𝔞₀ ⊕ 𝔨`). A finite-dimensional `K`-stable `F₀` generates `V` over `U(𝔤)`, by
irreducibility. The center of `U(𝔤)` acts by scalars, and `U(𝔞₀)` is finite over the image
of the center modulo `𝔫U(𝔤)`; this is Harish-Chandra's finiteness argument. Hence
`V = U(𝔫) F` with `F = ∑ a_j F₀` for finitely many monomials `a_j ∈ U(𝔞₀)`. The statement
is recorded because the nonvanishing half is expected to need it.
