# Reduction of Vogan's theorem for large Langlands quotients of `GL_n(ℂ)`

Lean module: `Langlands.Representation.Archimedean.ComplexLargeStandardIrreducibleReduction`.

This module splits `Langlands.Archimedean.ComplexLargeLanglandsQuotientInjectiveStatement`
(V1′ of `ComplexLargePrincipalSeriesReduction.md`) into two statements about complex standard
modules. It proves the implication `complexLargeStandardIrreducibleReduction` (the two
statements imply V1′) and the lemmas that proof uses. No hypothesis is added to V1′. The two
statements are only stated here; the Library proves them in
`ComplexStandardLanglandsQuotientProof` and `ComplexLargeStandardIrreducibleProof`. The split is the complex counterpart of
`RealLargeLanglandsQuotientReduction`, which splits the real statement
`RealLargeLanglandsQuotientInjectiveStatement` in the same way, and it reuses that module's
definitions `IsGKSubmodule` and `IsMaximallyFaithfulModulo` and its lemma
`isMaximallyFaithfulModulo_ker_iff`.

All declarations are in the namespace `Langlands.Archimedean`.

Notation: `𝔤 = ℂ ⊗_ℝ 𝔤𝔩_n(ℂ)`, `K = U(n)`, and `U(𝔤)` is the enveloping algebra. For a list
`ds` of complex characters (`ComplexCharacterData`, `z ↦ (z/|z|)^l |z|^{2t}`), `I(ds)` is the
`(𝔤𝔩_n(ℂ), U(n))`-module `(complexStandardRep n ds, complexStandardLie n ds)` of the Library
(module `PrincipalSeriesGKModule`). It acts on the `U(n)`-finite functions
`kFiniteFunctions (complexStandardModule n ds) (unitaryCompact ℂ n)` of the Library's standard
module by right translation and right derivatives. `IsComplexLanglandsOrdered ds` says that the
real parts of the exponents do not increase, `Re t₁ ≥ ⋯ ≥ Re t_n`
(`ComplexGenericParameterReduction.md`).

## Lemmas proved here

General lemmas, for any group `K`, complex Lie algebra `L`, complex vector spaces `X`, `V`, and
a linear map `B : X → V` with `B(σ(k) f) = ρ(k) B(f)` and `B(τ(Y) f) = π(Y) B(f)` for all
`k ∈ K`, `Y ∈ L`:

* `isGKSubmodule_ker_of_intertwine`: `ker B` is a `(𝔤, K)`-submodule (`IsGKSubmodule σ τ`).
* `isGKSubmodule_map_of_intertwine`: `B` maps `(𝔤, K)`-submodules of `(σ, τ)` to
  `(𝔤, K)`-submodules of `(ρ, π)`.
* `eq_ker_of_largest_proper`: if moreover `(ρ, π)` is irreducible (`IsIrreducibleGKModule`),
  `B ≠ 0`, and `R` is a `(𝔤, K)`-submodule of `X` with `R ≠ ⊤` that contains every
  `(𝔤, K)`-submodule `W ≠ ⊤`, then `R = ker B`. Proof: `ker B` is a `(𝔤, K)`-submodule, and
  `ker B ≠ ⊤` because `B ≠ 0`, so `ker B ≤ R`. The image `B(R)` is a `(𝔤, K)`-submodule of the
  irreducible `(ρ, π)`, so it is `⊥` or `⊤`. If `B(R) = ⊤`, then for every `f` there is `r ∈ R`
  with `B(f) = B(r)`. Then `f - r ∈ ker B ≤ R`, so `f ∈ R`, and `R = ⊤`, which is false. So
  `B(R) = ⊥`, that is, `R ≤ ker B`.

Lemmas for the complex case:

* `isComplexLargeGKModule_iff_isMaximallyFaithful`: `IsComplexLargeGKModule n π` (module
  `ComplexGenericStandardInjectiveReduction`) is `IsMaximallyFaithful π`. The proof is `Iff.rfl`:
  both say that every `u ∈ U(𝔤)` acting by zero lies in the left ideal of `U(𝔤)` generated by the
  central elements acting by zero.
* For `A` with `IsComplexStandardIntertwining n ρ π ds A`:
  * `IsComplexStandardIntertwining.map_complexStandardRep`:
    `A(complexStandardRep n ds k f) = ρ(k) A(f)` for `k ∈ U(n)`;
  * `IsComplexStandardIntertwining.map_complexStandardLie`:
    `A(complexStandardLie n ds Y f) = π(Y) A(f)` for every `Y ∈ ℂ ⊗_ℝ 𝔤𝔩_n(ℂ)`. The predicate
    only states this for the real directions `1 ⊗ X`; the general case follows by induction on
    the tensor `Y`, using `c ⊗ X = c · (1 ⊗ X)` and the `ℂ`-linearity of both sides.

## The two statements

1. `ComplexStandardLanglandsQuotientStatement` (L). Let `ds` be a list of complex characters
   with `ds.length = n` and `IsComplexLanglandsOrdered ds`. Then `I(ds)` has a largest proper
   `(𝔤, K)`-submodule. That is, there is a `(𝔤, K)`-submodule `R ≠ ⊤` that contains every
   `(𝔤, K)`-submodule `W ≠ ⊤`. The quotient `I(ds) / R` is the Langlands quotient.

   Sources:
   * Badulescu–Renard 2010, Theorem 3.3(i) (printed pp. 1124–1125), for Harish-Chandra modules
     of `GL(n, A)` with `F = A = ℂ` allowed (§2.2) and `K = U(n)` (§2.3). For `GL_n(ℂ)` the
     essentially square-integrable representations are the characters
     `δ(z) = |z|_ℂ^β (z/|z|)^m` of `GL_1(ℂ) = ℂˣ` (§9.1, p. 1132: `D = D_1 = Irr_1`), so the
     standard representations are principal series. If `d = (d_1, …, d_l)` is written in
     standard order, `e(d_1) ≥ … ≥ e(d_l)` (Definition 3.2; the inequalities are `\geq` in the
     arXiv source 0905.4143 held in the same folder), then `λ(d) = d_1 × … × d_l` "has a unique
     irreducible quotient `Lg(d)`, appearing with multiplicity one in a Jordan–Hölder sequence
     of `λ(d)`". Since `ν = |det|_ℂ`, the character `(z/|z|)^l |z|^{2t} = (z/|z|)^l |z|_ℂ^t` has
     `e = Re t`, so the standard order is `IsComplexLanglandsOrdered`.
   * Knapp 1994, Theorem 4(a) (p. 405): if `Re t₁ ≥ ⋯ ≥ Re t_n`, the principal series
     `I(σ₁, …, σ_n)` of (4.2) has a unique irreducible quotient `J(σ₁, …, σ_n)`. Knapp
     attributes the theorem to Želobenko and Naimark.

   From the source to statement L: `λ(d)` has finite length (it has a Jordan–Hölder sequence),
   so every proper submodule lies in a maximal proper submodule `M`. Then `λ(d)/M` is an
   irreducible quotient, so `λ(d)/M ≅ Lg(d)`. Suppose there were two different maximal
   submodules `M_1` and `M_2`. Then `M_1 + M_2 = λ(d)`, so
   `λ(d)/(M_1 ∩ M_2) ≅ λ(d)/M_1 × λ(d)/M_2 ≅ Lg(d) × Lg(d)`. This would make `Lg(d)` occur
   twice in a Jordan–Hölder sequence, which contradicts multiplicity one. So there is exactly
   one maximal proper submodule `R`, and it contains every proper submodule. `R ≠ ⊤` because
   `λ(d) ≠ 0`.

   A proof of L must also identify the Library's `complexStandardModule n ds`, its
   `U(n)`-finite part and the order `IsComplexLanglandsOrdered` with the classical principal
   series, its Harish-Chandra module and the standard order. The conventions of the Library's
   left-equivariant model (normalized induction from the upper triangular Borel subgroup; the
   quotient form of the classification uses non-increasing real parts) are explained in
   `ComplexLanglandsQuotientReduction.md`.

2. `ComplexLargeStandardIrreducibleStatement` (V). Let `ds` be a list of complex characters
   with `ds.length = n` and `IsComplexLanglandsOrdered ds`. Let `R` be a largest proper
   `(𝔤, K)`-submodule of `I(ds)`, with exactly the three properties produced by L. If
   `IsMaximallyFaithfulModulo (complexStandardLie n ds) R`, that is, if the Langlands quotient
   `I(ds) / R` is maximally faithful, then `R = ⊥`. In other words, `I(ds)` is irreducible.

   Source: Vogan 1978, Theorem 6.2 (p. 89), proof of (a) ⇒ (f) (p. 93). The theorem is stated
   for quasisplit `G`, "linear and reductive, with abelian Cartan subgroups; it need not be
   connected". `GL_n(ℂ)`, as a real group with maximal compact subgroup `U(n)`, is such a
   group. In the proof, `X` is an irreducible large `(𝔤, K)`-module. Translation (Zuckerman
   periodicity and the Borho–Jantzen translation principle) reduces to nonsingular
   infinitesimal character. By the Langlands classification, `X` is the Langlands quotient of
   a standard module `Ind(δ ⊗ ν ⊗ 1)`. If that standard module were reducible, then by
   Speh–Vogan ([11] in Vogan's paper), Theorems 6.15 and 6.16, the τ-invariant of `X` would be
   nonempty. This contradicts largeness. So the standard module is irreducible.

   From the source to statement V: the irreducible quotient `I(ds) / R` is admissible, so it
   has an infinitesimal character `ξ`. Its maximal faithfulness then says that its annihilator
   is `U(𝔤) · ker ξ`, the minimal primitive ideal. That is Vogan's "large" (Definition 6.1,
   p. 89; see `ComplexGenericStandardInjectiveReduction.md` for `IsComplexLargeGKModule`).
   Vogan's conclusion is that the standard module of `I(ds) / R` is irreducible. Since `R ≠ ⊤`
   and `R` is a `(𝔤, K)`-submodule, this gives `R = ⊥`. Vogan's standard module is the one
   attached to the quotient by the Langlands classification. For `GL_n(ℂ)`, Knapp's Theorem
   4(c) says that the parameters are unique up to permutation, and principal series whose
   parameters differ by a permutation have the same composition factors (Badulescu–Renard
   2010, Remark 2.3 and Proposition 2.4: `π_1 × π_2 = π_2 × π_1` in the Grothendieck group). So
   irreducibility of one standard module of `I(ds) / R` implies irreducibility of `I(ds)`. A
   proof of V needs the same identification of the Library's standard modules as L, and it
   must carry out Vogan's reduction to nonsingular infinitesimal character or argue otherwise
   in the singular case.

## The reduction

`ComplexLargeStandardIrreducibleReductionStatement` (universe `uV`) is the implication
L → V → V1′, and the theorem `complexLargeStandardIrreducibleReduction` proves it. The proof
assumes the hypotheses of V1′: `A ≠ 0` with `IsComplexStandardIntertwining n ρ π ds A`, an
irreducible `(ρ, π)` with `IsComplexLargeGKModule n π`, `ds.length = n` and
`IsComplexLanglandsOrdered ds`. The admissibility and `IsComplexGKModule` hypotheses of V1′ are
not used. The steps are:

1. `A` is onto, by the Library's `surjective_of_isComplexStandardIntertwining`.
2. L gives the largest proper `(𝔤, K)`-submodule `R` of `I(ds)`.
3. `eq_ker_of_largest_proper`, with `map_complexStandardRep` and `map_complexStandardLie`,
   gives `R = ker A`.
4. `isMaximallyFaithfulModulo_ker_iff` (`A` is onto and intertwines `complexStandardLie` with
   `π`) and `isComplexLargeGKModule_iff_isMaximallyFaithful` turn the largeness of `π` into
   `IsMaximallyFaithfulModulo (complexStandardLie n ds) R`.
5. V gives `R = ⊥`. So `ker A = ⊥`, and `A` is injective.

In step 3 the identification `ker A = R` is the abstract counterpart of "`(ρ, π)` is the
Langlands quotient of `I(ds)`". The proof gets it from the maximality in L and needs no
uniqueness of Langlands data.

## Provenance

Derived. L is the Langlands quotient theorem for `GL_n(ℂ)` (Badulescu–Renard 2010, Theorem
3.3(i); Knapp 1994, Theorem 4(a)) in largest-proper-submodule form, by the finite-length
argument above. V is Vogan 1978, Theorem 6.2, proof of (a) ⇒ (f), in submodule form,
specialized to `GL_n(ℂ)`. Both are the complex counterparts of
`RealStandardLanglandsQuotientStatement` and `RealLargeStandardIrreducibleStatement` (module
`RealLargeLanglandsQuotientReduction`). The split into L and V and all the lemmas are internal
to this module and proved.
