# A polynomial in the coordinate square sum on a complex standard vector

Take a vector \(f\) in the unitary-finite part of the complex standard
module. Its compact orbit spans a finite-dimensional subspace of the
space of functions with the topology of pointwise convergence. The
subspace is closed and stable under right unitary translation.
For a skew-Hermitian direction \(X\), the curve \(\exp(tX)\) is unitary.
The pointwise limit of the difference quotients of its right translates
therefore belongs to the same subspace. The derivative along this curve
is the right derivative in direction \(X\).

The compact square sum preserves the compact orbit span inside the
standard module. The trace Casimir acts by a scalar because complex
standard modules have an infinitesimal character. The coordinate square
sum consequently preserves the same finite-dimensional subspace
containing \(f\). Cayley–Hamilton gives a monic polynomial in that
operator which kills \(f\), with polynomial evaluation compatible with
the restriction to the subspace. The finite-dimensional invariant
subspace is also available directly for the differential system.
