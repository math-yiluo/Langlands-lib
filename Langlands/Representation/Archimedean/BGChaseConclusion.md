# Conclusion of the Bernstein–Gelfand chase

Module: `Langlands.Representation.Archimedean.BGChaseConclusion`, namespace
`Langlands.Archimedean.BGChase`. It completes the formalization of Bernstein–Gelfand 1980, proof
of Theorem 5.9 in 5.10, for the bimodule statement. `U = U(𝔤𝔩_n(ℂ))` and `M = M⁻(λ)`; `c_m`,
`rmulE`, `exists_h` are from `BGChaseCore`, and `BData`, `pmap` (`p`), `kE` (`κ`) and `rhoR` (`ρ`)
are from `BGChaseSetup`/`BGChaseEnveloping`. Everything here is proved.

## Truncations and the subspace `F` (abstract `E`)

These steps are stated for an abstract type `E`, which keeps instance synthesis on subspaces of
`E ⊗ U` working. They are applied with `E` the subspace of the bimodule statement.

- `truncE E d`: the image of `E ⊗ U_d`, `U_d` the Library's degree filtration. It is monotone
  (`truncE_mono`), exhausts `E ⊗ U` (`exists_mem_truncE`), is stable under `κ ⊗ ad`
  (`tensorLie_mem_truncE`, since `ad` preserves `U_d`), and is finite-dimensional for
  finite-dimensional `E` (instance).
- `exists_F`: let `K ⊆ E ⊗ U` be `κ ⊗ ad`-stable, and suppose every `e ⊗ gen` is `c_gen(k)` for
  some `k ∈ K`. Then some finite-dimensional `κ ⊗ ad`-stable `F ⊆ K` has the same property. Take
  `F = K ∩ truncE E d` with `d` large enough for the elements chosen for a basis of `E`.
- `exists_mem_cM_eq`: assume in addition that `K` is stable under right multiplication, that `κ`
  has integral weights, and the five inputs used by `exists_h`. Then for every `e ∈ E` some
  `h ∈ K` satisfies `c_m(h) = e ⊗ m` for all `m ∈ M`. Apply `exists_h` to `F` from `exists_F`,
  with the restricted representation and the inclusion. `F` has integral weights by
  `intSpan_restrictLie_of_le`, because `κ ⊗ ad` has integral weights (`intSpan_tensorLie`,
  `intSpan_adU`). The span of the `x · u`, `x ∈ F`, lies in `K`.

## The bimodule data

For `D : BData n V`:

- `cM_gen_rmul_of_lt`: `c_gen(x · E_ab) = 0` for `b < a`;
- `exists_ker_cM_gen`: suppose `E` generates `V` under `ℓ` and the `ℓ(E_pq)v`, `p < q`, span `V`.
  Then every `e ∈ E` has some `k ∈ ker p` with `c_gen(k) = e ⊗ gen`. Write `e = p(y)` with `y` in
  the span of the `x · E_qp`, `q > p` (possible by `ℓ_pmap` and surjectivity of `p`), and take
  `k = e ⊗ 1 - y`; then `c_gen(y) = 0`.
- `pmap_eq_zero_of_cM`: assume Weight and Duflo, `E` finite-dimensional, and that the center acts
  through `ρ` by a character `χ` that is also the central character of `M`. If `c_m(x) = 0` for
  all `m`, then `p(x) = 0`. Each coordinate `u_i = (b_i^* ⊗ 1)(x)` acts by zero on `M`
  (`lid_rTensor_lTensor`), so lies in the ideal generated by the `z - χ(z)`
  (`mem_span_of_action_eq_zero`). There `ρ` vanishes (`rhoR_eq_zero_of_mem_span`), and
  `p(x) = Σ_i ρ(u_i) b_i` (`pmap_eq_sum`).

## The theorem

`complexJacquetBimodule_of_inputs`: the six statements `LowerVermaWeightStatement`,
`LowerVermaDufloStatement`, `KostantMultiplicityStatement.{uV}`, `CentralCharacterStatement`,
`LowerVermaHomBoundStatement.{uV}` and `LowerVermaProjectiveStatement.{uV, uV}` imply
`ComplexJacquetBimoduleStatement.{uV}`.

Proof. Suppose the `ℓ(E_pq)v`, `p < q`, span `V`.

1. `V ≠ 0` because `E ≠ 0`, so the center acts through `ρ` by a character `χ`
   (`exists_rhoR_character`).
2. `CentralCharacterStatement` gives a `Z`-extremal `λ` with central character `χ`.
3. `κ` on `E` has integral weights (`intSpan_kE`). The kernel `K` of `p` is stable under right
   multiplication and `κ ⊗ ad` (`rmul_mem_ker`, `adP_mem_ker`), and `exists_ker_cM_gen` applies.
   So `exists_mem_cM_eq` gives, for each `e ∈ E`, some `h ∈ ker p` with `c_m(h) = e ⊗ m`.
4. Then `c_m(h - e ⊗ 1) = 0` for all `m`, so `pmap_eq_zero_of_cM` gives
   `0 = p(h - e ⊗ 1) = -e`.
5. Hence `E = 0`, a contradiction.

This differs from Bernstein–Gelfand's presentation in one respect. They argue with the functor
`X ↦ X ⊗_U M` and the bimodule `L(M, M)`; here everything is made explicit through the map
`p : E ⊗ U → V`.
