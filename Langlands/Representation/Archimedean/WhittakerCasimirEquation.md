# The first-order equation from the Casimir

In the positive-root expansion of the test Casimir, apply
the differentiated Whittaker covariance to the outer
upper-root field. Nonsimple roots have zero derivative.
For a simple root, the real and imaginary fields combine
with their real trace duals to give the coefficient
`a • 1 - b • I` in the opposite simple root. The sum over
simple roots gives one real matrix direction. The terms
from the diagonal fields and the commutators form the
diagonal test operator.

This proves the exact equation between distribution values
on the Casimir, the diagonal operator, and the derived
negative-root direction, with the factor `2 * I` from the
two opposite-root terms and the character derivative.
It is the differential calculation in Shalika (1974),
Proposition 2.10 before restricting to a Bruhat cell.
