# The Jacquet module of an irreducible admissible `(𝔤𝔩_n(ℂ), U(n))`-module is finite-dimensional

Module `Langlands.Representation.Archimedean.ComplexJacquetFinite`.

## Statements

All three statements use the binders of the Library's `ComplexJacquetModuleFiniteStatement`
(module `ComplexJacquetReduction`). They range over:
- every `n : ℕ` and every complex vector space `V : Type uV`;
- every `ρ : Representation ℂ (Matrix.unitaryGroup (Fin n) ℂ) V`;
- every `π : (ℂ ⊗[ℝ] Matrix (Fin n) (Fin n) ℂ) →ₗ⁅ℂ⁆ Module.End ℂ V`;

and assume `IsComplexGKModule n ρ π`, `IsIrreducibleGKModule ρ π` and `IsAdmissibleGKModule ρ`.
`complexJacquetSubspace n π` is the span `𝔫V` of the vectors `π(1 ⊗ X) v`, with `X` a strictly
upper triangular complex matrix.

- `ComplexJacquetModuleFiniteDimensionalStatement`: `V ⧸ complexJacquetSubspace n π` is
  finite-dimensional.
- `ComplexJacquetModuleNonzeroStatement`: `complexJacquetSubspace n π ≠ ⊤`, i.e. `𝔫V ≠ V`.

`ComplexJacquetModuleFiniteStatement` is exactly their conjunction.

Provenance: Wallach 1979, printed p. 77 (PDF p. 80):
- 4.14(1) states `V ≠ 𝔫V`.
- 4.14(2) with 4.15–4.16 gives `dim V/𝔫₀V < ∞` for the minimal parabolic of a finitely
  generated admissible module; an irreducible module is generated by any nonzero vector.

The statements here specialize these to `GL_n(ℂ)`, `K = U(n)` and the upper triangular Borel
subgroup, exactly as the Library's `ComplexJacquetModuleFiniteStatement` does. The split into
two statements is ours. It separates the finiteness half, proved here, from Casselman's
nonvanishing theorem, which is not proved here.

## Results

- `complexJacquetModuleFiniteDimensional : ComplexJacquetModuleFiniteDimensionalStatement.{uV}`
  proves the finiteness half, with no hypotheses beyond those of the statement.
- `complexJacquetModuleFinite_of_nonzero : ComplexJacquetModuleNonzeroStatement.{uV} →
  ComplexJacquetModuleFiniteStatement.{uV}` pairs the finiteness theorem with a proof of
  nonvanishing.

The nonvanishing half (Casselman's theorem proper) is **not** proved in this module.

## Proof of finiteness

Everything except the last section is in namespace `Langlands.Archimedean.ComplexJacquetFinite`.
Wallach 1979, 4.13, only refers to proofs elsewhere. The route implemented here is the
standard one: an infinitesimal character, a spanning argument, and the finiteness of the Cartan
part over the image of the center. Its details are ours. It avoids the Poincaré–Birkhoff–Witt
theorem: it works with operators on `V` and with explicit Gelfand elements.

### 1. Gelfand's recursion modulo a subspace

This section concerns a family `e : Fin n → Fin n → End(V)` satisfying the `𝔤𝔩_n` relations
`[e_ab, e_cd] = δ_bc e_ad − δ_da e_cb` (`IsGlFamily`). Let `E` be the matrix of the `e_ab`.
- `comm_pow`: by induction on `k`, `[e_ac, (E^k)_xy] = δ_xc (E^k)_ay − δ_ay (E^k)_xc`.
- Fix a subspace `N` such that `e_ab V ⊆ N` for `a < b` and `e_aa N ⊆ N`.
- `upper_pow`: for `c < b`, `(E^k)_cb V ⊆ N`. Write `(E^{k+1})_cb = Σ_d e_cd (E^k)_db`:
  - the terms with `d > c` lie in `N` because `e_cd V ⊆ N`;
  - the term `d = c` lies in `N` by stability under `e_cc`;
  - for `d < c`, move `e_cd` to the right with `comm_pow`.
- `gelPoly n k a` are the polynomials with `g_0(a) = 1` and
  `g_{k+1}(a) = (X_a + a) g_k(a) − Σ_{c<a} g_k(c)`. Here `a` is the 0-based index.
- `diag_pow`: for every algebra map `ψ` sending `X_a` to `e_aa`, `(E^k)_aa v − ψ(g_k(a)) v ∈ N`.
  The induction splits `(E^{k+1})_aa v = Σ_c e_ac (E^k)_ca v` into three parts:
  - `c > a`: these terms lie in `N`;
  - `c = a`: this term gives `e_aa (E^k)_aa v`;
  - `c < a`: here `e_ac (E^k)_ca v = (E^k)_ca e_ac v + (E^k)_aa v − (E^k)_cc v`, by `comm_pow`,
    and the first summand lies in `N` by `upper_pow`.
- `totalDegree_gelPoly` and `totalDegree_gelPoly_sub`: `g_k(a)` has total degree `≤ k`, and
  `g_{k+1}(a) − X_a^{k+1}` has total degree `≤ k`.

### 2. Reduction modulo power-sum relations

- `Close I f d`: `f` is congruent modulo the ideal `I` to a polynomial of total degree `≤ d`. It
  is stable under sums, products (degrees add), constants and renaming (`Close.rename`).
- `esymm_close`: suppose every power sum `p_b`, `1 ≤ b ≤ n`, is `Close` in degree `b − 1`. Then
  so is every elementary symmetric polynomial `e_k`, `1 ≤ k ≤ n`, in degree `k − 1`. This uses
  Newton's identities (`MvPolynomial.mul_esymm_eq_sum`) and induction on `k`.
- `xpow_close`: then `X_a^n` is `Close` in degree `n − 1`. Evaluate Vieta's formula
  `∏_i (T + X_i) = Σ_j e_j T^{n−j}` (`MvPolynomial.prod_C_add_X_eq_sum_esymm`) at `T = −X_a`.
- `mem_boxSpan_sup`: suppose every variable satisfies `X_s^m ≡ (degree < m)` modulo `J`. Then
  every polynomial lies in `boxSpan + J`, where `boxSpan` is the span of the monomials whose
  exponents are all `< m` (`boxSet`). The proof is a strong induction on total degree: a
  monomial with some exponent `≥ m` is `X^β X_s^m`, which is congruent to `X^β r` of smaller
  degree.

### 3. The representation

- `ComplexGenericLarge.prodAction π` is the action of `𝔤𝔩_n(ℂ) × 𝔤𝔩_n(ℂ)` obtained by
  transporting `π` along `z ⊗ X ↦ (zX, zX̄)`.
- `ee π i p q` is its value on the matrix unit `E_pq` of factor `i`.
  - `isGlFamily_ee`: these operators satisfy the `𝔤𝔩_n` relations.
  - `commute_ee`: the diagonal ones of both factors commute.
- `prodAction_apply_eq` writes `prodAction π Y` as a combination of the operators `π(1 ⊗ Z)`,
  with `Z = Y₀, iY₀, Ȳ₁, iȲ₁`. Consequently:
  - `ee_up`: for `p < q`, `ee π i p q` maps `V` into `𝔫V` (`prodAction_mem`).
  - `ee_diag`: `ee π i p p` maps `𝔫V` into itself (`prodAction_diag_mem`, `pi_diag_mem`). This
    uses the Library's `diag_mem_complexJacquetSubspace`.
- `diagAct π` is the action of polynomials in the `2n` variables `X_(i,a)` through the diagonal
  generators. It is defined through `diagAlg π`, the commutative algebra they generate, which
  carries the instance `diagAlgCommRing`.
- `annIdeal π` is the ideal of polynomials `f` with `diagAct π f V ⊆ 𝔫V`.
- `envelopingAction_pw` and `trace_scalar`: the image of Kostant's element
  `Kostant.gel … i k = tr (E^{(i)})^k` is `Σ_p (E^k)_pp`. This element is central
  (`Kostant.gel_mem_center`), so it acts by a scalar (`prodAction_hasInfinitesimalCharacter`;
  this is where irreducibility and admissibility enter).
- `side_rel`: combined with `diag_pow` for `N = 𝔫V`, the renamed polynomial
  `Σ_a g_k(a) − c_k` lies in `annIdeal π`.
- `psum_close` and `xpow_rel`: since `Σ_a g_k(a) − p_k` has degree `< k`, every power sum is
  `Close` in the side ideal. By step 2, each `X_(i,a)^n` is `Close` modulo `annIdeal π` in
  degree `n − 1`.
- `diagAct_mem_boxImage_sup`: for every polynomial `f` and `w ∈ W`, `diagAct π f w` lies in
  `boxImage π W + 𝔫V`. Here `boxImage π W` is the sum, over the finitely many box monomials
  `X^α`, of `diagAct π X^α (W)`. It is finite-dimensional when `W` is
  (`finiteDimensional_boxImage`).

### 4. Spanning

- `skew_stable` (section `Derivative`): every `U(n)`-stable subspace `W` is stable under
  `π(1 ⊗ Y)` for skew-Hermitian `Y`.
  - `exp(tY)` is unitary by the imported
    `ComplexDuality.LieInvariance.exp_smul_mem_unitaryGroup`, rewriting `star Y` as `Yᴴ`.
  - Its entries have derivative `Y i j` at `0` (`hasDerivAt_expCurve`).
  - If a functional `ℓ` vanishes on `W`, then `t ↦ ℓ(ρ(exp tY) w)` vanishes identically. By the
    derivative clause of `IsComplexGKModule` its derivative is `ℓ(π(1 ⊗ Y) w)`, so that value
    is `0`. Since `ℓ` was arbitrary, `π(1 ⊗ Y) w ∈ W`
    (`Submodule.exists_dual_map_eq_bot_of_notMem`).
- `exists_skew_add_upper`: every matrix is a skew-Hermitian matrix plus an upper triangular
  one (`Upper`).
- `upperClosure π W` is the smallest subspace containing `W` that is stable under
  `π(1 ⊗ X)` for upper triangular `X`.
  - `upperClosure_skew`: it is stable under the skew-Hermitian directions. The proof considers
    the subspace of vectors whose images under all skew-Hermitian directions stay in the
    closure. That subspace contains `W` and is upper-stable, because
    `[1 ⊗ Y, 1 ⊗ X] = 1 ⊗ (YX − XY)` splits again as skew-Hermitian plus upper triangular
    (`complex_map_commutator`).
  - `upperClosure_pi_stable`: hence it is `π`-stable.
- `upperClosure_eq_top`: let `G` be the smallest `π`-stable subspace containing `W`.
  - `G` is `ρ`-stable, by `ComplexGenericLarge.rho_mul_pi`.
  - So `G = V` by irreducibility, and therefore the upper closure is `V`.
- `diagSpan π W` is the span of the vectors `diagAct π f w` with `w ∈ W`.
  - `upper_stable`: `diagSpan π W + 𝔫V` is upper-stable. Split an upper triangular `X` as its
    diagonal plus a strictly upper part. The strictly upper part maps `V` into `𝔫V`. The
    diagonal part is a sum of terms `π(1 ⊗ E_jj c)`, and each equals
    `diagAct π (c X_(0,j) + c̄ X_(1,j))` (`pi_single_diag_eq`).
  - `diagSpan_sup_eq_top`: hence `diagSpan π W + 𝔫V = V`.

### 5. Conclusion

`complexJacquetModule_finiteDimensional` takes `W = FiniteVectors.orbitSpan ρ v` for some
`v ≠ 0`. This `W` is finite-dimensional by the first clause of `IsComplexGKModule` and is
`U(n)`-stable. Then
`V = diagSpan π W + 𝔫V ⊆ boxImage π W + 𝔫V`,
so `V/𝔫V` is the image of the finite-dimensional space `boxImage π W`.
`complexJacquetModuleFiniteDimensional` restates this in the form of the statement.
