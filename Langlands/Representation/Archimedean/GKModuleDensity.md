# Schur's lemma and Jacobson density for irreducible admissible `(𝔤, K)`-modules

Lean module: `Langlands.Representation.Archimedean.GKModuleDensity`.

Worker 140 wrote this module for Task 7063 (`f2d2e257-dd4b-4820-96ab-faa9c832ffb8`), the proof of
`ComplexStandardLanglandsQuotientStatement`. It is field-independent. In Task 7063 it is applied to
the irreducible tempered complex standard modules (`ComplexTemperedStandardIrreducibleStatement`)
to separate variables in the Langlands lemma along a block parabolic subgroup.

Setting: `K` is a group with a topology, `L` a complex Lie algebra, `V` a complex vector space,
`ρ : K → End_ℂ V` a representation and `π : L → End_ℂ V` a map of complex Lie algebras (`End_ℂ V`
with the commutator bracket). `IsIrreducibleGKModule ρ π` (module `GKModule`) says that `V ≠ 0`
and the only subspaces stable under all `ρ(k)` and `π(X)` are `0` and `V`; `IsAdmissibleGKModule ρ`
says that for every continuous matrix representation `τ : K → M_d(ℂ)` the space
`gkIntertwiners ρ d τ` of linear maps `T : ℂ^d → V` with `T(τ(k) x) = ρ(k) T(x)` is
finite-dimensional. Declarations without a prefix are in the namespace
`Langlands.Archimedean.GKModuleDensity`.

## Definitions

* `gens ρ π : K ⊕ L → End_ℂ V` sends `k` to `ρ(k)` and `X` to `π(X)`.
* `heckeAlgebra ρ π` is the subalgebra of `End_ℂ V` generated by these operators;
  `V` is a module over it by evaluation (`heckeAlgebra_smul_def`).
* `Hyp ρ π` (structure): irreducible, admissible, every vector lies in a finite-dimensional
  `ρ`-stable subspace, and every function `k ↦ ℓ(ρ(k) v)` (`ℓ` a linear functional) is continuous.
* For a finite-dimensional `ρ`-stable subspace `F` with basis `b` (`b : Basis (Fin d) ℂ F`):
  `matrixRep F hF b : K →* M_d(ℂ)` is the matrix of `ρ(k)|_F` in `b`, and `inclusion F b : ℂ^d → V`
  sends `x` to `∑ x_j b_j`.

## The statement

`GKModuleDensityStatement`: for `K`, `L`, `V` (types in `Type`) and `(ρ, π)` irreducible, admissible,
with every vector in a finite-dimensional `ρ`-stable subspace and continuous matrix coefficients, and
for every finite set `s ⊆ V` and every linear map `f : V → V`, there is an element `a` of the free
`ℂ`-algebra on `K ⊕ L` (a noncommutative polynomial in the symbols `ρ(k)`, `π(X)`) such that its
image under `FreeAlgebra.lift ℂ (gens ρ π)` agrees with `f` on `s`. Proved as
`Langlands.Archimedean.gkModuleDensity`.

Provenance. Standard: Schur's lemma for admissible irreducible `(𝔤, K)`-modules and the Jacobson
density theorem (Mathlib `jacobson_density`, after Lorenz). No literature claim beyond these
standard facts.

## Proof

1. **Matrix representations** (`matrixRep`, `inclusion_mem`, `continuous_matrixRep`). For a
   finite-dimensional `ρ`-stable `F` with basis `b`, `k ↦ [ρ(k)|_F]_b` is a monoid homomorphism
   (`LinearMap.toMatrix_id`, `LinearMap.toMatrix_comp`); `inclusion F b` intertwines it with `ρ`
   (`LinearMap.toMatrix_mulVec_repr`); and its entries are `ℓ_i(ρ(k) b_j)` for extensions `ℓ_i` of
   the coordinate functionals (`LinearMap.exists_extend`), hence continuous.
2. **Schur's lemma** (`exists_eq_scalar`). Let `T` commute with all `ρ(k)` and `π(X)`. Take
   `v₀ ≠ 0`, a finite-dimensional `ρ`-stable `F ∋ v₀` with basis `b`, and `τ = matrixRep F hF b`.
   By admissibility `gkIntertwiners ρ d τ` has a finite basis `S_1, …, S_r`. Let `W` be the span of
   the vectors `S_i(e_j)`: it is finite-dimensional, it contains every `S(e_j)` for `S` an
   intertwiner, hence is mapped into itself by `T` (`T ∘ S` is again an intertwiner), and it is
   nonzero because `inclusion F b` is an intertwiner with `inclusion F b (e_0) = b_0 ≠ 0`. So `T`
   has an eigenvalue `c` on `W` (`Module.End.exists_eigenvalue`). The kernel of `T − c` is
   nonzero and stable under `ρ` and `π`, so by irreducibility it is `V`: `T = c`.
3. **Simplicity** (`isSimpleModule`). A submodule of `V` over `heckeAlgebra ρ π` is a complex
   subspace (scalars lie in the algebra) stable under all `ρ(k)` and `π(X)`, hence `0` or `V`.
4. **Density** (`exists_freeAlgebra_eq`). Every endomorphism of `V` over `heckeAlgebra ρ π` is
   complex linear and commutes with the generators, so it is a scalar by step 2. Hence `f` commutes
   with all of them and defines an endomorphism of `V` over `End_{heckeAlgebra} V`; Mathlib's
   `jacobson_density` (for the simple, hence semisimple, module `V`) gives `r ∈ heckeAlgebra ρ π`
   with `r v = f v` for `v ∈ s`. Finally `heckeAlgebra ρ π` is the range of `FreeAlgebra.lift`
   (`Algebra.adjoin_range_eq_range_freeAlgebra_lift`), so `r` is the image of some `a`.
