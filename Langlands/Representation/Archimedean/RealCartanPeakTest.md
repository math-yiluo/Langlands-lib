# Compact tests peaked at the identity in lower matrix coordinates

The test at radius `ε` is `max(0, ε − ‖L(k)‖)` times the product of
the nonnegative parts of the diagonal entries of the orthogonal
matrix `k`. Here `L` selects its strictly lower entries. The continuous
test is positive at the identity when `ε` is positive and has positive
Haar integral. Dividing by that integral gives mass one.

The closed subset of the orthogonal group with nonnegative diagonal
entries contains a single zero of `L`, namely the identity. An
orthogonal matrix at such a zero is upper triangular and normal,
so the fixed-flag diagonal lemma makes it diagonal. Its diagonal
entries have square one and are nonnegative. In the closed complement
of any open neighborhood of the identity, the portion with nonnegative
diagonal entries is compact. Its image under `L` is closed and omits
zero. This proves that all tests of sufficiently small radius vanish
throughout that complement.

The lower-coordinate form allows the compact column integrals to use
a Euclidean change of variables directly on the projected argument.
The diagonal factor is a continuous weight with value one at the
identity. This is an internal construction of the tests for the
regular Cartan limit.
