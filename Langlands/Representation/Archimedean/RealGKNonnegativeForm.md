# Nonnegative invariant forms on irreducible real modules

`RealGKNonnegativeFormStatement` starts with a nonzero invariant
sesquilinear form on an irreducible real `(𝔤, K)`-module. Its diagonal
values are real and nonnegative. The conclusion is infinitesimal
unitarity in the sense of the Library's positive-form predicate.

The diagonal determines a sesquilinear form, so a real diagonal makes
the form equal to its adjoint. The radical is a submodule stable under
the compact group and the complexified Lie action. It is proper
because the form is nonzero, and irreducibility makes it zero.
For a vector \(v\) with zero diagonal, if \(B(v,w)\ne0\), one can
choose \(a\) so that the two cross terms in the diagonal of \(w+av\)
are both \(-(\operatorname{Re}B(w,w)+1)\). Its diagonal would be negative.
Thus \(v\) belongs to the radical, and the form is definite.

The construction turns the sign and reality of the localized diagonal
into the positive invariant form. The final compact invariance and
real Lie skewness are the identities in Knapp–Vogan (1995),
Introduction, display (0.5), printed p. 6 (PDF p. 28).
