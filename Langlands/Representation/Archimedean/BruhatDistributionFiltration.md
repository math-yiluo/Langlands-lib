# Descending the Bruhat filtration

The long permutation has the maximal inversion count, so its cell is open by the published filtration geometry. For a lower cell, the union with all cells of strictly higher count is open. The induction hypothesis and the local support lemma give vanishing on the higher cells within this neighborhood. The local lower-cell theorem then gives an open vanishing neighborhood at each point of the cell.

Every invertible matrix belongs to a cell. Local vanishing therefore implies that the distribution is zero on the entire matrix test space. Applying the result to the difference of two distributions gives equality when their eigenvalues and covariance agree and they agree on tests supported in the long cell. This is the filtration step of Shalika's distribution argument.
