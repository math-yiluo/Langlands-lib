# Lower Verma modules for `𝔤𝔩_n(ℂ)` and the Bernstein–Gelfand inputs

Module `Langlands.Representation.Archimedean.LowerVerma`, namespace
`Langlands.Archimedean.VermaGL`.

This module defines the lower Verma modules `M⁻(λ)` of `𝔤𝔩_n(ℂ)`. It states, without proof, six
standard facts about them and about the universal enveloping algebra `U = U(𝔤𝔩_n(ℂ))`, plus
their conjunction. These are the inputs of the Bernstein–Gelfand comparison between
Harish-Chandra bimodules and category `𝒪` (Bernstein–Gelfand 1980, Theorem 5.9 and its proof
in 5.10). They were introduced for the proof of Casselman's theorem for `(𝔤𝔩_n(ℂ), U(n))`-modules, which needs Casselman's nonvanishing `V ≠ 𝔫V` for
irreducible admissible `(𝔤𝔩_n(ℂ), U(n))`-modules. The planned proof sends such a module, viewed
as a Harish-Chandra bimodule, to `V ⊗_U M⁻(λ)` and shows this is nonzero. Nothing in this
module is proved. Every declaration is a `def` or `abbrev`, and each `…Statement` is a
`Prop`-valued definition.

**Source.** J. N. Bernstein and S. I. Gelfand, *Tensor products of finite and infinite
dimensional representations of semisimple Lie algebras*, Compositio Math. 41 (1980), 245–285
(literature id `bernstein-gelfand-1980-tensor-products`). Its PDF page `k` is printed page
`k + 243`. BG treat a semisimple Lie algebra `𝔤` with a Cartan subalgebra `𝔥`, the upper Borel
subalgebra `𝔟 = 𝔥 + 𝔫` and Verma modules `M_χ = U / U(I_{χ-ρ} + 𝔫)` (1.9). This module adapts
those statements in three ways:

1. The Lie algebra is the reductive `𝔤𝔩_n(ℂ)`, with diagonal Cartan subalgebra. A weight is a
   vector `λ ∈ ℂⁿ`, the value on `E_pp` being `λ_p`.
2. The Borel subalgebra is the *lower* triangular one. The Verma generator is killed by
   `𝔫⁻ = span{E_pq : q < p}`, and weights are not `ρ`-shifted. So `M⁻(λ)` is BG's Verma module
   for the opposite Borel subalgebra with highest weight `λ`, and its weights lie in `λ + Q₊`.
3. Finite-dimensional representations are restricted by the algebraic condition
   `HasIntegralWeights`. For `𝔤𝔩_n(ℂ)` this makes them semisimple, with irreducible summands
   that are representations of `GL_n(ℂ)` (see below). BG's semisimple setting needs no such
   condition.

For `𝔤𝔩_n = 𝔰𝔩_n ⊕ ℂ·1` the BG statements for `𝔰𝔩_n` transfer directly. We have
`U(𝔤𝔩_n) = U(𝔰𝔩_n) ⊗ ℂ[1]` and `Z(𝔤𝔩_n) = Z(𝔰𝔩_n) ⊗ ℂ[1]`, and the identity matrix acts on
`M⁻(λ)` by the scalar `Σ_p λ_p`. The transfer is standard but not spelled out in BG, so the
provenance of every statement here is "derived from BG".

## Definitions

* `glU n` is `UniversalEnvelopingAlgebra ℂ (Matrix (Fin n) (Fin n) ℂ)`, for the commutator Lie
  algebra structure on matrices. `unitU n p q` is the image `ι(E_pq)` of the matrix unit
  `Matrix.single p q 1`. `center n` is `Subalgebra.center ℂ (glU n)`.
* `lowerVermaIdeal n λ` is the left ideal (a `Submodule (glU n) (glU n)`) spanned by the
  `ι(E_pq)` with `q < p` and the `ι(E_pp) - λ_p`. `LowerVerma n λ` is the quotient left module
  `M⁻(λ) = U / (U𝔫⁻ + Σ_p U(E_pp - λ_p))`. `gen n λ` is the class of `1`. `action n λ` is the Lie
  algebra action `X ↦ (m ↦ ι(X) • m)`, i.e. the composite of `ι` with left multiplication. So
  `envelopingAction (action n λ)` is the `U`-module structure of `M⁻(λ)`.
* `posRootCone n` is the additive submonoid `Q₊ ⊆ ℂⁿ` generated by the positive roots
  `ε_p - ε_q = Pi.single p 1 - Pi.single q 1` with `p < q`. The matrix unit `E_pq` (`p < q`)
  raises weights by `ε_p - ε_q`, so the weights of `M⁻(λ)` lie in `λ + Q₊`.
* `IsZExtremal n λ` holds when every `μ ≠ λ` with `λ - μ ∈ Q₊` has a central element `z` that
  acts on `gen λ` by some scalar `c` but does not act on `gen μ` by `c`. Since `Z` acts on
  `M⁻(μ)` by its central character `χ_μ` (BG 1.9(c)) and `gen μ ≠ 0`, this says
  `χ_μ ≠ χ_λ` for all `μ < λ`. That is, `λ` is minimal in its linkage class
  `{μ : χ_μ = χ_λ}` for the order given by `Q₊`. For the lower Borel subalgebra this is the
  analogue of BG's dominance condition in 5.9 and 1.11 (`χ` maximal in its `W`-orbit). It is
  exactly what the projectivity and Hom-bound arguments use. It is stated with explicit
  central elements, so the definition needs no Harish-Chandra homomorphism.
* `HasIntegralWeights σ`, for a Lie algebra representation `σ` of `𝔤𝔩_n(ℂ)` on `W`, says that
  `W` is spanned by simultaneous eigenvectors `w` of the `σ(E_pp)` with integer eigenvalues:
  `σ(E_pp) w = m_p w` with `m ∈ ℤⁿ`. Then the diagonal subalgebra acts semisimply. When `W` is
  finite-dimensional this forces it to be a semisimple `𝔤𝔩_n(ℂ)`-module (Weyl's theorem for
  `𝔰𝔩_n`, together with semisimplicity of the central element `Σ E_pp`), whose irreducible
  summands have integral highest weights. In the intended application, `W` is built from a
  `U(n)`-stable finite-dimensional subspace of a `(𝔤, K)`-module, where integrality comes from
  the torus of `U(n)`.
* `zeroWeightSpace σ` is `W₀ = ⋂_p ker σ(E_pp)`, BG's "zero weight" subspace (1.7).
* `tensorEnd a b` is `a ⊗ 1 + 1 ⊗ b` on `W ⊗ N` (`rTensor N a + lTensor W b`). For
  representations `σ`, `τ`, the map `Y ↦ tensorEnd (σ Y) (τ Y)` is the tensor product
  representation of BG 2.1.

## Statements

All statements quantify over every `n : ℕ` and every `λ : Fin n → ℂ`. Where present, the
finite-dimensional representation `W` lives in an arbitrary universe `uW` and the target `N`
in `uN`.

### `LowerVermaWeightStatement`

`gen n λ ≠ 0`, and every `m ∈ M⁻(λ)` with `ι(E_pp) • m = λ_p m` for all `p` is a scalar
multiple of `gen n λ`. This is the lower-Borel form of BG 1.9(c)
(`End_𝔤(M_χ) = k`). It follows from Poincaré–Birkhoff–Witt: `M⁻(λ)` is free of rank one over
`U(𝔫⁺)` on `gen λ`, and the `λ`-weight space is `U(𝔫⁺)₀ · gen λ = ℂ · gen λ`. Its second half
implies that every central element acts on `M⁻(λ)` by a scalar, because `z • gen λ` has weight
`λ`.

### `LowerVermaDufloStatement`

`IsMaximallyFaithful (action n λ)`, using the Library predicate of
`RealGenericLargeReduction`: every `u ∈ U` acting by zero on `M⁻(λ)` lies in the ideal
generated by the central elements that act by zero. This is Duflo's theorem, BG 1.13(b): for
`θ = ζ(χ)`, every nonzero `u ∈ U_θ = U / U ker θ` acts nontrivially on `M_χ`. So
`Ann M_χ = U · ker θ`. Here it is stated for lower Verma modules of `𝔤𝔩_n(ℂ)`.

### `KostantMultiplicityStatement`

Let `W` be finite-dimensional with integral weights (`σ`), and let `χ : Z → ℂ` be any
character of the center. Then there are `dim W₀` linear maps `f_i : W → U` such that:

* each `f_i` is equivariant for the adjoint action:
  `f_i(σ(Y) w) = ι(Y) f_i(w) - f_i(w) ι(Y)`;
* their images in `U / U ker χ` are linearly independent: if `Σ_i c_i f_i(w)` lies in
  `Ideal.span {z - χ(z) : z ∈ Z}` for every `w`, then `c = 0`.

Because the generators are central, this left ideal is the two-sided ideal `U ker χ`. This is
the lower bound in Kostant's theorem, BG 1.7, which cites Kostant, *Lie group representations
on polynomial rings*, Amer. J. Math. 85 (1963) (BG reference [18]): `Hom_𝔤(L, U_ad)` is a free
`Z`-module of rank `dim L₀`. Kostant also shows that `U` is free over `Z` with an ad-stable
complement (the harmonic elements). Reducing modulo `ker χ` therefore gives
`dim Hom_𝔤(W, U / U ker χ) = dim W₀`. Equivariant maps into the quotient lift to `U` because
`U_ad` is a locally finite semisimple `𝔤𝔩_n`-module. The integrality hypothesis provides the
semisimplicity of `W` that BG's semisimple setting supplies automatically.

### `CentralCharacterStatement`

Every algebra homomorphism `χ : Z → ℂ` is `χ_λ` for some `Z`-extremal `λ`: that is,
`z • gen λ = χ(z) • gen λ` for all `z ∈ Z`. Harish-Chandra's theorem (BG 1.6: `ζ` is onto and its
fibres are `W`-orbits) gives some `λ₀` with `χ_{λ₀} = χ` and shows that the linkage class
`{μ : χ_μ = χ}` is finite. A minimal element `λ` of that class within `λ₀ - Q₊` is then
`Z`-extremal. A proof may replace the finiteness of linkage classes by the Casimir element
`C = Σ_{p,q} E_pq E_qp`. Its eigenvalue on `gen μ` is `Σ_p (μ_p + δ_p)² - Σ_p δ_p²` with
`δ_p = p - (n-1)/2` (`p` counted from `0`). Write `μ = λ₀ - β` with `β ∈ Q₊`, an integer vector
with coordinate sum `0`. Equal Casimir eigenvalues give
`Σ_p β_p² = 2 Σ_p Re(λ₀ + δ)_p β_p`, which bounds `β`. So only finitely many `μ ∈ λ₀ - Q₊` have
`χ_μ = χ`.

### `LowerVermaHomBoundStatement`

Let `λ` be `Z`-extremal and `W` finite-dimensional with integral weights. Then every linearly
independent family `g : Fin d → (W →ₗ End M⁻(λ))` of equivariant maps
(`g_i(σ(Y) w) = action(Y) ∘ g_i(w) - g_i(w) ∘ action(Y)`) has `d ≤ dim W₀`.

Proof sketch from BG, a standard argument:

* `Hom_𝔤(W, End M) ≅ Hom_𝔤(M, W* ⊗ M)`.
* A map out of `M⁻(λ)` is determined by the image of `gen λ`, a vector of weight `λ` killed by
  `𝔫⁻`.
* `W* ⊗ M⁻(λ)` has a filtration with quotients `M⁻(λ + ν)`, where `ν` runs over the weights of
  `W*` with multiplicity (BG 1.9(f)).
* `Hom(M⁻(λ), M⁻(λ + ν)) ≠ 0` forces `λ + ν ∈ λ - Q₊` with `χ_{λ+ν} = χ_λ` (BG 1.11(c), 1.9(c)).
  By `Z`-extremality this means `ν = 0`, and that summand contributes at most one dimension
  (`LowerVermaWeightStatement`).

Linear independence of a family is used instead of `finrank` of the Hom space, because that
space need not be finite-dimensional a priori, and `Module.finrank` is `0` on
infinite-dimensional spaces.

### `LowerVermaProjectiveStatement`

Let `λ` be `Z`-extremal, `W` finite-dimensional with integral weights, `τ` a representation of
`𝔤𝔩_n(ℂ)` on `N`, and `f : W ⊗ M⁻(λ) → N` a surjective linear map intertwining
`tensorEnd (σ Y) (action λ Y)` with `τ Y`. Suppose `x ∈ N` is killed by every `τ(E_pq)` with
`q < p` and satisfies `τ(E_pp) x = λ_p x`. Then `x = f(t)` for some `t ∈ W ⊗ M⁻(λ)` that is
itself killed by the strictly lower matrix units and has weight `λ`.

This is the projectivity of `M⁻(λ)` for `Z`-extremal `λ`, the lower-Borel form of the fact that
`M_χ = P_χ` is projective in `𝒪` for dominant `χ`. By BG 1.11(c)–(d), `P_χ` has a Verma flag in which `M_ψ` occurs only for `ψ ≥ χ` linked to `χ`; for dominant `χ` only `ψ = χ` remains. BG use this projectivity in 5.9–5.10. It is phrased for
surjections out of `W ⊗ M⁻(λ)`, which is all the application needs. The standard proof:

1. `Z` acts locally finitely on `W ⊗ M⁻(λ)`, with the finitely many characters `χ_{λ+ν}`
   (BG 1.9(f)).
2. Project a weight-`λ` preimage of `x` onto the generalized `χ_λ`-eigenspace. This projection
   is a polynomial in finitely many central elements, so it commutes with `f`.
3. Suppose `𝔫⁻` does not kill the result. Then a vector of minimal weight in `U(𝔫⁻)𝔫⁻ t` is
   killed by `𝔫⁻`. It has weight `μ < λ` and central character `χ_μ = χ_λ`, contradicting
   `Z`-extremality.

### `BernsteinGelfandInputsStatement`

`BernsteinGelfandInputsStatement.{uW, uN}` is the conjunction of the six statements above, with
the universe parameters passed to the statements that quantify over `W` and `N`.
