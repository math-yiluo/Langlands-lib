# Compact integration in opposed block coordinates

The Borel-modulus weight is the signed orthogonal extension of the
constant compact function. Its exponents are the full modulus
exponents, obtained by adding the Iwasawa shift to a second copy
of that shift. The weight is unchanged by right orthogonal
multiplication and by left diagonal signs.

The proof uses triangular–orthogonal factorization to transport
the signed orthogonal part under right multiplication and left
multiplication by diagonal signs.
For a compact function invariant under left diagonal signs, the
principal-series extension covariance gives the full Borel modulus.

The diagonal sign average of a polynomial test is again a polynomial
test, since a sign multiplies each entry in a row by a fixed scalar.
The average is invariant under left diagonal signs, so its extension
qualifies for the smooth opposed parabolic integration formula.
Averaging preserves the integral against compact Haar measure. In the
opposed coordinates it also preserves the integral, by conjugation
on the lower unipotent coordinates and left translation on the
compact block group. The conjugation preserves Lebesgue measure
and the radial weight.

The chart weight is integrable by the smooth parabolic formula for
the radial extension of the constant function. It is continuous and
equals one at the zero coordinate. The sup norm of a test bounds
its weighted integral by an integrable multiple of this weight in
absolute value. This gives continuity of the chart integral and
allows Stone–Weierstrass density of the matrix polynomials to extend
the identity to every continuous compact test.

This is an internal consequence of the published parabolic integration
formula, the signed principal-series extension, and the measure-preserving
compact Levi action on opposite unipotent coordinates.
