# Irreducibility of real standard modules whose blocks have equal real exponent

Lean module: `Langlands.Representation.Archimedean.RealTemperedIrreducibleStatement`.

Worker 5220 wrote this module for Task 6749 (`5dd3db87-6f14-4d0f-b9a7-3d0065415fd8`), the proof
of `RealStandardLanglandsQuotientStatement` (L, module `RealLargeLanglandsQuotientReduction`).
It states one proposition and proves nothing. It is the real analogue of the Library's
`ComplexTemperedStandardIrreducibleStatement` (module `ComplexTemperedIrreducibleStatement`).
The proposition is the input L needs at ties of the Langlands order (equal real exponents); see
"Why L needs it" below.

Notation: `GL_n(ℝ)` is `Langlands.GeneralLinear n ℝ`, `K = O(n)` is `unitaryCompact ℝ n`
(as a group, `Matrix.unitaryGroup (Fin n) ℝ`), and `𝔤 = ℂ ⊗_ℝ 𝔤𝔩_n(ℝ)`. A `RealBlock` is either

* `.character d` with `d : RealCharacterData`: the character `x ↦ sgn(x)^ε |x|^t` of `ℝˣ`, with
  `ε = d.parity` and `t = d.exponent`; it has rank `1`;
* `.induced d` with `d : ComplexCharacterData`: the block attached to the character
  `z ↦ (z/|z|)^m |z|^(2t)` of `ℂˣ`, with `m = d.weight` and `t = d.exponent`; it has rank `2`.

`realRank ds` is the sum of the ranks of the blocks of `ds`. `realBlockExponent x` (module
`RealGenericParameterReduction`) is `Re t` for both kinds of block. `realStandardModule n ds`
(module `StandardModule`) consists of the functions `f` in the Library's smooth normalized
principal series `principalSeries ℝ n (realPositionCharacter ds)`, which satisfy
`f(b g) = χ(b) δ_B^{1/2}(b) f(g)` for `b` in the upper triangular Borel subgroup. In addition,
for every induction block of weight `m` at the positions `p, p + 1`, the Fourier coefficients of
`θ ↦ f(r_p(θ) g)` of weight `|k| < |m|` vanish for every `g`. Here `r_p(θ)` is the rotation in
the coordinates `p, p + 1`. The position characters are `χ` at the position of `.character χ`,
and `sgn^(|m|+1) |·|^(t+|m|/2)`, `|·|^(t-|m|/2)` at the two positions of an induction block. Positions at or past
`realRank ds` get the trivial character. `realStandardRep n ds` (right translation by `O(n)`) and
`realStandardLie n ds` (right derivatives, extended `ℂ`-linearly to `𝔤`) act on its `O(n)`-finite
functions `kFiniteFunctions (realStandardModule n ds) (unitaryCompact ℝ n)` (module
`RealStandardModuleGKModule`). `IsIrreducibleGKModule ρ π` (module `GKModule`) says that the
space is nonzero and its only subspaces stable under all `ρ(k)` and all `π(Y)` are `0` and
everything.

## The statement

`RealTemperedStandardIrreducibleStatement`: for every `n : ℕ` and every list `ds : List RealBlock`
with `realRank ds = n` and `realBlockExponent x = realBlockExponent y` for all `x, y ∈ ds`,

`IsIrreducibleGKModule (realStandardRep n ds) (realStandardLie n ds)`.

For `n = 0` the only list is `ds = []`, the module is one-dimensional (functions on the trivial
group), and the statement holds trivially.

The rank hypothesis cannot be dropped, because positions past the rank carry the trivial
character. For `n = 2` and `ds = [.character d]` with `d` the character `|·|` (parity `0`,
exponent `1`), the single block trivially satisfies the exponent hypothesis. Then
`realStandardModule 2 ds` is the full principal series of the characters `(|·|, 1)`. It is
reducible, by Badulescu–Renard (9.1) below with `p = 1`.

## Source

A. I. Badulescu and D. Renard, *Unitary dual of GL(n) at archimedean places and global
Jacquet–Langlands correspondence*, Compositio Math. 146 (2010) (`badulescu-renard-2010-unitary-dual`;
PDF page = printed page − 1113):

* §2.3–§2.4 (printed p. 1123, PDF 10): `M(G)` is the category of Harish-Chandra modules for the
  maximal compact subgroup `O(n)` of `GL(n, ℝ)`. `π₁ × π₂ = i_(n₁,n₂)(π₁ ⊗ π₂)` is normalized
  parabolic induction from the parabolic subgroup generated by the block diagonal Levi subgroup
  and the upper triangular Borel subgroup. Proposition 2.4 (printed p. 1124): the
  Grothendieck ring `(R, ×)` is commutative.
* Theorem 3.1 (printed p. 1124, PDF 11): for `d = (δ₁, …, δ_l)` with every `δᵢ` irreducible and
  square integrable modulo center (`Dᵘ`), `δ₁ × δ₂ × ⋯ × δ_l` is irreducible (and tempered). The
  authors attribute the archimedean case to the theory of R-groups (Knapp–Zuckerman 1982), which
  are trivial for the groups `GL_n`.
* §9.2 (printed pp. 1132–1133, PDF 19–20), `A = ℝ`: `D = D₁ ⊔ D₂`. `D₁` consists of the
  characters `δ(α, ε) = |·|^α sgn^ε` of `ℝˣ`. `δ₁ × δ₂` is reducible exactly when
  `α₁ − α₂ = p ∈ ℤ ∖ {0}` and `ε₁ − ε₂ ≡ p + 1 (mod 2)` (9.1). Then (9.2) its constituents are a
  finite-dimensional representation and `η(δ₁, δ₂) ∈ D₂`. Definition 9.2 writes
  `η(α₁, α₂) = η(|·|^α₁, |·|^α₂ sgn^(α₁−α₂+1))`, and `η(α₁, α₂)` depends only on `{α₁, α₂}`.
  Remark 9.3 and (9.4): `η(x, x) := δ(x, 0) × δ(x, 1)`, the limit of discrete series, is an
  irreducible principal series.

So for `GL_n(ℝ)` Theorem 3.1 says the following. Let each `δᵢ` be a unitary character of `ℝˣ`
(`Re α = 0`) or a discrete series `η(x, y)` of `GL₂(ℝ)` with unitary central character
(`Re(x + y) = 0`). Then `δ₁ × ⋯ × δ_l` is irreducible.

From the source to the statement (standard derivation, not a quotation):

* **Model.** `StandardModule.md` ("Derived choices", items 1–5 and 7) identifies
  `realStandardModule n ds` with the standard module `σ₁ × ⋯ × σ_r` induced in stages, the
  blocks taken in the order of `ds`. Here `σ_j` is the character `χ` for `.character χ`, and
  `D_|m| ⊗ |det|^t` for an induction block of weight `m ≠ 0` and exponent `t`. The latter is the
  discrete-series constituent of the principal series of `(sgn^(|m|+1) |·|^(t+|m|/2), |·|^(t−|m|/2))`.
  In the notation of §9.2 it is `η(t + |m|/2, t − |m|/2)`. Indeed this pair is the twist by
  `sgn^(|m|+1)` of the pair of Definition 9.2, and that twist fixes the discrete series
  (`StandardModule.md`, item 2). An induction block of weight `0` gives the principal series of
  `(sgn |·|^t, |·|^t)`, that is `δ(t, 1) × δ(t, 0)`, the same standard module as the two
  characters `sgn|·|^t`, `|·|^t` in this order (item 4). Its `O(n)`-finite part with
  `realStandardRep` and `realStandardLie` is the Harish-Chandra module (item 5), as in the source.
* **Twist.** Let every block of `ds` have real exponent `e`. Multiplication by the smooth function
  `g ↦ |det g|^(−e)` multiplies the position characters by `|·|^(−e)`. It commutes with left
  rotations (`|det r_p(θ)| = 1`), so it preserves the Fourier conditions. Hence it is a linear
  bijection from `realStandardModule n ds` onto `realStandardModule n ds⁰`, where `ds⁰` is `ds`
  with every exponent `t` replaced by `t − e`. It commutes with right translation by `O(n)` and
  preserves `O(n)`-finiteness. It intertwines the right derivatives up to adding the scalar
  `−e tr(X)` to the action of `X`, which does not change the set of `(𝔤, K)`-stable subspaces.
  So irreducibility for `ds` is irreducibility for `ds⁰`, whose blocks all have real exponent `0`.
* **Unitary blocks.** For `ds⁰` every character block is a unitary character, in `D₁ᵘ`. Every
  induction block of weight `m ≠ 0` is `η(t + |m|/2, t − |m|/2)` with `Re t = 0`, whose central
  character `sgn^(|m|+1) |·|^(2t)` is unitary, so it lies in `D₂ᵘ`. Every weight-zero block
  contributes the two unitary characters `δ(t, 1), δ(t, 0)`. So `realStandardModule n ds⁰` is a
  product `δ₁ × ⋯ × δ_l` of elements of `Dᵘ` in a fixed order, which is irreducible by
  Theorem 3.1. If one reads Theorem 3.1 for a single ordering of the multiset only, the other
  orderings follow: all orderings have the same image in `R` (Proposition 2.4), so each has
  exactly one composition factor.

## Why L needs it

The published `RealStrictCharacterLanglandsQuotientStatement` (module
`RealStrictCharacterLanglandsQuotient`) proves L for lists of character blocks with strictly
decreasing real exponents. The planned route for general Langlands-ordered lists in Task 6749
uses the block parabolic subgroup whose Levi blocks collect the blocks of equal real exponent.
The Langlands lemma on this parabolic subgroup reduces L to the irreducibility of the Levi
module. Its factors are standard modules of lists whose blocks all have the same real exponent,
which is the present statement.

Conversely, L at such a list implies the statement at that list, by a standard argument. Up to
the twist, the module is the Harish-Chandra module of a unitary representation, induced unitarily
from square-integrable ones. So every `(𝔤, K)`-submodule `W` has the `(𝔤, K)`-stable complement
`W^⊥` (the `K`-isotypic components are finite-dimensional). A largest proper submodule would
contain both `W` and `W^⊥` for any proper nonzero `W`. So any proof of L contains a proof of this
statement, and it cannot be avoided. This paragraph is an explanation and is not used by the
Lean statement.
