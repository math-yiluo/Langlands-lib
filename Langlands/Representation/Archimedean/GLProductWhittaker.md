# Kostant's Whittaker theorem for finite products of `𝔤𝔩_n(ℂ)`

Lean module: `Langlands.Representation.Archimedean.GLProductWhittaker`.

This module states Kostant's theorem (Kostant 1978, Theorem 3.9 = Theorem D) for the complex
reductive Lie algebra `𝔤 = ∏_{i ∈ ι} 𝔤𝔩_{n_i}(ℂ)` with `ι` finite. It contains one
proposition, `Langlands.Archimedean.GLProductWhittakerMaximallyFaithfulStatement`, and no
proof of it. It serves two tasks at once: Task 6748 needs the case `ι = Unit`, through
`ℂ ⊗_ℝ 𝔤𝔩_n(ℝ) ≅ 𝔤𝔩_n(ℂ)`, and Task 6757 needs the case `ι = Fin 2`, through
`ℂ ⊗_ℝ 𝔤𝔩_n(ℂ) ≅ 𝔤𝔩_n(ℂ) × 𝔤𝔩_n(ℂ)` (see "Intended applications" below).

## The Lie algebra

The Lie algebra is the Lean type `∀ i, Matrix (Fin (n i)) (Fin (n i)) ℂ`, for `ι : Type` with
`[Fintype ι]` and `n : ι → ℕ`. An element `X` is a tuple of square matrices `X i`, and `X i p q`
is the `(p, q)` entry of the `i`-th matrix. The bracket is the commutator of the pointwise
product, `⁅X, Y⁆ i = X i * Y i - Y i * X i`. It comes from the local instance
`LieRing.ofAssociativeRing` (used throughout the Library's archimedean modules) applied to the
product ring. Mathlib has no separate Lie ring instance on Pi types, so no second bracket
competes with this one. The scalar action of `ℂ` is the pointwise one.

## Predicates used

The two predicates come from `Langlands.Representation.Archimedean.RealGenericLargeReduction`
and work for any complex Lie algebra `L` and Lie algebra map `π : L → End(V)`:

* `HasInfinitesimalCharacter π`: every element `z` of the centre `Z(L)` of the universal
  enveloping algebra `U(L)` acts on `V` by a scalar. This is Kostant's "V admits an
  infinitesimal character" (§3.9, p. 142; §0.2, p. 103).
* `IsMaximallyFaithful π`: every `u ∈ U(L)` acting by `0` on `V` lies in the left ideal
  `Ideal.span Z_V` generated by `Z_V`, the central elements acting by `0`. This is Kostant's
  "maximally faithful", `U Z_V = U_V` (§3.9, p. 142; p. 105). The inclusion `U Z_V ⊆ U_V` always
  holds.

## The statement

`GLProductWhittakerMaximallyFaithfulStatement.{uV}` says: for every finite `ι : Type`, every
`n : ι → ℕ`, every complex vector space `V : Type uV` and every Lie algebra map
`π : (∀ i, Matrix (Fin (n i)) (Fin (n i)) ℂ) →ₗ⁅ℂ⁆ Module.End ℂ V`, assume

1. `HasInfinitesimalCharacter π`;
2. `ℓ : V →ₗ[ℂ] ℂ` is a nonzero linear functional;
3. `a : ∀ i, Fin (n i) → ℂ` satisfies `a i p ≠ 0` whenever `p + 1 < n i`;
4. for every blockwise strictly upper triangular `X` (that is, `X i p q = 0` whenever `q ≤ p`)
   and every `v ∈ V`,
   `ℓ(π(X) v) = (∑ᵢ ∑ₚ ∑_q [q = p + 1] a i p · X i p q) · ℓ(v)`,
   that is, `ℓ(π(X) v) = ψ(X) ℓ(v)` with `ψ(X) = ∑ᵢ ∑_{p + 1 < n i} a i p · X i p (p+1)`.

Then `IsMaximallyFaithful π`.

The values `a i p` with `p + 1 = n i` never enter `ψ`, so they are unconstrained.

## Relation to Kostant's theorem

Kostant's Theorem 3.9 (pp. 142–143; Theorem D, p. 104) reads: let `U` be the enveloping algebra
of a complex semisimple Lie algebra, `Z` its centre and `ξ` a central character. If a `U`-module
`V` admits `ξ` as infinitesimal character and either `V` or its full dual `V'` has a nonzero
Whittaker vector, then `U_V = U Z_ξ` (3.9.7), where `U_V` is the annihilator of `V` and `Z_ξ`
is the kernel of `ξ`. A Whittaker vector is a vector `w` with `x w = η(x) w` for all `x ∈ 𝔫`
(3.1.2), where `𝔫` is the nilradical of a Borel subalgebra and `η : 𝔫 → ℂ` is a non-singular
character, that is, a Lie algebra homomorphism with `η(e_α) ≠ 0` for every simple root vector
`e_α` (2.3.3), p. 121.

The statement above matches this as follows.

* Borel subalgebra and simple roots. In `𝔤 = ∏ᵢ 𝔤𝔩_{n_i}(ℂ)` take the blockwise upper triangular
  Borel subalgebra. Its nilradical `𝔫` is the blockwise strictly upper triangular tuples, the
  hypothesis on `X` in item 4. The simple root vectors are the elementary matrices
  `E_{p,p+1}` in block `i`, for `p + 1 < n i`.
* The character. `ψ` vanishes on `⁅𝔫, 𝔫⁆`, whose elements have zero superdiagonal, so `ψ` is a
  Lie algebra character of `𝔫`. Its value on `E_{p,p+1}` in block `i` is `a i p`, which item 3
  makes nonzero. So `ψ` and `η = -ψ` are non-singular.
* The dual action. The full dual `V'` is a `U`-module through (3.9.4), with the anti-automorphism
  `u ↦ ǔ` of (3.8.4) that sends `x ∈ 𝔤` to `-x`. So `(x · ℓ)(v) = ℓ(-x v)`, and item 4 says that
  `x · ℓ = -ψ(x) ℓ` for `x ∈ 𝔫`. Thus `ℓ` is a nonzero Whittaker vector in `V'` for the
  non-singular character `η = -ψ`.
* Infinitesimal character and maximal faithfulness. Item 1 gives a central character `ξ` with
  `Z_V = Z_ξ` (the module `V` is nonzero because `ℓ ≠ 0`). Kostant's conclusion `U_V = U Z_ξ`
  is then `IsMaximallyFaithful π`.

From semisimple to reductive. Kostant states the theorem for semisimple `𝔤`. The statement here
is the routine extension to the reductive `𝔤 = 𝔰 ⊕ 𝔠`, with `𝔰 = ∏ᵢ 𝔰𝔩_{n_i}(ℂ)` semisimple and
`𝔠` spanned by the block identities `Iᵢ`. Here `U(𝔤) = U(𝔰) ⊗ ℂ[𝔠]` and `Z(𝔤) = Z(𝔰) ⊗ ℂ[𝔠]`.
Since `𝔫 ⊆ 𝔰` and `Z(𝔰) ⊆ Z(𝔤)`, Kostant's theorem applies to `V` as an `𝔰`-module. Each `Iᵢ`
acts by a scalar `cᵢ`, and every element of `U(𝔤)` is congruent to an element of `U(𝔰)` modulo
the left ideal generated by the central elements `Iᵢ - cᵢ`, which act by `0`. So
`U_V(𝔤) = U(𝔤) Z_V(𝔤)` follows from `U_V(𝔰) = U(𝔰) Z_V(𝔰)`. The blocks with `n i ≤ 1` contribute
no simple roots, and a block with `n i = 0` is the zero Lie algebra.

## Intended applications

* Task 6748 (`RealWhittakerMaximallyFaithfulStatement`): `ι = Unit`, through the isomorphism
  `ℂ ⊗_ℝ 𝔤𝔩_n(ℝ) ≅ 𝔤𝔩_n(ℂ)`, `z ⊗ X ↦ zX`. The functional of `IsRealGenericGKModule` then has
  the character with `a () p = i aₚ`.
* Task 6757 (`ComplexGenericLargeStatement`): `ι = Fin 2`, through the isomorphism
  `ℂ ⊗_ℝ 𝔤𝔩_n(ℂ) ≅ 𝔤𝔩_n(ℂ) × 𝔤𝔩_n(ℂ)`, `z ⊗ X ↦ (zX, z X̄)`. The functional of
  `IsComplexGenericGKModule` (character `i Re(∑ aₚ X_{p,p+1})` on real directions) then has the
  character with `a 0 p = (i/2) aₚ` and `a 1 p = (i/2) āₚ`.

Both applications also need the transport of `HasInfinitesimalCharacter` and
`IsMaximallyFaithful` along a Lie algebra isomorphism; that is done in the proofs that use this
statement, not here.

## Provenance

Derived. The statement is Kostant 1978, Theorem 3.9, for the case of the full dual `V'`,
specialized to the semisimple part of `∏ᵢ 𝔤𝔩_{n_i}(ℂ)` with its standard Borel subalgebra and
extended to the reductive algebra as explained above. Stating it for finite products, so that it
covers both the real and the complex case, is internal to this project.
