# Geometry of the longest Bruhat cell in matrix space

This module supplies internal matrix-space coordinates for the open-cell step of the proof of archimedean Whittaker multiplicity one (Shalika 1974, Theorem 3.1; `UnitaryWhittakerMultiplicityOneStatement`). The setting is square matrices of size `n + 1` over an `RCLike` field, including the real and complex cases. Shalika (1974), Proposition 2.11 and its proof, printed pp. 182–183, use the longest cell and the identity that Weyl-conjugated transpose fixes its long-Weyl times diagonal representatives. The formal constructions here use the existing upper Bruhat coordinates and topology of the Library; they do not yet give a distribution-invariance theorem.

`longestBruhatCell F n` is the image in matrix space of the upper Bruhat cell for `Fin.revPerm`. This cell has no strictly higher inversion-count cell: reversal inverts every ordered pair of distinct indices. Its openness follows from the Library's open Bruhat neighborhood and the open embedding of units. All its points are invertible. The internal `matrixUnipotentPairMulAction` is the product group action `(u, v) • x = u x v⁻¹`, with the factors viewed in the matrix algebra. This action is jointly continuous. The chart `longestBruhatCellChart` is a homeomorphism from `((U × U) × T)`, with `U` the actual upper unipotent subgroup and `T` the tuple of nonzero diagonal entries. The restricted-right unipotent factor in the general Bruhat chart is all of `U` for reversal. Reordering factors and using inverse on the right gives the formula `((u, v), d) ↦ u (w₀ d) v⁻¹`.

The action preserves the cell and changes the group coordinate by left multiplication without changing the diagonal coordinate. For any two compact subsets of the cell, `exists_compact_longestBruhatCell_transport` places every group element transporting a point of the first into the second in a compact subset of `U × U`: take the image of the product of their projected chart coordinates under `(a, b) ↦ b a⁻¹`.

`matrixWeylTranspose` is the real continuous linear equivalence `x ↦ (x_{rev(j), rev(i)})_{i,j}` on the ambient matrix space. It is involutive, reverses products, and agrees on invertible matrices with the Library's Weyl-conjugated transpose. `matrixUnipotentPairTranspose` is the homeomorphism `(u, v) ↦ (τ(v⁻¹), τ(u⁻¹))`; it is involutive and multiplicative. Ambient transpose interchanges the action with its transport by this homeomorphism. Finally each point of the longest cell is a translate of a representative `w₀ d` fixed by ambient transpose. In particular transpose preserves the cell.
