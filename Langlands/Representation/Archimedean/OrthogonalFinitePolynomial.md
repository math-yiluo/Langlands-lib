# `O(n)`-finite continuous functions on `O(n)` are polynomial

Lean module: `Langlands.Representation.Archimedean.OrthogonalFinitePolynomial`.

This module proves that a continuous complex-valued function on the orthogonal group `O(n)`
whose right translates span a finite-dimensional space is the restriction to `O(n)` of a
polynomial in the `n²` matrix entries. It states this as
`Langlands.Archimedean.OrthogonalFinitePolynomialStatement` and proves it as
`Langlands.Archimedean.orthogonalFinitePolynomial`. It also proves a form for functions on
`GL_n(ℝ)`: if a continuous function on `GL_n(ℝ)` is `O(n)`-finite under right translation, then
on every coset `g · O(n)` it is a polynomial in the entries of the `O(n)` variable
(`exists_mvPolynomial_of_mem_kFiniteVectors`).

Notation: `O(n)` is `Matrix.unitaryGroup (Fin n) ℝ`, the real `n × n` matrices `k` with
`k · kᵀ = 1`, with the subspace topology from the matrices. `GL_n(ℝ)` is
`Langlands.GeneralLinear n ℝ`. `Unitary.toUnits` maps `O(n)` into `GL_n(ℝ)`, and
`unitaryCompact ℝ n` is its image as a subgroup of `GL_n(ℝ)`. Right translation by `s` sends a
function `F` to `x ↦ F(x s)` (`Whittaker.rightTranslation`).

## The statement

`OrthogonalFinitePolynomialStatement`: for every `n : ℕ` and every continuous
`f : O(n) → ℂ` such that `Submodule.span ℂ (Set.range fun k => fun x => f (x * k))` is
finite-dimensional, there is a polynomial `P : MvPolynomial (Fin n × Fin n) ℂ` with
`f k = P((k_{ij})_{i,j})` for every `k ∈ O(n)`. Here `P((k_{ij}))` is
`MvPolynomial.eval (fun ij => (k ij.1 ij.2 : ℂ)) P`: the variable `(i, j)` is replaced by
the real entry `k_{ij}`, viewed as a complex number.

Provenance. The statement is derived content: it is a standard fact about compact linear
groups that the continuous functions whose translates span a finite-dimensional space (the
representative functions, or matrix coefficients of finite-dimensional representations) are
polynomials in the matrix entries and their complex conjugates. For `O(n)` the entries are real,
so no conjugates are needed. No source is cited for this exact form. The proof below is
self-contained and uses neither the Peter–Weyl theorem nor representation theory of `O(n)`;
its analytic input is the Stone–Weierstrass theorem. The statement is intended as infrastructure for the
analyticity of `O(n)`-finite matrix coefficients in the proof of the Langlands-quotient
statement `RealStandardLanglandsQuotientStatement` (Task 6749): an `O(n)`-finite vector of a
real principal series is, on `O(n)`, a polynomial in the entries, and hence extends
holomorphically along complexified one-parameter families.

The statement is not restricted to `n ≥ 1`; for `n = 0` it holds trivially (`O(0)` is one
point and constants are polynomials).

## Definitions

All definitions are in the namespace `Langlands.Archimedean.OrthogonalFinitePolynomial`.

* `entry n (i, j)`: the matrix entry `k ↦ k_{ij}` as a continuous function
  `C(O(n), ℂ)`.
* `polynomialStar n`: the star subalgebra of `C(O(n), ℂ)` generated by all the entries
  (`StarAlgebra.adjoin`). Since the entries are real-valued, it equals the subalgebra
  generated by the entries (`polynomialStar_toSubalgebra`).
* `polynomialFunctions n`: the subalgebra of all functions `O(n) → ℂ` generated by the
  entries (`Algebra.adjoin`). Its elements are exactly the functions `k ↦ P((k_{ij}))` for
  `P : MvPolynomial (Fin n × Fin n) ℂ`; the direction used is
  `exists_mvPolynomial_of_mem_polynomialFunctions`, from
  `Algebra.adjoin_range_eq_range_aeval` and evaluation at a point being an algebra map
  (`MvPolynomial.comp_aeval`).
* `translateSpan f`: the span of the right translates `x ↦ f (x * k)`, `k ∈ O(n)`.
* `separated n`: the span, in the functions `O(n) → O(n) → ℂ`, of the products
  `(x, k) ↦ α(x) β(k)` with `α ∈ polynomialFunctions n` and `β ∈ C(O(n), ℂ)`.

The instance `compactSpace_orthogonal` records that `O(n)` is compact (a closed subset of the
product of closed unit balls, by `entry_norm_bound_of_unitary`).

## Proof

Fix a Haar measure `μ` on `O(n)` (the main theorem uses `Measure.haar`). Continuous functions
are integrable since `O(n)` is compact (`integrable_of_continuous`).

1. **Density** (`eq_zero_of_integral_polynomial_mul_eq_zero`). If `g ∈ C(O(n), ℂ)` satisfies
   `∫ φ g dμ = 0` for all `φ ∈ polynomialStar n`, then `g = 0`. The entries separate points of
   `O(n)` (`polynomialStar_separatesPoints`), so by the Stone–Weierstrass theorem
   (`ContinuousMap.starSubalgebra_topologicalClosure_eq_top_of_separatesPoints`)
   `polynomialStar n` is dense in `C(O(n), ℂ)` for the uniform topology. The functional
   `φ ↦ ∫ φ g dμ` is continuous for this topology (it factors through
   `ContinuousMap.toLp` into `L¹` and the integral), so it vanishes on all of `C(O(n), ℂ)`, in
   particular at `φ = ḡ`. Then `∫ |g|² dμ = 0`, so `|g|² = 0` almost everywhere, hence
   everywhere because `|g|²` is continuous and Haar measure charges every nonempty open set.
2. **Point evaluations** (`exists_sum_eval`). On a finite-dimensional space `V` of functions,
   every linear functional `ℓ` on the space of all functions agrees on `V` with
   `s ↦ Σ_j c_j s(p_j)` for finitely many points `p_j` and scalars `c_j`. This is the Library's
   `exists_sum_of_separating` with the point evaluations as separating family.
3. **Continuity of coefficients** (`continuous_functional_translate`). If `f` is continuous and
   `translateSpan f` is finite-dimensional, then for every linear functional `ℓ` the function
   `k ↦ ℓ(x ↦ f (x k))` is continuous: by step 2 it equals `k ↦ Σ_j c_j f(p_j k)`.
4. **Averages stay in the span** (`integral_translate_mem_translateSpan`). For continuous `φ`,
   the function `F_φ(x) = ∫ φ(k) f(x k) dμ(k)` lies in `translateSpan f`. Take a basis `b_i` of
   `V = translateSpan f` and extend its coordinate functionals to linear functionals `ℓ_i` on
   all functions (`LinearMap.exists_extend`). Expanding the translate `x ↦ f(x k) ∈ V` in the
   basis gives `f(x k) = Σ_i ℓ_i(f(· k)) b_i(x)`, with coefficients continuous in `k` by step
   3. Integrating term by term, `F_φ = Σ_i (∫ φ(k) ℓ_i(f(· k)) dμ(k)) b_i ∈ V`.
5. **Separation of variables** (`mul_mem_separated`, `inv_mul_mem_separated`). The space
   `separated n` is closed under pointwise products (`Submodule.span_mul_span`, since the
   generators are). For `φ` in the algebra generated by the entries, `(x, k) ↦ φ(x⁻¹ k)` lies
   in `separated n`, by induction on the algebra (`Algebra.adjoin_induction`): for a generator,
   `x⁻¹ = xᵀ` on `O(n)` gives `(x⁻¹ k)_{ab} = Σ_c x_{ca} k_{cb}`, a sum of products of an entry of
   `x` and an entry of `k`; constants, sums and products are handled by the closure properties.
6. **Integration in the second variable** (`integral_separated_mem_polynomialFunctions`). For
   `h ∈ separated n` and continuous `f`, each `h(x, ·)` is continuous and
   `x ↦ ∫ h(x, k) f(k) dμ(k)` is in `polynomialFunctions n`; by induction on the span, a
   generator `α(x) β(k)` gives `(∫ β f dμ) · α`.
7. **Averages are polynomial** (`convolution_mem_polynomialFunctions`). For
   `φ ∈ polynomialStar n` and continuous `f`, left invariance of `μ`
   (`integral_mul_left_eq_self`, substituting `k ↦ x k`) gives
   `F_φ(x) = ∫ φ(x⁻¹ k) f(k) dμ(k)`, which is polynomial in `x` by steps 5 and 6.
8. **Main theorem** (`mem_polynomialFunctions_of_finiteDimensional`). Let `S` be the span of the
   `F_φ`, `φ ∈ polynomialStar n`. By step 4, `S ⊆ translateSpan f`; by step 7, `S` consists of
   polynomial functions. Suppose `f ∉ S`. Choose a linear functional `ℓ` with `ℓ(f) ≠ 0` and
   `ℓ = 0` on `S` (`Submodule.exists_le_ker_of_notMem`), and write `ℓ = Σ_j c_j ev_{p_j}` on
   `translateSpan f` (step 2). Put `g(k) = Σ_j c_j f(p_j k)`, a continuous function. For
   `φ ∈ polynomialStar n`, since `F_φ ∈ S ⊆ translateSpan f`,
   `∫ φ g dμ = Σ_j c_j F_φ(p_j) = ℓ(F_φ) = 0`. By step 1, `g = 0`; but `f ∈ translateSpan f`
   (the translate by `1`), so `ℓ(f) = Σ_j c_j f(p_j) = g(1) = 0`, a contradiction. Hence
   `f ∈ S ⊆ polynomialFunctions n`.

`exists_mvPolynomial_of_finiteDimensional_span` turns this into the polynomial form, and
`orthogonalFinitePolynomial` is the statement.

## The form on `GL_n(ℝ)`

`exists_mvPolynomial_of_mem_kFiniteVectors`: let `f : GL_n(ℝ) → ℂ` be continuous and
`O(n)`-finite, i.e. `f ∈ kFiniteVectors (Whittaker.rightTranslation ℂ GL_n(ℝ)) (unitaryCompact ℝ n)`
(the right translates of `f` by `unitaryCompact ℝ n` span a finite-dimensional space). Then for
every `g ∈ GL_n(ℝ)` there is a polynomial `P` with
`f(g · Unitary.toUnits k) = P((k_{ij}))` for all `k ∈ O(n)`. Proof: the function
`f'(x) = f(g · x)` on `O(n)` is continuous (`continuous_unitary_toUnits`), and its translate by
`k` is the image of the right translate of `f` by `k` under the linear map
`F ↦ (x ↦ F(g · x))` (`LinearMap.funLeft`). So `translateSpan f'` lies in the image of a
finite-dimensional space and is finite-dimensional, and the main theorem applies to `f'`.
