## The new statement V1′: `ComplexLargeLanglandsQuotientInjectiveStatement`

For every `n : ℕ`, every complex vector space `V : Type uV`, every
`ρ : Representation ℂ (Matrix.unitaryGroup (Fin n) ℂ) V`, every
`π : (ℂ ⊗[ℝ] Matrix (Fin n) (Fin n) ℂ) →ₗ⁅ℂ⁆ Module.End ℂ V`, every
`ds : List ComplexCharacterData` and every complex linear map
`A : kFiniteFunctions (complexStandardModule n ds) (unitaryCompact ℂ n) →ₗ[ℂ] V`, assume:

* `IsComplexGKModule n ρ π`, `IsIrreducibleGKModule ρ π` and `IsAdmissibleGKModule ρ`;
* `IsComplexLargeGKModule n π`: the annihilator of `V` in the enveloping algebra is generated by
  its central elements, i.e. it is the minimal primitive ideal;
* `ds.length = n` and `IsComplexLanglandsOrdered ds`: the real parts of the exponents do not
  increase, `Re t₁ ≥ Re t₂ ≥ ⋯ ≥ Re t_n`;
* `A ≠ 0` and `IsComplexStandardIntertwining n ρ π ds A`: `A` carries right translation by
  `U(n)` and right derivatives along `𝔤𝔩_n(ℂ)` to `ρ` and `π(1 ⊗ ·)`.

Then `A` is injective.

The hypotheses are those of the Library's `ComplexStandardSurjectionInjectiveStatement` (V2),
with two changes: "the module has a standard-module parameter" is replaced by "the module is
large", and "`A` is surjective" by "`A ≠ 0`". The second change does not alter the content: by
the Library's `surjective_of_isComplexStandardIntertwining`, a nonzero intertwining map into an
irreducible module is surjective. The statement is the complex analogue of the Library's
`RealLargeLanglandsQuotientInjectiveStatement` (module `RealGenericLargeReduction`), which has
the same shape for `GL_n(ℝ)`.

### Source

Vogan 1978, Theorem 6.2, proof of (a) ⇒ (f), printed p. 93 (PDF page 19; folder
`provided-01cbf718…`, file `Vogan--Gelfand-Kirillov dimension for Harish-Chandra modules--1978.pdf`).
The theorem (p. 89, PDF page 15) is stated for a quasisplit `G`, "linear and reductive, with
abelian Cartan subgroups; it need not be connected". `GL_n(ℂ)`, as a real group with maximal
compact subgroup `U(n)`, is such a group. The proof of (a) ⇒ (f) runs as follows. Let `X` be
large. A translation argument reduces to nonsingular infinitesimal character. By the Langlands
classification, `X` is the Langlands quotient of a standard module `Ind_{P'}(δ ⊗ ν ⊗ 1)`.
Vogan then writes: "Suppose [the standard module] is reducible. By [11], Theorems 6.15 and
6.16, it is easy to see that `τ(X)` is non-empty. … This contradicts `τ(X) = ∅`, so [the
standard module] is irreducible". Here `τ(X)` is the Borho–Jantzen–Duflo τ-invariant (p. 84,
PDF page 10). Modules whose annihilator is the smallest one, `I₁`, for their infinitesimal
character are "characterized by the property `τ(X) = ∅`" (pp. 85–86, PDF pages 11–12), so
`τ(X) = ∅` for a large module.

For `GL_n(ℂ)` the standard modules of the Langlands classification are the principal series
`I(σ₁, …, σ_n)` of characters `σᵢ(z) = (z/|z|)^{lᵢ} |z|^{2tᵢ}` with
`Re t₁ ≥ ⋯ ≥ Re t_n` (Knapp 1994, (4.1)–(4.3) and Theorem 4 (a), p. 405, PDF page 415). The
Library's `complexStandardModule n ds` with `IsComplexLanglandsOrdered ds` is this principal
series, as explained in `ComplexGenericParameterReduction.md`. If `A ≠ 0`, then `A` is
surjective, so `V` is an irreducible quotient of `I(ds)`. By Knapp's Theorem 4 (a) it is the
Langlands quotient `J(ds)`. Vogan's argument then shows that `I(ds)` is irreducible, and the
kernel of `A` is a proper invariant subspace of an irreducible module, hence `0`.

Two remarks on the passage from Vogan's text to V1′:

* Vogan's argument concerns the standard module attached to `X` by the Langlands
  classification. V1′ speaks of every Langlands-ordered `ds` with a nonzero map
  `I(ds) → V`. For `GL_n(ℂ)` these agree: by Knapp's Theorem 4 (a) and (c), `V ≅ J(ds)`
  determines `ds` up to permutation, and principal series whose parameters differ by a
  permutation have the same composition factors. The second theorem below makes this
  precise inside the Library: V1′ follows from V1 together with V2, which records exactly
  this uniqueness (see the informal argument for V2 in
  `ComplexGenericStandardInjectiveReduction.md`).
* Vogan reduces to nonsingular infinitesimal character by translation before using the
  Langlands classification. A proof of V1′ must carry out this reduction, or give another
  argument in the singular case.

## The reduction: `complexLargePrincipalSeriesReduction`

`ComplexLargePrincipalSeriesReductionStatement` (universe `uV`) is the proposition

  `ComplexLanglandsQuotientStatement → ComplexLargeLanglandsQuotientInjectiveStatement →
    ComplexLargePrincipalSeriesStatement`.

The theorem `complexLargePrincipalSeriesReduction` proves it. Take `n`, `V`, `ρ`, `π` with the
GK-module, irreducibility, admissibility and largeness hypotheses of V1.
`ComplexLanglandsQuotientStatement`, applied to the first three, gives a list `ds` with
`ds.length = n`, `IsComplexLanglandsOrdered ds`, and a map `A` with `A ≠ 0` and
`IsComplexStandardIntertwining n ρ π ds A`. V1′, applied to all of these and to largeness,
gives that `A` is injective. Then `ds.length = n`, the injectivity of `A` and the intertwining
property of `A` are exactly `HasComplexStandardParameter n ρ π ds`
(`hasComplexStandardParameter_iff` holds by `Iff.rfl`), so the anonymous constructor
`⟨ds, hlen, A, injectivity, hA⟩` proves the conclusion.

## The converse direction: `complexLargeLanglandsQuotientInjective_of_principalSeries`

This theorem proves V1′ from `ComplexLargePrincipalSeriesStatement` (V1) and
`ComplexStandardSurjectionInjectiveStatement` (V2). Take the hypotheses of V1′. V1, applied to
the GK-module, irreducibility, admissibility and largeness hypotheses, gives a standard-module
parameter for `V`. `surjective_of_isComplexStandardIntertwining`, applied to irreducibility, to
the intertwining property of `A` and to `A ≠ 0`, shows that `A` is surjective. V2, applied to
these and to `ds.length = n` and `IsComplexLanglandsOrdered ds`, gives that `A` is injective.

So, given the Langlands classification and V2, V1 and V1′ are equivalent. V1′ asks for
nothing beyond what V1 and V2 already assert. The point of the split is that V1′ is the
injectivity step of Vogan's proof, separated from the Langlands classification, which is
handled elsewhere in the Library (`ComplexLanglandsQuotientReduction`).

## Provenance

V1′ is derived from Vogan 1978, Theorem 6.2, proof of (a) ⇒ (f) (p. 93), specialized to
`GL_n(ℂ)` with the Langlands classification in Knapp's form (Knapp 1994, Theorem 4, p. 405).
The adaptations are: the Library's algebraic `(𝔤, K)`-modules and `complexStandardModule`;
Vogan's large modules in the form `IsComplexLargeGKModule`; and quantification over every
Langlands-ordered `ds` with a nonzero map, justified above by Knapp's Theorem 4 (a), (c). The
split of V1 into the Langlands classification and V1′, and both implications, are internal to
this module and proved.
