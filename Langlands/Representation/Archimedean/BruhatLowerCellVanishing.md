# Local vanishing on a lower Bruhat cell

The nonzero normal simple-root direction gives the straightened matrix chart at the specified cell point. Restrict its source so that the zero normal slice equals the actual cell and the target lies in the given open neighborhood. The complementary and diagonal fields are tangent in these coordinates, and the normal field becomes differentiation in the last coordinate.

The chart Casimir equation proves vanishing on each fixed rectangle of real tests. Transport and real and imaginary decomposition give an open neighborhood of the cell point where the ambient distribution vanishes. This is the local assertion of Shalika's Proposition 2.10 under the scalar Casimir and covariance hypotheses.
