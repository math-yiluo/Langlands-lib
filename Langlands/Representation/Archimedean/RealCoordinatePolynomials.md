# Polynomial functions in real linear coordinates

The complex algebra generated by the real linear coordinates on a finite-dimensional real vector space consists of smooth functions and is preserved by complex conjugation. For a finite-dimensional real algebra the value of each such polynomial on a product is a finite sum of products of coordinate polynomials in the two factors. The generator calculation expands the first factor in a real basis. Addition and multiplication combine the finite index sets by disjoint union and product.

A polynomial times a fixed vector therefore has a finite-dimensional span of right translates. These elementary facts are used to construct nonzero compact-finite sections for an archimedean block induction, as required in the inducing representation of Langlands, *On the notion of an automorphic representation* (1979), Lemma 1 and Proposition 2, printed pp. 203–205, Corvallis Part I PDF pp. 198–200.
