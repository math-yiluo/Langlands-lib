# Kernel averaging in the genuine smooth-vector topology

Fix a Borel measure finite on compact sets on the unit group of a finite-dimensional real Banach algebra. For a jointly continuous complex Banach representation of this group, a continuous compactly supported kernel gives a bounded averaging operator. Uniform boundedness controls the action on the inverse of the compact support. The Bochner norm estimate bounds the operator by this common bound times the integral of the absolute inverse kernel. When the kernel is smooth and the measure is left invariant, the output consists of genuine smooth vectors. The continuous smooth-range lift makes this an operator from the Banach space into the genuine word topology.

For a smooth input, the coordinate at each word of the average is the Bochner integral of that coordinate of the weighted translated orbit. To prove this, integrate any finite collection of coordinates in its finite product of Banach spaces. A closed submodule of that finite product containing the projected orbit also contains the integral, as follows by mapping to the normed quotient. Finite coordinate neighborhoods then show that the tuple of integrals lies in the full closed derivative-coordinate image. Its empty coordinate identifies its unique preimage with the established smoother. Applying the same argument to the closed image of an invariant submodule shows that smooth averaging preserves that submodule.

The averaging and closed-image comparisons are internal derivations from the published kernel convolution and derivative-coordinate topology, using the continuous lift for smooth-range operators.
