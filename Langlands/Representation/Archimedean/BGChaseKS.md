# Lifting equivariant maps into `End M⁻(λ)` (helpers for the Bernstein–Gelfand chase)

Module: `Langlands.Representation.Archimedean.BGChaseKS`, namespace
`Langlands.Archimedean.BGChase`. It uses the lower Verma modules `M⁻(λ) = LowerVerma n λ` and the
input statements of the Library module `LowerVerma` (namespace `VermaGL`). Everything here is
proved from those input statements, which appear as hypotheses.

- `envelopingAction_action`: the enveloping action of the `𝔤𝔩_n(ℂ)`-action on `M⁻(λ)` is its
  `U`-module structure.
- `mem_span_of_action_eq_zero`: assume `LowerVermaWeightStatement` and `LowerVermaDufloStatement`,
  and let `χ` be the central character of `M⁻(λ)`. Then an element of `U` acting by zero on
  `M⁻(λ)` lies in the left ideal generated by the `z - χ(z)`, `z` central. Duflo gives the ideal
  generated by the central elements acting by zero. Such a `z` acts on the generator by `χ(z)`,
  and the generator is nonzero, so `χ(z) = 0`.
- `mem_span_of_linearIndependent_bound`: an elementary linear-algebra lemma. If `d` linearly
  independent vectors satisfy a property `P` and no `d + 1` linearly independent vectors do, then
  every vector satisfying `P` lies in their span. It is stated over an additive commutative
  monoid, with the group structure recovered locally, to avoid slow instance unification for
  spaces of maps into `End M⁻(λ)`.
- `exists_lift` (Bernstein–Gelfand 1980, 1.11 and the proof of 5.10): let `λ` be `Z`-extremal,
  `χ` the central character of `M⁻(λ)`, and `σ` a finite-dimensional representation on `W` with
  integral weights. Then every equivariant `g : W → End M⁻(λ)` (`g(σ(Y)w) = [Y, g(w)]`) is the
  action composed with some linear `g' : W → U`.

  Proof: `KostantMultiplicityStatement` gives `dim W₀` equivariant maps `f_i : W → U` that are
  independent modulo the ideal generated by the `z - χ(z)`. Composed with the action, they stay
  linearly independent by `mem_span_of_action_eq_zero`. `LowerVermaHomBoundStatement` says no
  `dim W₀ + 1` independent equivariant maps exist, so `g` is a linear combination of these
  composites, and `g'` is the same combination of the `f_i`.
