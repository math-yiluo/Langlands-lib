# Block partitions for selected compact rotation factors

A finite set of starting positions describes disjoint adjacent
coordinate pairs. A coordinate is labeled by the first position of its
pair, when it is the second coordinate of an active pair, and by its
own position otherwise. The map onto its label type is monotone.

Each label fiber injects into a two-element set by subtracting the
first position. Every chosen pair gives a fiber of size two, and a
label outside the chosen set has a fiber of at most one. This identifies
the number of pair labels with the number of chosen starts.

The orthogonal matrices diagonal by these blocks form a closed
subgroup. Its multiplication law follows from the block support of
matrix products, and inversion uses transposition. Every diagonal
sign reflection and every rotation of one of the active pairs lies
in this group. These internal facts record the exact refined
partition used for a term of the compact Fourier filter.

If the source Cartan matrix has more two-coordinate slots than there
are chosen starts, the fixed-flag placement theorem precludes the
identity among the compact arguments for this subgroup. The source
slots and regularity satisfy the fixed-flag theorem's normality,
Gram, and nonzero skew-entry hypotheses. The image of the compact
family of arguments is closed and excludes the identity. Every test
supported in the complementary open neighborhood therefore vanishes
on all those arguments.
