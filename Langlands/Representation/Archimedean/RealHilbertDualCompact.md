# Compact-finite restriction of the real continuous Hilbert dual

Let \(g:V\to H\) identify an algebraic orthogonal-group module with a
dense injective model in a real general linear Hilbert action. Restriction
of continuous complex-linear functionals along \(g\) intertwines the
norm compact action on the inverse-transpose continuous dual with the
algebraic dual action. The real inverse transpose fixes the orthogonal
group. Continuity and norm density make restriction injective.

When all vectors of \(V\) are compact-finite with continuous coefficients
and \(V\) is compact-admissible, the continuous dual twist is compact-
admissible. Any finite matrix type intertwiner in the Hilbert dual
restricts to a finite type intertwiner in the algebraic finite dual.
Injectivity of restriction and admissibility of that finite dual give
the required finite-dimensional space of norm intertwiners.

When the original compact action is unitary, the norm compact-finite
continuous functionals separate \(V\). For nonzero \(v\), the Riesz
functional of \(g(v)\) is nonzero at \(g(v)\). It is compact-finite
because Riesz intertwines the compact actions conjugate linearly and
preserves finiteness of the orbit span. The separating submodule lemma
then gives every algebraic finite dual functional by restriction.

For a jointly continuous action, a Borel Haar measure on the group and
a left invariant Borel probability measure on the compact subgroup
give genuine smoothness of every norm compact-finite vector of the
admissible dual. The empty word coordinate identifies the norm and smooth
compact-finite carriers. Composing this identification and the bijective
restriction yields `smoothHilbertDualEquiv`.

This is an internal comparison of the actual continuous Hilbert dual
with the previously defined algebraic finite dual.
