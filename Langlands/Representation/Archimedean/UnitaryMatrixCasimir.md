# The trace Casimir of a unitary matrix representation

The matrix trace pairing is invariant under conjugation. The genuine
derived representation conjugates each direction by the inverse unit
when differentiating a translated vector. Contracting a word of length
two against the trace pair therefore gives an operator that commutes
with the full group action.

Differentiating the constant inner product of two translated smooth
vectors proves skew symmetry for each real direction. Moving two
derivatives across the inner product reverses their order and cancels
the signs. The trace contraction is invariant under swapping the two
families, so the Casimir is symmetric on the smooth vectors.

The scalarity theorem assumes a jointly continuous, unitary and
topologically irreducible action on a nonzero complex Hilbert space.
Haar smoothing on the Borel matrix group gives norm density of the
smooth vectors. The graph comparison lemma applies to their inclusion
and to the Casimir on them and gives a bounded intertwiner extending
the Casimir. The Hilbert commutant theorem makes that intertwiner
scalar, which gives the scalar action on the smooth vectors used to
prove the distribution eigencondition. This real trace contraction
and graph argument supply the representation-side input to the use of
Shalika (1974), Theorem 2.1, in the proof of Theorem 3.1.
