# Polynomial operator growth for archimedean general linear groups

The height of a unit is the maximum of one and the norms of its value and inverse. It is continuous, submultiplicative, and unchanged by inversion. Its sublevel sets are compact in a proper normed ring because the pairs of mutually inverse elements form a closed subset of the product of the ring and its opposite.

A jointly continuous representation of `GL_n(ℝ)` or `GL_n(ℂ)` by bounded operators on a complex Banach space has an operator norm bounded by a polynomial in this height. Polar decomposition and the Hermitian spectral theorem write a matrix as two unitary factors around a positive real diagonal factor. The unitary factors have uniformly bounded height by compactness. Submultiplicativity then bounds the diagonal entries and their reciprocals by a fixed multiple of the matrix height. Taking a positive diagonal root of order one more than the ceiling of the logarithm of that bound places the root and its inverse in a fixed height sublevel set. Joint continuity and Banach–Steinhaus bound the representation operators uniformly there and on the unitary factors. Raising this bound to the number of factors gives the polynomial estimate.

This internal result uses the Library's positive polar factors, positive diagonal powers, and the matrix row-sum operator norm of the actual general linear model.
