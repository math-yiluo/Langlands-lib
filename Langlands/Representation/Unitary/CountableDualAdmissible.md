# Irreducible admissible subrepresentations from a countable smooth dual

Module: `Langlands.Representation.Unitary.CountableDualAdmissible`, namespace
`Langlands.CountableDual`. Author: Worker 1827.

This internal module proves an abstract representation-theoretic existence result and a
transfer lemma. They are used to find an irreducible admissible finite slice inside an
actual cuspidal dual (see [CuspidalContragredientSlice](../../Automorphic/CuspidalContragredientSlice.md)).
No Harish-Chandra finiteness, no continuity of compact actions and no topology on the
carrier is used.

## Setting

`G` is a topological group, `K` is a group, and `V` is a complex vector space with no
topology. `ρ : Representation ℂ G V` and `κ : Representation ℂ K V` commute. The data are:

* `ρ` is smooth (`Langlands.IsSmooth`: every vector is fixed by an open subgroup);
* every vector is `κ`-finite (`FiniteVectors.IsFiniteVector κ v`: its `κ`-orbit spans a
  finite-dimensional space);
* `b : InvariantPositiveForm ρ` is a positive definite Hermitian form, conjugate linear in
  the left variable, invariant under `ρ` and also under `κ`;
* every open subgroup of `G` contains a compact open subgroup;
* the dual hypothesis: every complex subspace of `Module.Dual ℂ V` that consists of functionals
  smooth for `ρ.dual` and finite for `κ.dual` has rank at most `ℵ₀`.

## Main theorem

`exists_irreducible_admissible` concludes that, if `V` is nontrivial, there is a
subrepresentation `A ≠ ⊥` of `ρ` such that `A.toRepresentation` is irreducible
(Mathlib's `Representation.IsIrreducible`) and admissible (`Langlands.IsAdmissible`, the
all-open-subgroups convention).

## The implemented proof

1. **Orthogonal complements** (section `Form`). For the core of `b`, `rightFunctional`
   is the linear functional `x ↦ ⟪y, x⟫`, and `orth q` is the right orthogonal of a subspace.
   `inf_orth` uses definiteness. `sup_orth` shows `q ⊔ orth q = ⊤` for finite-dimensional `q`.
   Pairing against a basis of `q` gives a map to `Fin d → ℂ` whose restriction to `q` is
   injective, hence surjective by dimension. Subtracting a preimage puts every vector in
   `q ⊔ orth q`.
2. **Semisimplicity over the group algebra** (section `CompactSide`). The carrier gets the
   group-algebra module structure of `κ.asModule` (`algModule`), with `of k • v = κ k v`.
   `toAlg` turns a `κ`-stable subspace into a `ℂ[K]`-submodule. If `b` is `κ`-invariant,
   `orth` of a `κ`-stable subspace is `κ`-stable (`orth_stable`). Every orbit span is finite
   dimensional. Inside it, `orth` cut down to the orbit span complements each submodule
   (modular law). So every orbit span is a semisimple `ℂ[K]`-module, and
   `isSemisimpleModule` follows from Mathlib's `isSemisimpleModule_of_isSemisimpleModule_submodule'`.
3. **Compact averaging** (section `Averaging`). The averaging map is the Library's
   `Langlands.Compact.averageMap` over a compact open subgroup `U` (the normalized mean of
   the finite `U`-orbit). New facts: it is unchanged by translating its argument by `U`
   (`averageMap_apply_rep`, since both orbits are the same set). It preserves
   invariant subspaces (`averageMap_mem`). And `⟪y, e_U x⟫ = ⟪y, x⟫` for `U`-fixed `y`
   (`inner_averageMap`).
4. **Finite multiplicity** (`finiteDimensional_isotypic_fixed`). Fix a simple
   finite-dimensional `ℂ[K]`-submodule `S`. Let `C` be the intersection of its isotypic
   component with the `U`-fixed vectors (`U` compact open). `C` is a `ℂ[K]`-submodule,
   isotypic of type `S`. By Mathlib, `C ≃ (ι →₀ S)` for some `ι`. If `ι` is infinite, take
   a complement `D` of `C`, the projection `π` onto `C`, and a functional `ℓ` with
   `ℓ s₀ ≠ 0`. For `μ : ι → ℂ`, set `L μ = (x ↦ Σᵢ μ i · ℓ(xᵢ)) ∘ e ∘ π ∘ e_U`. Each
   `L μ` is fixed by `U` (step 3). Each is `κ`-finite: `κ.dual k (L μ)` replaces `ℓ` by
   `ℓ ∘ (of k⁻¹ •)`, because `e_U`, `π` and `e` commute with `K`. So its orbit lies in the
   image of the finite-dimensional `Dual ℂ S`. Evaluating on preimages of `single i s₀`
   shows `μ ↦ L μ` is injective. Its range then has rank `#(ι → ℂ) ≥ 𝔠 > ℵ₀`
   (`rank_fun_infinite`, `Cardinal.mk_complex`). This contradicts the dual hypothesis.
   Hence `ι` is finite and `C` is finite dimensional.
5. **Minimal fixed dimension** (`exists_irreducible_admissible_le`). Let `A` be a nonzero
   subrepresentation whose intersections with fixed vectors of compact open subgroups are
   finite dimensional. Choose a compact open `U₀` fixing a nonzero vector of `A`. Among
   subrepresentations `B ≤ A` with nonzero `U₀`-fixed part, take `B₀` whose fixed part has
   minimal dimension (`Nat.find`). Let `A₀` be generated by that fixed part. Take a
   subrepresentation `C ≤ A₀`. If it has nonzero fixed vectors, minimality forces its fixed
   part to be all of `B₀`'s, so `C = A₀`. Otherwise `e_{U₀}` kills `C`. Step 3 and
   invariance then make `C` orthogonal to the generators of `A₀`, so `C = ⊥` by
   definiteness. `isIrreducible_of_forall` converts this dichotomy into irreducibility of
   `A₀.toRepresentation`. Admissibility follows from the finite-dimensionality hypothesis
   via compact refinements.
6. **Assembly** (`exists_irreducible_admissible`). Take a simple submodule `S` of a nonzero
   orbit span (Mathlib's `eq_bot_or_exists_simple_le`). Let `A` be the isotypic component
   of `S`. It is a subrepresentation of `ρ` because isotypic components are fully invariant
   and each `ρ g` is `ℂ[K]`-linear (`algRep`). Steps 4 and 5 then apply.

## Transfer lemma

`exists_dual_subrepresentation` takes a second smooth representation `σ` of `G` on `W`
and a linear injection `β : W → Dual ℂ V` that is equivariant for `σ` and `ρ.dual`. It
assumes every functional `conjFunctional b h = ⟪h, ·⟫` is attained by `β`. Under these
hypotheses, every nonzero irreducible admissible subrepresentation of `ρ` has a nonzero
irreducible admissible image in `σ`. The proof chooses `Φ h` with `β (Φ h) = ⟪h, ·⟫`. It
shows that `Φ` is a conjugate-linear (`starRingEnd ℂ`) injective map, equivariant by
`conjFunctional_apply_rep`. It then transports the subrepresentation, its lattice of
subrepresentations (using `forall_of_isIrreducible`) and its finite-dimensional fixed spaces
(using finite generation of semilinear images).

Step 5 could alternatively be derived from the Library's
`Langlands.admissibleUnitarySemisimplicity` (admissible unitarizable representations are
semisimple) applied to `A`; the implemented elementary argument is self-contained and needs
finite-dimensional fixed spaces only for compact open subgroups.

Auxiliary results: `isIrreducible_of_equiv` (irreducibility is invariant under an actual
equivalence), `conjFunctional_injective`.

## Provenance

Internal and derived. The ingredients are standard facts used in an elementary
combination, implemented directly from Mathlib and the Library: semisimplicity from an
invariant positive form, isotypic decomposition of semisimple modules
(`IsIsotypicOfType.linearEquiv_finsupp`), uncountable dimension of `ι → ℂ` (Erdős–Kaplansky,
`rank_fun_infinite`), and the minimal-fixed-dimension construction of an irreducible
subrepresentation of a unitary admissible representation. No source theorem is quoted.
