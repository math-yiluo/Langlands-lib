# Galois induction of a compatible Hecke-character family

`IsGaloisInductionOfCharacter` compares an actual continuous representation over `L_λ` with finite-index induction from an actual rank-one representation. The subgroup is the range of absolute Galois restriction along the specified compatible closure embedding. The statement records the descended character on this subgroup by its equality on the range-restriction map, then requires an invertible intertwiner with the published equivariant-function representation `FiniteRepresentation.induce`. The finite-index witness uses the image of the absolute group of the finite extension in the base absolute group. `MatchesHeckeInductionEulerPolynomials` gives the almost-everywhere comparison with the Library's actual-place Euler denominator for every choice of local uniformizers.

`HeckeCharacterGaloisInductionStatement` takes a compatible family of rank-one realizations of a Hecke character `χ` on `E`, over the single coefficient number field `L` embedded in `ℂ`, and the actual finite extension `E/K`. It returns a family of continuous representations of `Gal(K̄/K)`, of rank `[E:K]`. Every member is induced from the given member of the rank-one family. Its common raw Euler polynomials at good places are those from induction of `χ`, using geometric Frobenius. The exceptional set for compatibility is independent of `λ`.

Over a good base place `v`, geometric Frobenius permutes the coset-coordinate lines with one cycle of length `f(w|v)` for each place `w` over `v`. Its return map on that line is geometric Frobenius at `w`, evaluated by the inducing character. The cycle contributes `1 - χ_w(ϖ_w) X^{f(w|v)}` to the Euler polynomial. The places where the extension ramifies or the original system is excluded lie over a finite set of base places. For continuity, the subgroup is open and there are finitely many cosets, so the coset permutation is locally constant and the matrix entries on each coset cell come from the continuous character. These arguments give the same common coefficient polynomials for all the supplied `λ`.

The source is Harris 1998 (`harris-1998-local-langlands`), Lemma 1.6, printed pp. 188–189, companion TeX lines 704–742, and Definition 1.3(b) with its unramified induction comparison. The statement expresses the elementary Galois induction step of that proof using the actual finite-index and Frobenius interfaces, including when `E/K` is nonnormal.
