# Weight alignment for pure Frobenius systems

`PureFrobeniusWeightAlignmentStatement` takes actual continuous finite-dimensional representations `R i` at one coefficient place `λ` of `L`, indexed by `Fin m`, together with raw Euler polynomials `P i v` over `L` and integer weights `w i`. Outside a finite set and away from the residue characteristic of `λ`, each polynomial is the raw Euler polynomial of the corresponding unramified representation at geometric Frobenius. The eigenvalues of the `i`th polynomial have weight `w i` at the chosen embedding `ι : L → ℂ`. The comparison polynomials `P₀ v` over the same field have constant term one and eigenvalues of weight `w₀`. The unitary normalization of the product of the `P i v` agrees with that of `P₀ v` on these good places.

The conclusion gives integers `a i`. Each positive-rank summand has `w i + 2 a i = w₀`. On good places the product of the Euler polynomials with eigenvalues multiplied by `q_v ^ (a i)` equals `P₀ v` over `L`. In a later representation comparison this scalar is the geometric Frobenius value of the `(-a i)`th power of the cyclotomic character.

## Derived argument

At a good degree-one prime `v` above a rational prime `p` with `√p ∉ ι(L)`, compare the coefficients of `X` in the two normalized Euler polynomials and multiply by `p^(w₀/2)`. The contribution of the summands of the same weight parity as `w₀` is in `ι(L)`. The remaining contribution is `p^(-1/2)` times the image of the sum of their raw `X` coefficients weighted by `p^((w₀ + 1 - w i)/2)`. These exponents are integers. The two subspaces of `ℂ` are independent over `ι(L)`, and the comparison term is in `ι(L)`, so the weighted sum of the coefficients of the other parity is zero in `L`.

Map this equality into `L_λ`, where the raw first coefficients are negative Frobenius traces. For arbitrary precision, take a finite extension of `K` whose absolute group acts sufficiently close to the identity on the finitely many `R i`. Take its normal closure over `ℚ` together with sufficiently many `ℓ`-power roots of unity, where `ℓ` is the residue characteristic of `λ`. By Chebotarev, choose a rational prime splitting completely there, off the finite excluded places and off the finitely many primes with `√p ∈ ι(L)`. Its places in `K` have degree one, the Frobenius traces are arbitrarily close to the ranks, and all the integer powers of `p` in the weighted sum are arbitrarily close to one. The zero weighted sum then forces the sum of the ranks of the other parity to be zero. Each such rank is a natural number, so each is zero. The rank-zero raw polynomials are all one on good places. With `a i = (w₀ - w i)/2` for the remaining summands, their eigenvalues all have weight `w₀` after the indicated scalings. The initial unitary equality now gives equality of the raw polynomial products, by undoing their common positive scaling and using injectivity of `ι`.

## Source and use

This is a derived comparison for the normalization in Harris 1998, pp. 183–185, Definition 1.1 and Theorem 1.2, and the restriction comparison in Lemma 1.6, pp. 188–189, companion TeX lines 704–744 (`harris-1998-local-langlands`). In the Library the theorem supplies a finite sum of pure families and `WeakAssociation` divides each eigenvalue by its absolute value. The weight alignment supplies the extra argument needed before an equality of raw Frobenius polynomials can be used. The choice of split primes uses the finite Chebotarev consequence, as in Serre, *Abelian l-Adic Representations and Elliptic Curves*, Chapter I §2.2 (`serre-abelian-l-adic-1989`). A subsequent comparison can pass to a common coefficient field, restrict the actual representations to the step field, and apply this statement there before invoking semisimplification and Frobenius rigidity.
