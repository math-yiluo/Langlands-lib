# Finite coefficient models from the representation trace

`Langlands.GaloisRepresentation.finiteCoefficientModel` proves
`Langlands.GaloisRepresentation.FiniteCoefficientModelStatement`. This is the
algebraic finite-coefficient step in the Library's reduction from Harris–Taylor,
Theorem VII.1.9, pp. 230–231, as explained in the
[statement documentation](FiniteCoefficientModelStatement.md).

The proof uses the image of the group algebra in the endomorphisms of the
original carrier. The surjection onto the image transports semisimplicity to
the carrier as a module over this image. Evaluating at a finite carrier basis
embeds the regular left module of the image algebra into a finite product of
copies of that semisimple module. The image algebra is therefore semisimple.
For a left-orthogonal element \(x\) of the trace pairing, the left ideal it
generates is generated by an idempotent \(e\). As a left multiple of \(x\),
\(e\) has zero trace by cyclicity. The trace of an idempotent is the
dimension of its image, so characteristic zero gives \(e = 0\), and hence
\(x = 0\). Symmetry gives nondegeneracy.

The images of group elements span the image algebra, so the proof chooses a
basis from these images. The change-of-basis matrix to its trace-dual basis
has entries \(\operatorname{tr}(\rho(g_j g_i))\) in the base field \(K\).
The inverse change-of-basis matrix also has entries in \(K\), so the trace
coordinates \(\operatorname{tr}(\rho(g g_i))\) give \(K\)-valued coordinates
for every \(\rho(g)\) in the chosen image basis.

In the finite basis of the original carrier, adjoin the entries of the
finitely many image-basis endomorphisms to \(K\). Each entry is algebraic
over \(K\), so this intermediate field has finite degree. Its compositum
with the prescribed finite intermediate field \(\Lambda_0\) still has finite
degree and contains the entries of every group element's matrix, since those
entries are \(K\)-linear combinations of the adjoined entries. The proof keeps
the three independent universes of the statement and permits a zero-dimensional
carrier.
