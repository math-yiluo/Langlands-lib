# Labelled Hodge–Tate decomposition from grades and inverse twist

This is the local linear algebra at the end of the even-degree Harris route. It is derived from the Library's conventions for `HasLabelledHodgeTateDecomposition`, `HasLabelledHodgeTateAt`, and `twist`; it is not a statement of a geometric comparison theorem.

For the actual local pullback `B` over its coefficient completion `F`, `labelledGrade p F B j` is the `F`-subspace of `C_F ⊗[F] B` on which the semilinear Galois action is multiplication by the `j`th power of the cyclotomic character. The `C_F`-linear comparison on a grade sends `c ⊗ x` to `c • x`. If the specified grades at distinct exponents `k i` are finite-dimensional of common rank `a` over `F` and the sum of those maps is bijective, `HarrisLabelledHTFromGradesStatement` asks for the actual semilinear decomposition with each `k i` repeated `a` times. Bases of the grades over `F`, followed by extension to `C_F`, give the intended proof; arbitrary bases of the tensor product would not suffice.

`HasCharacterTateExponentAt` refers to the actual completion-valued character pulled back along the same label as `B`. Its nonzero period `x` satisfies `χ(g) g(x) = cyclotomic(g)^t x`. Multiplying the original comparison by `x⁻¹` gives the comparison for the inverse twist, with exponents `k i − t`, by `HarrisLabelledInverseTwistStatement`. No finite-order or split-prime hypothesis is imposed here. Positivity of the multiplicity and existence of the rank-one exponent are separate inputs in the Harris application. `HarrisLabelledGradeAndTwistStatement` groups the two proof targets.

Faltings (1988), Chapter III, Theorem 4.1, p. 298, as used by Harris (1999), proof of 5.7(i), pp. 313–314, supplies the geometric comparison used upstream. Harris–Taylor (2001), VII.1.9, pp. 227–228, illustrates how a specified embedding enters a graded dimension computation. Neither reference is used here to infer these grade ranks from an unlabelled decomposition. A geometric proof must still identify the grades of the same Hecke multiplicity object, including at coefficient places over nonsplit rational primes, and compute the scalar avatar's Tate exponent at the chosen label.
