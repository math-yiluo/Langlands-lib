# Determinant products and equality of pure weights

The determinant character composes the determinant with the actual
representation. On a finite basis, continuity follows from continuity
of the action and the matrix determinant. A linear intertwiner
preserves the determinant of each action. After a map to the complex
field, the determinant is the product of all roots of the mapped
characteristic polynomial, counted with multiplicity.

When those roots have pure weight \(m\) at residue cardinality \(q\),
their absolute values equal \(q^{m/2}\). The product of the determinant
and its complex conjugate is therefore \(q^{dm}\), where \(d\) is the
degree of the characteristic polynomial.

Two continuous embeddings of coefficient completions into the same
p-adic algebraic closure can induce conjugate complex embeddings on
the coefficient number field. At the common good places, the two
determinants then map to conjugate products of roots of the pure
characteristic polynomial. The product of the continuous determinant
characters equals the inverse cyclotomic character to power \(dm\)
on the degree-one geometric Frobenius classes. Density extends the
equality to the full Galois group.

Scalar extension maps the determinant to the new field, so an
equivalence over that field gives equality of the mapped local
determinants. For two families of the same positive rank, use the
two conjugate identifications in the local comparison and multiply
the determinant equalities. At a geometric lift away from the
cyclotomic prime, this gives \(q^{dm}=q^{dn}\). Since \(q>1\) and
\(d>0\), the weights \(m\) and \(n\) coincide.
