# Parity of pure weight differences

The arithmetic step in comparing weights uses the fact that a
rational prime \(p\) outside a number field's discriminant cannot be
a square in that field. A hypothetical square root is integral. At a
prime above \(p\) it belongs to the prime ideal, so \(p\) belongs to its
square and the ramification index differs from one. The discriminant
condition gives unramifiedness and ramification index one.

If the complex radius \(p^{d/2}\) belongs to the coefficient field and
\(d=2k+1\), division by \(p^k\) puts \(\sqrt p\) in that field. The
ramification argument rules that out and leaves an integral
half-weight difference.

For two pure families weakly associated to the same numerical
spectrum, fix a member of each family. Their pure Euler polynomials
differ outside a finite set by substitution of the complex radius
for the weight difference. Enlarge the exceptional set by places
above the coefficient primes and the discriminant of the
compositum of the complex coefficient images.

The trace character of the second member is continuous and has
nonzero value at the identity by its positive rank in characteristic
zero. Density of the degree-one geometric Frobenius conjugates away
from the enlarged set implies that its trace is nonzero at one such
place. The ratio of the linear Euler coefficients at that place is
the radius, and both coefficients lie in the compositum. The residue
cardinality is a rational prime outside its discriminant, so the
arithmetic step gives the integral half-weight difference.
