# Reduction of the Harris pairwise comparison to the canonical comparison

`HarrisSupercuspidalLocalComparisonReductionStatement` is an internal
consequence of the Library's canonical local parameter existence and
absolute irreducibility propositions for Harris (1999), Theorem 5.9(ii).
It takes both propositions as premises. Its conclusion is the published
`HarrisSupercuspidalLocalComparisonStatement` in the same four independent
universes. The theorem `harrisSupercuspidalLocalComparison_of_canonical`
proves this implication.

For each of the two cuspidal realizations, the canonical parameter
statement supplies a pure compatible family. Equality of the local
smooth classes gives equivalences between their scalar-extended local
restrictions at every compatible coefficient identification. These
equivalences identify the mapped determinants on a local geometric
Frobenius lift. Multiplication of the determinant equalities at the
two conjugate identifications forces the canonical weights to agree.

The proof embeds the supplied coefficient completion in a \(p\)-adic
algebraic closure and extends the given complex embedding of its number
field to a field identification. That identification selects compatible
coefficient completions of both canonical witnesses. The second premise
supplies absolute irreducibility for the first canonical restriction.
For each pair of a canonical and a supplied family, the proof transfers
weak association to the same spectral presentation. The half-difference
of the weights is an integer by the parity lemma for the first pair.
Since the two pairs have the same weight difference, this integer
defines one continuous inverse cyclotomic twist of both supplied
families. Their twisted Euler polynomials match those of the respective
canonical families at almost all geometric Frobenius lifts after
mapping coefficients.

The common-twist comparison extends these identities to trace equality
by Chebotarev density and yields two actual local equivalences by
irreducible character comparison. It composes them with the canonical
equivalence and descends the resulting intertwiner to the supplied
coefficient completion. Cancelling the common character gives the
intertwiner of the original local actions.
