# Local comparison after a common twist

This reduction combines the two Euler comparisons needed in the Harris argument. The original representations share a coefficient field and are twisted by the same continuous character. Each twisted representation has matching rank and matching Euler polynomials outside a finite set with a respective auxiliary representation when embedded in the common algebraically closed Hausdorff field. At the selected place the two extended auxiliary restrictions are equivalent, and the first auxiliary restriction is absolutely irreducible.

The proof transfers absolute irreducibility to the shared field and through the auxiliary equivalence. Each cofinite Euler comparison then gives an actual equivalence of extended local restrictions. Composing the three equivalences identifies the extended twisted original restrictions. The extended twisted source is irreducible, so reflection of irreducibility and coefficient descent supply an actual intertwiner over the original field. That intertwiner also intertwines the untwisted local actions by cancellation of the common character.

The normalization and the equivalence of the auxiliary restrictions enter as premises. This reduction uses the same twist for both original representations.
