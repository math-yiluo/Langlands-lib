# Compatible rank-one realizations of algebraic Hecke characters

`IsCompatibleLambdaAdicRealization` relates a continuous complex Hecke character `χ` of a number field `K` to actual continuous characters of `Gal(K̄/K)` into the units of `L_λ`, for every finite place `λ` of a coefficient number field `L`. One finite set of exceptional places of `K` works for every `λ`. At a place `v` outside that set, `χ` is unramified and its value on a uniformizer is the image of a common coefficient `a` under `ι : L → ℂ`. For `λ` of residue characteristic different from that of `v`, the `λ`-adic character is unramified at `v` and its value on every lift of geometric Frobenius is the image of `a` in `L_λ`. Existence of a geometric Frobenius lift then gives the Library's `IsLambdaAdicRealization` for each `λ`.

`AlgebraicHeckeLambdaAdicRealizationStatement` gives this family for every Hecke character over a totally complex number field whose archimedean part is an integral infinity type (`Harris.IsAlgebraic`). One chooses a finite conductor and a coefficient number field containing the values of the algebraic ideal character. The conductor and the infinity type give an algebraic character of the Serre group. The ideal character evaluates this algebraic character on the rational Frobenius points of that group. Equivalently, in the idelic construction one replaces the infinite algebraic factor by the corresponding algebraic factor at the places over the coefficient residue characteristic. Finite reciprocity for the quotients of the resulting continuous character gives the `λ`-adic character. At an unramified place away from `λ`, its evaluation is the original uniformizer value. Serre uses arithmetic Frobenius. Inverting the reciprocity convention translates the construction to geometric Frobenius as used here. A finite conductor and the common number field work for the entire family.

The source is Serre, *Abelian l-Adic Representations and Elliptic Curves*, 1989 reprint (`serre-abelian-l-adic-1989`), Chapter II §§2.3–2.7, especially the Frobenius formula in §2.5, pp. II-18–II-19 (PDF pp. 68–69), and the identification with Weil’s type `A₀` characters in §2.7, p. II-24 (PDF p. 74). Harris 1998 (`harris-1998-local-langlands`), Lemma 1.6, p. 188, companion TeX lines 704–708, uses this family on the inducing field. The statement is the rank-one specialization of the construction in the Library's realization convention.

In the Proposition 4.8 singleton branch the input is the type `A₀` character `ξ · ‖·‖^(([E':E]-1)/2)` on `E'`. Harris's remark on p. 191, companion TeX lines 866–875, accounts for this twist when the original infinity type is half-integral. The positive norm exponent yields the multiplier `q_v^{-([E':E]-1)/2}` at a uniformizer. Finite-order characters on a CM field also satisfy the algebraicity premise because its archimedean idele factor is connected. This application needs the rank-one construction and its unramified comparison on these characters. The statement makes no assertion about an absolute idelic Artin map or a ramified local correspondence.
