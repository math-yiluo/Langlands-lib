# Comparing Euler polynomials of pure weakly associated families

These comparison lemmas arise in the passage from the unitary Euler
polynomials of Harris (1999), (5.6), to the arithmetic Euler polynomials
of two supplied families in the general-coefficient comparison at a
split supercuspidal place (`HarrisSupercuspidalLocalComparisonStatement`).
They use the published purity and
weak association predicates on actual representations.

At a place of residue cardinality \(q\), all roots of the reverse of a
pure Euler polynomial have absolute value \(q^{m/2}\) at the given
complex embedding. Its constant coefficient is one whenever it matches a
representation. Thus reversing twice restores the original polynomial.
Compose the equality of unitary normalizations with
\(X \mapsto q^{m/2}X\) to obtain the arithmetic comparison. The remaining
substitution on the polynomial of weight \(n\) is
\(X \mapsto q^{(m-n)/2}X\).

Two matching polynomial systems for one family agree at a good place by
evaluating at a geometric Frobenius lift for one coefficient place and
using injectivity of the map into its completion. This transfers weak
association to the polynomials that witness purity. The scalar comparison
then holds outside the combined finite exceptional sets, away from the
residue characteristics of both chosen members of the families. Spectral
comparison for two actual presentations of the same cuspidal class
allows either presentation to supply the numerical spectrum.
