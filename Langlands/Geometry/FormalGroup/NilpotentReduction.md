# Reduction modulo the distinguished element

For a commutative ring R and π ∈ R, `ResidueRing R π` is the actual
quotient R/(π). The name denotes a quotient ring and does not impose
field or reducedness hypotheses. For a commutative R-algebra A,
`Nilp.reductionIdeal π A` is the principal ideal generated by the image
of π, and `Nilp.Reduction π A` is A modulo that ideal.

`reductionQuotient` is the canonical surjective R-algebra map. It kills
the image of π. An R-algebra homomorphism preserves the principal
ideals, so `reductionMap` constructs its quotient map, with the formula
[f(a)] for the image of [a]. Identity and composition follow by checking
their composites with the surjective quotient map. The natural algebra
structure over R/(π) is also constructed, and `residueReductionMap`
is linear over this quotient coefficient ring.

`Nilp.residueReductionFunctor` is an actual functor from `Nilp R π`
to `CommAlgCat (ResidueRing R π)`. `reductionFunctor` keeps the same
quotient algebras as objects of `Nilp R π`, since π is zero in each
of them. The canonical quotient maps form `reductionNatTrans` from
the identity functor to this endofunctor. Finally,
`reductionIdeal_isNilpotent` proves that the quotient is a nilpotent
thickening on every Nilp object: if the image of π has nth power zero,
the nth power of its principal ideal is zero. No exponent is chosen
as part of the object data.

This is derived algebraic infrastructure for the special-fiber terms
in Drinfeld, *Coverings of p-adic Symmetric Regions* (1976), §2 A,
printed p.109: the definition of G(B) uses the ring B/(π), a coefficient
homomorphism into it, and the reduction of a formal module to it.
The present functors construct that quotient and its functoriality for
the generalized category `Nilp R π`. They do not construct formal module
reductions or moduli triples yet. The proofs use Mathlib's actual ideal
quotients, quotient algebra structures and principal-ideal powers.
