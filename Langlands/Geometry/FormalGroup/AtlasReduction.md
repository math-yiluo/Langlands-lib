# Reduction of actual formal O-module atlases

This is internal infrastructure specializing `AtlasBaseChangeAlong` and
`AtlasRationalBaseChangeAlong` to the actual quotient maps of
`ModuleReduction` and `NilpotentReduction`. It makes no additional
original-literature assertion.

Let `O → R` be a homomorphism of commutative rings, `π ∈ R`, and `A` an
object of `Nilp R π`. The coefficient algebras carry the structural composite
`O`-algebra structures. The reduction of `A` is the actual quotient of its
ring by the principal ideal generated by the image of `π`; its structural
map sends `π` to zero. The ideal is nilpotent by
`Nilp.reductionIdeal_isNilpotent`, without a chosen exponent.

For a formal `O`-module atlas `X` over `A`, `BasicAtlas.reduction` extends
the actual coefficients along `Nilp.quotientO O A`. Its principal
generators are exactly the images of the original generators under the
quotient map. Equal original covers give equal reduced covers. The finite
coordinate types of two compared atlases may differ. Tests of an atlas
have the same universe as its coefficient ring.

`reductionPointEquiv` identifies the complete compatible families of the
reduced atlas on a quotient-algebra test with the original families on that
test restricted along the quotient map. This is an `O`-linear equivalence.
It is natural in every further test map; `reductionPointFunctorIso`
packages the resulting natural isomorphism of module-valued point
functors. This compares families on the reduced tests and does not
identify the points on `A` with the points on `A/(π)`.

`Hom.reduction` reduces actual integral formal atlas maps, preserving
identity and composition. Its forward and inverse whole-family comparison
squares follow from the supplied-target extension squares.
`Iso.reduction` extends both actual formal maps and both inverse equations;
its projection laws identify them with the reductions of the original
forward and inverse maps.

For a field `O`-algebra `F`, `RationalHom.reduction` is `F`-linear on the
global tensor `F ⊗[O] BasicAtlas.Hom`. It commutes with rationalization of
integral maps and preserves rational identity and composition. For `O` a
domain and `F` its fraction field, `QuasiIsogeny.reduction` reduces both
global rational maps and preserves their two inverse equations. It
preserves identity, inverse, composition and rationalization of integral
isomorphisms; its projection laws expose the two reduced rational maps.
All these laws specialize the existing supplied-target extension laws.

The construction concerns the specified actual atlas and its quotient
extension. It does not assert a comparison of reduction with changing the
Nilp base, invariance under arbitrary refined atlas comparisons,
full-order equivariance, local rational gluing, height, framing, rigidity
or representability.
