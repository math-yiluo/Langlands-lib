# Prime-power reduction of schematic Tate points

The transfer between two finite levels followed by inclusion is multiplication by the prime raised to the difference of their indices. The inclusion is injective on affine points, so the reverse composite has the same scalar formula. The actual short exact sequence at level zero makes its point group trivial. These facts show that level `n` is killed by `p ^ n`.

If the projection of a Tate section to level `b` vanishes, its component at `b + n` belongs to the image of the level `n` inclusion by the actual short exact sequence. The resulting level `n` points are compatible. The shift square follows by postcomposing with the injective inclusion and applying the scalar formula. They define a Tate section whose `p ^ b` multiple is the original section.

The projection kernel on an affine test is therefore the subgroup of multiples of the corresponding prime power. On an algebraically closed field test the projection is also surjective. Thus the quotient of schematic Tate sections modulo `p ^ n` is canonically the actual point group at level `n`.

The argument uses the scalar torsion system of Rapoport and Zink, *Period Spaces for p-divisible Groups* (1996), §2, printed pp. 50–51 (PDF pp. 65–66). The section group is evaluated on schemes. For Harris's use of finite Tate levels on the rigid generic fibre, see his 1997 Appendix, printed p. 115 (PDF p. 41).
