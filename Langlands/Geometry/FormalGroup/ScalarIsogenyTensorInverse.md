# Scalar-denominator isogenies in the tensor Hom and the rational Hom sheaf

A `RationalHom.ScalarIsogenyPresentation π z` supplies an integral isogeny `f` and an exponent `n` such that the image of `f` in `F ⊗[O] Hom(M,N)` is `π^n z`. Assume the image of `π` in the fraction field `F` is nonzero and both formal modules are `π`-divisible. This module constructs an actual invertible tensor with forward map `z` and gives its inverse its own scalar-denominator presentation.

The integral dual theorem supplies an isogeny `g` at exponent `n + k`, where `k` is its eventual threshold. Each composite of `f` and `g` in the appropriate order is the integral scalar endomorphism by `π^(n+k)`. The rationalization of that endomorphism is `π^(n+k)` times the tensor identity. Thus `π^(-k)` times the rationalization of `g` is the two-sided tensor inverse of `z`. The same `g` and `k` give a presentation of the inverse.

On the whole affine spectrum, the local quasi-isogeny of the original presentation lifts to a pair of invertible sections. This pair agrees with the pair obtained by sheafifying the tensor quasi-isogeny. Their forward sections are both the image of `z`, which determines the inverse section. In particular the local quasi-isogeny of the inverse presentation equals the inverse of the original local quasi-isogeny.

The comparison is derived from the integral dual and local inverse theorems in the Library. It applies to scalar-divisible formal module laws with finite coordinate types and an explicit global scalar-isogeny presentation. Rapoport and Zink, *Period Spaces for p-divisible Groups* (1996), Definition 2.8, printed p. 52, uses the local rational homomorphism formulation for p-divisible groups.
