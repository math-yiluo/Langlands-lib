# Relative Frobenius on the primal rigidifier

Harris, *Supercuspidal representations in the cohomology of Drinfel'd upper half spaces* (1997), Appendix, printed p. 114 (PDF p. 40), gives the Weil operation (A.4) by inverse precomposition with relative Frobenius. Rapoport–Zink, *Period Spaces for p-divisible Groups* (1996), Lemma 6.44 and its proof, printed pp. 309–310 (PDF pp. 321–322), apply this formula to the primal rigidifier of the split pair.

Choose presentations of the division images of the fixed frame and the reduced affine target. `primalFrobeniusSectionIso` restricts the actual invertible Frobenius section of the frame. Its forward section comes from relative Frobenius of the primal frame itself, by naturality of the coordinate power map. The input image presentations are pulled back and transported through the actual coefficient square to give the image presentations of the twisted point.

`splitSpecialWeilFrobeniusPoint_primalFraming_hom` computes the primal rigidifier of that point. The ambient coordinate comparisons cancel against the transported inclusions and projections. Restriction then commutes with coefficient pullback and with composition because inverse Frobenius intertwines the projectors. The resulting section is the pullback of the old primal rigidifier precomposed with inverse relative Frobenius on the primal frame.

If the primal frame has positive scalar height and satisfies the relative Frobenius rank proposition, its inverse Frobenius has raw \(p\)-height \(-m\dim\Phi_{\mathrm{primal}}\). The height theorem adds that contribution to an existing height function of the primal rigidifier and pulls back the sum. This is the rank change on the chosen formal image presentations.

When \(m\) is the residue degree and the primal dimension is the division degree, their product is the denominator for normalization. Rapoport–Zink, (3.4) and Lemma 3.53, printed p. 105 (PDF p. 119), give the negative sign for the corresponding right-action coordinate. `splitSpecialWeilFrobeniusPoint_hasPrimalComponentHeight` expresses the same rank change as an increment of one in that coordinate. The final theorem starts with a specified first split valuation coordinate realizing this negative normalized height. The first component of right multiplication by the element in Harris (A.3) adds one because the first unit is a uniformizer of order one. The new coordinate therefore gives the updated primal height.
