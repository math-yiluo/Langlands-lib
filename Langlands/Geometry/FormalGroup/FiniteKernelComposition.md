# Finite projective kernels of composites of formal-module maps

The theorem `Langlands.FormalGroup.ModuleLaw.Hom.finiteKernelCompositionStatement`
proves the published proposition
`Langlands.FormalGroup.ModuleLaw.Hom.FiniteKernelCompositionStatement M N P`.
It holds for every commutative coefficient ring `O`, commutative `O`-algebra `R`
and formal `O`-modules `M`, `N`, `P` with finite coordinate types `σ`, `τ`, `υ`,
with no hypotheses beyond the statement's own.

Take maps `f : M → N` and `g : N → P` with `f` affine fppf locally surjective,
and let `K`, `L` be finite projective models of the kernels of `f` and `g`.
The proof builds a `FiniteKernelModel` `H` of the kernel of `g ∘ f` and shows
`H.rank p = K.rank p * L.rank p` at every prime `p` of `R`.

The statement's Markdown anticipates a torsor-and-descent route. The
implemented proof takes a different route: it writes down an explicit finitely
presented coordinate algebra for the composite kernel. It then checks finiteness,
flatness and ranks after a faithfully flat base change, so it never descends
algebras or points. The commutative-algebra input is in the helper module
`Langlands.Geometry.FormalGroup.FiniteFlatRankDescent`; see its Markdown.
All other declarations listed below are in the namespace
`Langlands.FormalGroup.ModuleLaw.FiniteKernelComposition`.

## 1. Coordinates and a uniform nilpotence bound

- `coord M x i` is the `i`-th coordinate of an algebra point `x`. The lemmas
  `coord_algebraMapPoints`, `coord_zero` and `ext_coord` record how coordinates
  behave under maps, at zero, and for equality of points.
- `coord_add_mem` shows that each coordinate of `x + y` lies in the ideal
  generated by the coordinates of `x` and `y`. The proof maps to the quotient by
  that ideal, where both points become `0`.
- `pow_eq_zero_of_mem_span_range` and `coord_add_pow_eq_zero` turn this into an
  explicit exponent: if all coordinates of `x` (resp. `y`) have vanishing
  `(a+1)`-st (resp. `(b+1)`-st) powers, then those of `x + y` have vanishing
  `(2·#σ·(a+b)+1)`-st powers. This uses `Ideal.sum_pow_mem_span_pow`.
- `exists_kernel_bound`: if a kernel is corepresented by an algebra `X`, then
  all its points have coordinates killed by one fixed power. Every kernel point
  is the image of the universal point, whose finitely many coordinates are
  nilpotent.
- `exists_comp_kernel_bound`: the kernel of `g ∘ f` also has such a uniform
  bound `n`. Let `x` be a point of the composite kernel over `A`. Then
  `f(x)` is classified by a map `L → A`. The fppf lift `x0` of the universal
  point of `L`, over a faithfully flat `L`-algebra `B`, gives a pushout
  `A → B ⊗[L] A`, which is injective by faithful flatness
  (`exists_pushout_injective`). After pushout, `x` differs from the image of
  `x0` by a point of the kernel of `f`. Combining the bound for `K` with the
  bound for the coordinates of `x0` bounds the coordinates of `x` after the
  injective pushout, and hence before it.

## 2. An explicit finitely presented model

Let `h = g ∘ f` and let `n` be the uniform bound for its kernel.

- `truncAlg R σ n` is the quotient of `MvPolynomial σ R` by the ideal generated
  by the `X i ^ n`. `truncPoint M n` is the tautological point with
  coordinates `X i`.
- `kerAlg h n` is the quotient of `truncAlg R σ n` by the ideal generated by
  the coordinates of `h(truncPoint M n)`. `kerPoint h n` is the image of the
  tautological point, and `kerPoint_mem` shows that it lies in the kernel.
  Both algebras are finitely presented over `R`.
- `kerLift` sends a kernel point `x` over `A` to the algebra map
  `kerAlg h n → A` evaluating the variables at the coordinates of `x`. The
  uniform bound guarantees that the `n`-th powers of these coordinates vanish.
  `kerLift_point` says that this map carries `kerPoint` to `x`, and
  `kerAlg_hom_ext` says that maps out of `kerAlg h n` are determined by the
  image of `kerPoint`.
- `kerRepresents hn` assembles these into a corepresentation of
  `h.kernelSetFunctor` by `kerAlg h n`, natural in the test algebra.

## 3. Comparison after a faithfully flat base change

- `classify E x hx` is the algebra map out of a corepresenting algebra that
  classifies a kernel point. By `classify_point` it moves the universal point to
  `x`, and by `algHom_ext_of_universalPoint` maps out of a corepresenting
  algebra are determined by where they send the universal point.
- `tensorObj`, `tensorInl` and `tensorInr` are the tensor product over an
  intermediate algebra and its two inclusions, as `R`-algebra maps.
- `nonempty_tensor_equiv` proves the key comparison. Let `C` corepresent the
  composite kernel, viewed as an `L`-algebra via the map classifying `f`
  applied to its universal point. Let `B` be faithfully flat over `L`, carrying
  a lift `x0` of the universal point of `L`. Then there is a `B`-linear
  equivalence

  `B ⊗[L] C ≃ B ⊗[R] K`.

  The proof defines two points. The first is the composite-kernel point
  `(universal point of K) + x0` over `B ⊗[R] K`, classified by
  `v : C → B ⊗[R] K`. The second is the `f`-kernel point
  `(universal point of C) - x0` over `B ⊗[L] C`, classified by
  `k : K → B ⊗[L] C`. The map `v` is `L`-linear, which is checked on the
  universal point of `L`. The induced maps `αR : B ⊗[R] K → B ⊗[L] C` and
  `βR : B ⊗[L] C → B ⊗[R] K` are mutually inverse. Their composites fix both
  tensor factors, which is checked on universal points using the uniqueness
  statements above. `βR` is also `B`-linear.

## 4. Conclusion

In `finiteKernelCompositionStatement`, let `H` have coordinate algebra
`kerAlg (g.comp f) n` and corepresentation `kerRepresents`. Make it an
`L`-algebra through the map classifying `f` applied to the universal point, and
apply `nonempty_tensor_equiv` with the fppf lift supplied by the hypothesis on
`f`. Then `FiniteFlatRank.descent_of_tensor_equiv` shows that `kerAlg` is
finite and flat over `L`, with rank at every prime `q` of `L` equal to the rank
of `K` at the contracted prime of `R`. Transitivity gives that `kerAlg` is
finite and flat over `R`. Being finitely presented as an algebra and finite as a
module, it is a finitely presented module, hence projective
(`Module.Flat.projective_of_finitePresentation`). Finally
`FiniteFlatRank.rankAtStalk_eq_mul_of_tower` gives
`H.rank p = K.rank p * L.rank p` for every prime `p` of `R`.

The theorem uses only the standard axioms `propext`, `Classical.choice` and
`Quot.sound`.
