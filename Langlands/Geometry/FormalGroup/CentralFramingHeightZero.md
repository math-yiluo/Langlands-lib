# Zero height under change to the central scalar law

Suppose an order-linear local quasi-isogeny has a prime-normalized rank presentation of height zero. The ranks of the numerator kernel and of the denominator scalar kernel are then equal. Given the actual uniformizer height of the central source and an identity \(p=u\varpi^e\), the old denominator kernel is naturally equivalent to the central \(\varpi^{en}\)-kernel, where \(n\) was the original prime-power exponent. This uses the same natural-number scalar endomorphism and then the automorphism defined by \(u^n\).

Change the integral numerator to the central law and multiply by \(u^{-n}\). The actual finite kernel rank is preserved. This corrected numerator presents the centralized section with denominator \(\varpi^{en}\), and the two actual ranks give height zero in the central normalization. Applying the construction on each witnessing open proves preservation of local zero height for the central section glued from its genuine numerator presentations.

This is the rank comparison needed for the zero-height primal division-module quasi-isogeny in Rapoport–Zink, (6.17) and Lemma 6.44, printed pp. 309–310.
