# Cyclic expansion in the local division order

A nonzero coefficient from the unramified subfield has valuation equal
to a power of the base uniformizer value with exponent divisible by the
reduced degree. This follows from finite étaleness on integer rings and
the expression of an element of the fraction field as a unit times an
integer power of the uniformizer. The power relation for the division
uniformizer gives it the same reduced-norm value as the base
uniformizer's valuation.

The monomials in a cyclic expansion have exponents in distinct
congruence classes modulo the reduced degree. The largest nonzero
monomial value therefore equals the sum's value. This proves injectivity
of the expansion, and the equality of field dimensions proves
surjectivity.

When the sum lies in the actual division order, every monomial has
value at most one. An exponent of the form \(dn+i\), with \(0\le i<d\),
can be nonnegative only if \(n\ge 0\). Thus the field coefficients of an
integral element are themselves integral. The cyclic expansion restricts
to a linear equivalence with the actual division order over the base
integer ring. In particular, a ring homomorphism out of the order is
determined by its values on the coefficient ring and the uniformizer.

The expansion derives the integral generation used in Drinfel'd's cyclic
presentation from the given unramified subfield and power relation.
[V. G. Drinfel'd, *Coverings of p-adic symmetric regions*, 1976, §2B, p. 110](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01077936)
