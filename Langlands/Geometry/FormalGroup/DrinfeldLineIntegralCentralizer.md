# The integral commutant of the reduced Lubin–Tate line

The scalar digit step recursively gives partial scalars and commuting remainders. The difference of successive partial scalars is divisible by the corresponding power of the original uniformizer. Adic completeness of the original integer ring gives their common limit. On any nilpotent point, a scalar power kills the point, so the limit and the given endomorphism agree by the corresponding finite congruence. Extensionality on all coefficient algebras then gives equality of the actual homomorphisms.

The resulting ring equivalence sends a scalar to its scalar endomorphism. Injectivity follows by writing a nonzero scalar as a unit times a uniformizer power, cancelling the fppf locally surjective uniformizer power, and taking the derivative of the identity. This is a step in the endomorphism calculation for the special frame in Drinfeld, *Coverings of p-adic Symmetric Regions* (1976), §2 A–B, printed pp. 109–110.
