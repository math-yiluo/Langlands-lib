# Coefficient twist of the polarized affine class functor

Let \(W\) be a \(\mathbf Z_p\)-algebra, let \(\pi\) be its distinguished
element, and let \(\sigma\) be a \(\mathbf Z_p\)-algebra automorphism of
\(W\) fixing \(\pi\). Write \(\bar\sigma\) for the induced automorphism of
\(W/(\pi)\). For a polarized frame \(Y\) over \(W/(\pi)\) and an affine
test with structural algebra map \(j\colon W\to T\), `twistFrameNatIso`
identifies the polarized affine functor for \(\bar\sigma^*Y\) on \((T,j)\)
with the functor for \(Y\) on \((T,j\circ\sigma)\).

The forward coefficient square uses the identity on the underlying test
ring and the identity on representatives of its reduction. The equation
for the residue coefficient maps gives an integral polarized comparison
of the two framing sources with identity coordinate series. The inverse
square uses the inverse algebra equivalences and the reversed residue
coefficient equation. Its source comparison also has identity coordinates.
The two composites are the squares of the identity test maps. Along each
test morphism the two routes have equal coefficient maps and equal forward
coordinate series of the framing isometries. The composition rule on
isomorphism classes then gives naturality.

This is the coefficient comparison used in Michael Harris,
*Supercuspidal representations in the cohomology of Drinfel'd upper half
spaces* (1997), Appendix (A.4), printed p. 114 (PDF p. 40). Under the
canonical identifications of the two test rings and the two reductions
it retains the polarized target and expresses the rigidifying section
using the extended frame. In Harris's formula the inverse of the relative
Frobenius first changes the source of that section from \(Y\) to
\(\bar\sigma^*Y\).
