# The cyclic characters of the unramified integer subfield

Drinfeld's grading uses the unramified integer subfield of degree \(d\) and its
arithmetic Frobenius. The integral rank of this subfield over the base integer
ring is its fraction-field degree. Formal unramifiedness identifies its
maximal ideal with the extension of the base maximal ideal. Its actual residue
field is consequently the base change of the finite free integer subfield to
the base residue field, and also has degree \(d\).

The valuation inequality in the supplied presentation puts the difference
between the restricted automorphism and the residue-cardinality power in the
maximal ideal. The actual residue maps of those two elements agree. Arithmetic
Frobenius on this finite residue extension has order \(d\).

An embedding of the subfield residue field into the framing coefficient field
gives the degree-zero character. Precomposition with the first \(d\) powers of
inverse residue Frobenius gives distinct characters of the integer subfield.
At cyclic successor, precomposition with inverse integral Frobenius gives the
same character, including the step from degree \(d-1\) to degree zero.

For distinct degrees, choose an integer subfield element on which the two
characters differ. Their difference is a unit in the framing coefficient
field, so its image in the test algebra modulo \(\pi\) is a unit. The difference
between the lifted values is a unit in the test algebra because the reduction
ideal is nilpotent.

Drinfeld, *Coverings of p-adic symmetric regions* (1976), §2B, printed p. 110
(PDF p. 4), gives the unramified subfield and Frobenius grading.
