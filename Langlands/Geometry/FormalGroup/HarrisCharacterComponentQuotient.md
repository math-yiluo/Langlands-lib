# The actual character quotient and its two valuation coordinates

Harris, *Supercuspidal representations in the cohomology of Drinfel'd's symmetric space* (1997), Appendix (A.2), printed p. 114 (PDF p. 40), describes the component factor as the split similitude group modulo the common absolute-value kernel of its rational characters. `CharacterComponentGroup` is this quotient. Its subgroup is normal because each defining condition is the inverse image of a subgroup of the abelian scalar units.

The regular determinant character on a split pair is the scalar-field algebra norm of the inverse first unit viewed in the original algebra. Its evaluation is the determinant of right multiplication by that inverse in the opposite algebra. The tensor-unit isomorphism followed by the opposite-algebra comparison conjugates the operator to left multiplication in the original algebra, which preserves the determinant. The multiplier character is the scalar coordinate.

For a pair in the common character kernel, the regular determinant evaluated on the pair and its inverse gives determinant norm of absolute value one for the first unit and its inverse. The first unit therefore belongs to the scalar-field maximal compact subgroup of the division algebra. The module topology over `F` is also the module topology over `k`, by finite dimensionality and continuity of the scalar inclusion. Thus the scalar-field maximal compact is a compact subgroup for the `F`-module topology and lies in the maximal compact defined over `F`. The multiplier character gives the integral-unit condition on the scalar coordinate. Together with compactness of the integral split subgroup, this proves equality of the two kernels.

The resulting quotient equivalence preserves representatives. Using the canonical reduced-norm order over `F` and the scalar order from `Langlands.FML.normalizedMultiplicativeValuation k`, units of order one identify the quotient with two integer coordinates. Right translation by the component of (A.3), printed p. 114, adds `(1,f)`. Right translation by the determinant correction of (A.6), printed p. 115 (PDF p. 41), adds `(-ord det,0)`. These are the algebraic component translations appearing in the formal moduli formulas.

The local comparison assumes the topology of the local fields, a continuous inclusion `k → F`, the compatible scalar tower, and the `F`-module topology on the division algebra. The integer coordinates use the central division algebra over `F` with a specified finite basis of its opposite over `k` and the two order-one units.
