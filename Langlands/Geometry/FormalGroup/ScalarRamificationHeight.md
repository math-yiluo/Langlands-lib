# Scalar ramification and height normalization

Drinfeld 1976, §1, printed p. 108 (PDF p. 2), specifies the scalar action on the tangent space, the residue cardinality, and a uniformizer. Section 2 A, printed p. 109 (PDF p. 3), imposes height `d²` on the special division modules in the moduli problem. The rank convention `q^(n*h)` here is `ModuleLaw.HasPiHeight` from `PiDivisible.lean`. Tate 1967, Definition 2.1, printed pp. 160–161 (PDF p. 2), uses orders `p^(n*h)` for the levels of a p-divisible group. Harris 1997, Appendix, printed p. 112 (PDF p. 38), uses logarithms base `p` of kernel orders for quasi-isogeny heights. Rapoport–Zink 1996, printed p. 109 (PDF p. 123), immediately before Definition 3.57 gives the `[F : ℚ_p]` factor between the two ranks of the crystals. The comparison here derives the scalar-kernel rank formulas from the actual finite group schemes.

Multiplication by a scalar unit is a formal automorphism. Composing with it identifies the actual kernels before and after multiplying a scalar by a unit. If `a = u * ϖ^e` and the uniformizer height is `h` with residue cardinality `p^f`, the rank of the kernel of `a^n` is `p^(n*e*f*h)`. For the converse, take the scalar kernel degree of `ϖ^n` at each residue field. Its `e`-th power is the degree of `a^n` by multiplicativity. When `e` is positive, injectivity of the `e`-th power on natural numbers gives the stated rank, which is detected on the original finite projective kernel.

The forward height theorem takes scalar divisibility for `a`. In mixed characteristic `a` is the image of `p`, with `e` the ramification index and `f` the residue degree. The converse uses `p`-height `e*f*h` and scalar divisibility for the uniformizer. A separate lemma constructs uniformizer divisibility from divisibility by `a` and finite locally free uniformizer-power kernels. The required nilpotence and torsion follow by cancelling the scalar units, and the local lifting comes from factoring `a` through the uniformizer.
