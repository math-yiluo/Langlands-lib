# Scalar-power reductions of compatible torsion points

In the affine module of compatible scalar torsion points, multiplication by any power `π^n` is injective. If two multiplied families agree, the transition from index `n+i` to `i` shows that their original points at index `i` agree.

If the point of a family at index `n` is zero, its point at `n+i` is killed by `π^i`. Taking these points in the smaller kernels gives a compatible family whose `π^n` multiple is the original one. Consequently the kernel of evaluation at `n` is exactly the submodule of `π^n` multiples.

The first isomorphism theorem identifies reduction modulo `π^n` with the image of evaluation in the actual `π^n`-torsion. On a representative the equivalence is evaluation at `n`. Test-algebra maps preserve scalar multiples and descend to the quotient. Under the image comparison their effect is the actual map on torsion points.

The calculation uses only the actual inverse system on affine coefficient tests. Harris, *Supercuspidal representations in the cohomology of Drinfel'd's symmetric space* (1997), Appendix, printed p. 115 (PDF p. 41), uses reductions of the Tate module on the rigid generic fiber. An identification with the entire torsion kernel at a test point requires surjectivity of evaluation there.
