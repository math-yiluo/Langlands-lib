# Height of the polarized framing section

Harris, *Supercuspidal representations in the cohomology of
Drinfel'd upper half spaces* (1997), Appendix, printed pp. 112–113
(PDF pp. 38–39), uses the logarithmic rank difference for the framing
quasi-isogeny and normalizes it by the division degree times the
inertia degree. The normalized value has Harris's sign. See also
Rapoport–Zink, *Period Spaces for p-divisible Groups* (1996),
Lemma 3.53, printed p. 105 (PDF p. 119), for the height divisibility
in the isocrystal setting.

On an affine framed triple the raw relation uses \(p\) both as
denominator scalar and as the base of the logarithmic ranks. A framed
isomorphism postcomposes the framing with the reduction of an actual
integral isomorphism. On each witnessing open the numerator can be
postcomposed with that integral isomorphism. Its kernel rank is
preserved by the corresponding isomorphism of arrows and the
denominator stays the same. Thus the verified height functions
descend to the isomorphism class.

`normalizedHeightFunctions` takes the positive product \(df\) as
normalization and requires \(df\,h\) to be a verified raw height
function. Local constancy of \(h\) follows by canceling \(df\) on the
open neighborhoods where the raw height is constant. This is the
height relation on the affine classes. Totality and uniqueness of
the height map and divisibility for the split special moduli remain
separate assertions.
