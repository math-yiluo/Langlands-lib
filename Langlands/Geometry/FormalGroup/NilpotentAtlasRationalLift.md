# Lifting rational atlas maps across nilpotent reduction

Drinfeld's *Coverings of p-adic Symmetric Regions* (1976), §1 and §2A, printed p. 109 (PDF p. 3), allows formal modules glued from localizations and uses the rational tensor Hom for their framing. This module derives a lifting theorem for two atlases on a common finite principal cover whose generators have distinct images under a surjective coefficient map with nilpotent kernel. The source charts are \(\pi\)-divisible and the target charts have \(\pi\)-power-torsion points. Every atlas admits a finite principal subcover with distinct images. The original generators selected over the image cover still generate the unit ideal, and an expression for \(1\) uses only finitely many of them.

For a test algebra \(A\), the reduction test is \(A/IA\), where \(I\) is the kernel of the base coefficient map. Surjectivity gives its scalar structure over the smaller coefficient ring and the universal factorization of coefficient maps to algebras over that ring. The reduction square is natural in the test. When the original generators have distinct images, the inverse image of each image generator selected by the atlas construction is the original generator. The actual reduced chart map therefore has the same series in the original coordinates on a reduced test. Its naturality and transition equations are transported by these coordinate comparisons.

To lift an integral map on the reduced atlas, use target \(\pi\)-power torsion to lift a scalar multiple on the canonical localization of each original generator. Take the maximum of their lifting exponents and multiply the local lifts by the extra powers needed to reach that exponent. On any test, extend the lifted series from the canonical localization and use the coefficient triangle to transport the chart laws. Naturality of reduction identifies the reduction of each such extension with the chosen multiple of the given reduced map. The source divisibility and target torsion hypotheses give integral rigidity on each overlap, so the lifts satisfy the transition equations. Factoring the identity map on tests over the smaller coefficient ring verifies that the assembled atlas map reduces to the desired integral multiple.

With an \(O\)-algebra field \(F\) in which the image of \(\pi\) is nonzero, divide each tensor coefficient by the corresponding scalar power to lift it. Flatness of \(F\) over \(O\) transports integral injectivity to the tensor Hom and gives the linear equivalence under the same chart hypotheses. The actual atlas endomorphisms commute with coefficient extension, so injectivity also reflects commutation with their rationalizations and hence all the order equations.

For the full-order quasi-isogeny bijection, \(O\) is a domain, \(F\) is its fraction field, \(\pi\) is nonzero, and both atlases have \(\pi\)-divisible charts. Divisibility supplies the needed torsion in both directions. Lift the two rational maps and use injectivity on each endomorphism space to reflect the inverse equations.
