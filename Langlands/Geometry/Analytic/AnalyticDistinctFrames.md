# Distinct frames in a compact analytic fibre power

The distinct locus of an ordered analytic fibre power consists of points whose pairs of different coordinates avoid the diagonal in the actual self-pullback. For a compact quasi-étale map the diagonal is a closed analytic domain. Closedness follows because its source is compact and the self-pullback is Hausdorff. The inverse images of the diagonal by the finitely many pair maps form a closed analytic-domain union. Its complement is a distinguished open of the original compact fibre power.

At a full point valued in a complete algebraically closed field, a pair of coordinates lands in the ordinary diagonal image precisely when the two full coordinate points agree. If the pair lands in that image, the full point lifts to the diagonal domain. Its projections then agree. Conversely, equal coordinates identify the pair with the full point of the diagonal by preservation of the quasi-étale pullback. Thus distinctness is equivalent to injectivity of the full coordinate tuple. The permutation action preserves the locus because it permutes the pair maps.

This is a derived construction for the frame proof of finite Galois refinement. It uses the analytic diagonal and finite étale germs of Berkovich, *Étale cohomology for non-Archimedean analytic spaces* (1993), Proposition 3.3.7 and Theorem 3.4.1, printed pp. 63 and 65.
