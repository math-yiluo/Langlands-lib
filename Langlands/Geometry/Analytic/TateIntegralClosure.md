# Integral closedness and minimal polynomials over the actual Tate algebra

Author: Worker 56.

The preceding unique-factorization theorem supplies integral closedness
of the actual Tate algebra through Mathlib's rational-root theorem.
For an actual finite algebra over this ring, finite generation proves
that each element is integral. The standard constructed minimal
polynomial is consequently monic and annihilates that element.
The statement `minpoly_monic_aeval` uses a commutative algebra `B` with
`Module.Finite`; it does not require that `B` be a domain or that its
scalar action be torsion-free. Its coefficient field need not be complete,
as recorded by the explicit `omit [CompleteSpace K]` in Lean.

For the next two results the coefficient field is complete, and `B` is
a domain with `Module.IsTorsionFree` over the Tate algebra. Mathlib's
integrally closed minimal-polynomial results identify the evaluation
kernel with the ideal generated by this polynomial (`minpoly_eval_ker`).
After constructing
the fraction-field scalar map by the localization universal property, the
same polynomial maps to the minimal polynomial over the fraction field.
This is `minpoly_fractionRing_eq`, using `FractionRing.liftAlgebra`.
Thus its coefficients are elements of the actual Tate algebra, rather
than assumed coefficients chosen from an ambient fraction field.

The finite algebra and torsion-free scalar action are hypotheses of these
reusable consequences; they are supplied in the intended application by
the previously constructed finite injective normalization map into a
domain. These results establish the algebraic minimal-polynomial step.
They do not prove bounds on its coefficients from spectral inequalities.
That analytic comparison remains necessary for the general power-bound
implication.

The integral-closure and fraction-field comparison steps correspond to
the domain case in the proof of Proposition 2.6.1, page 11, used in
Proposition 2.6.4, pages 11-12 of the retained Berkovich-course notes.
Integral closedness follows from the factoriality in Corollary 2.3.3,
page 6. The evaluation-kernel identity is an additional reusable
algebraic consequence supplied by Mathlib's `minpoly.ker_eval`, not a
separate statement in that source passage.
