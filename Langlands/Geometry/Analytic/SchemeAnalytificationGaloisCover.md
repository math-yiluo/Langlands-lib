# Analytification of algebraic étale Galois covers

`Langlands.Analytic.SchemeAnalytification.analytificationGaloisCover` starts with a surjective étale map \(p:Y\to X\) between separated schemes locally of finite type over a complete nontrivially normed ultrametric field \(K\). A group \(\Gamma\) acts by scheme automorphisms over \(X\). For each pair of field-valued points of \(Y\) with the same composite in \(X\), the construction requires a unique element of \(\Gamma\) carrying the first point to the second. It constructs `AnalyticGaloisCover Γ` for the analytification of \(p\), using the images of the specified automorphisms under the analytification functor. The field-valued tests have the scheme universe, and the deck group has an independent universe.

Each algebraic deck graph in \(Y\times_XY\) is the étale diagonal followed by the automorphism of the pullback induced by acting on its second factor. It is therefore an open immersion. A point of the pullback lies in the image of a graph exactly when its residue-field test factors through that graph. The two projections of the test select a unique graph by the field-valued transport hypothesis.

`localizedAnalytification_map_isPullback` supplies the analytic self-pullback with the actual analytified projections. The two projections identify the analytified graph with the graph of the induced analytic automorphism. `range_analytification` expresses every graph image as the inverse image of its algebraic image under the center map. These inverse images give the covering and disjointness fields of `AnalyticGaloisCover`. The same range formula proves surjectivity of the analytified cover, and the small-index affine comparison gives its analytic étaleness.

The torsor interpretation is the definition of étale Galois coverings following Proposition 4.1.8 in Berkovich, *Étale cohomology for non-Archimedean analytic spaces* (1993), §4.1, p. 85. The analytic pullback gluing uses Proposition 1.4.1, pp. 29–30, of the same work. The étaleness and surjectivity assertions specialize Berkovich, *Spectral theory and analytic geometry over non-Archimedean fields* (1990), §3.4, Proposition 3.4.6(3) and (7), pp. 65–66. The Lean construction derives the graph partition using algebraic residue-field tests and the center image formula.
