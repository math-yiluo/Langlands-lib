# Finite sites of spectral-invariant positive-radius rational charts

Authors: Worker 56 and Worker 36. Internal derivation adapting their
published `RationalNormInvariantSite` construction to the actual
represented-domain spectral actions and their invariant rational diagrams.
The earlier construction in turn adapts Worker 36's strict
`InvariantRationalSite`.

The inputs are a complete ultrametric normed field `K`, an actual
`PresentedAffinoidAlgebra K`, an arbitrary topological group `G`, a
`DomainSpectralAction G A` named `ρ`, and an open subgroup `H`. The
imported full category `SpectralInvariantChart ρ H` consists of the actual
positive-radius rational charts `d` satisfying
`H ≤ ρ.locusOpenSubgroup d.toDomain`. Its arrows are all the original
bounded rational restrictions over `A`. The spectral-continuity input is
continuity of the action homomorphism for the represented-domain
spectral automorphism topology. No continuity of the individual algebra
orbits for the quotient norm is assumed.

`mem_spectralLocusOpenSubgroup_inter` proves that any ambient group
element preserving two actual loci also preserves their constructed
weighted rational intersection. The proof exposes the chart loci in
the represented-domain stabilizer conditions, uses the original
intersection-locus identity, and uses injectivity of the ambient
spectrum operator to commute its image with intersection.

In the `SpectralInvariantChart` namespace, `whole` retains the original
empty-numerator whole-chart quotient. Surjectivity of the ambient
spectrum operator proves its invariance, and `locus_whole` identifies
its locus with the entire ambient spectrum. `toWhole` retains the
original bounded restriction from the whole-chart quotient.
`wholeIsTerminal` uses uniqueness of bounded restrictions to prove
terminality in the full invariant chart category.

`inter` retains the original product-radius rational intersection
quotient, with invariance supplied by the stabilizer lemma.
`inter_obj` and `locus_inter` identify its original chart and the
intersection of the two loci. `interLeft` and `interRight` retain
the original bounded restrictions. `interLift` applies the published
bounded quotient pushout to any pair of simultaneous restrictions.
`pullbackCone` uses this same intersection over any common target;
uniqueness of bounded restrictions proves its commutative square.
`pullbackConeIsLimit` uses the constructed bounded lift and restriction
uniqueness to prove its universal property. The resulting terminal
object and pullbacks supply `HasFiniteLimits`. These are limits in
the chart category over the fixed presented affinoid algebra.

`IsFiniteCovering` means that a sieve contains a family of invariant
chart restrictions indexed by a finite type in the field universe,
whose actual loci cover the target locus. The index type may be
empty. `isFiniteCovering_top` uses the singleton identity restriction.
`IsFiniteCovering.pullback` intersects every chart in a chosen finite
cover with the source of the given arrow. The intersection remains
invariant by the proved stabilizer lemma, uniqueness of bounded
restrictions identifies the two triangles, and the intersection-locus
identity proves point coverage. `IsFiniteCovering.transitive` uses a
dependent sum of the two finite indexing types for the composite
cover. These constructions prove the axioms of the Grothendieck
topology `SpectralInvariantChart.finiteRationalTopology`.
`mem_finiteRationalTopology` is its defining sieve-membership equivalence.

`ofArrows_mem_finiteRationalTopology_iff` characterizes the covering
sieve generated by a finite family exactly by coverage of the original
target locus. In the forward direction, membership in the generated
sieve factors each chart of a chosen cover through a generating arrow;
that factor's locus inclusion transfers coverage. In the reverse
direction, the generating family itself witnesses finite covering.
`bot_mem_finiteRationalTopology_iff` proves that the empty sieve covers
exactly a chart with empty locus. An empty index type constructs the
cover in that case; conversely, any point in the target would produce
an arrow belonging to the empty sieve.

`familyCovers_iff_jointlySurjective` identifies actual locus coverage
with joint surjectivity of the unchanged maps of
`spectralInvariantSpectrumFunctor ρ H`. This equivalence applies to
any family indexed in the field universe, without a finiteness
hypothesis. It reuses the published ordinary rational-chart theorem,
whose proof uses the original quotient-spectrum/locus homeomorphisms
and their restriction equations over the base. The imported spectral
diagram supplies joint continuity and equivariance of these same
maps. No new action operators or spectrum maps are introduced here.

The geometric conventions are Berkovich, *Non-archimedean analytic
spaces* (Trieste lectures, 2009), Definition 2.2.1 and Exercise
2.2.2(ii)-(iii), printed/PDF p.9, and Fact 2.2.3(i) and Definition
2.2.7, p.10. Registered source:
`berkovich-2009-trieste-analytic-spaces`, PDF SHA256
`8cfe4f6f35d5e717ac67c5c8f5ef9ff9185f460bbe5f9a10aeacd84cddeeb2ca`.
These passages give represented domains, positive-radius rational
quotients, their intersections, spectrum identification and domain
embeddings. The actual algebras, bounded maps, pushout and
homeomorphisms used here are imported from the published weighted
rational-chart modules. The invariant finite limits and finite
covering topology are internal derivations, not numbered assertions
of those passages.

The manuscript does not state this finite rational-site construction.
The imported action topology uses finite represented-affinoid-domain
tests; this contribution asserts no comparison with all compact-domain
tests or the full analytic automorphism topology.

Field and group universes remain independent. Arbitrary positive real
polyradii, trivial valuations, nilpotents, zero quotient algebras, empty
spectra and loci, and empty covers of empty targets remain included.
Rational presentation data retain explicit unit generation, using its
implication of absence of common zeros without asserting the converse.
This constructs a local finite rational site. Analytic-algebra
sheafness, general analytic atlases, full analytic group topology,
analytic etale and quasi-etale morphisms and sites, compact and
distinguished-domain lifting, and the Harris tower remain separate.
