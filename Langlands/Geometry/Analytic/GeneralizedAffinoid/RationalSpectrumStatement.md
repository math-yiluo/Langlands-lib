# Spectrum identification target for weighted rational presentations

Authors: Worker 56 and Worker 36. Worker 56 formulates the weighted target
without coefficient completeness. Its clause structure and proposed proof
route adapt Worker 36's strict `RationalSpectrum/Statement` and
`RationalSpectrum` modules. The generic `Langlands.Analytic.fractionSubalgebra`
is imported from `RationalSpectrum/Definitions`, without redefining it.

`Langlands.Analytic.PresentedAffinoidAlgebra.RationalPresentation.SpectrumIdentificationStatement`
is a proposition definition. This module states the density and spectrum
identification properties; it does not prove them.

Let `K` be an ultrametric normed field and let `A` be a
`PresentedAffinoidAlgebra K`. Thus `A` is the actual quotient of the finite
restricted-series algebra at `r = A.polyradius` by a specified closed ideal,
equipped with its quotient norm. Write `q` for its presentation map. Choose
any positive polyradius `s : PositivePolyradius (Fin m)`, a series `g` in
the old variables and a tuple of old series `f j`. Set `gbar = q g` and
`fbar j = q (f j)`.

Write `R = algebra K A s g f`. This is the constructed restricted-series
algebra at `r.append s`, modulo the closure of the ideal generated by the
included old ideal and the equations `i(g) Y_j - i(f j)`. The map `i`
includes the old variables in the first block. Its contractive base map is
`alpha = baseMap K A s g f : A -> R`. The new quotient coordinates `y_j`
satisfy `alpha(gbar) * y_j = alpha(fbar j)` and `norm y_j <= s j`.
These objects and laws are supplied by the imported weighted rational
presentation construction.

The proposition has the following clauses after the common quantifiers
over `K`, `A`, `m`, `s`, `g` and `f`.

1. The actual subalgebra

   ```
   D = {b in R | there exist k : natural numbers and a in A
                   with alpha(gbar)^k * b = alpha(a)}
   ```

   is dense in `R` for the quotient norm topology. This clause has no
   unit-ideal hypothesis. It asserts density of `D`, with no assertion that
   `alpha` itself has dense range or is injective.
2. If `GeneratesUnitIdeal K A g f` holds, the range of
   `spectrumMap K A s g f` is exactly `locus K A s g f`. Here generation
   means `Ideal.span (insert gbar (Set.range fbar)) = top` in `A`, and the
   locus is

   ```
   {x in BanachSpectrum A | for every j, x(fbar j) <= s j * x(gbar)}.
   ```

   The spectrum map sends a point `z` of `BanachSpectrum R` to the actual
   composite seminorm `z` after `alpha`. Thus every locus point is required
   to have a preimage, in addition to the forward containment already
   established by the imported construction.
3. Under the same generation hypothesis, that spectrum map is a closed
   embedding for the pointwise spectrum topologies. Together with clause 2
   this identifies the quotient spectrum homeomorphically with the locus
   carrying the subspace topology.

Spectrum points are normalized multiplicative seminorms dominated by the
ambient norm. A realizing field, a lift of a point, injectivity and
density are not inputs. The old and new variable counts may be zero;
zero quotients and trivially valued coefficient fields are allowed.
Both polyradii consist of arbitrary strictly positive real numbers, with
no value-group condition. No coefficient completeness assumption occurs.

The proposed proof route uses the actual quotient coordinates. Old
coordinates lie in `D` with exponent zero, and new coordinates lie in `D`
with exponent one by the fraction equations. Its topological closure
therefore contains all projected monomials; the published weighted
monomial expansions and surjectivity of the quotient projection give
density. These expansions hold without coefficient completeness.

For a point `x` of the locus, unit-ideal generation forces `x(gbar)` to be
nonzero. In the constructed completed residue field `H(x)`, the fractions
`fbar j / gbar` then have norms bounded by `s j`. Finite products satisfy
the corresponding weighted monomial bound. A weighted rational extension
into `H(x)` would give a spectrum point above `x`. Completeness is needed
in that target and is supplied by its construction. Equality of two
restricted seminorms forces equality on `D` by cancellation of a nonzero
power of the denominator; continuity and density extend this equality to
all of `R`. Compactness and Hausdorffness of the constructed spectra,
available for normed rings without completeness, would give the closed
embedding. This paragraph describes a proposed derivation, not a proof
in this statement module. In particular, the separately stated weighted
rational extension theorem remains a proof prerequisite.

The original context is Berkovich, *Non-archimedean analytic spaces*
(Trieste lectures, 2009), Exercise 2.2.2(ii), printed/PDF page 9, and
Fact 2.2.3(i),(iv), page 10, read directly at the registered fingerprint.
The exercise uses common-denominator relations `g T_i - f_i` with
arbitrary positive radii; the facts describe the spectrum identification
and density for affinoid domains. The common denominator in the present
inequalities is the same `g` appearing in those relations.

This is a derived formulation. The source works over complete fields and
uses relative restricted series over an affinoid algebra, with absence of
common spectral zeros in the rational-domain hypothesis. Here the
quotient is the specified field-coefficient presentation, and the explicit
algebraic unit-ideal hypothesis is retained for the spectrum clauses.
The subalgebra `D` uses powers of the chosen denominator, whereas the
source's `A(V)` in Fact 2.2.3(iv) inverts all functions nonvanishing on
`V`. Density of this explicit `D`, including without generation, is
motivated by the coordinate argument above rather than asserted to be a
verbatim source theorem.

The statement does not identify the construction with relative series,
prove that absence of common spectral zeros implies unit-ideal generation,
or assert independence of arbitrary base presentations. It supplies no
analytic-domain factorization property, structure sheaf, atlas or etale
site. Those remain separate obligations.
