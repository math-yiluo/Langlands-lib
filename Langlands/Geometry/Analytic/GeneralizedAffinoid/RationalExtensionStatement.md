# Bounded extension target for weighted rational presentations

Authors: Worker 56 and Worker 36. Worker 56 formulates the weighted target,
including coordinate-specified extension without a generation hypothesis and
the absence of coefficient completeness. The denominator and base-extension
clauses adapt Worker 36's strict `RationalPresentation/Statement` interface.

`Langlands.Analytic.PresentedAffinoidAlgebra.RationalPresentation.BoundedExtensionStatement`
is a proposition definition. It specifies five properties of the constructed
weighted rational quotient; this module does not prove the proposition.
Its imports provide the actual quotient, maps, coordinates and locus, and
the actual weighted monomial-bound predicate.

Let `K` be an ultrametric normed field and let `A` be a
`PresentedAffinoidAlgebra K`: a finite positive-polyradius series algebra
modulo a specified closed ideal, with its quotient norm. Write `r` for
`A.polyradius`, `n` for `A.arity`, and `q` for its presentation map. Fix a
finite positive polyradius `s : PositivePolyradius (Fin m)`, an old-series
representative `g`, and representatives `f j` for `j : Fin m`. Put
`gbar = q g` and `fbar j = q (f j)`.

The imported `RationalPresentation.algebra K A s g f`, denoted here by `R`,
is the actual series algebra at `r.append s` modulo the closure of the ideal
generated by the included old ideal and the equations `i(g) Y_j - i(f_j)`.
Here `i` includes the old variables in the first coordinate block. The map
`alpha = baseMap K A s g f : A -> R` is bounded and contractive, and
`y_j = fraction K A s g f j` is the new quotient coordinate. Its established
laws are `alpha(gbar) * y_j = alpha(fbar j)` and `norm y_j <= s j`.
These constructions do not require unit-ideal generation.

The predicate `GeneratesUnitIdeal K A g f` means precisely
`Ideal.span (insert gbar (Set.range fbar)) = top` in `A`. The locus consists
of spectrum points `x` satisfying `x(fbar j) <= s j * x(gbar)` for every `j`.
The spectrum uses normalized multiplicative seminorms dominated by the
ambient norm. The following five clauses are conjoined after the common
quantifiers over `K`, `A`, `m`, `s`, `g` and `f`.

1. Under `GeneratesUnitIdeal`, `alpha(gbar)` is a unit of `R`.
2. Under `GeneratesUnitIdeal`, every point `x` of the locus satisfies
   `x(gbar) != 0`.
3. Under `GeneratesUnitIdeal`, any two bounded `K`-algebra maps from `R`
   to a normed commutative `K`-algebra `B` are equal if their composites
   with `alpha` are equal. This clause does not require `B` to be complete
   or ultrametric.
4. Let `B` be a complete ultrametric normed commutative `K`-algebra,
   `phi : A -> B` a bounded algebra map, and `b : Fin m -> B` a tuple with
   `HasBoundedMonomials s B b`. If
   `phi(gbar) * b j = phi(fbar j)` for every `j`, there exists exactly one
   bounded algebra map `psi : R -> B` satisfying both
   `psi.comp alpha = phi` and `psi(y_j) = b j` for every `j`.
   This clause has no unit-ideal-generation hypothesis; uniqueness is
   among maps satisfying both the base restriction and coordinate values.
5. Under `GeneratesUnitIdeal`, the same target assumptions, bounded tuple
   and equations give exactly one bounded algebra map `psi : R -> B`
   satisfying `psi.comp alpha = phi`. Here uniqueness is among all maps
   with that base restriction. The tuple is part of the existence
   hypotheses, while coordinate values are not part of this clause's
   conclusion.

The monomial-bound hypothesis means that there exists a real `C > 0` such
that for every finitely supported exponent `d : Fin m -> natural numbers`,

```
norm (product_j (b j)^(d j)) <= C * product_j (s j)^(d j).
```

These are actual finite products; the empty monomial is included and
therefore bounds the norm of the target unit. The predicate does not assume
an extension map, convergence theorem or spectral-radius comparison.
The proposition asks for a bounded extension but specifies no quantitative
operator bound for it.

The coefficient and target universes are independent. No clause assumes
coefficient completeness, a nontrivial valuation, radii in the value group,
nonzero targets or normalized target unit norm. Completeness and
ultrametricity of the target appear only in clauses 4 and 5. Either variable
count may be zero, and either quotient ideal may be the unit ideal.
The denominator-one Weierstrass case is included by taking `g = 1`.

The proposed derivation combines unit-ideal generation with the fraction
equations: in `R`, every generator of the transported unit ideal is a
multiple of `alpha(gbar)`. For a locus point with `x(gbar) = 0`, the
inequalities force every numerator to lie in its proper seminorm kernel,
contradicting generation. For existence, the images under `phi` of the old
coordinates have a weighted monomial bound because `phi` is bounded. Append
them to the prescribed tuple `b`, apply weighted series evaluation, and
descend through the included old ideal and the rational equations.
Coordinate uniqueness yields clause 4; invertibility of the denominator
forces the new coordinates from the base restriction and yields clauses 3
and 5. This describes a proposed proof route. Neither the weighted
relation-quotient universal property nor the desired rational extension is
assumed as an input to this proposition.

The original context is Berkovich, *Non-Archimedean analytic spaces*
(Trieste lectures, 2009): Example 1.2.2(v), printed/PDF page 4, supplies
positive-polyradius restricted series; the quotient-norm paragraph and
Definitions 2.1.1–2.1.2, page 7, supply affinoid presentations; Exercise
2.1.5(i), pages 7–8, gives bounded extension into affinoid targets with
prescribed variables; and Exercise 2.2.2(ii), page 9, gives the rational
equations and rational-domain context. These passages were read directly.

The source uses complete fields, relative affinoid series, a spectral-radius
condition for extension, and absence of common spectral zeros for the
rational-domain theorem. This derived target instead concerns the explicit
field-coefficient presentation, an algebraic unit-ideal hypothesis where
needed, and explicit weighted monomial bounds in general normed targets.
Its scope without coefficient completeness is motivated by direct series
evaluation in the complete target and descent through a closed kernel.
It does not assert equivalence with the source's spectral hypotheses,
comparison with relative series, independence of representatives or
presentations, reverse spectrum inclusion, or analytic-domain
representability. Forward spectrum containment is already proved in the
imported construction and is not repeated as an unproved clause here.
