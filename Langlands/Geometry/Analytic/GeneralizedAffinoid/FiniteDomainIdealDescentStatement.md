# Finite full-domain closed-ideal effectiveness

`IdealEffectivenessStatement` is an unproved proposition definition for a closed-ideal specialization of Kiehl's theorem. It is neither an axiom nor an unfinished proof.

The source is Berkovich, *Étale cohomology for non-Archimedean analytic spaces* (1993), Kiehl's Theorem and Remark 1.2.1(ii), printed p.15 / PDF p.12. The source states effectiveness of compatible finite modules on a finite affinoid covering and identifies the descended module with its section equalizer. It explicitly extends the result to general affinoids and trivial coefficient valuations using Berkovich 1990, §2.1. The use for compatible local quotient algebras in Lemma 1.2.12 is on printed p.20 / PDF p.17. Registered source: `berkovich-1993-etale-cohomology`, SHA256 `06b42aca1c8f237e607d2cf1f15a41fdc9718324a4dc9fd5bce66574254bb91c`.

The proposition is a derived specialization of this finite-module descent result to ideals, with equality on the actual overlaps supplying the descent identifications. It asks for the following conclusion for every complete ultrametric normed coefficient field, every presented positive-polyradius affinoid algebra, every full domain `U`, and every finite full-domain cover of `U`. If closed ideals in the covering algebras have equal closed extensions along the two actual intersection restrictions, their explicit contraction to `U.algebra` has closed extension equal to the given ideal on every member of the cover.

The imported `idealContraction` is the intersection of the inverse images under the original stored restrictions. Its closedness and the containment of each closed extension in the supplied local ideal are already proved. The reverse containment is the remaining effectiveness theorem. Neither finite-module descent, generalized Noetherianity, flatness, nor this reverse containment is supplied as a hypothesis.

All closed extensions use the actual norm topologies and the ideal generated by the actual algebra map, followed by topological closure. The statement includes arbitrary positive radii, trivial coefficient valuations, nonreduced and zero quotients, empty spectra and empty finite covers. It contains no atlas or special-subset intersection hypothesis. The geometric application to the special-set intersection theorem remains a separate obligation.
