# The relative four-variable Laurent overlap comparison

Author: Worker 37.

This definition unit constructs the bounded equivalence between the actual
four-variable relative rational product overlap and the published two-variable
Laurent overlap. All declarations are in
`Langlands.Analytic.PresentedAffinoidAlgebra.BinaryLaurent`.

## Inputs and actual quotient

Let `K : Type u` be a normed field with an ultrametric norm and let
`A : PresentedAffinoidAlgebra K` have any positive-polyradius presentation.
Let `f : A`. The preliminary definitions, relations and monomial bounds do
not require completeness of `K`; the maps and equivalence assume
`[CompleteSpace K]`, which makes both target quotient algebras complete.

The numerator tuple is exactly

```lean
productNumerators K A f : Fin 4 → A := ![f, 1, f ^ 2, f]
```

`ProductOverlap K A f` abbreviates
`RelativeRationalPresentation.Algebra K A (PositivePolyradius.unit (Fin 4)) f
(productNumerators K A f)`. Thus it is the actual relative restricted-series
algebra in four unit-radius variables modulo the closure of the ideal
generated by `f*Z0-f`, `f*Z1-1`, `f*Z2-f²`, `f*Z3-f`, with coefficients
interpreted through the constant-series inclusion. Its carrier, ring
operations and norm are the existing closed-ideal quotient's, and not data
assumed to satisfy a universal property. `productBase` and `productZ` are
abbreviations of the actual rational `baseMap` and `fraction`.

`Overlap K A f` is imported unchanged from `BinaryLaurentDiagram`: the
relative two-variable quotient by the closure of the ideal generated by
`X-C(f)` and `C(f)*Y-1`. Its `overlapBase`, `overlapX` and `overlapY` remain
exactly the published coefficient map and coordinates.

## Constructed maps and proof

`productBase_mul_productZ` specializes the actual rational fraction equation
to all four generators. `productBase_mul_productZ_one` extracts `f*Z1=1`.
A private cancellation proof multiplies an equality `f*x=f*y` by `Z1` and
uses commutativity and `f*Z1=1`. It therefore works in every commutative
quotient ring, including a zero ring. This proves `productZ_zero : Z0=1`,
`productZ_two : Z2=productBase(f)` and `productZ_three : Z3=1`.
The square relation uses preservation of powers by the coefficient map.

`productForwardTuple` is exactly `(1,overlapY,overlapX,1)`.
`productForwardTuple_relations` checks all four rational equations: the
first and fourth are multiplication by one; the second is the existing
`overlapBase_mul_overlapY`; and the third uses `overlapX_eq` and
preservation of squares. `productToOverlap` applies
`RelativeRationalPresentation.evaluate` to this tuple and `overlapBase`.
That published construction evaluates the convergent relative coefficient
sum and descends it through the closed generated ideal. Its coefficient
and fraction laws are `productToOverlap_baseMap` and
`productToOverlap_fraction`.

`productReverseTuple` is exactly `(Z2,Z1)`.
`productInverseEvaluation` constructs the bounded series map with
`GeneralizedTateAlgebra.evaluateCoefficientsHom`, using `productBase` as
coefficient map. `productInverseEvaluation_equations` proves that it
annihilates the two actual overlap generators. Evaluation of `X-C(f)` is
`Z2-productBase(f)=0`; evaluation of `C(f)*Y-1` is
`productBase(f)*Z1-1=0`. `overlapToProduct` is then
`RelativeRelations.descend` applied to this series map. Continuity and
closed-ideal descent annihilate the closure, not just the displayed
polynomial relations. The inverse coefficient and coordinate laws follow
from evaluation on constants and variables:
`overlapToProduct_baseMap`, `overlapToProduct_overlapX` and
`overlapToProduct_overlapY`.

For the bounds, the imported coefficient maps are contractive, and every
quotient coordinate has norm at most one. The forward unit entries use
`norm_one_overlap_le`; `norm_one_productOverlap_le` follows by applying the
contractive product coefficient map to the unit of `A`, whose norm is at
most one. `norm_productForwardTuple_le` and
`norm_productReverseTuple_le` check the displayed coordinate bounds.
`productForwardTuple_monomial_bound` and
`productReverseTuple_monomial_bound` apply the published
`GeneralizedTateAlgebra.monomialValue_norm_le_weight` to these bounds and
the relevant unit bound. This explicitly covers the empty monomial without
assuming that either quotient has a norm-one instance or is nonzero.
`norm_productToOverlap_le` uses the rational evaluation contraction;
`norm_overlapToProduct_le` uses relative coefficient evaluation contraction
and `RelativeRelations.norm_descend_le` with bound one. Both directions
are contractive for the actual quotient norms.

`overlapToProduct_comp_productToOverlap` proves the product composite is
the identity by `RelativeRationalPresentation.hom_ext`, checking all
coefficients and the four fractions. The first and fourth fractions use
`Z0=Z3=1`; the second and third return `Z1` and `Z2` directly.
`productToOverlap_comp_overlapToProduct` proves the other composite is
the identity by the published `overlap_hom_ext`, on coefficients, `X`
and `Y`. Finally, `productOverlapEquiv` packages these two constructed
bounded maps and their proved inverse identities with
`BoundedAlgEquiv.ofHomInv`.

## Public equivalence interface

The resulting declaration is

```lean
productOverlapEquiv K A f :
  BoundedAlgEquiv K (ProductOverlap K A f) (Overlap K A f)
```

`productOverlapEquiv_baseMap` is the pointwise coefficient law on the
actual relative rational `baseMap`. `productOverlapEquiv_fraction` bundles
all four forward coordinate laws as the tuple equality
`Z_j ↦ ![1,overlapY,overlapX,1] j` in the specified `Fin 4` order.
`productOverlapEquiv_symm_baseMap` is the inverse coefficient law;
`productOverlapEquiv_symm_overlapX` and
`productOverlapEquiv_symm_overlapY` send the overlap coordinates to the
actual product fractions at `2` and `1`, respectively. These laws are
marked `simp`.

use a gluing or exactness conclusion.

Zero coefficient and overlap algebras, nonreduced algebras, nilpotent `f`
and trivial scalar valuations remain in scope. There is no nonzero,
unit-ideal, nonempty-locus or valuation premise. This unit constructs the
relative presentation equivalence and its elementary laws only. It does
not assert exactness, gluing, descent of finite modules, arbitrary-radius
variable comparison or a comparison to the strict scalar presentation.
