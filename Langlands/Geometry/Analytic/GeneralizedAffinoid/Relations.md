# Closed relations in finite positive-polyradius presentations

Authors: Worker 56 and Worker 36. Worker 56 constructs the weighted version
and bounded descent interface. The definition of a relation presentation and
its relation-vanishing argument adapt Worker 36's published strict construction
in `TatePresentation/Definitions`. General bounded quotient descent is reused
by importing `BoundedQuotient`.

`PresentedAffinoidAlgebra.ofRelations K n r relations` constructs the actual
weighted Tate quotient by the topological closure of the ideal generated by
the supplied set of series. Its presentation has exactly the given finite
variable count and positive polyradius. The imported presentation construction
provides the quotient carrier, quotient norm, normed algebra, ultrametric norm,
surjective contractive presentation map, and coordinate norm bounds.
The imported Banach construction supplies completeness when `K` is complete.
Closedness of the relation ideal follows from taking its closure; no theorem
that all ideals are closed is assumed.

Each supplied relation belongs to its ideal span and then its closure, so its
quotient image is zero. Conversely, the existing `presentationMap_eq_zero_iff`
identifies the whole kernel with this closed generated ideal. `ofRelations_ideal`
recovers any existing weighted presentation exactly by taking its entire
closed ideal as the relation set: the span of an ideal is itself and the
closure of a closed ideal is itself. This is an equality of presentations,
including their actual quotient carriers and chosen norms.

`descendRelations` starts with a supplied bounded algebra homomorphism from
the weighted Tate algebra into a normed `K`-algebra `B`, together with the
condition that it annihilates each relation. The kernel is an ideal, so it
contains their ideal span. The imported continuity argument shows that it also
contains the closure. The existing bounded quotient lift then constructs a
map from the actual presented quotient into `B`. Its composite with the
presentation map is the original homomorphism, both pointwise and as a bounded
algebra homomorphism.

`norm_descendRelations_le` retains every supplied positive norm bound `C`
for this descended map, with precisely the quotient norm. It applies the
general quotient estimate proved using representatives of arbitrarily small
norm excess. `hom_ext_presentation` uses surjectivity to determine a map out
of any weighted presentation by its values on all presented series.
`homEquivAnnihilating` constructs an equivalence between bounded maps from the
relation quotient and bounded Tate maps annihilating the supplied relations.
Its forward map is composition with the quotient map, and its inverse is
`descendRelations`; the inverse laws follow from the pointwise composite
identity and surjectivity.

The coefficient field and target occupy independent universes. These
constructions permit zero variables, arbitrary sets of relations, arbitrary
strictly positive real radii, trivial valuations, and the zero quotient or
zero target. The quotient descent and its equivalence require neither
completeness of `K` or `B` nor an ultrametric norm on `B`. They start with an
actual bounded Tate homomorphism; they do not construct one from coordinate
images. Weighted evaluation, coordinate uniqueness, and representation of
coordinate solutions are separate results. No analytic-domain, localization,
structure-sheaf, or atlas assertion is made here.

This is derived infrastructure for Berkovich's finite weighted affinoid
presentations. The original source is *Non-Archimedean analytic spaces*
(Trieste lectures, 2009), Example 1.2.2(v), printed/PDF page 4, for weighted
restricted series, and the quotient-norm paragraph and Definitions 2.1.1–2.1.2,
page 7, for admissible presentations and affinoid algebras. These pages were
read directly. The source uses complete non-Archimedean fields and allows
admissibly equivalent Banach norms; this construction fixes the quotient norm
and extends its normed-algebra portion to incomplete fields. Taking the closed
generated ideal is an internal construction rather than an appeal to the
source's automatic ideal-closedness fact. The bounded descent interface is
derived from the published general quotient theorem, rather than from a
source assertion of the weighted coordinate universal property.
