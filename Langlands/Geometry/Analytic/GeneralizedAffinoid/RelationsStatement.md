# Coordinate solutions for finite weighted relation quotients: exact target

Authors: Worker 56 and Worker 36. Worker 56 formulates the weighted target,
including the quantitative estimate and the absence of coefficient
completeness. The three-part statement adapts Worker 36's strict
`TatePresentation/Statement` interface.

`Langlands.Analytic.PresentedAffinoidAlgebra.RelationsUniversalPropertyStatement`
is a proposition definition. It specifies the coordinate universal property
of the actual quotient `ofRelations K n r relations`; this module does not
prove that proposition or construct its asserted evaluation map.
The imported quotient is the finite weighted Tate algebra modulo the closure
of the ideal generated by the supplied relations, with its quotient norm.
The coordinates are its actual projected variables. The other imported
definitions are the weighted monomial bound and the infinite-sum formula
`GeneralizedTateAlgebra.evaluate`.

The coefficient field and target algebra occupy independent universes.
Throughout, `K` is an ultrametric normed field and is not assumed complete.
The finite variable count may be zero, and each radius is an arbitrary
strictly positive real number. The relation set may be infinite or empty,
and the generated closed ideal may be the unit ideal. The target is a
normed commutative `K`-algebra; it may be the zero ring and need not have
normalized unit norm. No nontrivial valuation or value-group condition on
the radii is imposed.

The first clause requires a complete ultrametric target and a tuple `b`
with bounded weighted monomials at which every supplied relation evaluates
to zero. It asks for an actual bounded algebra homomorphism from the quotient
to the target. The map agrees with the evaluation formula on every projected
series, sends each coordinate to the prescribed tuple entry, and satisfies
`norm (φ a) ≤ C * norm a` for every quotient element `a` and every positive
`C` bounding all monomials by `C * r.weight d`. The quantifier over `C` is
inside the properties of the one asserted map. Thus that same map retains
every valid supplied bound with no additional factor. The empty monomial is
included in the hypothesis and controls the norm of the target unit.

The second clause requires neither target completeness nor an ultrametric
target norm. It asserts that the coordinate images of every bounded map
satisfy the weighted monomial bound, and that two bounded maps with the same
coordinate images are equal. These are required conclusions, not extra
assumptions about the maps.

The third clause again requires a complete ultrametric target. It asserts
that the conjunction of the weighted monomial bound and the vanishing of
the supplied relations is equivalent to existence of a unique bounded map
with those coordinate images. The condition is stated using actual elements,
finite monomial products, and evaluated series; it does not assume the
desired map as input. This is an equivalence of propositions, not a constructed
equivalence of types.

This target combines the separate weighted Tate evaluation obligations with
the implemented bounded descent for closed relation quotients. The intended
route is to descend weighted evaluation and its quantitative bound through
`descendRelations` and `norm_descendRelations_le`. Coordinate uniqueness
should follow by precomposing with the surjective presentation map and using
weighted Tate coordinate uniqueness, for which direct polynomial approximation
does not need coefficient completeness. Necessity of relation vanishing
should follow by identifying the precomposed map with evaluation. These
explanations describe a proposed derivation, not a proof in this file. The
weighted evaluation proposition is not assumed as a hypothesis of this
target. Convergence is specified separately by that evaluation target; the
current first clause asserts agreement with the imported sum formula.

The original context is Berkovich, *Non-Archimedean analytic spaces*
(Trieste lectures, 2009), Example 1.2.2(v), printed/PDF page 4, for finite
positive-polyradius series and their Gauss norm; the quotient-norm paragraph
and Definitions 2.1.1–2.1.2, page 7, for affinoid presentations; and Exercise
2.1.5(i), pages 7–8, for unique bounded extension with prescribed variables.
These passages were read directly. The source uses complete fields and
affinoid targets with a spectral-radius criterion. This target is a derived
field-coefficient quotient form using explicit weighted monomial bounds and
more general normed targets. It does not assert equivalence with the source's
spectral criterion or the relative-affinoid extension theorem. The closure
of the generated ideal is taken explicitly, without assuming automatic
closedness of all ideals. No analytic-domain, spectrum-locus, structure-sheaf,
or atlas assertion is included.
