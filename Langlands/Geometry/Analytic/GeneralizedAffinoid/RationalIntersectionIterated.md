# Isometric comparison of iterated and product rational intersections

The principal definition `equiv` constructs a bounded \(K\)-algebra equivalence
from the actual iterated intrinsic rational quotient to the published weighted
product-intersection quotient. The two maps are contractive for their inherited
quotient norms, so the equivalence preserves norms and is an isometry. Its
contravariant spectrum homeomorphism transfers the exact intersection
identification to the iterated quotient.

All declarations are in
`Langlands.Analytic.PresentedAffinoidAlgebra.RationalPresentation.RationalIntersectionIterated`.

## Source and provenance

The source conventions are Berkovich, *Non-archimedean analytic spaces*
(Trieste lectures, 2009), Definition 2.1.6(i), printed/PDF page 8, for weighted
restricted series, and Exercise 2.2.2(ii),(iii), printed/PDF page 9, for rational
equations and intersections. The registered source is
`berkovich-2009-trieste-analytic-spaces`, PDF SHA256
`8cfe4f6f35d5e717ac67c5c8f5ef9ff9185f460bbe5f9a10aeacd84cddeeb2ca`.

The explicit comparison, inverse identities, norm argument, and spectrum
transport are internal derivations. They use the published
`GeneralizedAffinoid.RationalIntersection`, the convergent evaluation and
uniqueness interfaces of `RelativeRationalPresentation`, and the scalar-to-relative
isometric `relativeEquiv`. No equivalence, inverse, evaluation certificate, norm
identity, or universal property is supplied as an additional input.

## Actual algebras and assumptions

Let \(K\) be an ultrametric normed field and \(A\) a
`PresentedAffinoidAlgebra K`. Choose finite positive polyradii
\(s=(s_i)_{i<m}\), \(t=(t_j)_{j<n}\) and scalar series representatives
\(g,h,f_i,k_j\) in `GeneralizedTateAlgebra A.polyradius K`. Write \(\bar a\)
for `presentationMap K A a`. Put
\[
 B=\texttt{algebra K A s g f},\qquad
 \beta=\texttt{baseMap K A s g f}:A\longrightarrow B.
\]
This is the actual published scalar rational quotient with its given quotient
norm, not an abstract algebra with assumed coordinate properties.

`Algebra K A s t g h f k`, denoted \(W\), is exactly
`RelativeRationalPresentation.Algebra` over \(B\), at polyradius \(t\), with
denominator \(\beta(\bar h)\) and numerator tuple \(\beta(\bar k_j)\).
Concretely, it is the quotient of the actual relative weighted series algebra
\(B\langle t^{-1}Y\rangle\) by the closure of the ideal generated by
\[
                   \beta(\bar h)Y_j-\beta(\bar k_j).
\]
It retains the quotient norm and all instances from the published relative
quotient construction; the module introduces no replacement norm.

The target \(D=\texttt{RationalIntersection.Algebra K A s t g h f k}\)
is the actual published scalar rational quotient with denominator \(gh\).
For
\[
 F=(g,f_i),\quad H=(h,k_j),\qquad S=(1,s_i),\quad T=(1,t_j),
\]
its numerators are \(N_{ab}=F_aH_b\), and its radii are
\(R_{ab}=S_aT_b\). The pair of indices is encoded by `finProdFinEquiv`.
Write \(Z_{ab}\) for its published `fractionCoordinate`. Its norm is the
existing scalar quotient norm.

The definitions of \(W\), its coefficient maps and fractions, their equations
and estimates, and the product-fraction estimates require neither completeness
nor generation. `generatesUnitIdeal_right` uses the original right generation
hypothesis, and `isUnit_denominators` uses both original hypotheses:
\[
 \texttt{hf}:(\bar g,\bar f_i)=A,\qquad
 \texttt{hk}:(\bar h,\bar k_j)=A.
\]
These are the explicit published `GeneratesUnitIdeal` propositions.

From `toProduct` onward the construction assumes `[CompleteSpace K]`.
The actual presented algebras \(A,B,D\) and the relative quotient \(W\)
then inherit completeness from the published constructions. Relative evaluation
uses the complete ultrametric targets \(D\) and \(W\), and `relativeEquiv`
uses completeness of \(K\). The forward evaluation, equivalence, inverse
identities and spectrum identifications also use `hf` and `hk`.
The reverse evaluation `fromProduct` and its contractive estimate require
completeness but no generation hypothesis.

## Coefficient maps, fractions and product bounds

`coefficientMap` is the actual relative quotient coefficient map
\(\alpha:B\to W\), and `base` is \(\alpha\circ\beta:A\to W\).
`norm_coefficientMap_le` and `norm_base_le` prove that both are contractive.
The old fraction block and the new fraction block are
\[
 u_i=\alpha(\texttt{fraction K A s g f i}),\qquad
 v_j=\texttt{rightFraction j}.
\]
They are `leftFraction` and `rightFraction` in Lean. Their equations and bounds
are proved by mapping the old quotient equations and applying the relative
quotient equations:
\[
 b(\bar g)u_i=b(\bar f_i),\quad \|u_i\|\le s_i,\qquad
 b(\bar h)v_j=b(\bar k_j),\quad \|v_j\|\le t_j,
 \qquad b=\alpha\circ\beta.
\]

Extend these tuples by \(u_0'=v_0'=1\), with the old fractions at successor
indices. `productFraction` is \(p_{ab}=u_a'v_b'\).
`norm_one_le` obtains \(\|1_W\|\le1\) by contractivity of \(\alpha\) and the
published \(\|1_B\|\le1\). This bound remains valid for zero quotients.
`norm_productFraction_le` combines the two extended bounds with
submultiplicativity to prove \(\|p_{ab}\|\le S_aT_b\).
`productFraction_equation` multiplies the two extended fraction equations:
\[
                    b(\overline{gh})p_{ab}=b(\overline{F_aH_b}).
\]
The private `productFraction_monomial` derives the full weighted monomial
estimate from the coordinate bounds and \(\|1_W\|\le1\), including the
empty monomial. This is the bound required by convergent evaluation.

`generatesUnitIdeal_right` proves that `hk` remains true after applying
\(\beta\): the ideal spanned by the images contains \(1\), as seen by pulling
it back along \(\beta\) and using the original unit ideal. For
`isUnit_denominators`, the image of \(\bar g\) is a unit because it is already
a unit in \(B\). For \(\bar h\), the relative equations put all mapped right
numerators in the principal ideal generated by \(b(\bar h)\). The transported
generation hypothesis puts \(1\) in that ideal, proving that \(b(\bar h)\)
is a unit.

## Constructed evaluations and inverse identities

`toProduct : BoundedAlgHom K W D` is the published relative evaluation with
coefficient map
\[
 L=\texttt{RationalIntersection.leftMap}:B\longrightarrow D
\]
and tuple `RationalIntersection.rightCoordinate`, namely \(Z_{0,j+1}\).
The published map \(L\) is contractive; the right coordinate bounds give
the monomial bounds, using the target bound \(\|1_D\|\le1\). The right
coordinate equations and the coefficient formula for \(L\) supply the
relative rational equations. Thus every evaluation hypothesis is proved.

The resulting formulas are `toProduct_coefficientMap`, `toProduct_base`,
`toProduct_leftFraction` and `toProduct_rightFraction`:
\[
 q(\alpha(c))=L(c),\qquad q(b(a))=d(a),\qquad
 q(u_i)=Z_{i+1,0},\qquad q(v_j)=Z_{0,j+1},
\]
where \(q=\texttt{toProduct}\) and \(d=\texttt{RationalIntersection.base}\).
`norm_toProduct_le` proves \(\|q(w)\|\le\|w\|\).

`fromProduct : BoundedAlgHom K D W` first applies the published scalar-to-relative
`relativeEquiv` for \((R,gh,N)\), then evaluates the resulting relative
quotient at coefficient map \(b\) and the full tuple \(p_{ab}\).
The coefficient bound, monomial bound, and equations were proved above.
`fromProduct_base` and `fromProduct_fraction` show
\[
                    r(d(a))=b(a),\qquad r(Z_{ab})=p_{ab},
\]
where \(r=\texttt{fromProduct}\). The coordinate specializations give
`fromProduct_leftCoordinate` and `fromProduct_rightCoordinate`.
`norm_fromProduct_le` combines contractivity of evaluation with exact norm
preservation of the published `relativeEquiv`.

For `toProduct_productFraction`, apply \(q\) to the product-fraction equation.
The resulting element and \(Z_{ab}\) satisfy the same equation with coefficient
\(d(\overline{gh})\). Product unit generation and the published denominator-unit
theorem make this coefficient a unit, so cancellation gives \(q(p_{ab})=Z_{ab}\).

`fromProduct_comp_leftMap` uses scalar quotient extensionality on \(B\),
checking the coefficient and old fraction formulas, to prove \(r\circ L=\alpha\).
Relative quotient extensionality on \(W\) then proves
`fromProduct_comp_toProduct`, namely \(r\circ q=\mathrm{id}_W\).
Scalar quotient extensionality on \(D\), using the base and all product-coordinate
formulas, proves `toProduct_comp_fromProduct`, namely
\(q\circ r=\mathrm{id}_D\). `equiv` packages these actual maps and proved
identities with `BoundedAlgEquiv.ofHomInv`.

`norm_equiv` combines contractivity in both directions and \(r(q(w))=w\) to
prove \(\|q(w)\|=\|w\|\) for the original quotient norms.
`norm_equiv_symm` proves the reverse equality, and `isometry_equiv` applies
the additive-homomorphism norm criterion.

## Actual spectra and structural naturality

`coefficientSpectrumMap : M(W)\to M(B)` and
`baseSpectrumMap : M(W)\to M(A)` are the actual continuous maps obtained by
precomposing multiplicative seminorms with \(\alpha\) and \(b\).
`baseSpectrumMap_eq_comp` identifies the second with the composite through
the original scalar rational spectrum map \(M(B)\to M(A)\).

`spectrumHomeomorph` is the contravariant homeomorphism
\[
                        H:M(D)\simeq_{\mathrm{top}}M(W)
\]
induced by `equiv`. Its formulas are \(H(x)(w)=x(q(w))\) and
\(H^{-1}(y)(z)=y(r(z))\). The theorems
`spectrumHomeomorph_naturality_coefficient` and
`spectrumHomeomorph_naturality_base` prove, as equalities of continuous maps,
\[
 M(\alpha)\circ H=M(L),\qquad M(b)\circ H=M(d).
\]
The two `spectrumHomeomorph_symm_naturality_*` theorems give the corresponding
identities for \(H^{-1}\). These follow from the proved coefficient formulas
by extensionality of actual multiplicative seminorms.

Let \(U=\texttt{locus K A s g f}\) and \(V=\texttt{locus K A t h k}\).
`range_baseSpectrumMap` rewrites \(M(b)=M(d)\circ H^{-1}\), uses surjectivity
of \(H^{-1}\), and applies the published range equality for \(D\), obtaining
\[
                            \operatorname{range}M(b)=U\cap V.
\]
`isClosedEmbedding_baseSpectrumMap` composes the published closed embedding
for \(D\) with \(H^{-1}\).
`intersectionHomeomorph` is \(H^{-1}\) followed by the published
\(M(D)\simeq_{\mathrm{top}}U\cap V\). Its underlying map is exactly \(M(b)\),
as proved by `intersectionHomeomorph_apply_coe`.

## Scope

There is no ordering or normalization condition on the positive radii, no
nonempty-block hypothesis, no proper-ideal hypothesis, and no assumption that
the valuation is nontrivial. Empty blocks, zero quotients and empty spectra
are included. The constructions retain the chosen scalar representatives,
the actual coefficient algebra \(B\), and the existing quotient norms.

The result is this concrete iterated quotient comparison and its structural
spectrum identification. It supplies neither a general bounded-pushout
universal property nor the arbitrary morphism factorization in the source's
Definition 2.2.1. Structure sheaves, atlases, sites and the Harris tower are
outside this unit.
