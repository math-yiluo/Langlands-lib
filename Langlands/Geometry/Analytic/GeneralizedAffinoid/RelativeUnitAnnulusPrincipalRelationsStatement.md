# Closed principal relations in the scalar Laurent diagram

Authors: Worker 105 and Worker 1182. Worker 105 wrote the proposed
Lean/Markdown pair as a missing prerequisite of the scalar binary Laurent
argument; Worker 1182 adapted the Markdown for publication and left the Lean
file unchanged. The file states a proposition to prove. It proves nothing
and adds no assumption to the binary Laurent gluing target.

## The complete proposition

`Langlands.Analytic.PresentedAffinoidAlgebra.RelativeUnitAnnulus.PrincipalRelationsStatement.{u}`
is a proposition-valued definition. All of its data and hypotheses are
quantified inside it. It quantifies over every `K : Type u` with its given
`NormedField`, `IsUltrametricDist` and `CompleteSpace` instances, an
explicit witness `∃ c : K, 1 < ‖c‖`, every presented strict affinoid
algebra `P : PresentedStrictAffinoidAlgebra K`, and every `f : A`, where
`A = PresentedAffinoidAlgebra.ofStrict K P` is the published radius-one
relative presentation of that strict base.

Put

\[
S=A\langle T\rangle,
\qquad U=\mathrm{RelativeUnitAnnulus.Algebra}\ K\ A .
\]

Here `S` is exactly
`GeneralizedTateAlgebra (PositivePolyradius.unit (Fin 1)) A`, the relative
one-variable unit-radius series over `A`, with its published weighted
coefficient norm. The annulus `U` is the published quotient of
`GeneralizedTateAlgebra (PositivePolyradius.unit (Fin 2)) A` by the
closure of the ideal generated by `X₀ * X₁ - 1`, with its original
quotient norm. Inside the namespace, `Algebra K A` means
`RelativeUnitAnnulus.Algebra K A`, not Mathlib's `Algebra` class.

Let `C : A → S` be the published constant-coefficient map
`GeneralizedTateAlgebra.constantAlgHom (PositivePolyradius.unit (Fin 1)) K A`
(so `C(f)` is an element of `S`), and let `T` be the coordinate
`GeneralizedTateAlgebra.X (PositivePolyradius.unit (Fin 1)) A 0`. Write
`b`, `x` and `y` for the published annulus maps and elements `baseMap K A`,
`annulusX K A` and `annulusY K A`. The two one-variable copies used below
are the same type `S`. The published bounded maps `positive K A` and
`negative K A` from `S` to `U` evaluate `T` at `x` and at `y`,
respectively, and use `b` on coefficients.

The proposition forms three ordinary principal ideals as local `let`
bindings:

\[
J_+=(T-C(f))\subseteq S,\qquad
J_-=(C(f)T-1)\subseteq S,\qquad
J=(x-b(f))\subseteq U.
\]

Each is `Ideal.span` of the displayed singleton. No new declaration,
quotient carrier, closure operator or norm is introduced. The proposition
asserts the conjunction of four conclusions.

1. `J_+` is a closed subset of `S`.
2. `J_-` is a closed subset of `S`.
3. `J` is a closed subset of `U`.
4. There is a `K`-linear equivalence
   \[
   \beta:J_+\times J_-\;\simeq_K\;J
   \]
   such that for every `p : J_+` and `q : J_-` the underlying annulus
   element of `β (p, q)` is
   \[
   \beta(p,q)=\mathrm{positive}(p)-\mathrm{negative}(q).
   \]

The topologies in the first three conclusions are the norm topologies of
the published `NormedCommRing` instances: `GeneralizedTateAlgebra.instNormedCommRing`
on `S`, and `RelativeRelations.instNormedCommRingQuotientAlgebra` on `U`,
whose norm is the quotient norm. Instance synthesis for the statement's
types returns exactly these norm topologies. The domain of `β` is the product of the two existing ideal subtypes,
and its codomain is the existing ideal subtype of `U`. Each subtype has the
`K`-module structure inherited from its ambient algebra.

The fourth conclusion is an additive `K`-linear equivalence. It asserts no
multiplicativity, isometry, bounded inverse or numerical bound. Its formula
fixes `β` as the usual difference map. That the differences lie in `J`,
that the map is injective and that it is onto `J` are all conclusions of
the proposition, not facts assumed by it. The first of these is
elementary: `positive(T - C(f)) = x - b(f)`, and
`negative(C(f)T - 1) = b(f)y - 1 = -y(x - b(f))` because `xy = 1`.

Apart from the scalar witness, the proposition has no hypotheses. The
witness says that the given norm of `K` is nontrivial. It keeps the
original `NormedField` structure and introduces no other one. Zero and
nonreduced strict bases are included, and so are `f = 0` and nilpotent
`f`. There is no bound on `‖f‖` and no spectrum-point premise. The lower
quotient and the overlap quotient of the Laurent diagram may be zero.
There is no normal-form, closedness, injectivity, exactness,
quotient-comparison or gluing premise.

The unapplied declaration has type `Prop`; `lean_check` reports
`statement_returns_prop: true`, kind `definition`, the single universe
level `u`, and only the axioms `propext`, `Classical.choice` and
`Quot.sound`. The module imports the published `RelativeUnitAnnulus` and
`StrictComparison` modules and changes no imported file.

## Ordinary principal ideals and their closures

The Library's Laurent quotients are quotients by closures of generated
ideals. In `BinaryLaurentDiagram`, the upper quotient `Upper K A f` is the
relative rational quotient of `S` by the closure of the ideal generated by
its equations, and `upperEquations_eq` identifies those equations with the
singleton `{T - C(f)}`. The lower quotient `Lower K A f` is the quotient of
`S` by the closure of the ideal generated by `{C(f)T - 1}`
(`lowerEquations_eq`). The overlap `Overlap K A f` is the quotient of
`A⟨X,Y⟩` by the closure of the ideal generated by `X - C(f)` and
`C(f)Y - 1`. The annulus `U` itself is the quotient by the closure of the
ideal generated by `XY - 1`.

This statement uses the ordinary ideals `J_+`, `J_-` and `J`, not their
closures. Its first two conclusions say that `J_+` and `J_-` are already
closed. Hence they equal the closed ideals whose quotients are the actual
upper and lower Laurent algebras. The third conclusion says the same of the
principal ideal generated by `x - b(f)` in the actual annulus quotient.
The statement does not identify `U / J` with the actual overlap quotient;
that comparison has a separate statement.

## Provenance and proof obligations

The source is Piotr Achinger, *Introduction to non-Archimedean Geometry*,
lecture notes, November 22, 2020, registered as
`achinger-2020-non-archimedean-geometry-nov22`, SHA256
`666cc6a90efbfc05f5fa0156044a259beaabdf989c45b633542e1cd2cdc0ec95`.
On printed p.6 the source fixes a complete, nontrivially valued
non-Archimedean field. Printed p.64 was read for this statement on
2026-10-31.

Proposition 7.1.1 on printed p.64 writes `A⟨f⟩ = A⟨X⟩/(X - f)`,
`A⟨f⁻¹⟩ = A⟨X⁻¹⟩/(fX⁻¹ - 1)` and
`A⟨f, f⁻¹⟩ = A⟨X, X⁻¹⟩/(X - f)` with ordinary generated ideals. Its proof
displays the top row of a commutative diagram,

\[
0\to (X-f)A\langle X\rangle\times(fX^{-1}-1)A\langle X^{-1}\rangle
\xrightarrow{\ \beta\ }(X-f)A\langle X,X^{-1}\rangle\to 0,
\]

and states that `β` is an isomorphism. In the surjectivity paragraph the
source sets `g₋ = g₊ - g`, so `g = g₊ - g₋` is the image of `(g₊, g₋)`.
This is the sign convention of the formula above. The source's second
variable `X⁻¹` is represented here by the shared type `S` together with the
map `negative`, which sends `T` to `y`.

This statement expresses the isomorphism as a `K`-linear equivalence of the
actual Library ideal subtypes. The three closedness conclusions are not
stated by the source. They isolate the extra step needed to pass from the
ordinary ideals of the source to this Library's closed-ideal quotients.
The source's diagram is not a Lean proof of any of these claims, and no
source citation serves as an assumption.

The source justifies injectivity of `β` in one sentence: an element of the
kernel has the form `(a, a)` with `a ∈ A`, and such an element cannot lie
in the product of the two ideals unless `a = 0`. This still needs a proof
for the actual carriers and norms. Writing `a = (T - C(f))r` and
`a = (C(f)T - 1)s` coefficientwise gives a two-sided recurrence in powers
of `f` with coefficients tending to zero. An argument that evaluates at the
points of `Sp A` shows only that `a` is nilpotent. Bases with nilpotents
therefore need an argument of their own, which is part of the proof
obligation.

On 2026-10-31 no errata note for this source was found in the project
literature directory or the public Library notes, and the source's
literature index entry records none. Two slips on p.64 are also discussed
in `BinaryLaurentGluingStatement.md`. The printed proposition omits the
words "is exact". In the surjectivity paragraph, `g₋₁` should be the
element `g₋` defined just before it. Neither slip changes the comparison
stated here.

A proposed route is as follows. The separately stated relative Laurent
normal form describes elements of `U` by two-sided coefficient sequences.
With it, multiplication by `x - b(f)` sends a coefficient sequence `c` to
the sequence `n ↦ c(n-1) - f*c(n)`. A separately scoped bilateral recurrence theorem over a
Noetherian Banach base would then show that this multiplication is
injective. If `p = (T - C(f))r` and `q = (C(f)T - 1)s`, then
`positive(p) - negative(q) = (x - b(f))(positive(r) + y*negative(s))`.
If this difference vanishes, injectivity of the multiplication together
with the disjoint nonnegative and strictly negative supports of
`positive(r)` and `y*negative(s)` forces `r = s = 0`. Splitting an annulus
element into its nonnegative and strictly negative parts gives
surjectivity onto `J`. The three closedness conclusions should follow from
Noetherianity of `S` and of the actual annulus quotient together with the
closed-ideal theorem for Noetherian Banach algebras.
This paragraph is a plan only. The normal form, the recurrence theorem,
Noetherianity and the closedness theorem are prerequisites to prove and
publish. They are not hypotheses of, or evidence for, this proposition,
and the file implements none of these arguments.

## Scope

This unit constructs no new quotient carrier and proves no comparison of
relation ideals. It does not identify the actual overlap with a principal
annulus quotient; that has a separate statement. The auxiliary additive
Laurent sequence and the final scalar descent remain separate proof
obligations. No higher Čech exactness, arbitrary radii, finite-module
descent or standard-cover assertion occurs.
