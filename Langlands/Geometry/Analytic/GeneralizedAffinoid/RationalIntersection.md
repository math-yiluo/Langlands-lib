# Product presentations for positive-polyradius rational intersections

The main definition `intersectionHomeomorph` identifies the spectrum of an
actual weighted rational quotient with the intersection of two original
weighted rational loci. Over a complete ground field, the module also constructs
contractive restriction homomorphisms from both original rational quotients.
All declarations are in
`Langlands.Analytic.PresentedAffinoidAlgebra.RationalPresentation.RationalIntersection`.

## Source and provenance

The conventions are those of Berkovich, *Non-archimedean analytic spaces*
(Trieste lectures, 2009), Definition 2.1.6(i), printed/PDF page 8, for weighted
restricted series, and Exercise 2.2.2(ii),(iii), printed/PDF page 9, for rational
equations and intersections of domains of the same type. The registered source
is `berkovich-2009-trieste-analytic-spaces`, with PDF SHA256
`8cfe4f6f35d5e717ac67c5c8f5ef9ff9185f460bbe5f9a10aeacd84cddeeb2ca`.

The explicit product tuple, product radii, closed quotient, and restriction
maps below are internal derivations using the published scalar rational
presentation, its spectrum theorem, and its isometric comparison with the
intrinsic relative rational quotient. They are not quoted constructions from
the exercise. The source's full affinoid-domain universal property in
Definition 2.2.1 is a separate obligation.

## Inputs and actual quotient

Let \(K\) be an ultrametric normed field and \(A\) a
`PresentedAffinoidAlgebra K`. Choose finite positive polyradii
\(s=(s_i)_{i<m}\), \(t=(t_j)_{j<n}\), and representatives
\(g,h,f_i,k_j\) in `GeneralizedTateAlgebra A.polyradius K`. A bar denotes their
images in \(A\) under `presentationMap K A`.

The original loci are
\[
 U=\{x\in M(A):x(\bar f_i)\le s_i x(\bar g)\text{ for every }i\},\qquad
 V=\{x\in M(A):x(\bar k_j)\le t_j x(\bar h)\text{ for every }j\}.
\]
Here \(M(A)\) means the actual `BanachSpectrum A`, and these sets are the
published `locus K A s g f` and `locus K A t h k`.

Extend both numerator tuples by their denominators and both radius tuples by
one:
\[
 F=(g,f_0,\ldots,f_{m-1}),\quad H=(h,k_0,\ldots,k_{n-1}),\qquad
 S=(1,s_0,\ldots,s_{m-1}),\quad T=(1,t_0,\ldots,t_{n-1}).
\]
`numerators` is the tuple \(N_{ab}=F_aH_b\), and `productRadii` is the positive
polyradius \(R_{ab}=S_aT_b\). The pair \((a,b)\) is encoded in
`Fin ((m + 1) * (n + 1))` by `finProdFinEquiv`; `numerators_pair` and
`productRadii_pair` give the corresponding evaluation formulas.

`Algebra K A s t g h f k`, denoted \(D\), is the published scalar rational
quotient `algebra K A R (g * h) N`. Concretely it adjoins the product-coordinate
block to the original scalar weighted series presentation and quotients by
the closure of the ideal generated by the old relations and
\[
                  gh Z_{ab}-F_aH_b.
\]
Thus it is an actual closed quotient, rather than a carrier equipped with
assumed coordinate properties. `base : BoundedAlgHom K A D` is its constructed
structural map, and `fractionCoordinate a b` is its actual coordinate
\(Z_{ab}\). The construction requires no generation hypothesis.
`norm_base_le`, `coordinate_equation`, and `norm_fractionCoordinate_le` prove
\[
 \|\operatorname{base}(a)\|\le\|a\|,\qquad
 \operatorname{base}(\overline{gh})Z_{ab}
      =\operatorname{base}(\overline{F_aH_b}),\qquad
 \|Z_{ab}\|\le S_aT_b.
\]

## Generation and the intersection locus

The hypotheses `hf` and `hk` used for the intersection identification are
\[
 (\bar g,\bar f_i)=A,\qquad(\bar h,\bar k_j)=A.
\]
These are the explicit `GeneratesUnitIdeal` propositions. There is no
assumption equating this algebraic condition with absence of common spectral
zeros.

`range_mapped_numerators` identifies the set of all \(\overline{F_aH_b}\)
with the pointwise product of the two extended sets of generators.
`generatesUnitIdeal` applies `Ideal.span_mul_span`: the product of the two
unit ideals is the unit ideal. The product denominator \(gh=N_{00}\) already
belongs to the numerator tuple.

`locus_eq_inter` proves that the rational locus for \((R,gh,N)\) is \(U\cap V\).
For the forward implication, unit generation and the published
`denominator_ne_zero` make \(x(\overline{gh})\) positive. Multiplicativity
and nonnegativity give positivity of both \(x(\bar g)\) and \(x(\bar h)\).
The inequalities for \(N_{i+1,0}=f_i h\) and \(N_{0,j+1}=g k_j\) can therefore
be cancelled to recover the two original families of inequalities.
For the reverse implication, extend each original inequality family by the
tautological denominator inequality and multiply the two inequalities for
each pair of indices.

All pairwise numerator products and their product radii are retained. In
particular, the mixed \(f_i k_j\) equations are needed for the generation
argument; using only \(f_i h\) and \(g k_j\) could leave unwanted points at
which both denominators vanish.

## Spectrum identification

`baseSpectrumMap` is the actual continuous seminorm-precomposition map
\[
                 M(D)\longrightarrow M(A)
\]
induced by `base`. Under `hf` and `hk`, `range_baseSpectrumMap` combines the
published scalar rational spectrum range theorem with `locus_eq_inter`.
`isClosedEmbedding_baseSpectrumMap` uses the corresponding published
closed-embedding theorem.

`intersectionHomeomorph` is the embedding's homeomorphism onto its range,
followed by the proved equality of that range with \(U\cap V\):
\[
                  M(D)\simeq_{\mathrm{top}} U\cap V.
\]
Its forward map is the structural spectrum map, as recorded by
`intersectionHomeomorph_apply_coe`. The point-lifting, injectivity and compact
Hausdorff arguments needed here are those already proved in
`GeneralizedAffinoid.RationalSpectrum`; this module supplies the product
generation and exact locus calculation.

## Constructed restrictions

`leftCoordinate i` is \(Z_{i+1,0}\), and `rightCoordinate j` is \(Z_{0,j+1}\).
Their norm bounds are respectively \(s_i\) and \(t_j\).
`isUnit_denominators` first uses product generation to show that the image
of \(gh\) in \(D\) is a unit, then deduces that the images of both \(g\) and
\(h\) are units. Cancelling the appropriate unit in `coordinate_equation`
proves `leftCoordinate_equation` and `rightCoordinate_equation`:
\[
 \operatorname{base}(\bar g)Z_{i+1,0}=\operatorname{base}(\bar f_i),\qquad
 \operatorname{base}(\bar h)Z_{0,j+1}=\operatorname{base}(\bar k_j).
\]

For this paragraph assume additionally `[CompleteSpace K]`, `hf`, and `hk`.
Let \(B=\texttt{algebra K A s g f}\) and
\(C=\texttt{algebra K A t h k}\).
`leftMap : BoundedAlgHom K B D` and
`rightMap : BoundedAlgHom K C D` are compositions of the published isometric
`relativeEquiv` with `RelativeRationalPresentation.evaluate`. The target
coefficient map is `base`, and the respective evaluation tuples are the
left and right coordinates just constructed. The private monomial estimates
follow from the coordinate bounds and the target bound
`PresentedAffinoidAlgebra.norm_one_le`; this also handles the empty monomial
when \(D\) is the zero ring.

The coefficient and fraction identities are `leftMap_base`,
`rightMap_base`, `leftMap_fraction`, and `rightMap_fraction`.
`norm_leftMap_le` and `norm_rightMap_le` prove contractivity using the
evaluation bound and the exact norm preservation of `relativeEquiv`.
`leftMap_comp_baseMap` and `rightMap_comp_baseMap` express the two structural
composition identities as equalities of bounded algebra homomorphisms.
`leftSpectrumMap` and `rightSpectrumMap` are their actual continuous
contravariant spectrum maps. The two `*_naturality` theorems prove that
composition with the original structural spectrum maps gives
`baseSpectrumMap`.

## Assumptions and remaining scope

The quotient, coordinate equations and estimates, generation, locus
identification, and `intersectionHomeomorph` require no completeness assumption
on \(K\). This is a preliminary extension of the algebraic and spectral
constructions beyond the source's complete-field setting. The restriction
homomorphisms use completeness through the published scalar-to-relative
comparison and convergent relative evaluation.

There is no radius ordering, proper-ideal, nontrivial-valuation, or nonempty
coordinate-block hypothesis. Zero quotients and empty loci are allowed.
All quotients and maps use the chosen original scalar presentations; no
independence from those choices is asserted here.

This unit establishes the product quotient, its structural spectrum
intersection, and the two contractive restrictions. A bounded pushout
universal property, an isometric comparison with an iterated relative
rational quotient, and factorization of arbitrary morphisms whose spectral
images lie in the intersection remain separate obligations. Analytic
structure sheaves, atlases, etale sites, equivariant sites, and the Harris
tower are not constructed in this module.
