# Scalar descent for the actual relative binary Laurent diagram

Author: Worker 105.

The module proves

```lean
theorem Langlands.Analytic.PresentedAffinoidAlgebra.BinaryLaurent.gluing :
    Langlands.Analytic.PresentedAffinoidAlgebra.BinaryLaurent.GluingStatement.{u}
```

The unapplied theorem has exactly the published proposition as its type,
at every universe `u`, with no additional parameters or hypotheses.
`BinaryLaurentGluingStatement` and its imported closure are unchanged.

The proposition quantifies internally over a complete ultrametric normed
field `K` with its given `NormedField` structure and an explicit witness
`hK : ∃ c : K, 1 < ‖c‖`, a presented strict affinoid algebra `P`, and
`f : A`, where `A = PresentedAffinoidAlgebra.ofStrict K P`. For every
`sUpper : Upper K A f` and `sLower : Lower K A f` whose images under the
actual `left` and `right` maps agree, it gives a unique `a : A` mapping to
those sections under the actual `upperBase` and `lowerBase` maps.

## Published analytic inputs and actual quotients

The proof imports and applies these published theorems by Lean name:

- `RelativeUnitAnnulus.normal_form : NormalFormStatement.{u}`, from

- `RelativeUnitAnnulus.principal_relations :

  `RelativeUnitAnnulusPrincipalRelationsProof`.

These are proved inputs, not assumptions of `gluing`. The final assembly
uses the coefficient laws of the normal form, the kernel clause of the
overlap presentation, and the closedness and linear equivalence supplied
by the principal-relations theorem.

Write `S = GeneralizedTateAlgebra (PositivePolyradius.unit (Fin 1)) A`,
with its coordinate `T` and constant inclusion `C`, and write
`U = RelativeUnitAnnulus.Algebra K A`. This is the actual two-variable
unit-radius series quotient by the closure of the ideal generated by
`XY - 1`, with its original quotient norm. Write `x = annulusX K A`
and `b = baseMap K A`. Let

\[
J_+=(T-C(f))\subset S,\qquad
J_-=(C(f)T-1)\subset S,\qquad
J=(x-b(f))\subset U.
\]

These are ordinary generated ideals. The upper and lower algebras are
the existing quotients by their closures. The overlap remains the
existing two-variable quotient by the closure of the ideal generated
by `X-C(f)` and `C(f)Y-1`. No carrier, norm, coefficient map or
restriction map is replaced.

`principal_relations K hK P f` proves that all three ordinary ideals
above are closed in their original norm topologies and supplies

\[
\beta:J_+\times J_-\simeq_K J,\qquad
\beta(p,q)=\mathrm{positive}(p)-\mathrm{negative}(q).
\]

The proof names the actual upper and lower quotient projections
`Qpos` and `Qneg`, using `RelativeRationalPresentation.quotientMap`.
The published kernel criterion `quotientMap_eq_zero_iff`, the equations
`upperEquations_eq` and `lowerEquations_eq`, and
`Ideal.closure_eq_of_isClosed` therefore give

\[
Q_{\rm pos}(s)=0\iff s\in J_+,\qquad
Q_{\rm neg}(t)=0\iff t\in J_-.
\]

Closedness is discharged by the published analytic proof; it is never
added as a premise.

## Equal positive and negative series are constants

The private lemma `equal_positive_negative_are_constants` works over
any presented base `A`. Suppose that
`positive K A s = negative K A t`. Take the linear isometry equivalence
`e : U ≃ₗᵢ[K] C₀(ℤ, A)` from `normal_form K A`. Its published laws say
that the positive image has the coefficients of `s` on nonnegative
indices and is zero on negative indices, while the negative image has
the coefficients of `t` on nonpositive indices and is zero on positive
indices.

Evaluate the equality at `n = 0` to equate the constant coefficients.
At `n = m > 0` the coefficient of degree `m` of `s` vanishes; at
`n = -m < 0` the coefficient of degree `m` of `t` vanishes. Thus each
series is `C(a)` for the common constant coefficient `a`.

The private lemmas `one_variable_ext` and `one_variable_coeff_constant`
make this argument directly in the existing restricted series:
every multi-index on `Fin 1` is `Finsupp.single 0 m`, and the
coefficient of `C(a)` is `a` at degree zero and zero otherwise.
Only the linear equivalence and its coefficient formulas are used.
Pointwise multiplication of functions in `C₀(ℤ, A)` is never treated
as multiplication of Laurent series.

## Injectivity at the base

The local assertion `hbaseinj` proves that

\[
a\longmapsto
(\mathrm{upperBase}(a),\mathrm{lowerBase}(a))
\]

is injective. If `a` and `b` have the same image, put `c = a-b`.
Then `Qpos(C(c)) = Qneg(C(c)) = 0`, so the constant `C(c)` gives
an element `p` of `J₊` and an element `q` of `J₋`.
Both its positive and negative images are the same annulus constant,
by `positive_constant` and `negative_constant`. The formula for `β`
therefore gives `β(p,q) = 0`.

Injectivity of the linear equivalence `β` forces `(p,q) = 0`,
hence `C(c) = 0` as a series. The published
`isometry_constantAlgHom` is injective, so `c = 0` and `a = b`.
This proves uniqueness without assuming any injectivity of either
individual base map.

## Existence by correction of representatives

Lift `sUpper` and `sLower` to series `s,t ∈ S` using the actual
quotient projections' surjectivity. Put

\[
d=\mathrm{positive}(s)-\mathrm{negative}(t)\in U.
\]

The identities `toOverlap_positive` and `toOverlap_negative`,
together with the given compatibility of the quotient sections,
show that `d` belongs to the kernel of the actual map
`RelativeUnitAnnulus.toOverlap K A f`. The kernel clause of
`overlap_presentation K A f` identifies this kernel with the closure
of `J`. Closedness of `J`, already supplied by `principal_relations`,
then gives `d ∈ J`. This step needs neither the overlap theorem's
surjectivity clause nor its approximate norm-lift clause.

Surjectivity of `β` supplies `p ∈ J₊` and `q ∈ J₋` with

\[
\mathrm{positive}(p)-\mathrm{negative}(q)=d.
\]

Subtracting these relations from the representatives gives
`positive(s-p) = negative(t-q)`. The constant-intersection lemma
provides `a : A` with `s-p = C(a)` and `t-q = C(a)`.
The projections kill `p` and `q` by the kernel criteria above.
Consequently `upperBase(a) = sUpper` and `lowerBase(a) = sLower`,
using the published formulas `upperBase_apply` and `lowerBase_apply`.
Finally `hbaseinj` proves that any other such `a` is equal to this one.

in scope.

of the upper and lower quotients. It makes no claim of surjectivity

admissibility, arbitrary-radius gluing or finite-module descent.

are separate results.
