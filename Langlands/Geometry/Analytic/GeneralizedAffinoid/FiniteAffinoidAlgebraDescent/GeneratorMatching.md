# Bounded matching realization from global generators and effective kernels

This internal construction proves that the global presented generator
quotient is boundedly equivalent to the entire complete matching algebra.
It assumes only that the finite matching tuple generates each original
local algebra as a module over its original cover algebra, with the
already supplied finite Banach structures there.

The original weighted cover pulls back to a finite full-domain cover of
the global quotient. The canonical local quotient equivalences give
bounded maps from the matching algebra into every member of that cover.
Their compatibility is proved on the actual quotient pullback of the
original literal intersection. Further pushout and quotient comparisons
retain both original restriction maps, and the original transition
identifies the two comparisons. Applying its injective algebra equivalence
reduces compatibility to the entire original matching equation.
Any other actual full-domain overlap test factors through that domain by
its exact locus containment and full-domain restriction uniqueness.

The published finite-domain bounded-map gluing construction now gives
`matchingInverse`, retaining each original local bounded inverse. The
canonical matching map after this inverse is the identity, as checked on
all matching projections using the local comparisons. The opposite inverse
identity follows from the already proved algebraic injectivity of the
matching map. Thus `matchingEquiv` has constructed bounded maps in both
directions, and `matchingPresentation` is a genuine output presentation
of the entire matching algebra.

This is derived from explicit closed-kernel effectiveness, bounded quotient
base-change comparisons and scalar full-domain bounded-map gluing. It
establishes the affinoid realization from global generators; it does not
yet establish global norm-controlled finite-module coefficients. All original
full-domain cover maps, arbitrary positive polyradii, trivial valuations,
zero quotients, and empty covers or spectra are retained.
