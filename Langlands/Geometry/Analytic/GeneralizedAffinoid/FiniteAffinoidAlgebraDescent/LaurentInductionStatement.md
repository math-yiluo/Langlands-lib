# Induction along iterated real-radius binary Laurent splits

`Langlands.Analytic.PresentedAffinoidAlgebra.FiniteCoverBinaryLaurentInductionStatement`
is an unproved proposition definition. It is an intermediate statement for the
proof of `FiniteAffinoidAlgebraDescentStatement`, not an axiom.

## Statement

Fix a complete ultrametric normed field `K` (trivial valuations allowed), an
actual presented positive-polyradius affinoid algebra `R`, and a finite
full-domain cover `c` of `Domain.whole K R` with at least one member
(`0 < c.size`). Let `P` be any predicate on full domains of `R`
(`Domain K R → Prop`) satisfying:

- **Base.** `P U` holds for every full domain `U` whose locus is contained in
  the locus of some cover member `c.obj i`.
- **Closure.** `P U` holds for a full domain `U` as soon as there are an
  element `a` of `U.algebra` and a real radius `ρ > 0` such that `P U₁` and
  `P U₂` hold for all full domains `U₁`, `U₂` of `R` whose loci are
  `U.spectrumMap '' {y | y a ≤ ρ}` and `U.spectrumMap '' {y | ρ ≤ y a}`
  (the subsets `{|a| ≤ ρ}` and `{|a| ≥ ρ}` of the locus of `U`).

Then `P (Domain.whole K R)` holds.

Equivalently: the whole spectrum admits a finite binary tree of splits
`{|a| ≤ ρ}`, `{|a| ≥ ρ}` (each split taken inside the current node, with `a` in
the algebra of a full domain realizing that node and a positive real radius)
whose leaves all lie inside members of `c`. A leaf may have empty locus; it then
lies inside every member, which is why at least one member is required. The
statement is phrased as an induction principle so that it needs no auxiliary
tree definitions. The radii are arbitrary positive reals, which is necessary
when `K` is trivially valued or `R` is not strict.

The hypothesis `0 < c.size` cannot be dropped. For the zero algebra (the
quotient of the zero-variable Tate algebra by the unit ideal) the spectrum is
empty, the empty cover is a finite full-domain cover, and the constantly false
predicate satisfies both premises vacuously. Users with a possibly empty cover
must treat that case separately; for instance the global generation statement
`FiniteAffinoidAlgebraGlobalGenerationStatement` is vacuous for an empty cover.

## Provenance

This is a derived internal statement; no source states it in this form.

Its ingredients come from the following sources.

- Laurent domains with arbitrary positive real radii, cut out by weak
  inequalities `|a| ≤ ρ` and `|a| ≥ ρ`, are the domains of Berkovich,
  *Spectral theory and analytic geometry over non-Archimedean fields* (1990),
  Remark 2.2.2(i), printed p.27 / PDF p.41 (registered source
  `provided-a03a0182668f6b92b01deacac09a9992b3edf55e23a0a2194554384ae44bd8d4`).
- The classical reduction of a finite affinoid covering to binary Laurent
  splits, in the strictly affinoid case over a nontrivially valued field, is
  the proof of Tate's acyclicity theorem in Piotr Achinger, *Introduction to
  non-Archimedean Geometry*, lecture notes, November 22, 2020, §7.2–§7.3,
  pp.65–67 (registered source `achinger-2020-non-archimedean-geometry-nov22`):
  Gerritzen–Grauert and Lemma 7.2.2 reduce to a rational covering generated by
  `f₀, …, fₙ` without common zero; the proof of Theorem 7.0.1 then inducts on
  the number of non-units, splitting `X = X(f) ∪ X(f⁻¹)` with `f = fₙ/t^N`
  for a scalar `t` with `|t^N|` below `inf_x maxᵢ |fᵢ(x)|`, and finishes with
  the Laurent covering by the quotients `fᵢ/fⱼ` when all `fᵢ` are units.
- The general (non-strict, possibly trivially valued) case is treated in the
  cited descent passages by reduction to the strict nontrivially valued case,
  not by a real-radius refinement: Berkovich (1990), proof of Proposition
  2.2.5, printed p.30 / PDF p.44 (Tate acyclicity, via Proposition 2.1.2(ii)),
  and Berkovich, *Étale cohomology for non-Archimedean analytic spaces* (1993),
  §1.2 and Remark 1.2.1(ii), printed p.15 / PDF p.12 (registered source
  `berkovich-1993-etale-cohomology`).

The proposed route for this statement replaces the small scalar `t^N` by a
positive real radius `ρ` below `inf_x maxᵢ |fᵢ(x)|`, splitting along
`{|fₙ| ≤ ρ}` and `{|fₙ| ≥ ρ}`, so no scalar of small nonzero absolute value
is needed. The first step would use the Library's
`Domain.FiniteCover.relativeRationalRefinement` (built on the published
unconditional finite rational coverage) to refine `c` by rational domains.
