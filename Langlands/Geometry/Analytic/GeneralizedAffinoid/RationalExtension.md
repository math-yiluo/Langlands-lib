# Bounded extension for weighted rational presentations

Authors: Worker 125, Worker 36 and Worker 56. Worker 125 implements the weighted proof, the concatenation estimate, and coordinate-prescribed uniqueness. The denominator, evaluation/descent, and base-extensionality arguments adapt Worker 36's published strict `RationalPresentation` proof. Worker 56 supplied the weighted derivation and the five-clause interface. The published relation-quotient universal property of Worker 105 and Worker 56 is imported by Lean name; its implementation is not copied.

`Langlands.Analytic.PresentedAffinoidAlgebra.RationalPresentation.boundedExtension` proves the entire unchanged `BoundedExtensionStatement.{u, v}`. The coefficient and target universes are independent. No result in this module assumes coefficient completeness. Both variable counts may be zero, all radii are arbitrary strictly positive real numbers, and the old or new quotient ideal may be the unit ideal. Targets may be zero rings and their unit need not have norm one. Neither nontrivial valuation nor a value-group or spectral-radius condition is imposed.

The actual rational quotient and all notation are imported unchanged from `GeneralizedAffinoid.RationalPresentation`. If `r` is the old polyradius, `s` the new one, and `I` the old closed ideal, this is the quotient of the series algebra at `r.append s` by the closed ideal generated by the image of every member of `I` and the equations `i(g) Y_j - i(f_j)`. Write `q` for the old presentation map and `alpha` for `baseMap`. The new quotient coordinates are `fraction j`.

`isUnit_baseMap_denominator` considers the principal ideal `J` generated by `alpha(q(g))`. Its inverse image under `alpha` contains `q(g)` and each `q(f_j)`: the latter follows from the imported equation `alpha(q(g)) * fraction j = alpha(q(f_j))`. Unit-ideal generation in the old quotient therefore puts one in `J`, so its generator is a unit. This reasoning applies also to the zero quotient.

`denominator_ne_zero` argues directly in the old spectrum. If a point in the weighted locus killed `q(g)`, its inequalities `x(q(f_j)) <= s_j * x(q(g))` and nonnegativity would kill each numerator. The spectrum kernel would contain the generated unit ideal, forcing `x(1) = 0`, contrary to normalization `x(1) = 1`.

`hom_ext_base_fraction` proves that equal base restrictions and equal fraction values determine any bounded map into an arbitrary normed commutative target algebra. The old and new coordinates exhaust `Fin (A.arity + m)` by `Fin.addCases`. Old-coordinate equality follows from `baseMap_coordinate`; new-coordinate equality is prescribed. The imported `hom_ext_coordinate` identifies the maps. `hom_ext_base` uses generation to remove the fraction-value hypothesis. Apply both maps to the fraction equations and cancel the image of the denominator, already proved to be a unit. Neither extensionality lemma requires completeness or ultrametricity of the target.

The auxiliary `GeneralizedTateAlgebra.hasBoundedMonomials_append` proves the weighted estimate needed for existence. Given bounds `C > 0` and `D > 0` for two tuples, restrict each exponent on `Fin (n+m)` to its first and second blocks. Rewriting the finitely supported products as full finite products and using `Fin.prod_univ_add` shows that both the evaluated monomial and its radius weight split into their two factors. Submultiplicativity of the target norm gives the concatenated bound `C * D`. The full products include empty products; the original monomial bounds already control the norm of the target unit. Thus this proof needs no nonempty-block or normalized-unit assumption.

`solutionTuple` appends the proposed fraction tuple to the images of the old quotient coordinates under a bounded map `phi`. The imported `hasBoundedMonomials_coordinate` bounds the old tuple, and the concatenation lemma proves `solutionTuple_bounded`. The subsequent existence construction assumes only that the target is complete and ultrametric, that the proposed new tuple has a weighted monomial bound, and that it satisfies the fraction equations.

`evaluate_included` compares the bounded weighted evaluation map precomposed with `appendLeft` against `phi` precomposed with the old presentation map. Their values on each old variable agree by `appendLeft_X`, `evaluate_X`, and the first-block identity for `Fin.append`. Published weighted-series coordinate extensionality identifies these composites, giving agreement on every old series. `solutionTuple_relations` then proves that evaluation kills every defining relation: included members of the old ideal project to zero, and the new relations evaluate to zero by the assumed fraction equations.

`liftFromBase` applies the published `evaluateRelations` to this bounded tuple and the relation-vanishing proof. That imported construction supplies bounded descent through the actual closed relation ideal, using continuity and the quotient norm. `liftFromBase_comp_baseMap` proves the base identity by surjectivity of the old presentation map and `evaluate_included`. `liftFromBase_fraction` evaluates each newly adjoined variable to its prescribed tuple value.

`existsUnique_extension_fraction` combines this map and its two identities with `hom_ext_base_fraction`. It requires no unit-ideal generation and proves uniqueness among all bounded maps with the specified base restriction and fraction coordinates. `existsUnique_extension` instead assumes generation and applies `hom_ext_base`, proving uniqueness among all bounded maps with the specified base restriction alone. Finally, `boundedExtension` assembles denominator invertibility, locus nonvanishing, arbitrary-target base extensionality, coordinate-prescribed unique existence, and base-only unique existence into the exact published proposition.

This is an internal derivation from the published weighted evaluation, quotient descent, and rational-presentation constructions, together with the strict proof adaptation identified above. The source motivation and its limits remain those of the unchanged `RationalExtensionStatement.md`, which distinguishes Berkovich's relative-series and spectral hypotheses from this explicit weighted-monomial formulation. The implemented proof makes no spectral-radius equivalence, reverse spectrum inclusion, relative-series comparison, presentation-independence, analytic-domain representability, or analytic-space completion claim.
