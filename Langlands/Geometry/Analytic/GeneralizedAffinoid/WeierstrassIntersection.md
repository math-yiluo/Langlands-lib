# Combining two actual positive-radius Weierstrass quotients

This module constructs an isometric bounded algebra equivalence between two
successive Weierstrass quotients and the scalar quotient on the concatenated
tuple. It also identifies the spectrum of the iterated quotient with the
intersection of the two original Weierstrass inequality loci, using its actual
structural map.

All declarations are in
`Langlands.Analytic.PresentedAffinoidAlgebra.RationalPresentation.WeierstrassIntersection`.
The implementation imports the published `RelativeRationalComparison` and
`RationalSpectrum` modules.

## Algebras and structural maps

Let \(K\) be an ultrametric normed field and \(A\) an actual
`PresentedAffinoidAlgebra K`. Let \(s\) and \(t\) be arbitrary positive
polyradii indexed by `Fin m` and `Fin n`, respectively, and let \(f_i\) and
\(h_j\) be elements of the scalar restricted-series algebra presenting \(A\).
Write \(\bar f_i,\bar h_j\) for their classes in \(A\).

The abbreviation `firstAlgebra` is the published scalar Weierstrass quotient
\[
 B=A\{s^{-1}Y\}/\overline{(Y_i-\bar f_i)}.
\]
This notation describes the equations; the implementation uses the concrete
scalar presentation, including the original relations of \(A\).
Its coefficient map is the published `baseMap K A s 1 f`, denoted \(i_s\).
The function `secondTuple` sends \(j\) to \(i_s(\bar h_j)\).
The abbreviation `Algebra` is the actual intrinsic relative quotient
\[
 W=B\{t^{-1}Z\}/\overline{(Z_j-i_s(\bar h_j))}.
\]
Here and below the relation ideal is the closure of the ideal generated by
the displayed equations. The relative series and quotient carry their
constructed norms and ultrametric normed algebra structures.

The abbreviation `combinedAlgebra` is the scalar Weierstrass quotient
\[
 D=A\{(s\mathbin{\mathrm{append}}t)^{-1}U\}/
       \overline{(U_k-\overline{(f\mathbin{\mathrm{append}}h)_k})}.
\]
The append operation orders the \(f\)-block before the \(h\)-block.

The maps `iteratedBase : B → W` and `base : A → W` are the genuine bounded
coefficient maps, with `base` their composite through \(B\).
The elements `leftCoordinate` and `rightCoordinate` are the images of the old
coordinates \(Y_i\) and the new coordinates \(Z_j\).
Their equations identify them with `base` applied to \(\bar f_i\) and
\(\bar h_j\). The coefficient maps contract, and the coordinate norms are
bounded by \(s_i\) and \(t_j\). These definitions and estimates do not require
completeness of \(K\).

The elements `combinedLeft` and `combinedRight` are the two blocks of actual
coordinates in \(D\), indexed using `Fin.castAdd` and `Fin.natAdd`.
Their equations and norm bounds are inherited from the scalar quotient.
The theorem `combinedLocus_eq_inter`, also without a completeness assumption,
splits the concatenated inequalities into their two blocks:
\[
 V_{s\mathrm{\ append\ }t}(f\mathrm{\ append\ }h)
       =V_s(f)\cap V_t(h)\subseteq\mathcal M(A),
 \qquad
 V_s(f)=\{x:\ x(\bar f_i)\leq s_i\text{ for all }i\}.
\]

## Constructed comparison and exact norms

Assume now that \(K\) is complete.
The private helper `scalarEvaluate` composes the published isometric
scalar-to-relative comparison with intrinsic bounded evaluation.
It constructs a contracting map out of a scalar Weierstrass quotient from
a contracting coefficient map and a tuple satisfying the actual equations
and weighted monomial bounds.

The map `fromCombined : D → W` uses this evaluation at `base` and the
concatenation of the left and right coordinates of \(W\).
The map `firstMap : B → D` uses the combined coefficient map and the left
coordinate block of \(D\).
Finally, `toCombined : W → D` uses intrinsic evaluation with coefficient
map `firstMap` and the right coordinate block of \(D\).
The module proves each map's coefficient and coordinate formulas and its
contractive norm estimate.

The monomial estimates follow from the individual coordinate bounds.
They use the proved inequality \(\lVert1\rVert\leq1\), including the empty
monomial, rather than requiring the quotient to be nonzero.
For \(W\) this unit bound follows by contraction of `iteratedBase` from \(B\);
for the scalar quotients it is already published.

Scalar coefficient-and-fraction extensionality proves
`fromCombined_comp_firstMap = iteratedBase`.
Relative coefficient-and-fraction extensionality then proves
`fromCombined_toCombined`, while scalar extensionality on the concatenated
blocks proves `toCombined_fromCombined`.
Thus `equiv : BoundedAlgEquiv K W D` packages the two explicitly constructed
inverse maps. It sends `base` to the coefficient map of \(D\), and the two
coordinate blocks to `combinedLeft` and `combinedRight`. Its restriction
along `iteratedBase` is `firstMap`.

The theorem `norm_equiv` proves equality of the actual quotient norms:
\[
 \lVert\operatorname{equiv}(w)\rVert=\lVert w\rVert.
\]
One inequality is contraction of `toCombined`; the other uses the inverse
identity and contraction of `fromCombined`.
The module also proves the inverse norm equality and `isometry_equiv`.

## Spectra and the intersection subspace

The maps `iteratedSpectrumMap`, `firstSpectrumMap` and `baseSpectrumMap`
are actual continuous pullbacks of bounded multiplicative seminorms along
`iteratedBase`, `firstMap` and `base`.
The bounded algebra equivalence induces the contravariant homeomorphism
\[
 \operatorname{spectrumHomeomorph}:\mathcal M(D)\simeq_t\mathcal M(W).
\]
Its evaluation formula is \(x\mapsto x\circ\operatorname{equiv}\).
The theorem `spectrumHomeomorph_naturality` states that the two resulting
continuous maps from \(\mathcal M(D)\) to \(\mathcal M(B)\) agree.
The additional structural formulas identify the maps to \(\mathcal M(A)\)
through this homeomorphism and its inverse.

The denominator is \(1\), so the concatenated tuple generates the unit
ideal automatically. The proof places \(1\) in the actual generating set.
The published scalar rational spectrum theorem therefore identifies the
image of \(\mathcal M(D)\) with its Weierstrass locus.
Transport through `spectrumHomeomorph`, followed by
`combinedLocus_eq_inter`, proves the exact image formula
`range_baseSpectrumMap`:
\[
 \operatorname{range}(\mathcal M(W)\longrightarrow\mathcal M(A))
       =V_s(f)\cap V_t(h).
\]
This proves both containments, using the genuine homeomorphism to lift
points from the scalar quotient.

The structural map of \(W\) is injective because the structural map of
\(D\) is injective and the spectrum homeomorphism respects both.
Continuity, compactness of the source spectrum and the Hausdorff target
then prove `isClosedEmbedding_baseSpectrumMap`.
The definition `intersectionHomeomorph` takes the homeomorphism onto this
proved embedding's range and transports it along the exact range equality:
\[
 \mathcal M(W)\simeq_t V_s(f)\cap V_t(h).
\]
Its projection to \(\mathcal M(A)\) is exactly `baseSpectrumMap`, as the
final simplification theorem records.

## Source and scope

The registered original is Vladimir Berkovich, *Non-Archimedean analytic
spaces* (Trieste lectures, 2009), source ID
`berkovich-2009-trieste-analytic-spaces`, SHA-256
`8cfe4f6f35d5e717ac67c5c8f5ef9ff9185f460bbe5f9a10aeacd84cddeeb2ca`.
Definition 2.1.6(i), printed/PDF page 8, supplies the relative weighted-series
construction. Exercise 2.2.2(i), printed/PDF page 9, gives the denominator-one
equations and positive-radius Weierstrass inequalities; part (iii) supplies
the context of intersections of domains of the same type.

This module is a derived construction for chosen scalar representatives.
The comparison maps, exact quotient norms, spectrum naturality and
intersection homeomorphism are proved using the published concrete
algebras, their evaluation and extensionality, and their spectrum theorem.
They are not attributed to a stronger numbered assertion in the source.
The source's full affinoid-domain universal property in Definition 2.2.1
remains a separate obligation. This unit does not construct a structure
sheaf, an analytic atlas or an etale site.
There is no radius ordering, proper-ideal, nontrivial-valuation or nonempty
coordinate-block hypothesis; both blocks may be empty, and zero quotients
are retained.
