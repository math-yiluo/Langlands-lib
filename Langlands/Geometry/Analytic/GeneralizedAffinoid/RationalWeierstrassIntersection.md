# A Weierstrass quotient over an actual rational quotient

This module constructs an isometric bounded algebra equivalence between a
Weierstrass quotient over a positive-radius rational quotient and a single
rational quotient over the original presented algebra. It identifies the
structural spectrum image with the intersection of the original rational and
Weierstrass inequality loci. The construction uses the actual closed quotient
algebras and their quotient norms.

The namespace is
`Langlands.Analytic.PresentedAffinoidAlgebra.RationalPresentation.RationalWeierstrassIntersection`.
The main definition is `equiv`; the final topological identification is
`intersectionHomeomorph`. The direct imports are the published
`RelativeRationalComparison` and `RationalSpectrum` modules.

## Quotients, coordinates and assumptions

Let `K` be an ultrametric normed field, `A` a
`PresentedAffinoidAlgebra K`, `s` a positive polyradius on `Fin m`, and `t` an
independent positive polyradius on `Fin n`. Let `g`, `f i`, and `h j` be scalar
restricted-series representatives in the original presentation of `A`. Write
`i` for the first quotient's coefficient map. The constructions are

\[
 B=A\{s^{-1}Y\}/\overline{(gY_i-f_i)},\qquad
 W=B\{t^{-1}Z\}/\overline{(Z_j-i(h_j))},
\]

and

\[
 D=A\{(s\mathbin{\mathrm{append}}t)^{-1}U\}/
   \overline{(gU_i-f_i,\ gU_{m+j}-g h_j)}.
\]

Here scalar representatives are mapped to `A` by `presentationMap` wherever
necessary. The notation for each closed ideal means the closure of the ideal
generated by the displayed equations, together with the relations required by
the original presentation in the scalar constructions. It does not mean an
additional assumed quotient algebra or an algebra with an assigned norm.

`firstAlgebra` is the published scalar rational quotient `B`.
`secondTuple` maps `h j` through `presentationMap` and the actual coefficient
map to `B`. `Algebra` is the published intrinsic relative Weierstrass quotient
`W`. `combinedNumerators` is `Fin.append f (fun j => g * h j)`;
`combinedAlgebra` is the scalar rational quotient `D`, with denominator `g`
and radii `s.append t`. The left block is indexed by `Fin.castAdd n`; the right
block is indexed by `Fin.natAdd m`.

The equivalence and the final spectrum identification require `CompleteSpace K`
and

\[
 \operatorname{Ideal.span}(\{g\}\cup\{f_i\mid i\in\operatorname{Fin}(m)\})=\top
 \quad\text{in }A.
\]

This is the published predicate `GeneratesUnitIdeal K A g f`, with each
representative mapped into `A`. It is an explicit hypothesis; it is not a
conclusion from the absence of common spectral zeros. `combined_generates`
proves the same condition for `(g,f,g*h)` by inclusion of the original
generators. No generation assumption on `h` is needed.

`iteratedBase : B → W` is the actual coefficient map of the second quotient;
`base : A → W` is its composite with `A → B`. `leftCoordinate` is the image of
`Y_i` in `W`, and `rightCoordinate` is its newly adjoined `Z_j`.
`leftCoordinate_equation` proves `base(g) * leftCoordinate i = base(f i)`.
`rightCoordinate_eq_base` proves `rightCoordinate j = base(h j)`. The respective
coordinate norms are at most `s i` and `t j`; `norm_iteratedBase_le` and
`norm_base_le` prove that the coefficient maps contract.

`combinedLeft` and `combinedRight` are the two blocks of actual coordinates in
`D`. Their norm bounds are again `s i` and `t j`. The left block satisfies the
original rational equations. Under generation, the denominator is a unit in
`D`, by the published `isUnit_baseMap_denominator` and `combined_generates`.
Cancellation in the right fraction equation then proves
`combinedRight j = (A → D)(h j)`. These preliminary constructions, equations,
bounds and generation facts do not require completeness of `K`.

## Exact inequality locus

`combinedLocus_eq_inter`, also without a completeness assumption, proves under
original generation that

\[
 X((s\mathbin{\mathrm{append}}t)^{-1}(f,g h)/g)
   =X(s^{-1}f/g)\cap X(t^{-1}h).
\]

The left block of the combined inequalities gives membership in the original
rational locus. On that locus, the published `denominator_ne_zero` and
nonnegativity of the actual multiplicative seminorm give a strictly positive
value of `g`. Multiplicativity changes the right block to
`x(g) * x(h j) ≤ t j * x(g)`; cancellation gives the Weierstrass inequality.
Conversely, multiplying each Weierstrass inequality by the nonnegative `x(g)`
gives the right block. The proof splits the finite appended index set and uses
the actual appended radius values. It does not impose a relation between `s`
and `t`.

## Construction of the comparison and exact norm

From this point `K` is complete. The private `scalarEvaluate` composes the
published isometric scalar-to-relative comparison `relativeEquiv` with the
intrinsic convergent `RelativeRationalPresentation.evaluate`. It accepts a
contractive coefficient map, weighted monomial bounds, and the actual equations
`φ(g) * b j = φ(q j)`. The helper's coefficient, fraction, and contraction
lemmas follow from those of evaluation and the exact norm of `relativeEquiv`.
All target algebras used below have the published ultrametric completeness
instances.

`fromCombined : D → W` evaluates `D` at `base` and the concatenation of the two
coordinate blocks of `W`. Its equations follow from the rational left equation
and the identity of the right coordinate with `base(h j)`. This map and its
contraction estimate do not require generation.

`firstMap : B → D` evaluates the original rational quotient at `A → D` and
`combinedLeft`. It also contracts and needs no generation. `toCombined : W → D`
uses intrinsic evaluation with coefficient map `firstMap` and new coordinates
`combinedRight`. Its Weierstrass equations use the cancellation identity above,
so this map and its contraction estimate require original generation.

For each evaluation, coordinate bounds imply weighted monomial bounds by the
published `monomialValue_norm_le_weight`. The empty monomial requires the unit
norm bound `≤ 1`: for the iterated quotient it follows by contraction of
`iteratedBase` applied to one, and for `D` it follows from
`PresentedAffinoidAlgebra.norm_one_le`. Thus no `NormOneClass` or nonzero
quotient assumption is introduced.

The lemmas `fromCombined_base`, `fromCombined_left`, and `fromCombined_right`
identify evaluation on the coefficient map and both blocks. `firstMap_base`
and `firstMap_fraction` give the first quotient's corresponding identities.
`toCombined_iteratedBase`, `toCombined_base`, `toCombined_left`, and
`toCombined_right` give the reverse map's identities.

`fromCombined_comp_firstMap` uses published scalar coefficient/fraction
extensionality to identify the composite `B → D → W` with `iteratedBase`.
Relative coefficient/fraction extensionality then proves
`fromCombined_toCombined`. Scalar coefficient/fraction extensionality on `D`,
split over its appended blocks, proves `toCombined_fromCombined`. Hence
`equiv` is `BoundedAlgEquiv.ofHomInv` on these constructed maps. The lemmas
`equiv_iteratedBase`, `equiv_base`, `equiv_left`, and `equiv_right` state its
structural and coordinate formulas.

`norm_equiv` proves equality of the actual quotient norms: one inequality is
contraction of `toCombined`; the other applies contraction of `fromCombined`
to the proved inverse identity. `norm_equiv_symm` proves the inverse norm
identity, and `isometry_equiv` follows from additive norm preservation. This
proves an isometric equivalence for the specified quotient norms, rather than
only bounded equivalence with an unspecified constant.

## Actual spectra and structural image

`iteratedSpectrumMap : M(W) → M(B)`,
`firstSpectrumMap : M(D) → M(B)`, and
`baseSpectrumMap : M(W) → M(A)` are the genuine continuous pullbacks of the
constructed bounded coefficient maps on bounded multiplicative seminorms.
`baseSpectrumMap` therefore restricts a seminorm along the actual composite
`A → B → W`.

`spectrumHomeomorph : M(D) ≃ₜ M(W)` is induced contravariantly by `equiv`.
`spectrumHomeomorph_apply` states that it evaluates a point `x` at
`equiv a`. `spectrumHomeomorph_naturality` identifies its composite with
`iteratedSpectrumMap` as `firstSpectrumMap`. `baseSpectrumMap_homeomorph` and
`spectrumMap_homeomorph_symm` identify the two structural maps to `M(A)` in
both directions.

Under original generation, the published scalar `range_spectrumMap`, applied
to `D` using `combined_generates`, identifies its structural image with the
combined locus. `combinedLocus_eq_inter` identifies that locus with the desired
intersection. The two branches of `range_baseSpectrumMap` transport points
through `spectrumHomeomorph` and its inverse, proving the exact equality

\[
 \operatorname{range}(M(W)\longrightarrow M(A))
   =\operatorname{locus}(K,A,s,g,f)
       \cap\operatorname{weierstrassLocus}(K,A,t,h).
\]

`isClosedEmbedding_baseSpectrumMap` transports injectivity from the published
scalar `spectrumMap_injective`; continuity, compactness of the source spectrum
and the Hausdorff target imply a closed embedding. `intersectionHomeomorph`
is its homeomorphism onto the range, followed by the set equality above. Its
forward projection is exactly `baseSpectrumMap`, as stated by
`intersectionHomeomorph_apply_coe`. Empty spectra are covered by the same
argument.

## Original source and scope

The registered source is Vladimir G. Berkovich, *Non-archimedean analytic
spaces* (Trieste lectures, 2009), literature identifier
`berkovich-2009-trieste-analytic-spaces`, retained PDF SHA-256
`8cfe4f6f35d5e717ac67c5c8f5ef9ff9185f460bbe5f9a10aeacd84cddeeb2ca`.
Definition 2.1.6(i), printed/PDF page 8, gives the relative weighted restricted
series and weighted maximum norm. Exercise 2.2.2(i), printed/PDF page 9, gives
the positive-radius denominator-one Weierstrass equations; part (ii) gives
common-denominator rational equations and inequalities. Part (iii) supplies the
context of intersections of domains of the same type. The unit-ideal condition
used here is explicit in Lean, rather than identified with the source's
no-common-zero condition by an unproved equivalence.

The specific common-denominator tuple `(f,g*h)`, its actual comparison maps,
both inverse identities, exact norm equality, and structural spectrum
intersection are internal derivations from the published concrete quotient,
evaluation, scalar-relative comparison, and spectrum results. They are not
attributed to a numbered source theorem. Closure of generated ideals is the
published construction convention used here. The early constructions over a
possibly incomplete field are an internal extension; completeness is imposed
for the equivalence and its final spectrum homeomorphism.

The result allows either coordinate block to be empty, a top presentation ideal,
a zero quotient, and a trivial valuation; it assumes no ordering between the
positive radii. It is a construction for chosen presentations. It does not
prove the full affinoid-domain factorization property of Definition 2.2.1,
printed/PDF page 9, for all bounded maps with the specified spectral image.
Analytic structure sheaves, atlases, analytic morphisms, etale and quasi-etale
sites, equivariant sites and the Harris tower remain separate prerequisites.
