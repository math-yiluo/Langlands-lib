# Relative weighted series with additional equations: comparison target

Author: Worker 56.
Documentation correction (P3-062): Worker 1057.

Let `K` be a complete normed field with an ultrametric norm, let
`A : PresentedAffinoidAlgebra K`, let `m : ℕ`, and let
`s : PositivePolyradius (Fin m)`. Let `L` be any set of scalar weighted
restricted series in `GeneralizedTateAlgebra (A.polyradius.append s) K`.
Write `ρ = Adjoin.relativeSeriesEvaluation K A s` for the published
evaluation that sends old variables to constant series with their values
in `A` and new variables to the corresponding relative series variables.
All new declarations are in
`Langlands.Analytic.PresentedAffinoidAlgebra.Adjoin`.

## Actual algebras and natural map

`relativeEquationRelations K A s L` is the image set `ρ '' L`.
`relativeEquationAlgebra K A s L` abbreviates the published actual quotient

\[
Q=A\{s^{-1}T\}/\overline{(\rho(L))}
\]

with its quotient norm: in Lean this is
`RelativeRelations.QuotientAlgebra K A s (relativeEquationRelations K A s L)`,
whose ideal is the closure of the ideal span of the image set.
Its normed commutative ring, normed `K`-algebra, coefficient `A`-algebra,
scalar tower, ultrametric and complete structures are inherited from that
quotient. No new carrier or norm is chosen.

`relativeEquationEvaluation K A s L` is the bounded `K`-algebra map `q ∘ ρ`,
where `q` is `RelativeRelations.quotientMap` for this image set.
`relativeEquationEvaluation_apply` records this formula.
`norm_relativeEquationEvaluation_le` proves its contraction by composing
the published contraction estimates for `q` and `ρ`.

`relativeEquationEvaluation_appendLeft` says that evaluation on the old
scalar variable block is the composite of `presentationMap K A` with
`RelativeRelations.baseMap` into `Q`. The proof applies the published
`relativeSeriesEvaluation_appendLeft` before quotienting.
`relativeEquationEvaluation_equation` says that every `f ∈ L` maps to
zero: `ρ f` belongs to the defining image set and is killed by `q`.
`relativeEquationEvaluation_relations` combines this with vanishing on
the included old ideal, using the exact kernel of `presentationMap K A`.
Thus it annihilates the actual set `Adjoin.relations K A s L`, which is
the union of the included old ideal and `L`.

`relativeEquationMap K A s L` is constructed by the published scalar
`descendRelations` applied to this vanishing. Its source is the actual
scalar presentation `Adjoin.algebra K A s L`, obtained by quotienting the
enlarged scalar series algebra by the closure of the ideal generated by
that union. Its target is `Q`.

The following intrinsic laws are proved here:

- `relativeEquationMap_presentationMap` gives the representative formula
  `relativeEquationMap (presentationMap f) = q (ρ f)`, definitionally.
  `relativeEquationMap_comp_presentationMap` expresses it as an equality
  of bounded maps.
- `norm_relativeEquationMap_le` gives contraction for the actual two
  quotient norms. It applies `norm_descendRelations_le` with bound `1`
  to the contraction of `q ∘ ρ`.
- `relativeEquationMap_baseMap` sends the scalar adjunction's coefficient
  map to the relative quotient's coefficient map. The proof uses a scalar
  representative of each coefficient and the old-block evaluation law.
- `relativeEquationMap_newCoordinate` sends each new scalar adjunction
  coordinate to the corresponding relative quotient coordinate. This
  follows from the published evaluation formula on the new variable block.

## The comparison proposition

`RelativeEquationComparisonStatement` is a `Prop`-valued definition
universally quantifying over the displayed `K`, `A`, `m`, `s`, and `L`.
It asserts all three conclusions:

1. `relativeEquationMap K A s L` is surjective.
2. That same natural map is an isometry for the actual quotient norms.
3. For every enlarged scalar series `f`,
   `relativeEquationEvaluation K A s L f = 0` if and only if
   `f ∈ (Adjoin.algebra K A s L).ideal`.

The final ideal is exactly the closure of the scalar ideal generated by
the included old ideal and the extra equations. This file proves the map
construction and intrinsic laws above and states the three comparison
conclusions; it does not prove them.

All radii are arbitrary positive real numbers. Both variable blocks may
be empty, the valuation may be trivial, and the old or new relation ideal
may be the whole ring, so zero quotients are included. No coefficient
lifting, inverse comparison or output equivalence is supplied as an input.
This statement does not establish arbitrary presentation independence,
analytic domains, sheaves, étale sites or the Harris tower construction.

## Status

The proposition is proved by the published theorem
`Langlands.Analytic.PresentedAffinoidAlgebra.Adjoin.relativeEquationComparison`
in `Langlands.Geometry.Analytic.GeneralizedAffinoid.RelativeEquationComparison`.
The proof keeps every case above. It adds no nontrivial-valuation,
proper-ideal, nonempty-block or radius hypothesis. The same module constructs
the reverse bounded map `relativeEquationInverse`, which is contractive
(`norm_relativeEquationInverse_le`). It is a two-sided inverse of
`relativeEquationMap` (`relativeEquationInverse_map` and
`relativeEquationMap_inverse`). These are results of that proof module, not
of this statement file. Neither the statement nor its proof establishes
presentation independence, analytic domains, sheaves, étale sites or the
Harris tower construction.

## Source and provenance

The original relative series convention is Berkovich, *Non-Archimedean
analytic spaces*, Trieste lectures (2009), Definition 2.1.6(i), printed/PDF
page 8: relative weighted restricted series over an affinoid algebra,
with weighted coefficient decay and the weighted Gauss norm.
Part (ii) defines an affinoid algebra over `A` using an admissible
epimorphism from such a series algebra. The source passage was read
directly. The inherited finite-variable cofinite decay convention agrees
with decay as total degree tends to infinity.

The actual image equations, closed quotient, natural descended map,
intrinsic laws and precise isometric comparison target here are internal
derived infrastructure, rather than a quotation of a numbered theorem
in that source. They use the published `Adjoin`, `Relations`,
`RelativeSeriesMap` and `RelativeRelations` constructions by Lean name.
The fixed quotient norms are part of the target; no claim that every
Banach norm chosen in the literature is equal to these norms is made.
