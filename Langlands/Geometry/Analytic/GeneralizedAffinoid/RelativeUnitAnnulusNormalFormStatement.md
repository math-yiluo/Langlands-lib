# A Laurent coefficient normal form for the actual relative unit annulus

Authors: Worker 105 and Worker 1182. Worker 105 wrote the proposed
Lean/Markdown pair as a missing prerequisite of the scalar binary Laurent
argument; Worker 1182 adapted it for publication. The file states a
proposition to prove. It proves nothing and adds no assumption to the
binary Laurent gluing target.

## Exact scope and carrier

`Langlands.Analytic.PresentedAffinoidAlgebra.RelativeUnitAnnulus.NormalFormStatement.{u}`
is a proposition-valued definition. All of its parameters are quantified
inside it. It is not a theorem, and nothing uses it as an assumption. Let
`K : Type u` have its given `NormedField`, `IsUltrametricDist` and
`CompleteSpace` instances, and let `A : PresentedAffinoidAlgebra K` be
arbitrary. The statement uses the published carrier

\[
U=\mathrm{RelativeUnitAnnulus.Algebra}\ K\ A
 = A\langle X,Y\rangle/
   \overline{\operatorname{span}\{XY-1\}},
\]

that is, relative two-variable unit-radius series over `A` (Gauss norm equal
to the supremum of the coefficient norms) modulo the topological closure of
the ideal generated by `X₀ * X₁ - 1`. It keeps its original closed-ideal
quotient norm. Its coefficient map and coordinates are exactly the published
`baseMap K A`, `annulusX K A` and `annulusY K A`, written below as
\(b,x,y\). No equivalent presentation or new norm is substituted. Inside the
namespace, `Algebra K A` means `RelativeUnitAnnulus.Algebra K A`, not
Mathlib's `Algebra` class.

The proposition asserts that a `K`-linear isometric equivalence exists:

\[
e:U \simeq_{\!K}^{\mathrm{isom}} C_0(\mathbb Z,A).
\]

The right side is Mathlib's `ZeroAtInftyContinuousMap ℤ A`, written
`C₀(ℤ, A)`. The integers have their usual discrete topology, and the space
has its inherited coefficient module structure and supremum norm. Vanishing
at infinity means tending to zero along the cocompact filter. On the
discrete integers this is the cofinite filter, so the coefficients tend to
zero in both integer directions. Surjectivity, injectivity and preservation
of the norm are part of `≃ₗᵢ[K]`. They are conclusions of this proposition
and remain to be proved.

There is no nontrivial-valuation, strictness, Noetherianity, reducedness,
nonzero-base, proper-ideal or ideal-closedness hypothesis, and no exactness
or gluing hypothesis. Zero and nonreduced presented bases are included. The
completeness of `K` gives the published completeness instances for `A` and
for the annulus. It is also what the published `positive` and `negative`
evaluation maps require. All field, base and quotient norms are the given
ones.

## The six required coefficient laws

The same equivalence `e` must satisfy every law below.

1. For `a : A`, `i j : ℕ` and `n : ℤ`,
   \[
   e(b(a)x^i y^j)(n)=
   \begin{cases}a& n=(i:\mathbb Z)-(j:\mathbb Z),\\0&\text{otherwise}.
   \end{cases}
   \]
   The powers have natural exponents; their difference is taken in the
   integers.
2. For `z : U` and `n : ℤ`, multiplication by the actual first coordinate
   shifts the coefficients:
   \(e(xz)(n)=e(z)(n-1)\).
3. Multiplication by the actual inverse coordinate shifts them the other
   way:
   \(e(yz)(n)=e(z)(n+1)\).
4. For `a : A`, `z : U` and `n : ℤ`, the actual coefficient map acts by
   multiplying every coefficient:
   \(e(b(a)z)(n)=a\,e(z)(n)\).
5. For every actual relative one-variable series
   `h : GeneralizedTateAlgebra (PositivePolyradius.unit (Fin 1)) A`,
   `positive K A h` has the coefficients of `h` in nonnegative degrees
   and zero coefficients in negative degrees:
   \[
   e(\mathrm{positive}(h))(n)=
   \begin{cases}
   [T^{\,n.\mathrm{toNat}}]h & 0\le n,\\
   0 & n<0 .
   \end{cases}
   \]
6. For the same type of `h`, `negative K A h` has the coefficients of
   `h` in nonpositive degrees and zero coefficients in positive degrees:
   \[
   e(\mathrm{negative}(h))(n)=
   \begin{cases}
   [T^{\,(-n).\mathrm{toNat}}]h & n\le 0,\\
   0 & 0<n .
   \end{cases}
   \]

In the last two laws the coefficient \([T^m]h\) is, in Lean, exactly
`MvPowerSeries.coeff (Finsupp.single (0 : Fin 1) m)
(GeneralizedTateAlgebra.series (PositivePolyradius.unit (Fin 1)) A h)`.
Both maps keep the degree-zero coefficient of their input.

The structure asserted is a linear isometric equivalence, not an algebra
isomorphism. Mathlib's multiplication on `C₀(ℤ, A)` is pointwise, and the
statement does not identify it with multiplication in the annulus. The
multiplicative content of the statement is laws 1–4, which involve the
displayed annulus elements.

The unapplied declaration has type `Prop`; `lean_check` reports
`statement_returns_prop: true`. The module imports the published
`RelativeUnitAnnulus` module and the Mathlib modules for linear isometric
equivalences, zero-at-infinity continuous maps and the topology of the
integers. It changes no imported file.

## Provenance and intended proof boundary

The source context is Piotr Achinger, *Introduction to non-Archimedean
Geometry*, lecture notes, November 22, 2020, registered as
`achinger-2020-non-archimedean-geometry-nov22`, SHA256
`666cc6a90efbfc05f5fa0156044a259beaabdf989c45b633542e1cd2cdc0ec95`.
On printed p.6 the source fixes a complete, nontrivially valued
non-Archimedean field. In the proof of Proposition 7.1.1 on printed p.64 it
works with the auxiliary algebra
`A⟨X,X⁻¹⟩ = A⟨X,Y⟩/(XY-1)`, writes its elements as Laurent series
\(\sum_{n\in\mathbb Z}c_nX^n\), and computes the images of the positive
series \(\sum_{n\ge0}a_nX^n\) and the negative series
\(\sum_{n\le0}b_nX^n\) coefficientwise. On 2026-10-31 no errata note for
this source was found in the project literature directory or the public
Library notes, and its literature index entry records none. Two known slips
on p.64, discussed in `BinaryLaurentGluingStatement.md`, do not concern this
coefficient description: the printed proposition omits the words "is
exact", and `g₋₁` in the surjectivity paragraph should be the element `g₋`
defined just before it.

This statement is an internal derived interface for the Library's
closed-ideal quotient. The source writes the quotient by the generated ideal
\((XY-1)\) and its affinoid algebras are taken over a nontrivially valued
field; the Library's annulus uses the closure of the generated ideal over an
arbitrary presented base, and this statement does not claim that the
generated ideal is closed. The precise isometry onto `C₀(ℤ, A)` and the six
coefficient laws are not a theorem stated separately by Achinger, and they
are not attributed to the source.

The expected proof sends a two-variable monomial \(X^iY^j\) to the integer
degree \(i-j\) and sums the coefficients along each diagonal. Completeness
and the ultrametric inequality make these sums converge, and the result is
a contractive map to `C₀(ℤ, A)` that kills `XY - 1` and hence the closure of
the ideal it generates. The inverse puts the nonnegative coefficients on the
`X`-axis and the strictly negative ones on the `Y`-axis. Polynomial density
and reduction of monomials should identify the kernel with the closure of
the generated ideal, and the norm-preserving section then gives the isometry
for the existing quotient norm. This is a proposed route only. No
diagonal-sum map, kernel equality, rearrangement of series, section or norm
estimate is proved by this file. The absence of a Noetherian or
nontrivial-valuation hypothesis reflects this direct argument. It does not
assume that an arbitrary ideal is closed.

After a separate proof, the shift and coefficient laws can be combined with
the separately scoped bilateral recurrence theorem in Noetherian Banach
algebras. That later argument concerns multiplication by
`x - baseMap K A f`; neither it nor the injectivity of that multiplication
is established here. No principal-relation comparison, identification of the
binary overlap with another quotient, Laurent exact sequence or gluing
theorem is proved or assumed.
