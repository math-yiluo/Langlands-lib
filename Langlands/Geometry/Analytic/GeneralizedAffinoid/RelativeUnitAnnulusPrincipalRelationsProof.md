context: closedness from Noetherianity; injectivity of multiplication by
`x - b(f)` from the Laurent normal form and the bilateral recurrence

## What is proved

The module proves

```
theorem principal_relations : PrincipalRelationsStatement.{u}
```

in namespace `Langlands.Analytic.PresentedAffinoidAlgebra.RelativeUnitAnnulus`.
The type of the unapplied theorem is exactly the published proposition
`PrincipalRelationsStatement.{u}` from
`Langlands.Geometry.Analytic.GeneralizedAffinoid.RelativeUnitAnnulusPrincipalRelationsStatement`.
The theorem has no parameters or hypotheses of its own. The statement
module and everything it imports are used unchanged.

The notation follows the statement's Markdown. `K` carries its given
`NormedField`, `IsUltrametricDist` and `CompleteSpace` instances and comes
with a witness `hK : ∃ c : K, 1 < ‖c‖`. `P` is a presented strict affinoid
algebra and `A = ofStrict K P`. `S` is the unit-radius one-variable algebra
`GeneralizedTateAlgebra (PositivePolyradius.unit (Fin 1)) A`, with
coordinate `T` and constant map `C`. `U = Algebra K A` is the actual annulus
quotient by the closure of the ideal generated by `X₀X₁ - 1`, with its
quotient norm. `b`, `x` and `y` are `baseMap K A`, `annulusX K A` and
`annulusY K A`. The ideals are `J₊ = (T - C(f))` and `J₋ = (C(f)T - 1)`
in `S`, and `J = (x - b(f))` in `U`. All three are ordinary `Ideal.span`s.

The proof uses these published results by their Lean names:

  `positive_X`, `negative_X`, `positive_constant`, `negative_constant`,

## Structure

The main argument is the theorem

```
theorem principal_relations_of_normalForm (hNF : NormalFormStatement.{u}) :
    PrincipalRelationsStatement.{u}
```

All the work happens there. The final theorem is
`principal_relations_of_normalForm normal_form`, so the published proof
discharges the hypothesis `hNF`. The normal form is used only through the
six laws its statement asserts. The proof uses none of the internal helper
declarations of the normal-form proof.

The helper lemmas sit in the sub-namespace
`RelativeUnitAnnulus.PrincipalRelations`. They are stated for an arbitrary
presented base `A : PresentedAffinoidAlgebra K` where possible. Only the
closedness lemmas and the final assembly specialise to `A = ofStrict K P`.

## Closedness of `J₊` and `J₋`

The published theorem `strict_relative_series_closed K hK P m I` says that
every ideal `I` of `GeneralizedTateAlgebra (PositivePolyradius.unit (Fin m)) (ofStrict K P)`
is closed. With `m = 1` this is exactly `S`, so the theorem applies to
`I = J₊` and `I = J₋`. The topology is the norm topology of the published
`NormedCommRing` instance on `S`, which is the topology the statement uses.
That published proof builds the `NontriviallyNormedField` structure from the
given `NormedField` and the witness. It does not change the field's norm.

## Closedness of `J`

`isClosed_annulus_ideal` shows that every ideal of `U` is closed. Its steps:

1. The two-variable algebra
   `GeneralizedTateAlgebra (PositivePolyradius.unit (Fin 2)) (ofStrict K P)`
   is Noetherian (`strict_relative_series_noetherian K P 2`).
2. The published projection `quotientMap K A` onto `U` is surjective
   (`quotientMap_surjective`). Its underlying ring homomorphism therefore
   makes `U` Noetherian, by Mathlib's `isNoetherianRing_of_surjective`.
3. The proof adds a local `NontriviallyNormedField K` instance,
   `{ (inferInstance : NormedField K) with non_trivial := hK }`. This
   instance keeps the given norm and field operations and only records
   the witness.
4. `U` is a complete normed commutative ring and a normed `K`-algebra under
   the published instances of the closed-ideal quotient. Its norm is the
   original quotient norm. So `isClosed_ideal_of_noetherian_banach`
   applies to every ideal of `U`, and `J` in particular is closed.

No closedness of the ordinary ideal generated by `X₀X₁ - 1` is used. The
carrier of `U` remains the quotient by its closure.

## The difference map lands in `J`

`positive` and `negative` are bounded `K`-algebra homomorphisms. They send
`T` to `x` and to `y`, and `C(a)` to `b(a)`. Every element of `J₊` has the
form `r(T - C(f))` with `r ∈ S`, and every element of `J₋` has the form
`s(C(f)T - 1)` (`Ideal.mem_span_singleton'`). The proof shows

\[
\mathrm{positive}(r(T-C(f)))=\mathrm{positive}(r)\,(x-b(f))
\]

(`positive_mul_upper`) and

\[
\mathrm{negative}(s(C(f)T-1))=\mathrm{negative}(s)\,(b(f)y-1)
=-\bigl(y\,\mathrm{negative}(s)\bigr)(x-b(f)).
\]

(`negative_mul_lower`). The last equality is a `linear_combination` of the
published identity `xy = 1` (`annulusX_mul_annulusY`). Therefore

\[
\mathrm{positive}(p)-\mathrm{negative}(q)
=\bigl(\mathrm{positive}(r)+y\,\mathrm{negative}(s)\bigr)(x-b(f))\in J .
\]

So the formula of the statement defines a map
`L : (J₊ × J₋) →ₗ[K] J`. It is additive and `K`-linear because `positive`
and `negative` are. The `K`-module structures are those the ideal
subtypes inherit from `S` and `U`.

## Consequences of the normal form

Fix the equivalence `e : U ≃ₗᵢ[K] C₀(ℤ, A)` given by `hNF K A`. The proof
uses its laws `e(xz)(n) = e(z)(n-1)`, `e(yz)(n) = e(z)(n+1)`,
`e(b(a)z)(n) = a·e(z)(n)`, and the positive and negative coefficient
formulas. In those formulas, `e(positive(h))(n)` is the `T^n` coefficient
of `h` when `n ≥ 0` and `0` otherwise, and `e(negative(h))(n)` is the
`T^(-n)` coefficient when `n ≤ 0` and `0` otherwise. Pointwise
multiplication in `C₀(ℤ, A)` is never used.

**Multiplication by `x - b(f)` is injective** (`eq_zero_of_mul_eq_zero`,
for any base with `[IsNoetherianRing A]`). Suppose `z(x - b(f)) = 0` and
put `c(n) = e(z)(n)`. The shift and coefficient laws give
`c(n-1) = f·c(n)` for every `n ∈ ℤ`. Since `e(z)` vanishes at infinity on
the discrete space `ℤ`, and the cocompact filter there is the cofinite
filter (`cocompact_eq_cofinite`), `c` tends to `0` along the cofinite
filter. With the same local `NontriviallyNormedField K` instance as above,
`bilateral_recurrence_eq_zero K f c` gives `c = 0`. That theorem needs a
complete normed `K`-algebra that is a Noetherian ring. Hence `e(z) = 0`
and `z = 0`. The recurrence theorem allows nilpotent `f` and nonreduced
`A`, so no reducedness or spectrum-point argument is needed.

For `A = ofStrict K P`, Noetherianity comes from the published instance
on the strict algebra `P`, transported along the `K`-algebra equivalence
`strictAlgEquiv K P : ofStrict K P ≃ₐ[K] P`
(`isNoetherianRing_ofStrict`).

**The two parts determine `r` and `s`**
(`eq_zero_of_positive_add_eq_zero`). Suppose
`positive(r) + y·negative(s) = 0`. Evaluating `e` at `n` gives
`e(positive(r))(n) + e(negative(s))(n+1) = 0`. At `n = k ≥ 0` the second
term vanishes because `k + 1 > 0`, so the `T^k` coefficient of `r` is
zero. At `n = -k-1 < 0` the first term vanishes, and the second is the
`T^k` coefficient of `s`. A one-variable series with all coefficients
zero is zero (`eq_zero_of_coeff`: every multi-index on `Fin 1` is
`Finsupp.single 0 k`). Hence `r = s = 0`.

**Splitting** (`exists_positive_add`). Let `w ∈ U` and `c = e(w)`. The
proof defines `upperSeries c`, the series with `T^k` coefficient `c(k)`,
and `lowerSeries c`, the series with `T^k` coefficient `c(-k-1)`. Both
coefficient families tend to zero cofinitely, because `c` does and the
index maps `k ↦ k` and `k ↦ -k-1` are injective. The weight of the unit
polyradius is `1`, so this is exactly the membership condition of `S`
(`seriesOf`). The coefficient formulas then give
`e(positive(upperSeries c) + y·negative(lowerSeries c))(n) = c(n)` for
every `n`: the first summand gives the values for `n ≥ 0` and the second
those for `n < 0`. By injectivity of `e`,
`w = positive(r) + y·negative(s)` with `r = upperSeries c` and
`s = lowerSeries c`.

## Injectivity and surjectivity of the difference map

*Injectivity.* Suppose `L(p, q) = 0` with `p = r(T - C(f))` and
`q = s(C(f)T - 1)`. By the identity above,
`(positive(r) + y·negative(s))(x - b(f)) = 0`. Multiplication by
`x - b(f)` is injective, so `positive(r) + y·negative(s) = 0`. Hence
`r = s = 0`, and therefore `p = q = 0`. The kernel of `L` is zero.

*Surjectivity.* An element of `J` has the form `w(x - b(f))`
(`Ideal.mem_span_singleton'`). Split `w = positive(r) + y·negative(s)` and
take `p = r(T - C(f)) ∈ J₊` and `q = s(C(f)T - 1) ∈ J₋`. By the identity
above, `L(p, q) = w(x - b(f))`.

The proof then sets `β = LinearEquiv.ofBijective L`. For all `p` and `q`,
the underlying annulus element of `β (p, q)` is
`positive K A p - negative K A q` by definition (`rfl`). This is the
fourth conclusion of the statement (`exists_beta`).

## Assembly and scope

`principal_relations_of_normalForm` introduces `K`, its three instances,
`hK`, `P` and `f`. It returns the four conclusions in the order of the
statement: `strict_relative_series_closed K hK P 1` twice,
`isClosed_annulus_ideal`, and `exists_beta`.

The proof covers zero and nonreduced strict bases, nilpotent or zero `f`,
and any `‖f‖`. None of these cases is treated separately. The only use of
the witness `hK` is to build the nontrivially normed field instance
described above. The proof adds no closedness, normal-form, injectivity,
Noetherianity, exactness, quotient-comparison or gluing premise. The
normal form, Noetherianity and the recurrence theorem are imported from
their published proofs. `β` is only a `K`-linear equivalence. No
multiplicativity, isometry or inverse norm estimate is proved. The
overlap comparison, the surjectivity of the upper/lower difference map,
and the final scalar gluing remain separate obligations.

`lean_check` inspects `principal_relations` as a theorem at the single
universe level `u`, using only the axioms `propext`, `Classical.choice`
and `Quot.sound`.

## Provenance

This proof proves an internal derived statement. The statement's Markdown
discusses the source: Achinger, *Introduction to non-Archimedean
Geometry* (November 22, 2020), Proposition 7.1.1 on printed p.64. It also
explains why the three closedness conclusions and the `K`-linear
formulation go beyond the source. That proposition asserts the
isomorphism `β` of principal ideals with the sign convention
`g = g₊ - g₋` that the statement uses. Printed p.64 was reread for this
proof on 2026-11-01 (SHA256
`666cc6a90efbfc05f5fa0156044a259beaabdf989c45b633542e1cd2cdc0ec95`).
On that date the project literature index entry recorded no errata for
this source. The slips on p.64 noted in the statement's Markdown do not
affect this proof.

The surjectivity step follows the source. For `h = Σ cₙXⁿ`, the source
takes `g₊ = (X - f)Σ_{n≥0} cₙXⁿ` and
`g₋ = (fX⁻¹ - 1)Σ_{n≤0} c_{n-1}Xⁿ`. Here
`r = upperSeries c` has `T^k` coefficient `c(k)`, and `s = lowerSeries c`
has `T^k` coefficient `c(-k-1)`. Under `negative`, which sends `T` to
`y = X⁻¹`, these are the same two series.

The implemented route differs from the source in two places. First,
injectivity is not derived from the source's one-sentence argument about
kernel elements of the form `(a, a)`. It is derived from the normal form
together with the published bilateral recurrence theorem, which covers
bases with nilpotents. Second, closedness of the ordinary ideals is
proved from Noetherianity of the actual series algebras and of the
annulus quotient, via the closed-ideal theorem for Noetherian Banach
algebras. The source's quotients are by ordinary ideals, and it states no
such step. No source statement is used as an assumption.
