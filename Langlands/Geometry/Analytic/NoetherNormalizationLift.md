# Normalization with a proved lift into the original Tate algebra

Author: Worker 56.

Let K be a complete ultrametric normed field and let a bounded K-algebra
map from the n-variable Tate algebra into a nontrivial normed commutative
K-algebra B be finite as a ring homomorphism. The theorem constructs
m ≤ n and a bounded K-algebra map from the m-variable Tate algebra into
the original n-variable algebra. This ambient map preserves the Gauss norm
exactly, and its composite with the given map is finite and injective.
B need not be complete, ultrametric, or an affinoid quotient; its scalar
structure is an algebra structure, without a normed-algebra premise.
The norm on K may be trivial.

At each noninjective step, a nonzero kernel element is transformed by the
constructed isometric coordinate automorphism into a distinguished series. The
smaller-variable inclusion is the inverse coordinate automorphism composed
with the actual coefficient inclusion from regrouping. Both factors preserve
norms, so their composite does too. The proved distinguished-series
finite-module theorem gives finiteness of the composite target map. Induction
composes these actual ambient embeddings; the injective and zero-variable
cases use the identity.

This retained map is needed when the target is a further quotient: a
polynomial relation over the normalizing Tate algebra can be mapped back into
the original algebra without increasing the coefficient norms. No bounded lift
is assumed, and the quotient map need not admit an algebra section.

The construction is the ambient-coordinate part of Proposition 2.4.1, page 6,
in the retained Berkovich-course notes. It returns the required embedded
smaller Tate algebra; it does not additionally package a full list of ambient
chart coordinates.
