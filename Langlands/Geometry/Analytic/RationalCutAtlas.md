# Rational cuts of an atlas with a greatest chart

`RationalCutAtlas.atlas a t ht` constructs an actual affinoid atlas on the same topological space as `a`. The input `t` is an original chart and `ht` says that every original chart range is contained in its range. The coefficient field is complete and nonarchimedean, as required by the input atlas. No condition on the resulting atlas, rational-cut representability, or restriction maps is assumed.

For each original index `i` and actual positive-radius `RationalChart d` over the greatest-chart algebra, `cut a t i d` is the literal ambient subset

```
range (a.chart i) ∩ a.chart t '' d.locus.
```

`Index a t` is the subtype of subsets of the ambient space which have such a presentation. Thus its universe is that of the ambient space, independently of the universe of the old indices. `Index.original` and `Index.rational` choose one witnessing pair for a literal locus. `Index.cut_original_rational` records exactly what they present. `family` is the range of these literal subsets. `Index.ofCut`, `Index.ofOriginal` and `Index.ofRational` supply indices for arbitrary cuts, original chart loci and rational loci of the greatest chart. The last two range assertions are proved in `atlas_ofOriginal_range` and `atlas_ofRational_range`; the chosen quotient presentations need not be definitionally the original algebras.

The topological argument first proves `greatest_chart_surjective`: the original net covers the space and every member is contained in the greatest chart. The original embedded chart therefore gives `greatestChartHomeomorph` onto the entire ambient space. `family_eq_intersectionFamily` identifies the cut family with the intersection family of the original chart net and the rational net transported by this actual homeomorphism. The published `IsNet.imageFamily` and `IsNet.intersectionFamily` prove `isNet_family`, including the relative quasinet condition on all intersections. This argument applies also when the spectrum and ambient space are empty.

The algebras are constructed explicitly. `pullbackChart a t ht i d` pulls the rational presentation back along the original bounded restriction from the greatest-chart algebra to the `i`th algebra. The proved rational pullback locus formula identifies its locus with the inverse image of `d.locus`. Its quotient is a finite positive-polyradius presented affinoid algebra with a full domain representation. `cutDomain` extends that representation along the original chart containment. It retains the actual rational quotient algebra; its structural map to the common greatest-chart algebra is the composite of the quotient structural map and the original restriction. `chart_image_cutDomain_locus` proves that this domain represents exactly the desired ambient cut. `cutDomain_spectrumMap` identifies the structural spectrum map with the two actual spectrum maps composed, and `isEmbedding_cutDomain_spectrumMap` proves embedding by composing their proved embeddings.

For a literal index, `Index.domain` selects the preceding constructed domain for its witnessing pair. `Index.chart_image_domain_locus` and `Index.domain_locus_eq_preimage` identify its ambient and greatest-chart-coordinate loci exactly. `chart` composes its structural spectrum map with the original greatest-chart embedding. `range_chart`, `isEmbedding_chart` and `chartDomain_injective` prove that these are embedded charts indexed without duplicate ranges. The ambient locally Hausdorff property is inherited from the original atlas; each chart spectrum is already compact Hausdorff.

For every containment of two new ambient chart ranges, `domain_locus_subset` pulls it back through the injective greatest-chart map. `inclusion` uses the full domain universal property to construct an actual bounded restriction between the selected quotient algebras, and `restriction` retains that map. `restriction_comp_baseMap` is its equation over the common base, and `restriction_chart` is the resulting equality on actual chart points. To prove `restriction_universal`, compose an arbitrary bounded affinoid test map with the larger domain's structural map. The smaller domain's full representation supplies its unique factor. The larger domain's full representation then cancels its structural map to prove equality of the entire bounded maps. Uniqueness of the factor follows by the smaller representation. The argument tests on every presented positive-polyradius affinoid, including nonstrict ones. It uses the full representation universal properties rather than inferring algebra-map equality from equality on spectrum points.

The domain inclusion hom types are subsingletons by their full universal properties. Comparing the constructed inclusions with identity and composite inclusions proves `restriction_id` and `restriction_comp`. `atlas` assembles these proved obligations, and `atlas_chartFamily` records precisely the resulting family.

The original algebras are retained up to actual bounded comparison. `original_domain_locus` identifies the full domain associated to an original chart containment with the selected full domain at that original ambient locus. `originalAlgebraEquiv` constructs their bounded algebra equivalence from the full representations in both directions. `originalAlgebraEquiv_comp_restriction` proves its equation over the greatest-chart algebra. `originalSpectrumHomeomorph` is the induced homeomorphism of actual spectra, and `originalSpectrumHomeomorph_chart` proves that it commutes with the original and new maps to the ambient space. No isometric comparison or equality of quotient presentations is asserted.

The source is Berkovich, *Étale cohomology for non-Archimedean analytic spaces* (1993), section 1.2, Definition 1.2.3 and Remark 1.2.4(i), printed p.16 / PDF p.13, for affinoid atlases and bounded restrictions. Proposition 1.2.6, printed pp.17–18 / PDF pp.14–15, motivates extending an atlas by domains. This is an internal construction derived from the existing full rational-domain representation, coefficient pullback, original-chart domain extension, common-base domain comparison and net operations. Its proof route uses the common greatest-chart algebra. Its scope is an enlargement by these rational cuts under the explicit greatest-original-chart hypothesis. The unrestricted saturation of Proposition 1.2.6, extension across arbitrary containing charts, and general Tate gluing remain separate obligations; this module does not prove them.
