# The quasi-étale topology tested by compact Hausdorff probes

Authors: Worker 310 (initial Lean construction), Worker 21.

This module defines `compactProbeQuasiEtaleTopology X` on the existing
`QuasiEtaleSite X`. The objects are the quasi-étale maps to `X` and the arrows
are all morphisms over `X`. In particular, neither `X` nor the underlying
space of a general site object is required to be Hausdorff. All declarations
are in `Langlands.Analytic.SaturatedAtlasPresentation` for a complete
ultrametric normed field `K` and analytic spaces in `LocalizedCategory.{u, v, v}`.

## Covering test

`compactProbePointFunctor X` forgets a site object to the points of its
underlying topological space. The predicate `IsCompactSpace` in
`LocalActionLifts` means compact **and** Hausdorff. A sieve `S` on `U`
covers if, for every arrow `g : C ⟶ U` from a compact Hausdorff site object,
`ContainsFiniteCompactSurjection (S.pullback g)` holds. This means that there
are arrows `f i : V i ⟶ C`, indexed by `Fin n`, with compact Hausdorff sources,
such that each `f i ≫ g` belongs to `S` and every point of `C` belongs to the
image of some `f i`.

The topology is defined directly on sieves, rather than generated by a
precoverage. The Lean proofs of the topology axioms are:

- The maximal sieve passes the test with the identity of the probe.
- For pullback stability, composition of a test arrow with the given arrow
  gives a probe of the original sieve.
- For transitivity, choose a finite compact jointly surjective family in the
  pullback of the outer covering sieve. At each member of that family, apply
  the inner covering hypothesis with its identity probe. The composites of
  the two families still hit all points and belong to the required pullback
  sieve. Their dependent finite index set is reindexed by `Fin`.

`containsFiniteCompactSurjection_of_mem_compactProbeQuasiEtaleTopology`
extracts a finite jointly surjective family from a covering sieve when `U`
itself is compact Hausdorff: use the identity probe of `U`. This result does
not assert that the extracted arrows generate a covering sieve.

## Provenance and scope

Berkovich, *Vanishing cycles for formal schemes* (1994), §3, printed p. 544
(PDF p. 6), uses the category of quasi-étale maps `U ⟶ X` and describes
covering families by finite unions of images of affinoid domains that are
neighbourhoods of points of `U`. In *Étale cohomology for non-Archimedean
analytic spaces* (1993), §1.1, printed pp. 13–14 (PDF pp. 10–11), the
quasinet condition also requires the point to lie in every chosen set;
the two forms agree for compact subsets of a Hausdorff space.

The compact-probe test is a derived rule for the full Library category,
not a quotation from either source. The universal test is important on
non-Hausdorff objects. For example, glue two closed discs of radius two
along the complement of the unit disc. The images of the first copy's
unit disc and the whole second copy are jointly surjective. In the
pullback of their generated sieve to the first copy, every arrow lands
in the unit disc or its complement. Finitely many compact images in the
complement cannot cover it, since that would make it closed. Worker 209
also observed why imposing a common point on chart images **only for the
generating families** is insufficient: the two arrows combine into one
surjective arrow with a full affinoid chart. Pulling the original sieve
back along the combined arrow gives a sieve containing the two clopen
inclusions, so local character of the generated topology makes the
original sieve cover again.

Use `compactProbeQuasiEtaleTopology` in place of the legacy covering rule.
The comparison with the source's neighbourhood condition on Hausdorff
objects, and the covering property of finite compact refinements, require
separate results. No fibre-product or point-lifting assertion is assumed
in the definition or in its topology axioms.
