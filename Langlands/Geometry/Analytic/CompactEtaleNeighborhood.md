# Compact source representatives of étale neighborhoods

For an étale map and a specified source point, every neighborhood of the point contains a compact Hausdorff analytic domain through it. The intersection of this domain with the preimage of a suitable open neighborhood of the image point is open in the original source. The induced map from that open slice to the target open is étale.

On a strong representative of the étale map, choose a finite restriction between open neighborhoods, keeping the source inside a Hausdorff neighborhood of the point. A finite union of saturated target charts in a Hausdorff neighborhood of the image point gives a compact analytic domain inside the finite locus. The image point lies in its interior. The preimage within the finite restriction is the union of the corresponding finite recognized source pieces, so it is compact. It is Hausdorff as a subspace of the chosen source neighborhood. Intersecting it with the inverse image of the interior of the target domain recovers the same open slice as for the finite restriction. Arrow isomorphisms transport the construction to the localization. The étale claim uses the isomorphism identifying a nested domain with its extension to the ambient source and cancels the target open immersion.

This construction is derived from the small finite neighborhoods of an étale map, using Berkovich, *Étale cohomology for non-Archimedean analytic spaces* (1993), Lemma 3.1.2, printed pp. 54–55, and Proposition 3.3.7, printed p. 63. It supplies compact representatives for finite Galois refinements of analytic étale germs in the setting of Theorem 3.4.1, printed p. 65.
