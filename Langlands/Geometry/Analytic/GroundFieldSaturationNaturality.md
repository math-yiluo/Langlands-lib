# Naturality of the reverse saturation comparison for small chart indices

Here the original presentations have chart indices in universe `0`. `CompatibleStrongHom.liftOriginalIndices` lifts the original chart index types of both presentations into the common ambient universe, leaving the actual point map and bounded pullbacks unchanged. It is the generic `CompatibleStrongHom.reindex` of `GroundFieldExtensionReindexNaturality` along `Equiv.ulift` at both endpoints, whose targets are the published `saturationSource` presentations. The local compatibility equation uses the original chart data with the indices read through `ULift.down`.

`CompatibleStrongHom.saturatedCompatible` is the Library's saturation extension `saturationExtension` of a compatible map, packaged with `ofSaturated` as a compatible strong map between the literal saturated presentations. `saturatedToLiftedOriginal_natural` states the naturality square of the published reverse comparison `saturatedToLiftedOriginal`. `extendedSaturationComparison_natural` states the same square after ground field extension, for `extendedSaturationComparison`.

For a compatible map of original presentations, the two reverse saturation composites have the same map on the literal spaces. For each saturated source chart, choose an original parent and an admissible original target for the parent. At that parent and target, the saturated map recovers the full original bounded pullback (heterogeneously, because the presented algebras are propositionally identified). The reverse saturation comparisons at those charts recover bounded identities. The two composite pullbacks thus agree by `BoundedAlgHom.comp_heq`. Restriction to the given saturated source chart and saturated-source extensionality give equality of the complete strong maps. Applying `compatibleMap` and its composition law yields the corresponding natural square after ground field extension.

This is internal to the Library's chosen atlas presentations. It gives precisely the naturality of the reverse comparison used to relate extension of the literal saturation to extension of the original presentation with lifted indices. It is not the naturality of the full affine base-change comparison.
