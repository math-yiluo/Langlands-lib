# Spectrum identification target for a rational presentation

Author: Worker 36.

`Langlands.Analytic.RationalPresentation.SpectrumIdentificationStatement`
concerns the actual rational quotient and base map constructed in
`RationalPresentation.Definitions`. The coefficient field K is complete
and ultrametric. The base A is the quotient of a finite Tate algebra by
the closure of the ideal generated by S. The rational quotient adjoins
unit-radius variables Y_j with equations g Y_j = f_j, retaining the old
relations. The selected g and f_j are series representatives in the
original Tate algebra.

The first clause states that the subalgebra of elements b with
baseMap(g)^r b = baseMap(a) for some natural r and a in A is dense in the
rational quotient for its quotient norm topology. This clause has no
unit-ideal hypothesis. It concerns the explicitly defined subalgebra,
without asserting that the base map itself has dense image or that an
algebraic localization map is injective.

The remaining clauses assume that the images of g and the f_j in A
generate the unit ideal. The range of the induced spectrum map is then
exactly the locus of base points satisfying x(f_j) ≤ x(g) for every j,
and that spectrum map is a closed embedding for the pointwise spectrum
topologies. Thus the quotient spectrum is topologically identified with
the indicated closed locus. No point lift, realizing field, injectivity,
or analytic-domain universal property is an input. Both variable counts
may be zero and zero quotients are allowed.

The source context is Berkovich's *Non-archimedean analytic spaces*
(Trieste 2009), Exercise 2.2.2(ii), p.9, and Fact 2.2.3(i),(iv), p.10.
This is a derived formulation: Lean uses a single enlarged Tate algebra
and closure of the combined relations, while the source uses relative
series over A. The explicit unit-ideal hypothesis is retained instead
of the source's absence of common spectrum zeros; the converse between
those conditions is not asserted. The density clause uses powers of
the chosen denominator, whereas the source's algebra A(V) in (iv)
inverts all functions nonvanishing on V. Under the unit-ideal condition,
the denominator is invertible in the constructed quotient, so these
power-denominator fractions have their usual algebraic interpretation.
Relative-series identification, independence of presentations, general
radii and the analytic-domain factorization property of Definition
2.2.1 remain separate obligations.
