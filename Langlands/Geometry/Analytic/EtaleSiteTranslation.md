# Translation of the étale site along isomorphisms

Author: Worker 21.

The Library's `EtaleSite` defines the étale site `Ét(X)` of an analytic space
`X` and the translation `EtaleSite.translate e : EtaleSite X ≌ EtaleSite Y`
along an isomorphism `e : X ≅ Y`, which composes structure morphisms with `e`.
It proves that translation preserves and reflects étale coverings. This
module proves that translation is compatible with the étale Grothendieck
topologies: it is continuous and cocontinuous, both translation and its
inverse are dense subsite functors, and translation identifies the sheaf
categories. For an automorphism `g` of `X`, these give the pullback `g^*` and
the pushforward `g_*` of étale sheaves along `g`, used to define sheaves with
a group action.

## Source and provenance

Berkovich, *Étale equivariant sheaves on p-adic analytic spaces*
(unfinished manuscript, 1995–1996), registered as
`provided-7bb3903652336bd799aad8853b8efd7ff3bcc0c7652745417f4745a14ac08e90`
(folder SHA256
`e046cadfb9dcec4b0cf6624f80d6316bf532416153b82023f5100bd1ab0726cd`; file
`Berkovich--Etale equivariant sheaves on p-adic analytic spaces (unfinished manuscript)--1996.pdf`),
§1.4, printed p.7: for a discrete group `G`, an étale `G`-sheaf is an étale
sheaf `F` "endowed with a system of isomorphisms τ(g) : F ⥲ g^*F, g ∈ G, such
that τ(gh) = h^*(τ(g)) ∘ τ(h). In other words, F is a G-sheaf if for each
quasi-étale (resp. étale) morphism U → X and for each g ∈ G there is a
functorial bijection F(U) ⥲ F(^gU) : f ↦ ^gf, where ^gU = U ×_{X,g⁻¹} X".
Here `^gU` and `^gf` carry a left superscript `g` in the original.

Berkovich uses without comment that `g^*` sends sheaves to sheaves, that is,
that `U ↦ ^gU` is compatible with the étale topology. This module proves that
compatibility for the Library's translation. The general lemma and the
proofs are derived; they use Mathlib's sites API. As explained in
`EtaleSite`, `translate g` sends `U → X` to the composite `U → X → X` with `g`,
which is isomorphic over `X` to Berkovich's `^gU = U ×_{X,g⁻¹} X`.

Direction of the sheaf equivalence. Mathlib's `Equivalence.sheafCongr` for
`translate e` has forward functor `F ↦ (translate e).inverse.op ⋙ F` and
inverse functor `F ↦ (translate e).functor.op ⋙ F`, on underlying
presheaves. For an automorphism `g` of `X` (with `e = g`):

- the forward functor of `sheafTranslate g A` sends `F` to the sheaf with
  sections `F(g⁻¹U)` over `U`, since the inverse of translation composes the
  structure morphism with `g⁻¹`; this is the pushforward `g_*F`;
- its inverse functor sends `F` to the sheaf with sections `F(^gU)` over `U`;
  this is Berkovich's `g^*F`, whose sections over `U` are those of `F` over
  `^gU`, as in the displayed bijection `F(U) ⥲ F(^gU)`.

So a system `τ(g) : F ⥲ g^*F` is a system of isomorphisms from `F` to the
image of `F` under the inverse functor of `sheafTranslate g`.

## A criterion for cocontinuity

`Langlands.Analytic.isCocontinuous_toGrothendieck_of_equivalence`: let
`e : C ≌ D` be an equivalence and let `J`, `K` be precoverages on `C` and `D`.
Assume that for every `K`-covering `R` of `V`, the image `R.map e.inverse` is a
`J`-covering of `e.inverse.obj V`. Then `e.functor` is cocontinuous for the
Grothendieck topologies `J.toGrothendieck` and `K.toGrothendieck` generated by
`J` and `K`.

Proof.

1. Let `T` be Mathlib's restricted topology `e.inverse.restrictedTopology`
   of `J.toGrothendieck` on `D`. A sieve `S` on `V` is in `T` exactly when
   `S.functorPushforward e.inverse` is in `J.toGrothendieck`
   (`Functor.mem_restrictedTopology_iff`; the inverse of an equivalence is
   cover-dense).
2. `K.toGrothendieck ≤ T`. By the Galois connection
   `Precoverage.toGrothendieck_le_iff_le_toPrecoverage` it suffices that the
   sieve generated by each `K`-covering `R` is in `T`. Its pushforward along
   `e.inverse` is the sieve generated by `R.map e.inverse`
   (`Sieve.generate_map_eq_functorPushforward`), which is a `J`-covering.
3. Let `S` cover `e.functor.obj U` for `K.toGrothendieck`. By step 2,
   `S.functorPushforward e.inverse` covers `e.inverse.obj (e.functor.obj U)`.
   By Mathlib's `Sieve.functorPushforward_inverse` this sieve is the
   `e.functor`-pullback of `S` pulled back along the counit. Pulling it back
   along the unit `U ⟶ e.inverse.obj (e.functor.obj U)` gives
   `S.functorPullback e.functor`, by the triangle identity of the equivalence.
   Pullback stability of `J.toGrothendieck` concludes.

## Translation

All declarations are in `Langlands.Analytic.SaturatedAtlasPresentation.EtaleSite`.
The field `K` is a complete ultrametric normed field (trivial valuation
allowed), and `X`, `Y` are objects of `LocalizedCategory.{u, v, v}`.

- `etalePrecoverage_eq_comap_translate_inverse e` and
  `etalePrecoverage_eq_comap_translate_functor e`: the étale precoverage of
  `Y` is the pullback of that of `X` along the inverse of `translate e`, and
  the étale precoverage of `X` is the pullback of that of `Y` along
  `translate e`. Translation in either direction changes only the structure
  morphism, so the point functors agree (`Precoverage.comap_comp`).
- `isCocontinuous_translate_functor e` and `isCocontinuous_translate_inverse e`
  (instances): translation along `e` and its inverse are cocontinuous for the
  étale topologies, by the criterion above applied to `translate e` and to
  its symmetric equivalence.
- `isDenseSubsite_translate_functor e` and `isDenseSubsite_translate_inverse e`
  (instances): both are dense subsite functors, by Mathlib's
  `Equivalence.isDenseSubsite_functor_of_isCocontinuous` and
  `Equivalence.isDenseSubsite_inverse_of_isCocontinuous`.
- `isContinuous_translate_functor e`: translation is continuous for the
  étale topologies (from the dense subsite instance).
- `sheafTranslate e A : Sheaf (etaleTopology X) A ≌ Sheaf (etaleTopology Y) A`
  is Mathlib's `Equivalence.sheafCongr` for `translate e`, for any coefficient
  category `A`. Its forward functor precomposes with the inverse of
  translation and its inverse functor precomposes with translation (see the
  direction paragraph above: for an automorphism `g`, these are `g_*` and
  `g^*`).

## Scope

This module does not define sheaves with group actions, the twisted category
in which group elements act on objects of `Ét(X)`, or the equivariant
sheaves of Berkovich's §1.4; these belong to the equivariant sheaf branch of
the parent task. It does not treat the quasi-étale site.
