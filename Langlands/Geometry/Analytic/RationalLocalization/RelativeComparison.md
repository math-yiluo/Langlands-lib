# Strict rational quotients as unit-radius relative rational quotients

Author: Worker 966.

This module constructs an actual bounded algebra equivalence between the
strict rational quotient `RationalLocalization.algebra K P m g n` of a
presented strict affinoid algebra and the intrinsic relative rational
quotient of its radius-one generalized presentation. All declarations are
in `Langlands.Analytic.RationalLocalization`.

## Inputs and the two quotients

Fix a complete ultrametric normed field `K`, an actual
`P : PresentedStrictAffinoidAlgebra K`, a natural number `m`, an element
`g : P` and a tuple `n : Fin m → P`. Write

- `A = PresentedAffinoidAlgebra.ofStrict K P`, the published radius-one
  generalized presentation of `P` from `GeneralizedAffinoid.StrictComparison`;
- `e = PresentedAffinoidAlgebra.strictEquiv K P : A ≃ P`, its published
  bounded algebra equivalence;
- `ḡ = e⁻¹ g` and `n̄_j = e⁻¹ (n j)`;
- `u = PositivePolyradius.unit (Fin m)`.

The source is the published strict quotient
`RationalLocalization.algebra K P m g n`. Its carrier is the closed-ideal
quotient of the strict Tate algebra in `P.arity + m` variables by the
original closed relations of `P` and the equations `g̃ Y_j - ñ_j`, where
`g̃ = seriesLift K P g` and `ñ_j = seriesLift K P (n j)` are the published
chosen strict representatives. Its structure maps are
`RationalLocalization.baseMap` and `RationalLocalization.fractionCoordinate`.

The target is the published intrinsic quotient

```lean
PresentedAffinoidAlgebra.RelativeRationalPresentation.Algebra K A u ḡ n̄
```

that is, `A{Y}` at unit radii modulo the closure of the ideal generated by
`C(ḡ) Y_j - C(n̄_j)`, with `RelativeRationalPresentation.baseMap` and
`RelativeRationalPresentation.fraction`.

These two quotients have different actual carriers. The module does not
identify them definitionally. It constructs the comparison in three steps
and proves the coefficient and coordinate formulas.

## Step 1: equal data relabelling

`PresentedAffinoidAlgebra.RationalPresentation.dataEquiv K A s g g' f f' hg hf`
takes proofs `hg : g = g'` and `hf : f = f'` of equalities of actual elements
and tuples in an arbitrary `A : PresentedAffinoidAlgebra K`, at any positive
radii `s`. It substitutes both equalities and returns `BoundedAlgEquiv.refl`.
The imported `dataEquiv_baseMap` and `dataEquiv_fraction` formulas hold by
`rfl` after substitution. This step is used once, at the end, to change the
data of the intrinsic quotient from presentation-map images to `ḡ` and `n̄`.
The common relabelling and these two formulas are public in
`GeneralizedAffinoid/RationalPresentationEquivalence` and reused here.

## Step 2: the scalar bridge

`scalarLift K P a` is the unit-radius generalized Tate series
`(GeneralizedTateAlgebra.unitAlgEquiv K).symm (representative K P a)`.
It keeps the coefficients of the published representative chosen in
`BoundedEquivalence`. `presentationMap_scalarLift` proves

```lean
presentationMap K A (scalarLift K P a) = e.symm a
```

by `strictAlgEquiv_symm_presentationMap` and
`presentationMap_representative`.

`scalarAlgebra K P m g n` abbreviates the published generalized scalar
quotient

```lean
PresentedAffinoidAlgebra.RationalPresentation.algebra K A u
  (scalarLift K P g) (fun j => scalarLift K P (n j))
```

Its relations are the old closed relations of `A` included in the larger
weighted series algebra, together with the scalar rational equations at
these lifts.

The bridge consists of two bounded maps built from the published extension
properties of the two quotients. No inverse is assumed.

- `toScalar` is `RationalLocalization.liftFromBase`. On coefficients it
  uses the generalized scalar `baseMap` composed with `e.symm`, and it
  sends the fraction coordinates to the scalar fractions. The hypotheses
  are discharged as follows. `scalarFraction_bounded` shows that every
  monomial in the scalar fractions has norm at most one. It uses
  `RationalPresentation.norm_fraction_le`, which bounds each fraction by its
  radius `1`, and then `GeneralizedTateAlgebra.monomialValue_norm_le_weight`
  together with `PresentedAffinoidAlgebra.norm_one_le`. The equation
  hypothesis `toScalar_equation` is the published scalar
  `RationalPresentation.fraction_equation`, rewritten with
  `presentationMap_scalarLift`.
- `ofScalar` is the generalized
  `PresentedAffinoidAlgebra.RationalPresentation.liftFromBase` from
  `GeneralizedAffinoid.RationalExtension`. On coefficients it uses the strict
  `baseMap` composed with `e`, and it sends the scalar fractions to the
  strict fraction coordinates. `fractionCoordinate_bounded` converts the
  published strict monomial bound `RationalPresentation.newCoordinates_bounded`
  (`RationalTransport`) to the unit-radius weighted bound, using
  `GeneralizedTateAlgebra.hasBoundedMonomials_unit`. The equation
  hypothesis `ofScalar_equation` is the published strict
  `RationalLocalization.coordinate_equation`, rewritten with
  `presentationMap_scalarLift` and `e (e.symm a) = a`.

`toScalar_baseMap`, `toScalar_fractionCoordinate`, `ofScalar_baseMap` and
`ofScalar_fraction` evaluate the published lift formulas
(`liftFromBase_comp_baseMap`, `liftFromBase_fractionCoordinate` and
`liftFromBase_fraction`) at coefficients and coordinates.

The inverse identities are proved by extensionality on both sides.

- `hom_ext_fractionCoordinate` states that two bounded maps out of the
  strict quotient into any normed `K`-algebra are equal when they agree on
  `baseMap a` for every `a : P` and on every `fractionCoordinate j`. It
  applies the published strict
  `RationalPresentation.hom_ext_base_coordinates`. Since the strict
  `baseMap` is the presentation base map after `(modelEquiv K P).symm`,
  agreement is evaluated at `modelEquiv K P a`. This needs no unit-ideal
  hypothesis.
- `ofScalar_toScalar` applies `hom_ext_fractionCoordinate` to
  `ofScalar ∘ toScalar` and the identity. On coefficients the composite is
  `baseMap (e (e.symm a)) = baseMap a`; on coordinates it is the identity.
- `toScalar_ofScalar` applies the published generalized
  `PresentedAffinoidAlgebra.RationalPresentation.hom_ext` from
  `GeneralizedAffinoid.RelativeRationalComparison`, in the same way, with
  `e.symm (e a) = a`.

`scalarEquiv` packages `toScalar` and `ofScalar` with
`BoundedAlgEquiv.ofHomInv`. Its four simp lemmas
`scalarEquiv_baseMap`, `scalarEquiv_fractionCoordinate`,
`scalarEquiv_symm_baseMap` and `scalarEquiv_symm_fraction` restate the
lift formulas. In the equivalence, both the forward map and the inverse
are bounded algebra maps.

## Step 3: the relative comparison

`relativeEquiv K P m g n` is the composite

```lean
((scalarEquiv K P m g n).trans
    (PresentedAffinoidAlgebra.RationalPresentation.relativeEquiv K A u
      (scalarLift K P g) (fun j => scalarLift K P (n j)))).trans
  (PresentedAffinoidAlgebra.RationalPresentation.dataEquiv K A u _ _ _ _
    (presentationMap_scalarLift K P g)
    (funext fun j => presentationMap_scalarLift K P (n j)))
```

The middle factor is the published comparison between the scalar rational
quotient and the intrinsic relative rational quotient at the classes
`presentationMap K A (scalarLift K P g)` and
`presentationMap K A (scalarLift K P (n j))`. It is built from
`GeneralizedAffinoid.RelativeSeriesComparison` and
`GeneralizedAffinoid.RelativeEquationComparison`, and it uses the intrinsic
quotient of `GeneralizedAffinoid.RelativeRationalPresentation`. The last
factor relabels these classes as `ḡ` and `n̄_j` along the proved equalities
`presentationMap_scalarLift`. Its type is

```lean
BoundedAlgEquiv K (RationalLocalization.algebra K P m g n)
  (PresentedAffinoidAlgebra.RelativeRationalPresentation.Algebra K A u ḡ n̄)
```

The public formulas are:

- `relativeEquiv_baseMap`: `relativeEquiv (baseMap a)` equals
  `RelativeRationalPresentation.baseMap K A u ḡ n̄ (e.symm a)`;
- `relativeEquiv_fractionCoordinate`:
  `relativeEquiv (fractionCoordinate j)` equals
  `RelativeRationalPresentation.fraction K A u ḡ n̄ j`;
- `relativeEquiv_symm_baseMap`: the inverse sends the relative
  `baseMap a`, for `a : A`, to the strict `baseMap (e a)`;
- `relativeEquiv_symm_fraction`: the inverse sends the relative fraction
  `j` to `fractionCoordinate j`.

The first two are proved by unfolding the composite with `change` and
rewriting with `scalarEquiv_baseMap` (respectively
`scalarEquiv_fractionCoordinate`), the published
`RationalPresentation.relativeEquiv_baseMap` (respectively
`relativeEquiv_fraction`), and the relabelling formula. The inverse
formulas follow from the forward ones and `symm_apply_apply`.

## Scope and assumptions

`CompleteSpace K` is required by the published strict quotient
`RationalLocalization.algebra` and by the published relative comparison
`RationalPresentation.relativeEquiv`. `RationalPresentation.dataEquiv`,
`scalarLift`, `presentationMap_scalarLift`, `scalarFraction_bounded` and
`toScalar_equation` do not use it. The module imposes no unit-ideal,
nontrivial-valuation, nonzero-quotient, proper-ideal or nonempty-arity
condition. Zero algebras, `m = 0` and `P.arity = 0` are allowed.

The module proves boundedness in both directions only. It states no
isometry or norm formula for `relativeEquiv`. It does not compare
quotients for different choices of representatives beyond the stated
formulas. It also makes no claim about spectra, loci, binary diagrams,
overlaps, gluing, sheaves or exactness.

## Provenance

The retained original is Vladimir Berkovich, *Non-Archimedean analytic
spaces*, Trieste lectures (2009), registered as
`berkovich-2009-trieste-analytic-spaces`, PDF SHA256
`8cfe4f6f35d5e717ac67c5c8f5ef9ff9185f460bbe5f9a10aeacd84cddeeb2ca`. No
errata are registered for it. Printed/PDF pages 8–9 were read directly.

- Definition 2.1.6(i), page 8, defines the relative weighted-series algebra
  `A{r⁻¹T}`. At unit radii, this is the source of the intrinsic target
  quotient.
- Exercise 2.2.2(ii), page 9, defines the rational quotient
  `A{p⁻¹ f/g} = A{p⁻¹T}/(gT_i - f_i)`. Here `p = 1`, the denominator is `g`
  and the numerators are `n_j`. The printed exercise uses the index `m` for
  `p` and `n` for the numerators. It also writes `|g_i(x)|` for the single
  denominator `g`. The Lean data use one denominator and `m` numerators.

The original defines a single quotient. It does not state a comparison
between a strict scalar presentation and a relative presentation. This
equivalence is derived internal infrastructure. It is proved here from the
published extension properties, the extensionality theorems and the
published scalar-to-relative comparison named above, by their Lean names.
