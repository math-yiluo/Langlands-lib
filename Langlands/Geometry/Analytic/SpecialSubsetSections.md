# Actual Banach sections and canonical restrictions on special subsets

This module constructs the Banach section algebra of each literal special subset from an actual finite special covering, proves defining-family independence by constructed bounded equivalences, constructs bounded restrictions for literal inclusions, and identifies the canonical continuous spectrum maps. Original chart subsets recover their unchanged original algebras.

The source motivation is Berkovich, *Étale cohomology for non-Archimedean analytic spaces* (1993), §1.2, printed pp.20–21 / PDF pp.17–18, the paragraph after Lemma 1.2.12 defining the compatible-tuple Banach algebra, its canonical covering independence and continuous map to its spectrum, and the subsequent recognition boundary before Proposition 1.2.13. The registered source is `berkovich-1993-etale-cohomology`, PDF SHA256 `06b42aca1c8f237e607d2cf1f15a41fdc9718324a4dc9fd5bce66574254bb91c`.

This is derived infrastructure on the already defined special subsets. It does not prove Lemma 1.2.12, infer that arbitrary finite coverings of a special set satisfy its overlap hypotheses, or assert that every special section algebra is affinoid. It uses the represented intersections of the chosen actual special covering and the Hausdorffness included in specialness. It does not use the unproved intersection statement.

`SpecialSubset` is the subtype of literal ambient subsets satisfying the proved-input special-set definition. `gluingData` chooses one finite special-cover witness from that proposition. Its literal union equals the original subset, and the witness supplies Hausdorffness. `SectionAlgebra` is precisely the actual closed finite-product compatible-tuple equalizer of this chosen family. Its operations and norm are inherited from the genuine original quotient algebras, and completeness and ultrametric distance follow from the existing equalizer construction. No output Banach algebra is supplied.

`spectrumMap` transports the previously constructed canonical continuous finite-union map along equality of the two literal subspaces. Its domain retains the actual subset topology. The containing union is Hausdorff by specialness; the ambient space need not be Hausdorff.

For nested special subsets, `restriction` applies the constructed Hausdorff finite-union restriction after transporting the literal containments. Every needed original-chart subordinate cover and the bounded maps were constructed by that earlier descent theorem. `restriction_self` and `restriction_comp` are full bounded-map identities, inherited from the proved finite-union laws. `comap_spectrumMap` identifies the canonical point maps through the contravariant restriction and the literal subset inclusion.

`comparisonEquiv` compares the chosen equalizer with every other finite original-chart family having represented overlaps and the same literal union. Such a family need not be supplied as a special-cover witness: the already proved Hausdorff equality comparison applies. Both bounded maps and their inverse laws are constructed. `comparisonEquiv_spectrumMap` identifies the two actual canonical spectrum maps at every point over the unchanged ambient value. In particular, different special-cover choices give canonically bounded equivalent section algebras, without assuming comparison objects or inferring algebra-map equality from spectrum points.

`original` uses the actual singleton special-cover theorem to form the special subset of each original chart. Its independently chosen defining covering need not be that singleton. `originalAlgebraEquiv` applies full-domain bounded descent and original-chart equalizer recognition to recover the entire unchanged parent algebra. `original_spectrumMap` identifies the special-subset canonical map with the spectrum homeomorphism induced by this actual bounded equivalence.

Coefficient, ambient-point and original-index universes remain independent. Zero charts and empty special subsets are permitted whenever witnessed by the definitions. Neither reducedness nor a nontrivial coefficient valuation is assumed. These constructions provide actual Banach section algebras and contravariant bounded restrictions; proving the general special-domain intersection theorem, maximal affinoid recognition and the subsequent analytic sites remains separate.
