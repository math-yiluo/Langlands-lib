# Statement: covering sieves of compact quasi-étale objects have finite compact refinements

Author: Worker 21.

This module states, without proof, that on the quasi-étale site of a
Hausdorff analytic space `X`, every covering sieve of a compact object
contains a finite family of arrows from compact objects that already
generates a covering sieve. This is condition (A),
`HasFiniteCompactQuasiEtaleRefinements X`, of the published module
`Langlands/Sheaf/Equivariant/AnalyticQuasiEtaleRefinements` (Worker 26). That
module derives finite equivariant refinements for quasi-étale `G`-sheaves from
(A) and two conditions on lifts of the action. The statement is the
`Prop`-valued definition
`Langlands.Analytic.SaturatedAtlasPresentation.QuasiEtaleCompactRefinementsStatement`.
Nothing in the module proves it.

## The statement

`QuasiEtaleCompactRefinementsStatement K`, for a complete ultrametric normed
field `K`, is the proposition: for every analytic space `X` in
`LocalizedCategory.{u, v, v}` whose underlying topological space is Hausdorff,
`HasFiniteCompactQuasiEtaleRefinements X` holds.

Unfolded, `HasFiniteCompactQuasiEtaleRefinements X` says: for every object
`U` of `QuasiEtaleSite X` with `IsCompactSpace U.left` (compact and
Hausdorff), and every sieve `S` on `U` in `quasiEtaleTopology X`, there are a
natural number `n`, objects `V 0, …, V (n-1)` of `QuasiEtaleSite X` and arrows
`f i : V i ⟶ U` such that

- each `(V i).left` is compact (`IsCompactSpace`),
- each `f i` lies in `S`, and
- the sieve `Sieve.ofArrows V f` generated by the `f i` lies in
  `quasiEtaleTopology X U`.

Here `QuasiEtaleSite X` is the Library's category `Qét(X)` of quasi-étale
morphisms to `X` with all morphisms over `X`, and `quasiEtaleTopology X` is the
Grothendieck topology generated by the precoverage `quasiEtalePrecoverage X`.
That precoverage consists of the families such that every point of `U` has a
neighbourhood that is a finite union of images of charts of the members
(module `QuasiEtaleSite`).

## Correspondence with the sources

The statement is derived from Berkovich's definition of the topology. The
following argument is not formalized.

1. The topology is generated by a pretopology, so a sieve `S` on `U` is
   covering exactly when it contains a covering family `{f_i : U_i → U}`.
2. Let `U` be compact. Every point of `U` has a neighbourhood
   `f_{i_1}(V_1) ∪ … ∪ f_{i_n}(V_n)` with affinoid domains `V_k ⊂ U_{i_k}`.
   The interiors of these neighbourhoods cover `U`, so finitely many of them
   suffice. Collect the finitely many affinoid domains `W_1, …, W_m` that
   occur, with `W_k ⊂ U_{i_k}`.
3. Each `W_k → U_{i_k} → X` is quasi-étale, because embeddings of analytic
   domains are quasi-étale (p.543) and so are compositions (Lemma 3.1 (i)). So
   `W_k` is an object of `Qét(X)`. It is compact, being affinoid. The
   composite `g_k : W_k → U` lies in `S`, because `S` is a sieve that contains
   `f_{i_k}`.
4. The images `g_k(W_k)` cover `U`, and each `W_k` is an affinoid domain of
   itself. So `U` is a neighbourhood of each of its points of the required
   form, and `{g_1, …, g_m}` is a covering family. Its sieve is therefore
   covering.

Step 1 uses that the coverings form a pretopology: they are stable under base
change. This needs fibre products and Lemma 3.1 (i). In the Library, the
topology is generated by a precoverage. A proof must therefore also handle
pullbacks of covering sieves, and this is where fibre products enter. The
published `FibreProductPullbackStatement` (Berkovich 1993, Proposition 1.4.1,
for Hausdorff spaces) and base change of quasi-étale morphisms are not proved
in the Library. In step 4, the Library's coverings use images of charts of
the members. A proof would cover each compact `W_k` by finitely many of its
charts, which form a net.

Hausdorffness of `X` is an added hypothesis. Berkovich's §3 does not assume
it. It is imposed here for two reasons: the Library states fibre products
only for Hausdorff spaces, and `QuasiEtaleActionLiftStatement` and the
consumers of condition (A) assume a Hausdorff `X`. The added hypothesis makes
the proposition weaker than the unrestricted one.

## Scope

Only the proposition is defined. The pretopology property, the reduction
above, and fibre products of analytic spaces are not formalized here.
