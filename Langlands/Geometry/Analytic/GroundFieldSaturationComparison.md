# The reverse saturation comparison at small chart-index width

For an actual presentation `A` over a complete ultrametric normed field `K` with chart index in `Type`, `saturatedToLiftedOriginal` is a compatible strong map from the literal constructed saturation of `A` to `A.saturationSource`. The latter is the original presentation with only its indices lifted to the ambient universe. In `A.fromSaturation` the enlarged source already has the same index universe, but has an extra `ULift.{0}` in its chart index type. Reindexing that source by the inverse of `Equiv.ulift` removes this lift and retains the chosen saturated atlas itself; this does not take the saturation of the reindexed original atlas or change its chosen representatives. The comparison has the identity ambient map and is a full quasi-isomorphism by `isQuasiIso_fromSaturation` and reindexing. Compatibility follows from saturation of the source.

At a chart indexed by `i` in `A`, its whole local domain is the chosen saturated representative of its range. The corresponding bounded pullback under the comparison agrees with the bounded identity on the original chart algebra, up to this equality of presented algebras. The proof uses subsingleton uniqueness of local-domain inclusions and not equality of the induced functions on spectrum points.

`extendedSaturationComparison` applies the actual `GroundFieldExtension.compatibleMap` to this comparison for any complete ultrametric normed `L` with `NormedAlgebra K L`. It commutes with the scalar-extension projections to `A.space`. The extended original pairs remain admissible, and the bounded pullback at each is the scalar extension of the identity just computed, hence the bounded identity after identifying the endpoints. The definitions and formulas do not require nontrivial valuations or global Hausdorffness.

This internal adapter provides the actual map needed to compare affine scalar extension on a saturated presentation with scalar extension before saturation. It proves the full quasi-isomorphism claim before field extension; proving that the extended map is a quasi-isomorphism requires the separate preservation theorem. There is no assertion here of an isomorphism in the localized category or of compatibility with analytification.
