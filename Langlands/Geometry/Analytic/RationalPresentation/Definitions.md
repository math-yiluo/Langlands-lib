# Construction of a unit-radius rational presentation

Author: Worker 36.

The coefficient field K is complete and ultrametric. Start with the actual
quotient A of the finite Tate algebra K{X_1,...,X_n} by the closure of the
ideal generated by an arbitrary relation set S. Choose series g and f_j.
`RationalPresentation.GeneratesUnitIdeal` says their images in A generate
the unit ideal. This is an algebraic hypothesis on the chosen inputs;
it supplies no localization, inverse denominator, or universal property.

The construction adjoins finitely many unit-radius Tate variables Y_j.
`relations` consists of the included old relations and the new equations
g Y_j - f_j. `algebra` is the actual quotient of the enlarged Tate algebra
by the closure of the ideal generated by these relations, with its
quotient norm. Its definition does not require the unit-ideal hypothesis.
The old-variable inclusion is reused from WeierstrassPresentation.Definitions.

To construct `baseMap`, compose that bounded inclusion with the enlarged
quotient projection. It kills the old relations, their ideal span, and
then their closure by continuity. Bounded quotient descent gives the map
from A. `baseMap_presentationMap` describes it on representatives, and
`baseMap_coordinate` gives the old-coordinate formula. Applying the new
quotient projection to g Y_j - f_j gives `coordinate_equation`. These
are intrinsic equations for the constructed quotient and maps.

`locus` is the set of points x in the bounded multiplicative spectrum of
A satisfying x(f_j) at most x(g) for every j, evaluated on the quotient
images of the chosen series. Neither a spectrum realization of this
locus nor denominator invertibility is assumed in the definitions.
Variable counts may be zero and zero quotients are allowed. The choices
of series representatives remain part of the presentation.

The source is Berkovich's *Non-archimedean analytic spaces* (Trieste 2009),
Exercise 2.2.2(ii), p.9, with the strict quotient-norm conventions of
Definitions 2.1.1-2.1.2, p.7. The present construction is derived: it
uses a single enlarged Tate algebra and the closure of the combined
relations, whereas the source uses relative series over A. All radii
here are one. The source assumes no common zero of g and the f_j;
the later bounded-extension statement uses the stronger explicit
unit-ideal condition. No converse from absence of spectrum zeros to
that ideal condition is asserted. Identification with relative series,
presentation independence, and the analytic-domain universal property
of Definition 2.2.1 remain separate obligations.
