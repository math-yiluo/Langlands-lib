# Étale morphisms of analytic spaces and the étale site `Ét(X)`

Author: Worker 21.

The Library's `LocalizedCategory` is the category of analytic spaces: the
localization of saturated atlas presentations with strong maps at their
quasi-isomorphisms (`SaturatedStrongLocalization`). The Library's
`StrongHom.IsEtaleAllPieces` (`FullRecognitionEtaleComposition`) is
Berkovich's étaleness of a strong map, flat and unramified at every point
over all affinoid pieces.

This module transfers étaleness to `LocalizedCategory`. It then constructs,
for each analytic space `X`, Berkovich's category `Ét(X)` of étale morphisms
`U → X`, its étale coverings and the étale Grothendieck topology. It also
constructs the translation of `Ét(X)` along an isomorphism `X ≅ Y`, which for
automorphisms is the operation `U ↦ gU` of Berkovich's equivariant
manuscript.

The following parts are derived; the reasons are explained below.

- Étaleness of a morphism of `LocalizedCategory` is defined through strong
  representatives up to isomorphism of arrows.
- The topology is generated from the covering families by Mathlib's
  `Precoverage.toGrothendieck`, without first proving that the coverings
  form a pretopology.
- The translation is the composite with the isomorphism, which is
  isomorphic over `X` to Berkovich's fibre product `U ×_{X,g⁻¹} X`.

## Étale morphisms of analytic spaces

All declarations are in the namespace
`Langlands.Analytic.SaturatedAtlasPresentation`. The field `K` is a complete
ultrametric normed field; its valuation may be trivial. The category is
`LocalizedCategory.{u, v, v}`, whose objects are saturated presentations with
index types in the universe `v` of the ambient spaces.

- `etale : MorphismProperty LocalizedCategory`. A morphism `φ` is étale if
  there are atlas presentations `A`, `B` and a strong map
  `g : ofSaturation A ⟶ ofSaturation B` such that `g` is étale over all
  pieces (`StrongHom.IsEtaleAllPieces`) and the arrow
  `localizationFunctor.map g` is isomorphic to `φ` in the arrow category.
  Such an isomorphism consists of isomorphisms of the sources and of the
  targets that commute with the two arrows.
- `etale_localizationFunctor_map`: the image of an étale strong map is étale.
- The `RespectsIso` instance: composing the arrow isomorphism shows that
  `etale` is invariant under isomorphisms of arrows.
- `exists_iso_localizationFunctor_obj_ofSaturation`: every analytic space is
  isomorphic to the image of the constructed saturation of an atlas
  presentation. It is the image of an object `S` by `localizedObjectEquiv`,
  and the Library isomorphism `S.saturationIso` relates `S` to
  `ofSaturation S.presentation`.
- `etale_of_isIso`: every isomorphism is étale. For an isomorphism `φ` from
  `X`, choose `A` as above with `e : L(ofSaturation A) ≅ X`. The identity
  strong map is étale (`isEtaleAllPieces_id`), and `e` and `e ≪≫ asIso φ`
  form an arrow isomorphism from its image to `φ`.
- The `ContainsIdentities` instance follows.

Provenance of the definition. Berkovich defines étaleness for morphisms of
k-analytic spaces, which are the morphisms of the category of fractions of
strong morphisms with respect to quasi-isomorphisms (printed p.20 / PDF
p.17). A strong morphism (Definition 1.2.7) requires every chart of the
source to map into a single chart of the target; this is the Library's
`StrongHom`. An arbitrary morphism of analytic spaces need not be strong for
the given atlases. The definition is justified as follows.

1. By Proposition 1.2.10 the quasi-isomorphisms admit a calculus of right
   fractions. Berkovich then describes the morphisms (printed p.20 / PDF
   p.17): for a net `σ ≺ τ` there is a canonical quasi-isomorphism
   `(X, 𝒜_σ, σ) → (X, 𝒜, τ)`, and
   `Hom((X, 𝒜, τ), (X′, 𝒜′, τ′)) = lim_{σ≺τ} Hom_{strong}((X, 𝒜_σ, σ), (X′, 𝒜′, τ′))`.
   So every morphism is `s⁻¹ f` with `s` a quasi-isomorphism from a refined
   source and `f` strong. In the Library this is
   `exists_localizedRightFraction`: every morphism of `LocalizedCategory` is
   `(localizedQuasiIso s hs).inv ≫ localizationFunctor.map f` with `s` a full
   quasi-isomorphism from a saturated presentation and `f` a strong map.
   Since `localizationFunctor.map s` is an isomorphism, such a morphism is
   isomorphic in the arrow category to `localizationFunctor.map f`.
2. Berkovich's Proposition 1.2.8 (printed p.18 / PDF p.15) extends every
   strong morphism uniquely to the local saturations `τ̄`, the families of
   all affinoid domains in members of `τ` (Proposition 1.2.6, printed p.17 / PDF p.14).
   This local saturation is in general smaller than the family `τ̂` of all
   affinoid domains of Proposition 1.2.13; no maximal atlas is used here.
   In the Library, every saturated presentation `S` is isomorphic to
   `ofSaturation S.presentation` by
   `SaturatedAtlasPresentation.saturationIso`. Conjugating `f` by these
   isomorphisms gives a strong map `g` between constructed saturations with
   `localizationFunctor.map g` isomorphic to the given morphism as arrows.
3. `IsEtaleAllPieces` is the Library's étaleness of strong maps between
   constructed saturations (Definitions 3.2.5 and 3.3.4). Étaleness of a
   morphism of analytic spaces does not change under composition with
   isomorphisms.

Therefore the definition asks for an étale strong representative between
constructed saturations up to isomorphism of arrows, and every morphism has
strong representatives of this form. Independence of the chosen
representative is not proved; the definition is existential and does not
need it. Stability under composition and base change in `LocalizedCategory`
is not proved here.

## The category `Ét(X)` and its points

For `X : LocalizedCategory`:

- `EtaleSite X` is Mathlib's `(etale).Over ⊤ X`. Its objects are morphisms
  `U ⟶ X` that are étale. Its morphisms are all morphisms over `X`
  (commuting triangles), with no condition on the morphism; Berkovich's
  remark that these are automatically étale is not formalized.
- `EtaleSite.points X : EtaleSite X ⥤ Type v` sends `U ⟶ X` to the underlying
  set of `localizedSpaceFunctor.obj U`, and a morphism to its underlying map
  (`points_obj`, `points_map`). `localizedSpaceFunctor` is the Library's
  descended ambient-space functor, which on images of original presentations
  gives their literal ambient spaces and continuous maps.

## Étale coverings and the étale topology

- `etalePrecoverage X : Precoverage (EtaleSite X)` is the pullback along
  `points X` of Mathlib's `Types.jointlySurjectivePrecoverage`. A family of
  morphisms `f_i : U_i ⟶ U` covers `U` when the images of the point maps of the
  `f_i` cover the points of `U`, that is, `U = ∪ f_i(U_i)`:
  - `mem_etalePrecoverage_iff` for presieves;
  - `ofArrows_mem_etalePrecoverage_iff` for indexed families.
- The `HasIsos` and `IsStableUnderComposition` instances are inherited from
  the pulled-back precoverage: a single isomorphism covers, and coverings of
  the members of a covering compose to a covering.
- `etaleTopology X : GrothendieckTopology (EtaleSite X)` is
  `(etalePrecoverage X).toGrothendieck`, the smallest Grothendieck topology
  whose covering sieves include the sieves generated by the étale coverings.
- `generate_mem_etaleTopology`: the sieve generated by an étale covering is
  covering.
- `mem_etaleTopology_of_jointlySurjective`: a sieve whose arrows have jointly
  surjective point maps is covering.
- `isSheaf_etaleTopology_iff`: a presheaf of types is a sheaf for the étale
  topology exactly when it satisfies the sheaf condition for every pullback
  of a sieve generated by an étale covering (Mathlib's
  `Precoverage.isSheaf_toGrothendieck_iff`).

Provenance of the topology: Berkovich's topology is the one generated by his
pretopology. The topology generated by a family of covering families does not
depend on whether that family is a pretopology, so `toGrothendieck` gives his
topology. Mathlib's construction needs no fibre products in `Ét(X)`. The
pretopology axioms that need fibre products (stability of coverings under
base change), and the fibre products themselves, are not proved here.

## Translation along isomorphisms

In the namespace `EtaleSite`, for an isomorphism `e : X ≅ Y`:

- `translate e : EtaleSite X ≌ EtaleSite Y` is Mathlib's
  `MorphismProperty.Comma.mapRightIso` for `e`. It sends `U ⟶ X` to the
  composite `U ⟶ X ⟶ Y`, which is étale because `etale` respects
  isomorphisms. It does not change morphisms, and its inverse composes with
  `e.inv`. The lemmas `translate_functor_obj_left`,
  `translate_functor_obj_hom` and `translate_functor_map_left` record this.
- `translatePoints e : (translate e).functor ⋙ points Y ≅ points X` is the
  identity: translation does not change the underlying points.
- `translateTrans e e'` and `translateRefl` are the natural isomorphisms
  between translation along a composite and the composite of translations,
  and between translation along the identity and the identity functor. Their
  components are identities of the underlying objects.
- `map_translate_mem_etalePrecoverage_iff`: a presieve on `U` is an étale
  covering exactly when its image under the translation is an étale covering
  of the translated object. The proof identifies `etalePrecoverage X` with
  the pullback of `etalePrecoverage Y` along the translation, by
  `Precoverage.comap_comp` and the equality of point functors.

For an automorphism `g` of `X`, `translate g` is Berkovich's `U ↦ gU`. The
projection `U ×_{X,g⁻¹} X → U` is an isomorphism, because `g⁻¹` is one, and
the structure map `U ×_{X,g⁻¹} X → X` corresponds under it to the composite
of `U → X` with `g`. This identification is derived and is not formalized,
since fibre products are not yet available in `LocalizedCategory`.

## Scope

Not proved here: composition and base-change stability of `etale` in
`LocalizedCategory`; independence of the strong representative; fibre
products in `Ét(X)`; the morphism of sites induced by a morphism of analytic
spaces; quasi-étale morphisms and the quasi-étale site; continuous group
actions and the equivariant sites `X(G)_ét` and `X(G)_qét` of Berkovich's
manuscript §1.2.
