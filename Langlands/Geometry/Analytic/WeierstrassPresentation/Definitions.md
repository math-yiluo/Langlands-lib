# Construction of a unit-radius Weierstrass presentation

Author: Worker 36.

Let K be a complete ultrametric normed field. The base is the actual quotient
of the finite-variable strict Tate algebra by the closure of the ideal
generated by a set S of series. Choose finitely many series f_j in that Tate
algebra. These definitions construct a second presentation by adding variables
Y_j and imposing the included old relations and the equations Y_j - f_j = 0.
All variables have radius one. The new algebra uses the quotient norm of the
closure of this generated ideal.

`adjoinVariables` includes the old variables in the first block of the enlarged
Tate algebra. It is constructed by the published bounded evaluation map:
the chosen variables have norm one, so their monomials are bounded.
`adjoinVariables_X` gives its value on each old variable.
`relations` is the union of the image of S and the range of the new equations.
`algebra` applies the existing closed-relation quotient construction to this set.

`baseMap` composes the old-variable inclusion with the new quotient projection.
That composite annihilates S, hence its ideal span, and then its closure by
continuity. The published bounded quotient lift gives an actual bounded
K-algebra map from the base quotient. The formula
`baseMap_presentationMap` is its value on representatives.
`baseMap_coordinate` identifies the old coordinates in the larger quotient.
`coordinate_equation` applies the quotient map to each new relation and
rearranges its vanishing to identify Y_j with the base image of f_j.
These are intrinsic laws of the constructed maps.

`locus` is the subset of the base spectrum where the value of each projected
f_j is at most one. It is a set of the existing bounded multiplicative
seminorms, not an assumed representing domain. Zero old or new variable
counts, arbitrary sets of relations, and a zero quotient are allowed.
The definitions depend on the chosen series representatives; no independence
of presentations or representatives is asserted here.

The source context is Berkovich's *Non-archimedean analytic spaces* (Trieste
2009), Exercise 2.2.2(i), p.9: adjoining variables with equations T_i - f_i
gives the Weierstrass case of the Laurent construction. The quotient norm and
strict unit-radius conventions are those of Definitions 2.1.1-2.1.2, p.7.
This implementation is derived presentation-level infrastructure: it uses
one enlarged Tate algebra over K and the closure of the combined relations.
It does not construct the source's relative series algebra A{Y}, identify
these two presentations, or use the theorem that every affinoid ideal is
closed. Its bounded extension property and spectrum image are separate
targets. In particular it does not yet establish the analytic-domain
factorization property of Definition 2.2.1.
