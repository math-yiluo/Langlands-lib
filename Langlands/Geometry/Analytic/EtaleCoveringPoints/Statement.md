# Underlying points of covering sieves in the analytic étale site

Author: Worker 284.

This module states
`Langlands.Analytic.SaturatedAtlasPresentation.EtaleCoveringPointsStatement`.
It says that every covering sieve `S` on any object `U` of `EtaleSite X`
is jointly surjective on the underlying analytic points: for every point
`x` of `U`, there are an object `V`, an arrow `f : V ⟶ U` in `S`, and a
point of `V` mapping to `x`. It is a proposition definition without a proof.

All sites and point maps are the published constructions from `EtaleSite`.
In particular, `etaleTopology X` is the Grothendieck topology generated by
the jointly surjective precoverage, and
`(EtaleSite.points X).map f` is the existing underlying point map.
The quantification ranges over every complete ultrametric normed field `K`,
every analytic space `X`, every étale object over `X`, and every covering
sieve. It imposes no compactness, group action, or extra point axioms.

## Provenance

The registered source is Vladimir G. Berkovich, *Étale cohomology for
non-Archimedean analytic spaces* (1993),
`berkovich-1993-etale-cohomology`, SHA-256
`06b42aca1c8f237e607d2cf1f15a41fdc9718324a4dc9fd5bce66574254bb91c`.

The first paragraph of §4.1, printed p.82 / PDF p.79, defines the étale
topology from the pretopology whose covering families have jointly
surjective point maps. For that pretopology, a covering sieve contains a
covering family and is therefore jointly surjective. This proposition is the
derived sieve formulation in the Library model, rather than a separately
numbered assertion in the source. No corresponding source erratum was found
in the Library's errata notes.

The Library uses `Precoverage.toGrothendieck` and does not yet establish
base-change stability of its analytic precoverage. Thus the result is not
an immediate consequence of the definition that has already been proved.
Proving it requires the missing geometric lifting or base-change facts, or
another argument that validates all the generated covering sieves.
The published theorem `mem_etaleTopology_of_jointlySurjective` proves only
the reverse implication.

## Role and scope

Together with openness of étale structure maps, this statement supplies
finite subcovers over compact analytic spaces. This is a geometric input
to compact degree-zero smoothness for étale `G`-sheaves. It neither assumes
nor proves the cohomology comparison. It makes no assertion that all
underlying points are geometric points of the étale topos.
