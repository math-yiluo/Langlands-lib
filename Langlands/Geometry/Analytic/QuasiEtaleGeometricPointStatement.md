# Geometric points are points of the quasi-étale site

Author: Worker 21.

This module states, without proof, that geometric points of an analytic space
give points of its quasi-étale site. It contains one Prop-valued definition,
`Langlands.Analytic.SaturatedAtlasPresentation.QuasiEtaleSite.GeometricPointStatement`.
It is the quasi-étale counterpart of `EtaleSite.GeometricPointStatement`
(module `EtaleGeometricPointStatement`). Worker 26's quasi-étale `G`-sheaves
(Task 26) take the point axioms as a hypothesis `h` for every `L`-valued
point. This statement makes that hypothesis an explicit open obligation.

## Statement

For every ground field `K` (a complete ultrametric normed field, the valuation
possibly trivial), every normed field `L` over `K` (`[NormedAlgebra K L]`) that
is ultrametric, complete and algebraically closed, every analytic space
`X : LocalizedCategory.{u, v, v} (K := K)` and every `L`-valued point
`x : (localizedFieldPointFunctor L).obj X` (module `FieldValuedPoints`), the
fibre functor `QuasiEtaleSite.pointFibre L x` on `Qét(X)` satisfies
`QuasiEtaleSite.PointAxioms L x` (module `OverSitePoints`):

1. its category of elements is cofiltered;
2. for every covering sieve `R` of an object `U` of `Qét(X)` for
   `quasiEtaleTopology X` (module `QuasiEtaleSite`), and every `L`-valued point
   `p` of `U` over `x`, there are a morphism `f : V ⟶ U` in `R` and an
   `L`-valued point `q` of `V` over `x` with `f` sending `q` to `p`.

With a proof `h`, `QuasiEtaleSite.toPoint L x (h K L X x)` is a point of the
quasi-étale site in Mathlib's sense, and `QuasiEtaleSite.pointEquivariance`
makes these points compatible with a group action on `X`.

## Original source and derived provenance

Berkovich, *Vanishing cycles for formal schemes*, Invent. Math. 115 (1994),
registered as `berkovich-1994-vanishing-cycles-formal-schemes`, SHA256
`f4f7bc00cc46a0a5f72a69d7bbb220bce1d3ed9fd465aeda629f37dfc054bed1`, §3,
printed p.544 / PDF p.6, defines the site:

- "For a k-analytic space X, let Qét(X) denote the category of quasi-étale
  morphisms U → X. The quasi-étale topology on X is the Grothendieck topology
  on the category Qét(X) generated by the pretopology for which the set of
  coverings of (U → X) ∈ Qét(X) is formed by the families {U_i →^{f_i} U}_{i∈I}
  such that each point of U has a neighborhood of the form
  f_{i_1}(V_1) ∪ … ∪ f_{i_n}(V_n) for some affinoid domains
  V_1 ⊂ U_{i_1}, …, V_n ⊂ U_{i_n}."
- Lemma 3.1: "(i) Quasi-étale morphisms are preserved under compositions,
  under any base change functor and under any ground field extension functor.
  (ii) If Y and Z are quasi-étale over X, then any X-morphism Z → Y is
  quasi-étale."

Berkovich, *Étale equivariant sheaves on p-adic analytic spaces* (unfinished
manuscript, 1996), registered as
`provided-7bb3903652336bd799aad8853b8efd7ff3bcc0c7652745417f4745a14ac08e90`,
§1.3, printed p.6, states the corresponding fact for the étale topology: "It
is easy to see that if we fix a geometric point x over each point x ∈ X, then
the family {x} is a conservative family of points of the étale topos of X(G)
(see [SGA4], Exp. IV, 6.4.1)."

Berkovich does not state that geometric points are points of the quasi-étale
site. The statement is derived: it is the quasi-étale analogue of the étale
statement, in the formulation of `EtaleSite.GeometricPointStatement`
(Mathlib's site points, that is, the fibre functors of SGA 4 IV 6.3, indexed by
`L`-valued points for an arbitrary complete algebraically closed `L`). Its
expected proof uses:

- for (1): `(X, x)` is an element; two elements `(U, p)` and `(V, q)` receive
  maps from `(U ×_X V, (p, q))`, which lies in `Qét(X)` by Lemma 3.1(i); two
  parallel maps `f, g : (U, p) ⟶ (V, q)` are equalized by the pullback of the
  diagonal of `V → X` along `(f, g)`. That diagonal is quasi-étale: on an
  affinoid domain of `V` identified with an affinoid domain of a space `W`
  étale over `X`, it is the restriction of the diagonal of `W`, an open
  immersion, so it is locally an affinoid domain embedding;
- for (2): the underlying point of `p` lies in some `f_i(V)` with `V` an
  affinoid domain of `U_i`. The morphism `f_i` is quasi-étale (Lemma 3.1(ii)),
  so the completed residue field of a preimage point is a finite separable
  extension (residue fields of étale morphisms, Berkovich 1993 §3.4, and
  affinoid domains do not change completed residue fields). Since `L` is
  algebraically closed, the embedding into `L` extends. Stability under
  pullback and composition of covering families then gives (2) for every
  covering sieve of the generated topology.
