# Étale descent of analytic morphisms

This module contains only the Prop-valued definition
`Langlands.Analytic.SaturatedAtlasPresentation.EtaleHomSheafStatement`: the
presheaf of analytic morphisms to a fixed analytic space is a sheaf on the small
étale site of every analytic space. The file states the proposition and does not
prove it.

## Exact statement

`EtaleHomSheafStatement.{u, v} : Prop` asserts: for every field `K : Type u`
with `NormedField K`, `IsUltrametricDist K` and `CompleteSpace K`, and all
`X T : LocalizedCategory.{u, v, v} (K := K)`, the presheaf

```lean
(MorphismProperty.Over.forget _ _ X ⋙ Over.forget X).op ⋙ yoneda.obj T
```

on `EtaleSite X` is a sheaf for `etaleTopology X` (`Presieve.IsSheaf`).

- The field may carry the trivial norm: only `NormedField K` is assumed, the
  hypotheses under which the Library defines `LocalizedCategory`, `etale`,
  `EtaleSite` and `etaleTopology`.
- The field universe `u` and the universe `v` of the analytic spaces are
  independent.
- `EtaleSite X` is the Library's `(etale (K := K)).Over ⊤ X`: objects are
  étale morphisms `U ⟶ X` of the localized category, morphisms are all
  morphisms over `X`. This is Berkovich's category `Ét(X)`.
- `etaleTopology X` is the Grothendieck topology generated by the families of
  morphisms over `X` whose underlying point maps
  (`localizedSpaceFunctor`) are jointly surjective. These are Berkovich's
  étale coverings `{Uᵢ → U}` with `U = ⋃ fᵢ(Uᵢ)`.
- The presheaf sends an étale object `U ⟶ X` to the type of morphisms
  `U.left ⟶ T` of the localized category; restriction along a site arrow
  `f : U ⟶ V` sends `g : V.left ⟶ T` to `f.left ≫ g`.

Thus compatible families of morphisms to `T` on a covering sieve have unique
amalgamations. Compatibility and equality concern morphisms of the localized
category, not only their underlying point maps. The target `T` need not be étale
over `X`, and its morphisms are not required to be over `X`. The statement uses
the generated topology as published; it assumes neither a pretopology instance
nor the existence of pullbacks in the site.

## Original sources and field scope

Berkovich, *Étale cohomology for non-Archimedean analytic spaces* (1993),
registered as `berkovich-1993-etale-cohomology`, SHA256
`06b42aca1c8f237e607d2cf1f15a41fdc9718324a4dc9fd5bce66574254bb91c`
(printed page = PDF page + 3):
- §1.2, printed p.14 (PDF p.11): "Throughout the paper we fix a
  non-Archimedean field k. (We don't assume that the valuation on k is
  nontrivial.)"
- §4.1, printed p.82 (PDF p.79): the étale topology on `Ét(X)`, generated by
  the families `{Uᵢ → U}` with `U = ⋃ fᵢ(Uᵢ)`; the big flat quasifinite site
  of `X`, and the remark that a sheaf on it restricts to a sheaf on the étale
  site.
- Lemma 4.1.1, printed p.82 (PDF p.79): a presheaf on the big flat quasifinite
  site is a sheaf if it is a sheaf for the G-topology of every `k`-analytic
  space over `X` and satisfies descent along finite faithfully flat morphisms of
  `k`-affinoid spaces.
- Proposition 4.1.3, printed p.83 (PDF p.80): a presheaf representable by a
  `k`-analytic space good over `X` is a sheaf on the big flat quasifinite site,
  hence on the étale site of `X`. Its proof of uniqueness of the descended
  morphism states that it does not use the goodness assumption.
- Corollary 4.1.4, printed p.84 (PDF p.81): the étale topology on `Ét(X)` is
  weaker than the canonical one.
- Remark 4.1.5, printed p.84 (PDF p.81): Proposition 4.1.3 should hold without
  goodness; it would suffice that the image of an affinoid domain under a finite
  étale morphism of `k`-affinoid spaces be a finite union of affinoid domains.

Conrad–Temkin, *Non-archimedean analytification of algebraic spaces* (2009),
registered as `conrad-temkin-2009-nonarchimedean-analytification`, SHA256
`ecfb9dad9c7e27ec36e3a40111ea140fc3bf2589f41b58f97e036e4e2ce6394e`
(printed page = PDF page):
- §1.5, p.4: with Berkovich spaces the absolute value of the ground field may
  be trivial (unlike the rigid-analytic setting).
- p.18, the paragraph before Theorem 4.1.2: representable functors on
  `k`-analytic spaces are étale sheaves within good spaces by [Ber2, 4.1.5],
  and in general once Theorem 4.1.2 is proved.
- Theorem 4.1.2, pp.18–19: the image of a quasi-compact analytic domain under a
  finite étale map is a finite union of affinoid domains; this is the input
  named in Berkovich's Remark 4.1.5. Its proof passes by an analytic extension of
  the base field to the strict case with nontrivial absolute value; a
  nontrivial absolute value is not a hypothesis of the theorem.
- p.19, the paragraph after Example 4.1.3: consequently representable functors
  are étale sheaves on the category of `k`-analytic spaces (used for quotients).

## Why the statement is expected to hold

By Conrad–Temkin, Theorem 4.1.2 with Berkovich 1993, Proposition 4.1.3 and
Remark 4.1.5, the functor `Y ↦ Hom(Y, T)` on `k`-analytic spaces is a sheaf for
the étale topology, over any non-Archimedean ground field, trivially valued
included. Restricting it to the étale objects over a fixed `X` gives the
presheaf of this statement, so both uniqueness and existence of amalgamations
are predicted by the sources. A proof must establish this descent for the
Library's localized category and its generated topology.

## Provenance: derived

The proposition is a derived interface to representable étale descent, not a
verbatim theorem of either source. The big-site functor `U ↦ Hom(U, T)` is
restricted to the small site of étale objects over `X`. In Berkovich's
over-base formulation the same functor is represented by `X × T` over `X`;
this explains the provenance, and no such product is constructed or assumed
here.

## Place in the Library

At universes `.{u, u}` the proposition is word for word the second premise of
`Langlands.Analytic.SpaceAnalytification.ExistenceReduction.SpaceAnalytificationExistenceFiveInputReductionStatement`,
so `EtaleHomSheafStatement.{u, u}` can be passed as that premise of
`spaceAnalytificationExistenceFiveInputReduction` without conversion. Its
presheaf is the one in the sheaf hypothesis of the module
`Langlands.Geometry.Analytic.EtaleHomGluing`. It does not provide stability of
étaleness under composition or base change, existence of analytic pullbacks, or
surjectivity onto matching pairs of points of a pullback.
