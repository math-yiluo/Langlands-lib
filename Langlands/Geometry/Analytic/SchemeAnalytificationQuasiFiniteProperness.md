# Properness detection for quasi-finite scheme morphisms over an affine base

Let `K` be complete, ultrametric and nontrivially normed. For affine `X`
locally of finite type over `Spec K`, `affineTopChartHomeomorph` identifies
the affine polynomial-locus top chart with the glued scheme space. Under
this identification the center map is the chart center followed by the
algebraic top chart isomorphism. The affine constructible-closedness
theorem therefore detects closedness of constructible subsets using the
glued center map.

For an open immersion `j` of schemes locally of finite type over `Spec K`,
compatibility of centers places the analytic image over the algebraic
image. Conversely, an affine open of the source containing a preimage of
the center has affine open image. The chart range criterion puts the given
analytic point in that image chart. The algebraic isomorphism between the
two affine opens gives inverse maps on their polynomial-locus spaces.
Gluing yields a point in the analytic image of `j`.

Suppose `q` is separated, quasi-compact, locally quasi-finite and locally of
finite type, between schemes locally of finite type over `Spec K`, with
affine target. Factor it as an open immersion into an affine scheme finite
over the target. The analytic composite is the analytification of `q` by
uniqueness and composition of the glued maps. The intermediate affine
analytification is Hausdorff by its top chart. If the composite is
topologically proper, the first analytic map is also proper and has closed
image. This image is the full center preimage of the quasi-compact open
image of the algebraic immersion. That open is constructible, so it is
closed by the affine center comparison. The first algebraic map is then a
closed immersion, and `q` is finite.

This proof combines the finite form of Zariski's Main Theorem with the
Library's chart and center comparisons. It proves the affine-target,
quasi-finite case of properness reflection underlying Berkovich,
*Spectral theory and analytic geometry over non-Archimedean fields* (1990),
Proposition 3.4.7(3), printed p. 66 (PDF p. 80), registered as
