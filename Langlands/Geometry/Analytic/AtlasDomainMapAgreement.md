# Full bounded-map agreement across containing local domains

This internal construction uses the actual common-container local-domain intersection, full-domain base change, and bounded finite-cover descent. It retains the independent coefficient, ambient-point and original-index universes.

`restriction_eq_of_common_container` takes two local domains S and T, each included in both U and V, and bounded maps from the unchanged S and T algebras into an arbitrary actual presented affinoid test algebra. Agreement after the U restrictions implies agreement after the V restrictions. The proof forms the actual common-container intersection of S and T inside U. The bounded pushout lift factors the two maps through its unchanged quotient algebra. The computed quotient-leg restrictions recover the original maps. The two composite local-domain inclusions from this intersection to V are equal by uniqueness; composing their restriction identity with the lift proves the desired equality. This argument proves equality of full algebra maps, including on nilpotents; equality of spectrum-point maps alone is not used for algebra-map agreement.

`CommonSubdomainFamily U V` is finite data of local domains included in both containers. It does not require that the whole of U or V be covered. For a bounded test map from U.algebra into R, `CoversTest` says its actual spectrum image, expressed by U's ambient map, lies in the union of these common domains. Pulling their full relative-domain representations back along that test map constructs `testCover`, an actual finite full-domain cover of the whole test algebra R.

Each `testLocalMap` is the original V restriction followed by the actual base-change map. On every full-domain overlap in the test cover, the two maps from U.algebra agree by their original base-change squares. The preceding common-container theorem transports this agreement to V.algebra. Bounded finite-cover descent constructs `testFactor : V.algebra → R`; its full local identities and uniqueness from those identities are proved. Norm control comes from the published finite-cover inverse restriction bound, so no open mapping theorem or nontrivial coefficient valuation is required.

The module assumes no global intersection presentation of U and V. It does not establish ideal effectiveness, the affinoidness of their literal intersection, or tensor admissibility. It derives reusable agreement and test-map gluing for the eventual representation proof. All radii, zero quotients, empty spectra, nonreduced algebras and empty finite covers are permitted.
