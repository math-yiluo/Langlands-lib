# Induction on equivariant étale sites

Fix a complete ultrametric normed field \(K\) and an open subgroup \(H\) of a topological group \(G\). Let \(X\) be a Hausdorff \(K\)-analytic space with a continuous \(H\)-action by \(K\)-analytic automorphisms. Induction sends an equivariant étale object over \(X\) to its disjoint union of copies over the induced \(G\)-space. It acts on morphisms by the induced \(K\)-analytic map on each copy. The continuity and étale preservation theorems for induced spaces supply these objects and maps.

The induced étale functor is an equivalence. Its full faithfulness follows by placing it inside the induction equivalence on the equivariant quasi-étale sites. For essential surjectivity, a quasi-étale preimage of an étale object is étale. Indeed, compose the induced structure arrow with the inclusion of one source copy. The result is étale and equals the original arrow followed by the target copy inclusion. The latter is étale, so right cancellation gives étaleness of the original arrow.

The point map of an induced arrow leaves the coset coordinate fixed and applies the original map to the point in the copy. Thus a jointly surjective family remains jointly surjective on each copy, and testing any one copy gives the original jointly surjective family.

The equivalence transports the Grothendieck topologies. Each covering sieve contains a jointly surjective family. Conversely, any jointly surjective sieve covers. An arrow of a pushed-forward sieve factors through the induction of an arrow of the original sieve. If the composite hits a point, the induced arrow also hits it. This identifies covering sieves along the induction equivalence.

This is the étale site equivalence of Berkovich, *Étale equivariant sheaves on p-adic analytic spaces* (unfinished 1995–1996 manuscript), §1.2, Example 1.2.1(i), printed/PDF p. 3, restricted to actions over the fixed ground field.
