# Actual special finite chart coverings and original-chart examples

This module implements the special-cover condition for a finite family of actual original charts: the literal finite union is compact Hausdorff, its pairwise intersections are represented by original charts, and the actual completed tensor maps to those overlaps are admissible epimorphisms. It constructs those maps from the stored original restrictions and proves that every original chart is a special subset by an actual singleton covering.

The source is Berkovich, *Étale cohomology for non-Archimedean analytic spaces* (1993), §1.2, printed p.20 / PDF p.17, the definition of special subsets and coverings immediately before Lemma 1.2.12. The registered source is `berkovich-1993-etale-cohomology`, PDF SHA256 `06b42aca1c8f237e607d2cf1f15a41fdc9718324a4dc9fd5bce66574254bb91c`. This definition uses the source's convention that compact spaces are Hausdorff.

The tensor and admissibility meanings are those of Berkovich, *Spectral Theory and Analytic Geometry over Non-Archimedean Fields* (1990), §1.1, printed pp.11–12 / PDF pp.25–26: admissibility is equivalence of the actual residue seminorm and image seminorm, and the nonarchimedean tensor is the completion for the infimum of finite maximum factor-norm products. The registered source is `provided-a03a0182668f6b92b01deacac09a9992b3edf55e23a0a2194554384ae44bd8d4`, PDF SHA256 `9b543e7ffae8ec6febf154a60c4d11a288f7ecc3795c66f004ba3200a6824e79`.

`FiniteChartGluingData` already stores finitely many original indices and actual original indices representing each literal pairwise intersection. Its `locus` is their literal finite union, whose compactness has been proved from the compact original spectra. `overlapTensorMap` now constructs the source's genuine completed-tensor homomorphism by extending the two stored bounded restrictions. Both original leg equations hold as full bounded-map identities.

`overlapPresentationMap` constructs the same map in the finite closed weighted Tate presentation of the tensor. `overlapPresentationMap_comp_fromCompletedTensor` proves its equality with the genuine completed-tensor map after the constructed bounded comparison. Thus the completed tensor in the definition is an actual projective completion, with a proved affinoid presentation comparison, rather than an arbitrary supplied output algebra.

`IsSpecialCover` adds Hausdorffness of the actual literal union and admissibility of every constructed overlap map. These are additional source conditions; neither is inferred from represented intersections alone. Compactness follows from the existing finite-family construction, so compactness together with the explicit Hausdorff condition matches the source convention. No separation assumption is placed on the ambient space.

`singleton` constructs a finite gluing family whose member and overlap are the unchanged original parent chart. Its locus is exactly the original chart range, with the inherited subspace topology. The original chart embedding identifies this range with its compact Hausdorff Banach spectrum and supplies Hausdorffness of the singleton union.

For `isSpecialCover_singleton`, the original restriction to the same chart is the full bounded identity. The genuine tensor overlap map consequently has a bounded right inverse given by the first actual completed-tensor leg. The previously proved bounded-section criterion establishes admissibility. This proves singleton specialness from the constructed maps and bounds, including the zero-algebra/empty-chart degenerate case permitted by the project, without an open mapping theorem or a nontrivial-valuation hypothesis.

`IsSpecialSet` expresses existence of such a finite original-chart covering with literal union equal to the specified subset. `isSpecialSet_chart_range` supplies the actual singleton witness. The final compactness and Hausdorff results unpack a witness and transport the proved properties across equality of literal subsets.

Coefficient, ambient-point and original chart-index universes remain independent. This module defines specialness and proves original-chart examples. It does not prove Lemma 1.2.12, which extends represented intersections and admissibility to all original charts contained in a special subset and uses Kiehl's theorem in the source. It does not claim that every finite chart family is special, nor assume affinoid recognition or the maximal atlas as an output object.
