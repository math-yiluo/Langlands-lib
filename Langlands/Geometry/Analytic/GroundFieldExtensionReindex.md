# Reindexing the quotient for ground field extension

Let `a` be an indexed affinoid atlas over a complete ultrametric normed field `K`, and let `L` be a complete ultrametric normed extension, given by `NormedAlgebra K L`. The ambient space of `a` is unchanged by reindexing. The point space of its scalar extension, however, is the quotient of a disjoint union of the extended chart spectra. Its type depends on the choice and universe of the chart indices.

Given an equivalence `e : J ≃ a.Index`, `reindexPointMap` sends the class of the point `s` in chart `i` of the extension of `a.reindex e` to the class of the same `s` in chart `e i` of the extension of `a`. `glued_reindex_iff` identifies the two gluing relations: a witnessing common subchart maps forward along `e`, and a witness in the old indices lifts along `e.surjective`. The same equivalence of gluing relations proves injectivity of the quotient map, and every chart representative has a preimage because `e` is surjective. The quotient topology and continuity of the inclusions of the chart spectra give continuity in both directions, yielding `reindexHomeomorph`.

The homeomorphism commutes with projection to the original ambient space (`projection_reindexHomeomorph`). Transporting the extended reindexed atlas along this homeomorphism gives exactly the reindexing of the original extended atlas (`transport_atlas_reindex`): the chart maps, actual algebras and bounded restrictions agree, not just the underlying chart loci. The universes of the two fields, the original ambient space and the two index types are independent. No global Hausdorffness, saturation, or nontrivial norm is required.

This comparison is derived for the Library's quotient implementation of gluing in ground field extension, following Berkovich, *Étale cohomology for non-Archimedean analytic spaces* (1993), §1.4, printed p. 30 (PDF p. 27), `berkovich-1993-etale-cohomology`. It makes no descent or analytification assertion. It allows the chart index universe to change before field extension while retaining the original spectra, bounded restrictions and projection afterwards.
