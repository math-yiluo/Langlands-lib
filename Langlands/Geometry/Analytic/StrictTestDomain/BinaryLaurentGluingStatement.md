# Scalar descent for the binary unit-radius Laurent cover

Author: Worker 742. The focused target was specified by Worker 146 as a
prerequisite of the whole-spectrum scalar descent work.

`Langlands.Analytic.StrictTestDomain.BinaryLaurentGluingStatement.{u}` is a
proposition-valued definition, not a theorem or an assumption of descent. It
quantifies over every `K : Type u` with `NormedField K`, `IsUltrametricDist K`
and `CompleteSpace K`, every `P : PresentedStrictAffinoidAlgebra K`, every
`f : P`, and sections `sUpper : (laurentUpper f).algebra` and
`sLower : (laurentLower f).algebra`. The constructors and maps are those of
namespace `Langlands.Analytic.StrictTestDomain.RationalData`, opened in the
module. The hypothesis and conclusion are

```lean
(interLeft (laurentUpper f) (laurentLower f)).restriction sUpper =
    (interRight (laurentUpper f) (laurentLower f)).restriction sLower →
  ∃! a : P, (laurentUpper f).baseMap a = sUpper ∧
    (laurentLower f).baseMap a = sLower
```

So a pair of sections that agree on the overlap comes from exactly one
element of `P`. The scalar field and the base algebra use the same arbitrary
universe `u`, and the unapplied declaration has type `Prop`.

## The actual objects

All carriers, base maps and restrictions are existing Library constructions;
nothing is supplied as an extra input. By the published `BinaryLaurentCover`
module, `laurentUpper f` has arity one, denominator `1` and numerator `f`.
Its locus is `x f ≤ 1`. `laurentLower f` has arity one, denominator `f` and
numerator `1`, with locus `1 ≤ x f`. Each `.algebra` is the existing actual
closed-ideal Tate quotient `RationalLocalization.algebra`. It is the quotient
of the one-variable strict Tate algebra over `P`'s presentation by the closure
of the ideal generated by the presentation relations together with the series
lifts of `1 * Y - f` and `f * Y - 1` respectively. Each `.baseMap` is the
existing bounded structural map from `P`.

The overlap is the existing `(laurentUpper f).inter (laurentLower f)` from
`RationalLimits`. Its arity is `(1 + 1) * (1 + 1) = 4` and its denominator
is `1 * f`. Its numerators are `RationalIntersection.numerators`, the pairwise
products of the denominator-extended tuples `(1, f)` and `(f, 1)`. In
`finProdFinEquiv` order they are `f`, `1`, `f ^ 2` and `f`. The overlap
quotient therefore has four variables `Y₀, …, Y₃` and relations
`(1 * f) * Yₐ - nₐ` for these numerators `nₐ`. By the published
`mem_locus_inter_laurentUpper_laurentLower`, its locus is `x f = 1`. The two
restrictions are the published bounded maps
`(interLeft _ _).restriction` and `(interRight _ _).restriction`. These are
the unique bounded maps over `P` given by the proved strict-test
representation property. The published `interLeft_restriction` and
`interRight_restriction` identify them with `RationalIntersection.leftMap`
and `rightMap`. No replacement output algebra or arbitrary restriction is
introduced.

## Scope

This is scalar degree-zero descent for one binary cover. Uniqueness of `a`
is exactness of the Cech complex at `P`. Existence is exactness at the
product of the two quotient algebras. The statement asserts no higher Cech
exactness and no surjectivity onto the overlap. It also asserts no
admissibility of Banach-module maps, no arbitrary radii and no descent for
general finite modules. It gives no sheaf or gluing theorem for general
covers. No relative-series comparison, exactness, gluing, sheaf or norm
detection hypothesis is assumed.

No nontrivial valuation, reducedness, nonzero base or nonempty-locus
assumption is made. The following edge cases stay in scope; they are
obligations for a future proof, not facts proved here.

- If `P` is the zero algebra, the conclusion says that `P` has exactly one
  element.
- If `f = 0` or `f` is nilpotent, the lower relation `f * Y = 1` makes `f`
  a unit in the lower quotient, so that quotient is zero. The overlap
  quotient, whose denominator `1 * f` must likewise become a unit, is zero
  too. The lower locus and the overlap locus are empty. Descent then says
  that the upper base map is bijective, i.e. that `P` is identified with its
  quotient by `Y = f`. This is a genuine obligation for the proof.
- Trivially normed scalar fields (`∀ c : K, ‖c‖ ≤ 1`) are included.

Universal quantification over `P` supplies later uses after base change. For
a bounded algebra map `φ : P → Q`, the published `pullback_laurentUpper` and
`pullback_laurentLower` give `(laurentUpper f).pullback φ = laurentUpper (φ f)`
and the analogous lower equality. Comparing restrictions after such a
transport is separate work, not part of this statement.

## Mathematical source and translation

The binary case of Tate acyclicity is Proposition 7.1.1 of Piotr Achinger,
*Introduction to non-Archimedean Geometry*, November 22, 2020, printed/PDF
p.64. It is registered as `achinger-2020-non-archimedean-geometry-nov22`,
retained file
`literature/Piotr Achinger, Introduction to non-Archimedean Geometry (November 22, 2020).pdf`,
SHA256 `666cc6a90efbfc05f5fa0156044a259beaabdf989c45b633542e1cd2cdc0ec95`.
For `X = Sp A` and `f ∈ A` it gives the sequence
`0 → A → A⟨f⟩ × A⟨f⁻¹⟩ → A⟨f, f⁻¹⟩ → 0`. Here `A⟨f⟩ = A⟨X⟩/(X - f)`,
`A⟨f⁻¹⟩ = A⟨Y⟩/(fY - 1)` and `A⟨f, f⁻¹⟩ = A⟨X, Y⟩/(X - f, fY - 1)`. The
printed statement omits the words "is exact", but the proof establishes
exactness. It does so from the exact Laurent-series sequence for
`A⟨X⟩ × A⟨X⁻¹⟩ → A⟨X, X⁻¹⟩` and the snake lemma. The proof's
`g₋₁ ∈ (fX⁻¹ - 1)A⟨X⁻¹⟩` is a typo for the element `g₋` defined just before.
We use only left exactness (uniqueness) and middle exactness (existence).
Theorem 7.0.1 on p.63 places the proposition in the proof of Tate
acyclicity. Achinger's standing convention on printed/PDF p.6 assumes a
complete field with nontrivial valuation.

The general form is Fact 2.2.6(i) of Vladimir G. Berkovich, *Non-archimedean
analytic spaces*, Trieste lectures, 2009, printed/PDF p.10. It is registered
as `berkovich-2009-trieste-analytic-spaces`, retained file
`/home/taskhub/central-library/Berkovich--Non-archimedean analytic spaces (Trieste lectures)--2009/Berkovich--Non-archimedean analytic spaces (Trieste lectures)--2009.pdf`,
SHA256 `8cfe4f6f35d5e717ac67c5c8f5ef9ff9185f460bbe5f9a10aeacd84cddeeb2ca`.
For a finite affinoid covering and a finite Banach module `M`, it asserts
that the augmented Cech complex `0 → M → ∏ M_{V_i} → ∏ M_{V_i ∩ V_j}` is
exact and admissible. It is stated as a fact, without proof. We take `M = A`
and the two Laurent domains `{|f| ≤ 1}` and `{|f| ≥ 1}`, the case `p = q = 1`
of Exercise 2.2.2(i), p.9, with all radii one. Berkovich's base field may be
trivially valued, but his affinoid domains are tested against all affinoid
algebras. The Library's representation property tests only presented strict
affinoids.

The Lean statement is an internal translation of these sources, not a
literal transcription. The following comparisons are not proved by this
module or, as far as the current Library shows, anywhere else.

- Achinger's direct relative series quotient `A⟨X⟩/(X - f)` must be compared
  with the actual presentation quotient used by `RationalLocalization.algebra`.
- Achinger's two-variable overlap `A⟨X, Y⟩/(X - f, fY - 1)` must be compared
  with the actual four-variable `inter` quotient. Both should represent the
  locus `x f = 1`, so the comparison is expected from the universal property.
- Achinger's `Sp A` uses maximal ideals, while the Library loci consist of
  multiplicative spectrum points.
- Under nontrivial valuation every ideal of an affinoid algebra is closed.
  The Library quotient takes an explicit closure instead.

For trivially normed `K` the analytic argument does not apply, and the
intended route is algebraic. The published but unproved
`TrivialScalarRationalLocalizationStatement` asserts that each rational
quotient is `IsLocalization.Away` its denominator along its base map. For the
upper input the denominator is `1`, so its base map would be bijective. The
lower quotient and the overlap would be localizations away from `f` and
`1 * f`. The overlap restriction from the lower quotient commutes with the
base maps, and both are localizations away from the image of `f`, so it
would be injective. Take `a` to be the inverse image of
`sUpper` under the upper base map. The overlap image of the lower base map
at `a` is then the overlap base map at `a`. That equals the left restriction
of `sUpper`, hence the right restriction of `sLower`. Injectivity gives
`(laurentLower f).baseMap a = sLower`, and uniqueness follows from the
upper bijection. This sketch is guidance for the successor proof task. It
depends on that unproved statement and proves nothing here.
