# Comparison of the strict and relative binary Laurent diagrams: target

Author: Worker 966. Worker 572 specified the target as a prerequisite for the
proof of the unchanged `BinaryLaurentGluingStatement`. This module contains
one proposition-valued definition and nothing else. It proves no comparison,
and it changes no imported Library pair.

## The proposition

`Langlands.Analytic.StrictTestDomain.BinaryLaurentDiagramComparisonStatement.{u}`
is a `def` of type `Prop`, with the single universe parameter `u`. It
quantifies over

- every `K : Type u` with `NormedField K`, `IsUltrametricDist K` and
  `CompleteSpace K`;
- every `P : PresentedStrictAffinoidAlgebra K`;
- every `f : P`.

Inside the module the namespace
`Langlands.Analytic.StrictTestDomain.RationalData` is opened, and
`PresentedAffinoidAlgebra` resolves to
`Langlands.Analytic.PresentedAffinoidAlgebra`. Below, `BinaryLaurent` means
`Langlands.Analytic.PresentedAffinoidAlgebra.BinaryLaurent`. The statement
introduces these local `let` abbreviations, in this order:

```lean
let A := PresentedAffinoidAlgebra.ofStrict K P
let e := PresentedAffinoidAlgebra.strictEquiv K P
let fRel : A := e.symm f
let dUpper := laurentUpper f
let dLower := laurentLower f
```

Each `let` is definitional shorthand only; it adds no hypothesis. The
conclusion asserts that the following exist together:

```lean
eUpper : BoundedAlgEquiv K dUpper.algebra (BinaryLaurent.Upper K A fRel)
eLower : BoundedAlgEquiv K dLower.algebra (BinaryLaurent.Lower K A fRel)
eOverlap : BoundedAlgEquiv K (dUpper.inter dLower).algebra
  (BinaryLaurent.Overlap K A fRel)
```

and they must satisfy the conjunction of five pointwise squares:

```lean
(∀ a : P, eUpper (dUpper.baseMap a) =
  BinaryLaurent.upperBase K A fRel (e.symm a)) ∧
(∀ a : P, eLower (dLower.baseMap a) =
  BinaryLaurent.lowerBase K A fRel (e.symm a)) ∧
(∀ a : P, eOverlap ((dUpper.inter dLower).baseMap a) =
  BinaryLaurent.overlapBase K A fRel (e.symm a)) ∧
(∀ sUpper : dUpper.algebra,
  eOverlap ((interLeft dUpper dLower).restriction sUpper) =
    BinaryLaurent.left K A fRel (eUpper sUpper)) ∧
(∀ sLower : dLower.algebra,
  eOverlap ((interRight dUpper dLower).restriction sLower) =
    BinaryLaurent.right K A fRel (eLower sLower))
```

The three equivalences and all five laws are existence conclusions. Nothing
is assumed about them. A `BoundedAlgEquiv` is a `K`-algebra equivalence that
is bounded in both directions. The target does not require isometries.

## Direction of the base comparison

The published `strictEquiv K P : BoundedAlgEquiv K (ofStrict K P) P` runs from
the radius-one positive-polyradius presentation to the strict presentation.
Its inverse therefore carries strict elements to the coefficient algebra of
the relative diagram. So the relative coefficient is `fRel = e.symm f`, and
every coefficient square compares the strict base image of `a : P` with the
relative coefficient image of `e.symm a`. In the two restriction squares the
strict restriction comes first, then `eOverlap`. On the other side the relative
arrow `left` or `right` is applied after `eUpper` or `eLower`.

## The strict diagram

All strict objects and maps are existing published constructions:

- `laurentUpper f` (module `BinaryLaurentCover`) is the rational datum with
  arity one, denominator `1` and numerator `f`. Its locus is `x f ≤ 1`.
- `laurentLower f` has arity one, denominator `f` and numerator `1`. Its locus
  is `1 ≤ x f`.
- Each `.algebra` is `RationalLocalization.algebra`. This is the actual
  closed-ideal quotient of the strict Tate algebra in `P.arity + 1` scalar
  variables. The ideal is the closure of the ideal generated by the image of
  `P.ideal` and the equation `g * Y - n`, written with the chosen series lifts
  `seriesLift` of the denominator `g` and numerator `n`. Each `.baseMap` is the bounded
  structural map from `P`.
- The overlap is `dUpper.inter dLower` from `RationalLimits`. It has arity
  `(1 + 1) * (1 + 1) = 4` and denominator `1 * f`. Its numerators are the
  pairwise products of the denominator-extended tuples `(1, f)` and `(f, 1)`.
  In `finProdFinEquiv` order they are `f`, `1`, `f ^ 2` and `f`. So the strict
  overlap quotient has four fraction variables `Z₀, …, Z₃` with relations
  `(1 * f) * Zₐ - nₐ`. The target keeps exactly this four-variable
  intersection; no other overlap presentation replaces it.
- `(interLeft dUpper dLower).restriction` and
  `(interRight dUpper dLower).restriction` are the published bounded
  restriction maps of `RationalCategory`. Representability characterizes
  them as the unique bounded maps over `P`. Published `interLeft_restriction`
  and `interRight_restriction` identify them with `RationalIntersection.leftMap`
  and `rightMap`.

`CompleteSpace K` is needed for the strict rational quotients, `inter`, the
restriction maps and the relative arrows `left` and `right`.

## The relative diagram

Over `A = ofStrict K P` the published `BinaryLaurentDiagram` module supplies
the actual unit-radius relative-series quotients:

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{Upper}&=A\langle X\rangle/\overline{\langle X-C(f_{\mathrm{Rel}})\rangle},\\
\mathrm{Lower}&=A\langle Y\rangle/\overline{\langle C(f_{\mathrm{Rel}})Y-1\rangle},\\
\mathrm{Overlap}&=A\langle X,Y\rangle/
  \overline{\langle X-C(f_{\mathrm{Rel}}),\,C(f_{\mathrm{Rel}})Y-1\rangle}.
\end{aligned}
\]

It also supplies the contractive coefficient maps `upperBase`, `lowerBase` and
`overlapBase`, and the contractive arrows `left : Upper → Overlap` and
`right : Lower → Overlap`. The relative overlap has exactly two variables and
two relations. The target uses these actual objects and arrows.

## Why the overlap translation stays in the conclusion

The published general comparisons relate two presentations of the same
rational data:

- `StrictComparison` gives `strictEquiv` between `ofStrict K P` and `P`.
- `RelativeSeriesComparison` and `RelativeEquationComparison` compare scalar
  variable adjunction over `A` with relative series quotients over `A`.
- `RelativeRationalComparison` gives `RationalPresentation.relativeEquiv`, a
  bounded equivalence from a scalar rational presentation over `A` to
  `RelativeRationalPresentation.Algebra` with the same denominator and
  numerators.

The intended proof reuses these interfaces through a derived presentation
comparison. The strict rational quotient over `P` is transported to a scalar
rational quotient over the radius-one presentation `A`. The author's search of
the current Library found no published equivalence of exactly this kind:
`StrictComparison` and `StrictComparisonFunctor` compare the base
presentations and their maps only. The scalar rational quotient is then
compared with the relative quotient for the same data. For the upper and lower
data this gives relative quotients with denominator and numerator `(1, fRel)`
and `(fRel, 1)`. These are the data of `BinaryLaurent.Upper` and
`BinaryLaurent.Lower`, up to identifying the actual coefficient elements.

For the overlap, the same route stops at the relative rational quotient in
four variables

\[
A\langle Z_0,\dots,Z_3\rangle/
\overline{\langle f_{\mathrm{Rel}} Z_a - n_a \rangle},\qquad
(n_0,n_1,n_2,n_3)=(f_{\mathrm{Rel}},1,f_{\mathrm{Rel}}^2,f_{\mathrm{Rel}}).
\]

That is not `BinaryLaurent.Overlap`. The variables, the relations and the
denominator all differ. No published general interface identifies the two
quotients, because each one compares presentations of the same rational data
and nothing more. The four-variable to two-variable translation is therefore a
genuine additional step. It is part of the conclusion, through `eOverlap` and
its squares, and not an assumption. The expected construction uses that the
denominator becomes a unit on both sides (`fRel * Z₁ = 1`, respectively
`fRel * Y = 1`). It then substitutes `(Z₀, Z₁, Z₂, Z₃) ↦ (1, Y, X, 1)` and
`(X, Y) ↦ (Z₂, Z₁)`. Every substituted value has norm at most one. This
sketch is guidance for the proof successor and is proved nowhere here.

The two restriction squares are kept as separate conclusions, as the task
requires. They are not independent of the coefficient squares. In a private
development check, which is not part of this artifact, the author derived the
`interLeft` square from the upper and overlap coefficient squares. The check
used the published `RationalData.hom_ext`, `BinaryLaurent.left_upperBase` and
`Inclusion.restriction_comp_baseMap`; the `interRight` square is symmetric.

## Scope: unit radius and scalar sections

Every radius is one. The strict Tate algebras have radius one, `ofStrict` uses
`PositivePolyradius.unit`, and the relative quotients use the unit polyradii on
`Fin 1` and `Fin 2`. The target compares structure algebras, meaning scalar
sections, and their structural maps. It says nothing about finite modules.

The scalar field is any complete ultrametric normed field, and trivially normed
fields are included. There is no nontrivial-valuation, nonzero, reducedness or
nonempty-locus hypothesis. No analytic exactness, gluing, sheaf or assumed
quotient-comparison premise appears. Degenerate inputs stay in scope. If `f`
is zero or nilpotent, the strict lower and overlap quotients and the relative
`Lower` and `Overlap` are zero algebras, and the target then asks for
equivalences between those zero algebras that satisfy the squares.

This statement is not the strict binary gluing target. It does not change
`BinaryLaurentGluingStatement`, and it asserts no exactness, descent, higher
Čech exactness, finite-module descent, arbitrary-radius statement or
whole-spectrum descent. A separate proof task, published by Worker 572 after
this statement is published, will prove it.

## Original sources and provenance

The objects come from the following retained originals. Neither source states
this Library's concrete presentation equivalences, so the comparison target is
derived infrastructure.

Piotr Achinger, *Introduction to non-Archimedean Geometry* (November 22,
2020), is registered as `achinger-2020-non-archimedean-geometry-nov22`, SHA256
`666cc6a90efbfc05f5fa0156044a259beaabdf989c45b633542e1cd2cdc0ec95`. On printed
p.64, Proposition 7.1.1 and the first lines of its proof define
`A⟨f⟩ = A⟨X⟩/(X − f)`, `A⟨f⁻¹⟩ = A⟨Y⟩/(fY − 1)` and
`A⟨f, f⁻¹⟩ = A⟨X, Y⟩/(X − f, fY − 1)`. They also give the binary sequence
`0 → A → A⟨f⟩ × A⟨f⁻¹⟩ → A⟨f, f⁻¹⟩ → 0`. These are exactly the relative
presentations of the `BinaryLaurent` diagram. The source presents its overlap
directly in two variables. It neither writes the four-variable product
presentation nor compares it with the two-variable one. Printed p.6 assumes a
complete, nontrivially valued field. This target drops that assumption, since
the comparison concerns presentations and bounded maps and uses no
small-scalar reduction. The registered literature-index entry keeps the
nontrivial-valuation hypothesis only for that reduction.

Vladimir G. Berkovich, *Non-Archimedean analytic spaces*, Trieste lectures
(2009), is registered as `berkovich-2009-trieste-analytic-spaces`, SHA256
`8cfe4f6f35d5e717ac67c5c8f5ef9ff9185f460bbe5f9a10aeacd84cddeeb2ca`.

- Definition 2.1.6(i), printed p.8, defines the relative restricted series
  algebra `A{r⁻¹T}` with its weighted Gauss norm. Here all radii are one.
- Exercise 2.2.2(i), printed p.9, presents the Laurent domain algebra as
  `A{p⁻¹T, qS}/(Tᵢ − fᵢ, gⱼSⱼ − 1)`. With `p = q = 1` and one equation of each
  kind with `g = f`, this is the relative overlap `Overlap`.
- Exercise 2.2.2(ii) presents a rational domain algebra as
  `A{p⁻¹T}/(gTᵢ − fᵢ)`. With unit radius this gives `Upper` (`g = 1`,
  numerator `f`) and `Lower` (`g = f`, numerator `1`). These are also the
  denominator and numerator choices of the strict `laurentUpper` and
  `laurentLower`.

Exercise 2.2.2(iii) says only that an intersection of rational domains is
rational; it gives no presentation. The Library's product-numerator
presentation of `inter` is internal. In both sources the two overlap
presentations would describe the algebra of the same domain `{|f| = 1}`. That
motivates the comparison but does not state it. Fact 2.1.3(i), printed p.7,
says that ideals of affinoid algebras are closed. The Library quotients take
explicit closures instead. The strict side is a quotient of scalar Tate series
over the presentation of `P`, and the relative side a quotient of relative
series over `A`. Relating the two is the derived presentation comparison
described above.

The retained originals were read directly: Achinger printed pp.6 and 63–65,
and Berkovich printed pp.7–9. When checked on 2026-10-29, neither source had a
registered errata note in the project literature index, the project notes or
the current Library notes.
