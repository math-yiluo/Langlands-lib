# Flatness of the chart algebras of affine analytification

`Langlands.Analytic.AffineAnalytification.affineAnalytificationChart_flat`
proves `AffineAnalytificationChartFlatStatement.{u,v}`. For a complete
ultrametric nontrivially normed field `K : Type u` and a commutative
`K`-algebra `A : Type v` with a finite polynomial presentation `P`, the
theorem proves flatness of the actual map `PolynomialLocus.algebraMap`
for every chart index `m`.

## Flatness at unit radius

Write \(R=K[X_1,\ldots,X_n]\), \(S=K\langle X_1,\ldots,X_n\rangle\), and
let \(f:R\to S\) be the polynomial map at unit radius. For a maximal ideal
\(M\) of \(S\), set \(I=f^{-1}(M)\). The field \(S/M\) is finite over \(K\)
by strict affinoid Noether normalization. The normalizing Tate algebra
must itself be a field. In positive arity its constant-coefficient
homomorphism kills a nonzero coordinate, so its arity is zero.

Each coordinate in \(S/M\) has quotient norm at most one. For an element
\(x\) of a finite normed field algebra over \(K\) with \(\|x\|\le 1\), the
proof bounds all roots of its minimal polynomial in the algebraic closure with
the extended spectral norm. Each root is the image under a \(K\)-algebra
embedding. The embedding is bounded by finite dimensionality and
contractive by the bound for spectral points. The root bound puts the
coefficients of the monic minimal polynomial in the scalar norm unit ball
\(K^\circ\). Raising the resulting equations to the \(a\)-th power shows
that the coordinates of \(R/I^a\) are integral over \(K^\circ\).

More generally, for a polynomial quotient whose coordinates are integral
over \(K^\circ\), the \(K^\circ\)-algebra generated by those coordinates
is a finite \(K^\circ\)-module. The images of polynomials of Gauss norm at
most one belong to that module. A finite generating family bounds those images in
a chosen finite-dimensional \(K\)-vector-space norm. Dividing a nonzero
polynomial by a coefficient of maximal norm converts this bound to a
linear norm bound. The quotient map therefore extends as a continuous
linear map on the strict Tate algebra.

For a quotient by an ideal \(H\subset R\), this linear extension vanishes
on the extended ideal \(HS\) by polynomial density applied after
multiplication by each relation. It supplies injectivity of the canonical
map \(R/H\to S/HS\). The image of that map is dense by polynomial density
and closed by finite dimensionality, using closedness of ideals in the
Noetherian Banach algebra \(S\). This gives the quotient isomorphism for
\(H=I^a\). At \(a=1\), surjectivity also gives \(IS=M\). Indeed a lift in
\(R\) of the class of any element of \(M\) in \(S/IS\) belongs to \(I\).

Thus \(R/I^a\) identifies with \(S/M^a\) at every \(a\), and the
localization quotient isomorphism identifies this with \(S_M/\mathfrak n^a\)
for the maximal ideal \(\mathfrak n\) of \(S_M\). The isomorphisms commute
with the transition maps because they agree with the polynomial map on
representatives. Taking the adic limits identifies the two completions as
\(R\)-algebras. The \(I\)-adic completion of \(R\) is flat over the
Noetherian ring \(R\), so the \(\mathfrak n\)-adic completion of \(S_M\)
is \(R\)-flat. The latter completion is faithfully flat over \(S_M\).
Descent gives flatness of \(S_M\) over \(R\), and the target-local flatness
criterion gives flatness of \(f\).

## Rescaling and passage to the chart quotient

The chart radius is the norm of
\(c=(\texttt{AnalyticAffineSpace.scale}\ K)^{-m}\). Rescaling each variable
by \(c\) gives an algebra isomorphism from the weighted Tate algebra \(T\)
to the strict Tate algebra \(S\). On polynomials it agrees with the
polynomial automorphism that multiplies each coordinate by \(c\), followed
by \(f\). This proves flatness of the weighted polynomial map \(R\to T\).

For the presentation ideal \(J=\ker(P.\mathrm{map})\), the chart ideal is
the closure of \(JT\). Since \(T\) is Noetherian by rescaling and is a
Banach algebra, \(JT\) is closed. The proof identifies the chart ring with
\((R/J)\otimes_R T\) and identifies \(A\) with \(R/J\). On a polynomial
representative \(p\), it checks the compatibility of the actual chart map
with the base change map using \(1\otimes f_T(p)=[p]\otimes 1\), where
\(f_T:R\to T\) is the weighted polynomial map. Flatness under base change
and transport along the two ring isomorphisms then give the theorem.
