# Comparing the two orders of lifting and saturation

Fix an arbitrary indexed affinoid presentation `A` over a complete ultrametric normed field. Saturate `A` then lift its space and saturated indices to a common larger ambient width, or lift `A` and then saturate it there. The two objects have the same literal lifted ambient space. Their chosen saturation algebras need not be definitionally equal.

For an original saturation chart, its actual local-domain representative transports to a local domain in the lifted presentation. The published `LocalDomainSaturatedTransport` identifies the corresponding chart range and gives a bounded algebra equivalence to the new chosen saturation algebra, with its chart equation and restriction naturality. `liftSaturationHom` uses the identity on the lifted space; at an arbitrary admissible target chart it restricts to this corresponding chart and then uses the inverse bounded equivalence. The restriction square is checked using the transported equivalence's restriction law and the two restriction chains leading from the same large target chart to the same small chart. Postcomposing a chart restriction by a bounded equivalence preserves its full represented domain, proving the quasi-isomorphism at every admissible pair. `localizedLiftSaturationIso` is the image of this quasi-isomorphism in the actual localization. The inverse still induces the identity on the lifted space, because the forward map is the identity there and the space functor preserves the inverse law.

At a chart coming from the original atlas, the two chosen saturated representatives are its whole domain in the respective presentations. To compute the bounded equivalence there, apply `ofReindexFunctor` to the relevant inclusion between local domains. Its composite with the reindexing counit arrow is an endomorphism of the transported whole domain, hence the identity by subsingleton uniqueness. This proves the full bounded identity for the comparison pullback at these original charts, after identifying the presented algebras. For a compatible strong map of original presentations, use one such pair of original charts containing the source and image. Both composites in `liftSaturationHom_naturality` have the same bounded pullbacks there, by the bounded identity formulas and recovery of original pullbacks under saturation. Restrict to each saturated source chart and use strong-map extensionality to obtain the whole naturality square. The square is proved before localization.

This is an internal comparison of the Library's choices, not a source claim about the effect of a field extension.
