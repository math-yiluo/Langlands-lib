# Spectrum identification target for a Weierstrass presentation

Author: Worker 36.

`Langlands.Analytic.WeierstrassPresentation.SpectrumIdentificationStatement`
concerns the actual enlarged finite Tate quotient and bounded base map in
`WeierstrassPresentation.Definitions`. The coefficient field K is complete
and ultrametric. The base is the quotient of K{X_1,...,X_n} by the closure
of the ideal generated by an arbitrary set S. The selected f_j are finitely
many series representatives. The constructed quotient adjoins unit-radius
variables Y_j, includes the old relations, and imposes Y_j - f_j = 0.

The proposition states three properties. The bounded map from the base
algebra has dense range for the quotient norm topology. The image of the
induced spectrum map is exactly the set of base points x with x(f_j) at
most one for every j, where f_j is evaluated through the base quotient
projection. Finally, this spectrum map is a closed embedding for the
published pointwise spectrum topologies. The last two properties identify
the quotient spectrum topologically with the indicated closed subspace.
The density assertion concerns the base algebra in this Weierstrass
quotient, not its image in an individual completed residue field.

Both variable counts may be zero, and zero quotients are allowed; their
normalized multiplicative spectrum is empty. No spectrum point, field
realizing a point, inverse map, dense-range witness, or analytic-domain
universal property is an input.

The source context is Berkovich's *Non-archimedean analytic spaces*
(Trieste 2009), Exercise 2.2.2(i), p.9, for the unit-radius Weierstrass
quotient and inequalities, and Fact 2.2.3(i),(iv), p.10, for spectrum
identification and dense base image. This is a derived formulation: the
Lean quotient uses one enlarged Tate algebra over K and the closure of
the combined relations, while the source uses relative convergent series
over the base. The source's domain theorem is stronger. The statement
does not identify relative series with this presentation, remove the
chosen representatives, or prove factorization of arbitrary affinoid
maps from containment of their spectrum images. The latter is part of
Definition 2.2.1, p.9, and remains a separate obligation.
