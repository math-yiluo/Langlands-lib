# Obstruction from pulling back a two-piece quasi-étale cover

Take an arrow `f : U ⟶ V` and a presieve of two arrows `d` and `e` into
`V`. The definition `twoPiecePullbackSieve f d e` generates the sieve
of this presieve and pulls it back along `f`. Its arrows have composites
with `f` that factor through `d` or `e`. Write \(D'\) and \(E'\) for
the inverse images along `f` of the underlying images of `d` and `e`.
Each arrow of `twoPiecePullbackSieve f d e` has image contained in \(D'\)
or \(E'\).

Suppose `U` is Hausdorff and a point \(x\) of `U` lies outside \(E'\)
with \(D'\) failing to be a neighbourhood of \(x\). For a finite family
of compact subsets of \(D'\) or \(E'\), the union of the subsets
contained in \(E'\) is closed and misses \(x\). If the whole union were
a neighbourhood of \(x\), removing that closed set would give a
neighbourhood of \(x\) contained in \(D'\).

The theorem `not_exists_precoverage_le_twoPiecePullback` applies this
argument to the finite union of chart images required at \(x\) by the
precoverage. A chart range is compact, and its image under a site
morphism is compact. The theorem `not_dense_iUnion_range_of_twoPiecePullback`
applies the same argument to a finite family from compact objects. The
union of the images is closed, so density would make it the whole
space.

The conclusion of
`not_hasFiniteCompactQuasiEtaleRefinements_of_twoPieceCover` is the
failure of `HasFiniteCompactQuasiEtaleRefinements X` for the topology
defined in `QuasiEtaleSite`. It assumes two inputs.

1. A two-arrow precoverage family with a pullback along `f` from a
   compact Hausdorff object `U`, and a point `x` satisfying the two
   boundary conditions above. The generated topology makes the
   pulled-back sieve covering.
2. Pointwise lifting for the underlying point functor of the site.
   This makes covering sieves jointly surjective. Generating presieves
   are jointly surjective by their neighbourhood condition. Pointwise
   lifting preserves joint surjectivity under pullback, and the top and
   transitivity cases follow by choosing points directly.

Every arrow of a sieve generated by a family factors through a member
of the family. Joint surjectivity therefore gives a dense union of
images of its generators, which contradicts the boundary argument.
`not_hasFiniteCompactQuasiEtaleRefinements_of_twoPieceCover_of_dense`
uses just the density of covering finite compact families on `U` in
place of the pointwise lifting input.

## Doubled-disc example

Take the closed radius-two disc \(B\) and its closed unit subdisc \(D\).
The Gauss seminorms of radii greater than \(1\) approach the radius-one
Gauss seminorm, so the radius-one Gauss point belongs to \(D\) and lies
in the closure of \(B\setminus D\).

Glue two copies of \(B\) along \(B\setminus D\). Folding the copies
together is locally an isomorphism and gives a quasi-étale object over
\(B\). The arrow from \(D\) into the first copy and the inclusion of
the whole second copy have images whose union is the double. The two
full affinoid charts therefore satisfy the finite-union rule for the
published precoverage. Pulling back along the first copy inclusion
gives the inverse-image loci \(D\) and \(B\setminus D\). The radius-one
Gauss point satisfies the boundary conditions for these loci. The
doubling and pointwise lifting enter the Lean result through the two
inputs above.

This internal reduction records an obstruction for the finite-union
rule as formalized in the Library. Berkovich, *Vanishing cycles for
formal schemes* (1994), §3, p. 544 gives the finite-union wording.
Berkovich, *Étale cohomology for non-Archimedean analytic spaces* (1993),
Proposition 1.3.3(a), p. 24 gives the open gluing used in the example.
His quasinet condition in §1.1, pp. 13–14 also requires membership of
the point in every selected set. At the first copy of the radius-one
Gauss point, only the image from \(D\) contains the point, and that
image fails to be a neighbourhood.
