# Actual field coordinates and the full centralizer action for type R1 roots

`QuasiSplitR1RootFieldCoordinatesStatement` is an unproved existence proposition for the type R1 structural prerequisite of the apartment construction. It applies to every field `k : Type u`, every actual affine quasi-split connected reductive group, and every prescribed actual maximal split torus inclusion `i`. There is no valuation, topology, henselianity, characteristic, residue-field, rank or split-group restriction.

The root `a` is an actual indivisible relative root, using the Library's `RelativeRoot.indivisibleRoots`, and `2 • a` is absent from the actual relative roots. Indivisibility means that `a` is not a positive integral multiple of order greater than one of another actual root. Together with the absence of its double, this selects type R1 in the source's relative root system. Roots of type R2 and their divisible type R3 doubles are outside this proposition.

For every actual group-scheme representative `j : Z → G` of the full all-test torus centralizer and every factor `s : S → Z` satisfying `s ≫ j = i`, the proposition requires a `RootFieldCoordinates i j s a`. This package contains an actual finite separable field extension `E/k`, a multiplicative equivalence from `Multiplicative E` to the existing `rootGroupPoints i a`, and an actual rational character `β` of the base change `Z_E`. Here `Multiplicative E` denotes the additive group of the field, not its ordinary multiplicative group. The restriction of `β` along `s_E` is exactly the base change of the root character `a`, as an equality of scheme characters. For every `z ∈ Z(k)` and `x ∈ E`, the package requires

`j(z) · e(x) · j(z)⁻¹ = e(β(z_E) · x)`

as an equality in the original ambient rational-point group. The point `z_E` is the existing actual rational scalar extension. The field, its finite-dimensional and separable structures, the equivalence, and the character are outputs. A centralizer datum, character-evaluation bijectivity, field coordinates, an action identity, or a valuation is not an extra premise. The statement is universal in the supplied actual centralizer presentation, so a consumer can use any constructed representative and factor.

The source is Kaletha–Prasad, *Bruhat–Tits Theory: A New Approach* (2023), section 2.6, Notation 2.6.11 and equation (2.6.1), printed pp.96–97 (PDF127–128). A type R1 relative root lifts to a single Galois orbit of pairwise orthogonal absolute roots. Choosing one lift gives its finite separable field of definition `E`, an `E`-defined absolute root line, and the restriction-of-scalars identification of the actual relative root group. A nonzero vector in that absolute root line gives the additive coordinate; conjugation by the maximally split maximal torus acts through the corresponding absolute root character. This character is defined over `E`, restricts to `a`, and on the coordinate is scalar multiplication. These observations give the displayed rational-point consequence of the source's scheme-level identification. The statement records that consequence; it does not assert the stronger natural isomorphism on arbitrary coefficient algebras.

Construction 2.9.16, equation (2.9.2), printed p.117 (PDF148), gives coordinates compatible with a Chevalley–Steinberg system in the semisimple simply connected case. Construction 6.1.17, printed pp.205–206 (PDF236–237), composes the inverse of those field coordinates with the unique extended valuation. The present target asks only for one root and its whole-centralizer action, so it neither assumes nor asserts a compatible Chevalley–Steinberg system. The general connected reductive scope of the root-group identification comes from section 2.6 under Assumption 2.6.10, rather than from the narrower hypotheses of section 2.9(c).

The 2024 corrections, PDF25 and PDF32–33, retain the positive character-valuation convention and replace the later reductive apartment reduction by the adjoint group. They do not change the type R1 identification used here. This proposition alone supplies neither a proof of its existence conclusion nor the global reflection and commutator laws, multipliable-root coordinates, normalizer-stable equipollence class, centralizer character-evaluation bijectivity, or apartment existence. Those remain separate obligations of `QuasiSplitIntegerApartmentExistenceStatement`.
