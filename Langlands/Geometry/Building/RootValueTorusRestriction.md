# Actual scalar root functions under split-torus restriction

This is an internal prerequisite for the actual building comparison. It starts
with an existing upper-torus root datum valuation and constructs a scalar family
on the original rational points for the smaller torus. Its finite level sets
are exactly the canonical actual subgroups from `RootDepthTorusRestriction`.
It proves normalization, all parts of V1, and uniqueness from the level sets.

Let `k : Type u` be any field. Let `G/k` be affine and smooth, let `S` and `T`
be split commutative group schemes, and let `i : T.toGrp ⟶ G` be monomorphic.
Retain `RelativeRoot.HasOppositeFactorPairs i` and an existing upper family
`φ` with `hφ : IsRootDatumValuation i φ`. A homomorphism `f : S ⟶ T` gives the
smaller-torus map `j = groupHom f ≫ i`. The scalar construction and its
properties need no monomorphism of `f` or `j`, no maximality hypothesis, and
no premise asserting a lower valuation. The universe `u` is arbitrary.

## Coordinate bounds and attained cutoff

Write `H(α,r) = torusRestrictedRootDepthSubgroup hφ f α r`. This canonical
subgroup is generated by the upper root filtrations at depths `m * r`, where
an actual upper root `χ` restricts to `m • α` with `m > 0`.

For a nonzero smaller-torus character `α`, take any injective finite list `a`
whose range is exactly the roots indivisible in the selected restriction
semigroup, and multipliers `m` satisfying `f ≫ a j = m j • α`. For any tuple
`X` in the original upper root groups with ordered product `g`,
`mem_torusRestrictedRootDepthSubgroup_iff_coordinate_bounds` proves

`g ∈ H(α,r) ↔ ∀ j, m j * r ≤ φ (a j) (X j)`.

The values and inequalities on the right are in `WithTop ℝ`. This theorem
uses the exact range, injectivity, and restriction equalities; it imposes no
additional positivity hypothesis on the supplied multipliers. The later
threshold theorem uses the existing enumeration theorem, which supplies
positive multipliers automatically.

For an actual smaller-torus root point `g ≠ 1`,
`exists_torusRestrictedRootDepth_threshold` supplies `t : ℝ` such that, for
every real `r`,

`g ∈ H(α,r) ↔ r ≤ t`.

Thus the cutoff is finite and attained. This result applies to every nonzero
character `α` and actual point of its intrinsic root group, whether or not
`α` is an actual relative root. The unique-cutoff theorem
`existsUnique_torusRestrictedRootDepth_threshold` shows that `t` depends only
on the canonical subgroup family. No enumeration, ordering, or auxiliary
cocharacter is part of its characterization. There is no discreteness,
continuity, or sign assumption on the depths.

## Actual scalar family and V1

`torusRestrictedRootFunctionFamily hS hT hE hφ f` is a
`RootFunctionFamily S G` on the unchanged group `rationalPoints G`.
For an actual lower relative root `α` and a nonidentity point of
`rootGroupPoints j α`, its value is the real cutoff just constructed, viewed
in `WithTop ℝ`. Its value is top at the identity, outside that actual root
group, and for every character which is not an actual lower relative root.
The off-root and off-group normalization theorems state these properties
explicitly. On an actual root group,
`torusRestrictedRootFunctionFamily_eq_top_iff` proves that the value is top
exactly at the identity.

`le_torusRestrictedRootFunctionFamily_iff` identifies every real scalar lower
bound with membership in `H(α,r)` on an actual root group.
`rootFiltration_torusRestrictedRootFunctionFamily` then identifies the full
lower `rootFiltration` with this actual subgroup. The three theorems ending
in `one_mem`, `mul_mem`, and `inv_mem` prove the identity, multiplication,
and inversion parts of V1. Together with the exact top-value criterion, this
is all of V1 in the existing `IsRootDatumValuation` convention.

`torusRestrictedRootFunctionFamily_unique` proves that any scalar family with
the same off-root and off-group normalizations and these finite level sets
is the constructed family. This characterizes the construction without its
implementation choices.

## Implemented proof and provenance

This is a source-free internal derivation from existing Library constructions,
not a translation of a new literature assertion. Intrinsic coordinate
uniqueness identifies any filtered factorization with the fixed tuple `X`.
The exact ordered filtered-product theorem gives the converse membership
implication.

For the finite cutoff, restrict the finite coordinate set to those whose upper
values are finite. It is nonempty for `g ≠ 1`: otherwise the upper exact
top-value law makes every coordinate the identity and their product is `1`.
Choose real representatives `v j` of these finite values and take a coordinate
minimizing `v j / m j`. Its existence follows from finiteness. Positivity of
`m j` turns the simultaneous scaled bounds into `r` bounded above by that
minimum. Coordinates with top value impose no bound. Evaluating two cutoff
characterizations at their own endpoints proves uniqueness.

The scalar definition chooses the cutoff in the nonidentity root case and
uses top elsewhere. Its cutoff characterization yields the exact finite
filtrations. Subgroup closure supplies V1, and equality of all real lower
bounds determines a `WithTop ℝ` value, proving scalar-family uniqueness.

V0, reflection, commutator, double-root and opposite laws for the lower family
are not proved here. Neither split-torus compatibility nor a full lower
`IsRootDatumValuation` is asserted. The unit also supplies no initial valuation,
apartment, geometric building witness, or special point and unique-fixation
comparison for the supplied integral model. The unchanged
`BuildingComparisonStatement` remains unproved.
