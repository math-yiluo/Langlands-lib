# KP Lemma 2.6.27 for the actual minimal Levi subgroup, from its structural inputs

Worker 1532. This module is an internal conditional assembly toward the first
conjunct of
`Langlands.Geometry.Building.ZeroSubgroupDatum.MinimalLeviZeroComparisonStatement`,
namely `E.CentralizerZeroNormKernelStatement`: if the ambient group `G` is simply
connected, the actual minimal-Levi zero subgroup `Z(F)^0` equals the norm-one
subgroup `Z(F)^1`. It is the local-field part of Kaletha–Prasad, *Bruhat–Tits
Theory: A New Approach*, Lemma 2.6.27 (printed page 105). It does not prove the
second conjunct (the intersection assertion of KP Corollary 2.6.28), and it does
not prove the structural inputs listed below.

Throughout, `K = UnramifiedClosure F`.

## Declarations

### `characterValuationKernel_le_derivedRange_sup_torusNormIwahori`

This is the `K`-level core of KP's argument. It is stated without zero data, so
that other assemblies can reuse it. It takes `hC hF hB1`, a simply connected `G'`
over `K`, a split subtorus `a : A → G'`, a connected reductive `H` with
`jH : H → G'` representing the centralizer of `a`, and a derived subgroup
`dH : D → H`. It also takes a maximal torus `t : T → H` with `T` affine, and a
finite Galois `L ⊆ F^sep` over `K` such that `T_L` is split. Its conclusion:

`characterValuationKernel (fixedFieldValuation F) H ≤ (rationalPoints.map dH).range ⊔ (torusNormIwahori K L (intermediateValuation F L) T).map (rationalPoints.map t)`.

In words, every `m ∈ H(K)^1` lies in the subgroup generated by `D(K)` and the
image of `T(K)^0`. No maximal-splitness of `t` is assumed.

Proof as implemented:

1. `hC` gives `q : H → D'`, an affine fppf quotient by the range of `dH`. For `t`
   and `L`, `D'_L` is split and `ν ↦ ν ≫ (t ≫ q)_L` is surjective. By `hF`,
   `D'` is induced.
2. `map_characterValuationKernel_le` puts `q(m)` in the character-valuation
   kernel of `D'`. By `hB1` this kernel is `D'(K)^0`. By `torusIwahoriSurjectivity`
   it is the image of `T(K)^0` under `t ≫ q`. Choose `s ∈ T(K)^0` with
   `q(t s) = q(m)`.
3. Put `n = (t s)⁻¹ m`. Then `q(n) = 1`, so `n` lies in the range of `dH`, by
   `mem_range_kernelι_iff` and the fppf-quotient identification of the kernel of
   `q` with that range.
4. `m = t(s) · n` lies in the supremum (`Subgroup.mem_sup_right`,
   `Subgroup.mem_sup_left`).

### `ZeroSubgroupDatum.isClosedSubgroup_centralizer` and `ZeroSubgroupDatum.isAffine_centralizer`

Fix an actual `E : ZeroSubgroupDatum i j`. Then the inclusion `j : Z → G`
represents the centralizer of the split torus `S`. The Library theorem
`fieldCentralizerRepresentability` gives a closed subgroup `c : C → G` that also
represents it. Here `G` is affine over `F` because it is connected reductive, and
`S` is affine over `F` because `i` is a closed subgroup of `G`.
`IsRepresentedBy.isoOfIsRepresentedBy_hom_comp` writes `j` as an isomorphism
followed by `c`, and `IsClosedSubgroup.iso_comp` shows that `j` is a closed
subgroup. Hence `Z` is affine (`IsClosedSubgroup.isAffineHom`,
`isAffine_of_isAffineHom`).

### `ZeroSubgroupDatum.centralizerZeroNormKernel_of_inputs`

`centralizerZeroNormKernel_of_inputs E hC hF hG hB1 hsc : E.CentralizerZeroNormKernelStatement`.
This is the main declaration. It is a proof-valued `def` (with `linter.defProp`
disabled), following the Library convention for conditional reductions.

Proof as implemented. Assume `G` is simply connected. The inclusion
`Z(F)^0 ≤ Z(F)^1` is the Library theorem
`centralizerZero_le_rationalCommonNormKernel`; we prove the reverse inclusion.
Let `m ∈ Z(F)^1`, let `b` be the base map of `K`, and let `ZD = E.centralizerDatum`.
`ZD` is the actual `UnramifiedZeroDatum` of `Z_K`. It consists of a maximally
split maximal torus `ZD.i : T → Z_K`, a finite Galois `L` splitting `T`, the
derived subgroup `ZD.d : D → Z_K` and the simply connected cover `ZD.π : C → D`.

1. *Base change.* By `hsc`, `G_K` is simply connected. The image of `S` in `G_K`
   is a split subtorus (`IsSplitTorus.baseChange`,
   `IsClosedSubgroup.baseChange`). `torusCentralizer_baseChange` and
   `IsRepresentedBy.baseChange` show that `j_K` represents its centralizer.
2. *Derived subgroup.* By `hG`, `D` is simply connected. The central isogeny
   `ZD.π` is therefore an isomorphism (`IsSimplyConnected.central_isogeny_isIso`,
   using that `C` is semisimple). So `D(K) = (rationalPoints.map ZD.d).range` is
   contained in `Z(K)^♮` (`mem_simplyConnectedImage`, with the point `π⁻¹(y)`).
3. *Descent.* `characterValuationKernelDescent_proof` rewrites `m ∈ Z(F)^1` as
   the statement that the image `m_K` of `m` in `Z(K)` lies in the
   character-valuation kernel of `Z_K`.
4. *Core.* The core theorem, applied to `G_K`, `S_K`, `j_K`, `Z_K` (connected
   reductive by the datum), `ZD.d`, `ZD.i` (maximal) and `L`, puts `m_K` in
   `D(K) ⊔ T(K)^0`. By step 2 and `simplyConnectedImage_le_zeroPoints`,
   `map_torusIwahori_le_zeroPoints`, this supremum lies in
   `Z(K)^0 = Z(K)^♮ · T(K)^0`. Since `Z(F)^0` is defined as the preimage of
   `Z(K)^0` (`centralizerZero`, `mem_anisotropicZeroPoints`), `m ∈ Z(F)^0`.

The core theorem and the main declaration both raise `maxHeartbeats` to 1000000.
Elaborating these proofs exceeds the default limit, mainly in the application of
`torusIwahoriSurjectivity`.

## Relation to KP's proof

KP prove `M_0(K)^1 ⊆ M_0(K)^0`. They take a maximally split maximal `K`-torus
`T ⊂ M_0`, which is induced, put `T_sc = T ∩ M_{0,der}`, and note that `T/T_sc` is
induced. They then apply Lemma 2.5.18 to the image in `T/T_sc` and lift by Lemma
2.5.20.

Here the quotient `T/T_sc` is replaced by the cocenter `D' = Z_K / D`. The inputs
`hC` and `hF` state that it exists and is induced. Lemma 2.5.20(1) is applied to
the composite `T → Z_K → D'`, whose cocharacter map is surjective by `hC`. KP's
step "`t⁻¹m ∈ M_{0,der}(K) ⊂ M_0(K)^0`" becomes steps 2 and 4: `D` is simply
connected (`hG`), so `D(K)` is the cover image. The route therefore does not use
that `T` itself is induced, nor that `T_sc` is induced.
