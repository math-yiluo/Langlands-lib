# Split integral pinnings from root parametrizations over a field

Let `k` be a field, `I` a finite type and `coordTorus k I` the coordinate split
torus `splitTorus (Spec k) I`, viewed as a commutative group scheme. Let `G` be a
group scheme over `Spec k` with affine structure map and `i : splitTorus I ⟶ G`
a homomorphism whose underlying morphism of schemes is a monomorphism. Let
`P : RootDatum J (I → ℤ) (I → ℤ)` be a coordinate root datum. For a map
`w : 𝔾ₐ ⟶ G` and `c ∈ k`, `fieldRootPoint w c ∈ G(k)` is the value of `w` at `c`
(`rootPoint w` at the base point, with `c` read as a global function through
`baseRingEquiv`). For a root `α`, `rootChar P α` is the character
`splitTorus.exponentCharacter (P.root α)` of the coordinate torus.

This module builds a split integral pinning (`SplitIntegralPinning`, module
`Langlands.Geometry.Building.SplitIntegralPinning`) of `i` from a family of root
parametrizations `w α` of the roots of `P`. The torus `i` is maximal and split,
`P` is the root datum of `i`, and the simple Weyl points of a base act by the
simple reflections. The integral subgroup `K` and three integrality conditions on
it are hypotheses. The module is meant to be applied to the base change to a
field of the root data of a split integral model.

## Provenance

This is an internal construction of the Library; no published source states it
in this form. It records the Chevalley-system part of Kaletha–Prasad (*Bruhat–Tits
Theory: A New Approach*, §6.1(a), printed pp. 196–198) in the split case. There
the root parametrizations `u_{o,a}` come from an integral model, and the integral
group is generated by the integral torus points and the `u_{o,a}(𝔬)`. The
rank-one relation `m(u_a(c)) = a^∨(c) · m(u_a(1))` is the standard identity in
`SL₂`; here it is derived from the Library's rank-one root-cell relation. Generation of the Weyl group by the simple
reflections is the Coxeter presentation of the relative Weyl group, which the
Library proves as `relativeWeylCoxeter`.

## Definitions

- `coordTorus k I`: the coordinate split torus as a commutative group scheme.
- `baseRingEquiv : k ≃+* Γ(Spec k, ⊤)`, the inverse of `Scheme.ΓSpecIso`.
- `fieldRootPoint w c`: the rational point `w(c)`.
- `rootChar P α`: the character of the root `α`. It is injective in `α`
  (`rootChar_injective`).
- `rootFamily P w a c`: equal to `fieldRootPoint (w α) c` if `a = rootChar P α`
  (`rootFamily_rootChar`), and `1` if `a` is not the character of a root.
- `simpleWeylPoint b hwS j hj`: for `j` in the support of a base `b`, the point
  `w(-j)(-1) · w j (1) · w(-j)(-1)` as an element of the normalizer points of `i`.
  Here `-j` is the index `P.reflectionPerm j j`.
- `coordEval`: evaluation of a linear form on the translation space of `i` at the
  coordinate cocharacters `e_l`, `l ∈ I`.
- `doubleRhoCheck P b`: the exponent cocharacter of the sum of the positive
  coroots, `2ρ^∨`.

## Main construction

`SplitIntegralPinning.ofRootParametrizations v hG hi hred hP b hu hwS K hK_u
hK_torus hK_closure : SplitIntegralPinning (S := coordTorus k I) v i` has the
following hypotheses:

- `v : Valuation k ℤₘ₀`, `[DecidableEq I]`, `[Fintype J]`,
  `[Mono i.hom.hom.left]`;
- `hG : IsConnectedReductive G` and `hi : IsMaximalTorus i`;
- `hred : P.IsReduced` and
  `hP : IsRootDatumOf i (CoordinateRootDatum.fieldRealization k P)`;
- `b : P.Base`;
- `hu : ∀ α, IsRootParametrization i (P.root α) (w α)`: each `w α` represents
  the root subgroup of `rootChar P α`, is a closed subgroup and is
  torus-equivariant;
- `hwS : ∀ j ∈ b.support, IsWeylPoint i (weylPoint (w j) (w (-j)))
  (P.reflection j)`;
- `K : Subgroup G(k)` with
  - `hK_u : ∀ α c, fieldRootPoint (w α) c ∈ K ↔ v c ≤ 1`;
  - `hK_torus`: if `t ∈ S(k)` has `ord(a(t)) = 0` for every relative root `a`,
    and `i(t)` lies in `characterValuationKernel v G`, then `i(t) ∈ K`;
  - `hK_closure`: `K` lies in the subgroup generated by
    `i(characterValuationKernel v S)` and the sets `w α {c | v c ≤ 1}`.

The pinning has integral subgroup `K`, root family `rootFamily P w` and simple
roots `rootChar P '' b.support`.

## How the fields are proved

- Roots: `relativeRoots_eq_range` says that the relative roots of `i` are the
  characters `rootChar P α`. Every root therefore has a unique index `α`
  (`exists_eq_rootChar`).
- `u_add`, `u_mem`, `u_surjective`, `u_eq_one`, `torus_conj`
  (`rootFamily_add`, `rootFamily_mem`, `rootFamily_surjective`,
  `rootFamily_eq_one`, `rootFamily_torus_conj`): additivity of `rootPoint`, the
  representing property of `w α` and its injectivity on points, and the
  equivariance field of `hu α` with
  `ChevalleyRootFamily.charValue_eq_rationalCharacterEval`.
- `rankOne` (`fieldRootPoint_rankOne`): the Library theorem
  `exists_fieldWeylNormalization` gives a scalar `d ≠ 0`. For `v' = d · w(-α)`, the
  pair `(w α, v')` satisfies the rank-one relation `RootCell.RelationOver` with
  the coroot `α^∨`, and `weylPoint (w α) v'` acts by the reflection of `α`. Put
  `m(c) = v'(-c⁻¹) w α (c) v'(-c⁻¹)`. The relation at `X = c`, `Y = 1 - c⁻¹`,
  together with `α^∨(c) v'(-1) α^∨(c)⁻¹ = v'(-c⁻²)` (since
  `⟨α, α^∨⟩ = 2`), gives `m(c) = α^∨(c) · m(1)` (`weylProduct_eq`). The
  factor pairs are `(v'(-c⁻¹), v'(-c⁻¹))` for `w α (c)` and `(v'(-1), v'(-1))`
  for `w α (1)`, and the torus point is `t = α^∨(c)`, with `α(t) = c²`
  (`torusCharEval_corootPoint`). The products lie in the normalizer because
  `m(1)` is a Weyl point and `α^∨(c)` centralizes the torus.
- `two_smul_not_mem`: `hasNoHigherRoots_fieldRealization` for the reduced datum.
- `u_mem_K`, `torus_mem_K`, `K_le_closure`: from `hK_u`, `hK_torus`,
  `hK_closure`. For `K_le_closure`, `w α {v ≤ 1}` is the image of `{v ≤ 1}` under
  the root family at `rootChar P α`.
- `simple_subset`, `weyl_mem`: `rootChar_mem` and `hwS` with
  `IsWeylPoint.mem_normalizerPoints`. `weylProduct_rootFamily` identifies the
  product of root-family values with `weylPoint (w j) (w (-j))`.
- `simple_linearIndependent` (`linearIndependent_rootForm_simple`): `coordEval`
  sends the root form of `rootChar P α` to the real vector `P.root α`
  (`coordEval_rootForm`). The simple roots of a base are linearly independent
  over `ℤ` (`b.linearIndepOn_root`), hence over `ℝ`
  (`linearIndependent_algebraMap_comp_iff`). `LinearIndependent.of_comp` then
  gives independence of the root forms.
- `induction` (`induction_rootChar`): Mathlib's
  `RootPairing.Base.IsPos.induction_on_reflect`, applied to positive roots, and to
  negative roots through the predicate `x ↦ Q(-x)`. The simple Weyl point of `j`
  acts on characters by the reflection `s_j` (`weylAction_exponentCharacter`,
  `weylAction_simpleWeylPoint_rootChar`).
- `weyl_generated` (`exists_weylAction_eq_of_simpleWeylPoints`): `2ρ^∨` pairs to
  `2 · height(α)` with each root (`latticePairing_doubleRhoCheck`), so it is
  generic. Its simple relative roots are characters of simple roots of `b`
  (`simpleRelativeRoots_doubleRhoCheck_subset`): a positive non-simple root `α`
  has a simple `j` with `α - j` a positive root, so `α` is a sum of two positive
  roots. The Library theorem `relativeWeylCoxeter` says that the simple relative
  reflections generate `N/Z`. The class of a simple Weyl point is the relative
  reflection of its root (`isRelativeReflection_simpleWeylPoint`, through
  `IsOppositeFactorPair.isRelativeReflection`), and relative reflections at a
  simple root are unique (`eq_of_isRelativeReflection_of_mem_simple`). Hence
  every class in `N/Z` is the class of an element `m` of the subgroup generated by
  the simple Weyl points. Two normalizer points with the same class have the same
  Weyl action (`weylAction_eq_one_of_mem_centralizerPoints`).
