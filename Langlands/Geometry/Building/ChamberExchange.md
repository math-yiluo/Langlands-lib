# Exchange of the opposite root-group products at a chamber point

This derived, internal unit proves the range half of the sharp Iwahori
factorization at a chamber point, an internal step toward Kaletha–Prasad
Proposition 7.3.12. Let `x` be a point of a chamber and `mu` a generic
cocharacter. The unit shows that the sharp fixer `G(k)^♯_x` lies in the set
product `X_x(Φ_{-mu}) · X_x(Φ_mu) · Z(k)^♯_x`. Here `X_x(P)` is the subgroup
generated by the root filtration groups `U_{b,x,0}` with `b ∈ P`. Opposite
factor pairs (the conclusion of Kaletha–Prasad Proposition 2.11.17) remain a
hypothesis, as in `ChamberRankOne`.

The core of the argument, `KPApartmentData.sharpFixer_subset_mul_of_exchange`,
holds at an arbitrary point `x` of the apartment and for an arbitrary subgroup
`H` on the right. It assumes the rank-one exchanges
`U_{-c,x,0} · U_{c,x,0} ⊆ U_{c,x,0} · U_{-c,x,0} · H` for all `c ∈ Φ`. At a
chamber point, `ChamberRankOne` supplies them with `H = Z(k)^♯_x`.

## Statement

`ChamberSharpExchangeStatement` quantifies over an arbitrary-universe field
`k`, a commutative group scheme `S` and an affine group scheme `G` over
`Spec k`, a monomorphism `i : S → G`, a map `ω : kˣ → ℝ` and apartment data
`D : KPApartmentData i ω`. It assumes

- `S` is a split torus (`IsSplitTorus S.toGrp`);
- the set `Φ = relativeRoots i` of relative roots is finite;
- the rationalizations of `Φ` and of the restricted torus characters span the
  rational character space;
- every nonidentity root point has an opposite factor pair
  (`RelativeRoot.HasRankOneFactorization i`);
- the extended affine roots of `D` form an affine root system with rank-zero
  data (`AffineRootSystem.IsAffineRootSystemWithRankZero`);
- their derivative roots are exactly the linear forms `rootForm i a` of the
  relative roots;
- AS 4 in the form: every extended affine root `ψ` of `D` satisfies
  `ψ ∈ D.affineRoots ∨ 2 • ψ ∈ D.affineRoots`.

For every chamber `C` of `D.affineRoots`, every `x ∈ C` and every generic
cocharacter `mu` (`IsGenericCocharacter i mu`: no relative root vanishes on
`mu`), it concludes the set inclusion

`G(k)^♯_x ⊆ (⨆_{b ∈ Φ_{-mu}} U_{b,x,0}) · (⨆_{b ∈ Φ_mu} U_{b,x,0}) · Z(k)^♯_x`,

where `G(k)^♯_x` is `D.sharpFixer {x}`, `Φ_mu` is
`positiveRelativeRoots i mu`, the factors are `D.chamberRootGroup x false b`
(that is, `U_{b,x,0} = D.filtration x b 0`), and `Z(k)^♯_x` is
`D.sharpCentralizer {x}`. The theorem `chamberSharpExchange` proves it. This
is the hypothesis `hcore` of Worker 1267's
`KPApartmentData.chamberSharpConclusion_of_core`.

## Definitions

- `realPositiveRoots i w` is `{c ∈ Φ | 0 < ⟨c, w⟩}` for a real vector
  `w ∈ V(S)`. `IsRealGeneric i w` says that no relative root vanishes on `w`.
- `IsRayCrossing i w w′` says that any two relative roots separating `w` and
  `w′` (with `⟨c, w⟩⟨c, w′⟩ < 0`) are proportional as linear forms on `V(S)`.
- `KPApartmentData.rootSpan x P` is `X_x(P) = ⨆_{b ∈ P} U_{b,x,0}`.
- `KPApartmentData.exchangeSet x H w` is
  `Y_x(H, w) = X_x(Φ_{-w}) · X_x(Φ_w) · H` for a subgroup `H` of `G(k)`, with
  `Φ_w = realPositiveRoots i w`.

## Supporting results

General lemmas:

- `le_normalizer_iSup_of_commutator_le`: a subgroup `K` with
  `⁅K, L j⁆ ≤ ⨆ j, L j` for all `j` normalizes `⨆ j, L j`.
- `eq_of_forall_single_crossing`: for a finite set `Λ ⊆ ℝ`, if `f s = f s′`
  whenever `s < s′` lie outside `Λ` with at most one point of `Λ` strictly
  between them, then `f s = f s′` for all `s < s′` outside `Λ`.
- `mem_Ioo_of_mul_neg` and `combination_pos_of_sameSign`: real sign
  arithmetic.

On real vectors: `IsRealGeneric.neg`, `IsRayCrossing.neg`,
`IsRayCrossing.symm` and `isRayCrossing_of_forall_pos` are elementary closure
properties. `combination_pos_of_crossing` says that a root `p c + q b` with
`c` separating `w, w′` and `b` positive on both is positive on both.
`tmul_ne_neg_smul_of_crossing` says that such a `b` is not a negative multiple
of `c`. `eval_injective` says that evaluation on `V(S)` is injective on the
characters of a split torus, via the perfect pairing
`CocharacterUniformizer.latticePairing_isPerfPair`.

On the apartment, for `x ∈ D.Apartment`:

- `filtration_le_rootSpan`, `rootSpan_mono`, `rootSpan_le_sup` and
  `rootSpan_eq_bot` are elementary properties of `X_x`.
- `filtration_le_normalizer_rootSpan` and `rootSpan_le_normalizer_rootSpan`:
  for generic `w, w′` with the ray-crossing property, `U_{c,x,0}` normalizes
  `X_x(Φ_w ∩ Φ_{w′})` for every separating root `c`. This is V 3 through the
  published `filtration_commutator_le`, and so is any subgroup generated by
  such groups.
- `eq_or_double_of_eval_eq_smul`: if relative roots `c, d` satisfy
  `⟨d, ·⟩ = t ⟨c, ·⟩` with `t > 0`, then `d = c`, `d = 2c` or `c = 2d`. The
  coroot integrality of the affine root system gives `t z₁ = 2` and
  `z₂ = 2t` with integers `z₁ z₂ = 4`. So `t ∈ {2, 1, 1/2}`, and
  `eval_injective` concludes.
The next four results share the following hypotheses: `S` is a split torus,
`Φ` is finite, the extended affine roots form an affine root system with
derivative roots `Φ`, `Φ = -Φ` (`hneg`), and for a point `x` and a subgroup
`H` the rank-one exchanges
`U_{-c,x,0} · U_{c,x,0} ⊆ U_{c,x,0} · U_{-c,x,0} · H` hold for every `c ∈ Φ`
(`hex`).

- `rootSpan_cross_mul_subset` (the rank-one step): with
  `D₁ = Φ_w ∩ Φ_{-w′}` and `D₂ = Φ_{-w} ∩ Φ_{w′}`,
  `X_x(D₂) · X_x(D₁) · H ⊆ X_x(D₁) · X_x(D₂) · H`.
- `exchangeSet_subset` and `exchangeSet_eq`: for generic `w, w′` with the
  ray-crossing property, `Y_x(H, w) = Y_x(H, w′)`.
- `exchangeSet_tmul_eq_neg`: for a generic cocharacter `mu`,
  `Y_x(H, 1 ⊗ mu) = Y_x(H, -(1 ⊗ mu))`.
- `sharpFixer_subset_mul_of_exchange`: for a generic cocharacter `mu`,
  `G(k)^♯_x ⊆ X_x(Φ_{-mu}) · X_x(Φ_mu) · H`.

Also, `mul_mem_exchangeSet` says that `X_x(Φ_{-w})` multiplies `Y_x(H, w)` into
itself on the left. Finally, `sharpFixer_subset_mul_of_mem_chamber` is the
conclusion of the statement. It is the case `H = Z(k)^♯_x` at a chamber point.
There, `-c ∈ Φ` comes from `neg_mem_relativeRoots_of_rankOne` and the exchanges
come from `filtration_neg_mul_filtration_subset` of `ChamberRankOne`, which
uses AS 4 and the chamber.

## Proof

*Rank-one step.* If `D₁` is empty, then so is `D₂`, because `-d ∈ D₁` for
`d ∈ D₂`. Here `-d ∈ Φ` by `hneg`. Both spans
are then trivial. Otherwise choose `c₁ ∈ D₁` with minimal `⟨c₁, w⟩`. Every
`d ∈ D₁` separates `w, w′`, so `⟨d, ·⟩ = t ⟨c₁, ·⟩` with `t > 0`. By
`eq_or_double_of_eval_eq_smul`, `d = c₁` or `d = 2c₁`. The case `c₁ = 2d`
contradicts minimality. Hence `X_x(D₁) = U_{c₁,x,0}` by
`filtration_double_le`. In the same way, `X_x(D₂) = U_{-c₁,x,0}`. The
inclusion is then the assumed exchange `hex` for `c₁`, followed by
`H · H = H`.

*One crossing.* Let `R = Φ_w ∩ Φ_{w′}` and `R⁻ = Φ_{-w} ∩ Φ_{-w′}`. By
genericity of `w′`, `Φ_{-w} ⊆ R⁻ ∪ D₂` and `Φ_w ⊆ R ∪ D₁`. The roots of
`D₁` and `D₂` separate `w, w′`, and they separate `-w, -w′`. So `X_x(D₂)`
normalizes `X_x(R⁻)` and `X_x(R)`, and `X_x(D₁)` normalizes `X_x(R)`. Hence
`X_x(Φ_{-w}) ⊆ X_x(R⁻) X_x(D₂)` and `X_x(Φ_w) ⊆ X_x(R) X_x(D₁)`. Also
`X_x(D₂)` and `X_x(D₁)` commute with `X_x(R)` as sets. Then

`Y_x(H, w) ⊆ X(R⁻) X(D₂) X(R) X(D₁) H = X(R⁻) X(R) X(D₂) X(D₁) H`
`⊆ X(R⁻) X(R) X(D₁) X(D₂) H = X(R⁻) X(D₁) X(R) X(D₂) H ⊆ Y_x(H, w′)`.

The middle inclusion is the rank-one step. The last
one holds because `R⁻ ∪ D₁ ⊆ Φ_{-w′}` and `R ∪ D₂ ⊆ Φ_{w′}`. Swapping `w`
and `w′` gives equality.

*Path from `ν` to `-ν`.* Let `ν = 1 ⊗ mu`. For pairs of relative roots
`(c, d)`, set `ℓ_{c,d} = ⟨d, ν⟩ ⟨c, ·⟩ - ⟨c, ν⟩ ⟨d, ·⟩`. Choose
`eta ∈ V(S)` with `ℓ_{c,d}(eta) ≠ 0` for every pair with `ℓ_{c,d} ≠ 0`. A
finite union of proper subspaces is not the whole space
(`Module.Dual.exists_forall_ne_zero_of_forall_exists`). Set
`w(s) = eta - s ν` and `λ_c = ⟨c, eta⟩ / ⟨c, ν⟩`, so that
`⟨c, w(s)⟩ = ⟨c, ν⟩ (λ_c - s)`. Then `w(s)` is generic for `s` outside the
finite set `Λ = {λ_c}`. A root separates `w(s)` and `w(s′)` exactly when
`λ_c` lies strictly between `s` and `s′`. Equal crossing values `λ_c = λ_d`
force `ℓ_{c,d} = 0`, that is, proportional roots. So two parameters with at
most one point of `Λ` between them have the ray-crossing property, and
`eq_of_forall_single_crossing` gives `Y_x(H, w(-B)) = Y_x(H, w(B))` for `B`
larger than every `|λ_c|`. No root separates `ν` from `w(-B)`, or `w(B)` from
`-ν`. So `Y_x(H, ν) = Y_x(H, -ν)`.

*Conclusion.* Here `Φ_ν = Φ_mu` and `Φ_{-ν} = Φ_{-mu}`, so `Y_x(H, ν)` is
the right-hand side of `sharpFixer_subset_mul_of_exchange`. Let `K` be the
subgroup of those `g` with `g Y_x(H, ν) ⊆ Y_x(H, ν)` and
`g⁻¹ Y_x(H, ν) ⊆ Y_x(H, ν)`. Every `U_{α,x,0}` with `α ∈ Φ` lies in `K`. If
`⟨α, mu⟩ < 0`, it is a factor of `X_x(Φ_{-ν})` and `mul_mem_exchangeSet`
applies. If `⟨α, mu⟩ > 0`, the same holds for `Y_x(H, -ν) = Y_x(H, ν)`.
Since `G(k)^♯_x` is generated by the `U_{α,x,0} = D.rootFixer α {x}`, it lies
in `K`. As `1 ∈ Y_x(H, ν)`, every `g ∈ G(k)^♯_x` lies in `Y_x(H, ν)`. The
chamber statement is the case `H = Z(k)^♯_x`, with `hneg` and `hex` supplied
as described under Supporting results.

## Provenance

The proof follows the structure of the proof of Kaletha–Prasad Proposition
7.3.12(1) (printed pp. 250–251, PDF pp. 281–282). That proof shows that
`X⁻ · X⁺ · N′` does not depend on the positive system, by replacing one
simple root at a time by its negative and using Lemma 7.3.11. It then
concludes from invariance under left multiplication by every `U_{a,x,f}`.
The Lean argument has three internal differences. It moves through the
positive systems along the line `s ↦ eta - s ν` instead of by simple
reflections. At each step it exchanges a whole ray `{c₁, 2c₁}` that crosses
the line. It uses `Z(k)^♯_x` in place of the group `N′` generated by the
rank-one groups `N^{(a)}_{x,f}`. So it needs neither AS 1 nor a
normalization of the root groups by `Z(k)^♯_x`. The rank-one input is
`ChamberRankOne`, which follows Kaletha–Prasad Proposition 7.1.1(1). V 3
enters through `filtration_commutator_le`. AS 4 enters through the chamber
hypothesis of the rank-one exchange. The specialization is the concave
function `f = 0`. The general core keeps the rank-one exchange with an
arbitrary subgroup `H` as a hypothesis. At a non-chamber point, Kaletha–Prasad
take `H` to contain the rank-one groups `N^{(a)}_{x,f}`. This module proves
the exchanges only at chamber points.
