# Root conjugation at positive depth

This derived construction proves that conjugating an element of the original `U_{-a,x,r}`, at positive `r`, by an element of `U_{a,x,0}` remains in the original Moy–Prasad subgroup. It constructs actual coordinates in `U_{-a,x,r} × Z(F)_r × B_{a,x,r}`, retaining both factors of the original positive block.

`filtration_same_commutator_le_double` applies V3 with both directions equal. A generic cocharacter verifies that they are not negative real multiples. The root-system bound on positive multiples forces the two commutator coefficients to be one. Thus the commutator lies in the actual doubled-root filtration at the sum depth, including the identity subgroup when that doubled direction is absent.

`exists_rankOne_real_depth_factorization` applies the published rank-one factorization at arbitrary real input depths with positive sum. It retains the input depth bounds and the central factor's preservation of the ordinary root valuation. Identity inputs are handled separately. `exists_double_real_depth_factorization` additionally applies the accompanying proved higher positive-difference estimate. Its positive output is an ordinary factor at depth `r+s` followed by the original doubled factor at depth `r`. The second input depth may be negative; no nonnegativity is assumed in this helper.

`conj_mem_filtration_of_translation` evaluates the actual normalizer action on the root-function family. It expresses the output depth as the input depth minus the translation evaluation in the output direction. `inv_conj_mem_filtration_of_preserves` uses the centralizer's trivial Weyl action and its valuation-preservation equation to retain the same depth under inverse conjugation.

For the main positive-coordinate estimate, write a nonidentity depth-zero input `u` using its actual opposite factor pair, with normalizer element `n = p1 u p2`. If the input valuations are `t ≥ 0` and `t' ≥ r > 0`, V5 gives depth `-t` for both pair factors. Set `c = v⁻¹ p2⁻¹ v p2`. The same-direction V3 bound puts `c` in the actual negative doubled-root filtration at depth `t'-t`. The reflection and translation formulas then put `n v n⁻¹` in the positive ordinary filtration at depth `2t+t'` and, when present, `n c n⁻¹` in the positive doubled-root filtration at depth `3t+t'`.

Real-depth mixed exchange moves the latter factor past `p1`, retaining a positive block at depth `r`; its absence is treated as the identity case. Ordinary real-depth exchange moves the remaining positive ordinary factor past the resulting negative factor. Valuation preservation by the first central factor keeps the positive ordinary coordinate at depth `r` when that central factor is collected. Closure of the actual original block subgroup absorbs its product with the retained block. This proves `conjugate_opposite_mem_root_centralizer_block`.

`rootConjugationMoyPrasadCoordinates` compares this alternate decomposition with the ordinary filtered rank-one decomposition. Actual opposite-cell uniqueness identifies the positive coordinate with the original block just bounded. The original filtered decomposition supplies the negative coordinate at depth `r` and, by commutator friendliness, the actual centralizer coordinate at depth `r`. Its product is exactly `u v u⁻¹`. The defining generators of the original Moy–Prasad subgroup give `conjugate_opposite_mem_moyPrasad`.

The full apartment axioms are supplied, and the final coordinates and Moy–Prasad containment also take commutator friendliness. No centralizer filtration is constructed. The result concerns the exactly opposite ordinary directions `a` and `-a`; it does not assert all negative proportional directions, full root normalization, covariance or the host simultaneous normal basis. All definitions, subgroups, depths and universes remain the original ones. Provenance is internal derivation from V3, V5, actual normalizer action, the published rank-one decomposition and opposite-cell uniqueness, and the accompanying doubled-root refinement and block subgroup.
