# Zero-group pointwise fixers and sharp fixers over the maximal unramified field

`Langlands.Geometry.Building.UnramifiedZeroFixerSharpComparisonStatement.{u}`
is an unproved, universe-polymorphic proposition interface. This module defines
the statement; it supplies no proof of it.

## Exact scope and hypotheses

For every `F : Type u` with `Field F`, `ValuativeRel F`, `TopologicalSpace F`
and `IsNonarchimedeanLocalField F`, put `K = inertiaFixedField F`. This is the
actual maximal unramified extension in the chosen separable closure, also
called `UnramifiedClosure F` by the zero-subgroup API. Both characteristics
and every universe `u` are retained.

The statement ranges over every group scheme `H` over `Spec K` whose
structure morphism is affine, and every supplied pair

```lean
C : MaximalUnramified.BuildingData F H
E : UnramifiedZeroDatum F H
```

There is no requirement that `H` be a scalar extension of a group over `F`.
The datum `C` already supplies connected reductivity of `H`, a maximal
split torus `S` with inclusion `C.i`, an `N(K)`-stable equipollence class of
valuations of the root datum, the actual representing centralizer
`Z = Z_H(S)` with inclusion `j = C.centralizer.j`, and full compatibility
V6 with the normalized integer valuation `MaximalUnramified.valuation F`.
The valuation-of-root-datum axioms V0–V5 are part of its apartment class.
The statement does not require another building-existence premise or any
additional apartment axiom.

The datum `E` supplies a maximally split maximal torus, a finite Galois
splitting extension inside the separable closure, and a simply connected
cover of the derived group. Its subgroup `E.zeroPoints` is
`H(K)^♮ ⊔ T(K)^0`, with the torus factor embedded in `H(K)`. These choices
need not agree with those in `C`.

For every `Ω : Set C.Apartment`, the two hypotheses are:

1. `Ω.Nonempty`;
2. `∀ f : C.Apartment →ᵃ[ℝ] ℝ, BddAbove (f '' Ω)`.

The second is boundedness in every real affine coordinate. The apartment
is a torsor for the finite-dimensional real space `V(S)/V(S)^Φ`, KP's
`V(S′)`. Applying the hypothesis to `f` and `-f` bounds both sides of each
coordinate, so this is the usual boundedness required by KP.

## The conclusion

All groups below are subgroups of the actual rational-point group `H(K)`.
The conclusion is exactly

```lean
E.zeroPoints ⊓ C.pointwiseFixer (C.apartmentMap '' Ω) =
  (E.zeroPoints ⊓ C.pointwiseFixer (C.apartmentMap '' Set.univ)) ⊔
    C.sharpFixer Ω
```

`C.apartmentMap x = [(1,x)]` maps the apartment into the reduced-building
quotient of `H(K) × C.Apartment`. The subgroup `C.pointwiseFixer Y` fixes
every point of `Y` individually. Thus the left side is the pointwise
stabilizer of the image of `Ω` in `H(K)^0`. The first term on the right
fixes the image of the entire apartment in `H(K)^0`; `Set.univ` is a set
of apartment points. It is independent of `Ω`.

`C.sharpFixer Ω` is KP's `H(K)^♯_Ω`: the subgroup generated by the root
fixers `U_{a,Ω,0} = ⋂_{x∈Ω} U_{a,x,0}`. The symbol `⊓` is subgroup
intersection and `⊔` is the subgroup generated by both factors.

## Original sources and corrected setting

The original is Kaletha–Prasad, *Bruhat–Tits Theory: A New Approach*
(2023), registered as `kaletha-prasad-2023-bruhat-tits-new-approach`,
SHA256
`05ad9a284a2577b34d1a270228b74dfb28f520561ae7772d8f38dddb78938732`.
It is read with the authors' corrections of 1 November 2024, registered
as `kaletha-prasad-2024-corrections`, SHA256
`23293f51e58d5ceecb91f834743f68058b98431797d2531142415d013fdf61ed`.
Both originals are in the central literature folder
`Kaletha-Prasad--Bruhat-Tits theory, a new approach (book)--2023/`.

Chapter 7 opens over a Henselian discretely valued field `k` with
`ω(k×) = ℤ`, a connected reductive group and compatible apartment classes
with their prescribed normalizer action (printed pp. 235–236 / PDF
pp. 266–267). The correction on PDF p. 33 adds AS5 to these assumptions.
In particular, the simply connected normalizer must have image precisely
the affine Weyl group; containing that group is insufficient. The related
proof corrections on PDF p. 35 explicitly use AS5 in Theorem 7.5.1.
The correction on PDF p. 25 also requires a Henselian field with perfect
residue field for §2.6(e), which contains the zero-group definition.

Here `k = K`. The maximal unramified algebraic extension of a complete
discretely valued local field is Henselian, its normalized value group is
`ℤ`, and its residue field is the algebraic closure of the finite residue
field of `F`, hence perfect. The source therefore applies even though
`K` itself is not a nonarchimedean local field. No such instance is
introduced in Lean.

Every connected reductive group over this `K` is quasi-split by KP
Corollary 2.3.8 (printed p. 81 / PDF p. 112). This applies to the arbitrary
`K`-group `H`, using the connected reductivity already in `C`.
The canonical quasi-split class is that of Definition 6.1.27. KP
Proposition 6.2.6 (printed p. 216 / PDF p. 247) says that any two
`ω`-compatible root-datum valuations are equipollent. Consequently every
class supplied by `C`, which has V6, is that canonical class, rather than
an extra choice of a potentially incompatible building. For the
quasi-split class Propositions 6.3.13 and 6.6.2 give AS5, as the
correction on PDF p. 33 explains; Proposition 6.6.2 is on printed
pp. 231–232 / PDF pp. 262–263. The canonical class also satisfies V7.
Thus the corrected Chapter 7 hypotheses hold for every supplied `C`;
the statement neither assumes them as new premises nor proves them here.

## Why the constant factor is the embedded centralizer zero group

Write `A = C.Apartment`, and identify it with its image in the building
as in Remark 7.6.5 (printed p. 266 / PDF p. 297). Define, only for this
explanation,

```text
Q = H(K)^0 ∩ Fix_H(K)(A).
```

KP Proposition 7.6.8 (printed p. 266 / PDF p. 297) identifies the
pointwise stabilizer of the entire apartment in `H(K)^1` with embedded
`Z(K)^1`. Since `H(K)^0 ⊆ H(K)^1`, it follows that

```text
Q = H(K)^0 ∩ j(Z(K)^1) = j(Z(K)^0).
```

The second equality is Corollary 2.6.28 (printed pp. 105–106 / PDF
pp. 136–137), applied to the minimal Levi `Z = Z_H(S)`. This also
proves the reverse inclusion: `Z(K)^0` is contained in `H(K)^0` and in
`Z(K)^1`, whose image fixes all of `A`. In the present quasi-split
setting `Z` is a maximally split maximal torus, but the minimal-Levi
form of the corollary already suffices.

Equivalently, a whole-apartment fixer normalizes the apartment and thus
lies in `N(K)` by the first part of Proposition 7.6.8. The action of
`N(K) ∩ H(K)^0` on `A` has kernel `Z(K)^0`, as used in the proof of
Proposition 7.7.5(1) via Theorem 7.5.3 and Lemma 7.4.7. This gives the
same identification.

The apartment need not be bounded. The argument above uses the
whole-apartment Proposition 7.6.8 and does not apply the bounded-set
Proposition 7.7.5 to `Set.univ`. It also avoids identifying this factor
with the full apartment fixer in `H(K)`, which can be larger.

## Derivation of the comparison and coverage of every datum

Definition 7.4.1(4) (printed p. 255 / PDF p. 286) defines the parahoric
group for a nonempty apartment subset by

```text
P_Ω = H(K)^0_Ω = j(Z(K)^0) · H(K)^♯_Ω.
```

For nonempty bounded `Ω`, Proposition 7.7.5(1) (printed pp. 269–270 /
PDF pp. 300–301) identifies this with its pointwise stabilizer in
`H(K)^0`. The product in Definition 7.4.1 is a subgroup, so it equals
the subgroup join. Substituting the whole-apartment identity just proved
gives the displayed Lean equality.

The proof of Proposition 7.7.5(1) reduces to singleton subsets using
Lemma 7.7.4 (printed pp. 268–269 / PDF pp. 299–300); its normalizer step
uses Theorem 7.5.3 and Lemma 7.4.7 (printed pp. 256–257 / PDF
pp. 287–288). This is why the AS5 correction matters to this
comparison. Lemma 7.7.3 on PDF p. 299 also identifies the intersection
of an actual root group with the full fixer as `U_{a,Ω,0}`, consistent
with the Library root-fixer definition. Both 7.7.3 and 7.7.4 are
labelled **Lemma** in the original.

For any supplied `E`, Definition 2.6.23(1) (printed p. 104 / PDF
p. 135) identifies `E.zeroPoints` with `H(K)^0`. The torus subgroup is
the norm-image Iwahori subgroup of Definition 2.5.13, independent of
the splitting extension by Lemma 2.5.12 (printed pp. 89–90 / PDF
pp. 120–121). The simply connected image is independent of the
chosen derived cover by its uniqueness up to isomorphism. Lemma
2.6.24 (printed pp. 104–105 / PDF pp. 135–136) removes the choice
of maximally split maximal torus. The product is a subgroup and hence
is the join used in `E.zeroPoints`. In particular `E` need not use
the centralizer torus or any choices from `C`. The Library theorem
`unramifiedZeroPointsIndependence` in module
`Langlands.Geometry.Building.UnramifiedZeroIndependenceProof`
already establishes independence for all supplied `E`.

This is a derived interface combining the cited source results with
the existing Library data. It avoids supplying a second zero datum for
`C.centralizer.Z`. No comparison target or affine-root-sign target is
assumed. The statement leaves its Lean proof to a separate task and
asserts neither existence of `C` nor an integral model.
