# One-group fixers over the maximal unramified extension do not see closures

`Langlands.Geometry.Building.UnramifiedOneFixerClosureStatement` is an
unproved proposition. The module also contains:

- a private instance saying that the base change of an affine group scheme
  is affine;
- the definition `unramifiedApartmentTopology` of the real affine topology
  on the apartment;
- one proved lemma, the easy inclusion of the statement.

## The statement

The proposition is universe polymorphic. At each universe `u` it ranges over
the following data:

- every `F : Type u` with `Field F`, `ValuativeRel F`, `TopologicalSpace F`
  and `IsNonarchimedeanLocalField F`, in equal and mixed characteristic;
- every group scheme `G` over `Spec F` with `IsAffineHom G.X.hom`.

Write `K = inertiaFixedField F` for the maximal unramified extension inside
the chosen separable closure, and `G_K = baseChanged F K G`. The private
instance records that `G_K` is again affine, by pullback-stability of affine
morphisms. The statement then ranges over:

- `C : MaximalUnramified.BuildingData F G_K`, the published supplied building
  data of `G_K` for the normalized integer valuation of `K`. An element
  supplies the connected reductivity of `G_K`, a maximal split torus, its
  actual KP apartment class with a representing centralizer datum, and
  `ω`-compatibility. No separate reductivity assumption on `G`, apartment
  axiom premise on `C`, or restriction on `g` is added.
- `g` in the rational points of `G_K`.
- A subset `Ωa` of `C.Apartment`.

It has two hypotheses:

1. `Ωa` is nonempty;
2. `Ωa` is bounded: every real affine function `f : C.Apartment →ᵃ[ℝ] ℝ` is
   bounded above on `Ωa`.

The conclusion is the equality of subgroups of `G(K)`

```lean
characterValuationKernel (MaximalUnramified.valuation F) G_K ⊓
    C.pointwiseFixer ((fun x => g • C.apartmentMap x) '' Ωa) =
  characterValuationKernel (MaximalUnramified.valuation F) G_K ⊓
    C.pointwiseFixer ((fun x => g • C.apartmentMap x) ''
      @closure C.Apartment (unramifiedApartmentTopology C) Ωa)
```

Here `characterValuationKernel (MaximalUnramified.valuation F) G_K` is the
common kernel of the valuations of all rational characters of `G_K`, that is
`G(K)^1`. `C.pointwiseFixer Y` is the pointwise fixer of `Y` for the action
of `G(K)` on the reduced building `C.ReducedBuilding`.
`unramifiedApartmentTopology C` is the initial topology of all real affine
functions on `C.Apartment`. It is defined without choosing a norm or an
origin, in the same way as the Library's `affineApartmentTopology` over `F`.
On a finite-dimensional real affine space it is the usual topology.

In words: the pointwise fixer in `G(K)^1` of a nonempty bounded subset of a
translated apartment `g · A` equals the pointwise fixer in `G(K)^1` of the
translate of its closure.

Chapter 7 works with a connected reductive group over a discretely valued
Henselian field `k` with perfect residue field, together with an equipollence
class of valuations of its root datum. Here `k` is `K`, whose residue field
is an algebraic closure of the finite residue field of `F`. The group is
`G_K`, which is quasi-split. The apartment class of `C` is the source's
class: its valuations are `ω`-compatible, and any two `ω`-compatible
valuations are equipollent (Proposition 6.2.6, printed p. 216 / PDF p. 247).
The corrections on p. 33 add AS5 to the Chapter 7 hypotheses and explain
that it is known for quasi-split groups. Thus the mathematical source route
applies over `K`; no AS5 witness is an extra input to this Lean statement.
The statement does not prove quasi-splitness, AS5, or the identification
with the source's building.

The ingredients are:

- Notation 2.6.15 and Lemma 2.6.16(4), printed pp. 102–103 / PDF pp.
  133–134. `G(k)^1` is the set of `g ∈ G(k)` with `ω(χ(g)) = 0` for every
  rational character `χ`. This is `characterValuationKernel`.
- Proposition 6.2.4, printed p. 215 / PDF p. 246. `N(k)` acts on the
  apartment `A` by affine transformations.
- Lemma 7.4.4(3), printed p. 256 / PDF p. 287. `G(k)^♯_Ω` is generated by
  the affine root groups `U_ψ` for all `ψ ∈ Ψ` with `ψ(Ω) ≥ 0`.
- §7.7, printed p. 267 / PDF p. 298. `N(k)^1_Ω` is the pointwise
  stabilizer of `Ω` in `N(k)^1 = N(k) ∩ G(k)^1`, and
  `G(k)^1_Ω = G(k)^♯_Ω · N(k)^1_Ω`.
- Proposition 7.7.5(1) for `∗ = 1`, printed p. 269 / PDF p. 300. For a
  nonempty bounded `Ω ⊂ A`, `G(k)^1_Ω` is the pointwise stabilizer of `Ω` in
  `G(k)^1`.

These give the conclusion for `g = 1` as follows. Let `Ω` be a nonempty
bounded subset of `A` and `Ω̄` its closure.

1. Every affine root `ψ` is an affine function, hence continuous, so
   `ψ(Ω) ≥ 0` if and only if `ψ(Ω̄) ≥ 0`. By Lemma 7.4.4(3),
   `G(K)^♯_Ω = G(K)^♯_{Ω̄}`.
2. Each `n ∈ N(K)` acts on `A` by an affine transformation (Proposition
   6.2.4). On this finite-dimensional real affine space the action is
   continuous, and its fixed-point set is the closed equalizer of that
   transformation and the identity. So `n` fixes `Ω` pointwise if and only
   if it fixes `Ω̄` pointwise, and `N(K)^1_Ω = N(K)^1_{Ω̄}`.
3. By the definition in §7.7, `G(K)^1_Ω = G(K)^1_{Ω̄}`.
4. `Ω̄` is nonempty since it contains `Ω`. If an affine function `f` is
   bounded above by `M` on `Ω`, the closed set `f⁻¹((-∞, M])` contains
   `Ω` and hence `Ω̄`. Thus `Ω̄` satisfies the same boundedness condition.
   In finite dimension this condition is the usual boundedness, since it
   bounds each coordinate and its negative. Proposition 7.7.5(1) for
   `∗ = 1`, applied to `Ω` and to `Ω̄`, turns step 3 into the equality of
   the pointwise stabilizers in `G(K)^1`.

Translating by `g ∈ G(K)` conjugates both pointwise fixers by `g`. `G(K)^1`
is the kernel of a homomorphism, hence normal in `G(K)`. So the equality
persists for `g · Ω` and `g · Ω̄`.

The source's building is the quotient of `G(k) × A` of Remark 7.6.5 and
(7.6.2), printed p. 266 / PDF p. 297, whose apartment is the reduced
apartment. This is the Library's `C.ReducedBuilding`, whose apartment
`C.Apartment` is an affine space over `V(S)/V(S)^Φ`.

Provenance is derived. The source states the ingredients above separately,
and Proposition 7.7.5 does not mention closures. The statement records their
combination on the Library's existing data, with a translation by `g` added.

## The proved lemma

`characterValuationKernel_inf_pointwiseFixer_closure_le` proves the inclusion
`⊇` of the statement for every subset `Ωa`, bounded or not. `Ωa` lies in its
closure, so its translate lies in the translate of the closure. An element
fixing the larger set pointwise fixes the smaller one. The proof unfolds
`pointwiseFixer` with the Library's `KPApartmentData.mem_pointwiseFixer` and
uses `subset_closure`. Only the inclusion `⊆` is the content of the
unproved statement.

## Choices and scope

- The fixers are intersected with `G(K)^1`, the `∗ = 1` case of
  Proposition 7.7.5(1).
- `Ωa` is bounded because Proposition 7.7.5(1) is stated for bounded
  subsets.
- The closure is the topological closure in the apartment. It is not the
  set `cl(Ω)` of KP Definition 4.1.14 (printed p. 162 / PDF p. 193), the
  union of the closures of all facets meeting `Ω`.

## Intended consumers

The construction of the integral models for KP Axiom 4.1.20 (task
`cd2ad671-3edf-4905-a0e2-e7aa2b160b91`) builds its full models over the
integers of `K` with integral points
`characterValuationKernel ⊓ C.pointwiseFixer (g · Ω)` (the statement
`UnramifiedParahoricModelStatement`). It descends them to `𝒪[F]` and
compares them through their points over the integers of `K`. KP Axiom
4.1.22 needs a model homomorphism from the full model of `Ω'` to that of
`Ω` whenever `Ω ⊂ cl(Ω')` in a common apartment. By the smooth-model
extension criterion (KP Corollary 2.10.10), this needs the inclusion of the
`K`-points. The affine map from the apartment of `G` to `C.Apartment` is
continuous, so it carries `Ω` into the closure of the image of `Ω'`. The
statement then gives the inclusion.
