# Rank-one normalizers for integer-valued building data

This internal helper extends the published
`RankOneReflectionGeneration` construction and proofs to
`IntegerValuationBuildingData v G` over an arbitrary field `k : Type u`.
It uses the actual subgroup `reflectionNormalizer` of
`IntegerRootReflectionWeyl`. The supplied datum includes connected
reductivity and a maximal split torus; no topology or local-field
hypothesis is added. The proofs retain an explicit
`RelativeRoot.HasOppositeFactorPairs B.i` parameter, which can always be
supplied by `B.hasOppositeFactorPairs`.

`rootPairNormalizer a` consists of actual normalizer points sending the
character `a` to `a` or `-a`. `rankOneReflectionNormalizer a` is its
intersection with `reflectionNormalizer`. `rankOneReflectionCell a`
consists of products `u * n * v`, with `u, v` in the positive root group
and `n` in this intersection.

The proof first places representatives of either opposite root in the
middle subgroup. Existence of a nonidentity root point and its opposite
pair shows that the negative of an actual root is an actual root.
The cell is stable under left multiplication by positive root points.
For a negative root point, one opposite-pair factorization moves it into
the middle subgroup. Conjugating the remaining factor past the old middle
term either leaves it positive or requires a second opposite pair.
The two elementary group identities at the start of the file verify the
resulting products. Induction on subgroup closure then puts the subgroup
generated by the two opposite root groups into this cell.

If an actual normalizer point lies in the cell, quotienting by its middle
factor leaves a normalizer point expressed as a product of two opposite
root-group points. The published algebraic theorem
`RelativeRoot.normalizer_eq_one_of_mul_opposite` makes this residual
normalizer point the identity. Hence a normalizer point in the
opposite-root-generated subgroup lies in `reflectionNormalizer`.
The final two inclusions express this in the ambient rational point group,
first for normalizer points and then for centralizer points.

The provenance is the named published Library construction, ported without
changing its algebraic proof. The result is confined to the subgroup
generated by a single pair of opposite root groups and does not identify
the whole normalizer.
