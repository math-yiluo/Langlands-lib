# Split integral pinnings of a split torus

Let `k` be a field and `v : Valuation k (WithZero (Multiplicative ℤ))` a
valuation. `G` is a group scheme over `Spec k` with affine structure map. `S`
is a commutative group scheme over `Spec k`, and `i : S ⟶ G` a homomorphism
whose underlying morphism of schemes is a monomorphism. The relative roots are
`Φ = relativeRoots i`. For a character `a` of `S` and `t ∈ S(k)`,
`torusCharEval S a t = a(t) ∈ kˣ` is the value of the rational character
corresponding to `a`. `ord = IntegerValuation.order v` is the additive valuation
of a unit.

This module defines a *split integral pinning* of `i`. It is the part of a split
integral frame (`IntegerValuationBuildingData.SplitIntegralFrame`, module
`Langlands.Geometry.Building.SplitIntegralFrame`) that does not mention a
building datum. A pinning can therefore be built for a torus `i` that is not the
torus of a building datum, for example a torus obtained by base change from an
integral model. The module also proves that a pinning of the maximal split torus
of a building datum `B` is a split integral frame on `B` when that torus is a
maximal torus.

## Provenance

This is an internal definition of the Library; no published source defines it.
Its fields record, for a split reductive group over a discretely valued field
with an integral model, the Chevalley-type data of the integral points and the
root parametrizations used by Kaletha–Prasad (*Bruhat–Tits Theory: A New
Approach*, §6.1(a), printed pp. 196–198) to define the Chevalley valuation of
the root datum. The fields are exactly those of `SplitIntegralFrame`, except
for the two conditions that involve the centralizer datum of a building datum.

## The structure `SplitIntegralPinning v i`

Below, `a` ranges over `Φ`, `c` over `k`, and `t` over `S(k)`.

- `K : Subgroup G(k)`, the integral subgroup.
- `u : characterLattice S → k → G(k)`, the root parametrizations. Only the
  values at roots are constrained.
- `u_add`, `u_mem`, `u_surjective`, `u_eq_one`, `torus_conj`, `rankOne`,
  `two_smul_not_mem`, `u_mem_K`, `simple`, `simple_subset`,
  `simple_linearIndependent`, `weyl_mem`, `induction`, `weyl_generated`: the
  fields of the same names of `SplitIntegralFrame`, with `B.i` replaced by `i`
  and `B.charEval` by `torusCharEval S`. In particular `u a` is additive, takes
  values in the root group `U_a(k)` (`rootGroupPoints i a`) and is a bijection
  onto it, `t · u a c · t⁻¹ = u a (a(t) c)`, `u a c ∈ K ↔ v c ≤ 1`, and `rankOne`
  says `m(u_a(c)) = a^∨(c) · m(u_a(1))` for opposite factor pairs.
- `torus_mem_K`: if `t ∈ S(k)` has `ord(a(t)) = 0` for every `a ∈ Φ` and
  `i(t)` lies in `characterValuationKernel v G` (every rational character of `G`
  has valuation `1` at `i(t)`), then `i(t) ∈ K`.
- `K_le_closure`: `K` is contained in the subgroup generated by the image under
  `i` of `characterValuationKernel v S` (written `S(k)^1`) and the sets
  `u a {c | v c ≤ 1}` for `a ∈ Φ`.

## Results

Let `B : IntegerValuationBuildingData v G`.

- `IntegerValuationBuildingData.exists_map_eq_map_j`: if the maximal split torus
  `B.i` is a maximal torus (`IsMaximalTorus B.i`), every rational point `z` of
  the centralizer `Z` of `B.centralizer` satisfies `j(z) = i(t)` for some
  `t ∈ S(k)`. The proof uses the Library theorem `maximalTorusCentralizer`
  (the centralizer of a maximal torus is the torus).
- `IntegerValuationBuildingData.SplitIntegralPinning.toFrame`: if
  `IsMaximalTorus B.i`, a pinning `P : SplitIntegralPinning v B.i` gives a
  `B.SplitIntegralFrame` with the same `K`, `u` and simple roots.
  - `centralizer_mem_K`: for `z` in `characterValuationKernel v Z`, write
    `j(z) = i(t)`. The image of `z` in the normalizer fixes every apartment point
    (`isOmegaCompatible.smul_eq_self`). By the torus translation formula
    `rootForm_torusNormalizer_vsub` of the frame module, `ord(a(t)) = 0` for every
    root. The character condition passes from `z` to `j(z) = i(t)` by
    `IntegerValuation.map_mem_characterValuationKernel`. Hence `torus_mem_K`
    gives `i(t) ∈ K`.
  - `K_le_closure`: `S(k)^1` maps into `Z(k)^1` under the torus-to-centralizer
    map `s` of the centralizer datum, and `s ≫ j = B.i`.
