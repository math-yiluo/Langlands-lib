# The zero group acts without inversions on facets over the maximal unramified field

`Langlands.Geometry.Building.MaximalUnramified.ZeroFacetNoInversionsStatement` is an
unproved proposition, published as an interface. It is a `def` returning `Prop`. A separate
proof task will follow publication. The module also proves two consequences of the
proposition, described below. These are implications from the proposition, not proofs of it.

## The statement

The proposition is universe polymorphic: `ZeroFacetNoInversionsStatement.{u}`. At each
universe `u` it ranges over:

- every `F : Type u` with `Field F`, `ValuativeRel F`, `TopologicalSpace F` and
  `IsNonarchimedeanLocalField F`, in equal and mixed characteristic. Write
  `K = inertiaFixedField F`, the maximal unramified extension of `F` inside the chosen
  separable closure;
- every group scheme `H` over `Spec K` with `IsAffineHom H.X.hom`, the instance needed to
  form the existing data. `H` is any `K`-group, not necessarily a base change from `F`;
- every `C : MaximalUnramified.BuildingData F H`, which is
  `IntegerValuationBuildingData (MaximalUnramified.valuation F) H`: supplied KP building data
  of `H` for the constructed normalized integer valuation of `K`. An element supplies
  connected reductivity of `H`, a maximal split torus `S`, a `KPApartmentData` class (an
  `N(K)`-stable equipollence class of valuations of the root datum satisfying V 0–V 5 of KP
  Definition 6.1.2), a representing centralizer datum for `Z = Z_H(S)`, and V 6 for all of
  `Z(K)` on that class;
- every `E : UnramifiedZeroDatum F H`: a maximally split maximal torus `T` of `H`, a finite
  Galois splitting field `L ⊆ F^sep` of `T` over `K`, and a simply connected cover of the
  derived subgroup;
- every two points `x y : C.Apartment` and every `g : rationalPoints H`, an element of `H(K)`.

It has three hypotheses, in this order:

1. `y ∈ AffineRootSystem.facet C.affineRoots x`;
2. `g ∈ E.zeroPoints`;
3. `g • C.apartmentMap x = C.apartmentMap y`.

The conclusion is `y = x`.

The Library objects are the existing ones; nothing is replaced by an abstract action:

- `C.affineRoots` is the set `Ψ` of affine roots of KP Definition 6.3.4 for the class of
  `C`, as affine functions on `C.Apartment`.
- `AffineRootSystem.facet Ψ x` is the set of `y` such that `ψ x` and `ψ y` have the same sign
  (positive, zero or negative) for every `ψ ∈ Ψ`. These sets are the classes of an
  equivalence relation, so they partition `C.Apartment`. Two points have the same sign for
  `ψ` exactly when both lie on the hyperplane `ψ = 0` or both lie in the same open side of
  it. So these sets are the facets of the arrangement of affine root hyperplanes, which give
  the polysimplicial structure of KP Remark 1.3.16.
- `C.ReducedBuilding` is the reduced building `B(H)` of KP Remark 7.6.5: the quotient
  (7.6.2) of `H(K) × A` by `(g, x) ∼ (h, y)` iff `n x = y` and `g⁻¹ h n ∈ H(K)^♯_x` for some
  `n ∈ N(K)`. `H(K)` acts by left multiplication on the first factor, and
  `C.apartmentMap : C.Apartment → C.ReducedBuilding` is `x ↦ [(1, x)]`.
- `E.zeroPoints` is the subgroup of `H(K)` generated by the image `H(K)^♮` of
  `H_sc(K) → H(K)` and the image of `T(K)^0`. Here `T(K)^0` is the image of `T(L)^b` under
  the norm `T(L) → T(K)` (KP Definition 2.5.13). This is the group
  `H(K)^0 = H(K)^♮ · T(K)^0` of KP Definition 2.6.23(1).

In words: an element of `H(K)^0` cannot move a point of the apartment to a different point
of the same apartment facet.

### An equivalent form

The apartment map is injective by the published theorem `maximalUnramifiedApartmentInjective`
(`KPApartmentData.apartmentMap_injective` applied to `C`). So, given hypothesis 3, the
conclusion `y = x` holds exactly when `g • C.apartmentMap x = C.apartmentMap x`. If `g` fixes
`[x]`, hypothesis 3 gives `C.apartmentMap y = C.apartmentMap x`, and injectivity gives
`y = x`. Conversely, `y = x` and hypothesis 3 give `g [x] = [x]`; this is the corollary
`smul_apartmentMap_eq` below. The statement thus says: if `g ∈ H(K)^0` maps
`[x]` to a point of the image of the apartment facet of `x`, then `g` fixes `[x]`. The module
does not state this equivalence as a theorem; it is recorded here to compare with the source.

## Consequences proved in the module

- `ZeroFacetNoInversionsStatement.smul_apartmentMap_eq`. Assume the statement. If
  `y ∈ facet C.affineRoots x`, `g ∈ E.zeroPoints` and
  `g • C.apartmentMap x = C.apartmentMap y`, then `g • C.apartmentMap x = C.apartmentMap x`.
  The proof rewrites with hypothesis 3 and the conclusion `y = x`.
- `ZeroFacetNoInversionsStatement.mem_pointwiseFixer_of_isFacet`. Assume the statement. If
  `Y` is a facet of the reduced building (`C.IsFacet Y`), `g ∈ E.zeroPoints` and
  `(g • ·) '' Y ⊆ Y`, then `g ∈ C.pointwiseFixer Y`. Here `C.IsFacet Y` says that
  `Y = k · [Fa]` for some `k ∈ H(K)` and some apartment facet `Fa`. This is the facet form of
  KP 4.1.12(1) over `K`, for the Library's facets of `B(H)`. Only `g Y ⊆ Y` is assumed, which
  is weaker than `g Y = Y`.

  Proof. Let `x ∈ Fa`. By `g Y ⊆ Y` there is `x' ∈ Fa` with
  `k · [x'] = g k · [x]`. Then `x'` lies in the facet of `x`, because `Fa` is the facet of
  any of its points (`AffineRootSystem.facet_eq_of_mem`). The conjugate `k⁻¹ g k` maps `[x]`
  to `[x']`. It lies in `E.zeroPoints` because `E.zeroPoints` is normal in `H(K)`. This
  normality is the published `normal_of_simplyConnectedImage_le`: every subgroup containing
  `H(K)^♮` contains all commutators and is therefore normal. It does not use the
  independence of the choices in `E`. The statement then gives `x' = x`, so
  `g · (k · [x]) = k · [x]`.

### Chapter 4 describes the property; it does not prove it

Chapter 4 lists axioms that a building of `G(k)` should satisfy. It proves nothing about an
actual building. The relevant items are on pp. 157–161 / PDF pp. 188–192:

- **Axiom 4.1.1.** There is a building `B(G)` with an action of `G(k)` by polysimplicial
  automorphisms, whose apartments correspond equivariantly to the maximal split tori.
- **Axiom 4.1.9.** For an apartment `A` with a chamber `C`, `G = G(k)^0`, `I = G(k)^0_C` and
  `N = N(k) ∩ G`, the pair `(I, N)` is a saturated Tits system in `G` whose restricted
  building is `B(G)`. The corrections (their PDF p. 27) add "(B(G) as in Axiom 4.1.1)" after the
  first `B(G)`. They also replace "is precisely the" by "is a" in the last sentence. The
  corrected sentence reads: the stabilizer `G(k)^0_F` of a facet `F` of `B(G)` for the action
  of `G(k)^0` is a parabolic subgroup of this Tits system.
- **4.1.12(1)** (p. 161 / PDF p. 192). For a facet `F` of `B(G)`, `G(k)^0_F` is the
  stabilizer, as well as the pointwise stabilizer, of `F` in `G(k)^0`. KP derive it "from
  Axiom 4.1.9 and Proposition 1.5.13". The corrections do not change 4.1.12. This is the
  absence of inversions: an element of `G(k)^0` that stabilizes a facet fixes it pointwise.
  It comes from the type-preserving action of a group on the building of its Tits system:
  the stabilizer of a facet also stabilizes each face of it (Proposition 1.5.13). Axiom 4.1.9
  makes `B(G)` the building of such a Tits system for the action of `G(k)^0`.
- **4.1.12(5).** Two points lie in the same facet if and only if their stabilizers in
  `G(k)^0` agree. It is not used here.
- **Remark 4.1.13** (same page). KP derive it from Axiom 4.1.9 together with
  `G_sc(k)^0 = G_sc(k)` and `B(G) = B(G_sc)`: if `Ω` is a subset of a facet of `B(G)`, "the
  stabilizer of `Ω` in `G(k)^♮` fixes `Ω` pointwise". Moreover, an element of `G(k)^♮` that
  lies in `G(k)^1_Ω` lies in `G(k)^0_Ω`. Here `G(k)^♮` is the image of `G_sc(k) → G(k)`
  (Definition 2.6.20, p. 104 / PDF p. 135), for any connected reductive `G`. When `G` is
  simply connected, `G(k)^♮ = G(k)`. The corrections do not change this remark. The present
  statement does not use it. It concerns the group `G(k)^0`, which contains `G(k)^♮` by
  Definition 2.6.23, and rests on 4.1.12(1) and its Chapter 7 proof.

Chapter 4 does not establish these properties. KP prove the axioms in later chapters, first
for quasi-split groups. Remark 7.6.5 says that Proposition 7.6.4 completes the proof of
Axioms 4.1.1, 4.1.4 and 4.1.9 in the setting of Chapter 7. So the statement cannot be proved
by appeal to Chapter 4, and a supplied `BuildingData` object does not by itself provide any
axiom. The proof successor must establish the property over `K` from the constructed building
theory, discharging each property it needs.

### Where the property is proved: Chapter 7

Chapter 7 (p. 235 / PDF p. 266) works over a Henselian field `k` with a discrete valuation of
value group `ℤ`, for a connected reductive `G` with a maximal split torus `S` and an
equipollence class of valuations of the root datum.

- **Proposition 7.6.3** (pp. 264–265 / PDF pp. 295–296). The affine space `A`, with the
  polysimplicial structure from the affine root hyperplanes as in Remark 1.3.16, is a
  geometric realization of the standard apartment of `B(G)`. The parabolic subgroup for a
  facet `F ⊆ A` is the parahoric `G(k)^0_F` of Definition 7.4.5. So the facets of `A` for
  `Ψ` are facets of `B(G)`, and `A` embeds in `B(G)`.
- **Proposition 7.6.4(1)** (p. 265 / PDF p. 296). For a subset `Ω ⊆ A` of a facet,
  `G(k)^0_Ω` of Definition 7.4.1 is the stabilizer, as well as the pointwise stabilizer, of
  `Ω` in `G(k)^0`. The proof uses two facts. First, the action of `G(k)` on `B` preserves the
  polysimplicial structure, so the stabilizer of `Ω` is that of its facet `F`. Second, by
  Proposition 1.5.13, `G(k)^0_F` is the stabilizer of `F` in `G(k)^0` and stabilizes every
  face of `F`, so it fixes `F` pointwise. On p. 264 / PDF
  p. 295, KP extend the action from `G(k)^0` to `G(k)`, by Lemma 6.3.19: conjugation by
  `G(k)` preserves the parabolic subgroups of the Iwahori–Tits system.
- **Remark 7.6.5** (p. 266 / PDF p. 297). A geometric realization of `B(G)`, compatible with
  the action of `G_ad(k)`, is the quotient (7.6.2) of `G(k) × A`. This is the Library's
  `C.ReducedBuilding`, with `A → B(G)` given by `x ↦ [(1, x)]`, which is `C.apartmentMap`.

The corrections make no change to Propositions 7.6.3 and 7.6.4 or to Remark 7.6.5. The
replacement proof of Theorem 11.7.7(2) in the corrections (PDF p. 44) applies
Proposition 7.6.4(1) over `K`: for `G = G(K)^0` and the stabilizer `N` in `G(K)` of a
chamber `C` of `B(G_K)`, the group `G ∩ N` is the Iwahori subgroup of `C`.

### The setting over `K`

Here `k = K` and `G = H`. The field `K` is Henselian and discretely valued, as an algebraic
extension of the complete field `F`. Its normalized valuation has value group `ℤ` because
`K/F` is unramified. Its residue field is an algebraic closure of the finite residue field of
`F`, hence perfect. `H` is connected reductive by the data of `C`. It is quasi-split by KP
Corollary 2.3.8 (p. 81 / PDF p. 112). The class of `C` consists of `ω`-compatible
valuations, since V 6 is part of `C`. By KP Proposition 6.2.6 (p. 216 / PDF p. 247), any
two `ω`-compatible valuations are equipollent, so the class of `C` is the canonical class of
§6.1. The published sibling `UnramifiedZeroFixerAffineRootStatement` uses the same argument.
So the statement concerns the building of Chapter 7 for `H` over `K`. These are facts about
the source; the proof successor must prove in Lean any of them that it uses.

For every datum `E`, `E.zeroPoints` is `H(K)^0`. Each `E` makes the choices that Definition
2.6.23(1) allows, and the result does not depend on them by KP Lemma 2.6.24 (p. 104 /
PDF p. 135). In the Library this independence is the published theorem
`unramifiedZeroPointsIndependence`. So quantifying over every `E`, with `E` unrelated to the
torus `S` of `C`, adds nothing to the single-group source statement.

### The use in Chapter 9

The corrected proof of KP Proposition 9.3.24 (p. 343 / PDF p. 374) uses exactly this
property. An element `g ∈ G(K)^0 ∩ G(k)` that preserves a chamber `C` of the `k`-apartment
preserves the unique `Γ`-chamber of `B(G_K)` containing it. KP then write: "Since `g` lies
in `G(K)^0`, it fixes every point" of that chamber. The corrections (their PDF p. 40) replace
the first five lines of this proof and leave this step unchanged. The proof of Proposition
9.3.25 (same page) argues the same way for any facet: "Since `B(G_K)` is the building of a
Tits system of `G(K)^0` and `G(k)^0 ⊂ G(K)^0`, we see that `g` fixes every point" of the
facet. The facet of `B(G_K)` containing a `k`-chamber can be a facet that is not a chamber
of `B(G_K)`, so the descent argument needs the property for all facets over `K`, not only
chambers.

## Why the source results give the statement

Let `x`, `y` and `g` satisfy hypotheses 1–3. Let `Fa = facet Ψ x`, the facet of `A`
containing `x` and `y`.

1. By Proposition 7.6.3(1) and Remark 7.6.5, the image `F` of `Fa` in `B(H)` is a facet of
   `B(H)`, and it contains `[x]` and `[y]`.
2. `H(K)` acts on `B(H)` by polysimplicial automorphisms (Axiom 4.1.1, proved in Chapter 7;
   see the proof of Proposition 7.6.4). So `g F` is the facet containing `g [x] = [y]`. The
   facets of `B(H)` partition it, and `[y] ∈ F`, so `g F = F`.
3. By 4.1.12(1), in the form of Proposition 7.6.4(1) for `Ω = Fa`, an element of `H(K)^0`
   stabilizing `F` fixes `F` pointwise. Since `g ∈ E.zeroPoints = H(K)^0`, we get
   `g [x] = [x]`.
4. Hence `[y] = [x]`, and `y = x` because `A → B(H)` is injective (Proposition 7.6.3(1); in
   the Library, `maximalUnramifiedApartmentInjective`).

Conversely, the corollary `mem_pointwiseFixer_of_isFacet` recovers the facet form of
4.1.12(1) from the point form. So for the Library's facets of `B(H)` the two forms carry the
same information about `H(K)^0`.

## Provenance

The statement is derived, not quoted. KP state the absence of inversions as 4.1.12(1) for
facets of an axiomatic building, and prove it in Chapter 7 as Proposition 7.6.4(1) for
subsets of apartment facets. The statement rephrases this for one point and its image. It
combines the facet form with three ingredients: the facets of the Library's reduced building
come from apartment facets, `H(K)` acts on facets, and the apartment map is injective. The
argument above derives the point form from these. The interface is the one the task
specifies, unchanged.

## Choices and scope

- The statement concerns `H(K)^0` only. It says nothing about elements of `H(K)` outside
  `H(K)^0`. Such elements can stabilize a facet without fixing it. For example, in `PGL_2(K)`
  the image of `[[0, 1], [ϖ, 0]]`, with `ϖ` a uniformizer, swaps the two vertices of the
  standard chamber, and it does not lie in `PGL_2(K)^0`.
- It is stated over `K`, for every `K`-group `H` with supplied data `C`. Any comparison with
  groups over `F` belongs to the consumers.
- It quantifies over all facets of the apartment of `C`, not only chambers. By translation
  under `H(K)` and normality of `H(K)^0`, the corollary covers every facet of `B(H)` in the
  Library's sense `C.IsFacet`.
- The hypothesis is the point condition `g [x] = [y]` with `y` in the facet of `x`, not the
  stabilization of a whole facet. This is the form the consumer below applies, one point at a
  time.

## Relation to other Library statements

- `MaximalUnramified.SimplyConnectedChamberStabilizerStatement` concerns chambers only and
  simply connected ambient groups, where `H(K)^0 = H(K)`. The present statement allows any
  `H`, restricts to `H(K)^0`, and covers all facets.
- `UnramifiedZeroFixerAffineRootStatement` compares pointwise fixers in `H(K)^0` of two
  bounded subsets of an apartment. It does not say that a stabilizer fixes anything
  pointwise.

assert it.
