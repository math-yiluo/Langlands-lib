# Zero subgroups under central surjections

These internal lemmas develop the geometric step in the proof of
Kaletha–Prasad, *Bruhat–Tits theory: a new approach* (2023),
Proposition 11.5.4, printed page 431 (PDF page 462).

Norm naturality for different splitting fields follows by passing to their
compositum. Published independence for nested splitting fields identifies both
norm subgroups with those computed over the common extension.

For a homomorphism of connected reductive groups, the map of derived subgroups
composed with the source simply connected cover lifts through the target cover.
The published simply connected lifting theorem gives the lift, and hence
preservation of the simply connected images on rational points.

A compatible homomorphism of the chosen tori therefore preserves the joined
zero subgroups: the two generators are handled by the preceding lemmas.

For a central surjection to a quasi-split group, the image of the source
maximal split torus is a target maximal split torus. Extend it to a maximal
torus, which then represents its entire centralizer. The source maximal torus
centralizes its split subtorus; faithfully flat descent through the split-torus
image shows that its image centralizes the target split torus. Representability
therefore supplies a compatible homomorphism to the target maximal torus.

Every connected reductive group over the unramified closure is quasi-split.
Choose a splitting field for the target torus constructed above, apply the
compatible-torus lemma, and use published independence of the zero subgroup
to return to the supplied target datum. This proves preservation under central
surjections for every pair of defining data. No Kottwitz kernel equality is
asserted here.
