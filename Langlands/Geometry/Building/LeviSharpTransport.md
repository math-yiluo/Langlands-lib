# Levi sharp transport for apartment data over an infinite field

Author: Worker 118.

Lean module: `Langlands.Geometry.Building.LeviSharpTransport`.

This module proves a general form of the Levi sharp transport used for
`UnramifiedLeviSharpApartmentTransportStatement`, together with the auxiliary lemmas it
needs. Every declaration is a theorem; the module defines nothing new. It reuses the
Library definition `Langlands.GroupScheme.zeroRelativeRoots i ν`, the set of relative
roots `α` with `⟨α, ν⟩ = 0`.

## Setting and notation

Throughout, `k` is a field, `G` is a group scheme over `Spec k`, and `i : S → G` is a torus
embedding. `D : KPApartmentData i ω` is apartment data for `i` and an arbitrary function
`ω : kˣ → ℝ`. For a point `x` of the apartment `D.Apartment`:

- `U_{b,x,r} = D.filtration x b r` is the filtered root group;
- `G(k)^♯_x = D.sharpFixer {x}` is the sharp fixer;
- `H_x = D.sharpNormalizerStabilizer x` is the set of elements of
  `N(k)^♯_x = N(k) ∩ G(k)^♯_x` fixing `x`, where `N(k) = normalizerPoints i`;
- for a set `P` of roots, `X_x(P)` is the subgroup generated by the `U_{b,x,0}` with `b ∈ P`.

`Φ_{ν>0} = positiveRelativeRoots i ν`, `Φ_{ν<0} = positiveRelativeRoots i (-ν)` and
`Φ_{ν=0} = zeroRelativeRoots i ν` for a cocharacter `ν` of `S`. For a cocharacter
`χ : 𝔾_m → G`, `P_χ`, `U_χ` and `Z_χ` are the dynamic parabolic, unipotent and centralizer
subgroup functors (`Dynamic.parabolic`, `Dynamic.unipotent`, `Dynamic.centralizer`).
Their `k`-points are used.

## Auxiliary results

- `conj_mem_range_of_mem_normalizerPoints`: if `n` normalizes `i : T → G` over any base
  scheme, then conjugation by `n` maps base points of the image of `i` to base points of
  that image.
- `range_le_dynamicCentralizer`, `centralizerPoints_le_dynamicCentralizer`: base points of
  the image of `i`, and base points centralizing `i`, lie in `Z_{χ ∘ i}` for every
  cocharacter `χ` of `T`.
- `commute_of_mem_centralizerPoints`: a base point centralizing `i` commutes with
  `i(t)` for every base point `t` of `T`.
- `mem_centralizerPoints_of_forall_commute`: over an infinite field, with `G.X` affine and
  `A` split, a rational point commuting with `j(t)` for all `t ∈ A(k)` centralizes
  `j : A → G`. The proof uses `IsSplitTorus.hom_ext_kpoints`: the conjugate of `j` and `j`
  agree on `A(k)`, hence agree.
- `rootGroupPoints_le_centralizerPoints_of_comp_eq_zero`: if `f : A → S` and `f ≫ b = 0`,
  the rational root group of `b` centralizes `f ≫ i`. The image of `A` lies in the kernel
  of `b`, and the root group lies in the kernel centralizer of `b`.
- `exists_forall_latticePairing_ne_zero`: finitely many characters, each nonzero on some
  cocharacter, are all nonzero on one cocharacter. Induction adds a large multiple of a
  witness for the new character.
- `exists_latticePairing_refine`: for a finite set of characters and cocharacters `lam`,
  `mu'`, the cocharacter `mu = M·lam + mu'` with `M > Σ |⟨c, mu'⟩|` has the same strict
  sign as `lam` on every character on which `lam` is nonzero. On the characters on which
  `lam` vanishes, it agrees with `mu'`.
- `RootGroupFamily.le_normalizer_span_positiveRelativeRoots`: given commutator relations
  for a root-group family `U`, the group `U_c` of a root `c` with `⟨c, ν⟩ ≥ 0` normalizes
  the subgroup generated by `U_b`, `b ∈ Φ_{ν>0}`. Each commutator `[U_c, U_b]` lies in the
  span of the `U_{pc+qb}` with `p, q > 0`, and `⟨pc+qb, ν⟩ > 0`.

## Levi product decomposition of a point sharp fixer

`KPApartmentData.sharpFixer_subset_leviProduct` assumes `G` connected reductive and `i`
a maximal split torus. For every apartment point `x` and every cocharacter `lam` of `S`,
it proves

```
G(k)^♯_x ⊆ X_x(Φ_{lam<0}) · X_x(Φ_{lam=0}) · X_x(Φ_{lam>0}) · H_x.
```

Proof. The relative roots form a finite set. Choose `mu'` nonzero on every root (each
root is positive on some cocharacter of the split torus `S`). Refine it to
`mu = M·lam + mu'` with `exists_latticePairing_refine`. Then `mu` is generic, and a
root negative (positive) on `mu` is nonpositive (nonnegative) on `lam`. The Library
decomposition `sharpFixer_subset_mul_sharpNormalizerStabilizer` gives
`G(k)^♯_x ⊆ X_x(Φ_{mu<0}) X_x(Φ_{mu>0}) H_x`. By the commutator relations of the root
fixer at `x` (`rootFixer_hasCommutatorRelations`), `X_x(Φ_{lam=0})` normalizes
`X_x(Φ_{lam<0})` and `X_x(Φ_{lam>0})`. Hence

- `X_x(Φ_{mu<0}) ⊆ X_x(Φ_{lam<0}) ⊔ X_x(Φ_{lam=0}) = X_x(Φ_{lam<0}) · X_x(Φ_{lam=0})`;
- `X_x(Φ_{mu>0}) ⊆ X_x(Φ_{lam=0}) · X_x(Φ_{lam>0})`.

Merging the two middle factors gives the claim.

## Main theorem

`KPApartmentData.exists_leviSharp_transport` additionally assumes that `k` is
infinite and that `G` is connected reductive with maximal split torus `i`. Let `A` be a
split torus with a homomorphism `f : A → S`, and put `j = f ≫ i` (no injectivity of `f`
is required). Let `n ∈ G(k)` normalize `j`, and let `x, y` be apartment points with
`D.apartmentMap y = n • D.apartmentMap x` in `D.ReducedBuilding`. Then there is
`h ∈ G(k)` such that

1. `h` centralizes `j`;
2. `h` lies in the supremum of `U_{b,x,0}` over the relative roots `b` of `S` with
   `f ≫ b = 0` (the root groups of the Levi `Z_G(A)` at depth zero);
3. `n * h` normalizes `i`.

Proof as implemented.

1. *Quotient relation.* By `ApartmentQuotient.mk_eq_mk_iff` there is `m ∈ N(k)` with
   `n⁻¹ m ∈ G(k)^♯_x`.
2. *Adapted cocharacter.* Each nonzero restriction `f ≫ c` of a relative root `c` is
   positive on some cocharacter of the split torus `A`. So
   `exists_forall_latticePairing_ne_zero` gives `lam ∈ X_*(A)` nonzero on all of them. Put
   `lamS = lam ≫ f` and `χ = lamS ≫ i`. By `latticePairing_precomp`,
   `⟨c, lamS⟩ = ⟨f ≫ c, lam⟩`; therefore every root `c` with `⟨c, lamS⟩ = 0` satisfies
   `f ≫ c = 0`.
3. *Decomposition.* `sharpFixer_subset_leviProduct` with `lamS` writes
   `n⁻¹ m = v z w n'`. Here `v ∈ X_x(Φ_{lamS<0})`, `z ∈ X_x(Φ_{lamS=0})`,
   `w ∈ X_x(Φ_{lamS>0})` and `n' ∈ H_x ⊆ N(k)`. Put `m₁ = m n'⁻¹ ∈ N(k)`, so
   `v z w = n⁻¹ m₁`. By `positiveChamberRootGroups_le_sharpUnipotentPoints`,
   `v ∈ U_{-χ}(k)` and `w ∈ U_χ(k)`. The generators of `z` have roots vanishing on
   `lamS`, so `z ∈ Z_χ(k)`. By step 2 those roots are trivial on `A`, so `z` centralizes
   `j` (`rootGroupPoints_le_centralizerPoints_of_comp_eq_zero`). `z` also lies in the
   supremum of conclusion 2.
4. *Torus conjugation.* For `t ∈ A(k)` let `s = j(t)`. Because `n` normalizes the image
   of `j`, which lies in the image of `i`, and `m₁` normalizes the image of `i`, the
   element `τ = (v z w)⁻¹ s (v z w) s⁻¹ = m₁⁻¹ (n s n⁻¹) m₁ s⁻¹` is a point of the image
   of `i`. In particular `τ ∈ Z_χ(k)`.
5. *`v = 1`.* The element `v⁻¹ (s v s⁻¹)` lies in `U_{-χ}(k)`, because `s` centralizes `S`
   (`centralizer_conj_mem_dynamicUnipotent`). It also equals
   `(z w) τ (s (z w) s⁻¹)⁻¹ ∈ P_χ(k)`. By
   `Dynamic.eq_one_of_mem_opposite_unipotent_of_mem_parabolic` it is `1`, so `v` commutes
   with every `j(t)`. Since `k` is infinite and `A` is split, `v` centralizes `j`
   (`mem_centralizerPoints_of_forall_commute`). So `v ∈ P_{lam ≫ j}(k) = P_χ(k)`, and
   `v ∈ U_{-χ}(k) ∩ P_χ(k) = 1`.
6. *`w = 1`.* With `v = 1`, and `z` commuting with `s`, `τ = w⁻¹ (s w s⁻¹) ∈ U_χ(k)`.
   Since `τ ∈ Z_χ(k)`, `Dynamic.eq_one_of_mem_centralizer_of_mem_unipotent` gives
   `τ = 1`. Hence `w` commutes with `A(k)` and centralizes `j`. Then
   `w ∈ Z_χ(k) ∩ U_χ(k) = 1`.
7. *Conclusion.* Take `h = z`. Then `z = n⁻¹ m₁`, so `n h = m₁ ∈ N(k)`.

The infinite-field hypothesis is used only in steps 5 and 6, to pass from commuting with
`A(k)` to centralizing `A`.

## Provenance

This is internal supporting development. The main theorem is a derived algebraic
counterpart of Levi apartment transitivity: Kaletha–Prasad, *Bruhat–Tits Theory: A New
Approach* (2023), Propositions 7.6.4(2) and 7.7.5(4) applied in a Levi subgroup, and the
relative Levi decomposition of §9.7.4. Its proof does not follow those sources. It
avoids the Levi building and works directly with the decomposition of the point sharp
fixer and dynamic (parabolic, unipotent, centralizer) subgroups.
`sharpFixer_subset_leviProduct` refines the Library's
`sharpFixer_subset_mul_sharpNormalizerStabilizer`. That theorem is the range part of KP
Proposition 7.3.12(1) for the zero concave function at a point, as documented in
`GenericPointSharpExchange.md`.
