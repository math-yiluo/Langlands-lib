# Normalizer comparison from affine Weyl containment

This internal support draft for Task 6309 completes the passage from converse
normalizer affine Weyl containment to the bounded whole-apartment sharp-fixer
comparison. It works over any field with supplied `IntegerValuationBuildingData`.
No local-field instance on the valued field, order-one-unit witness, or extra
apartment axioms are used. The converse containment remains an explicit premise.

`exists_sharpFixer_normalizer_eq_affineReflection` lifts each ordinary affine
root reflection vanishing on Ω to an actual rational normalizer element in
`sharpFixer Ω`. The published extended-root description writes the root as an
actual jump functional. Choose an opposite factor pair. At every point of Ω,
its root element has valuation zero. V5 and its second-factor consequence put
both opposite factors at valuation zero. All three factors therefore lie in
the sharp fixer. The published actual reflection action, together with
uniqueness of coroots for finite spanning root derivatives, identifies its
apartment action with the specified affine reflection.

`normalizer_mem_kernel_sup_sharpFixer` treats a normalizer point n belonging to
an ambient subgroup H that contains every sharp fixer. Assume its apartment
action lies in the affine Weyl group and fixes finite nonempty Ω. The published
`AffineWeylPointStabilizer.exists_generic_point` supplies a point z fixed by n
such that roots vanishing at z vanish on Ω. The published stabilizer theorem
expresses the action of n in the subgroup generated by those reflections.
The preceding lifts give a normalizer point m in the sharp fixer with the same
action. Then m⁻¹n lies in H and in the kernel of the apartment action; multiplying
m by this kernel element gives the stated join membership.

`hasNormalizerSharpFixers_of_mem_affineWeylGroup` packages that conclusion as
the exact finite normalizer premise of `GenericZeroFixerNormalizerReduction`.
The theorem
`subgroup_pointwiseFixer_eq_wholeApartment_sup_sharpFixer_of_mem_affineWeylGroup`
combines it with `IntegerValuationFiniteSharpReduction` to give the comparison
for nonempty affinely bounded Ω. Its hypotheses are sharp-fixer containment
in H and affine Weyl containment for every normalizer point in H.

Finally,
`UnramifiedZeroDatum.wholeApartmentZeroFixer_sup_sharpFixer_eq_of_mem_affineWeylGroup`
specializes H to every supplied unramified zero subgroup. The sibling draft
`IntegerValuationSharpCoverImage` supplies sharp-fixer containment through actual
relative root lifting to the simply connected derived cover. Only the converse
normalizer affine Weyl containment remains as a conditional premise.

`UnramifiedZeroDatum.wholeApartmentZeroFixer_sup_sharpFixer_eq_of_simplyConnectedNormalizer`
discharges that containment with the actual zero-normalizer construction in
`UnramifiedZeroNormalizerComparison`. Its only universal proposition argument
is the explicit maximal-unramified simply connected normalizer assertion.
The given zero datum and full-compatible building datum are arbitrary. For
each nonempty affinely bounded subset, the resulting equality uses the original
zero subgroup, apartment image, pointwise action and sharp fixer. No equality
between the whole-apartment factor and a torus subgroup is needed or asserted.

The provenance is internal: this follows the published local-field proof in
`ZeroFixerNormalizerReduction`, retaining the original quotient action and
replacing its centralizer-zero calculation with the actual action kernel.
The arithmetic and reflection inputs are published in
`IntegerAffineRootLocalFiniteness`, `IntegerAffineRootArrangement`, and
`IntegerRootReflections`. The finite and bounded induction is implemented in
the sibling drafts. This module does not prove converse normalizer affine Weyl
containment unconditionally or the final affine-root-sign target. The final
specialization is internal composition of the independently submitted
zero-normalizer transfer with the finite and bounded comparison implemented
here; it retains the unproved simply connected assertion explicitly.
