# Proof: Iwahori decomposition and Levi intersection of `P_{x,r}` at positive depth

`Langlands.Geometry.Building.moyPrasadLevi` proves exactly
`MoyPrasadLeviStatement.{u}` at every universe `u`. This is Kaletha–Prasad,
Proposition 13.2.5(5),(6), for the supplied data `(P, Zf)` and `r > 0`. The
theorem is the pair of `moyPrasadIwahoriDecomposition`
(`MoyPrasadIwahoriDecompositionStatement.{u}`, part (5)) and
`moyPrasadLeviIntersection` (`MoyPrasadLeviIntersectionStatement.{u}`, part (6)).

The proof adds no premise. It uses only the hypotheses of the statement:
`P.SatisfiesApartmentAxioms`, `Zf.SatisfiesFiltrationAxioms` (only its first
component `Zf.IsCommutatorFriendly`), `0 < r`, a generic `ν` and
`J ⊆ simpleRelativeRoots P.i ν`, and for part (6) the Levi data `DM`, `xM` with
`IsLeviRestriction`. It does not assume `MoyPrasadProductStatement` or any other
unproved proposition. It imports the published theorem
`ParahoricBuildingData.CentralizerFiltration.range_orderedMoyPrasadProduct_eq`
(module `Langlands.Geometry.Building.MoyPrasadProductCoordinates`). That theorem
is about the definition `orderedMoyPrasadProduct` from module
`Langlands.Geometry.Building.KPMoyPrasadProductStatement`. Only this definition is
used from that module, not the proposition `MoyPrasadProductStatement`.

All auxiliary lemmas are in the namespace
`Langlands.Geometry.Building.MoyPrasadLeviProof`.

## Notation

Write `K = Zf.moyPrasad x r`, `M = standardLeviPoints P.i ν J`,
`N = standardUnipotentPoints P.i ν J`, `N⁻ = standardOppositeUnipotentPoints P.i ν J`
(by definition `standardUnipotentPoints P.i (-ν) (-J)`). Let
`V = standardRadicalDepth P x ν J r` be the subgroup generated by the
`U_{a,x,r}` for positive roots `a` outside the monoid generated by `J`, and
`V⁻ = standardRadicalDepth P x (-ν) (-J) r` the opposite one. Fix a standard
cocharacter `ζ` for `(ν, J)`. It comes from the published
`standardCocharacter` (`StandardCocharacterStatement`, proved in the Library).

## Step 1: covering (`moyPrasadRadicalDepthProduct_surjective`)

The ordered product `V⁻ × (K ⊓ M) × V → K`, `(v, m, n) ↦ v m n`
(`moyPrasadRadicalDepthProduct`), is surjective. The published
`moyPrasadRadicalDepthProduct_surjective_of_ordered_cover` reduces this to the
following fact. For every injective enumeration of the indivisible positive
roots of `-ν` and of `ν`, the ordered product of root blocks, centralizer
factor and root blocks has range exactly `K`. This is
`range_orderedMoyPrasadProduct_eq hP hZ x r hr ν hν`, with
`hZ = hZf.1 : Zf.IsCommutatorFriendly`.

## Step 2: part (5)

`moyPrasadIwahoriDecomposition` takes `ζ` from `standardCocharacter`. With the
surjectivity of Step 1, the published
`moyPrasad_intersections_and_iwahori_of_surjective` gives three facts:

- `hpos : K ⊓ N = V`;
- `hneg : K ⊓ N⁻ = V⁻`;
- the bijectivity `HasIwahoriIntersectionDecomposition K M N N⁻`.

That theorem uses uniqueness in the big cell, `standardTripleProduct_injective`.

### Clause 1: homeomorphism

`isTopologicalIwahoriDecomposition_of_bijective` turns the bijectivity into
`IsTopologicalIwahoriDecomposition`. It uses the topology
`rationalPointTopology F G.X` and the published
`rationalPoint_isTopologicalGroup`.

- **Comparison with the dynamic subgroups.** `standardCocharacter` identifies
  `M` and `N` with the rational points of `Dynamic.centralizer (ζ ≫ i)` and
  `Dynamic.unipotent (ζ ≫ i)`. The published
  `standard_eq_dynamic_of_relativeRootNegation` at `(-ν, -J, -ζ)`, together with
  `groupHom_neg_comp_eq_oppositeCocharacter`, identifies `N⁻` with the rational
  points of the unipotent radical of the opposite cocharacter.
- **Generic topological lemma.** `isHomeomorph_orderedIntersectionProduct_of_openCell`
  holds in any topological group `H` with subgroups `K, M, N, N̄, Q`, under three
  hypotheses:
  - the open-cell map `Q × N̄ → H`, `(p, v) ↦ p v⁻¹`, is an embedding;
  - `(m, n) ↦ m n` is a homeomorphism `M × N → Q`;
  - the ordered intersection product is bijective.

  Then the ordered intersection product is a homeomorphism.

  Proof of the lemma. The map `(v, m, n) ↦ v m n` equals
  `inv ∘ openCellMap ∘ e`, where `e : N̄ × M × N ≃ₜ Q × N̄` sends
  `(v, (m, n))` to `((m n)⁻¹, v)`. So this map is an embedding. Composing with
  the subspace inclusions shows that the intersection product is an embedding,
  and a surjective embedding is a homeomorphism
  (`isHomeomorph_iff_isEmbedding_surjective`).
- **Instances.** The lemma is applied with
  `HarishChandraSchwartz.isOpenEmbedding_openCellMap_rationalPoint` and
  `HarishChandraSchwartz.isHomeomorph_leviUnipotentMul_rationalPoint` for the
  cocharacter `ζ ≫ i`. The instance `IsOpenUnits F` that these need is built
  from three facts: `F` is Hausdorff (`Langlands.BruhatTits.localField_t2Space`),
  it has the right uniform structure of its additive group, and that structure
  is a uniform additive group (`isUniformAddGroup_of_addCommGroup`).

### Clause 2: weak contraction

`weakChamberContraction_nonnegativePart` proves `WeakChamberContraction` for
`standardComponentNonnegativePart P.i ν J q`, for every `q : A → S`. The proof
uses only `hpos` and `hneg`; the compatibility hypothesis on `A` is not needed.

- **From norms to orders.** Let `a ∈ A(F)` satisfy `|α(a)| ≤ 1` for all
  `α ∈ R(A, N_J)`. Then `localFieldOrder_nonneg_of_characterNorm_le_one`
  shows that the order of `α` at the image `q(a) ∈ S(F)` is nonnegative. It
  unfolds `characterNorm` as `fieldNormUnits` of the character value, writes
  this as `q_F^{-ord}` (`Plancherel.fieldNormUnits_zpow`), and uses
  `q_F > 1`.
- **Conjugation by `a`.** With `hpos`, conjugation by `a` maps `K ⊓ N = V`
  into itself, by the published `map_rootDepthGeneratedSubgroup_conj_le`.
- **Conjugation by `a⁻¹`.** With `hneg`, conjugation by `a⁻¹` maps
  `K ⊓ N⁻ = V⁻` into itself, by the same lemma. A generator root `β` of `V⁻`
  is a positive root for `-ν` outside the monoid generated by `-J`. Then `-β`
  is a positive root for `ν` (`relativeRootNegation`) outside the monoid
  generated by `J` (`neg_mem_closure_neg_image`). The norm hypothesis at `-β`,
  rewritten with `componentCharacterNorm_neg_inv`, gives the order condition
  for `β` at `a⁻¹`.

## Step 3: part (6)

`moyPrasadLeviIntersection` uses the following data:

- `j = Standard.leviHom P.i ν J` and `iM = Standard.leviTorusHom P.i ν J`, with
  `iM ≫ j = P.i` (`Standard.leviTorusHom_comp`);
- `j` is a closed subgroup (`Standard.isClosedSubgroup_leviHom`);
- `j` represents `Dynamic.centralizer (ζ ≫ i)` (`Standard.isRepresentedBy_leviHom`
  rewritten by `standardCocharacter`);
- `M_J` is smooth (`standardLevi_isConnectedReductive`).

The published `relativeRoots_centralizer_factor dynamicLie` gives
`Φ(M_J, S) = {a ∈ Φ(G, S) | ⟨a, ζ⟩ = 0}`.

Let `L` be the image under `j` of
`Zf.leviMoyPrasad j xM r = (Zf.image r).comap j ⊔ DM.sharpFiltration xM r`.

- **Filtration transport (`map_leviFiltration_eq`).** Let `a ∈ Φ(M_J, S)` and
  `s ∈ ℝ`, and assume `IsLeviRestriction j x xM`. Then `j` maps
  `U^M_{a,xM,s}` onto `U_{a,x,s}`. On root groups this is the published
  `map_leviRootGroupPoints`. The valuations agree by
  `xM.val a m = x.val a (j m)`.
- **`L ≤ K ⊓ M`.** Write `L` as the sup of two images; it suffices to treat
  each.
  - The image of `(Zf.image r).comap j` lies in `Zf.image r ≤ K`.
  - By `KPApartmentData.sharpFiltration_eq_iSup`, `DM.sharpFiltration xM r` is
    generated by the `U^M_{a,xM,r}` for `a ∈ Φ(M_J, S)`. Each maps onto
    `U_{a,x,r}`, which lies in `P.apartment.sharpFiltration x r ≤ K`.
  - Every `j m` lies in `M` (`Standard.leviPointsEquiv`).
- **`K ⊓ M ≤ L`.** Let `g ∈ K ⊓ M`.
  - **Coordinates.** As in the published
    `moyPrasadRadicalDepthProduct_surjective_of_ordered_cover`, enumerate the
    indivisible positive roots of `-ν` and of `ν`, with the roots in the monoid
    generated by `∓J` placed next to the centralizer factor
    (`exists_fin_enumeration_partition`).
    `range_orderedMoyPrasadProduct_eq` writes `g` as an ordered product of
    coordinates `p`.
  - **Regrouping.** `parabolicMoyPrasadCoordinates` regroups `p` into a triple
    `c ∈ V⁻ × (K ⊓ M) × V` with product `g`
    (`parabolicMoyPrasadCoordinates_product`).
  - **The middle factor lies in `L`.** By definition, the middle factor is
    `(∏ blocks of roots in the monoid generated by -J) · j_Z(z) · (∏ blocks of roots in the monoid generated by J)`.
    - Each such root `a` has `⟨a, ζ⟩ = 0`. This comes from
      `IsStandardCocharacter.pairing_eq_zero_of_mem_closure`, applied to `ζ`
      or to `-ζ` (`IsStandardCocharacter.neg`). So `U_{a,x,r} ⊆ L` by
      transport. The doubled root `2a`, when it is a root, also pairs to zero
      with `ζ`, so the whole block `U_{a,x,r} U_{2a,x,r}` lies in `L`.
    - The centralizer factor `j_Z(z)` lies in `M`
      (`centralizerFiltration_map_mem_moyPrasad_inf_standardLevi`), hence
      equals `j m` for some `m` (`Standard.leviPointsEquiv` is surjective).
      Then `m` lies in `(Zf.image r).comap j`, so `j_Z(z) ∈ L`.
  - **Cell uniqueness.** The triples `c` and `(1, g, 1)` both lie in
    `N⁻ × M × N` and have the same product. They are equal by
    `standardTripleProduct_injective`. So `g` equals the middle factor and
    lies in `L`.
