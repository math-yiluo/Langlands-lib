# Simple-root transitivity of `Z(k)` in quasi-split adjoint groups

`QuasiSplitAdjointSimpleRootTransitivityStatement` is an unproved proposition. It covers
every universe `u` and every field `k : Type u`, of any characteristic and not necessarily
perfect. It covers every group scheme `G` over `Spec k` with `[IsAffineHom G.X.hom]`,
`IsQuasiSplit G` and `IsAdjoint G`. It covers every commutative group scheme `S` with a
maximal split torus inclusion `i : S → G` (`IsMaximalSplitTorus i`), and every
`j : Z → G` that represents the centralizer functor `torusCentralizer i`. There is no
valuation, topology, splitness or perfectness hypothesis. No torus structure on `Z` is
supplied.

The proposition asserts that there is a finite set `Δ` of characters of `S` such that:

1. every `a ∈ Δ` is a relative root (`a ∈ relativeRoots i`);
2. every relative root lies in the additive subgroup of `characterLattice S` generated by
   `Δ`;
3. for every `a ∈ Δ`, and all `u, u' ∈ U_a(k)` (`rootGroupPoints i a`) that are not in
   `U_{2a}(k)` (`doubleRootGroupPoints i a`), there is `z ∈ Z(k)` (`rationalPoints Z`)
   with these two properties. First, `j(z)` commutes with every point of `U_b(k)` for every
   `b ∈ Δ` with `b ≠ a`. Second, `j(z) · u · j(z)⁻¹ = u' · w` for some `w ∈ U_{2a}(k)`.

Here `j(z)` is `rationalPoints.map j z`, and `Δ` is a `Finset (characterLattice S)`.
`rootGroupPoints i a` is the group of rational points of the Library's relative root
subgroup functor `RelativeRoot.subgroup i a`: the points whose conjugation orbit under `S`
factors through the character `a` and extends over the affine line with value `1` at `0`.
In the Library, `doubleRootGroupPoints i a` is `rootGroupPoints i (2 • a)` if `2 • a` is a
relative root, and the trivial subgroup otherwise. So for a root `a` whose double is not a
root, clause 3 says that the elements of `Z(k)` centralizing the other `U_b(k)` act
transitively on the nontrivial elements of `U_a(k)`. In general it says that for any two
elements `u, u'` of `U_a(k)` outside `U_{2a}(k)` some such element conjugates `u` into the
coset `u' · U_{2a}(k)`. The Library's `rootGroupPoints i a` contains `U_{2a}(k)`, which
matches the source convention `U_{2a} ⊂ U_a`.

The proposition does not say that `Δ` is a basis of simple roots. It does not say that `Z`
is a torus or an induced torus. It does not give coordinates on `U_a(k)`.

## Provenance

The provenance is derived. Kaletha–Prasad, *Bruhat–Tits Theory: A New Approach* (2023),
source `kaletha-prasad-2023-bruhat-tits-new-approach`, does not state this proposition as a
numbered result. It is the group-theoretic content behind Lemma 6.4.10 and the translation
part of Proposition 6.6.2. The relevant passages are:

- the paragraph before Definition 6.6.1, and Proposition 6.6.2 with its proof, printed
  pp.231–232 (PDF pp.262–263): for adjoint quasi-split `G`, `Z` is an induced torus, and the
  proof writes `T = Z`;
- Lemma 6.4.10 and its proof, printed pp.227–228 (PDF pp.258–259): the image of
  `T_ad(k)` in `V(S)` is described by the values `a(v)` for `a` in a set of simple roots
  `Δ`. The proof uses the fundamental coweights of the lifts of `a ∈ Δ`, which pair to zero
  with the other simple roots;
- Definitions 6.4.3–6.4.4 and Fact 6.4.5, printed pp.224–225 (PDF pp.255–256): the affine
  roots with gradient `a` at an adjusted Chevalley valuation are `a + J_{a/2a}`;
- §2.6, Notation 2.6.11, (2.6.1) and (2.6.2), printed pp.96–97 (PDF pp.127–128): the
  structure of `U_a` for roots of types R1 and R2, and `U_{2a} ⊂ U_a`.

The argument behind the proposition is as follows. For quasi-split adjoint `G`, `Z = T` is
an induced torus. Its character lattice has the Galois-permuted simple absolute roots as a
basis. Take `Δ` to be the relative simple roots. The factor of `T(k)` attached to the
Galois orbit of lifts of `a ∈ Δ` is `k_ã^×`. It centralizes `U_b` for every other simple
root `b`. It acts on `U_a(k)` (type R1), or on `U_a(k)/U_{2a}(k)` (type R2), through
multiplication by `k_ã^×` on `k_ã`. It therefore acts transitively on the nontrivial
elements. This argument is the intended route for a later proof. It is not formalized
here.

The corrections `kaletha-prasad-2024-corrections` (PDF p.32) make two changes to these
passages. The third line of §6.6 now reads "Let N be the normalizer and Z be the centralizer
of S in G", which is the `Z` used here. The cross-reference just before the proof of
Proposition 6.6.2 now points to Proposition 9.4.35. Neither change affects this content.
