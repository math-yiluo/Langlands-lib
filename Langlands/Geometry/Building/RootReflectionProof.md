# Proof that `m(u)` fixes the hyperplane `H_u` (KP Proposition 6.2.1, AS 1)

Module `Langlands.Geometry.Building.RootReflectionProof`.
The theorem `Langlands.Geometry.Building.rootReflection` proves exactly the
published proposition `Langlands.Geometry.Building.RootReflectionStatement.{u}`
at arbitrary universe `u`. For every nonarchimedean local field `F`, every
group scheme `G` over `F` with affine structure morphism and every
`B : ValuationBuildingData G`, it proves `B.HasRootReflections`. The theorem
`RootReflectionProof.hasRootReflections` gives this for one `B`. No hypothesis
is added and no apartment axiom is assumed. The kernel check reports only the
axioms `propext`, `Classical.choice` and `Quot.sound`.

## Source and route

The statement is Kaletha–Prasad, *Bruhat–Tits theory: a new approach* (2023),
Proposition 6.2.1, printed pp. 212–214 / PDF pp. 243–245. Its source ID is
`kaletha-prasad-2023-bruhat-tits-new-approach`, SHA-256
`05ad9a284a2577b34d1a270228b74dfb28f520561ae7772d8f38dddb78938732`.
KP prove the formula `φ − m(u)φ = φ_a(u)a^∨` with the valuation axioms. For
roots `b` that are not rational multiples of `a` they use an ordered product
of filtration subgroups that maps bijectively onto a commutative quotient
`U/V`. The Library does not prove these ordered products, the root-system
axioms of the relative roots, or the existence and uniqueness of factor pairs.

The Lean proof therefore takes a different route, with the same inputs (the
valuation axioms V 1, V 3 and V 5 and `N(F)`-stability of the apartment). It
proves only the fixed-point form that the statement asserts. It does not
prove KP's formula for points off the hyperplane. Every helper lemma in the
module is an internal step of this route and introduces no new definition
except the generating set `gens`.

## Notation

Fix `a ∈ Φ` (a relative root), `u ∈ U_a(F)` with `u ≠ 1`, an opposite factor
pair `p = (p.1, p.2)` with `p.1, p.2 ∈ U_{-a}(F)` and
`m = p.1 * u * p.2 ∈ N(F)`, and an apartment point `x` with
`φ := x.val` and `φ_a(u) = 0`. `V` is the real vector space `V S`, `d(v)` is
`eval S d v`, and `⟨d, λ⟩` is `latticePairing S d λ`. The group `N(F)` acts
on valuations by `(nφ)_d(g) = φ_{n⁻¹d}(n⁻¹ g n)`, and `v +ᵥ φ` is the
translate `(v +ᵥ φ)_d(g) = φ_d(g) + d(v)`.

## Step 1: reduction to a translation vector

The published `IsSplitTorus` part of `B.isMaximalSplitTorus` and
`IsSplitTorus.finite_relativeRoots` give that `Φ` is finite. The apartment is
an equipollence class that is stable under `N(F)`, so
`KPApartmentData.equipollent_of_mem` gives `v ∈ V` with `m • φ = v +ᵥ φ`.
By `IsRootDatumValuation.vadd_eq_self_iff` it is enough to prove `d(v) = 0`
for every `d ∈ Φ`. Then `m • x = x` follows by `KPApartmentData.Apartment.ext`.

`IsOppositeFactorPair.coroot_spec` (module `RootFactorPairConsequences`) gives
a cocharacter `ν` with `⟨a, ν⟩ = 2` such that `weylAction m` is the reflection
`χ ↦ χ − ⟨χ, ν⟩ a`. This reflection is an involution, so `weylAction m⁻¹` is
the same reflection. In particular `m` sends `a` to `−a`, `m⁻¹` sends `−a` to
`a`, and `−a ∈ Φ`.

## Step 2: depths and `a(v) = 0`

The lemma `apply_inv` proves `φ_d(g⁻¹) = φ_d(g)` from V 1 and the
normalization axioms. V 5 (`IsRootDatumValuation.opposite`) applied to
`p.1 u p.2 = m` gives `φ_{−a}(p.1) = −φ_a(u) = 0`. Applied to
`p.2⁻¹ u⁻¹ p.1⁻¹ = m⁻¹` and combined with `apply_inv`, it gives
`φ_{−a}(p.2) = 0`. Hence `m` lies in the subgroup generated by
`U_{a,φ,0} ∪ U_{−a,φ,0}`.

Put `y = m⁻¹ p.1 m`. It lies in `U_a(F)`, because `m⁻¹` sends `−a` to `a`
(`conj_mem_rootGroupPoints`). Then `m⁻¹ = y⁻¹ p.2⁻¹ u⁻¹`. V 5 for the root
`−a`, the element `p.2⁻¹ ≠ 1` (`IsOppositeFactorPair.factors_ne_one`) and the
pair `(y⁻¹, u⁻¹)` gives `φ_a(y) = 0`. Evaluating `m • φ = v +ᵥ φ` at `(−a, p.1)`
gives `φ_a(y) = φ_{−a}(p.1) − a(v)`, so `a(v) = 0`.

## Step 3: no root is negative on a translation vector

The internal lemma `not_eval_neg` carries the main argument. Let `n ∈ N(F)` be
such that `weylAction n⁻¹` is the reflection `χ ↦ χ − ⟨χ, ν⟩ a` with
`⟨a, ν⟩ = 2`. Suppose `n` lies in the subgroup generated by
`U_{a,φ,0} ∪ U_{−a,φ,0}` and `n • φ = w +ᵥ φ` with `a(w) = 0`. The lemma shows
that no `b ∈ Φ` has `b(w) < 0`. The proof has four parts.

1. *Extremal root* (`exists_extremal_root`). Among the finitely many roots
   `d` with `d(w) < 0`, choose one minimizing `⟨d, ν⟩ / (−d(w))`, and among
   those one maximizing `−d(w)`. Call it `τ`. For `m ∈ ℤ` and `n ≥ 1` with
   `m a + n τ ∈ Φ`, either `m a + n τ = τ` (`m = 0`, `n = 1`) or `m > 0`.
2. *Separating cocharacter* (`exists_separating_cocharacter`). The vector
   `2τ − ⟨τ, ν⟩ a` is nonzero, since it is negative at `w`. Then
   `IsSplitTorus.exists_pairing_eq_pos` and an integral combination with `ν`
   give a cocharacter `λ` with `⟨τ, λ⟩ < 0` and `⟨m a + n τ, λ⟩ > 0` for every
   `m a + n τ ∈ Φ` with `m > 0`.
3. *A subgroup normalized by `n`*. Fix `t ∈ ℝ`. `H` is the subgroup generated
   by the set `gens` of all `U_{m a + n τ, φ, n t}` with `n ≥ 1`. For any root
   `a'` with `a'(w) ≤ 0` the side condition of V 3 holds, since every
   generator root is negative at `w` (`not_mem_neg_ray`). V 3 then shows that
   `U_{a',φ,r}` normalizes `H` when the positive combinations stay of the
   indexed form (`commutator_le_closure_gens`, `mem_normalizer_closure_gens`).
   With `a' = ±a` and `r = 0` this shows that `U_{a,φ,0}` and `U_{−a,φ,0}`
   normalize `H` (`closure_le_normalizer`), and hence so does `n`.
4. *Depth bound* (`le_of_mem_closure_gens`). Let `K` be generated by the
   pieces with `m > 0`. By part 1, `H ≤ U_{τ,φ,t} ⊔ K`. By V 3 with
   `a' = τ`, `U_{τ,φ,t}` normalizes `K`, so every element of `H` is a product
   `y k` with `y ∈ U_{τ,φ,t}` and `k ∈ K`. By part 2 and
   `RelativeRoot.subgroup_le_dynamicUnipotent`, `K` lies in the dynamic
   unipotent subgroup of `λ`. If the product also lies in `U_τ(F)`, then
   `k ∈ U_τ(F)`, which lies in the parabolic of `−λ`. So `k = 1` by
   `RelativeRoot.eq_one_of_mem_subgroup_of_mem_negative_parabolic`. Hence
   every element of `H ∩ U_τ(F)` has `φ_τ`-depth at least `t`.

For the contradiction, `IsRootDatumValuation.exists_ne_top` gives
`h₀ ∈ U_τ(F)` of finite depth `t₀`. Let `s = τ − ⟨τ, ν⟩ a`, so that
`weylAction n⁻¹ s = τ` and `s(w) = τ(w)`. The element `g = n h₀ n⁻¹` lies in
`U_s(F)`, and evaluating `n • φ = w +ᵥ φ` at `(s, g)` gives
`φ_s(g) = t₀ − τ(w) =: t`. So `s ∈ Φ` and `g` is a generator of `H` (with
`m = −⟨τ, ν⟩`, `n = 1`). As `n` normalizes `H`, `h₀ = n⁻¹ g n ∈ H ∩ U_τ(F)`.
Part 4 gives `t₀ ≥ t = t₀ − τ(w) > t₀`, a contradiction.

## Step 4: conclusion

Let `d ∈ Φ`. If `d(v) < 0`, `not_eval_neg` with `n = m` and `w = v`
contradicts Step 2. If `d(v) > 0`, then `m⁻¹ • φ = −(m⁻¹ • v) +ᵥ φ`, and
`not_eval_neg` applies to `n = m⁻¹` and `w = −(m⁻¹ • v)`: `m⁻¹` lies in the
same generated subgroup, `a(w) = 0` because `m` sends `a` to `−a`, and the
root `m • d` satisfies `(m • d)(w) = −d(v) < 0`
(`eval_normalizer_smul` and the involution property). Hence `d(v) = 0` for
all `d ∈ Φ`, and Step 1 finishes the proof.
