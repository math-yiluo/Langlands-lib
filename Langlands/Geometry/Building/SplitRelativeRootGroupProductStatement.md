# Rational points of relative root groups in a split group: statement

Worker 163, for Task 6836 (8c04a3ed-3fa6-4680-a59f-856738cb578b).

`Langlands.Geometry.Building.SplitRelativeRootGroupProductStatement.{u}` is a
proposition definition. This module does not prove it.

## The statement

Let `K : Type u` be a field and `G` a group scheme over `Spec K` with
`IsConnectedReductive G`; the `letI` line only supplies the affine
structure-morphism instance that `roots` and `rootGroupPoints` use. Let `T` and
`S` be commutative group schemes over `Spec K`, `j : T.toGrp ⟶ G` a maximal torus
(`IsMaximalTorus j`) with `T` split, `S` split, and `f : S ⟶ T` any homomorphism.
Let `a` be a nonzero character of `S`.

Let `n : ℕ` and `β : Fin n → characterLattice T` be injective, with range exactly
the roots `χ ∈ roots j` whose restriction `f ≫ χ` is `m • a` for some natural
number `m > 0`. The order of the enumeration is arbitrary.

The proposition asserts two things.

1. A rational point `g` of `G` lies in `rootGroupPoints (groupHom f ≫ j) a`, the
   rational points of the actual relative root subgroup functor of `a` for the
   composite `S → T → G`, if and only if `g` is the ordered product
   `x 0 * x 1 * ⋯ * x (n-1)` of a family `x r ∈ rootGroupPoints j (β r)` of
   rational points of the absolute root groups `U_{β r}`. The product is
   `List.prod (List.ofFn ...)` in the order `0, …, n-1`.
2. Such a family is unique: two families with the same ordered product are equal.

For `n = 0` this says that the relative root group has only the identity point.

## Intended use

Task 6836 builds rank-one coordinates over an arbitrary field `k` by Galois
descent from a finite Galois splitting field `K` of the centralizer torus. A
rational point `y` of the relative root group `U_b` over `k` extends to a rational
point of the relative root group of `b_K` for the base-changed torus `S_K`, which
is not a maximal split torus of `G_K`. This statement writes that point uniquely as
a product of absolute root points over `K`. Galois invariance of `y` and uniqueness
then identify the factors with Galois-conjugate coordinates. This is how
surjectivity of the coordinate map onto `U_b(k)` will be proved.

## Source

Armand Borel and Jacques Tits, *Groupes réductifs*, Publ. Math. IHÉS 27 (1965),
source ID `borel-tits-1965-groupes-reductifs`.

In the proof of Théorème 3.17 (printed p. 82, PDF p. 29) the group is split over
`k`, `T` is a split maximal torus containing the torus `S`, and `μ` is the set of
positive roots of `T` whose restriction to `S` lies in a unipotent quasi-closed
set `ψ`. The proof states:

> Alors (2.3, 3.11) l'application produit induit un k-isomorphisme a de variétés
> du produit des U_a, a ∈ μ, […] sur U_ψ […], où les éléments de μ […] sont rangés
> dans un ordre quelconque.

Here `ψ = (a)`, the positive integral multiples of `a`. By 3.8 (ii) and 5.2
(printed pp. 76 and 96), `U_(a)` is the group generated by the absolute root groups
over `(a)`. The Library's `RelativeRoot.subgroup` is the dynamic description of
this group, identified with it by `relativeRootGroupAbsoluteGeneration` when `S` is
a maximal split torus; the proof of that theorem uses only that `S` and `T` are
split. On rational points, the isomorphism of varieties gives the bijection in
clauses 1 and 2.

## Provenance

Derived. Borel–Tits state the product decomposition as an isomorphism of
varieties inside a proof. This statement is its consequence on rational points,
phrased with the Library's relative and absolute root subgroup functors and an
arbitrary injective enumeration.

Borel–Tits take `S` to be a subtorus of `T`. Here `f : S ⟶ T` is any
homomorphism, and the statement still holds. Here is why; the proof itself is
left to the proof task. Choose a cocharacter `λ` of `S` with `⟨a, λ⟩ > 0`. A
point of the relative root group then lies in the unipotent radical of the
dynamic parabolic subgroup of `f ∘ λ`. That radical is the product, in any
order, of the root groups `U_β` with `⟨β, f ∘ λ⟩ > 0`. Under conjugation by
`f(s)`, the coordinate of `U_β` is multiplied by `(f ≫ β)(s)`. If the orbit
map factors through `a` with value `1` at `0`, then linear independence of
characters forces every nonzero coordinate to have `f ≫ β = m • a` with
`m > 0`. Conversely, `t ↦ x_β(t^m u)` extends the orbit of `x_β(u)` through
`a`. If `f = 0`, then no root satisfies the condition, `n = 0`, and the relative
root group is trivial.
