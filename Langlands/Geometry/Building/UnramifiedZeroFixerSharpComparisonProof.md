# Proof: zero-group pointwise fixers and sharp fixers over the maximal unramified field

`Langlands.Geometry.Building.unramifiedZeroFixerSharpComparison` proves the
unchanged published proposition
`Langlands.Geometry.Building.UnramifiedZeroFixerSharpComparisonStatement.{u}`
at arbitrary universe `u`. It has no additional premises.

## What is proved

For every nonarchimedean local field `F : Type u`, put
`K = inertiaFixedField F`. The theorem ranges over every group scheme `H` over
`Spec K` with affine structure morphism, every supplied
`C : MaximalUnramified.BuildingData F H` and `E : UnramifiedZeroDatum F H`, and
every `Ω : Set C.Apartment` that is nonempty and bounded above under every real
affine function `C.Apartment →ᵃ[ℝ] ℝ`. For these it proves

```lean
E.zeroPoints ⊓ C.pointwiseFixer (C.apartmentMap '' Ω) =
  (E.zeroPoints ⊓ C.pointwiseFixer (C.apartmentMap '' Set.univ)) ⊔ C.sharpFixer Ω
```

All quantifiers and hypotheses of the statement are kept as they are. The
statement Markdown explains what the statement means and which sources it
comes from.

## The implemented proof

The proof introduces the binders of the statement. It then applies the Library
theorem

```lean
UnramifiedZeroDatum.wholeApartmentZeroFixer_sup_sharpFixer_eq_of_simplyConnectedNormalizer
```

(module `Langlands.Geometry.Building.IntegerValuationNormalizerSharpReduction`)
to `E`, `C`, `hne` and `hb`. Its only extra premise is
`MaximalUnramified.SimplyConnectedNormalizerAffineWeylStatement.{u}`. The proof
supplies it with the unconditional Library theorem
`MaximalUnramified.simplyConnectedNormalizerAffineWeyl` (module
`Langlands.Geometry.Building.UnramifiedSimplyConnectedNormalizerAffineWeylProof`).
For every affine simply connected `K`-group and every supplied building datum,
that theorem says the image of the full normalizer is exactly the affine Weyl
group of the datum's affine roots.

The imported reduction proceeds as follows. These steps are already proved in
the Library; this module does not prove them again.

1. `E.sharpFixer_le_zeroPoints C` gives `C.sharpFixer Ω' ≤ E.zeroPoints` for
   every apartment subset `Ω'`. This is the containment `H(K)^♯_Ω ⊆ H(K)^0`.
2. `E.normalizer_mem_affineWeylGroup_of_mem_zeroPoints C hsc` shows that every
   normalizer element lying in `E.zeroPoints` acts on the apartment through the
   affine Weyl group. It writes `E.zeroPoints` as the simply connected image
   joined with a subgroup of normalizer points that act trivially, and then
   applies the simply connected normalizer statement `hsc`.
3. `IntegerValuationBuildingData.subgroup_pointwiseFixer_eq_wholeApartment_sup_sharpFixer_of_mem_affineWeylGroup`
   is applied to the subgroup `E.zeroPoints` with properties 1 and 2. For
   nonempty bounded `Ω` it gives the displayed equality. Inside that theorem,
   `hasNormalizerSharpFixers_of_mem_affineWeylGroup` handles finite nonempty
   sets. Take a normalizer element `n` in the subgroup that fixes such a set.
   Its affine Weyl action fixes a generic point `z` of the set, so it lies in
   the subgroup generated by the affine reflections through `z`. Each of those
   reflections lifts to a normalizer element in the sharp fixer of the set. If
   `m` is the product of these lifts, then `m⁻¹ * n` acts trivially on the
   apartment. Hence `n` lies in the join of the whole-apartment fixer and the
   sharp fixer.
   `subgroup_pointwiseFixer_eq_wholeApartment_sup_sharpFixer_of_bounded` then
   passes from bounded `Ω` to a finite `Ω' ⊆ Ω` that contains a chosen point
   and has the same sharp fixer. It then uses the finite comparison. The
   reverse inclusion holds because the whole-apartment fixer fixes `Ω`, and
   `C.sharpFixer Ω` lies in `E.zeroPoints` and fixes the image of `Ω`.

## Provenance

The proof only composes the two imported Library results above: the reduction,
and the proof of the simply connected normalizer statement. The source
comparison with Kaletha–Prasad (Definition 7.4.1(4) and Proposition 7.7.5(1),
with the 2024 corrections) is in the unchanged statement Markdown. The module
assumes nothing about the existence of `C` or `E` and builds no new building.
