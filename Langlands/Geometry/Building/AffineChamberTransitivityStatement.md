# Simple transitivity of the affine Weyl group on chambers

`Langlands.Geometry.Building.AffineChamberSimpleTransitivityStatement` is the
chamber consequence of Kaletha–Prasad, Lemma 1.3.17 (printed p.21, PDF p.52).
For any two chambers `C` and `D` of an affine root system `Ψ`, it asserts that
there is exactly one element `w` of the existing
`AffineRootSystem.affineWeylGroup Ψ` whose image of `C` is `D`.
The action is evaluation of the actual affine automorphism underlying `w`,
extended to subsets by direct image. No abstract replacement group or
independently specified action occurs.

The chamber hypotheses use the existing `AffineRootSystem.IsChamber`:
each chamber is the sign-equivalence class of a point on no root hyperplane.
The target concerns these subsets themselves and assumes no separate
action on a type of chambers.

The quantifiers allow independent universes for the real vector space and its
nonempty affine torsor; `AddTorsor V A` supplies nonemptiness without choosing
an origin. No reducedness assumption is imposed. The root-system assumption is the published
`AffineRootSystem.IsAffineRootSystemWithRankZero`: derivative roots span the
dual, every root is nonconstant, there are finitely many derivatives, coroot
reflections preserve the roots, coroot pairings are integral, and each
derivative fibre is infinite with finite bounded subsets. The corrected
infinite-fibre requirement is retained.

This predicate uses the derivative-spanning convention explained in
[RankZeroAffineRootSystem](RankZeroAffineRootSystem.md). KP's literal AR 1
says that the affine roots span all affine functionals; the empty set cannot
do this even on a point, because nonzero constant functionals remain.
Derivative spanning instead admits the empty system exactly when `V` is
subsingleton, as proved by `isAffineRootSystemWithRankZero_empty_iff`.
Then the nonempty torsor is also a singleton, its only chamber is the whole
torsor, and the group generated by the empty set of reflections is trivial.
This agrees with KP's rank sentence after Definition 1.3.1 and Remark
1.3.2(4). For a nonempty root system, the published
`IsAffineRootSystemWithRankZero.span_eq_top` recovers literal affine spanning:
corrected AR 4 supplies two distinct roots with the same derivative, whose
difference is a nonzero constant.

The source starts with a finite-dimensional real vector space. In the
existing algebraic formulation, finitely many derivative roots span the
dual, so the dual is finite-dimensional; injectivity of the canonical map
from `V` into its double dual then implies that `V` is finite-dimensional.
Thus allowing arbitrary carrier types adds no infinite-dimensional models
under the root-system premise. This is the mathematical explanation of the
existing hypothesis, not an additional theorem proved in this module.
The target introduces no topology or extra finiteness premise.

Only the assertion about two given chambers is formalized here. Chamber
existence, fundamental domains, and the five equivalent conditions for a
general facet in the rest of Lemma 1.3.17 are not asserted by this target.
This file states the proposition; it does not prove it.

The intended consumer is the chamber-splitting proof for the actual
Iwahori–Weyl quotient. That consumer must prove that its normalizer action
preserves chambers, establish its action-compatible affine-part comparison,
and transfer the chamber transport to its full chamber-stabilizer complement.
Those comparisons, a proof of this target, and the final splitting remain
separate proof obligations.
