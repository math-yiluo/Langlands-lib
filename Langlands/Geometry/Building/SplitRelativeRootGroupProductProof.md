# Rational points of relative root groups in a split group: proof

## Main theorem

`Langlands.Geometry.Building.splitRelativeRootGroupProduct :
SplitRelativeRootGroupProductStatement.{u}` proves the Library statement
`Langlands.Geometry.Building.SplitRelativeRootGroupProductStatement` (module
`Langlands.Geometry.Building.SplitRelativeRootGroupProductStatement`) at every
universe `u`, with its hypotheses unchanged. In words: let `G` be connected
reductive over a field `K`, `j : T ⟶ G` a split maximal torus, `f : S ⟶ T` a
homomorphism from a split torus, and `a ≠ 0` a character of `S`. Let `β` be an
injective enumeration of the roots `χ` of `T` with `f ≫ χ = m • a` for some `m > 0`.
Then (1) a rational point lies in `rootGroupPoints (groupHom f ≫ j) a` exactly when
it is an ordered product of one rational point of each `U_{β r}`, in the order
`0, …, n-1`, and (2) the factors are unique.

## Helper declarations (namespace `SplitRelativeRootGroupProduct`)

- `positiveMultiples f a : AddSubsemigroup (characterLattice T)` is the set of
  characters `χ` of `T` with `f ≫ χ = m • a` for some natural number `m > 0`.
  `mem_positiveMultiples` unfolds membership.
- `zero_not_mem_positiveMultiples`: for split `S` and `a ≠ 0`, the zero character is
  not in `positiveMultiples f a`. This uses `Limits.comp_zero` and
  `IsSplitTorus.nsmul_ne_zero`.
- `nsmul_mem_of_pos`: an additive subsemigroup of characters contains `m • x` for
  every `x` in it and every `m > 0`.
- `addClosure_singleton_eq_cyclicTail`: the additive subsemigroup generated by
  `{χ}` is `cyclicTail χ 1`, the set of positive multiples of `χ`.
- `cyclicTail_le_positiveMultiples`: if `β ∈ positiveMultiples f a`, then every
  positive multiple of `β` is in `positiveMultiples f a`.
- `not_mem_cyclicTail_of_ne`: in a connected reductive group with split maximal
  torus, a root `β` is not a positive multiple of a different root `δ`. If
  `β = m • δ` with `m > 0`, then `β` and `δ` are `ℤ`-linearly dependent, so the
  Library theorem `rootReducedness` gives `β = δ` or `β = -δ`. The first case
  contradicts `β ≠ δ`. In the second, `(m + 1) • δ = 0` contradicts
  `IsSplitTorus.nsmul_ne_zero`.

## Proof of the main theorem

Let `b = AutomorphicAction.conjugation j` be the conjugation action of `T` on `G`
and `A = positiveMultiples f a`.

1. **The realized unipotent group.** The Library theorem `semigroupRealization`
   (module `Langlands.GroupScheme.Rational.SemigroupRealizationProof`) applied to
   `b` and `A` gives a smooth connected closed subgroup `jU : U ⟶ G` representing
   `semigroupSubgroup b A`, stable under `b`. Since `0 ∉ A`, its unipotence clause
   says `U` is unipotent. Its maximality clause (applied to the subgroup itself)
   says that the action weight support of `semigroupSubgroup b A` lies in `A`. Let
   `c = b.onRepresentedSubgroup jU …` be the restricted action of `T` on `U`.
   `onRepresentedSubgroup_act_comp` gives the equivariance of `jU`.

2. **Weights of `U` are the roots `β r`.** `actionWeightSupport_map`
   (`ActionWeightTransport`) and `SubgroupFunctor.map_top` identify the support of
   `c` on all of `U` with the support of `b` on `semigroupSubgroup b A`. Let `χ` be
   in this support. Then `χ ∈ A`, so `χ ≠ 0`. Monotonicity
   (`actionWeightSupport_mono`) and `mem_actionWeightSupport_conjugation_iff` show
   that `χ ∈ roots j`. The enumeration hypothesis then puts `χ` in the range of
   `β`. Conversely every `β r` is a root lying in `A`.

3. **Ordered products in `U`.** `hasOrderedSemigroupProducts_of_unipotent`
   (`DynamicRootProductRayReduction`) applies to `c`, since `T` is split and `U` is
   smooth, connected, affine and unipotent. Then
   `HasOrderedSemigroupProducts.for_larger_support` (`SemigroupProductRestriction`)
   is applied with support `Set.range β` and the singleton blocks `{β r}`. The
   blocks cover the range (`Set.iUnion_singleton_eq_range`) and are pairwise
   disjoint by injectivity. For the cross condition, the closure of `{β s}` is
   `cyclicTail (β s) 1` (`addClosure_singleton_eq_cyclicTail`), and
   `not_mem_cyclicTail_of_ne` excludes `β r` from it for `r ≠ s`. The result is a
   family of closed subgroups `jH r : H r ⟶ U` representing
   `semigroupSubgroup c (closure {β r})`, whose ordered multiplication
   `∏ H r ⟶ U` is an isomorphism.

4. **The factors are the root groups.** `semigroupSubgroup_map_eq`
   (`SemigroupTransport`) carries `semigroupSubgroup c (cyclicTail (β r) 1)` along
   `jU` to `semigroupSubgroup b (cyclicTail (β r) 1)`. This uses
   `cyclicTail (β r) 1 ≤ A` (`cyclicTail_le_positiveMultiples` and
   `semigroupSubgroup_mono`). `cyclicRootFunctorComparison` identifies the latter
   with `subgroup j (β r)`. So `jH r ≫ jU` represents `subgroup j (β r)`
   (`IsRepresentedBy.comp`).

5. **The relative root group lies in `U`.**
   `subgroup_comp_torusHom_le_range_of_roots`
   (`RelativeRootGroupAbsoluteGenerationProof`) gives
   `subgroup (groupHom f ≫ j) a ≤ range jU`. Its hypothesis holds because each
   root `δ` with `f ≫ δ` a positive multiple of `a` satisfies
   `subgroup j δ = semigroupSubgroup b (cyclicTail δ 1) ≤ semigroupSubgroup b A`.

6. **Points.** Put `J r = jH r ≫ jU`. `orderedMultiplication_comp`
   (`RootProductTransport`) gives
   `orderedMultiplication J = orderedMultiplication jH ≫ jU`. It is a monomorphism
   because the first map is an isomorphism and `jU` is a monomorphism. Rational
   points are morphisms from `Over.mk (𝟙 (Spec K))`. A rational point of
   `H r` maps by `J r` into `rootGroupPoints j (β r)`, and every point of
   `rootGroupPoints j (β r)` lifts, because `J r` represents `subgroup j (β r)`.
   - *Existence.* A point `g` of the relative root group lifts to a point `u` of `U`
     (step 5). Let `p = u ≫ inv (orderedMultiplication jH)` and let `x` be its
     components (`finiteProductPointsEquiv`). `comp_orderedMultiplication`
     (`OrderedProductPoints`) writes `g` as the ordered product of the points
     `x r ≫ J r`.
   - *Products lie in the relative root group.* Each factor lies in
     `rootGroupPoints j (β r)`, which is contained in the relative root group by
     `subgroup_le_subgroup_comp_torusHom_of_nsmul`. A list product of members of a
     subgroup is a member (`Subgroup.list_prod_mem`).
   - *Uniqueness.* Lift both families to points of the factors `H r`. Equal
     ordered products become equal composites with `orderedMultiplication J`
     (`finiteProductPointsEquiv_symm_orderedMultiplication`). Cancelling this
     monomorphism and applying injectivity of `finiteProductPointsEquiv` shows the
     lifts are equal, and so are the families.

The proof follows the same pattern as `dynamicRootProductOfLargerSupport` in
`Langlands.GroupScheme.Rational.DynamicRootProductsLargerSupport`. That theorem
uses the positive half-space of a cocharacter and root-ray blocks. Here the
semigroup is `positiveMultiples f a` and the blocks are single roots, which
`rootReducedness` makes possible. No literature definition is transcribed. The
statement's Markdown gives the source, Borel–Tits 1965, proof of Théorème 3.17.
