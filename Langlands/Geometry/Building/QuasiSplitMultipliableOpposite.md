# The opposite-pair axiom at multipliable roots from piece symmetries

Author: Worker 4783.

Lean module: `Langlands.Geometry.Building.QuasiSplitMultipliableOpposite`.

This internal unit belongs to task 8ffe0456 and follows the units
`QuasiSplitMultipliablePieces` and `QuasiSplitMultipliableFactorization`. Over a field where
the torus `T` is split, it proves axiom V 5 for the torus restriction `ψ` of a point `xa` of a
normalizer-stable apartment class, at a multipliable character `a` of a split subtorus `S`.
It assumes a family of *piece symmetries* that preserve the values of `xa` and act
transitively on the roots over each multiple of `a`. Galois automorphisms of a splitting field
supply this family in the final unit, `QuasiSplitIntegerApartmentExistenceProof`.

## Setting and notation

- `f : S ⟶ T` is a homomorphism of tori. `j : T.toGrp ⟶ G` is a closed monomorphism of a
  smooth affine group.
- `P` is a finite root datum of `j` (`IsRootDatumOf j P`). `RestrictsToMultipliableLine f P a`
  says that every root restricting to `n • a` has `n ∈ {±1, ±2}`.
- `U_χ = rootGroupPoints j χ` and `[x, y] = x y x⁻¹ y⁻¹`.
- A piece (`IsMultipliablePiece f P a lp lq lm`) has roots `p ≠ q` over `a` and
  `m = p + q` over `2a`.
- `φ = xa.val` and `ψ = torusRestrictedRootFunctionFamily … f`. The restricted value of `ψ`
  at `a` is defined from the coordinates of a point along all roots over positive multiples
  of `a`.

## Orthogonality and commuting pieces

- `nsmul_add_nsmul_notMem_of_pairing_eq_zero`: two roots `β, β'` of a finite root datum with
  `⟨β, β'^∨⟩ = ⟨β', β^∨⟩ = 0` and `β + β'` not a root. Then no `n β + l β'` with `n, l > 0`
  is zero or a root. The proof descends in `n` and `l` by `root_sub_root_mem_of_pairingIn_pos`.
- `noStrictRootCombinations_of_orthogonal`: for orthogonal roots on the line of `a` (restriction
  coefficients in `{±1, ±2}`), the sum is not a root, so `NoStrictRootCombinations` holds. A
  root `β + β'` would restrict to `(c + c') a`, and its reflection by `β'` to `(c - c') a`.
  Both coefficients must lie in `{±1, ±2}`, which is impossible.
- `pieceRoots P lp lq lm` (definition): the set `{±p, ±q, ±m}`. `pieceGroup j P lp lq lm`
  (definition): the subgroup generated by the `U_χ` with `χ ∈ pieceRoots`.
  `rootGroupPoints_le_pieceGroup`: each such `U_χ` lies in it.
  `commute_of_mem_pieceGroup`: if all generating root groups of two pieces commute pairwise,
  then the two piece groups commute.
- `piece_cross_pairing`: two pieces whose indices over `a` are pairwise distinct are
  orthogonal (`⟨b, b'^∨⟩ = 0` for `b ∈ {p, q, m}`, `b' ∈ {p', q', m'}`). This uses
  `pairing_eq_zero_or_neg_one`, uniqueness of partners (`partner_unique`), and
  `m'^∨ = p'^∨ + q'^∨`.
- `pieceRoots_signed_index` and `pairing_signed_eq_zero` are bookkeeping for negated indices.
- `pieces_commute`: under these hypotheses, all root groups of the two pieces commute (the
  published `commute_of_no_strict_root_combinations`).

## Piece families and coordinates

- `PieceFamily f P a` (structure): a number `r` of pieces with indices
  `lp l, lq l, lm l` (`l : Fin r`). Each triple is a piece, and the indices over `a` of two
  different pieces are pairwise distinct. `lm` is injective. Every root over `a` is some
  `lp l` or `lq l`, and every root over `2a` is some `lm l`.
- `root`, `mult`, `enum`, `enumMult`, `idx`: the enumeration of the roots of all pieces over
  `Fin (r * 3)`, in the order `q, m, p` inside each piece, with multiplicities `1, 2, 1`.
  `restrict_root`, `restrict_enum`, `root_eq_idx` and `enum_finProd` are its basic
  identities.
- `root_injective` and `enum_injective`: the enumeration is injective when `n • a = m • a`
  forces `n = m`.
- `range_enum`: for a reduced root datum and a torsion-free character lattice, the
  enumeration is exactly the set of indivisible roots among the roots restricting to
  positive multiples of `a` (`semigroupIndivisibleRoots`). This is the input of the published
  coordinate theorem `mem_subgroup_comp_torusHom_iff_existsUnique_rootPointsProduct`.
- `list_ofFn_prod_finProd`: a product over `Fin (r * 3)` is the product of consecutive
  triples.
- `tripleProd x z y = ∏ₗ x_l z_l y_l` (definition, ordered product) and `coords`. Their link is
  `rootPointsProduct_coords`.
- `existsUnique_triples`: every point of the restricted root group of `a` is a product
  `∏ₗ x_l z_l y_l` with `x_l ∈ U_q`, `z_l ∈ U_m`, `y_l ∈ U_p`.
  `triples_eq_of_tripleProd_eq`: the triples are unique.
- `tripleProd_value`: if every `φ_m(z_l) = 2c` and every `φ_q(x_l), φ_p(y_l) ≥ c` (with
  `r > 0`), then `ψ_a(∏ₗ x_l z_l y_l) = c`. This uses the published coordinate bounds
  `mem_torusRestrictedRootDepthSubgroup_iff_coordinate_bounds` and
  `le_torusRestrictedRootFunctionFamily_iff`.

## Negation and existence of piece families

- `root_reflectionPerm_self`, `reflectionPerm_self_eq_iff`,
  `eq_reflectionPerm_self_of_root_eq_neg`: the index `P.reflectionPerm i i` of `-P.root i`.
- `PieceFamily.neg` (definition): the negated pieces form a piece family over `-a`, in the
  order `-q, -m, -p` inside each piece. `RestrictsToMultipliableLine.neg` is the analogue for
  the line hypothesis.
- `piece_decomposition_unique`: if two roots over `a` sum to the `m` of a piece, they are the
  `p` and `q` of that piece.
- `PieceFamily.nonempty_of_partners`: let `P` be reduced. Assume every root over `a` has a
  partner over `a` whose sum with it is a root, and every root over `2a` is a sum of two roots
  over `a`. Then a piece family exists. Enumerate the roots over `2a`, choose a
  decomposition of each, and use `partner_unique` for disjointness.

## Separation and opposite coordinates

- `eq_of_mul_mul_mem_normalizer`: let `n = g' g g''` normalize `j` and `groupHom f ≫ j`, with
  `g ≠ 1` in the restricted root group of `α` and `g', g''` in that of `-α`. If `Y g Z`
  normalizes `j` for `Y, Z` in the restricted root group of `-α`, then `g' = Y` and
  `Y g Z = n`. This is separation in opposite dynamic unipotent subgroups (published
  `normalizer_eq_one_of_mul_opposite_torusHom` and `IsOppositeFactorPair.inv_conj_mem`).
- `piece_factorization_uniform`: the two cases of `piece_factorization` have the same left
  factor `Y⁻¹ [Y, X_q] e`. Here `(Y, Y')` is the opposite pair of `X_m` and `e ∈ U_{-q}`. The
  middle factor normalizes `j` in both cases, so the degenerate case does not have to be
  excluded.
- `mem_pieceGroup_of_mem`, `list_ofFn_prod_inv_of_commute`, and `rootGroupPoints_le_comp`
  (roots over positive multiples of `c` lie in the restricted root group of `c`) are helpers.
- `opposite_coordinates`: let `g = ∏ₗ X_l Z_l Y_l ≠ 1` lie in the restricted root group of
  `a`, with every `Z_l ≠ 1`. Let `g', g''` lie in that of `-a`, with `g' g g''` normalizing
  `j` and `groupHom f ≫ j`. Then `g' = ∏ₗ E_l Y_l' D_l` in the coordinates of `PieceFamily.neg`
  (`E_l ∈ U_{-q}`, `Y_l' ∈ U_{-m}`, `D_l ∈ U_{-p}`), with
  `φ_{-m}(Y_l') + φ_m(Z_l) = 0` and `φ_{-p}(D_l) = φ_q(X_l) + φ_{-m}(Y_l')`.

  Proof. Factor each piece uniformly as `L_l N_l R_l`. The factors of different pieces
  commute (`pieces_commute`), so `g = (∏ L_l)(∏ N_l)(∏ R_l)` with `∏ N_l` normalizing `j`.
  Separation gives `g' = (∏ L_l)⁻¹ = ∏ₗ e_l⁻¹ W_l [W_l, X_l]⁻¹`. Here `W_l` is the first
  element of the opposite pair of `Z_l`, so `Y_l' = W_l` and `D_l = [W_l, X_l]⁻¹`. V 5 of
  `xa` gives the first value, and `piece_val_commutator_neg_m` the second.

## Piece symmetries

- `IsPieceSymmetry j P f a s σ` (structure, a proposition): `s` is an injective endomorphism
  of the rational points and `σ` an injective map on root indices. `σ` is additive on roots
  (`P.root i = P.root i' + P.root i''` implies the same for `σ i, σ i', σ i''`). It preserves
  the restriction to every integer multiple `n • a`. Finally, `s` carries `U_{P.root i}` into
  `U_{P.root (σ i)}`. `IsPieceSymmetry.neg` passes to `-a`.
- `PieceFamily.symmetry`: let a piece symmetry `(s, σ)` fix `∏ₗ x_l z_l y_l`, and let
  `σ (lm l) = lm l'`. Then either `σ` preserves the order of the two roots over `a` and `s`
  carries the triple of `l` to that of `l'`, or `σ` exchanges them, with `s y_l = x_{l'}`,
  `s x_l = y_{l'}` and `s (z_l [x_l, y_l]) = z_{l'}`. Proof: `σ` permutes the pieces, and
  `piece_reorder` rewrites each exchanged triple. The image product is a reordering of
  commuting piece triples; uniqueness of triples concludes.
- `PieceFamily.swap_of_symmetry`: if `σ (lp l) = lq l`, then `σ` fixes `lm l` and
  `s y_l = x_l`, `s x_l = y_l`, `s (z_l [x_l, y_l]) = z_l`.

## Main theorem

The setting adds: `G` connected reductive and locally of finite type, `j` a maximal split
torus with opposite factor pairs, `D` a normalizer-stable apartment class of `j`, and `xa` a
point of it.

- `PieceFamily.eq_one_of_swap`: in the situation of `swap_of_symmetry`, if `z_l = 1` then
  `x_l = y_l = 1`. Indeed `s [x_l, y_l] = 1`, so `[x_l, y_l] = 1`. If `x_l ≠ 1`, then also
  `y_l ≠ 1`, and the exact commutator value `piece_val_commutator`
  (`φ_m([x_l, y_l]) = φ_p(y_l) + φ_q(x_l)`) would be finite.
- `PieceFamily.half_le`: if `φ_m(z_l) = κ`, `φ_q(x_l) = φ_p(y_l)` and
  `φ_m(z_l [x_l, y_l]) = φ_m(z_l)`, then `φ_q(x_l) ≥ κ / 2`. Otherwise
  `φ_m([x_l, y_l]) = 2 φ_q(x_l) < κ`. The ultrametric equality
  `IsRootDatumValuation.apply_mul_eq_right_of_lt` would then give `φ_m(z_l [x_l, y_l]) < κ`.
- `torusRestricted_opposite_of_pieceSymmetries`: assume the following.
  - `S` is split, `P` is reduced, and the character lattice of `T` is torsion-free.
  - `a ≠ 0`, and `n • a = m • a` forces `n = m`.
  - Two roots `r₁, r₂` restrict to `a` and their sum is a root.
  - `(s τ, σ τ)` (`τ : Γ`) are piece symmetries with
    `φ_{P.root (σ τ i)}(s τ w) = φ_{P.root i}(w)`.
  - For every `n : ℤ`, any two roots restricting to `n • a` are related by some `σ τ`.

  Let `g ≠ 1` lie in the restricted root group of `a` and `g', g''` in that of `-a`. Let
  `g' g g''` normalize `j` and `groupHom f ≫ j`, and let `g` and `g'` be fixed by every
  `s τ`. Then `ψ_{-a}(g') + ψ_a(g) = 0`.

  Proof.
  1. Transitivity transports the pair `(r₁, r₂)` and its sum to every root over `a` and over
     `2a`, so `nonempty_of_partners` gives a piece family `F`.
  2. Write `g = ∏ₗ X_l Z_l Y_l`. For each `l`, a symmetry exchanging `p` and `q` gives
     `φ_q(X_l) = φ_p(Y_l)` and `φ_m(Z_l [X_l, Y_l]) = φ_m(Z_l)`.
  3. As `g ≠ 1`, `eq_one_of_swap` gives some `Z_{l₀} ≠ 1`, with finite value `κ`.
  4. A symmetry carrying `lm l₀` to `lm l` gives `φ_m(Z_l) = κ` in both cases of
     `PieceFamily.symmetry`, so every `Z_l ≠ 1`.
  5. By `half_le`, `φ_q(X_l), φ_p(Y_l) ≥ κ / 2`, so `ψ_a(g) = κ / 2` (`tripleProd_value`).
  6. `opposite_coordinates` writes `g' = ∏ₗ E_l Y_l' D_l` with `φ_{-m}(Y_l') = -κ` and
     `φ_{-p}(D_l) = φ_q(X_l) - κ ≥ -κ / 2`.
  7. A symmetry exchanging `-p` and `-q`, applied to the fixed point `g'`, gives
     `φ_{-q}(E_l) = φ_{-p}(D_l)`. Hence `ψ_{-a}(g') = -κ / 2`
     (`tripleProd_value` for `PieceFamily.neg`).

## Provenance

The main theorem is the type R2 case of axiom V 5 in Kaletha–Prasad, *Bruhat–Tits Theory: A
New Approach* (2023), Proposition 6.1.19 (printed pp. 206–207, PDF pp. 237–238). The
valuation there is that of Construction 6.1.17 (printed pp. 205–206, PDF pp. 236–237), and
the type R2 classification is in §2.6(c) (printed p. 96, PDF p. 127). Kaletha–Prasad reduce
to the rank-one group `SU₃` and its explicit valuation (§2.7, §3.2). This unit instead uses
an internal route through the split group. It combines piece coordinates, commutation of
different pieces, separation in opposite dynamic unipotent subgroups, the exact commutator
values of the preceding units, and the symmetries of a Galois-fixed point. All statements
are internal. Their proofs compose published Library results, Mathlib, and the units
`QuasiSplitApartmentOpposite`, `QuasiSplitMultipliablePieces` and
`QuasiSplitMultipliableFactorization` of this task.

## Scope

The symmetry and transitivity hypotheses are explicit premises of the main theorem. This unit
does not construct them; the final unit derives them from Galois theory for a finite Galois
splitting field. The degenerate case of `piece_factorization` is handled uniformly through
`piece_factorization_uniform` rather than excluded.
