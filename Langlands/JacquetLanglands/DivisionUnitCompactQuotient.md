# Compactness modulo scalars and right Haar invariance of division units

In any finite-dimensional division algebra over a nonarchimedean local field,
write a nonzero element in a fixed basis over the base field. Dividing by a
coordinate of maximal valuation puts all coordinates in the compact integer
ring and makes one coordinate equal to one. The set of normalized coordinate
vectors is compact and avoids zero. The module topology and the open embedding
of the unit group identify its inverse image with a compact set of division
units meeting every coset modulo scalar units.

Scalar units are central. The image of that compact set is the entire left
quotient by any subgroup containing the center, so the quotient is compact.
The intrinsic modular character is one on scalar units by left Haar invariance.
Its range is therefore the continuous image of the compact set of representatives.
Bounded powers force its value at each element and at its inverse to be at most
one, hence both values are one. This gives right invariance of regular left Haar
measures.

its center for division algebras. The argument here derives it directly from
