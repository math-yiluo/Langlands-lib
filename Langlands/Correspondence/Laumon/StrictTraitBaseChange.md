# Finite constant base change on the strict target

Let `S = Spec R` be a rational henselian end over a finite field `k`,
with fraction field `K'`, chosen separable closure `Ω_s`, and maximal
unramified field `K^nr`. Fix a finite subfield `k₁` of the geometric constants
in `Ω_s` containing the image of `k`. The strict target of `StrictTargetTrait`
uses the integral closure `R^nr` of `R` in `K^nr` and reduction modulo `t`.
This gives the pointed strict henselization `S^nr` of SGA 7 XIII,
(2.1.3)–(2.1.4), p. 100. Laumon (1987), (2.1.1), p. 151, describes
this trait by extension of the constants and factors the geometric
generic point through its generic point. The geometric fibre diagram
and transport of structure in SGA 7 XIII, (1.3.2.1)–(1.3.2.2), p. 94,
use the full normalization `S̄` in `Ω_s` of (0.2.5), p. 86. Its
geometric points factor through `S̄ → S^nr`, and the comparisons here
use their maps to `S^nr`.

`constantExtensionField` is the compositum `K₁ = K' k₁`, and
`constantExtensionRing` is `R₁ = R^nr ∩ K₁`. The maps
`constantExtensionMap`, `constantExtensionGenericHom` and
`constantExtensionConstantsHom` are the inclusions of `R` in `R₁`, `R₁`
in `K₁`, and `k₁` in `R₁`. `ConstantExtensionTraitStatement` says that
`R₁` is a henselian discrete valuation ring generated over `R` by `k₁`,
with fraction field `K₁` and maximal ideal generated by the image of `t`.
Reduction identifies its residue ring with `k₁`. The common maximal
unramified field is expressed by the equality `K^nr = K₁ k̄` inside `Ω_s`.
The integral closure of `R₁` in this field is the same `R^nr`, with
maximal ideal contracting to that of `R₁`. The two pointed strict traits
thus use the same geometric generic map through `Ω_s` and the same
special map given by `R^nr → R^nr/(t)`. The fixing subgroup of `K₁` is
open in `Gal(Ω_s/K')` and is the inverse image of the subgroup fixing
the image of `k₁` on the strict residue ring.

Given a chart `X → S`, `chartBaseChange` forms `X₁` by pullback to
`Spec R₁`. `strictChartBaseChangeIso` compares `X₁^nr` to `X^nr` by
pullback pasting over `S^nr`. The maps on the special fibre, the generic
fibre over `K^nr`, and the geometric generic fibre over `Ω_s` are
induced by this comparison and the identities on the corresponding
points of the strict trait. `strictChartBaseChangeModelHom` uses these
maps to compare the `NearbyCyclesModel` objects of `traitNearbyModel`
on the two charts.

For a complex `B` on `X` with commutative condensed coefficients, the
input on `X₁` is the derived pullback of `B`. The isomorphism
`strictChartBaseChangeInputIso` uses `pullbackSquareComparison` for the
two factorizations of `X₁^nr → X` to compare its strict pullback with
the pullback of `B^nr`. `strictChartBaseChangeNearby` compares the values
of `NearbyCyclesModel.nearbyCycles` by `Hom.pullbackExchange` followed
by this input identification. `strictChartBaseChangeSpecialRestriction`
uses the pullback comparison on the special square followed by the same
input identification. `StrictChartBaseChangeSpecializationStatement`
says that the three fibre maps and the pullback exchange are invertible,
the two comparisons on the complexes are invertible, and their square
with `nearbySpecialization` commutes. This compares the arrows obtained
from `twistSpecializationMap` using the geometric nearby cycles of
SGA 7 XIII, (2.1.2.3), p. 99, and the specialization arrow in (2.1.2.4),
p. 100.

`StrictChartBaseChangeGaloisCompatibility` states that the total-space
and geometric generic maps commute with the automorphisms induced by
an element of the fixing subgroup. On the special fibre its commutative
square compares the two `nearbyCyclesAction` maps using
`pullbackSquareComparison` for the special-fibre automorphisms. This
identifies the action on the extended chart with the restriction of
the original action to `Gal(Ω_s/K₁)`. The chart comparisons are defined
over compatible ring maps to the fixed `R^nr`.
`StrictTraitConstantBaseChangeStatement` combines these statements for
the actual finite constant extension. It expresses the strict-target
comparison underlying Laumon (1987), (2.5.2), p. 165, where the local
Fourier transforms commute with extension of the constants.
