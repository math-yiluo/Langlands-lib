# Completion of the decomposition field of a place of a Dedekind domain

`Langlands.Henniart.Laumon.PlaceCompletionStatement` compares a decomposition field with a
Laurent series field over a finite field. It assembles the finite-level decomposition and
inertia comparisons of Serre into a statement about separable closures and their Krull
topologies.

## The statement

Let `R` be a Dedekind domain (`IsDedekindDomain`), `K` its fraction field (`IsFractionRing`)
and `Ω` a separable closure of `K` (`IsSepClosure`), with compatible `R`-algebra structures
(`IsScalarTower R K Ω`). Assume `ringChar K ≠ 0`. Let `p` be a nonzero maximal ideal of `R`
with `R ⧸ p` finite. Write `B = integralClosure R Ω` and let `Q` be a maximal ideal of `B`
with `Q.comap (algebraMap R B) = p`. The decomposition field `K_Q` is
`IntermediateField.fixedField (MulAction.stabilizer Gal(Ω/K) Q)`.

There exist a finite field `κ` in the same universe as `R`, ring maps
`ι₀ : K_Q → LaurentSeries κ` and `ι : Ω → SeparableClosure (LaurentSeries κ)`, and a
topological group isomorphism
`φ : Gal(SeparableClosure (LaurentSeries κ)/LaurentSeries κ) ≃ₜ* Gal(Ω/K_Q)` with the
following properties.

1. `ι₀` has dense range in the `t`-adic topology of `LaurentSeries κ`. On `K_Q`, the map
   `ι` is `ι₀` followed by the canonical embedding into the separable closure.
2. For every `σ` and `a`, `ι (φ σ a) = σ (ι a)`. Thus `φ` acts by restriction along `ι`.
3. For every `b ∈ B`, `closureValuation` assigns `ι b` a value at most `1`. Its value is
   strictly less than `1` exactly when `b ∈ Q`.
4. Membership of `σ` in `absoluteInertia` is equivalent to membership of
   `AlgEquiv.restrictScalarsHom K (φ σ)` in `Q.inertia Gal(Ω/K)`.
5. There is a ring isomorphism `ψ : B ⧸ Q ≃+* AlgebraicClosure 𝓀`, where
   `𝓀 = localResidueField (LaurentSeries κ)`. If `c : closureIntegers` has underlying
   element `ι b`, then `ψ (b mod Q)` equals `residueClosureEquiv` applied to
   `IsLocalRing.residue _ c`. For `b, b' ∈ B` with `b' = φ σ b` in `Ω`, the equality
   `ψ (b' mod Q) = absoluteResidueHom σ (ψ (b mod Q))` gives the comparison of actions.

The local Galois objects are those of `Langlands.LocalGalois.SepClosed`, and the residue
field is `Langlands.Weil.Local.SepClosed.localResidueField`. The Laurent valuation uses the
local instance `Langlands.LocalField.Laurent.valuativeRel`. The residue clause binds
`localNormedField`, `localNormedField_ultrametric` and `localNormedField_complete` as local
instances. [Local Galois definitions](../../Galois/Local/AbsoluteDefinitions.lean),
[local Weil groups](../../Weil/Local/Assembly.lean),
[Laurent series fields](../../LocalField/LaurentSeries.lean)

## Derivation from the finite-level comparisons

The localization `R_p` is a discrete valuation ring. Its completion has equal positive
characteristic and the finite, hence perfect, residue field `κ = R ⧸ p`. Lifting `κ` to
the coefficient field and choosing a uniformizer identifies the completed ring with
`κ[[t]]`. Taking fraction fields gives a dense embedding of `K` into `F = κ((t))` that
induces `p` on `R`. (Serre, II §4, Theorem 2 and Proposition 8.)

Extend this embedding to `ι : Ω → F^sep`. Elements of `B` map into the valuation ring of
`F^sep` by integrality, and the preimage of its maximal ideal is a prime over `p`, hence
maximal. At every finite Galois level inside `Ω`, Serre I §7, Proposition 19 gives
transitivity on the primes above `p`. Compactness of `Gal(Ω/K)` gives a single
automorphism carrying the prescribed place to the one induced by the embedding, so
precomposition makes the induced place equal to `Q`. The valuation on `F^sep` is
Galois invariant by uniqueness at each finite level. (Serre, II §2, Proposition 3 and
Corollaries 2–3.)

The image `ι(Ω)` is stable under `Gal(F^sep/F)` because `Ω/K` is normal. At a finite
Galois subextension `M/K`, let `B_M` be the integral closure of `R` in `M` and
`Q_M = Q ∩ B_M`. The completion of `M` at `Q_M` is the compositum `F·ι(M)`, with
Galois group over `F` identified by restriction with the stabilizer of `Q_M`. Here the
localization of `B_M` over `R_p` is finite over `R_p` by separability. Its completion is
the product of the valuation rings of the completed fields at the primes over `p`.
(Serre, II §3, Theorem 1, Corollaries 1, 3–4 and Proposition 4.)

By Krasner's lemma, a finite separable extension of `F` can be generated by a root of
a separable polynomial over the dense subfield `K`. Indeed, one approximates a monic
minimal polynomial over `F` by one over `K` defining an isomorphic extension of `F`.
Such a root lies in `ι(Ω)`, so the fields `F·ι(M)` exhaust `F^sep`.
(Serre, II §2, Exercises 1–2.) The finite restriction isomorphisms are compatible, and
their inverse limit identifies `Gal(F^sep/F)`, with its Krull topology, with the closed
stabilizer of `Q`. By Galois correspondence this stabilizer is `Gal(Ω/K_Q)` under
restriction of scalars. Its fixed elements are precisely the preimages under `ι` of
elements of `F`. The restriction `ι₀` to `K_Q` therefore takes values in `F` and has dense
range because it contains the image of `K`.

For each `M`, the completion at `Q_M` preserves the residue field `B_M/Q_M` and its
decomposition action. Since the composita exhaust `F^sep`, reduction of `ι` maps `B/Q`
onto the residue field of `F^sep`. It is injective because the maximal ideal pulls back
to `Q`. Composing with `residueClosureEquiv` gives `ψ`. The equality
`ι (φ σ a) = σ (ι a)` descends to the residue fields, and the transported action is
`absoluteResidueHom`. The inertia comparison follows because both inertia groups are
the kernels of their respective actions on the residue fields. (Serre, I §7,
Proposition 20 and II §3, Theorem 1(ii) and Proposition 4.)

For an affine chart `W` of a smooth integral curve `C` over a finite field, take
`R = Γ(C, W)`, `K = C.functionField`, `Ω = SeparableClosure C.functionField` and `p`
the prime of a closed point in `W`. Unfolding `placeDecompositionField C W Q` gives
exactly the completion clause of `BoundaryStalkInertiaStatement`.
