# Henselian end traits and their completions

The end trait of `Langlands.Correspondence.Laumon.Traits` has fraction field `endField k`, the
separable closure of `k(t)` in `k((t))`. It is dense in `LaurentSeries k`, its completion.
Katz (1986), (1.4.8), pp. 81–82, applies the general fact that the fraction field of a
henselian discrete valuation ring "has the same Galois theory as its completion" to the union
of the fields `k'((t))` over the finite Galois constant extensions `k'/k`, whose completion is
`k^sep((t))`; Corollary 1.4.9, p. 82, is the resulting commutative comparison of the exact
sequences over the common residue Galois quotient `Gal(k^sep/k)`. This module applies the same
general fact to the end field `endField k` and its completion `LaurentSeries k`, a separate
specialization of it. Laumon (1987), (2.1.1), p. 151,
attaches to a henselian trait its Galois group `G`, inertia `I`, wild inertia `P` (the kernel
of the action on the roots of order prime to `p` of a uniformizer) and the upper filtration
`I^(λ)` of Serre, *Local Fields*, IV §3. The Library defines these groups for the local field
`LaurentSeries k`, with `Langlands.LocalField.Laurent.valuativeRel` installed as a local
instance. This module states that restriction to the henselian field carries them to the
corresponding structures of the end trait. It defines no second version of the ramification
groups: on the henselian side it states Serre's lower-group congruences directly, as the
predicate `HenselianLowerTest`, and compares them with the Library's groups of the completed
extensions.

## Restriction

- `endGenericClosure_isSepClosure`, `endGenericClosure_isGalois` and
  `endGenericClosure_normal` record that the separable generic closure
  `endGenericClosure k` is a separable closure of `endField k`, hence Galois over it (Mathlib's
  `separableClosure.isSepClosure` and `IsSepClosure.isGalois`).
- `completionRestriction k` sends `σ : Gal(SeparableClosure (LaurentSeries k)/LaurentSeries k)`
  to its restriction to `endGenericClosure k`, after restricting scalars to `endField k`
  (Mathlib's `AlgEquiv.restrictScalarsHom` and `AlgEquiv.restrictNormalHom`). The two
  separable closures are compatible by construction, so no embedding is chosen.

## `HenselianCompletionGaloisStatement`

For every field `k`, `completionRestriction k` is a homeomorphism for the Krull topologies;
being a monoid homomorphism, it is an isomorphism of topological groups. This is the general
completion fact used in Katz's (1.4.8), specialized to the end field. It holds for every field
`k`. The end field is henselian
(`EndTraitHenselizationStatement`) and separably closed in its completion `k((t))`. Every
finite separable extension of `k((t))` is therefore generated by an element separable over
the end field (Krasner's lemma), which gives injectivity. A finite Galois extension of the
end field stays a field after tensoring with `k((t))`, which gives surjectivity (Serre, *Local
Fields*, II §3, Theorem 1 and Proposition 4, pp. 30–32, for the comparison of extensions and
completions).

## `HenselianCompletionRamificationStatement`

Here `k` is a finite field, `p = ringChar k`, `q = Nat.card k`, `X = LaurentSeries k`,
`Ω_X = SeparableClosure X`, `Ω = endGenericClosure k` and `t` is the uniformizer of the end
trait in `Ω`. The statement has six clauses.

1. **Inertia.** The image of `Langlands.LocalGalois.SepClosed.absoluteInertia X Ω_X` is the
   fixing subgroup of `endUnramifiedField k`, the maximal unramified subextension of `Ω`. This
   is the inertia group `I = π₁(η₁, η̄)` of Laumon (2.1.1), read on the strict target trait.
2. **Residue map.** There is a ring isomorphism `φ` from the constants `endConstants k` (the
   algebraic closure of `k` in `Ω`) onto `AlgebraicClosure 𝓀[X]` such that the Library's
   `Langlands.LocalGalois.SepClosed.absoluteResidueHom X Ω_X σ` acts on `φ a` as the
   restriction of `σ` acts on `a`. Reduction of the integral elements of `Ω_X` maps the
   constants isomorphically onto the residue field of `Ω_X`, and the Library compares that
   residue field with `AlgebraicClosure 𝓀[X]`. The isomorphism is not unique; since the
   Galois group of the algebraic closure of a finite field is abelian, the transported action
   does not depend on it, so the clause determines the residue map.
3. **Degree.** For `w` in the Library's local Weil group
   `Langlands.Weil.Local.SepClosed.localWeilGroup X Ω_X`, with `n = localDegreeInt X Ω_X w`
   and `σ = localProjection X Ω_X w`, the restriction of `σ` satisfies
   `σ(a) ^ (q ^ n) = a` for `n ≥ 0` and `σ(a) = a ^ (q ^ (-n))` for `n < 0`, for every constant
   `a`. The Lean clause writes both as `σ(a) ^ (q ^ n.toNat) = a ^ (q ^ (-n).toNat)`. So an
   element of degree `n` acts on the constants as the `n`-th power of geometric Frobenius
   `a ↦ a^(1/q)`: the Library normalizes the degree by geometric Frobenius, with value `1`
   on `geometricFrobenius`, the inverse of `a ↦ a^q`.
4. **Wild inertia.** The image of `Langlands.Ramification.Local.localWildInertia X Ω_X`, the
   closure of the union of the upper groups of positive level, consists of the elements of
   the inertia group of clause 1 that fix every `ϖ ∈ Ω` with `ϖ ^ N = t` for some `N` prime to
   `p`. This is Laumon's description of `P` in (2.1.1) as the kernel of the tame character
   given by the action on the roots of the uniformizer. At finite levels the first lower
   group is the `p`-Sylow subgroup of inertia with cyclic quotient of order prime to `p`
   (Serre, IV §2, Proposition 7 and Corollaries 1–4, pp. 66–68). The upper groups of small
   positive level are the first lower group (IV §3, pp. 73–75). The Library's
   `Langlands.Ramification.Wintenberger.localAbsoluteUpperFiniteImage` gives their exact
   images in finite quotients.
5. **Lower groups at each finite level.** `HenselianLowerTest k L i g` is the lower
   ramification test of index `i` on the henselian elements of a finite Galois extension `L`
   of `X` inside `Ω_X`, i.e. on the field `L ∩ Ω`: for a uniformizer `ϖ` of `L ∩ Ω` (an element
   of valuation `< 1` whose valuation is maximal among such elements), `g` moves every element
   `b` of `L ∩ Ω` with valuation `≤ 1` by an element of valuation at most `v(ϖ)^(i+1)`. This
   is Serre's condition that `g` act trivially on the integers modulo the `(i+1)`-st power of
   the maximal ideal (IV §1, Lemma 1 and Proposition 1, pp. 61–62), on the henselian integer
   ring `L ∩ Ω ∩ {v ≤ 1}`; the valuation is the Library's `closureValuation`, which restricts
   to the valuation of the henselian field. The clause states that for every `L`, every
   `σ` and every `i ≥ 0`, the restriction of `σ` to `L`
   (`Langlands.Ramification.Local.absoluteFiniteRestriction`) lies in the Library's lower group
   `Langlands.Ramification.Local.lowerRamificationGroup (localTopologicalData X L) X i` of the
   completed extension exactly when `completionRestriction k σ` passes `HenselianLowerTest k L i`.
   So the lower filtrations of each finite henselian extension `L ∩ Ω` of the end field and of
   its completion `L` correspond (Serre, II §3, pp. 30–32, for the completions).
6. **Upper groups of every level.** For every real `v ≥ -1`, an automorphism `τ` of `Ω` over
   the end field lies in the image of
   `Langlands.Ramification.Local.localAbsoluteUpperRamificationGroup X Ω_X v` exactly when, for
   every finite level `L`, either `⌈ψ_L(v)⌉ < 0` or `τ` passes `HenselianLowerTest` of index
   `⌈ψ_L(v)⌉`, where `ψ_L` is the Library's inverse Herbrand function
   `Langlands.Ramification.Local.herbrandPsi` of `L/X`. This is Serre's upper numbering
   `G^v = G_{ψ(v)}` at each finite level and its inverse limit (IV §3, pp. 73–75, Proposition 14
   and its first remark), expressed through the congruences on the henselian integers. By
   clause 5, the Herbrand function of `L/X` is also that of the henselian extension `L ∩ Ω`,
   as both are computed from the orders of the same lower groups.

## Library declarations

The module imports `Langlands.Correspondence.Laumon.Traits`,
`Langlands.LocalField.LaurentSeries`, `Langlands.Weil.Local.Assembly` and
`Langlands.LocalFactor.Ramification.Herbrand.GeneralAbsolute`. It uses the Library's
`Langlands.LocalField.Laurent.valuativeRel` and `localField`,
`Langlands.LocalGalois.SepClosed.absoluteInertia` and `absoluteResidueHom`,
`Langlands.Weil.Local.SepClosed.localWeilGroup`, `localProjection`, `localDegreeInt` and
`localResidueField`, and `Langlands.Ramification.Local.localWildInertia`,
`localAbsoluteUpperRamificationGroup`, `absoluteFiniteRestriction`, `lowerRamificationGroup`,
`localTopologicalData` and `herbrandPsi`, and `Langlands.LocalGalois.SepClosed.closureValuation`. It uses no
declaration of weil-conjectures beyond those of `Langlands.Correspondence.Laumon.Traits`.
Every declaration of this module depends only on `propext`, `Classical.choice` and
`Quot.sound`.
