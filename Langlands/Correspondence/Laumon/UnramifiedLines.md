# Unramified lines and restriction to the local Weil group

Laumon (1987), (2.1.1), p. 151, gives the exact sequence `1 → I → G → Gal(k̄/k) → 1` of the
Galois group of a henselian trait, with the residue Galois group as quotient. By (2.1.5),
p. 154, a `G`-module is geometrically constant (unramified) when `I` acts trivially, i.e. when
the action factors through `Gal(k̄/k)`. By (2.5.2), p. 165, the local Fourier transforms
commute with the base changes `k → k₁`, for `k₁` an extension of `k` contained in `k̄`; Laumon
uses this to reduce the proof of the assertions (i) b), (ii) b) and (iii) b) of Théorème (2.4.3)
to the case of an algebraically closed `k`. The pullback of an arithmetic line on `Spec k` to
the punctured end traits, and the fact that its monodromy is the character composed with the
residue map, is a derived comparison: it follows from the residue quotient of (2.1.1) and the
functoriality of the field monodromy of `Langlands.Correspondence.Laumon.FieldMonodromy` along
`η_e → Spec k`.

Henniart (1988), §4.1, pp. 509–510, fixes a prime `ℓ` distinct from `p` and calls an ℓ-adic
representation of the Galois group `G_K` a continuous homomorphism `G_K → GL(V)`, with `V`
finite-dimensional over `ℚ̄_ℓ`, that is defined over a finite extension of `ℚ_ℓ`; when the
restriction to inertia is irreducible, the image of inertia is finite and some twist by an
unramified ℓ-adic character has finite image. §4.2, p. 510, compares the finite-image complex
and finite-image ℓ-adic representations of `G_K`, compatibly with twists by (unramified)
characters of finite order. §4.3, p. 510, uses that Laumon's local Fourier transform is
compatible with twists by unramified ℓ-adic characters, which follows from its geometric origin
(Henniart cites [Lm] 2.5.2 and 3.6.3). The line of an unramified character defined here is the
lisse sheaf behind such a twist: by the statement below, its pullback to the ends has monodromy
the character. The comparison of continuous Galois representations with their restrictions to
the Library's local Weil group (§1.1, p. 497, distinguishes the Weil group `W_K` from `G_K`) is
not taken from these passages; it is a derived comparison by density, explained after
`LocalWeilRestrictionStatement` below.

This module provides the arithmetic line of an unramified character and the comparison of
continuous Galois representations with their restrictions to the Library's local Weil group,
for `LaurentSeries k` with `k` finite (with the Library's
`Langlands.LocalField.Laurent.valuativeRel` as a local instance).

## The line of an unramified character

Let `χ` be in the Library's `Langlands.Henniart.LocalNumerical.ladicUnramifiedCharacters` of
`X = LaurentSeries k` and `Ω_X = SeparableClosure X`: a continuous character of the Galois group
with values in `Eˣ`, `E = PadicAlgCl ℓ`, trivial on inertia and with values in a finite
extension of `ℚ_ℓ`.

- `puncturedEndToBase k e` is the structure map `η → Spec k` of the punctured end trait,
  through the constants `endConstantHom k`.
- `unramifiedScalar k ℓ χ` is `χ` evaluated at `localProjection` of the Library's
  `Langlands.Weil.Local.SepClosed.localGeometricFrobenius`, a Weil element of degree `1`. This
  is the Weil scalar of `χ`. As `χ` is trivial on inertia, it does not depend on the choice of
  the Frobenius lift.
- `IsUnramifiedLineFor k ℓ c G` says that the stalk of the lisse sheaf `G` on `Spec k` at
  `fieldGeometricPoint k (AlgebraicClosure k)` has rank one and that the Library's geometric
  Frobenius `Galois.FiniteField.geometricFrobenius k` (the inverse of `a ↦ a^q`) acts on it,
  through `fieldMonodromy` of `Langlands.Correspondence.Laumon.FieldMonodromy`, by the scalar
  `c`. `AlgebraicClosure k` is a separable closure of the perfect field `k`.
- `unramifiedLine k ℓ χ` is a lisse sheaf with `IsUnramifiedLineFor` for the Weil scalar of `χ`,
  chosen when one exists, and the constant sheaf `ℚ̄_ℓ` otherwise. A lisse line on `Spec k` is
  determined up to isomorphism by the action of geometric Frobenius, as the Galois group of `k`
  is topologically generated by it. The definition uses this property and does not require a
  proof of existence; the existence is the first clause of the statement below.

## `UnramifiedLineMonodromyStatement`

For every finite field `k`, prime `ℓ` and `χ` as above:

1. `unramifiedLine k ℓ χ` is a line on which geometric Frobenius acts by the Weil scalar.
   Such a line exists because the Weil scalar is a unit in the ring of integers of a finite
   extension of `ℚ_ℓ`, so `Frob ↦ c` defines a continuous character of the Galois group of
   `k`, which the monodromy equivalence of `LadicFieldMonodromyStatement` realizes by a lisse
   sheaf.
2. For every end `e` (the four ends share the punctured trait), the pullback of the line along
   `puncturedEndToBase k e` has monodromy at the geometric generic point of the end trait given
   by `χ`: an element `completionRestriction k σ` of the Galois group of the end field acts by
   the scalar `χ σ`. So the pullbacks at the ends `0` and `∞` (and `0'`, `∞'`) are the same
   arithmetic line, and through the completion bridge of `Langlands.Correspondence.Laumon.Completion`
   their monodromy is `χ`, i.e. the character of `Gal(k̄/k)` composed with the residue map.
3. On the Library's local Weil group, `χ (localProjection w) = c ^ n` for every `w` of degree
   `n = localDegreeInt w`, with `c` the Weil scalar: the Frobenius of the line is the Weil scalar
   of `χ`.

## `LocalWeilRestrictionStatement`

For a finite field `k`, a prime `ℓ` and representations `A`, `B` in
`Langlands.FiniteRepresentation.{0, u, uW} E (Ω_X ≃ₐ[X] Ω_X)` satisfying the Library's
`IsLadicContinuous`:

1. a linear map `A → B` intertwines the Galois actions if and only if it intertwines the actions
   of the Library's local Weil group through `Langlands.Weil.Local.SepClosed.localProjection`;
2. `A` and `B` are isomorphic if and only if their restrictions
   (`Langlands.FiniteRepresentation.restrict` along `localProjection`) are;
3. `A` is irreducible if and only if its restriction is;
4. `A` satisfies `IsLadicDefinedOverFiniteExtension` if and only if its restriction does.

`LocalWeilRestrictionStatement` is a derived comparison of the Library's objects: the image
of `localProjection` is dense in the Galois group, the sets of Galois elements on which an
intertwining relation or the stability of a subspace holds are closed for a continuous action,
and a finite extension of `ℚ_ℓ` is closed in `E`. Continuity is the only hypothesis. The
comparison applies to arithmetic lisse monodromy via the continuity clause of
`LadicFieldMonodromyStatement`. It is the restriction used, together with scalar extension, by
the Library's `ladicWeilTransport`, which restricts along the same `localProjection`.

## The prime `ℓ`

Both statements hold for every prime `ℓ`, including `ℓ = p`: they concern field monodromy, the
residue Galois quotient and the restriction of continuous representations, none of which needs
`ℓ` invertible in `k` (the monodromy equivalence of `LadicFieldMonodromyStatement` holds for
every `ℓ`). Laumon's Fourier setting ((0.1), p. 134) and Henniart's §4.1 take `ℓ` distinct from
the residue characteristic; the statements here are used in that case.
