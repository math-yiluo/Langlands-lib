# Lifting graded unit characters to the Weil group

`Langlands.Henniart.Numerical.GradedUnitCharacters K l` is the character group of the Library's actual quotient `U_K^l / U_K^(l+1)`, with values in `ℂˣ`. It is notation internal to the conductor-count proof. At `l > 0`, a uniformizer identifies the quotient with the additive residue field. Finite abelian duality over `ℂ` gives the same cardinality for the group and its dual; `card_gradedUnitCharacters` records that cardinality as an extended natural, namely the residue cardinality of `K`.

For a geometric local reciprocity candidate, `upperUnitReciprocity` is the surjective homomorphism from the actual upper ramification group at a nonnegative real index `s` to the actual unit group at level `⌈s⌉`. It uses the published upper-numbering reciprocity theorem. At an integral index `l`, `upperGradedReciprocity` further composes this restriction of reciprocity with the quotient projection to the grade, and remains surjective.

Every graded character gives a character on `U_K^l` by composition with the projection. The published algebraic extension theorem for complex characters extends it to `Kˣ`. The extension is continuous because it is trivial on the open subgroup `U_K^(l+1)`. `liftGradedCharacterToField` chooses such an extension and `liftGradedCharacterToWeil` pulls it back along Weil reciprocity. For a geometric candidate its value at an element of the actual upper group at `l` is the given graded character evaluated through `upperGradedReciprocity`. At any real index `s > l` the corresponding unit level is at least `l+1`, so the lift is trivial on that upper group.

This implements the choice of character representatives in Guy Henniart, *La conjecture de Langlands locale numérique pour GL(n)*, Ann. Sci. École Norm. Sup. (4) 21 (1988), §3.4, p. 509 (registered source `henniart-1988-numerical`). The choices do not assert a homomorphic splitting of the restriction to the unit grade. The module neither identifies which scalar actions descend to a unit grade nor proves the conductor-count identity.
