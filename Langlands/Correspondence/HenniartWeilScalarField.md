# A finite Galois scalar field for a Weil representation

`Langlands.Henniart.Numerical.WeilScalarField K A` is internal proof data. It consists of a finite Galois intermediate field `M` of the common closure of `K`, a continuous character of the actual fixing Weil subgroup `W_M`, and two proofs: the given representation `A` restricts to that character times the identity on `W_M`, and every nonnegative upper ramification group acting by scalars on `A` lies in `W_M`.

`exists_weilScalarField` produces these data for any continuous irreducible finite-dimensional complex Weil representation, with the field and representation-carrier universes independent. It first uses the published unramified scalar normalization to put the representation in finite-image Galois form up to unramified twist. In the absolute Galois group it takes the inverse image of the center of the group of invertible endomorphisms on the normalized carrier. This normal subgroup contains the open kernel, so its fixed field is finite Galois. Elements of the fixing Weil subgroup commute in the representation with the irreducible normalized action. Schur's lemma makes them scalars, and the eigenline construction gives their character. The scalar character's kernel contains the pullback of the open kernel of the Galois representation, proving continuity. Restoring the unramified twist and transporting across the given intertwiner gives the scalar restriction of the original representation. Conversely, a scalar upper action at a nonnegative index remains scalar under normalization, since the upper group is in inertia. These scalar units are central, so the upper group lies in the fixing Weil subgroup.

This is the finite-field step for the descent in Guy Henniart, *La conjecture de Langlands locale numérique pour GL(n)*, Ann. Sci. École Norm. Sup. (4) 21 (1988), Proposition 3.3 (registered source `henniart-1988-numerical`), and the direct proof of `prop:H33` in the 16 November 2026 numerical LLC route note. The implementation uses the projective kernel of the finite-image normalization, rather than taking the subgroup generated by the representation kernel and a particular upper group. It does not prove descent through the norm on the unit grades.
