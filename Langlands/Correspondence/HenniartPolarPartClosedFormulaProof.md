# The closed formula for the polar-part count

`Langlands.Henniart.polarPartClosedFormula` proves
`Langlands.Henniart.PolarPartClosedFormulaStatement` in the published polar-part
model. The formula is Lemma 3.2 (`lem:nu`) of the operator's 16 November 2026

`operator-numerical-llc-route-2026-11-16` (TeX lines 234–249, PDF pp. 5–6).

## Counting the admissible maps

Work over a finite extension `L/k` of degree `f` and consider maps on
`{0, …, m}` with both endpoints nonzero. For such a map `c`, let `d` be the
degree of the intermediate field generated by its coefficients and let `g` be
`gcd(r, polarSupportGcd c)`. Then `g` divides `D = gcd(r, m)`. For `m > 0`
the nonzero last coefficient puts `m` in the support, and for `m = 0` every
natural number divides `m`.

The sum of `μ(f/i)` over `i ∣ f` with `d ∣ i` is the indicator of `d = f`.
The sum of `μ(e)` over `e ∣ D` with `e ∣ g` is the indicator of `g = 1`.
Both are instances of divisor Möbius inversion. Multiplying the indicators
and summing over the maps gives the admissible-map count in `ℤ`, expressed
as a double sum of weighted cardinalities.

For each `i ∣ f`, the proof chooses an intermediate field `E` of degree `i`
and uses the finite-field inclusion criterion to identify `d ∣ i` with
all coefficients lying in `E`. For each `e ∣ D`, it identifies `e ∣ g`
with vanishing at indices outside the multiples of `e`. Restricting to
those multiples and dividing their indices by `e` gives an equivalence
with the maps on `{0, …, m/e}` into `E` with nonzero endpoints. Its inverse
inserts zeros at the omitted indices. The restricted set has cardinality
`(|E| − 1)² |E|^(m/e − 1)` when `m/e > 0`. When `m/e = 0` there is a
single endpoint, so its cardinality is `|E| − 1`.

## Passage to the orbit count

A Galois automorphism fixing a map fixes every coefficient and hence their
generated field, which is `L` for an admissible map. The resulting free
action identifies the admissible-map type with the product of its orbit
type and `Gal(L/k)`, giving the cardinality factor `|Gal(L/k)|`.

For the fields in `polarPartCount`, `|k| = q`, the chosen extension has
degree `f`, and its Galois group has order `f`. Thus `|E| = q^i` and the
admissible-map count equals `f · ν(n,f,s;q)`. When `s > 0`, every inner index
has `m/e > 0`, and factoring out `(q^i − 1)²` gives the positive Swan
formula. When `s = 0`, every inner term has the single-endpoint factor
`q^i − 1`. The remaining divisor sum is `∑_{e ∣ n/f} μ(e)`. It is zero
when `f ≠ n` since then `n/f ≠ 1`, and it is one when `f = n`. This gives
both zero-Swan conclusions of the statement.
