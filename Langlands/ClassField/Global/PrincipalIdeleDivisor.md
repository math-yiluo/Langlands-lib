# Principal idele divisor compatibility

`principalIdeleDivisorFormula.{u}` proves the exact proposition
`PrincipalIdeleDivisorFormula.{u}` from
`Langlands.ClassField.Global.PrincipalIdeleDivisorStatement`. For every number
field `E : Type u`, finite place `w`, and nonzero element `a : Eˣ`, the order
of the actual principal idele at `w` equals the integer multiplicity of `w`
in the principal fractional ideal generated by `a`. This includes negative
orders and imposes no integrality condition on `a`.

The private helper `valuation_eq_exp_neg_count_spanSingleton` first proves
that the field valuation of a nonzero element is the exponential of the
negative multiplicity of its principal fractional ideal. It writes the element
as `n/d` with `n` in the ring of integers and `d` a non-zero-divisor.
Nonzeroness of the element implies `n ≠ 0`. The principal fractional ideal is
then the product of the principal fractional ideal generated by `d⁻¹` and
the integral ideal `(n)`. Mathlib's `FractionalIdeal.count_well_defined` gives
its multiplicity as the multiplicity of `(n)` minus that of `(d)`. The lemmas
`valuation_of_mk'` and `intValuation_if_neg` express the valuation as the
quotient of the two exponentials of negative multiplicities.
`WithZero.exp_sub` and integer arithmetic identify the two expressions.

For the final theorem, `ideleCoordinate_unitEmbedding` identifies the
principal idele's completion coordinate with `algebraMap E (w.adicCompletion E) a`.
The identity `algebraMap_adicCompletion`, specialized to the field itself,
converts this to the completion coercion used by
`valuedAdicCompletion_eq_valuation'`. Thus the completion valuation equals the
field valuation in the helper. Comparing this equality with `valued_units`
and applying `WithZero.exp_injective` shows that the local exponent is the
negative principal-ideal multiplicity. By definition, `ideleOrder` is the
negative of that exponent, giving the claimed equality.

The statement's arithmetic provenance is the prime-power idele map and
diagonal embedding in J. S. Milne, *Class Field Theory*, v4.03 (2020),
Chapter V §§4.1–4.2, printed page 170 (PDF page 179). The proof here follows
Mathlib's fraction-field and fractional-ideal definitions directly. The
registered source is `milne-2020-class-field-theory`, SHA256
`50d79af78250a9f1117ad9d337e0b231704a533fc707966ed1bfa52e13d498f5`.

This theorem establishes principal-element compatibility only. The map to
ideal classes, finiteness of the relevant quotient, and the idele-class
Herbrand quotient are further obligations.
