## Parabolics, double cosets and presentations

**B9.** For a connected reductive group over an arbitrary field and two supplied
rational parabolics, the theorem applies
`parabolicIntersectionSplitTorusStatement_of_centralizer` to
`parabolicIntersectionCentralizer`. Its conclusion supplies an actual maximal
split torus with group-scheme factor maps through both supplied parabolics.
This is the documented weakening of the centralizer form of the intersection
theorem, not an additional proof of Borel–Tits 4.18.

**D7.** Over an arbitrary field, for a connected reductive group, a supplied
maximal split torus, a **generic** cocharacter and a subset `J` of the resulting
simple relative roots, the target is an equality of subsets of `G(k)`.
Here `B` is the point subgroup of the empty standard parabolic, and `W_J` is
generated by the actual relative reflections for `J`. The proof combines
`standardParabolicEndpoints`, `standardParabolicPointsStatement_proof` and
`standardRelativeWeylGeneration` through
`titsStandardParabolicStatement_of_statements`. All three inputs of this
wrapper are discharged by published theorems. Its implemented route uses the
upstream relative Tits structure; it differs from the Levi Bruhat route in
Borel–Tits 5.17. This coverage check is not a new audit of the independence of
all upstream dependencies.

**D8.** The field is arbitrary and `G` is connected reductive. The statement
quantifies over supplied actual parabolics `p : P → G`, `q : Q → G` and rational
Levi maps `l : M → P`, `lQ : L → Q`. The literal double quotient by the
point-image subgroups is finite. Each class admits a representative `z` and a
maximal split torus `i` factoring through both `l ≫ p` and
`lQ ≫ q ≫ innerIso(z)`. Existence of rational Levis for every parabolic is
not an additional conclusion of this target.

The implemented assembly uses `rationalBruhat`, `genericMinimalParabolic`,
`minimalParabolicConjugacy`, `parabolicIntersectionCentralizer`,
`parabolicDynamic` and `smoothMaximalSplitTorusConjugacy`. A finite relative
Weyl covering gives finiteness. The proof then moves the common torus into the
chosen Levis, with representative `z = U g V⁻¹` and torus `U S U⁻¹`.
The proof Markdown records this convention; the statement Markdown describes
an equivalent route with inverse conventions. The conclusion is not a
structure theorem for the whole subgroup intersection.

**D11.** The two exact proofs are projections
`parabolicRootPresentation.2.1` and `.2.2`. Independence fixes the actual
parabolic, Levi and split-component map while allowing all supplied
root-presentation choices to vary. Transport fixes a compatible isomorphism
`e : A ≅ A'` with `e.hom ≫ a' = a` and supplies the six root/cone image
equalities, including pullback in both directions. Both conclusions concern
supplied compatible presentations; existence is the separate published
`parabolicRootPresentationExistence` theorem. These are cones of positive
or nonnegative combinations of restricted roots in the full real character
space, including central directions, rather than coroot chambers. Empty root
sets give the cone `{0}`.

## Rank and central directions

**C5 and E4.** The rank proof pairs `relativeSimpleRootBasis` with
`relativeRankIdentity`; the central-direction proof pairs
`relativeCharacterRootComplement` with `relativeSplitCentralDirections`.
Their field scope is arbitrary: `G` is connected reductive and the
torus is maximal split. The simple-root basis clause additionally takes a
generic cocharacter and gives a basis of the **root span**. Finite-dimensionality
is explicit. The split rank of `G`, valued in `ℕ∞`, equals the torus rank
after casting, and the torus rank is the sum of relative-root rank and rational
character rank.

The central-direction statements give rational and real direct complements of
the root span by restricted rational `G`-characters. Every actual split
component `A` has an actual closed map into the maximal split torus; its
induced real cocharacter map is injective with image the central annihilator.
The rationalized and real character-restriction maps from the torus to `A`
have kernels the corresponding root spans. Restriction from rational
`G`-characters to characters of `A` is bijective after tensoring with `ℚ`,
and the central ranks agree; integral restriction is not asserted to be bijective.
The relative quotient `N(k)/Z(k)` fixes the central directions pointwise.
There is no local-field, quasi-split or nonempty-root assumption. The paired
Markdown explains the arbitrary-field derivation from Borel–Tits and tensor
linear algebra, beyond Renard's local-field presentation.

## Root subgroups and ordered products

**G1.** `RelativeRoot.rootSubgroupStructure` is the tuple of six published
proofs: `characterFpqc`, `kernelCentralizerStructure`,
`quotientActionComparison`, `rootSubgroupRealization`,
`rootSubgroupUniqueness` and `rootSubgroupVector`, all in the
`RelativeRoot` namespace. Their hypotheses remain distinct. Characters need
not be primitive, so their kernels may be nonsmooth. The centralizer clause
has smooth affine scope without a reductivity hypothesis or a connectedness
conclusion. The comparison clause works over an arbitrary base with a
supplied representative and an fpqc character.

The final root-subgroup clauses concern a connected reductive field group
and a maximal split torus, using all positive integral weights on the root
ray. Uniqueness retains smoothness, connectedness and torus stability. When
`2a` is absent, `U_a` has the stated vector-group structure. When `2a` is
present, `U_{2a}` is central in `U_a` and the represented **fppf quotient**
is a vector group; surjectivity of the quotient map on points over each
original test algebra is not asserted.

**G2.** `RelativeRoot.rootProduct` is the tuple of
`semigroupOrderedProduct`, `relativeRootRayDecomposition` and
`dynamicRootOrderedProduct`. The first clause concerns a smooth connected
affine solvable group with a split torus action. The partition covers all
actual weights, including zero, and its separation condition is
`Ψᵢ ∩ AddSubsemigroup.closure Ψⱼ = ∅` for `i ≠ j`; it does not require the
two semigroup closures themselves to be disjoint. The ordered multiplication
map is a scheme isomorphism, without asserting it is a group homomorphism.

The reductive specialization uses indivisible actual relative roots, which
need not be primitive lattice characters. Its dynamic clause allows every
cocharacter, including zero and nongeneric cocharacters. `U_a` already includes
the `2a` contribution, so there is no extra `U_{2a}` factor in that product.
The conclusions retain actual representatives, split unipotence and equality
on all test algebras.
