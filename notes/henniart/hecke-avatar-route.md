# Uniform algebraic Hecke avatars and the finite Artin input

The Library states the rank-one construction in Serre, *Abelian l-Adic Representations and Elliptic Curves*, 1989 reprint, Chapter II §§2.3, 2.5 and 2.7 (pp. II-10–II-12, II-18–II-20 and II-23–II-24, `serre-abelian-l-adic-1989`). The uniform target uses the actual absolute Galois group, one coefficient field \(L\) with a fixed complex embedding, and continuous characters into \(L_\lambda^\times\) for every finite place \(\lambda\) of \(L\). It gives one finite conductor support and common values in \(L^\times\), compared with every geometric Frobenius lift outside that support and the coefficient residue characteristic. The paired theorem `IsUniformHeckeAvatar.isLambdaAdicRealization` gives the Library's one-coefficient-place comparison. The integral infinity type may have nonzero weight, as in Clozel 1991 §5.3, pp. 143–144 (`clozel-1991-selfdual-galois`). [Uniform statement and assembly](../../Langlands/Galois/AlgebraicHeckeAvatarStatement.md)

## From the finite part to the coefficient reductions

For an algebraic Hecke character \(\chi\), choose one \(L\) containing its finite idele values and the images of every complex embedding of the CM field \(K\). Open subgroups form a neighborhood basis at one in the finite ideles. The complex unit group has a neighborhood of one containing no nontrivial subgroup, so the finite part has open kernel and its image on the compact product of integral local units is finite. Use that finite image, the infinity-type formula on principal ideles, and finitely many representatives of `IntegralIdeleClassQuotient` to construct \(L\). The lift \(\varphi\) to \(L^\times\) has the same open kernel. The proved `HeckeFunctionalEquation.exists_finset_isUnramifiedAt` bounds the conductor support, which can then be taken to be the exact ramified set. [Common finite part](../../Langlands/Galois/HeckeAvatarFinitePart.md)

At \(\lambda\), multiply the embedded value of \(\varphi\) by the product of the local coordinates raised to the integral infinity exponents. For each lifted embedding, take the coordinate at the place of \(K\) below \(\lambda\) for that embedding and use `FiniteExtension.completionMap`. On principal ideles the finite part is the inverse of the infinity character. The diagonal identity for the completion maps gives cancellation and continuous descent to idele classes. At a finite place of different residue characteristic from \(\lambda\), the correction monomial is one on the embedded local multiplicative group. [Idele correction](../../Langlands/Galois/HeckeAvatarCorrection.md)

The valuation of the descended character kills the archimedean idele factor and the compact product of integral local units. The latter has finite image in the discrete valuation group \(\mathbb Z\). By `integralIdeleClassQuotientFinite`, the remaining valuation image is finite as well, so the corrected character takes values in the actual completed integer units. Its coefficient reductions are the discrete finite unit groups of the integer-ring quotients by \(\mathfrak m_\lambda^{n+1}\). At each fixed \(\lambda\), every reduction kills local units outside the union of the conductor support with the places over the residue characteristic of \(\lambda\). [Finite integral idele-class quotient](../../Langlands/ClassFieldTheory/Global/IntegralIdeleClassQuotientFinite.md), [coefficient reductions](../../Langlands/Galois/HeckeAvatarInverseLimit.md)

## Prescribed-kernel reciprocity and the geometric sign

Let \(\xi:C_K\to A\) be a corrected coefficient reduction into a finite discrete abelian group. Its kernel \(H\) is open and has finite index because \(C_K/H\) embeds in \(A\). Clause 3 of interface F, `FiniteAbelianClassFieldExistenceStatement`, produces an actual finite abelian subfield \(E\) of the algebraic closure and a continuous surjection \(\rho:C_K\to\operatorname{Gal}(E/K)\) with kernel \(H\) and arithmetic Artin normalization. Factor \(\xi=\theta\circ\rho\). The Galois character is \(g\mapsto\theta((g|_E)^{-1})\). Inversion converts the arithmetic normalization into the geometric comparison. The finite class-field inputs come from Milne, *Class Field Theory*, v4.03, Chapter I §1 and Chapter V §5 (Theorem 1.1, Lemma 5.1, Theorems 5.3 and 5.5, Summary 5.10, `milne-2020-class-field-theory`). [Interface F](../../Langlands/Global/Arithmetic/ClassFieldStatement.md), [finite lift](../../Langlands/Galois/FiniteHeckeAvatarLift.md)

The normalization in clause 3 applies at primes already known to be unramified. At a good place, equality of kernels gives \(\rho=1\) on the embedded local units. `FiniteArtinUnramifiedCriterionStatement` then gives unramifiedness at every prime above that place. `FiniteUnramifiedGaloisRestrictionStatement` uses the map from the actual local completion to the absolute Galois group of \(K\). It gives trivial restriction on inertia and arithmetic Frobenius for the inverse of every geometric lift. Thus the lifted character kills inertia and takes the value \(\xi(\pi)\) on every geometric lift for any local uniformizer \(\pi\). [Local-unit criterion](../../Langlands/Global/Arithmetic/FiniteArtinUnramifiedCriterion.md), [actual finite Galois restriction](../../Langlands/Galois/FiniteUnramifiedGaloisRestriction.md)

Clause 3 is the only clause of F assumed by the finite lift. The quotient's embedding into \(A\) supplies the finite-index fact from clause 2 for these kernels. Chebotarev supplies uniqueness of the Galois characters, so field rigidity from clause 4 is unnecessary. Clause 5 addresses finite-order complex characters, while the coefficient reductions have arbitrary finite abelian targets. For clause 1, the proof route for the local-unit criterion uses uniqueness of a normalized map to identify the map supplied by clause 3 with a canonical map on the same field. This is a step inside the proof of the criterion. The aggregate F statement and the full Artin-norm comparison stay outside the dependency chain. The proved relative Weil transfer gives descent for a given finite extension. Prescribed-kernel field existence and the normalized local comparison are the additional inputs. [Interface F audit](../reuse/nyll-global-cft-audit.md)

For uniqueness, take the finite abelian fixed field of the intersection of the open normal kernels of two candidate lifts. The proved `chebotarevPrimeRealizationStatement` realizes each element at an unramified prime outside any finite exceptional set. Realize the inverse of a desired element to evaluate it on a geometric lift by actual finite Galois restriction. The two candidates have the same value there, so they agree on the quotient. A higher-level lift reduced to a lower level satisfies the same comparison, and uniqueness gives coherence under `completionUnitTransition`. Adic completeness and the units topology give the continuous limit. Joint injectivity of the reductions then transfers inertia triviality and the Frobenius equality to the limit. This proves coherence at the level of coefficient characters using their common good places. [Chebotarev prime realization](../../Langlands/Global/Arithmetic/ChebotarevPrimeRealizationProof.md), [congruence limit](../../Langlands/Galois/HeckeAvatarInverseLimit.md)

## Consumers

The rank-one endpoint of the route to Harris's cuspidal existence theorem is `AlgebraicUnitaryHeckeCharacterRealizationStatement`; this note records no proof of it. The additional assertion is weight-zero purity for all complex embeddings of the common coefficient field. Unitarity makes the exponents in each CM pair opposite. Principal values then have absolute value one at every conjugate, and integral-unit values are roots of unity. Powers of finite idele representatives in the finite integral idele-class quotient extend the same calculation to the common values at uniformizers. [Harris rank-one statement](../../Langlands/Galois/HarrisRankOneStatement.md), [cuspidal proof route](../harris/theorem-12-cuspidal-route.md)

The CM central twist and coefficient-field comparison belong to Clozel's geometric construction. The uniform avatar applies to the integral infinity type of that scalar correction and gives the geometric Frobenius value at each common good place. The coefficient extension shared with the cohomology family and the resulting twist of its Frobenius polynomial belong to the geometric comparison.
