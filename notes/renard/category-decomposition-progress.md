# Renard A.IX: category decompositions and simple support

This note maps the whole source page of A.IX to the published constructions and seven proof units, with their actual size conditions. It records verified publications, not a declaration that A.IX or the whole-book program is complete.

The governing source is David Renard, *Représentations des groupes réductifs p-adiques*, French version dated 9 July 2026, A.IX, printed page 268 / PDF page 274, registered as `renard-2026-representations-groupes-reductifs-padiques`, SHA256 `7b539996793b7c3620e72e1e389588eb42d1a8e91d6f68b2eead5c2e2ffc182c`. The complete page was read, including the displayed proof. The conventions and support identities are in A.VI.2–3, printed pages 263–264 / PDF pages 269–270. The supplementary 2010-edition corrections, registered as `renard-2010-corrections-complements`, were checked at PDF pages 18–19: the old-page-310 letter correction is already incorporated in the 2026 supported-sum passage. This is a bounded comparison with the older errata, not a claim that the project audit is author-issued errata. See the separate [Appendix A discrepancy audit](appendix-a-corrections.md).

## Conventions and source map

All declaration names below are in `Langlands.Renard`. Unless a row says otherwise, the ambient category is `C : Type u` with `[Category.{v} C] [Abelian C]`. `ObjectClass C` is the actual isomorphism-class quotient; `simpleClasses C` represents the source's `Irr(C)`, and `simpleSubquotientClasses X` represents all simple subquotients, without a finite-length restriction.

The source assumes its Grothendieck convention, AB4* and AB5, together with AB6. In Lean, `IsSourceGrothendieck.{w} C` supplies `w`-indexed coproducts and directed-subobject-sum distributivity; it has no generator clause. `SourceAB6 C` is the canonical local finite-type condition, using all finite-type subobjects. These conventions are defined in [RenardGrothendieckAxioms](../../Langlands/CategoryTheory/RenardGrothendieckAxioms.md) and [LocallyFiniteType](../../Langlands/CategoryTheory/Renard/LocallyFiniteType.md), and must not be replaced silently by similarly named Mathlib classes.

Every row below refers to printed page 268 / PDF page 274, in source order. D1–D4 and P1–P7 refer to the publication tables below.

## Exact hypotheses and size limits

P1 is an explicitly derived generalization of the source context: it needs only an abelian ambient category and the actual `IsCategoryDecomposition P`. Its index universe `w`, Hom universe `v` and object universe `u` are independent. It assumes neither repleteness nor abelianness of the component full subcategories, global coproducts, a nonempty index, finite length, Grothendieck axioms or AB6. Internal simplicity uses the inherited zero morphisms. The proof supplies the comparison with ambient simplicity instead of assuming it.

P2–P5 retain `IsSourceGrothendieck.{w} C`, `SourceAB6 C` and the relevant subset-of-`simpleClasses` hypotheses in their exported targets, even where an internal helper proves more generally. P5 also retains disjointness and support of the actual objects or subobjects. It adds no smallness premise for those arbitrary supported objects.

P4 obtains the full supported-family coproduct from the source `w`-indexed coproducts using `Small.{w} (simpleSupportSubobjects S X)`. The construction D3 itself asks only for the coproduct of that particular family. Neither formulation permits silently assuming all coproducts indexed in `Type (max u v)`: that oversized global assumption forces every Hom type to be a subsingleton. The explanation of this issue is an internal size diagnosis, recorded in the independently published D3 companion correction; it is not a new assertion attributed to Renard.

P7 retains `IsSourceGrothendieck.{w} C`, `SourceAB6 C`, both inclusions `S ⊆ simpleClasses C` and `T ⊆ simpleClasses C`, and `Disjoint S T`. For every actual object `X`, it separately assumes `Small.{w} (simpleSupportSubobjects S X)` and `Small.{w} (simpleSupportSubobjects T X)`. Each premise supplies its own full-family coproduct from the source coproducts. The source-family universe `w`, Hom universe `v` and object universe `u` remain independent. Neither family is replaced by a finite or chosen subfamily. The conclusion is zero intersection and monicity, with no epimorphism, isomorphism or exhaustiveness claim. P7 proves this literal full-sum conclusion under both size premises; applications must discharge them or supply a separately verified size bridge.

P3 has independent universes `w`, `t`, `v`, `u`. Its general colimit component takes `J : Type w`, `[Category.{t} J]`, a diagram, an actual cocone and its `IsColimit` proof. No arrow-indexed coproduct or relation between `t` and `w` is added. Empty coproducts and empty diagrams are included.

P6 has independent source-family universe `r`, partition universe `w`, Hom universe `v` and object universe `u`. It assumes `Small.{r} I` and, for each partition block, `Small.{r} (simpleSupportSubobjects (S i) X)` and `Small.{r} (simpleSupportSubobjects (S i) Y)`. There is no corresponding smallness assumption on the presentation object `N`. The hypothesis that the partition splits `X` includes the actual partition and canonical-map data; the conclusion supplies the corresponding splitting at `Y`. The theorem adds no finiteness, generator, global enlarged coproduct or nonempty-partition premise.

## Implemented proof routes

P1 makes each leg of an actual coproduct cofan a split monomorphism by descending identity on that leg and zero on the others. Decomposing arbitrary ambient test objects and using off-diagonal vanishing makes internal monomorphisms ambiently monic. This establishes the simplicity comparison. A simple ambient object has a nonzero leg in its decomposition; simplicity makes that leg invertible and supplies a component representative. An isomorphism between simple representatives in distinct components would be zero, contradicting their nonzero identities, so the index is unique.

P2 uses the published simple-subquotient transport and subobject–quotient identity to prove the support predicate is a Serre class. Finite biproducts give finite-product closure. The inherited full-subcategory construction then gives its abelian structure, and the actual inclusion preserves finite limits and finite colimits. P3 proves coproduct support from the images of its injections and the published arbitrary-sum identity. The canonical map from the coproduct of diagram objects to any actual colimit vertex is epi; support transports across that epimorphism.

P4 identifies the actual full-family sum with a `Shrink`-indexed sum using both upper-bound laws, and applies the published arbitrary-sum support identity at the source family size. Genuine support plus the intrinsic upper-bound law yields the greatest-element assertion. P5 gives the image of a morphism both supports by epi and mono transport. Disjointness and the published empty-support zero-detection theorem make the image zero. The intersection and binary-map conclusions use actual subobject inclusions and cancellation.

P6 constructs the required endpoint coproducts and proves the canonical map at the subquotient both mono and epi. Its kernel argument uses disjoint-support zero detection; its cokernel argument uses pullbacks, supported images and the complement-summand cokernel calculation. The abelian mono-and-epi criterion gives invertibility. This is an internal alternate route to the source lemma, not an implementation of the printed intersection-sum calculation.

P7 applies `simpleSupportSumSupport` separately to the full `S` and `T` sums, using the corresponding smallness premise in each application. It then applies `inf_eq_bot_of_hasSimpleSupport_of_disjoint` to the resulting support proofs and `Disjoint S T`. The same intersection equality supplies `mono_biprod_desc_arrow_of_inf_eq_bot`. This is the exported literal-full-sum specialization of the published P4 and P5 results.
