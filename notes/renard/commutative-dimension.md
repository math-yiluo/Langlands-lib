## Source and conventions

The governing passage is the unnumbered lemma and its proof in David Renard,
*Représentations des groupes réductifs p-adiques*, French version dated
9 July 2026, Appendix C.I, printed pp.275–276 / one-based PDF281–282.
The registered original is
`renard-2026-representations-groupes-reductifs-padiques`, SHA256
`7b539996793b7c3620e72e1e389588eb42d1a8e91d6f68b2eead5c2e2ffc182c`,
retained at
`/home/taskhub/central-library/Renard--Representations des groupes reductifs p-adiques (2026 version)--2026.pdf`.
Both pages were read and their formulas visually checked.

For a complex vector space of finite dimension `k`, the source considers a
commutative subalgebra of `End(V)` generated by `l` elements and the identity.
It proves

```text
dim R ≤ f_l(k) = k^(2 - 2^(1-l)).
```

The formal statements use actual complex endomorphisms, actual unital
subalgebras and complex dimensions. Scalars and the identity belong to
`Algebra.adjoin ℂ (Set.range a)` without using an additional generator.
This lemma's unital convention is independent of the book's nonunital module
conventions elsewhere.

The supplementary 2010-edition corrections,
`renard-2010-corrections-complements`, SHA256
`fdf3c88716c294c451c1f6fad67f6b99c95dae670aae0b8ad191ab0a96e69863`,
were read on PDF18–19. Their C.I entries concern inequalities in the older
proof on printed pp.317–318. The statement and proof correspondence here is
checked against the governing 2026 pages. In particular, the latter use the
exponent comparison `2 - 2^(1-l) ≥ 1` for positive `l` and a strict
counterexample assumption. This is a reading record, not a claim that every
source intermediate has been formalized.

## Exact declarations and scope

All names in the following table are in namespace `Langlands.Renard`.
The statement pair is
[`Langlands/Algebra/RenardCommutativeEndomorphismDimensionStatement.lean`](../../Langlands/Algebra/RenardCommutativeEndomorphismDimensionStatement.lean)
and its same-stem Markdown.

| Statement | Published proof | Conclusion |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `CommutativeEndomorphismDimensionStatement.{uV}` | `commutativeEndomorphismDimension.{uV}` | The bound for `Algebra.adjoin ℂ (Set.range a)` |
| `CommutativeSubalgebraDimensionStatement.{uV}` | `commutativeSubalgebraDimension.{uV}` | The bound for an actual `R`, assuming `R = Algebra.adjoin ℂ (Set.range a)` |

Both quantify over `V : Type uV` with `AddCommGroup V`, `Module ℂ V` and
`FiniteDimensional ℂ V`, every `l : ℕ`, and
`a : Fin l → Module.End ℂ V`. The hypothesis is
`∀ i j, Commute (a i) (a j)`. The first conclusion is exactly

```text
(Module.finrank ℂ (Algebra.adjoin ℂ (Set.range a)) : ℝ) ≤
  Real.rpow (Module.finrank ℂ V : ℝ)
    (2 - Real.rpow 2 (1 - (l : ℝ))).
```

The second substitutes `Module.finrank ℂ R` on the left and adds the displayed
generation equality. Both powers and both subtractions are real operations;
there is no truncated natural subtraction in `1 - (l : ℝ)`.
Pairwise commutation of the generators makes their actual adjoin commutative.
Conversely, generators included from a commutative source subalgebra commute.
No distinctness, minimality, nilpotence, basis or diagonalizability premise is
added.

The arbitrary carrier universe, the empty generating family and the zero
vector space are retained. When `l = 0`, the adjoin is the scalar algebra and
the exponent is zero, so the right side is one. Its dimension is one for a
nonzero space and zero for the zero space. For the zero space and positive
`l`, the bound is `0 ≤ 0`; at the joint corner it is `0 ≤ 1`, using
`Real.rpow 0 0 = 1`.

## Implemented proof and reuse

The first proof is in
[`Langlands/Algebra/RenardCommutativeEndomorphismDimensionProof.lean`](../../Langlands/Algebra/RenardCommutativeEndomorphismDimensionProof.lean).
Its lemma `renardExponent_eq_dimensionExponent` proves, for every `l`,

```text
2 - Real.rpow 2 (1 - (l : ℝ))
  = Langlands.CommutingDimension.dimensionExponent l
  = 2 - 2 / (2 : ℝ)^l.
```

It uses `Real.rpow_sub` at the positive base `2`, followed by the identities
for exponent one and natural exponents. The main theorem then specializes
`Langlands.CommutingDimension.commuting_dimension` from
`Langlands.Algebra.CommutingDimensionPrimary` to `ℂ` and rewrites this
exponent. Mathlib supplies algebraic closure of `ℂ`; no extra hypothesis is
introduced. The separately published natural-power target
`Langlands.CommutingEndomorphismDimensionStatement` is not used.

The arbitrary-subalgebra proof is in
[`Langlands/Algebra/RenardCommutativeSubalgebraDimensionProof.lean`](../../Langlands/Algebra/RenardCommutativeSubalgebraDimensionProof.lean).
It substitutes the generation equality and applies the first theorem.
Each proof has the exact named published Prop-valued declaration as its type.

The reused general-field theorem follows the route documented from
Bernstein–Zelevinsky (1976), Lemma 4.10 and §4.12, printed/PDF pp.39–40.
The retained original's SHA256 is
`72dc22eb9991112936a88327f00d0f02a16a2bf8747518ca0ecfeeadd188e50c`;
those pages were read. The Lean route uses simultaneous generalized
eigenspaces, restriction to the resulting invariant subspaces, an injective
algebra map and superadditivity of the real dimension power. Its nilpotent
estimate uses induction on the generator count and strong induction on
dimension, and closes with `real_dimension_step`. It does not define Renard's
maximum-dimension function or prove his floor recurrence.

## Actual nilpotent maximum, transport and base cases

The published
[`RenardNilpotentDimensionMaximum`](../../Langlands/Algebra/RenardNilpotentDimensionMaximum.lean)
defines `nilpotentGeneratedDimensions l V` as the dimensions of actual unital
adjoins of commuting nilpotent `Fin l` tuples on `V`. It defines
`nilpotentGeneratedMaxDimension l V` as the natural supremum of that set and
`nilpotentGeneratedPhi l k` by specialization to `Fin k → ℂ`. The zero tuple
shows nonemptiness, and inclusion in `End(V)` bounds the set by `(dim V)^2`.
`nilpotentGeneratedMaxDimension_isGreatest` and
`nilpotentGeneratedMaxDimension_attained` prove that the supremum is a greatest
element with an actual attaining tuple. This discharges the maximum
construction and witness obligation.

The theorem
[`nilpotentDimensionTransport`](../../Langlands/Algebra/RenardNilpotentDimensionTransportProof.lean)
proves `NilpotentDimensionTransportStatement.{u,v}`. A complex linear
equivalence between carriers in independent universes preserves the entire
dimension set and its maximum; on every finite-dimensional carrier the maximum
equals `nilpotentGeneratedPhi l (Module.finrank ℂ V)`.
The implemented proof conjugates the original endomorphisms, preserves their
commutation and nilpotence, and uses the published injective-adjoin dimension
law. It obtains both set inclusions using the equivalence and its inverse.
Transport is an internal justification of the dimension-only notation.

The theorem
[`nilpotentDimensionBaseCases`](../../Langlands/Algebra/RenardNilpotentDimensionBaseCasesProof.lean)
proves `NilpotentDimensionBaseCasesStatement.{u}`. For the actual maximum on
every carrier it proves dimension zero gives zero, dimension one gives one,
the one-generator upper bound is `dim V`, and the empty-tuple maximum is zero
on the zero space and one otherwise. The corresponding four formulas for
`nilpotentGeneratedPhi` hold for every natural index. The source explicitly
uses the one-generator bound and `φ_l(1) = 1`; the zero-space, empty-tuple and
arbitrary-carrier clauses are derived extensions.
Worker 16's proof uses attainment, the ambient bound and the published
`Langlands.CommutingDimension.singleton_dimension` theorem. It evaluates the
empty adjoin as the scalar algebra and obtains the canonical formulas by
direct specialization. Nontriviality is derived locally when required.

These results include all natural generator counts, zero spaces, empty
tuples, and zero, repeated or redundant entries. The identity and scalars
are included without increasing the generator count.

## Actual filtration and published intermediate statements

The published
[`RenardNilpotentIdealFiltration`](../../Langlands/Algebra/RenardNilpotentIdealFiltration.lean)
constructs, in namespace `Langlands.Renard.NilpotentFiltration`,
`generatedAlgebra a`, `generatorIdeal a`, `idealPowerModule a n` and
`idealPowerSubspace a n`. They are the original unital algebra `R`, its
tuple-generated ideal `I`, and `I^n V` with its original evaluation action
and restricted complex scalars. Construction does not require finite
dimensionality, commutation or nilpotence. Its ideal is a left ideal for an
arbitrary tuple; tuple commutation makes `R` commutative and gives the
source's two-sided ideal.

The construction proves the zeroth-level, successor-action, descending-family
and stability laws, and that each generator maps `V` into `V_1`.
The source endpoint `V_(dim V) = 0` is a separate statement and proof obligation.
