## Published finite-subcoproduct characterization

At A.VIII.2, printed page 267 / PDF page 273, the finite-subsum
characterization is now proved by
`Langlands.Renard.smallProjectiveFiniteSupport` in
`Langlands.CategoryTheory.Renard.SmallProjectiveFiniteSupport`.
Its exact target is
`Langlands.Renard.SmallProjectiveFiniteSupportStatement` in the corresponding
`SmallProjectiveFiniteSupportStatements` module.

For `C : Type u`, `[Category.{v} C]`, `[Abelian C]` and
`[HasCoproducts.{w} C]`, it states, for every `P : C`,

```lean
HomSmallProjective.{w} P ↔
  HomProjective P ∧
    ∀ (I : Type w) (M : I → C) (φ : P ⟶ ∐ M),
      ∃ (s : Finset I) (g : P ⟶ ∐ (fun j : s => M j.val)),
        g ≫ Sigma.desc (fun j : s => Sigma.ι M j.val) = φ
```

The three universes are independent. The finite set may be empty, and the
factorization uses the actual canonical map into the full coproduct.
Projectivity remains on both sides. Here smallness is preservation of
`w`-indexed coproducts by the actual integer-module Hom functor.
No Grothendieck, AB5, generator or additional subobject-size hypothesis is
added.

The statement, by Workers 6 and 923, records three readings as author
qualifications. The ambient hypothesis is `HasCoproducts.{w} C` rather than
cocompleteness, and it includes the source situation. The source's "si" is
read as "if and only if", following its announcement of equivalent
characterizations. The printed `⊕_{j∈J} M_i` is read as `⊕_{j∈J} M_j`.

The implemented forward proof forms the submodule generated by the
summand images in the Hom group. Colimit uniqueness makes its quotient
zero; induction then gives finite-subcoproduct factorization. The converse
reuses the published theorem
`Langlands.Renard.preservesCoproduct_of_finiteFactor` by name. Candidate
`ea9a9a00-0ef2-493f-991d-a2cbf2203a55` also made that theorem public in
`SmallProjectiveFiniteType`, retaining its statement and proof. This route
uses no `w`-indexed coproducts in the target `ModuleCat.{v} ℤ`.
The source gives no proof of this characterization on the consulted page;
the paired proof Markdown documents this implemented route.

## Published actual Hom-equivalence statement and proof

The unnumbered theorem in A.VIII.2, printed page 267 / PDF page 273,
has a published proposition
`Langlands.Renard.SmallProgeneratorEquivalenceStatement`, in
`Langlands.CategoryTheory.Renard.SmallProgeneratorEquivalenceStatements`:

```lean
def SmallProgeneratorEquivalenceStatement (C : Type u) [Category.{v} C]
    [Abelian C] [HasCoproducts.{v} C] : Prop :=
  ∀ P : C, HomSmallProgenerator.{v} P →
    (preadditiveCoyonedaObj P : C ⥤ ModuleCat.{v} (End P)ᵐᵒᵖ).IsEquivalence
```

The hypothesis combines sequence exactness, coproduct preservation and
faithfulness of the actual integer-module Hom functor. The conclusion
concerns the specified endomorphism-module functor: its carrier at `X`
is `P ⟶ X`, its maps are postcomposition, and `op r • f = r ≫ f`.
Left modules over the opposite ring express unital right `End P`-modules.
No equivalence, fullness or essential surjectivity is assumed.

The object universe `u` stays independent of the Hom universe `v`.
Coproduct indices and target module carriers are in `Type v`.
Cocompleteness is read at this Hom size; in an abelian category,
`HasCoproducts.{v} C` gives all `v`-small colimits. The author qualifications
retain this size convention and the target-carrier restriction; there is
no claim about modules in larger universes.

The exact proposition is now proved by
`Langlands.Renard.smallProgeneratorEquivalence` in
`Langlands.CategoryTheory.Renard.SmallProgeneratorEquivalence`, from task
`fb2642d4-aac4-49ec-bfe7-725a122f9244`. Worker 709 read the complete
statement and proof pairs and verified proof candidate
`64006278-19ec-4d5b-b1e0-730ec453addc` as accepted and public on
1 November 2026. This supersedes this note's earlier staged-proof gate.

The implemented proof first uses the published projectivity and generator
comparisons to obtain projectivity and faithfulness of the module-valued
Hom functor. It transfers coproduct preservation from the integer-module
Hom functor through restriction of scalars. Writing `F` for the
module-valued Hom functor, a module map `F(∐ P) → F(Y)` is determined by its
values on the coprojections and lifts to a morphism `∐ P → Y` in `C`.
Applying this to the generator evaluation epimorphism
and descending through its kernel proves fullness. For any target module,
two module covers give a relation map between such coproducts. Its cokernel
in `C` realizes the module because the Hom functor preserves cokernels.
These three results assemble the exact `IsEquivalence` target.

The proof retains independent `u` and `v`, with all constructed coproduct
indices and target module carriers in `Type v`. It adds no Grothendieck,
AB5, commutativity or nonzero-object assumption. The source gives no proof
on the consulted page; the paired proof Markdown documents the implemented
argument without attributing it to the unread Pareigis reference.
Mathlib's `Functor.asEquivalence` then supplies an equivalence whose forward
functor is the specified Hom functor. The regular-module assertion, the
converse for categories already equivalent to module categories, the
finite-support/finite-type comparisons and the nonunital-module bridges
remain separate units; this forward proof establishes none of those.

### Finite-type comparison

The source says "Dans une catégorie de Grothendieck, un objet est un petit
projectif si et seulement s'il est projectif de type fini" and cites
Pareigis [37], 4.11, Lemma 1. The statement
`SmallProjectiveFiniteTypeStatement`, by Workers 6 and 937, in
`SmallProjectiveFiniteTypeStatements` is

```lean
def SmallProjectiveFiniteTypeStatement (C : Type u) [Category.{v} C] [Abelian C] :
    Prop :=
  ∀ hG : IsSourceGrothendieck.{w} C,
    letI : HasCoproducts.{w} C := hG.1
    ∀ (P : C) [Small.{w} (Subobject P)],
      HomSmallProjective.{w} P ↔ HomProjective P ∧ IsFiniteTypeObject P
```

`IsSourceGrothendieck.{w} C` is coproduct existence at size `w` together
with the source AB5 law, with no generator clause. The premise
`Small.{w} (Subobject P)` is a size condition, not a finiteness condition.
It lets every set of subobjects be reindexed in `Type w`. The statement
Markdown records that at `w = max u v` the coproduct hypothesis forces
every object to be zero. For large categories with small Hom types, the
intended size is `w = v`. The universes `w`, `v` and `u` are independent.

The proof `smallProjectiveFiniteType`, in `SmallProjectiveFiniteType`, is
by Worker 840. Pareigis was not read, and the proof does not rely on it.
For the converse direction, the source AB5 law and the finite-subcover form
of finite type factor every morphism into a `w`-indexed coproduct through a
finite subcoproduct. The public `preservesCoproduct_of_finiteFactor` then
builds the colimit cocone directly in `ModuleCat.{v} ℤ`. For the forward
direction, unions exist as indexed subobject sums after reindexing. For the
chain condition, take a projective section of the coproduct of a directed
chain of proper subobjects with union `⊤`. It lies in the span of the summand
images, so it factors through one member. That member is then `⊤`, which
contradicts properness.

### Regular module, transport and the converse

The remark reads "On a bien sûr la réciproque, puisque un anneau unitaire
`A` est un petit progénérateur de `M(A)`". The reason it gives is the
statement `RegularModuleSmallProgeneratorStatement`, by Workers 734 and 6,
in `RegularModuleSmallProgeneratorStatements`:

```lean
def RegularModuleSmallProgeneratorStatement (R : Type u) [Ring R]
    [HasCoproducts.{w} (ModuleCat.{max u v} R)] : Prop :=
  HomSmallProgenerator.{w} (ModuleCat.of R (ULift.{v} R))
```

`R` is any unital ring, possibly noncommutative or zero. `ModuleCat` is
Mathlib's category of unital left modules, which is the source's `M(A)`
for unital `A`. The regular module is lifted to carrier universe `max u v`.
Coproduct existence at the independent size `w` is an explicit hypothesis.

The proof `regularModuleSmallProgenerator`, by Worker 734, in
`RegularModuleSmallProgenerator` has three parts:

- Projectivity uses `homProjectiveComparison` and Mathlib's projectivity
  of the lifted free module.
- Generation is a direct separator proof by evaluation at `ULift.up 1`,
  followed by `homGeneratorComparison`.
- Evaluation at `ULift.up 1` is a natural isomorphism from the
  integer-module Hom functor to restriction of scalars along `ℤ → R`.
  Restriction of scalars is a left adjoint, so it preserves coproducts of
  every size. No relation between `w` and `max u v` is used.

The source gives no proof of the remark; this route is internal.

The remark tacitly moves the regular module across an equivalence. The
derived statement `SmallProgeneratorTransportStatement`, by Worker 709, in
`SmallProgeneratorTransportStatements` makes this step explicit:

```lean
def SmallProgeneratorTransportStatement
    (C : Type u) [Category.{v} C] [Abelian C] [HasCoproducts.{w} C]
    (D : Type u') [Category.{v'} D] [Abelian D] [HasCoproducts.{w} D] : Prop :=
  ∀ (e : C ≌ D) (P : C),
    HomSmallProgenerator.{w} P ↔ HomSmallProgenerator.{w} (e.functor.obj P)
```

The five universes are independent, and the index size `w` is the same on
both sides. The proof `smallProgeneratorTransport`, by Worker 937, in
`SmallProgeneratorTransport` handles each conjunct separately:

- Projectivity uses `homProjectiveComparison` and Mathlib's
  `Equivalence.map_projective_iff`.
- Coproduct preservation uses the published finite-subcoproduct
  factorization helpers of `SmallProjectiveFiniteSupport`, together with
  the adjunction bijection of `e` and the coproduct comparison of
  `e.inverse`.
- Generation uses the proof's cancellation characterization
  `homGenerator_iff_cancel`, so the two integer-module categories are
  never compared.

The reverse implication applies the forward one to `e.symm` and the unit
isomorphism.

The converse is `ModuleCategoryConverseStatement`, by Worker 709, in
`ModuleCategoryConverseStatements`:

```lean
def ModuleCategoryConverseStatement
    (C : Type u) [Category.{v} C] [Abelian C] [HasCoproducts.{w} C]
    (R : Type r) [Ring R] : Prop :=
  ∀ e : C ≌ ModuleCat.{max r s} R,
    HomSmallProgenerator.{w} (e.inverse.obj (ModuleCat.of R (ULift.{s} R)))
```

The source asserts that a small progenerator exists. The explicit
inverse-image witness and the five independent universes are a derived
realization of that assertion. The only coproduct hypothesis is on `C`.
The source's forward theorem uses right modules over `End P`. Taking `R` to
be an opposite ring gives that case here.

The proof `moduleCategoryConverse`, by Worker 937, in
`ModuleCategoryConverse` has three steps:

1. `hasCoproducts_moduleCat_of_equivalence` transfers coproducts at size `w`
   to the module category along `e.inverse`.
2. `regularModuleSmallProgenerator.{w, s, r}` applies at that size.
3. `smallProgeneratorTransport` for `e.symm` carries the result back to `C`.

### Presentations by a generator

The final lemma assumes a small progenerator `P` and says that "tout objet
`M` de `A` est quotient de deux objets isomorphes à des sommes directes
d'objets isomorphes à `P`". Its proof ends with an exact row
`⊕_J P → ⊕_I P → M → 0`. The statement `GeneratorPresentationStatement`,
by Workers 6 and 945, in `GeneratorPresentationStatements` is

```lean
def GeneratorPresentationStatement [HasCoproducts.{v} C] (P : C) : Prop :=
  HomGenerator P →
    ∀ M : C, ∃ (I J : Type v)
      (f : (∐ fun _ : J => P) ⟶ (∐ fun _ : I => P))
      (g : (∐ fun _ : I => P) ⟶ M)
      (h : f ≫ g = 0),
      (ShortComplex.mk f g h).Exact ∧ Epi g
```

Here `C : Type u` has `[Category.{v} C]` and `[Abelian C]`, and `u` and `v`
are independent. This is an internal strengthening of the lemma. It assumes
only the generator property and coproducts indexed in the Hom universe; a
small progenerator supplies the generator premise.

The proof `generatorPresentation`, by Worker 691, in `GeneratorPresentation`
first shows that `P` is a separator in Mathlib's sense. The evaluation map
`Sigma.desc fun a : P ⟶ M => a` is then an epimorphism. The first map is
the evaluation epimorphism onto the actual kernel, composed with the
kernel inclusion. `ShortComplex.exact_iff_epi_kernel_lift` proves
exactness. Indexing by morphisms `P ⟶ M` replaces the source's indexing
by elements of `M`. Projectivity and smallness are not used.

The right sides, respectively, are well-founded strict reverse inclusion

under the original action. The selected unitized action on the same

included; no original unit law, nondegeneracy, nontriviality or finiteness

## Published integer-unitization equivalence

The actual equivalence

```lean
NonUnitalModuleCat.{u, v} A ≌ ModuleCat.{v} (Unitization ℤ A)
```

is now public as
`Langlands.Algebra.IntegerUnitization.equivalence`, in
[`IntegerUnitizationCategory`](../../Langlands/Algebra/IntegerUnitizationCategory.md),
from task `49843f5a-4f6f-47e9-ac82-681aa223c63f`.
It applies to every `A : Type u` with `[NonUnitalRing A]`, independently
of the carrier universe `v`.

Its forward functor extends the stored action by
`(n, a) • m = n • m + a • m`, using the canonical integer action of the
additive group. Its inverse restricts along `Unitization.inr`.
Both functors keep the carrier, additive group and underlying additive maps.
The unit and counit use identity maps on carriers; their naturality and
the equivalence coherence are proved for these actual functors.
The selected module structures are local.

Zero original actions are included, even over a unital ring. The published
example with the zero action of `ℤ` on `ℤ` retains the zero action after
extension and restriction: the old unit `Unitization.inr (1 : ℤ)` acts
by zero, while the new unit of `Unitization ℤ ℤ` acts as the identity.
Thus this equivalence covers the whole additive-action category in the
source convention. The compatible complex-linear construction requires
separate data and comparison.

## Published categorical subobject correspondence

`Langlands.Algebra.NonUnitalModuleCat.subobjectOrderIso` in
[`NonUnitalModuleSubobject`](../../Langlands/Algebra/NonUnitalModuleSubobject.md)
constructs, for every `M : NonUnitalModuleCat.{u, v} A`,

```lean
CategoryTheory.Subobject M ≃o NonUnitalSubmodule A M
```

The left side is the actual categorical subobject type, with its order by
factorization of monomorphisms. The right side uses exactly the original
action stored in `M`, ordered by inclusion of underlying subsets.
The construction transports monomorphisms along the integer-unitization
equivalence, descends to subobjects, applies Mathlib's
`ModuleCat.subobjectModule`, then uses the inverse of
`NonUnitalSubmodule.intUnitizedOrderIso`.

The same unit proves that a morphism is a monomorphism iff its underlying
additive map is injective. The order isomorphism sends the class of a
monomorphism to its range; its inverse sends a stable additive subgroup
to the class of its actual subtype inclusion. The representative and
round-trip laws identify these maps, and factorization order corresponds
exactly to range inclusion. Ring and carrier universes remain independent.
Zero actions and proper nonzero subobjects of zero-action modules are
included.

## Published object-finiteness comparisons

The statement
`Langlands.Algebra.NonUnitalModuleFiniteness.NonUnitalModuleFinitenessStatement`
in
[`NonUnitalModuleFinitenessStatements`](../../Langlands/Algebra/NonUnitalModuleFinitenessStatements.md)
and its exact proof
`Langlands.Algebra.NonUnitalModuleFiniteness.nonUnitalModuleFiniteness` in
[`NonUnitalModuleFinitenessProof`](../../Langlands/Algebra/NonUnitalModuleFinitenessProof.md)
are both public. The aggregate contains the following five equivalences,
in this order. Every component internally binds arbitrary `A : Type uA`,
`[NonUnitalRing A]` and `M : NonUnitalModuleCat.{uA, uM} A`.

| Component | Left side | Right side |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `FiniteTypeStatement` | `Langlands.Renard.IsFiniteTypeObject M` | `Module.Finite (Unitization ℤ A) M` for the selected action |
| `FiniteSubcoverStatement` | `Langlands.Renard.HasFiniteSubcoverProperty M` | The same `Module.Finite` predicate |
| `NoetherianStatement` | `Langlands.Renard.IsNoetherianObject M` | `IsNoetherian (Unitization ℤ A) M` for the selected action |
| `FiniteGenerationStatement` | `Langlands.Renard.IsFiniteTypeObject M` | One `Finset M` generates the whole carrier among the original stable additive subgroups |
| `NoetherianGenerationStatement` | `Langlands.Renard.IsNoetherianObject M` | Every original stable additive subgroup has a finite set of stable generators |

The first three right sides locally install exactly

```lean
CompatibleUnitization.module
  (NonUnitalModule.toIntLinearEndHom (A := A) (V := M))
```

on the same carrier, as in `IntegerUnitization.extendObj A M`.
The last two right sides use the original action stored in `M`.
The whole-carrier generation condition is exactly

```lean
∃ S : Finset M, ∀ P : NonUnitalSubmodule A M,
  (↑S : Set M) ⊆ P → ∀ m : M, m ∈ P
```

The all-subgroups condition is exactly

```lean
∀ P : NonUnitalSubmodule A M,
  ∃ S : Finset M, (↑S : Set M) ⊆ P ∧
    ∀ Q : NonUnitalSubmodule A M, (↑S : Set M) ⊆ Q → P ≤ Q
```

The finite set may be empty and, in the last condition, depends on `P`.
Generation includes addition, negation and zero as well as the original
action. The containment of the generators in `P` is part of the conclusion.

The left sides retain the source-facing predicates from
[`RenardFiniteType`](../../Langlands/CategoryTheory/RenardFiniteType.md)
on the actual subobject type. Finite type includes existence of all
subobject unions and requires that a nonempty chain of proper subobjects
have a proper union. The finite-subcover predicate also includes union
existence; a cover of top must have a finite subcover, possibly empty.
The Noetherian predicate requires a maximal member of every nonempty
subset of subobjects. Its definition has no union-existence premise.
No small representative type replaces these subobject quantifiers.

The implemented proof forms `subobjectExtendOrderIso M` by composing the
categorical subobject correspondence with `intUnitizedOrderIso` and the
inverse of Mathlib's unitized-module subobject correspondence. Unions
transfer by taking images of sets under this order isomorphism and pulling
back their least upper bounds. Finite subcovers transfer by taking the
finite image under the inverse; maximal members transfer by order
preservation and injectivity. The published unital-module comparisons then
give components 2 and 3. The published
`Langlands.Renard.finiteTypeFiniteSubcover` gives component 1 from
component 2 in the original category. Components 4 and 5 compose
components 1 and 3 with the published original-action generation
comparisons in `IntegerOriginalSubmoduleGenerationProof`.

The proof assembles these five closed propositions in their exact order,
with independent universe parameters `uA, uM`. It adds no unit law,
nondegeneracy, commutativity, field, nontriviality, finiteness, Noetherian
or universe-relation hypothesis. Zero rings, zero carriers and zero
original actions are included. These are internal derived comparisons
for arbitrary additive actions; the complex scalar and nonunital-algebra
conventions remain separate.
