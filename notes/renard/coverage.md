It records inspected passages and preserves every other section as an explicit
audit backlog. It does **not** claim complete assertion-level inventory or
completed formalization. The detailed first-pass rows are in
[the initial passage audit](initial-passages.md).
Reading extents and status observations below describe that initial pass.
The replacement corrects its annihilator orientation and module-completion
scope. The dated updates below record selected later reading and later
developments; the remaining initial-pass rows retain their original scope.

## Scope and evidence rules

The book program covers Chapters I–VII, Appendices A–C, mathematical assertions
in the chapter notes, and unnumbered assertions.

For Chapters VI–VII, the chosen generality is an explicit framework over actual
groups, topologies, subgroups, decompositions, lattices, roots, Weyl actions and
maps. Chapter V uses and helps build the common framework of
the Library's algebraic and reductive group schemes (`Langlands/GroupScheme/`)
and its Kaletha–Prasad Bruhat–Tits foundation (`Langlands/BruhatTits/`). Its
structural assertions are precise Prop-valued declarations about those
frameworks' actual groups, rational-point topologies, parabolics, Levi and
unipotent subgroups, lattices, roots, Weyl actions, compact subgroups and maps.
Underlying data, intrinsic construction laws and external structural theorem
hypotheses remain distinct; parallel Renard definitions are not the target.

The finite Chapter V structural ledger is a temporary bridge while the shared
frameworks prove the external results. An assertion imported without proof in
the book may remain a named, itemized hypothesis, with its exact source
locator, external reference, actual declaration, consumer and discharge
status recorded. Chapters VI–VII may proceed conditionally with every such
hypothesis visible. In-book structural derivations remain proof obligations.
Representation-theoretic conclusions such as admissibility, Bernstein
decomposition, second adjointness and classification cannot be inserted into
that structural hypothesis list.

The completion target is the common general algebraic-group/Bruhat–Tits
framework and its independently verified construction, comparison and
representation-theoretic adapters, including the checked GL specialization
bridges. This replaces the former optional-realization scope rule. An
undischarged structural hypothesis is not a theorem, and a conditional
framework theorem is not an unconditional theorem for every rational-point
group.

The framework must retain positive-characteristic local fields wherever the
source permits them. GL-specific implementations are specialization candidates,
not proofs of arbitrary reductive statements. Every conditional result must
display its remaining hypotheses.

The ledger uses the following distinctions:

| Status | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Backlog | A section is located, but its individual assertions have not all been read and classified. |
| Read/open | The listed passage has been read; the stated implementation or correspondence obligation remains open. |
| Definition specified | A focused construction is specified; this alone supplies no Lean result. |
| Candidate | A Lean version exists that is not yet in the Library. |
| Reuse lead | An exact declaration or module has been inspected, but the complete Renard correspondence has not been closed. |
| Statement/proof covered | Reserved for exact current declarations of the Library and checked source correspondence. No new Renard row has this status in this initial ledger. |
| External structural input | Reserved for an itemized Chapter V external assertion with its original locator/reference and visible downstream hypotheses. The initial Chapter V rows are proposed classifications, not a completed accepted interface. |

A blank Lean-name
entry is an unresolved obligation, not an assertion that no suitable Mathlib
theorem exists. Independent universes, coefficient fields, support conditions,
topologies and normalization signs must be checked before assigning coverage.
