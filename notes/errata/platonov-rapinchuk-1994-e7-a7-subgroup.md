# Platonov–Rapinchuk §6.8: the `A_7` subgroup of a simply connected `E_7` is `SL_8/μ_2`

Source: V. Platonov and A. Rapinchuk, *Algebraic Groups and Number Theory* (1994),
registered as `platonov-rapinchuk-1994-algebraic-groups-number-theory`. In the
registered PDF the PDF page is the printed page plus 10. This note is a source
audit; its computations are elementary lattice calculations, not Lean theorems.

## The printed claims

- Proof of Proposition 6.21, printed p. 377 (PDF p. 387): for a group `G` of type
  `E_7`, the negative `−μ` of the maximal root and the simple roots `α_i`, `i ≠ 2`,
  generate a closed subsystem `R_0` of type `A_7`; the root groups `G_α`, `α ∈ R_0`,
  generate a subgroup `H` of type `A_7` containing the maximal torus `T`, and the
  text continues "one can easily see ... that `H ≃ SL_8` over `E`".
- Groups of type `E_7`, printed p. 386 (PDF p. 396): the same `H` is called "a simply
  connected subgroup of `G` of type `A_7` containing `T`", and the Hasse principle
  for `G` is deduced from Theorem 6.6 for `H`.

## The computation

Let `G` be simply connected of type `E_7` over an algebraically closed field, with
maximal torus `T`. Then the cocharacter group `X_*(T)` is the coroot lattice
`Q^∨(E_7)`. The subgroup `H` generated by the root groups `U_α`, `α ∈ R_0`, is
connected semisimple with root system `R_0`. It contains the images of the coroots
`α^∨`, `α ∈ R_0`; these span a sublattice of full rank 7 in `X_*(T)`, so they
generate `T` and `T` is a maximal torus of `H`. Hence `H` is simply connected exactly
when `X_*(T)` equals the coroot lattice `Q^∨(R_0)`.

Both root systems are simply laced, so coroot lattices may be computed as root
lattices with the Cartan matrix as Gram matrix. The Cartan matrix of `E_7` has
determinant 2 and that of `A_7` has determinant 8. For a full-rank sublattice,
`[Q(E_7) : Q(A_7)]^2 = 8 / 2 = 4`, so the index is 2. Therefore
`π_1(H) = X_*(T)/Q^∨(R_0) ≅ ℤ/2` and `H` is isomorphic to `SL_8/μ_2`, not to `SL_8`.
Equivalently, the 56-dimensional representation of `E_7` restricts to `H` as
`Λ^2 V ⊕ Λ^2 V^*` for the standard 8-dimensional `V` of the cover `SL_8`, on which
`−1 ∈ SL_8` acts trivially.

## Effect on the arguments

- In Proposition 6.21 only the 7-primary part of `H^1(L/E, T)` is computed. A
  quotient of `SL_8` by `μ_2` does not change 7-primary torsion, so that conclusion
  is not affected; the identification `H ≃ SL_8` should not be used literally.
- On printed p. 386 the Hasse principle of Theorem 6.6 is stated for simply
  connected groups and does not apply to `H`. In particular the local vanishing at
  the finite places, which Theorem 6.4 gives for simply connected groups, is not
  available for `H`, while Lemma 6.28 (printed p. 369) only makes the torus cocycle
  trivial at the archimedean places. The printed deduction of the Hasse principle
  for `E_7` is therefore incomplete as written.
- The analogous subgroups in the arguments for `G_2` (long roots, type `A_2`,
  printed pp. 368–369) and `F_4` (long roots, type `D_4`, printed p. 371) are simply
  connected: the coroots of the long roots are the short coroots, which span the
  whole coroot lattice in types `G_2` and `F_4`.

## A route that does not use `H`

The following outline is how the quadratic step can be organized instead; it is the
componentwise argument of the proof of Proposition 6.25 (printed pp. 394–395). Let
`L/K` be the quadratic extension, `σ` its generator and `T = B ∩ σ(B)` as in
Lemma 6.17 (printed p. 329), so that `σ` acts on `X(T)` as `−1`. Since `G` is simply
connected, the simple coroots form a basis of `X_*(T)`, and `T` is the product of
their images, each the norm-one torus `R^1_{L/K} G_m`. For a simple root `α` the
subgroup generated by `U_α` and `U_{−α}` is defined over `K`, simply connected of
type `A_1` and split by `L`, that is, `SL_1(Q)` for a quaternion algebra `Q`
containing `L`. Each component of a torus cocycle that is trivial at the archimedean
places is then trivial in the corresponding `SL_1(Q)` by the Hasse principle for
`SL_1(Q)`, and conjugating the torus one component at a time, as on printed p. 394,
trivializes the whole cocycle.
