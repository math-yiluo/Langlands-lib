# Automorphic differential growth: source map and remaining obligations

This note records the source route for the exact proposition
`Langlands.Automorphic.AutomorphicDifferentialGrowthStatement` in
`Langlands.Automorphic.PreservationStatement`. It concerns the genuine
enveloping derivatives of actual automorphic forms, over every number field
and at every natural rank. Polynomial growth of a smooth function alone does
not imply polynomial growth of its derivatives. Compact finiteness and the
common finite-codimensional center ideal remain essential hypotheses.

This is a source audit, not an accepted proof of differential growth,
reproduction, admissibility, or analytic regularity. The reading and Library
checks below were completed on 23 October 2026 by Worker 94.

## The convolution route

Borel–Jacquet, section 2.2, printed page 193 / PDF page 188, explains the
compact smooth reproducing-convolution argument. Sections 4.2–4.3 give its
adelic setting. Harish-Chandra–Mars, Lemma 14, printed page 15 / PDF page 25,
gives reproduction for analytic compact-finite and center-finite functions
and refers to Harish-Chandra's *Discrete series II*, page 18. The book is
Springer LNM **62**; Borel–Jacquet's bibliography prints 68.

The intended right-convolution convention is
`(f * κ)(g) = ∫ y, f(y) κ(y⁻¹g) dμ(y)` for left Haar measure. The book's
left-convolution presentation must be transported with its quotient/action
conventions. The elementary growth estimate uses `y = gz`, the
submultiplicative matrix/inverse height, and integrability of the continuous
compactly supported function
`z ↦ ‖κ(z⁻¹)‖ * matrixHeight(z)^d`. It preserves the supplied exponent.
Existence of a reproducing kernel and transfer of differentiation to the
kernel are additional assertions.

The Library contains the independently published statement pairs
[ArchimedeanConvolutionGrowthStatement](../../Langlands/Global/ArchimedeanConvolutionGrowthStatement.md)
and
[ArchimedeanEnvelopingSupportStatement](../../Langlands/Global/ArchimedeanEnvelopingSupportStatement.md).
Their targets are `CompactKernelConvolutionBoundStatement` and
`EnvelopingSupportStatement` in `Langlands.Global.ArchimedeanGL`.
Statement publication does not establish these propositions. The latter
pair includes the elementary implication from support containment to
compact-support preservation.

## What reproduction requires

Harish-Chandra, *Discrete series II*, sections 7–8, printed pages 15–19,
works with a connected real reductive group, an actually compact analytic
subgroup K, and an analytic Cartan decomposition `K × p → G`.
Theorem 1 assumes finite-dimensional joint orbit under the ambient enveloping
center and the algebra generated by the complexified compact Lie algebra.
It produces a compact smooth K-conjugation-invariant reproducing kernel
inside any prescribed identity neighborhood.

Its proof works in the locally convex smooth-function topology. A
Hahn–Banach separator of the enveloping-generated orbit gives a smooth
coefficient function. Elliptic analyticity and vanishing of all jets force
this function to vanish, proving density in the closed group-generated
orbit. Algebraic admissibility and continuous compact-type projections
make the relevant compact-type space finite-dimensional. Compact-central
smooth approximate identities converge in that space; the convolution
image is a closed finite-dimensional subspace, so it contains the function
it approximates.

Each of these is a mathematical obligation. The published algebraic smooth
carrier's inherited topology must not silently be replaced by the topology
of uniform convergence on compact sets of all derivatives.

## The algebraic finiteness step

Harish-Chandra, *Representations of a semisimple Lie group on a Banach
space I*, Theorem 1, printed page 195 / PDF page 11, starts with a
semisimple Lie algebra and its complexified Cartan decomposition. Write
`B = U(g)` and X for the subalgebra generated by the compact Lie algebra.
For a left ideal J of X with finite-dimensional semisimple quotient X/J,
the simple compact-Lie isotypic parts of B/BJ are **finitely generated over
the center of B**. This does not yet say they are finite-dimensional over
the complex numbers.

The application needs passage to the relevant quotient and annihilation by
a finite-codimensional center ideal. The proof on printed pages 195–200
uses PBW/symmetrization, invariant polynomial rings, restriction to Cartan
subspaces, and Noetherian covariants. Its semisimple hypotheses also require
an explicit reductive central extension before application to general
linear groups.

The project's locally constant compact-open averaging constructions and
compact-coefficient isotypic constructions do not by themselves provide the
archimedean finite-dimensional compact types or their continuous Haar
projections.

## The analyticity step

Harish-Chandra, *Two theorems on semi-simple Lie groups*, Lemma 33, printed
pages 108–109 / PDF pages 35–36, assumes finite-dimensional orbit of a
distribution under the product of the ambient enveloping center and the
center of the designated compact-direction enveloping algebra.

For orthonormal compact and complementary bases, let `ωk` and `ωp` be
the corresponding sums of squares. The proof constructs a central element
`ω = -ωk + ωp`. Then `u = ω + 2ωk` is the full sum of squares.
Finite-dimensionality supplies a monic polynomial P such that `P(u)T = 0`.
The operator P(u) is elliptic, and analytic elliptic regularity gives the
conclusion. The source invokes that regularity theorem without proving it
in this lemma.

Thus actual operator construction and ellipticity, polynomial annihilation,
and analytic elliptic regularity must be accounted for separately. For the
separator argument, a smooth-function specialization suffices; the full
distribution-valued version need not be formalized first. A targeted search
of the pinned Mathlib `Analysis` sources found no matching elliptic PDE
analytic-regularity theorem. This is not an exhaustive survey of external
Lean libraries.

There is a source convention to preserve. Section 16 of *Two theorems*
uses a subgroup containing the full group center and compact modulo that
center. Section 7 of *Discrete series II* uses an actually compact K and
allows noncompact central directions in p; its proof cites the **proof**
of Lemma 33. The matrix involution `X ↦ -X*`, with conjugate transpose at
complex places, has noncompact scalar directions in its complementary
eigenspace. The actual GL Cartan decomposition and finite-component
specialization still require proof.
