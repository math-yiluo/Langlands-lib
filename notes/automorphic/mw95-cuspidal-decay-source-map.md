## The uniform growth quantifiers

I.2.3, pp.24–25, distinguishes moderate growth from uniform moderate growth
of a smooth function and its enveloping derivatives. The latter has the
quantifier order

\[
 \exists r\in\mathbb R\quad
 \forall X\in\mathcal U\quad
 \exists c_X\in\mathbb R\quad
 \forall g\in G,\qquad
 |\delta(X)\phi(g)|\le c_X\|g\|^r.
\]

The exponent is independent of \(X\); the constant may depend on \(X\).
The enveloping action is the actual action generated by right translation.
Separate polynomial-growth assertions for each derivative allow the
exponent to vary and do not express this condition.

I.2.5, pp.26–27, provides the smoothing input. Convolution by a smooth
compactly supported test function takes a moderate-growth function to one
whose derivatives have uniformly moderate growth. The same conclusion holds
for the source's square-integrable vectors with a unitary central character.
The proof transfers enveloping derivatives to the compact kernel and uses
the counting/integration estimates of I.2.4. This is an analytic theorem,
not a consequence of formal differentiability of an \(L^2\) class.

I.2.17, pp.37–39, defines automorphic forms using moderate growth,
smoothness, compact finiteness and enveloping-centre finiteness. Its lemma
on p.38 gives a single smooth compactly supported, left and right
compact-finite test function that reproduces any specified finite-dimensional
space of such forms; it cites Harish-Chandra, *Discrete series II*, §8,
Theorem 1. Applying this to the span of one form and then applying I.2.5
gives the required uniform derivative growth. The uniformity in this last
sentence is the direct consequence of those two source statements.

The remark on p.39 explicitly postpones the automorphic finiteness theorem
because its proof uses the cuspidal theory. It also states that the
reproduction lemma is independent of that theorem. A proof route through
reproduction and I.2.5 therefore does not first assume the later cuspidal
finiteness conclusion.

## Constant-term subtraction and decay

I.2.10, pp.30–34, first treats a proper maximal standard parabolic
\(P=MU\), with its omitted simple root \(\alpha\).
For a fixed finite compact-open level, target enveloping element \(X\)
and \(t>0\), it selects finitely many derivative tests. Bounds for those
tests with input weight \(\lambda\) give a bound for
\(\delta(X)(\phi-\phi_P)\) with weight \(\lambda-t\alpha\), for
\(a\in A_G\) and \(g\in G^1\cap\mathcal S\).
The derivative tests can depend on the desired gain \(t\).
The proof uses a rational unipotent filtration, Fourier inversion on the
associated additive quotients, integration by parts and summability of
inverse powers on the resulting lattices. These steps require the actual
quotients and integrations.

I.2.11, p.34, combines the maximal-parabolic estimates for the finite
alternating sum

\[
 s\phi(g)=
 \sum_{P=M U,\ P_0\subseteq P\subseteq G}
 (-1)^{\operatorname{rg}(G)-\operatorname{rg}(M)}\phi_P(g).
\]

The term \(P=G\) is included. For each desired output weight and derivative
it asks for only finitely many input derivative estimates, all with a
common exponent. This finite dependence is why uniform derivative growth
supplies the hypotheses for every decay weight.

I.2.12, pp.34–35, defines rapid decrease on this fixed Siegel domain by

\[
 \exists r>0\quad
 \forall\lambda\in\operatorname{Re}X_{M_0}\quad
 \exists c>0\quad
 \forall a\in A_G\quad
 \forall g\in G^1\cap\mathcal S,\qquad
 |\phi(ag)|\le c\|a\|^r m_{P_0}(g)^\lambda.
\]

In particular, \(r\) precedes the choice of \(\lambda\). The estimate
retains central growth; it is not unrestricted decay in all directions.
The following corollary says that \(s\phi\) is rapidly decreasing for
smooth compact-finite \(\phi\) with uniformly moderate growth of its
derivatives. The corollary also has a separate function-field
compact-support assertion, which is not used here.

For a cuspidal form on \(G(k)\backslash G\), every proper constant term
vanishes, so the displayed sum is \(\phi\). This is the immediate
cuspidal specialization of the source's sum, rather than an extra
analytic hypothesis. I.2.18, p.39, explicitly applies I.2.12 to cusp forms
and discusses the corresponding central-character pairing and completion.
Those completion assertions require their own formal proofs.

## Comparisons and remaining formal work

The following obligations remain separate.

| Interface | Required comparison or proof |
| --- | --- |
| Uniform growth | Construct the genuine general-reductive enveloping action and height, and retain one exponent for all derivatives. Relate finite-level and archimedean descriptions to the global adelic estimate. |
| Root coordinates | Identify MW95's \(m_{P_0}(g)^\lambda\) with the actual relative-character coordinates used by the chosen Iwasawa decomposition. Prove the character/root comparisons and positivity properties. |
| Siegel domains | Relate the fixed source domain to all the Siegel sets quantified in GH Definition 9.6.1, with the necessary rational and compact-translation controls. A covering assertion alone supplies no analytic estimate. |
| Split centre | Identify the MW95 and GH central factors and their relation to \(G^1\); carry the normalization and central-character hypotheses through the comparison. |
| Cusp periods | Prove existence, normalization and regularity of the actual unipotent integrals, their compatibility with derivatives and rational translation, and vanishing of the proper terms. |
| Decay proof | Formalize the finite-level lattice/Fourier estimates, their uniformity on the compact parameters, and the maximal-parabolic subtraction argument. |
| Hilbert comparison | Use proved decay, integrability, smoothing and finite-dimensionality to compare classical cusp forms with the Hilbert cuspidal summand. A supplied Hilbert direct sum does not prove this comparison. |

Once the weight comparison has been proved, choosing weights in negative
simple-root directions is the intended route to the rootwise estimates
of GH 9.6.1. This is a plan, not an asserted equivalence of the two decay
definitions. No step here uses the unrestricted raw-representative
height bound of the printed GH 9.6.2.
