# Arthur L3 foundations: declarations and proof targets

This note is a block of the [global automorphic program](global-program-coverage.md). It records the ten modules verified in Library commit `f42cd5431418d9ab04000ccf365e7529bce80c54` and the fifteen propositions they state. A proposition in the Library is not a proof.

Arthur 2005 is *An introduction to the trace formula*, in `arthur-ellwood-kottwitz-2005-harmonic-analysis-shimura` (PDF page = printed page + 12). Arthur 1978 is *A trace formula for reductive groups I*, `arthur-1978-trace-formula-reductive-groups-I`. Arthur 1980 is *A trace formula for reductive groups II*, `arthur-1980-trace-formula-reductive-groups-II` (PDF page = printed page − 85). Arthur 1988 is *The invariant trace formula. I. Local theory*, `arthur-1988-invariant-trace-formula-I` (PDF page = printed page − 322). Lapid 2011 is *On Arthur's asymptotic inner product formula of truncated Eisenstein series*, `lapid-2011-asymptotic-inner-product-truncated-eisenstein` (PDF page = printed page + 9). The paired Markdown in the links gives the source comparisons, including the qualifications below.

## Root combinatorics and real parameters

- [ArthurRelativeCombinatorics](../../Langlands/GroupScheme/Rational/ArthurRelativeCombinatorics.md), `Langlands.GroupScheme.ArthurRelative`: `rootPairing` and `base` use the indivisible relative roots (also for non-reduced systems); `standardIndex` transports standard-parabolic indexing. `toCorootSpan` removes the central part, and `levelProjection`, `aSpace`, `relativeSpace`, `weight`, `tau`, `tauHat`, `sigma`, `epsilon` and `phi` implement the Arthur 1978 §1 and §6 and Arthur 2005 §§5 and 8 conventions. This module contains proved identifications, not a new proposition target. The abstract `Langlands.ArthurCombinatorics.ArthurCombinatoricsStatement` already has the public proof `Langlands.ArthurCombinatorics.arthurCombinatoricsStatement` in `Langlands.LinearAlgebra.ArthurCombinatoricsProof`; no target here duplicates it.
- [ArthurParabolicSpaces](../../Langlands/Global/ArthurParabolicSpaces.md), `Langlands.Global.ArthurParabolicSpaces`: `restrictToTorus` acts on `RealCharacterParameter`, and `toCharacterDual` is its transpose on cocharacters with values in `RealCharacterDual`. The [comparison statement](../../Langlands/Global/ArthurParabolicSpacesStatement.md) concerns these maps for the actual standard group schemes. `bijective_restrictToTorus_empty` is a proved consequence *of* that statement, not its proof. It supplies the `hρ` used to read the minimal-parabolic height in `𝔞₀` when the comparison is proved.

## `(G, M)`-families

[GMFamilies](../../Langlands/LinearAlgebra/GMFamilies.md) implements families of smooth functions on the imaginary dual, face restriction, the Euclidean lattice-covolume factors of `theta` and `thetaHat`, the sum `cM`, and the product and descent coefficients. Its root arrangement is an abstract Euclidean model. The [statements](../../Langlands/LinearAlgebra/GMFamiliesStatement.md) use `Langlands.GMFamily`.

For a proper field extension, rational character base change need not agree with extending a parameter by orthogonal projection for an arbitrary invariant metric on the larger root space, so the same-field target does not cover that case of Lemma 17.5. [GMFamilyFieldDescent](../../Langlands/Global/GMFamilyFieldDescent.md) and its [statement](../../Langlands/Global/GMFamilyFieldDescentStatement.md) treat it for a connected reductive `G` over a field `k` of characteristic zero, an extension `K/k`, an actual rational Levi `M` of `G`, and an actual rational Levi `M₁` of `G_K` factoring through `M_K`. The target concludes `HasActualFieldDescent`. This consists of a map of the actual split components over `K` and coordinates in which the parameter map is rational-character base change followed by restriction. It also includes the restriction of the fine root weights to the coarse ones, compatibility of the fine-center projection, and the special-subspace condition with Lie weight-space multiplicities (`Module.finrank`). These comparisons are conclusions, not hypotheses. The target then asserts the descent identity for the smooth extensions at every imaginary parameter, including `λ = 0` as in (17.13). It does not assume that `K/k` is proper or Galois.

The same-field splitting and descent identities use their defining expressions away from a finite union of proper hyperplanes, not separate propositions at `λ = 0`. Passing to all parameters uses continuity of the smooth extensions (including the extension of the primed term in Lemma 17.3). The field-extension target is not part of the conjunction `GMFamilyStatement`.

## Truncation and Siegel sets

[ArthurTruncation](../../Langlands/Global/ArthurTruncation.md) defines `truncation` and `relativeTruncation` on full adelic points as in Lapid 2011, §2, p.312, with Arthur's relative sign `(−1)^{dim 𝔞_Q^P}`. The height is read from the L1 `IsIwasawaCompact.height` for `P₀` using the comparison `hρ`. Constant terms are the L1 subgroup integrals with quotient Haar probability measure along the unipotent radical. [ArthurSiegelSets](../../Langlands/Global/ArthurSiegelSets.md) defines the sets using the L1 ordered product `N₀ M₀¹ A₀ K` with compact factors from `AdelicSiegelFactors`, and defines `siegelIndicator` on adelic points: it is `1` exactly when a left `P(F)`-translate lies in the truncated set. This pulls back the characteristic function of the image in `P(F) \ G(𝔸_F)`, not in the L1 quotient `X_P`.

[ArthurNormOneTruncation](../../Langlands/Global/ArthurNormOneTruncation.md) defines `normOneTruncation` for functions on `G(𝔸_F)¹`, the domain in Arthur 2005, (13.1), by applying `truncation` to the zero extension. `normOneTruncation_restrict` proves that, pointwise on `G(𝔸_F)¹`, it is the restriction of the full truncation, because rational points and the adelic points of standard unipotent radicals lie in the norm-one subgroup. [GroupSchemeNormOneSmooth](../../Langlands/Automorphic/GroupSchemeNormOneSmooth.md), in `Langlands.Automorphic.GroupSchemeFunctions`, defines the actual archimedean norm-one subgroup `infiniteNormOneSubgroup`. It also defines the data `NormOneLieAtlas` of an embedded Lie structure on it, the fixed-level condition `IsNormOneCr`, and left-invariant derivatives `normOneDifferential` given by words in a basis of the identity tangent space. It does not prove that such an atlas exists.

`TruncationSelfAdjointStatement` includes local boundedness, convergence of truncated constant terms, and the pairing with a continuous function of compact support modulo `G(F)`. `TruncationProjectionStatement` is pointwise constant-term vanishing and idempotence, not a bounded projection on `L²`. `ArthurSiegelSetsStatement` has **one** maximal compact witness for covering, both finite-support partitions, pointwise idempotence and rapid decay. Its decay conclusion is on norm-one slices, for restrictions of full-adelic smooth uniformly moderately growing functions.

`TruncationOrthogonalProjectionStatement` asserts one suitable maximal compact `K` and, for the reflection data and `hρ`, a regularity threshold for `T`. Beyond it there is a continuous linear `IsStarProjection` on `Lp ℂ 2` for each of the two actual L1 rational quotient Haar measures, on the norm-one quotient and on the full quotient. Its value agrees almost everywhere with the pointwise truncation (`normOneTruncation`, respectively `truncation`) of every measurable representative that is bounded on compact sets and square-integrable. The two `L²` spaces are treated separately; no `L²` class on the full quotient is restricted to the norm-one quotient. The pairing with non-square-integrable inputs and the Siegel-region identity used in the proof of Lapid's Lemma 9 are not stated.

`TruncationRapidDecayStatement` produces an embedded Lie atlas on the actual `G(F∞)¹` (it is not a premise) and one suitable `K`. It then states, for sufficiently regular `T`: (a) rapid decrease of `normOneTruncation` on every `S ∩ G(𝔸_F)¹` for every smooth uniformly tempered function on the norm-one group; (b) the fixed-level `C^r` family estimate of (13.5)–(13.6). In (b) finitely many operators, an order and a constant are chosen before the family. The integrals and supremum are in `ℝ≥0∞`, and the family has jointly measurable evaluation. The height in these estimates is the Library's closed-representation height `RepresentationGrowth.adelicSourceNorm`. No bound uniform in `T` or in the finite level `K₀` is asserted.

All fifteen exact targets in the tables are propositions. Of them the Library proves `TruncationFinitenessStatement` (as `truncationFiniteness`); this note records no proof of the other fourteen (searched by statement name).
